The Trump administration has revoked more than 175,000 visas from foreign nationals since the president took office, according to the State Department.

That’s quadruple the pace of the final year of the Biden administration, an analysis by *The Guardian* found. 

The majority of the revocations were “due to law enforcement encounters for a range of criminal activity, with assault, driving under the influence, theft, and drug crimes being the leading causes,” the State Department said in a fact sheet on Tuesday.

The announcement noted that officials revoked visas for offenses ranging from disorderly conduct to parents seeking to be “birth tourists” and obtain U.S. citizenship for their children.

Some lost their visas for their political opinions, the State Department acknowledged, which wrote that it targeted “multiple foreign nationals who celebrated the assassination of Charlie Kirk, including one who stated ‘when fascists die, democrats don’t complain’ and another who said he ‘died too late.’”

Critics dismissed the announcement as vague.

“The Department of State’s statement lumps foreign policy cases together with DUI arrests and people’s political speech, making the headline number almost meaningless and raises serious questions about how much discretion the administration is exercising without meaningful accountability,” American Immigration Council senior policy counsel Adriel Orozco told *The Guardian.*

In January, the department announced more than 100,000 visas had been revoked in the first year of the second Trump administration, in what it said was a record.

As *The Independent* has reported, the administration also made a point of revoking visas belonging to pro-Palestine activists.

The Trump administration has sought to limit the number of immigrants entering the U.S. and increase the number of those expelled from it.

![The Trump administration has used deportation raids, a refugee freeze and legal changes to reduce the number of immigrants inside the U.S.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/11/21/2026-07-30T100939Z_1094157823_RC28XIATCHMR_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-ASSAULT-CASES.JPG)

It has revoked Temporary Protected Status from thousands of immigrants in the U.S., who had previously been barred from removal because their home countries were deemed excessively unsafe or unstable, such as those from Haiti.

The president has also attempted to shut down the border to asylum-seekers and has paused most refugee resettlement, other than for white South Africans the administration has baselessly claimed are facing a genocide.

The administration has taken other steps, including launching a Justice Department effort to “prioritize and maximally pursue” denaturalization cases against American citizens and pushing to end birthright citizenship despite a recent Supreme Court ruling.
