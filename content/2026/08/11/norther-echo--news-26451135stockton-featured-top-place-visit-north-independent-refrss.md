A Teesside town has been named one of the top three places to visit in the North of England by The Independent thanks to its bold waterfront transformation and rich history.

Stockton ranked in The Independent's list of the "ten best places to visit in the north of England this summer", with the publication praising the town's ambitious approach to regeneration.

Travel writer Chris Moss highlighted the £23 million Stockton Waterfront Urban Park, created on the former site of the Castlegate Shopping Centre and the Swallow Hotel, as a key reason for the town's success.

  Stockton's Waterfront Urban Park. 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The new park covers an area three times the size of Trafalgar Square and has transformed Stockton riverside.

However, the Teesside town's appeal extends far beyond its newest landmark.

The town is home to the historic Stockton Market, which sits on what is widely regarded as one of the widest high streets in the UK.

The market attracts shoppers and visitors with its regular traders, artisan stalls and special events throughout the year.

Stockton has also built a reputation as one of the region's cultural hotspots, thanks largely to the Stockton International Riverside Festival (SIRF).

  Opening day of the Stockton International Riverside Festival at Stockton Waterfront urban park 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The annual event, regarded as the North East's leading free outdoor arts festival, brings together performers from across the UK and around the world to transform the town centre into a giant open-air stage.

In 2026, the festival featured more than 100 performances.

Visitors looking to delve into the area's heritage can explore Preston Park Museum and Grounds, one of Teesside's most popular attractions.

Set beside the River Tees, the park combines green space and incredible views with a museum dedicated to the history of Stockton and the surrounding area.

  Preston Park. 

*(Image: Gary Groom)*

ARC Stockton Arts Centre has become a cultural hub for the North East - hosting theatre productions, comedy, live music, cinema screenings and community events throughout the year.

Stockton also occupies an important place in Britain's transport history as one of the termini of the Stockton and Darlington Railway, which opened in 1825 and is widely regarded as the world's first passenger railway.

  The Darlington & Stockton Railway.

That legacy continues to draw history and train enthusiasts from across the country.

In its feature, The Independent suggested Stockton had taken a more radical approach to town centre regeneration than many of its counterparts, opting to create a major public park rather than replacing declining retail space with more shops or housing.

The recognition places Stockton alongside some of northern England's most celebrated destinations.
