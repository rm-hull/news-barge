# 'I run a dog bus - here are three ways owners can turn their dogs into better travellers'

## Travelling with dogs is becoming increasingly popular.

Yet, while coffee shops serve ‘puppuccinos’ and hotels launch dog-friendly stays, the car journey itself can be challenging.

Not all dogs are natural travellers. Many owners find themselves managing canine anxiety, toileting accidents and barking.

Last year, Laura Mohan, 45, launched Britain’s first dog bus. The Rawdon-based dog walker converted a school bus, transporting pets from across Leeds on special days out.

![Billy's Dog Bus has been a resounding success since it launched last year. Credit: Laura Mohan](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU0ZmFhMWQ5LTU4MmEtNGNhNi05YmY1LTM3YWJiYmNlYjBlYjowMzNiODUxMC0yMmNlLTRhYWEtOWVjYi1lYTVjMzJmODBkMmQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



No stranger to travelling with a dozen dogs at once, Laura has some tried-and-tested tips for keeping pooches happy on the road.

**Make travelling comfortable**

“If you want a happy dog, you’ve got to make sure they are comfortable when travelling,” said Laura.

According to her, it’s important not to overlook the basics. A comfortable seat with a secure harness allows dogs to safely settle, while a window seat might keep pups entertained.

![Laura Mohan. Credit: Laura Mohan](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjIzOWQ2NDc0LTIzOTctNGVkYS1hNzZhLTkzM2ViNjg2NzYyOTo1NWZhOTNmYy1iMThkLTRkYmUtOTY0Zi01NGZjNDE2OWE5MGU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Always check the air-conditioning is working and reaches the area where your dog will be sitting.

“If it’s too hot they shouldn’t be in the boot,” says Laura, adding that cooling mats are helpful in hot weather.

Emotional support is also important.

“We have had some really anxious dogs come on,” said Laura, explaining that some “pawrents” have worried that bus transportation might not work for their pets.

“Now they're bounding onto our bus not looking behind them.

“I think if you've got an anxious pup it's always important that somebody's sat with them in the back, just stroking them, just reassuring them.”

**Be proactive with toileting**

Toilet breaks are a common worry for anyone travelling with dogs. Billy’s Bus has only recorded three toileting incidents, one of which Laura chalked up to an “anxious first rider.”

Travelling with basic wipes or cleaning supplies might be wise. However, owners should always prioritise a short walk beforehand. For long drives, plan quiet stops along the way.

Laura said most travellers already plan regular stops when driving longer than four hours.

“They're making regular stops for toilet breaks for themselves anyway, but more importantly for the dog,” she said.

**Start small**

“Choose shorter trips to begin with, especially when they are puppies, because they need to go to the toilet a lot more,” said Laura.

Rescued or older dogs with limited experience of travelling by car might require a patient approach. Still, overcoming car anxiety is possible.

Some of the canine clients at Billy’s Bus have previously been involved in car accidents. After regular 45-minute drives, they now feel confident with travel again.

She advises owners to keep it simple: “Start with 10-minute rides, build it up to half an hour at a time, and see how they get on.”
