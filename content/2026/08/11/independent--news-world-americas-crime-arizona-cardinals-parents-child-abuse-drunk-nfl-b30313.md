An Arizona couple have been accused of getting drunk and forcing their toddlers to walk in 108-degree heat after the family watched an NFL practice last weekend.

Ryan and May Mullin were seemingly intoxicated, stumbling across a road in Glendale with their kids, ages two and four, when a bystander tried to help them, AZFamily reported, citing court documents.

The couple had been walking with their children in extreme heat for several hours after attending the Arizona Cardinals’ open practice at State Farm Stadium, according to local police.

The female bystander who tried to help the family called authorities Sunday evening, reporting that Ryan Mullin had crossed the road without the children and fallen over one of them. She also told authorities that May was possibly vomiting.

The woman told police she pulled up to the family and offered them a ride, but Ryan Mullin then got angry and punched her vehicle.

![Ryan and May Mullin have been accused of getting drunk and forcing their toddlers to walk in 108-degree heat during a family outing in Glendale, Arizona](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/11/17/02/7BIFYCOAINEQ3BX4HY2VHM5R4I.jpeg)

Ryan Mullin then reportedly fell and hurt his face. He had bruising and scrapes on his face in his mugshot.

When police arrived, an officer told Ryan Mullin to stop crossing the street with one of his kids, but he allegedly ignored the command.

The father then grabbed his child’s wrist, refusing to let go when the officer ran up to them. Ryan Mullin eventually let go of the kid when the officer threatened to use force, and he was restrained, according to police.

It’s unclear what the couple’s blood alcohol content was, but police reported that they smelled alcohol on Ryan and May Mullin’s breath, and they both had slurred speech and bloodshot eyes.

![The Arizona couple, who are now facing a child abuse charge, had attended the Arizona Cardinals’ open practice at State Farm Stadium with their kids on Sunday before the incident](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/11/18/41/GettyImages-2288576895.jpg)

May Mullin reportedly told police the family had been walking for roughly three hours after leaving the NFL practice.

When authorities asked whether the toddlers had enough water, May Mullin said they drank two bottles, according to court documents. But one of the bottles of water was still halfway full, police said.

It’s unclear what condition the kids were in when authorities found them. But when exposed to extreme heat, especially for extended periods, people can develop heat stroke, the most severe heat-related illness.

Heat stroke occurs when the body’s temperature rises rapidly and it is unable to cool itself. It can lead to permanent disability or even death if the person doesn’t receive medical attention right away, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention warns.

The couple now faces a child abuse charge, and Ryan was also hit with three counts of disorderly conduct.
