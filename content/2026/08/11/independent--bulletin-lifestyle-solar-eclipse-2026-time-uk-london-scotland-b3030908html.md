# What time is the solar eclipse in the UK? Here’s the full rundown

- The UK is set to witness its most complete solar eclipse in 27 years on Wednesday.
- Ahead of the eclipse, the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has issued guidance on how Britons can watch the eclipse safely, such as using special eclipse glasses, a pinhole viewer or colander to view its shadow.
- According to Royal Museums Greenwich, viewers in Scotland will be the first to see the eclipse, with so-called ‘first contact' – when the moon first covers the disc of the sun – at **6:08pm** .
- Meanwhile, in Manchester and Liverpool ‘first contact’ is at **6:13pm**, and in London, Bristol and Cardiff it is at** 6:17pm**. Those in Cornwall will see it at** 6:18pm** .
- The eclipse’s peak will occur between **7:05pm** and**7:16pm**, depending on viewers’ location, before ending for all by** 8.10pm** .
