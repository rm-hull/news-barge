Donald Trump demanded war compensation from Iran on Monday in a shocking reversal of the six-point ultimatum presented to Washington as conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran’s top security official said on Saturday that they would allow traffic to pass through the key channel only once the US meets six terms, including “payment in full” for damage inflicted during the war.

But Trump snubbed the proposal in a message on Truth Social, saying: “I see that Representatives of the Islamic Republic of Iran are asking for compensation for the damage done to them during the last five month Military Conflict ... even though it was never mentioned in any of our negotiations or meetings!”

“It is an interesting idea because now I am likewise demanding compensation from Iran,” Trump said, adding: “I have instructed my representatives to put this firmly into any, and all, future negotiations.”

Oil prices settled five per cent higher on Monday after the demands dimmed prospects for a deal to reopen the strait.

Among Iran’s other demands in its six-point ultimatum are a permanent end to fighting against Iran and its proxies and the lifting of the US naval blockade of Iranian ports.

## What to make of Trump's claims for compensation from Iran

Donald Trump on Monday responded to Iran's conditions for a peace deal with his own demands that Iran pay compensation for people killed in wars, attacks and protests, in a rhetorical escalation likely to complicate efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump said in a post on Truth Social that Iran had never mentioned such terms in their prior meetings. But the Iranian demands were largely in line with the terms of a preliminary peace deal signed in June, which has since broken down.

Referring to Tehran's crackdown on demonstrations in late 2025 and 2026, he also said Iran had killed some 52,000 protesters. The US based Human Rights Activists News Agency (HRANA) put the figure closer to 6,500 in February.

Trump also said Iran should compensate families of the 17 U.S. sailors killed in an October 2000 attack in Yemen on the naval vessel USS Cole, which has been blamed on al Qaeda, not Iran.

While Washington continues to insist a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz is coming, Iran says its terms must be met first. Also, an agreement with Oman would give Tehran more leverage over the key waterway than it had before the war began, further complicating future talks with the US.

## What are Iran's six demands of the US to reopen the Strait of Hormuz?

Iran’s top security official has said the Strait of Hormuz will remain shut until the US meets six demands, including compensation for the war.

Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, laid out Iran’s terms in a statement on Saturday.

In his words, these were:

1. Never, and in no language, threaten Iran or insult the sanctities of this nation.

2. Permanently end the war and aggression against Iran and Iran’s allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen and Iraq.

3. Lift the naval blockade and withdraw its military forces (naval and air) from around Iran.

4. Pay in full, without any reduction, the damages caused to Iran by the two wars of aggression and imposition.

5. Lift the unjust and illegal sanctions against the Iranian nation.

6. Unconditionally release the blocked and stolen assets of the Iranian people.

## Trump says US has cleared Strait of Hormuz of mines

Donald Trump now claims the US has already mineswept the entire Strait of Hormuz.

Iran has maintained a grip on traffic passing between the Arabian Sea and the Persian Gulf while insisting the narrow channel remains shut.

Intertanko, the tanker owners association, relayed estimates in June there were around 80 mines still in the strait hindering navigation and keeping ships to agreed Iranian routes.
