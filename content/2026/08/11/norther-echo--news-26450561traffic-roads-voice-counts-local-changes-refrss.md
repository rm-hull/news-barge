### What is the Public Notice Portal?

The Public Notice Portal carries statutory public notices published in local newspapers and is the fastest and most effective way of finding out what is happening in YOUR neighbourhood.

Search the Public Notice Portal
Have you ever noticed new yellow lines on your street, a change to a speed limit or a sudden road closure and wondered how it was decided?

The answer, in most cases, is that it wasn't decided quietly – and you had the right to have your say.

### What are traffic and roads notices?

Traffic management orders are official notices used to inform the public about proposed changes affecting roads in their area.

These can be permanent, such as new parking restrictions, altered speed limits or one-way systems, or temporary, such as a road closure for resurfacing work or a street party.

Councils and highways authorities are legally required to publicise these proposals before they take effect, giving residents a genuine opportunity to respond.

### Why does community input matter?

When a local authority proposes a change – whether it is introducing a 20mph zone near a school, removing a parking bay or closing a road for a major event – public consultation is not just a formality.

It ensures that you have the opportunity to make your views clear to those leading the project.

Community feedback can and does shape the outcome, making it important to engage while the proposal is still open.

### What kinds of change can be proposed?

- Traffic notices cover a wide range of proposals, including:
- Introducing or removing yellow lines and parking bays
- Changing speed limits or making streets one-way
- Placing zig-zag lines outside schools
- Installing speed bumps
- Closing roads for resurfacing, building works or new cables

### How to find notices near you

All traffic and roads notices published in printed local newspapers are uploaded to the Public Notice Portal – a free digital one-stop shop for public notices – by local publishers including The Northern Echo.

Search by postcode or browse a map, and sign up for email alerts so you never miss a proposal that could affect your street or commute.
