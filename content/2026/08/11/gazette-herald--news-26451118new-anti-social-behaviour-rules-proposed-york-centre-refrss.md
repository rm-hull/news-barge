New rules to tackle anti-social behaviour could soon be introduced in York city centre.

City of York Council is seeking public feedback on a proposed extension to its existing Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO), following a request from North Yorkshire Police for additional powers.

The proposals include five new conditions targeting behaviours such as intimidation, persistent requests for money, blocking pathways, invading personal space at cash machines, and disorderly group conduct.

Councillor Anna Perrett, Executive Member with responsibility for Safer Communities, said: "We are committed to ensuring that York remains a safe, welcoming and enjoyable place for residents, businesses and visitors.

"Your feedback will help us understand local experiences of anti-social behaviour and whether additional PSPO conditions would be an appropriate and proportionate measure to help address any identified issues."

The council said the PSPO would help address behaviours that negatively impact public quality of life, using powers available under the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014.

Breaching the proposed conditions within the PSPO area would be a punishable offence, subject to a fine.

The public consultation is open until September 18.

Residents can complete the survey online at www.york.gov.uk/local-democracy/say-current-consultations or request paper copies from Explore libraries or West Offices.
