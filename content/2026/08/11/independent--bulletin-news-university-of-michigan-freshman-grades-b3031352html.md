### University of Michigan drops grades for freshmen in the first semester to ‘curb mental health crisis’

# A university is combating its mental health crisis by dropping first-semester grades

- The University of Michigan will implement a pass or no-credit grading pilot program for first-semester freshmen in its College of Literature, Science, and the Arts beginning in fall 2027.
- Official transcripts will only record whether credit was earned, leaving grade-point averages unaffected, while faculty track grades internally for honors and scholarship eligibility.
- University officials state the initiative aims to address mental health challenges and ease the academic transition to college, following similar models at MIT and Caltech.
- The decision comes amid broader national debates over grade inflation, with 74.1 percent of Michigan undergraduate grades falling in the A range in 2022 compared to 57.6 percent a decade earlier.
- Reactions to the policy change are mixed, with student leaders welcoming reduced academic anxiety and critics expressing concern over declining academic standards.
