# The evolutionary reason we often have trouble sleeping in a new place

![One researcher recommends arriving a day early to acclimate before an important meeting or event](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/11/14/30/iStock-1477775597.jpg)

- The first-night effect is a widely documented phenomenon where individuals experience poorer, shorter, and more restless sleep when spending their initial night in an unfamiliar environment.
- Researchers attribute this phenomenon to an evolutionary adaptation that keeps a portion of the brain vigilant in new surroundings to protect against potential threats, similar to sleep patterns observed in animals like dolphins.
- Studies show that people sleep an average of 20 to 30 minutes less in new settings, with the effect being most pronounced in children and older adults, whereas individuals in their 20s are the least impacted.
- Additional factors such as travel fatigue, environmental disturbances like strange lighting or noise, and anxiety about sleeping poorly can further worsen sleep quality during the first night away.
- To mitigate the effect, sleep specialists recommend arriving a day early before critical events, maintaining consistent sleep routines, bringing familiar items like a personal pillow, and using mindfulness or visualization techniques.
