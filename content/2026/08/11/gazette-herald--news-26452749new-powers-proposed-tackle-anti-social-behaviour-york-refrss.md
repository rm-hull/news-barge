NEW powers could come into force to help tackle anti-social behaviour in York city centre.

City of York Council said it is considering expanding its existing Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO) following a request for additional powers from North Yorkshire Police.

PSPOs are legal tools intended to address behaviours that negatively affect the quality of life in a community, a council spokesperson said.

  


Five new conditions have been proposed, focusing on a range of behaviours which would be banned.

City of York Council said these are:

- pressuring or deliberately intimidating someone into giving money
- continuing to ask for money, for food, or pressure others to voluntarily do something, after an initial refusal
- following, nagging or blocking the path of an individual
- hindering free passage, impeding a doorway, or standing uncomfortably close to someone, such as directly beside them at a cash machine
- groups of people collectively engaged in shouting, aggressive or disruptive behaviour, or alcohol-fuelled behaviour likely to cause someone nearby to fear that disorder, abuse, or a confrontation may occur.

The council said violating any of these conditions within the city centre would be a punishable offence accompanied by a fine.

A public consultation is open until September 18 to gather views from residents and stakeholders.

Councillor Anna Perrett, executive member with responsibility for safer communities, said: "We are committed to ensuring that York remains a safe, welcoming and enjoyable place for residents, businesses and visitors.

"Your feedback will help us understand local experiences of anti-social behaviour and whether additional PSPO conditions would be an appropriate and proportionate measure to help address any identified issues.

"Your views are important, whether you support, oppose, or have concerns about extending the existing PSPO."

The public consultation runs until September 18.
