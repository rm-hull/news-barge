Long-term outdoor learning could be secured for children and young people across the region under new plans.

North Yorkshire Council aims to maintain outdoor education opportunities on the east coast while longer-term options are explored, following the closure of the East Barnby centre near Whitby in December 2025.

The closure was due to electrical and infrastructure issues that require significant investment to resolve.

Annabel Wilkinson, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for education, learning and skills, said: "For many children and young people, outdoor learning offers the chance to try new experiences, build friendships and develop skills that will stay with them throughout their lives.

"The proposals being considered seek to ensure there is no disruption to outdoor learning opportunities for children and young people while we consider options for the future delivery of the service on the east coast."

A proposal for an interim solution will be discussed by the council’s executive on Tuesday, August 18.

It suggests continuing residential outdoor learning on the coast through an arrangement with Birch Hall Scout Campsite in Scarborough.

This alternative site has already been used temporarily since the East Barnby closure, with schools transferring residential visits to Birch Hall.

The scout camp offers a range of activities, including coastal exploration, climbing, canoeing, and team-building exercises, which support the continuation of outdoor education programmes.

The East Barnby site, set against the backdrop of the North York Moors National Park, has provided generations of students with valuable experiences that complement classroom learning.

Many have left with increased confidence, stronger friendships, and lifelong memories.

The council also operates Bewerley Park, an outdoor education centre in the Nidderdale National Landscape near Harrogate.

The temporary arrangement with Birch Hall Scout Camp is intended to prevent disruption to existing outdoor learning opportunities while options for the future of the East Barnby service are explored.
