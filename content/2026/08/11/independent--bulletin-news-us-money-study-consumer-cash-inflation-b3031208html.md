# Money study paints bleak reality for US consumers

![US households are struggling in the current economic climate](<[https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/17/04/iStock-1428386188-(2](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/17/04/iStock-1428386188-(2)).jpg>)

- A survey by financial services firm Raisin revealed that 70 per cent of US consumers prefer receiving $1,000 immediately over $1,100 in a year due to acute household financial pressure.
- Consumers overwhelmingly favour accessible savings accounts over high-yield options that impose penalties for early cash withdrawals.
- Economic strain worsened after inflation topped 4 per cent following military action against Iran ordered by President Donald Trump, which drove petrol prices to multi-year highs.
- To manage rising living costs, Americans turned to discount stores, delayed vehicle maintenance, and bought fewer than 10 gallons of petrol per transaction.
- Inflation experienced its largest drop in six years in June, falling to 3.5 per cent and offering mild relief to struggling households.
