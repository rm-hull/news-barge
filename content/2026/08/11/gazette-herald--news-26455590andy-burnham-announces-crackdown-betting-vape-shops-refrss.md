Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced plans to crackdown on vape shops, betting shops and rogue businesses on British high streets.

The changes will mean that betting and vape stores will soon require planning permission before opening, giving councils and residents a direct say in shaping their high streets.

The new measures are part of a wider strategy to revitalise high streets and return decision-making power to communities.

Mr Burnham's plans will allow communities to have a say over what businesses can open in their town centres and how local high streets should look.

## Prime Minister announces crackdown on vape and betting shops

Under the proposed legislation, the Government plans to revoke the Gambling Act’s "Aim to Permit" rule, which limits councils’ ability to refuse new betting outlets even when there are strong objections from the community.

Adult Gaming Centres (AGCs), venues offering up to 24-hour access to slot and fruit machines, would also be subject to the same planning rules, with changes expected to take effect at the start of next year.

Currently, such businesses can open without planning permission as easily as a coffee shop or clothing retailer.

The Centre for Social Justice has reported that since 2016, nearly 1,800 pubs and bars have closed across the UK, while the number of vape and tobacco shops has risen to nearly 2,200.


Mr Burnham said: "For many people, the high streets they grew up with have been hollowed out and become unrecognisable over decades of decline.

"The rise of vape shops, betting shops and rogue operators have replaced the shops, services, and community spaces that people are crying out for.

"That’s not on.

"I said we would improve Britain’s high streets, and that’s exactly what we are starting to do.

"We're putting communities back in control and giving local people a real say over what opens on their high street.

"Local councils will be given the powers they need to rein in unwanted shops and bring town centres back to life.

"High streets are the beating heart of local communities, but for too long Westminster has stood by while they have been in decline.

"This government is committed to restoring our high streets and bringing back hope across Britain."


## Government shares plans to revitalise British high streets

Under the new measures, the government will also require planning permission for vape shops, giving locals a say on whether they should open and to stop them opening near schools.

  To make sure you are up to date on planning applications in your area, visit the **Public Notice Portal.**

The government also intends to double the maximum duration of Closure Orders from six to twelve months, giving police and councils more time to investigate businesses linked to organised crime.

Angela Rayner, secretary of state for housing, communities, and local government, said, "People know when their high street isn't working for them.

"Whether it’s the vape shop or betting shops that have opened with no say from locals, the shopfront that's seen better days, or the sense that nobody's in control.

"These problems have built up over years, but this government won't leave councils and communities powerless to crackdown on everyday eyesores that blight our high streets.

"That's why we're giving local leaders the power to say no to what doesn't belong on their high street, and the tools to fix it themselves."

  The government also plans to revoke the Gambling Act’s "Aim to Permit" rule 

*(Image: Getty Images)*

The Government also said it plans to work with councils to improve the appearance of town centres.

It aims to set clearer standards for shopfront design and signage, targeting issues like poor quality facades and garish neon lights that make high streets less appealing.

Over the next six months, councils will be supported in revitalising their high streets, drawing on successful examples such as Cross Street in Oswestry and Keynsham High Street.

The new measures come alongside other Government efforts to tackle rogue businesses linked to organised crime, including a £30 million campaign targeting money laundering, tax evasion, and illegal employment.

Additional actions include a 20 per cent business rates cut for pubs, social clubs, and live music venues across England from next year.

News Media Association Chief Executive Theo Bamber said: “The scrutiny of power that local news publishers and their journalists provide will be crucial to the success of the devolution agenda.

"Forty-two million people a month read local news across in print in digital. This illustrates the immense public appetite for the trusted local journalism and the value it has as a tool for proper democratic engagement.

"People around the country deserve full transparency from those making the decisions that affect their lives. That’s what local news delivers, and no democratically elected person should shy away from scrutiny or accountability.”
