Middlesbrough has formally submitted its bid to become UK City of Culture 2029, promising a year that would make the town “brighter, louder, safer, greener and more joyful”.

More than 2,000 residents, community groups, arts organisations and businesses helped shape the bid, which was submitted to the Government on Monday.

Young people are at the heart of the plans, with pupils from Newport Primary School among those playing a starring role after working with local poet Bob Beagrie on a letter backing Middlesbrough’s campaign.

The bid looks back at the town’s transformation from an industrial boomtown to what organisers describe as a modern centre for technology and innovation, while setting out how culture could help shape its next chapter.

Its introduction declares: “Just as we helped build the first industrial future, Middlesbrough is ready to help build what comes next.”

Middlesbrough is one of nine places longlisted for the 2029 title, which comes with a £10m prize fund to support a major year-long cultural programme.

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

A shortlist of four will be announced in October, with the winner revealed in January 2027.

The Middlesbrough bid has been developed by Middlesbrough Council, Teesside University, Middlesbrough Cultural Partnership and Tees Valley Combined Authority.

If successful, the River Tees and the area’s industrial landmarks could become stages for major events and spectacles, while the nearby coast and North York Moors would host outdoor cultural activities.

Middlesbrough FC would also feature prominently, with plans to weave a “Boro Red” thread through the town using permanent public art, lighting and installations.

Mass participation events would bring residents together under the banner “We Shall Be”.

Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke said the bid was “packed with hope and ambition for our future”.

He said: “It celebrates our global reputation for industry and innovation and shows how we can lead the way in using culture and creativity to change lives for the better.

“The investment and profile we’d attract by winning this title would have a positive effect for people young and old right across our communities.

“The benefits of being named UK City of Culture would be astronomical.”

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said Middlesbrough had “helped build the world” and continued to shape it.

“A City of Culture win would help us tell Middlesbrough’s story to the world, bring in new investment and create even more opportunities for local people,” he said.

Dr Laura Sillars, director of MIMA and Dean of Culture and Creativity at Teesside University, said Middlesbrough had always been a place of “makers, inventors and storytellers”.

She added: “Middlesbrough has never simply inherited the future; it has always set out to build it.”

The campaign has also attracted support from famous names with links to the area, including Middlesbrough manager Kim Hellberg, artist Mackenzie Thorpe and actor Mark Benton.

TV presenter Steph McGovern, Smoggie Queens creator Phil Dunning and Maximo Park frontman Paul Smith have also backed the bid.

Organisers hope winning the title would create more opportunities for local creative talent, encourage young people to build careers in the area and attract further investment.

The submission follows a busy period for Middlesbrough’s cultural scene, including its inaugural Storytelling Festival, BBC Proms events at the Town Hall and the tenth anniversary of Orange Pip Market.

Further events are on the way, with Middlesbrough Mela taking place in Albert Park and MIMA preparing to host the Turner Prize 2026 from September.

Middlesbrough Art Week will also return next month as the town waits to discover whether it has made the final four.
