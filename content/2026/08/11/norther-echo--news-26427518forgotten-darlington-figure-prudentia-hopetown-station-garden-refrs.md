**Today’s Object of the Week is a statue that once looked down on shoppers from high, then disappeared into storage for years before re-emerging.**

If you have walked through Hopetown, Darlington's railway museum, or stopped in the station garden lately, you may have seen a dignified classical figure in terracotta tones, standing quietly among the railway-side planting.

She is Prudentia, the personification of Prudence, and for decades she was an architectural flourish crowning the old Prudential Insurance Company building in Northgate.

She was installed there in 1911, a time when such symbolism mattered.

  The statue of Prudentia in her new setting at Hopetown station garden 

*(Image: ANDREW WHITE)*

Prudential was keen to project solidity, trust, and moral seriousness, and Prudentia - one of the four cardinal virtues - was an ideal emblem.

She was not just decoration.

Prudentia represents the ability to have the foresight to run one’s life using reason.

She was a statement of intent - all civic confidence and Victorian self-belief carried into the Edwardian era.

She has been the symbol of the Pru since it was founded in 1848, and she usually carries a convex mirror in her hand, which is a symbol of her wisdom.

For years she remained part of Darlington’s streetscape, visible from below but never really noticed by everyone who passed.

Then, in 1969, she came down from her perch with the advent of the third stage of the town's ring-road.

  Prudentia on the move in 1999 for her brief spell in the Cornmill 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Her journey after that was a little less dignified.

After several years in a packing case, from 1999, she had a brief spell at the Cornmill Centre, standing next to the elevators for four years.

Then she went back into her packing case, before being placed back into storage, where she remained for years.

  Mayor Bill Dixon with the Prudentia Statue in the Cormill Cente, Darlington, in 1999 

*(Image: CHRIS TINSLEY)*

And that, for many people, was that - a small piece of Darlington’s street history vanished from view.

But not for good.

Thanks to local interest in the town’s heritage, Prudentia has now been brought back into public sight.

She was placed in the station garden at Hopetown, Darlington's railway museum, last year (2025) - a fitting location for Prudentia as it is a site with gardening in its bones.

  Hopetown's station garden, continuing a legacy of horticulture on the Darlington site 

*(Image: ANDREW WHITE)*

As long ago as 1855, the land here included allotment gardens, and in 1895 the North Eastern Railway launched a station garden competition that proved so popular it was extended in 1907.

Special prizes were awarded to the best three stations on the NER, a sign of just how seriously these green spaces were taken.

Today’s station garden, funded by the Tees Valley Museums Group and created in consultation with the local community, carries that same spirit forward.

For a 115-year-old life-sized stone statue that has spent decades in storage, it is a very natural resting place, a setting shaped by the same mix of civic pride, public care, and quiet ambition that helped make station gardens such a feature in the first place.
