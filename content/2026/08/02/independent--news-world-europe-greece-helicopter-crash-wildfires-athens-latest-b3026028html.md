Two people have been killed after firefighting helicopters collided while fighting wildfires west of Athens on Sunday, Greece's fire department said.

Greek firefighters have been battling to control a huge wildfire that has destroyed over 100 homes northwest of Athens, while a fresh blaze forced evacuations on the popular tourist island of Kefalonia.

A Greek and a Danish national were killed in the helicopters collision, officials said. Greek media outlet Proto Theme reports that a British pilot and Greek liaison onboard the second helicopter both survived.

Officials said the crew of the second helicopter were picked up by an ambulance after being located.

The crash comes after three firefighters were killed while battling out-of-control wildfires on Crete and in mainland Greece.

In Crete, two firefighters died after becoming trapped between two fire fronts in the region of Rethymno on Wednesday. A third firefighter was found unconscious near the southern Peloponnese port town of Gytheio and was later pronounced dead.

## Greece the latest country to be hit by devastating wildfires

Greece has been the latest country struck by the devastating wildfires that have swept across southern Europe this week, in particular Spain and Italy.

Gale-force winds fuelling a fire around Porto Germeno, near Athens, have subsided.

But the flames crossed a mountain southwards and have reached the settlement of Veniza and a military firing range - causing unexploded ordnance to ignite.

Firefighters focused their efforts on preventing fire from advancing towards the coastal town of Megara, which has a population of around 30,000.

"There are moments when the force of nature and the intensity of weather conditions exceed all human planning and every operational capability,” Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis said in his regular Sunday message.

Greek authorities issued emergency alerts for evacuations for a number of settlements in western Attica, including an industrial estate near Megara.

Greek daily Protothema reported that a major effort was under way to prevent the fire from Veniza from reaching the nearby coastal town of Megara. It said more than 10,000 hectares of land had likely been destroyed by the fire so far.

## Two firefighting helicopters collide while battling Greece wildfire

Two firefighting helicopters collided in Greece while helping to control a huge wildfire on Sunday that has destroyed more than 100 homes northwest of Athens, while a fresh blaze forced evacuations on the popular tourist island of Kefalonia.

Europe has been ravaged by wildfires this summer after a period of record-breaking heatwaves and little rain – conditions that scientists say have been exacerbated by climate change.

Destructive blazes in France and Spain showed signs of easing over the weekend, but several wildfires have broken out in Greece after a period of relative calm there.

Two helicopters collided during a firefighting operation in the area of Psatha in the Attica region, west of Athens, Greece's fire service said in a statement.

Footage posted on the Forecast Weather Greece Facebook page appeared to show two helicopters closing to cross paths at low altitude, before the main rotor of the lower helicopter struck the underside of the one flying above it and crashed in flames. The second helicopter then dumped its load of water and flew away.
