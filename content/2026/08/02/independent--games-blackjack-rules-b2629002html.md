**Initial setup**

At first, players are dealt two cards face up, while the dealer receives one card face up and one face down (this is known as the hole card).

The goal is to have a hand total closer to 21 than the dealer, without exceeding 21. If you exceed 21, this is known as ‘going bust’, or ‘busting’.

**Card values**

Cards have different values in blackjack – some are self-explanatory, while others require learning and memorising.

Number cards (anything between 2 and 10) are worth their face value. Face cards (Jack, Queen, King) are worth 10 too.

Aces can be worth 1 or 11, depending on what benefits the hand.

**Blackjack (natural 21)**

A player automatically wins if they are dealt a ‘blackjack’, which as also known as a natural 21. This would consist of an Ace and a card with a value of 10.

However, you do not win if the dealer also has blackjack, in which case it’s a tie (this situation is also known as a ‘push’).

**Player options**

In this section, we will discuss what options each player has during their respective turns in blackjack.

**Hit**

A hit is when a player opts to take additional cards in order to get closer to 21. Of course, choosing to hit comes with the risk of busting if the total exceeds 21.

**Stand**

A player can choose to stand, meaning they keep their current hand and do not take additional cards, thereby staying on the current value.

**Double down**

A double down is the name given to the option to double the original bet and receive one additional card. This is a common move when the player has a hand total of 9, 10, or 11, as it is less likely that they will go bust.

**Split**

When a player has two cards of the same value, they can split them into two separate hands by placing an additional bet.

Once you split the cards into two hands, you’ll place your original bet with one hand and place an equal bet on the second hand. You will play the hand to your right first.

**Surrender**

Players also have the option to surrender, which is when a player forfeits half of their bet and ends their participation in the hand. This is typically used when the player has a weak hand against a strong dealer upcard.

**Side bets in blackjack**

Blackjack card game rules include side bets, which operate as optional wagers placed alongside the main blackjack game. They are usually staked at the beginning of the round, before the cards have been dealt, and they are placed alongside regular bets.

Many side bets come with fixed odds that typically offer higher payouts than the standard 2:1 reward for beating the dealer.

We have provided some detail on popular side bets below.

- **Perfect pairs:** This is a bet on whether the first two cards dealt to the player will form a pair.
- **21+3:** This bet combines the player’s two cards and the dealer’s upcard into a three-card poker hand (such as a flush or straight).
- **Insurance:** A bet offered when the dealer’s upcard is an Ace, which then pays out if the dealer has blackjack.

**Risk vs reward**

While side bets can offer large payouts, they also carry a higher house edge and should be played with caution.

This is a strategy for a more advanced blackjack player and not someone who is still learning the rules – when starting out, it is better to simply stick to ‘normal’ blackjack in order to avoid any losses potentially piling up.

There are no guaranteed methods to win at blackjack. Basic blackjack strategy is based on statistical probabilities rather than anything a player can influence, and this helps players make the best decision for each hand.

Overall, the strategy is aimed at reducing the house edge and maximising the player’s odds of winning.

**Strategy chart overview**

Blackjack strategy charts are large charts that provide odds and probabilities based upon the value of your hand. They essentially recommend whether to hit, stand, double down, or split based on the player’s hand and the dealer’s upcard.

It is useful to memorise the different probabilities (for example, the chance of going bust) on different card values, though remember to use common sense too.
