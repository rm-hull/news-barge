# I used to be Sports Minister - here's why I back Yorkshire CCC demutualisation: Nigel Adams

## Yorkshire County Cricket Club is one of the great institutions of English sport. Its history is envied and unmatched, its support unrivalled, and its ambitions should be every bit as bold as its past.

Yet sentiment alone will not secure Yorkshire’s future. The time has come for members to seriously consider the case for demutualisation.

Across county cricket, the financial landscape has changed dramatically. Competing at the highest level now requires substantial investment in facilities, coaching, academies, technology and world-class playing squads. The traditional members’ club model, while steeped in history, often limits a county’s ability to attract the capital needed to compete with the best.

![Nigel Adams shares his views on Yorkshire CCC's potential demutualisation](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjdhN2JjMWQ4LTk1NzgtNGQzZS1iNWFlLTMxZDYxMGU2M2Q4NDo3YTI1ODM5Yi00MjkxLTQwMjAtOWE4YS1jMjhiZjI0ODNmNGY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Since moving away from the mutual ownership model, the club has attracted significant private investment, transformed the Utilita Bowl into one of the country’s premier cricket venues, secured regular international fixtures, expanded its commercial operations and created financial stability that many counties envy.

Private investment has enabled long-term planning rather than short-term survival or managed decline. It has also not gone unnoticed that Hampshire has also appeared in plenty of finals and won more than its fair share of trophies in recent years.

Yorkshire should take note. This is not about selling Yorkshire’s soul.

It is about ensuring the White Rose thrives for the next 100 years.

A well-structured demutualisation could include safeguards that preserve Yorkshire’s identity, traditions and commitment to members while unlocking investment that simply cannot be achieved under the current structure.

As members, we understandably fear losing influence. But the reality is that influence without financial strength achieves little. Like many counties, our club’s financial challenges have been well documented. Cricket is becoming increasingly competitive, and counties that fail to modernise risk falling behind both on and off the field.

Investment could accelerate redevelopment of Headingley, strengthen the playing squad, enhance the academy, improve supporter facilities and ensure Yorkshire remains an attractive destination for international cricket and major events.

Demutualisation is not a choice between tradition and progress. It is about using progress to protect tradition.

Yorkshire has always been a county that leads rather than follows. It has produced generations of England’s greatest cricketers because it has never been afraid to evolve. The same spirit should guide today’s members.

Demutualisation is not a magic solution, nor should it be pursued at any cost. Any proposal must be transparent, independently valued and include protections for the club’s heritage and future governance. But rejecting change simply because it is new would be a mistake and threaten the club’s future.

Yorkshire deserves the financial backing to match its cricketing stature. Members have a rare opportunity to secure the club’s long-term future, ensuring that future generations inherit not merely a proud history, but a thriving, competitive and sustainable future for Yorkshire County Cricket Club.

Nigel Adams is a former UK Sport Minister and YCCC member
