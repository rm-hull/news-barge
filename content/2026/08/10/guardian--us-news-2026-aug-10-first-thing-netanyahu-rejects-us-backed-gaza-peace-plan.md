Benjamin Netanyahu has rejected a US-backed 15-point peace plan for Gaza in a rare public break with Donald Trump. In a statement, Israel’s prime minister said he was confronting a “wave of rumours about Israeli weakness and Israeli withdrawals on all fronts” and rebuked US pressure to end wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran without Israel’s buy-in.

Trump’s Board of Peace in July proposed the 15-point plan, which mandated that Hamas disarm in exchange for providing a full Israeli military withdrawal from the Gaza Strip and an eventual transition of power to an independent Palestinian national committee. Trump had said the agreement would be implemented in stages, but Netanyahu on Sunday said he wanted a full Hamas disarmament first in order to implement the deal.

- 
  **What other developments have happened in the region?** Iran has issued tough new demands for reopening the strait of Hormuz, while the United Arab Emirates said one of its ships was targeted by an Iranian missile over the weekend. Tehran said the strait of Hormuz would not open until the US “corrects its behaviour”. Yemen’s Tehran-aligned Houthis have claimed responsibility for an attack on a refinery in Saudi Arabia on Sunday morning.
- 
  **What impact is the conflict having on seafarers in Hormuz?** The US-Iran war has stranded up to 20,000 seafarers, mostly Indian and Filipino crews, with none knowing when the nightmare will end. Seventeen seafarers have died since hostilities began, the majority following Iranian strikes.

## **Top Democrats simulate election threats as Trump continues assault on voting**

![US Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/a1f28012cc28d99e7066c4b316e798974c23e494/972_0_5711_4569/master/5711.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Chuck Schumer, the US Senate minority leader, and senators including Alex Padilla, Adam Schiff, Mark Warner, Elissa Slotkin and Raphael Warnock were among those in the room as a team of legal experts offered scenarios they might face in the coming months: an assault in real-time on the integrity of November’s election results from the right wing and their Republican opponents.

- 
  **What sort of exercises did they take part in?** Justin Vail, who leads the DC office of the non-partisan advocacy group Protect Democracy, said: “These tabletop exercises are a standard planning tool for experts and stakeholders to prepare for potential scenarios. We’re not predicting exactly what would happen. But we do want to create a realistic context that can help us identify gaps, test our assumptions, and strengthen coordination, and then ultimately develop some recommendations to increase our preparedness.”
- 
  **How has Trump been working to undermine confidence in US elections?** Voting rights advocates pointed toward Trump’s July primetime address as indicative of the president’s intentions – rhetorical attacks on the integrity of voting machines and elections offices, potentially escalating into more direct interference. Trump threatened the broadcast licenses of networks that refused to carry that address live, and in his speech, he again implored Congress to pass the Save America Act, which would ban mail-in ballots nationwide and impose new identification requirements on voters when they register and report to polls.

## **Climate check: World’s seas have hit hottest temperature on record for July, say scientists**

![Durdle Door in Dorset, England.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/87c236446ba2900c4c834b9136187c04abcb7a9a/491_0_2916_2334/master/2916.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)
