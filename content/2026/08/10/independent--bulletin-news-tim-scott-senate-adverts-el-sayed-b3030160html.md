# Trump ally Sen. Tim Scott pressed on Abdul El-Sayed adverts

- Senator Tim Scott, who leads the Republican Party's Senate campaign arm, faced questions regarding recent ads highlighting Michigan Democrat Abdul El-Sayed’s full name, Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed.
- Critics, including Democrats and independent observers, contend that the ads are an Islamophobic tactic, deliberately referencing El-Sayed's Arab-American heritage and Muslim faith.
- Scott denied the Islamophobia accusation but could not explain the name usage, instead pivoting to label El-Sayed “dangerous” due to alleged sympathy for Hamas and his association with Hasan Piker.
- El-Sayed stated that the Republican attack lines echoed those used against Barack Obama, specifically noting the deliberate use of his full name.
- The progressive candidate, who recently secured Michigan's Senate primary, is also under scrutiny for his association with Hasan Piker, whom Republicans label an anti-Semite, though El-Sayed refused to disavow him.
