A math professor has weighed in on two popular Powerball strategies ahead of Monday’s draw, which features a jackpot of $905 million, the 16th-largest lottery prize in American history.

Monday's jackpot is the eighth-largest Powerball prize since the Powerball game launched in 1992, though Mega Millions has seen eight larger jackpots.

The Powerball jackpot reached the massive sum after 42 consecutive drawings without a single ticket matching all six numbers. Ticket sales for Monday night’s draw end at 10 p.m. ET, with the official drawing set for 10:59 p.m.

Ahead of the historic draw, USC mathematics Professor Emeritus Ken Alexander offered some advice to lottery players who hope to land the jackpot.

He first debunked a strategy used by some players to try to up their chances of winning.

![Monday's prize stands as the eighth-largest Powerball jackpot since the Powerball game launched in 1992](<[https://static.the-independent.com/2026/07/14/15/05/GettyImages-2252768467-(2](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/07/14/15/05/GettyImages-2252768467-(2)).jpg>)

Purchasing tickets at a retail location where large jackpot tickets were previously sold does not improve a player's chance of winning, according to Alexander.

"The chance that a given place will sell a winning lottery ticket is just related to how many tickets they sell," Alexander told *City News Service*.

However, players seeking a better chance of avoiding sharing a jackpot should choose numbers that are selected less frequently, Alexander advised.

Because participants often choose birth dates as their numbers, figures higher than 31 are played less often, Alexander noted.

## Odds of winning Monday’s Powerball jackpot

The Powerball jackpot was last won on May 2, when two tickets in Florida and Texas split a $20 million jackpot.

The odds of matching all five numbers, along with the Powerball number, stand at 1 in 292.2 million, according to the Multi-State Lottery Association, which conducts the game. Meanwhile, the overall chance of winning any prize is 1 in 24.9.

Powerball tickets cost $2 per play and are sold in 45 U.S. states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands, as well as the United Kingdom since July 21.

If a player from a participating U.S. lottery wins the jackpot, they can choose to receive their prize as an annuity, paid in 30 graduated payments over 29 years, or as a lump-sum payment. Both prize options are before taxes.
