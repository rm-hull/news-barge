This week’s headlines reveal a globe shaken by violence. The Burmese military opens fire on anti-government demonstrators, the Syrian army launches a major assault on Beirut, and the United States reveals harrowing evidence of the Srebrenica massacre. Yet somewhere, the sun is still shining. Sort of. As we prepare for our own upcoming eclipse this week, we look back to 1999 when Europe was captivated by two minutes of totality. Meanwhile, a bizarre 2003 blackout brings a different kind of awe to New York, replacing the city lights with a rare, starlit Manhattan sky.

## 10 August 1988 – Military opens fire on Burmese demonstrators

Security forces in Rangoon fire directly into crowds of peaceful protesters during nationwide demonstrations against dictator Ne Win. The violent clash marked a turning point in the “8888” uprising, which saw thousands of citizens killed across the country. The revolt was ultimately crushed weeks later by a bloody military coup that installed a junta that ruled for decades.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/02/The_Independent_1988_08_10_1-Burma-protests.jpeg)

## 14 August 1989 – Syrian army launches major assault on Beirut

Syrian forces unleash a relentless heavy artillery bombardment against Christian enclaves in Beirut, breaching lines held by General Michel Aoun. The violent escalation marked a climax in Aoun’s “War of Liberation”, which aimed to drive Syrian troops out of Lebanon. Although international mediation brokered the historic Taif Agreement months later, the conflict only fully ended in October 1990 when Syrian forces ousted Aoun into exile.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/26/The_Independent_1989_08_14_-Syria-army-crackdown-1.jpeg)

## 14 August 1990 – US faces pushback over unilateral Gulf blockade

The US finds itself diplomatically isolated after launching a unilateral naval interdiction to choke off Iraqi oil exports and imports. European allies including Britain initially refused to join the pre-emptive operation without a clear international mandate. The rift proved temporary, as the UN Security Council soon approved Resolution 665, uniting an unprecedented global coalition that eventually launched Operation Desert Storm.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/52/The_Independent_1990_08_14_1-US-Gulf-blockade.jpeg)

## 11 August 1995 – Evidence exposes mass atrocities in Srebrenica

The US reveals harrowing eyewitness testimonies and spy photos to the UN demonstrating that Bosnian Serb forces executed thousands of Muslim men and boys. The compelling body of evidence stripped away remaining doubts about the scale of the massacre. The mounting outrage over these exposed horrors broke diplomatic deadlocks, directly triggering a decisive Nato military intervention weeks later.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/15/The_Independent_1995_08_11_1-truth-about-srebrenica.jpeg)

## 12 August 1996 – Oasis play iconic concerts at Knebworth Park

Oasis perform to massive crowds across two nights at Knebworth Park in what becomes a defining live event of the 1990s Britpop era. Millions of fans applied for tickets in an unprecedented rush that represented the greatest demand for a concert in British history and shattered attendance records for the largest open-air rock concerts held in the country.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/04/The_Independent_1996_08_12_1-Oasis-iconic-conerts.jpeg)

## 12 August 1999 – Total solar eclipse of 1999

Millions of people across Europe stop to marvel as the moon perfectly blocks out the sun in a stunning two-minute celestial display. The highly anticipated event marked the first total solar eclipse visible from the United Kingdom since 1927. The spectacle remains a rare memory since the UK will not see another total eclipse until 2090.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/32/The_Independent_1999_08_12_1-solar-eclipse.jpeg)

## 16 August 2003 – Blackout brings Manhattan to a standstill

A massive power outage shuts down New York City and the wider northeast, bringing traffic to a halt but allowing rare starlight to shine above Manhattan. While the abrupt blackout had raised immediate fears of another coordinated terror attack after 9/11, the true cause was revealed to be a single overgrown tree in Ohio that brushed against a sagging high-voltage power line.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/59/The_Independent_2003_08_16_blackout-Northeastern.jpeg)

## 13 August 2008 – End of Russo-Georgian war

A resurgent Russia halts its swift military offensive against Georgia after sweeping into the breakaway region of South Ossetia and advancing deep into undisputed territory. The brief but brutal war left hundreds dead and forcibly displaced tens of thousands of civilians before European diplomats successfully brokered a fragile ceasefire agreement. The crushing Russian victory signalled the definitive return of aggressive great power politics.

![](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/07/18/39/The_Independent_2008_08_13_1-Putins-revenge.jpeg)
