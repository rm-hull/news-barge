Rats and mice are usually happy scurrying around but if you find an injured one, do you know what to do?

Rodents in your garden or home can be unpleasant and a challenge to get rid of but if you see one not doing too well, there are ways you can help.

This time of year, you may notice more rodents are about as they seek cover and warmer areas and the RSPCA has shared advice for anyone who comes across an injured rat or mouse.

The charity explains on its website: “The easiest way to confine a sick or injured small rodent is by carefully placing a towel over the animal, and lifting it into a box. Use a plastic box, as rodents can chew through cardboard quickly.”


You can find out more about rats and mice on the RSPCA website.

## What to do if you come across an injured rodent

If you find an injured rodent, be aware that they can have a sharp bite, especially if they can feel pain or are scared of something.

The RSPCA suggests being careful when you handle them and wear gloves and use a thick towel to do so.

It adds: “Keep the box in a warm quiet place, away from any pets, and call a vet or wildlife rehabilitator to find somewhere to take them.

## When you should rescue an animal

Help Wildlife has shared some advice for when it is safe to rescue a rat or mouse if they are injured.

If you see an animal that has been bitten by a cat, it says: "They must receive antibiotic treatment within a few hours or the bacteria on the cats teeth may cause fatal septicaemia."

You should try to rescue an injured animal when it "can be easily approached".

The website added: "An animal with a visible wound or injury such as a damaged limb will need help."
