Cricket governing bodies in Scotland and the Netherlands have strongly criticised the planned restructuring of next year’s 50-over World Cup, claiming that the proposed changes "risk weakening the integrity and credibility of international cricket".

At a board meeting in Edinburgh last month, the International Cricket Council (ICC) revealed that the central phase of the tournament in southern Africa will feature 12 nations split into two groups of six.

Although 14 sides remain involved, the competition will begin with an initial round featuring the three lowest-seeded qualifiers, with just a single team reaching the main group stage.

A preliminary qualifying tournament will run from 22 February until 23 March next year, with the top team advancing directly and those in second, third and fourth entering the opening round.

Cricket Scotland and the Royal Dutch Cricket Association (KNCB) released a joint statement expressing that they were "deeply disappointed" with the ICC’s move, announced under 18 months before the event begins.

The statement declared: "At a time when cricket is seeking to expand its reach, attract new audiences and strengthen its position on the international sporting landscape, reducing meaningful opportunities for emerging nations sends entirely the wrong message.

"We have significant concerns about both the impact of these changes and the process through which they have been introduced.

"The changes risk weakening the integrity and credibility of international cricket, and only highlight the importance of strong governance, transparent decision-making and effective engagement with members."

In the men’s ODI rankings, Scotland and the Netherlands are currently placed 13th and 14th respectively.

Monday’s statement follows similar concerns raised by the World Cricketers’ Association over the overhaul, though Cricket Scotland and the KNCB stressed they are "fully committed to working constructively with the ICC and fellow members to strengthen the global game".

The governing bodies added: "Following the announcement of the changes, Cricket Scotland, the KNCB and other associate members met twice with the ICC, seeking official written communication on the changes and greater clarity around the processes involved.

"We also requested positive action to strengthen future operational decision-making and an invitation to bilateral planning meetings with full members.

"Since the second meeting more than two weeks ago, there has been no further response from the ICC, which is disappointing and disrespectful given the significance of the issues raised."
