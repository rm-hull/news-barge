## Music legends behind some of the biggest 80s hits are heading to Scarborough

Scarborough Open Air Theatre has hosted numerous big-name acts this summer including Scissor Sisters last weekend. *(Image: Cuffe & Taylor)*

Music legends are set to perform at Scarborough Open Air Theatre this weekend.

Holly Johnson, the charismatic lead vocalist and lyricist of Frankie Goes to Hollywood, will take the stage on Friday, August 14.

The evening will also see performances from guest stars ABC and Heaven 17.

Johnson was the voice behind huge number one hits such as Relax, Two Tribes, and The Power of Love.

  Holly Johnson will be in Scarborough on Friday.

His energetic stage presence played a significant role in the band becoming one of the most successful of the era, known for merging social commentary with infectious dance rhythms.

Johnson has also enjoyed a thriving solo career with hits such as Love Train, Americanos, and Atomic City from his debut album Blast.

Beyond the music scene, Johnson is an internationally-recognised visual artist, writer and LGBTQ+ rights campaigner.

This year marked the 40th anniversary of Frankie Goes to Hollywood's debut album, Welcome To The Pleasuredome, an occasion Johnson celebrated with a UK and European tour.

Joining him will be ABC, best known for hits including Tears Are Not Enough, How To Be A Millionaire, and When Smokey Sings.

Founded in Sheffield, the band made a considerable impact on the music scene between 1981 and 1990 as pioneers of the sophisti-pop and new pop movements.

ABC's more recent accomplishments include their orchestral "Lexicon Of Love" tour.

Heaven 17, another Sheffield-born band, completes the line-up.

Known for their synth-pop hits Temptation, Crushed by the Wheels of Industry, and Come Live With Me, the band was formed by Martyn Ware, Ian Craig Marsh, and vocalist Glenn Gregory.

Ware and Marsh, originally founding members of The Human League, branched out to create Heaven 17.
