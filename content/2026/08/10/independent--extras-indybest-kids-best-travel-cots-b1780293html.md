Travelling with a young child can be hugely rewarding, but it never fails to amaze me how much equipment such a tiny person needs. As a new parent myself, travel cots were something I hadn’t given much thought to before having a baby. Once weekends away and family holidays were on the horizon, I quickly realised how important a familiar, reliable sleep space for the baby would be.

With a suitcase full of toys, clothes, nappies and bottles, it’s tempting to rely on the travel cot provided by your hotel or holiday rental. In my experience, though, that can be a gamble. The quality varies enormously, and when you’re trying to help a baby settle in unfamiliar surroundings, having a sleep space they know and that you trust can make all the difference in getting a good night’s sleep (for both parent and child).

To find the best travel cots, I tested a range of models over several months with children at different ages and stages, including a newborn, a five-month-old, and a two-year-old. I assessed portability, weight, ease of assembly and most importantly, whether the cot had a firm mattress, sturdy frame and breathable sides that are essential for safe sleep. I also considered long-term value, comparing newborn-friendly bassinets with designs that grow into toddler cots, and even playpens. After countless overnight stays and trips away, these are the travel cots that I would happily pack for my own family holidays.
