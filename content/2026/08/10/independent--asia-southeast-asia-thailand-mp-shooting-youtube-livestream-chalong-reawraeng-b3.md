A senior Thai politician has died after a former MP opened fire outside a government building on Monday, just days after the same region witnessed a major school shooting incident in which at least nine people died.

Thongchai Yenprasert, the president of the Nonthaburi Provincial Administrative Organisation, died at hospital while receiving treatment after he was shot multiple times, according to officials.

His driver was also injured in the attack that happened outside the Nonthaburi provincial office's building.

Police said Chalong Reawraeng, a former lawmaker, has been arrested.

He called a YouTuber who was livestreaming shortly after opening fire to claim responsibility for the attack. In a press conference from a police station, he apologised to the family and friends of Thongchai.

He called him a “good friend” but said he was angry with him over money-related issues.

"I can’t believe I’m in this position, sitting here. Throughout my life, I had never to come to a police station,” he said.

“Today I went to talk to my friend up in [his office]. We spoke and he didn’t listen to me. He didn’t talk. He didn’t acknowledge anything."

## Who is the suspect?

Chalong Reawraeng, who opened fire on two people on Monday, killing one, is a well-known politician in Nonthaburi.

He previously served as an MP of the Thai Citizen Party and later with the Pheu Thai Party.

He was defeated in 2023, when he stood for election under the Bhumjaithai banner.

## Victim succumbs to his injuries following shooting

Thongchai Yenprasert, a former police officer who was the president of a provincial administrative organisation, has died in hospital after being shot multiple times, allegedly by a former lawmaker.

He was allegedly shot by Chalong Reawraeng over a money-related dispute.

## Two shooting incidents in Nonthaburi spark debate on gun laws

The latest shooting happened in Nonthaburi province, where a teenage student on Friday killed six people and himself at a school after allegedly shooting dead his grandparents, in the country's worst mass killing in nearly four years.

The incidents of shootings have set off a debate on gun control laws in Thailand, which has the highest rate of gun ownership in Southeast Asia by a wide margin.

Under government rules, people in Thailand are required to obtain separate licences for owning and carrying firearms, and there are tough punishments in place for violators.

Nonetheless the country also has one of the highest rates for mass shootings in Asia, with recent incidents including at schools, a major Bangkok mall and a 2022 killing spree in the country's northeast that killed 36 people including 22 children.

## Shooter says he lent money to Thongchai

Chalong Reawraeng said he had previously helped Thongchai Yenprasert when Thongchai was in the real estate business and struggling financially.

Chalong told reporters that he had been receiving treatment for polycythemia since 2011 and had spent so much on medical care that he was left without an income. He said he approached Thongchai for financial help, but the situation escalated.

"He then pushed me, saying 'I don’t want to talk. I don’t want to talk'," Chalong said.

"Then he started walking. Then I followed him to his car."

He said: "I can’t believe I’m in this position, sitting here. Throughout my life, I had never to come to a police station.”

“Today I went to talk to my friend up in [his office]. We spoke and he didn’t listen to me. He didn’t talk. He didn’t acknowledge anything."

## Who is Thongchai Yenprasert?

Thongchai Yenprasert is the 71-year-old president of the Nonthaburi Provincial Administrative Organisation.

He was appointed chief of the provincial body in 2004.

He is a former police officer who joined the forces in 1984 and served as the deputy superintendent.

He also served as a superintendent of several district police stations in Nonthaburi, on the outskirts of Bangkok.

He was believed to be known to the alleged shooter, Chalong Reawraeng.

## Incident comes days after a teenager opened fire at his school

The latest shooting incident happened in Thailand just days after a 14-year-old boy opened fire at his school, killing seven people on the premises before turning the gun on himself.

Hours earlier, he had allegedly shot dead his grandparents at home before coming to the school in Nonthaburi province, just outside Bangkok, police officials said.

At least 14 people remain hospitalised, of whom seven remain in critical condition Saturday, said Thailand’s health ministry. It said most of those wounded were between 12 and 14 years old.

Authorities in Thailand are now facing a major challenge of tightening access and control of guns among its population.

The ministry of education said it would develop new safety protocols over the next three months, including mental health screenings and a system to refer at-risk people to specialists, drills for emergency response plans, a drive to stamp out bullying and better detection to stop items from being taken into schools.

## Victim in 'severe condition' at hospital, governor says

Thongchai Yenprasert has been hit by several bullets and remains in a "severe" condition, Nonthaburi's provincial governor has told Thai media.

One of the bullets hit Thongchai in his neck, governor Chettha Mosikarat said.

"He still has pulse and has been sent in for an operation because the bullet hit his neck and cut through major veins," Chettha said.

“The doctors decided to perform an operation to stitch the damaged veins."

Thongchai's driver was hit in his arm and the hand and is undergoing surgery at the hospital.

He said the alleged shooter had known Thongchai for sometime but he was not aware of any conflict between them.

## Shooter apologises, calls victim a 'good friend'

The alleged shooter, Chalong Reawraeng, has apologised to the family and friends of Thongchai Yenprasert in a press conference from the police station after he was arrested.

He called him a “good friend” but said he was angry with him for money-related issues.

"I told him to take responsibility on this. If you don’t have [the money] now, you can pay in instalments to my children... I told him I’m in trouble," he said.

"He then pushed me, saying 'I don’t want to talk, I don’t want to talk.'

"He said he then followed him to his car with a gun, but claims he did not do so with the intention of shooting him.

"When I pulled the gun out, I didn’t intend to shoot him. But then [Thongchai's driver] pulled his gun out and pointed it at me, so I fired."

"I’d like to apologise to the people of Nonthaburi who loved the PAO chief, or his family, or any other people," he said, referring to Provincial Administrative Organisation.

"I didn’t intend to do anything, because he’s a good friend of mine, but he didn’t want to talk to me,” he said.

"My family will be in deep sorrow. My daughter will surely be heartbroken," he said.

"They wouldn’t think this is something I would have done."

## Former lawmaker claims responsibility for attack

A well-known former lawmaker, Chalong Reawraeng, has claimed responsibility for the attack.

He first claimed it in a phone call to a popular political commentator, who was livestreaming on YouTube at the time, and later at a press conference.

During the call, the presenter Anchalee Paireerak asked the lawmaker: "Did he die? Did he die? You shot him in the head? You need to wait there for the cops.”

She appeared visibly shocked, while viewers were not able to hear the caller.

## Shooter has been arrested, police say

Police said Chalong Reawraeng, a former lawmaker from Thailand’s Nonthaburi province, has been arrested. His alleged target, Thongchai Yenprasert, is president of the Nonthaburi Provincial Administrative Organisation.

The victim and his driver have been hospitalised after being shot, with several local media reports suggesting they were seriously injured.

"There were two people wounded, who have been sent to hospital for treatment," major general Dejrapee Kongdee, the police commander of Nonthaburi, which borders Bangkok, said.
