**Police investigating the death of a Scottish woman whose body was found in a suitcase in Greece say they could determine how she died "in the coming days" as they are fast-tracking toxicology tests.**

Elisabeth-Jane Ross, 38, was found in an abandoned building in the Kypseli district of Athens on 18 July, reportedly by a homeless man.

The 38-year-old, known to her family as Lisa, is thought to have travelled to Greece from Edinburgh in late June, and went missing on 15 July.

Greek authorities are also looking to trace Lisa's American friends, who they said were also staying in Kypseli.

Police cannot yet confirm whether Lisa's death is considered murder.

A post-mortem examination is under way and toxicology tests to determine the cause of death could help confirm if foul play was involved.

Test results can take anywhere from several weeks to a few months depending on the complexity of the case and the workload of forensic laboratories.

However on Saturday, police spokesperson Konstantina Dimoglidou told BBC Scotland News: "We have made it a priority so we can have results in the coming days.

"Then we will know how she died. This can't wait."

Lisa was due to contact American friends in the Kypseli district of Athens

Lisa had initially stayed in the area of Keratsini, 5.9 miles (9.5km) west of central Athens, with Greek friends.

She had told them that on 15 July she would go to Kypseli, where her "American friends" or acquaintances were.

Police sources said officers were looking to identify these individuals.

Her mobile phone was transmitting a signal and sending some messages a few days after her death.

On Wednesday, the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) confirmed they were providing consular support and were in contact with the Greek authorities.

First Minister John Swinney called it an "absolutely appalling case", and said her family would be supported by the Scottish government and the FCDO.

Police Scotland said they were also providing support to Lisa's family.
