Relatives of the victims of Robert Bush call for funeral industry regulation outside his sentencing hearing at Hull Crown Court

**Tristan Essex is standing on the steps of Hull Crown Court, shoulder to shoulder with several others whose lives have been devastated by disgraced funeral director Robert Bush.**

The 27-year-old is fighting alongside his sister Claire for regulation of the funeral industry, a journey that has seen them take their campaign to Downing Street and resulted in a new pledge from the government "totake urgent action to crack down on rogue funeral operators".

Their grandmother Jessie Stockdale was one of Bush's victims – her body one of the 35 found at Legacy Independent Funeral Directors, five months after her funeral was held.

The news knocked the family off its feet, Tristan said. Even worse, they learned the ashes they had been given were not Jessie's remains.

![A young man in a blue t-shirt is smiling and crouching next to an older woman in a hospital bed wearing a pink dressing gown.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1440/cpsprodpb/64c5/live/1eb97f20-8c0e-11f1-8665-e382dc49dfdb.png)

Tristan Essex says his memories of his nana, Jessie Stockdale, are "tainted" after Bush kept her body for five months after her funeral

On Friday, Bush was jailed for 20 years for a litany of crimes. He failed to bury or cremate people, gave bereaved relatives the ashes of strangers and defrauded nearly 200 families out of more than £500,000.

It is nearly two years since Tristan and Claire launched their campaign. Outside court, the group's small white posters were printed with a QR code that linked to a parliamentary petition for "full statutory regulation" of the funeral industry.

"You can start your own funeral directors from your shed," said Tristan. "No regulations, no laws, no qualifications. The government's had forever to do it and we're still waiting."

Following sentencing, the Department for Health and Social Care said it would develop "proposals for formal regulation of the funeral sector", with families due to be consulted.

Yvette Cooper, the health secretary, said: "In the wake of this horrific case, we will now bring forward comprehensive proposals to ensure respect and dignity for the deceased in every setting, including proper regulation of the funeral sector."

Prime Minister Andy Burnham also backed regulation and said he wanted to ensure "the highest standards" applied.

![A man in a grey shirt is sitting behind a desk with his hand pressed to his face. The back of the head of a second man is in the foreground. The image is stamped with the logo of Humberside Police](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2560/cpsprodpb/d45a/live/33bd5ed0-8c10-11f1-aa8b-ebe30ab3ea99.jpg)

Bush himself highlighted the lack of industry regulation during police interviews

The funeral industry in England, Wales and Northern Ireland is self-regulating, relying on voluntary codes and inspections by trade associations. Anyone can set up as a funeral director without formal training, registration or mandatory oversight.

Bush himself highlighted the lack of regulation during his interviews with Humberside Police.

In footage released by the force, an officer asks: "Has it ever happened in your experience that someone from Bereavement Services, for example, has turned up to ask to inspect your fridges to make sure they are in order?"

Bush replies: "No, nobody."

The officer asks: "Is [inspection] a thing that happens?"

"When you join the National Association of Funeral Directors (NAFD) you have an inspection," Bush responds.

"They came round for spot inspections, but if you didn't come up to scratch there wasn't a penalty, you just weren't in their association any more."

Bush shrugs.

"They're also checking whether you've got cushions on your sofa or how many leaflets you have."

He later adds: "I know we're a highly unregulated industry."

There are two leather chairs, table and a fireplace in the background.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/650/cpsprodpb/bb06/live/434d5760-8c15-11f1-94b2-05bc97107049.png)

Police photographed scenes of chaos at Bush's business in Hessle Road, Hull

Bush admitted 67 offences, including 30 counts of preventing a lawful and decent burial.

The NAFD previously told the BBC how Legacy resigned its membership of its organisation in 2021.

In a statement, its CEO Andrew Judd described Bush's crimes as "deeply shocking" and said families in Hull had been let down by "his unspeakable actions."

Addressing calls for regulation, he added: "Our position at the NAFD has always been clear – we want to see independent statutory regulation of all funeral directors."

That need was illustrated by the chaos and neglect captured in images and body-worn camera footage taken by police at Legacy.

The inside of the Hessle Road premises was described in court as resembling a "horror scene".

![An artist's drawing of two men. The man on the left is wearing a white wig and red robe, the man on the left has his face obscured and is wearing a white wig and a black robe.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/3450/live/bca06670-8c10-11f1-aa8b-ebe30ab3ea99.jpg)

Judge Mr Justice Hilliard has called for an explanation about the absence of forms used to monitor burial or cremation in the Bush case

The question remains why Bush's abuse of the system was not spotted by those he needed to regularly work with to conduct cremations and burials.

His sentencing hearing was told forms used to monitor and record burials and cremations had not been returned and, according to the prosecution, "not followed up by the assigned registrar".

Judge Mr Justice Hilliard asked in court: "Why didn't they? What's the explanation? There's public interest in this. They want to know what's happened."

Prosecution barrister Steven Ball later read out a joint statement from Hull City Council and East Riding of Yorkshire Council, in which the authorities said registrars were meant to inform the national general register office about missing certificates, but "that did not happen".

However, the councils added: "Because the process as a whole doesn't provide the necessary oversight, it is not clear whether this would have changed the outcome of this tragic case."

The authorities said the current framework for the funeral sector relied heavily on trust and called "for reform and registration".

Sentencing Bush on Friday, the judge said "there were no checks over many years of the way in which he conducted his business" and "no oversight" of the condition in which the deceased were kept.

"A single visit might have been enough to bring some of what he was doing to a halt," he added.

Nigel Hotham with his sister Zoe

Nigel Hotham's uncle, Mark Hotham, died in September 2023. His body was later found in Bush's funeral parlour.

Speaking after the hearing, Nigel said it was "about time" the government acted and he was glad Burnham was listening.

"It's important that he does," he said. "Things do need to change and they need to change fast.

"It's easier to open a funeral director's than it is to open a sandwich shop – and there's more regulation around sandwich shops than there is funeral directors.

"We're campaigning massively for change."

![A woman with blond hair and a blue cardigan is standing on a grass field in front of trees](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/a4ad/live/a9ed4d30-8c11-11f1-aa8b-ebe30ab3ea99.jpg)

Karen Dry used Legacy to cremate the bodies of both of her parents

Karen Dry, who fears the ashes given to her by Legacy did not belong to her parents, Allan and Betty, said only a "full public inquiry" would satisfy her.

"There is an enduring sense of deep betrayal, emotional stress and damage caused by this individual to many families right across this city," she said.

Speaking after Bush was jailed, she added: "Without this regulation, I can guarantee you one thing: in five or 10 years time there will be another funeral director with another 300 families in a courtroom somewhere else getting another 20 years for doing exactly this.

"We've got to put a stop to it and it's got to stop now."

![A number of police cars and vans are surrounding a large grey warehouse which has the sign saying Legacy on its wall](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/ffe3/live/0aa0f360-8c18-11f1-94b2-05bc97107049.jpg)

Police discovered 35 bodies and more than 100 sets of ashes when they raided the premises in March 2024

Concerns about the treatment of the dead in both mortuary and funeral home settings did not begin with the Robert Bush case.

Disturbing cases of neglect and malpractice in Gosport and Leeds have previously prompted calls for regulation.

Meanwhile, the 2021 inquiry into the abuse by David Fuller at the mortuary of Maidstone and Tunbridge Wells NHS Trust concluded the funeral sector was, in effect, "an unregulated free-for-all".

It recommended, external the government establish an independent statutory regulatory scheme for funeral directors in England "as a matter of urgency".

In Friday's statement, the government referred to the Fuller inquiry, but made no mention of a statutory scheme.

![A grey haired man in a blue suit is sitting in front of a bunch of red roses and a lit candle. There is a stained-glass window in the background.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2560/cpsprodpb/25b7/live/b3919ac0-8c12-11f1-aa8b-ebe30ab3ea99.jpg)

Kevin Moxon, another funeral director in Hull, said Bush's actions had harmed the industry

Kevin Moxon, who runs Moxon's Family Funeral Directors in Hull's Anlaby Road, said he was concerned "trust in our industry" had been badly damaged.

"This case [Robert Bush] is not indicative of how our profession runs. It's a sacred duty to look after someone's loved one," he said.

"We need regulation bringing in pretty rapidly. We need inspectors coming round doing spot checks to ensure that we are all running to a set code of practice."

Following the government's announcement, Judd said the NAFD would make the case for statutory regulation as the best way to secure "meaningful change".

He told the BBC: "Our call for reform is about safeguarding the deceased and the bereaved. The dead cannot protect themselves.

"That responsibility falls to the rest of us."
