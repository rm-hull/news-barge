Sánchez condemns ‘selfish’ reaction of some EU countries to Ceuta border crisis as death toll rises – Europe live

In letter to Brussels, Spanish PM hits out at ‘polarising and illegal’ reaction after tens of thousands of people crossed from Morocco to Spanish enclave

Spanish PM criticises 'selfish' attitude of some EU countries over Ceuta crisis

The Spanish prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, has hit back at some EU countries for what he described as their “selfish, polarising and illegal reaction” to the mass border crossings at Ceuta, as he thanked Morocco for its help in dealing with the crisis.

In a letter to Brussels, he said several European states have chosen to “attack” Spain and request its temporary exclusion from the Schengen area, driven by “prejudice, fake news, ignorance, or political interests” that is contrary to European law, humanitarian law and the principles of EU solidarity, the Spanish El Pais newspaper reported.

Spanish soldiers assist a migrant who swam from Morocco to the Spanish enclave of Ceuta. Photograph: Antonio Sempere/AP

Sánchezargued that in less than 48 hours, his government managed to “fully restore control of the border, provide extensive humanitarian assistance, return virtually all migrants who crossed irregularly and prevent any unauthorised movement towards continental Europe”.

“All of this has been achieved in close coordination with the Moroccan authorities and with very little support from other European states,” he added.

He called for an emergency meeting of EU interior ministers “as a matter of urgency”.

El Pais reported Ceuta is “gradually returning to normal” this morning, with shops reopening, traffic moving freely and fuel supplies restored after days of disruption. While small groups of young people remained in parts of the city, the large crowds seen earlier this week had largely dispersed.

Grande-Marlaska said between 50,000 and 60,000 people crossed from Morocco to Spain’s Ceuta, but that “the vast majority have returned”.

El Mundo reported more of his remarks:

Those who entered irregularly on 30 July have no option to regularise their status, which is why they returned to their country in less than 48 hours. I know the images were very shocking, but that’s why I want to highlight the efficiency of our country in reversing the situation in just over 24 hours.”

As a reminder of the EU countries that have issued strong warnings or rebukes over the events in Ceuta, my colleagues Sam Jones in Madrid, Angela Giuffrida in Rome and Jakub Krupa reported the following:

Italy’s foreign minister, Antonio Tajani, blamed the situation in Ceuta on the Sánchez government’s decision to offer a now closed scheme to regularise the status of more than 1 million undocumented migrants and asylum seekers. He said the programme had incentivised human trafficking and was “profoundly wrong”.

The Swedish prime minister, Ulf Kristersson, urged Spain to “act swiftly to regain control of the situation and fulfil its commitments within Schengen cooperation”.

The Finnish interior minister, Mari Rantanen, said that as Spain had “completely failed to protect the Schengen area’s external border”, all EU countries should support Italy’s proposal to suspend its Schengen status.

It is true that most European Union countries have expressed their support for Spain in a difficult and complex situation that we have reversed in a short period of time, but there are other countries that have shown an absolutely selfish and unsupportive attitude.”

Spain interior minister accuse some EU countries of 'ignorance and bad faith'

The Spanish interior minister, Fernando Grande-Marlaska, is speaking now from Ceuta, I will bring you the latest lines.

He has echoed the Spanish prime minister’s criticism of some EU countries who have blasted Spain’s handling of the crisis (see post at 10:34), accusing them of “ignorance and bad faith”.

“We reversed the situation that arose on 30 July on our own, in collaboration with Morocco,” he said, according to the Spanish newspaper El Mundo.

“Morocco is not a threat to Ceuta, nor to Spain; it is a reliable partner.”

Death toll in Spain-Morocco border crisis in Ceuta climbs to 67

There has been an update by the Spanish government on the Ceuta border crisis, with officials saying the death toll has risen to 67. The government also announced plans to install a containment barrier extending from the breakwater into the sea to deter further crossings after tens of thousands of migrants entered the Spanish enclave from Morocco this week, AP reported.

Spanish police officers and soldiers escort a big group of migrants to the border in Ceuta. Photograph: Jon Nazca/Reuters

Nine people killed in Russian attack on Ukraine as Zelenskyy warns of low air defence supplies

Hello, and welcome to our Europe live blog. Nine people have been killed in Ukraine after Russia launched a barrage of missiles and drones at the capital Kyiv overnight, Ukrainian authorities have said.

Twenty-two people were also injured in the attack, which the Russian defence ministry described as a “massive strike” against “Ukrainian military-industrial complex facilities and logistics centres in Kyiv”. Andrii Sybiha, the Ukrainian foreign minister, said Russia was “intensifying its air attacks and terror against civilians”.

People look at a crater at the site of a Russian missile strike in Kyiv, Ukraine. Photograph: Sergey Dolzhenko/EPA

It came just two days after an onslaught of Russian missiles killed 10 people and injured more than 50 others across Ukraine, as Moscow looks to exploit Kyiv’s shortage of western-supplied air defence systems.

The scale of the problem was laid bare by the Ukrainian president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who said Kyiv managed to shoot down just one of the 27 ballistic missiles fired in the overnight attack because stocks of interceptor missiles for US Patriot systems had been depleted.

In a post on X, he said:

Overnight, the Russians launched 35 missiles at Ukraine, including 27 ballistic missiles, as well as 185 attack drones of various types. Kyiv was the primary target, but strikes also hit the Dnipro, Sumy, Kharkiv, and Poltava regions. Only one ballistic missile was intercepted, simply because there are no interceptors for the Patriot systems. And it is this shortage of ballistic missile interceptors that only encourages Russia to launch such attacks that take human lives.

He appealed to allies to urgently supply more Patriot interceptors, saying every additional missile saved lives and that shortages were leading to more civilian casualties.

Yesterday, he said he spoke to the US vice-president, JD Vance, and discussed a meeting with the president, Donald Trump, earlier this week in Washington, which Zelensky described as “positive and productive”.

More than 300 firefighters were battling a fast-moving wildfire near the coastal Greek resort of Porto Germeno, about 45 miles (70km) north-west of Athens, as strong winds continued to fuel the blaze and complicate firefighting efforts. Greek authorities said the fire had spread to the shoreline, with officials confirming to AFP that homes had been damaged. It is one of several wildfires burning across Europe, with France and Spain also battling major outbreaks as extreme heat, prolonged drought and the climate crisis create increasingly fire-prone conditions.

France and Italy have bolstered border controls after about 60,000 people crossed into the Spanish enclave of Ceuta from Morocco. The crisis has turned increasingly deadly, with at least 57 people killed attempting the crossing, according to reports, prompting Spain to deploy troops to help regain control of the border. The Spanish prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, blamed trafficking gangs for orchestrating the surge, accusing them of exploiting vulnerable migrants by encouraging the dangerous crossings.
