Donald Trump has said Israel is "very happy" with the agreement reached by the Board of Peace for the complete disarmament of Hamas and other armed Palestinian groups in Gaza.

Under the proposed phased deal, Hamas would disarm, Israeli forces would withdraw as the disarmament progresses, and an international stabilisation force would work with a new Palestinian police force until a new Palestinian government takes over governance in Gaza.

"We do have an understanding with Israel. Israel is very happy about it. Israel helped us, and they've been very good”, Trump said. "Will it go through its ups and downs? It's a very complex situation over there”.

Hamas has said the agreement stipulates that Israel must stop killing Palestinians and withdraw from Gaza before it disarms, according to Reuters.

Senior Hamas negotiator Ghazi Hamad said the group’s disarmament is conditional on Israel fulfilling its commitments under the agreement, including withdrawing from Gaza and allowing the establishment of a national committee and reconstruction efforts.

## Israel’s Yellow Line closing in on the space where Gaza civilians can still live

*A reminder of what has been going on in Gaza recently, while much of the focus has been on Iran.*

*Here is a report from three days ago:*

In Gaza’s Zeitoun district, Assem Dawla loaded mattresses and bags onto a truck as concrete yellow blocks marking the edge of Israel's expanding military zone stood nearby.

He said it was the seventh or eighth time since the war began that he and his family had been forced to leave a shelter because of the advance of what residents call the yellow line.

Zeitoun is one of at least five locations across Gaza where residents and Hamas officials say Israel has expanded areas under its control in recent days by moving forward the yellow line, a military demarcation established under a US-brokered ceasefire reached in October.

As men hoisted belongings onto a waiting truck, elderly relatives picked their way through debris. Continued Israeli military activity made it too dangerous to remain, Dawla said.

"We are taking our belongings out and going to leave the place because the occupation (Israel) has moved the yellow line all the way to Salah al-Din Street," he said.

## Israel refuses to withdraw before Hamas disarms after Trump celebrates ‘major milestone’ in peace process

Israel and Hamas remained at loggerheads over a supposed agreement to bring lasting peace to Gaza on Friday, after Donald Trump said his so-called ‘Board of Peace’ had firmed up a deal to disarm the militant group.

A senior Hamas official said on Friday that their disarmament would depend on Israel first withdrawing from the Gaza Strip - but a diplomatic source in Israel told The Independent that Hamas would have to give up its guns first.

The Board of Peace did little to clarify the situation, sharing a 15-point ‘roadmap’ to peace saying Hamas would give up heavy weapons and security responsibilities, and that Israel would carry out a “phased” retreat from Gaza.

Hamas also demanded that Israel stop its attacks in the Gaza Strip, as local health authorities said that at least one person had been killed and several others injured in at least two Israeli airstrikes and by gunfire in the enclave.

## 'Glimmer of hope': Palestinians see light at the end of the tunnel but say conditions remain dire

*Nedal Hamdouna reports from inside Gaza:*

Palestinians have spoken of a “glimmer of hope” in the strip after there appears to finally have been movement forwards in the ceasefire deal, following months of stagnation.

"There is a glimmer of hope that these negotiations and the agreement might mark the end of the tragedy we are living through in the Gaza Strip,” Abdul Hamid Awad, a 23-year-old from northern Gaza, told *The Independent*. 

“However, there is a deep-seated fear that the agreement will fail, leaving the Palestinian people under the oppression and tyranny of the Israeli occupation.”

Palestinians are living under “extremely harsh conditions”, he said, adding that most people “cannot afford their daily bread”.

“We are living in an undeniable catastrophe, dwelling in tents and destroyed homes—tents that offer no protection against the heat or the cold, causing skin diseases and fungal rashes, especially among children.”
