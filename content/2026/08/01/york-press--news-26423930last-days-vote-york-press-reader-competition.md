There are a handful of days for readers of The York Press to post off their votes before we select the winner in a popular annual competition.

The best pub in York as voted by you will be determined in the coming weeks and today is the last day we feature the ballot in the newspaper.

Since Tuesday, July 21, online and in print, we've featured each of the top 10 you shortlisted, which includes previous winners and nominees.

In no particular order, here are those names again.

The Lord Nelson in Nether Poppleton is the only pub in the village - the 300-year-old establishment was voted the best pub in York by our readers in 2024.

It is known for its local ales, Yorkshire Tapas menu, and live music nights.

The Knapton Lion in Main Street, Knapton, was brought back to life recently after years of closure.

The pub is run by Tom Brown, who also owns The Fox Inn and The Lord Nelson.

It is known for its fresh-made seasonal menus and unique drink offerings.

The Crooked Tap in Acomb was voted the best pub in York in 2025 by readers of The York Press.

The pub is known for its vibrant atmosphere, live music, and craft beer.

Ye Olde Starre Inne in Stonegate is thought to be one of the oldest continuously licensed premises in York - the Grade II-listed building dates back to the 16th century.

It is known for its character, friendly service, and historic atmosphere.

The Nag's Head in Heworth Road was recently named the best restaurant or gastropub in York by our readers.

The pub is known for its welcoming atmosphere, friendly staff, and wide selection of drinks.

The Walnut Tree in Heworth Village is known for its community spirit, friendly staff, and regular events.

Stone Roses Bar in King Street has been a staple of York's nightlife for almost 21 years.

The bar is known for its upbeat atmosphere, value-for-money drinks, and loyal customer base.

It was named Thwaites Pub of the Year in 2024.

The Rook and Gaskill in Lawrence Street is in a building which has been serving drinks to the community since the 18th century.

The pub is known for its selection of more than 50 beers and its Thursday night quiz.

It has been a proud member of CAMRA for the last 15 years.

The Burns Hotel in Market Street is a Samuel Smith’s pub known for its traditional beers.

The pub is a haven from the digital world, with no TVs or background music.

The Ship Inn in Acaster Malbis is a riverside pub known for its cosy setting and friendly service.

The pub has a long history dating back to the 1700s.

It is known for its well-kept cask ales and traditional menu.

  *The Press will name the winner of its readers' choice of Best Pub 2026 in August.*
