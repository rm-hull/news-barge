It's been a huge week for me here at TechRadar. I managed to convince my boss that it was a good idea for me to sit at my desk for five whole days and play all 200+ games available as part of the Apple Arcade service on my phone.

 ![TechRadar's Ultimate Guide to Apple Arcade.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/rgkcCt9eiG77s6VcrKCrXm.png) 


This article is part of our Ultimate Guide to Apple Arcade: the definitive guide to everything that you need to know about the gaming service. Be sure to browse the full series here.

If it sounds like a ridiculous idea, that's because it really is. Honestly, in my rush to get paid to play some games on my phone, I don't think I actually considered just how long the task would take. That became painfully apparent when I actually started on Monday, and the fact that I was about to spend hours and hours flicking through apps set in.

Still, I managed it — even if the quest ended up sapping up all of my free time. I spent entire evenings lying on the sofa planting crops in *Stardew Valley+*, whole meals with one finger guiding an escaping explorer in* Temple Run Legends*, and my hour commute with both eyes glued to* Suika Game+ . *If I had just one hand free at any given time this week, I was using it to play an Apple Arcade game. Thankfully, I didn't have to drive anywhere.

Now, to be clear, I didn't spend an equal amount of time in all of them. It's easy to sink a few hours into the pixel survival action of *Vampire Survivors+*, for example, but literal torture to do the same in* Play-Doh World *(which is basically just a sensory experience for toddlers).

Still, I can now say with confidence which ones are truly worth getting into and, while the following eight picks are by no means an exhaustive list of everything worth playing on the service (just look at the honorable mentions for even more greats), they are the games I've had the most fun with so far and will coming back to time and time again in the weeks and months to come.

### Top eight

## 8. Squiggle drop

![Squiggle Drop - Steam and Nintendo Switch Trailer Coming Aug 15th! - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/BvXRVw9mChc/maxresdefault.jpg) 

*Squiggle Drop* is one of the biggest surprises I've had while exploring Apple Arcade. It's an easy-to-understand puzzler that has you drawing shapes to complete different objectives and, with basic graphics and a spartan interface, looks like a pretty simple game.

That simplicity is one of its biggest strengths, however. The controls are eminently approachable; just drag your fingers around the screen to draw, and all of the levels have multiple solutions that reward creativity and mean that you never feel like you're truly at a loose end.

There's loads of variety to boot. One mission could have you drawing a container to save a birthday cake from the rain, while another might require a more complex creation to catch an underwater fish.

With more than 250 puzzles (some of which are pretty darn difficult), rotating daily challenges, a raft of achievements to obtain, and even an in-game town that you build as you progress, it's the perfect game to pick up and play when you get a few spare moments in an otherwise busy day.

## 7. Lego Star Wars: Castaways

 ![Lego: Star Wars Castaways](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/QtoAtYqsn37gUErUsctXx.png) 


*Lego Star Wars: Castaways* is yet more proof that you should never judge a book by its cover (or, I guess, an app by its icon art). What I thought was going to be a generic kids' game turned out to be a really approachable and quite charming MMO.

You create a custom Star Wars hero and drop into a shared hub world that you can explore while surrounded by other real players, before dropping into iconic locations from the movies (explained in-game as a training simulation) to battle waves of Stormtroopers, Tusken Raiders, Battle Droids, and fearsome bosses like Darth Vader either solo or with up to three friends.

There are four character classes to unlock, focusing on ranged laser blaster combat, powerful force skills, team support, and stealth, and they all have meaty upgrade trees in addition to a range of distinct weapons to try. Character customization is also strong, and you can unlock loads of different hats and outfits using currency you obtain by completing quests and missions.

I had a lovely time with it as an adult, but there are some smart safety features that make this a great pick for children as well. Your character name, for example, is chosen from a random selection of suitably Star Wars-sounding words (I foolishly flicked past 'Jar-Jar Skywalker' when it popped up as an option) and numbers. There's also no direct text or voice chat function, though you can communicate through a small selection of emotes.

## 6. Angry Birds Reloaded

 ![Angry Birds Reloaded](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/HqhefRe8rB3WEZJPBwFzQE.png) 


*Angry Birds* is a game that needs no introduction. This legendary early App Store sensation spawned countless sequels, spinoffs, theme park rides, a TV show, and even two popular animated films with a third set to release later this year.

*Reloaded* is basically the definitive *Angry Birds* game, bringing together the levels from the classic *Angry Birds* and its sequels *Angry Birds Seasons,**Angry Birds Space*, and* Angry Birds Stella* (the latter two aren't even available on the App Store in their original forms anymore, making this the only way to officially play them) in addition to loads of new ones.

It offers the classic bird-slinging action that made the series so popular, now spruced up with shiny new graphics that look lovely even blown up on a big screen. All of the new content means that it's a joy to dip into even if, like me, you completed all of these games back in the day.

For newcomers, it's the absolute best that the series has ever been, with the best-looking graphics, all the greatest levels, and absolutely no ad interruptions. Get it downloaded, and find out why it was such a hit.

### Top five

## 5. Disney Dreamlight Valley Arcade Edition

 ![Disney Dreamlight Valley on Apple Arcade.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/u962tfJm3XqudSJiTYUtZE.png) 


If after a busy day you just want to kick back and escape to a fantasy world, then *Disney Dreamlight Valley* is for you. This life simulator thrusts you into a magical town filled with iconic Disney characters that has fallen into disarray. A mysterious malevolent force is taking over in the form of dark roots that sap away memories, and it's up to you to get rid of them all and restore everything to its former glory.

From relaxing farming to designing your own cute cottage and customizing your outfits, everything here seems designed to bring nothing but the most chill vibes. There's a good sense of progression as new areas are steadily unlocked while you play, bringing the worlds of new Disney franchises into the mix.

On that note, it's hard to overstate the joy of interacting with some of the company's best characters. I've never been a massive fan of Mickey Mouse, Donald Duck, or Goofy, but even I can't stop myself from smiling when I unlock childhood favorites like Ratatouille's Remy, Wall-E's Eve, or Peter Pan's Tinker Bell.

Although you can pick up *Dreamlight Valley* on other platforms, the *Arcade Edition* comes packed with all of the latest free updates and access to the biggest paid expansions at no extra cost. It looks great and controls well on a phone, too.

## 4. Sid Meier' Civilization 7 Arcade Edition

![Sid Meier's Civilization VII - Arcade Edition | Official Launch Trailer - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/29RrpiojSVo/maxresdefault.jpg) 

The Arcade Edition of *Sid Meier's Civilization 7* is the real deal, bringing all the strategic brilliance of the latest*Civilization* game to mobile with few real compromises aside from some missing DLC.

If you're unfamiliar with the franchise, it tasks you with building and governing a historical society from the ground up in a randomly generated world. You build cities, conquer territory, develop new technology, and interact with other competing societies. Whether you form trade alliances and live peacefully alongside your neighbours or wage war is entirely up to you.

Unlike past games, *Civilization 7* added the ability for your empire to evolve, giving you the choice to swap between different cultures for unique bonuses, special buildings, or exclusive units.

The level of depth here is simply unparalleled in the mobile space, and as games can take tens of hours at a time, it's great to be able to take it all on the go.

### Top three

## 3. Taiko no Tatsujin Pop Tap Beat

 ![Taiko no Tatsujin Pop Tap Beat](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/2YDdMtiJNgzEC7C2hWMJB3.png) 


*Taiko no Tatsujin* is a name you'll recognize if you've even been to an arcade in Japan. It's a simple but addictive rhythm game all about hitting a big drum in time with the beat, a gameplay perfectly suited for a touchscreen.

Tap either side of the on-screen drum in time with the blue icons or either side of the middle for the red ones, with bigger notes needing both sides at once. It starts off simple, but as the difficulty ramps up, beats that you have to hold are introduced, alongside ones that have you hammering the screen as quickly as possible with your fingertips.

The song selection is brilliant if, like me, you love Japanese music with a good mix of J-pop hits, Vocaloid classics, and music from popular anime. If that's not your speed, there are plenty of original songs, a whole load of classical music, a few tracks from Western blockbusters like Indiana Jones, plus viral sensations such as Baby Shark.

Nothing quite compares to the joy of mastering a song and, with so many to choose from and a wide range of difficulty levels to beat, *Taiko no Tatsujin Pop Tap Beat* is almost impossible to put down.

## 2. Sonic Dream Team

 ![Sonic Dream Team on Apple Arcade.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/kFcPdus6rKn37PcsCMDCM.png) 


What if I told you the best new 3D *Sonic the Hedgehog* game of the last decade was an Apple Arcade exclusive? It sounds hard to believe, but *Sonic Dream Team* is so good that I would recommend it even if you're not a *Sonic the Hedgehog* fan.

This 3D platformer has the iconic blue hedgehog navigating a series of dense worlds with impeccable dream-like visuals, hopping between floating platforms, boosting your way down high-speed rails, and dispatching enemies with homing attacks. The action certainly isn't as fast as some of the 2D *Sonic the Hedgehog* games, but the precise controls mean that it feels excellent nonetheless.

It's not the longest experience on Apple Arcade, lasting just a couple of hours, but it is highly replayable with six swappable characters (including a rare Cream the Rabbit appearance), each with their own distinct pacing and unique roster of abilities to experiment with, a ranking system to chase high scores, tricky challenge stages, and unlockable powers.

There are also loads of collectibles to seek out, so true completionists have plenty to chip away at even after the credits have rolled.

## 1. What the Golf?

![WHAT THE GOLF? Launch Trailer - Out now Apple Arcade and PC! - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/NyNpPWvw1eU/maxresdefault.jpg) 

If I were forced to pin down one overall favorite among everything that I played this week, then *What the Golf?* is the obvious choice.

It's an unapologetically ridiculous physics-based game that is only really about golf in the loosest sense. Levels are incredibly creative, and no two are the same, having you putt everything from hot dogs and cars to live horses and entire houses into the hole.

It's consistently surprising and endlessly amusing, with mechanics and even the game's perspective shifting on a level-by-level basis. It's all underpinned by an expressive, minimalist art direction, a sleek interface, upbeat music, and a barrage of punny humor. The brilliant use of haptics also deserves some credit, giving everything you punt a distinct feeling of weight to it.

In addition to a lengthy main campaign, there is a generous selection of daily courses, bonus episodes, special events, online leaderboards, and more. Indie game fans will recognize loads of crossovers and references, too. It's nothing short of a joyous time.

### Even more picks

## Honorable mentions

 ![Puyo Puyo on Apple Arcade.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/RGasgc5aizE8HMfqLPCsS.png) 


I wanted to keep my recommendations concise and varied, with a good mix of genres, art styles, control methods, and so on represented. That doesn't mean that I don't have loads more recommendations, though, so here are some more strong picks that didn't quite make the full list for one reason or another.

- *Fantasian* is a sprawling RPG adventure with a beautiful miniature art direction.
- *Monument Valley+* and-*Monument Valley 2+* are two stunning indie puzzle games that play with perspective in interesting ways.
- *NFL Retro Bowl '26* offers a surprisingly meaty football simulator delivered through charming pixel-art graphics.
- *Stardew Valley+* combines relaxing farming with social simulation as you build relationships with a cast of memorable characters in a cozy town.
- *Suika Game+* has you merging different fruits in an easy-to-understand puzzler that's taken the internet by storm.
- *Puyo Puyo Puzzle Pop* is an exclusive entry in the long-running-*Puyo Puyo* series that's filled with its trademark-*Tetris*-like puzzle battles.
- *MySims* and-*MySims Kingdom* are both expansive, feature-complete ports of classic-*The Sims* spinoff games.
- *Vampire Survivors+* brings the viral wave survival game to mobile — be warned that hours can vanish in the blink of an eye.
