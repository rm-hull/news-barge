Remember when mobile gaming was fun?

Back in the day, you could just scroll the App Store, pick out an icon or two that you liked the look of, and have a new game downloaded and ready to play in just a few moments.

 ![TechRadar's Ultimate Guide to Apple Arcade.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/rgkcCt9eiG77s6VcrKCrXm.png)

Booting them up, you’re not confronted with a 29.68GB additional download that you have to stay on one screen to complete, a wall of sketchy in-app permissions to accept, or a page demanding that you sign in with some iffy third-party account — just a neat little self-contained experience designed to help you kill a few hours on the train.

Though they seemed simple, many of the best App Store games were quietly revolutionary. *Angry Birds* and *Fruit Ninja* pioneered intuitive touch-screen controls, while *Doodle Jump* smartly utilized the iPhone’s accelerometer to show how hardware features could become core gameplay mechanics.

Statista estimates that there are now more than 180,000 mobile games available on the App Store. But when was the last time you installed one that felt genuinely exciting, rather than like a thinly veiled scheme to force ad views, harvest personal data, or pressure you into pricey Gacha Pulls? That’s exactly where Apple Arcade comes in.

## Back in time

 ![Puyo Puyo on Apple Arcade.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/RGasgc5aizE8HMfqLPCsS.png) 


Having spent the last week diving into all 200+ of the titles available on the service, I can’t help but think that Apple’s highly curated library of mobile titles feels like a journey back in time to the App Store’s glory days.

The whole experience is completely seamless. You sign up for $6.99 / £6.99 / AU$9.99 a month and can immediately jump into browsing the games. They’re all contained in a dedicated Arcade tab in your iPhone’s App Store, so there’s no ambiguity about whether the app you’re looking at is part of the service or not.

Although the selection here is nowhere near as expansive as the regular games tab, it feels genuinely curated and cuts out huge amounts of clutter and spam.

No matter what you like to play, there’ll be at least a few games for you. An impressive variety of genres are represented in the catalog: there are plenty of massive role-playing games (RPGs) that you can sink hours into, be that *Trials of Mana+* or *Final Fantasy+*, fast-paced racers such as* Asphalt 8: Airborne+ *and* Sonic Racing*, head-scratching puzzlers in* Monument Valley+ *and* Lego Builder’s Journey*, and so much more.

 ![Jetpack Joyride 2 on Apple Arcade](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/M6UeTnmScHXcYZggNQKGT.png) 


Those iconic early mobile titles I mentioned earlier? They’re back too in new and improved forms that make them a nostalgic joy to revisit. There’s a full-on *Angry Birds* remake with enhanced graphics and extra levels, an expanded *Doodle Jump* sequel that updates the hand-drawn graphics and adds new environments, and a *Temple Run* successor that trades the endless format for challenging levels that are better-suited for short bursts of play.

The innovation of the early App Store is there too. RPG *Fantasian* wows with incredible art direction that sees you exploring hundreds of beautiful real-world dioramas that were digitally scanned into the game, while bonkers sports simulator *What the Golf?* takes full advantage of punchy iPhone haptics to add real weight to its wacky physics challenges.

## No distractions

 ![Taiko no Tatsujin Pop Tap Beat](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/2YDdMtiJNgzEC7C2hWMJB3.png) 


The games are all entirely devoid of ads and microtransactions, so you never have to deal with a frustrating splash screen or pop-up that takes you out of the action.

This means that most of the games will work entirely offline as well, just like they did back in the day. I had practically given up playing mobile games on my commute before getting into Apple Arcade simply because it was so hard to find titles that wouldn’t kick me out the second my train went into a tunnel.

There are no sign-in screens anywhere, and most games don’t even request permission to send push notifications. Your progress will even automatically sync to the cloud so you can continue playing on your iPad, Mac, or Apple TV when you get home.

Oh, and you don’t need to worry about your privacy in all this either. Apple says that all of the games in the service have no third-party tracking software and don’t collect any advertising data. All that, to me, is certainly worth paying for.
