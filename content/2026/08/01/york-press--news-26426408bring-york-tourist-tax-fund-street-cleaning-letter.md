**Bring in a York tourist tax to fund street cleaning**

HAVING recently returned from a short break in Paris, I was struck by how clean and well-maintained the city was. During my three-night stay, I paid around €50 in tourist taxes. At first, it seemed like an additional expense, but after seeing the benefits, it felt like money well spent.

The streets were spotless, public bins were regularly emptied, and even the canals and waterways were remarkably free from litter. Despite welcoming millions of visitors each year, Paris manages to maintain a standard of cleanliness that enhances the experience for both tourists and residents.

York faces many of the same challenges as a major visitor destination. Our city attracts millions of tourists annually, bringing valuable income to local businesses. However, the strain on public services is becoming increasingly visible. Too often, visitors and residents are greeted by overflowing bins, littered streets and areas that fall short of the standard a city of York's heritage deserves.

A modest tourist levy, similar to those already operating successfully across many European cities, could generate significant funds dedicated to keeping York clean and attractive. The revenue could be used for more frequent street cleaning, additional waste collections, improved maintenance of public spaces and investment in the city's historic environment.

York's tourism industry depends on the city looking its best. A small charge of a few pounds per visitor would be unlikely to deter tourists, but it could make a significant difference to the quality and appearance of the city.
