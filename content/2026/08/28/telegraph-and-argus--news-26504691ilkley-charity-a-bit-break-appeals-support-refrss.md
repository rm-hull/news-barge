AN Ilkley charity set up by a mother-of-two a decade ago after her own cancer diagnosis is appealing for support as it aims to provide a record number of breaks for people living with life-limiting illnesses.

A Bit of a Break (ABOAB) was founded in 2016 by Ilkley resident Angela Sturgeon, who was inspired by the comfort and value of spending time away with her family following a diagnosis of ovarian cancer.

While on a short trip to Ireland, Ange realised how important it was to escape the day-to-day challenges of living with a serious illness and create lasting memories with loved ones. After returning home and speaking with friends, she established the charity shortly before her death.

Today, ABOAB works with healthcare professionals and owners of self-catering accommodation to provide free short breaks for people with a palliative diagnosis and their families. Property owners donate stays, while the charity contributes towards running costs and cleaning expenses.

  A family enjoying a break provided by A Bit of a Break 

*(Image: submitted)*

Paula Hunt, chair of the charity's trustees, said: “We know how challenging it is to organise even a short break when life is so hard and complicated. This is what drives us, and it’s why we believe in the personal touch. We speak to each guest or their nominated contact before we begin our property search so that we can find them a break that works for them, whether that’s taking the whole family away and having fun at a holiday park or sitting quietly and watching the sea from the warmth of a living room with a fantastic view.

"We remain a small charity with friends, family and supporters at its heart. We’re just so happy that we can keep Ange’s spirit and legacy alive by gifting a break to others when they need it most.”

  Laura, Danny and their baby, who enjoyed a break in the Lake District after Laura was diagnosed with brain cancer 

*(Image: submitted)*

The impact of the breaks has been described as life-changing by recipients.

One guest said: “The opportunity to be away from everything, in total peace, in a beautiful property in a stunning location has been a godsend.”

Another added: “The holiday gave my children a chance to simply be children again.”

More than 300 people have been referred to the charity since its launch, and ABOAB expects to provide more than 75 breaks during 2026 alone.

However, growing demand has increased pressure on the charity’s finances. Each break costs around £350 to support, including contributions to property owners and travel expenses for guests.

Paula added: “The charity has become so successful in the last couple of years, and we hate the idea of turning anyone away.

"We’re now on a big fund-raising drive to ensure we can help support Ange’s legacy into the next decade and would appeal to anyone reading this article – individuals, businesses or other charities – to make a donation if you can.”

Donations can be made at www.justgiving.com/charity/abitofabreak. For more information, visit www.abitofabreak.com
