A DEVELOPER has submitted plans to build 55 homes on green belt land on the edge of a village.

Chartford Homes has submitted a full planning application to Braford Council for the demolition of an existing building in fields next to Addingham Cricket Ground, Ilkley Road, and the construction of 55 dwellings, along with associated access off Main Street.

The scheme would include a mix of two, three and four-bedroom properties, together with roads, parking, landscaping, public open space, play facilities and new pedestrian and cycle links.

A key feature of the proposal is the level of affordable housing. The developer has pledged that 50 per cent of the homes - 28 affordable properties - would be affordable, significantly above Bradford Council's local policy requirement of up to 30 per cent in Wharfedale. The affordable homes would comprise a mix of affordable rent and intermediate tenures.

The 3.12 hectare site lies on the south-eastern edge of the village and is currently agricultural land lying within both the green belt and Addingham Conservation Area.

In a planning statement submitted with the application, the developer argues that the land should be considered "grey belt" because it makes only a limited contribution to the purposes of the green belt and that developing it would not fundamentally undermine the wider green belt designation.

The document also points to Bradford Council's housing land supply position, claiming the authority can demonstrate only 2.08 years of deliverable housing land, well below the Government's five-year requirement.

The statement says Addingham also has an unmet housing requirement of around 75 homes under the district's planning policies.

Addressing environmental concerns, the applicant says the majority of the development would be located in Flood Zone 1, with areas at higher flood risk set aside for drainage features and open space. The scheme also promises at least 10 per cent biodiversity net gain, with existing trees and hedgerows retained where possible.

However, the plans acknowledge there would be some heritage impacts. The site lies within the Addingham Conservation Area and the applicant's assessment concludes the development would cause "less than substantial harm" to its significance. It argues those impacts would be outweighed by public benefits, including the delivery of new homes and affordable housing.

The scheme would also involve the loss of a barn, a non-designated heritage asset described in the application as being in poor condition.

A design statement provided by the applicant says the scheme has been informed by an assessment of local character, traditional building materials and the village's historic development pattern. Proposed homes would feature Yorkshire stone facades and grey slate-effect roofs intended to reflect the character of Addingham.

The developer states the scheme would support local services and businesses by increasing the village population while encouraging walking and cycling through new connections to existing routes. It also says the development would provide additional publicly accessible open space and opportunities for recreation.

The application will now be considered by Bradford Council. It can be viewed on the council's planning portal (Ref: 26/02592/MAF) with comments invited before Wednesday, September 16.
