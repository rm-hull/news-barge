Two cocaine dealers dubbed 'Snowball' and 'Jacko' sold drugs to undercover police at a Darlington pub.

The officers, posing as drug users, were able to buy cocaine from defendants Jordan Hopps and Jack Criddle at the Old Dun Cow, in Post House Wynd, in December 2024 and January 2025.

Teesside Crown Court heard the officers engaged in conversation with people in the pub and bought £100 and £70 deals from customers referred to as, ’Snowball’, (Hopps), and ‘Jacko’, (Criddle).

In the latter transaction, Criddle was able to supply the officers after taking delivery from someone who pulled up near the pub in a 2016-plate Mercedes.

Both were identified and covertly pictured before they were subsequently arrested.

*(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

Nigel Soppitt, prosecuting, said both claimed the drugs were intended for them but the conversations they had with the undercover officers indicated they were involved in dealing.

Hopps, 27, of Craddock Street, Spennymoor, admitted two counts of supplying a quantity of cocaine at an early appearance in the case before magistrates.

Criddle, 24, of Stocksmoor Close, Darlington, who has been in custody, pleaded guilty to a single charge of supplying cocaine, but only at a second scheduled appearance at Durham Crown Court, having failed to leave his cell and attend an earlier hearing via video link.

The court heard Criddle has 15 convictions for 22 offences on his record, many of them for minor violence, but one for burglary, in 2014, while he received a 40-month sentence in 2022 for robbery, for which he was on licence when the drug supply offence was committed.

Hopps has four convictions for six offences, including one for wounding, for which he received an eight-month prison sentence.

Chris Morrison, for Criddle, said when he was released from the robbery sentence he “fell back into old habits” of substance abuse, but since his remand in custody he has gone “drug free” and is on the waiting list for a place in a drug-free wing at Holme House Prison.

Mr Morrison said the defendant has made good use of his time in prison and has a place on a railway working course, which should give him a trade to go into upon his eventual release.

Tabitha Buck, for Hopps, said he was described as “intoxicated” and “under the influence” when the transactions took place and he was, “not someone advanced within this illicit operation.”

Miss Buck said both Hopps and his co-accused are “user-dealers”.

She told the court: “They are individuals who have been involved in illicit substance taking themselves, and often, more likely than not, end up involved in supplying drugs, with little awareness or understanding of the scale of the operation.”

Miss Buck said: "Drugs and alcohol have clearly been an issue for him, but he is in a much better position now."

She explained he has moved from the negative influences in Darlington, and is now living with a supportive partner in Spennymoor, who has, “put her foot down”.

“She has told him if he wants to continue with the relationship he would have to quit drugs.”

Miss Buck said the defendant has set up his own car valeting business and is, “in a more stable and better position than he has been in for all his life.”

Judge Richard Bennett said at the outset that both would receive 28-month prison sentences.

But he said as there are signs Hopps is addressing his drug misuse issues and is in a more settled position, with genuine prospects of rehabilitation, the sentence could be suspended in his case, for two years.

He must, however, perform 200 hours’ unpaid work and attend 27 rehabilitation activity days overseen by the Probation Service.

Hopps must also comply with a drug rehabilitation requirement, with monthly monitoring hearings at the court for the entire 28-month period of the sentence, the first on September 24.

Judge Bennett said Criddle must serve about 40-per cent of the 28-month sentence before being released on licence.
