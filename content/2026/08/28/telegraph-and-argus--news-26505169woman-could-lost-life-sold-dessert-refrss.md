A WOMAN “could have lost her life” after a popular drive-thru sold her a “nut free” dessert that contained traces of Nutella.

The woman was rushed to hospital after an allergic reaction to the Strawberry Pot that staff at Cha Cha Chai on Ingleby Road in Bradford had told her contained Belgian chocolate sauce, with no nuts.

On Friday Israr Nabi, the director of the business, appeared at Bradford Magistrates Court, pleading guilty to a number of charges relating to the incident, which happened last May.

This included sending West Yorkshire Trading Standards falsified certificates showing staff had been trained in food safety matters – one of which claimed the training had taken place in September 2026.

Nabi, 46, of Ashwell Road in Heaton, and the business will have to pay fines and cost totalling more than £15,000.

## Staff said dessert did not contain nuts

The court was told that on May 4 last year the woman attended the drive thru and asked for a strawberry cup – a dessert made up of strawberries covered in sauce.

The sauce is supposed to be Belgian chocolate, and before she ordered the woman asked if it contained nuts.

Staff confirmed there were no nuts in the Belgian sauce, and served her the dessert.

## 'Severe allergic reaction'

Mrs Julian, prosecuting on behalf of Trading Standards, said: “When she took a bite she had a severe and immediate allergic reaction.

"An epi-pen did not help and an ambulance was called.

"When she was in hospital it was so serious that an ICU consultant was called.

"Fortunately, she made a recovery.”

## Woman to afraid to eat desserts any more

The woman said her throat felt like it had been cut up, and the anaphylactic shock caused a loss of potassium.

"She was given a potassium infusion, which took six hours to complete, to bring her levels back to normal.

She was discharged the next day.

The court heard that the woman had told prosecutors she is too afraid to eat desserts any more, and that the incident had caused damage to the lining of her stomach.

## Nutella dessert sauce used

In response to the incident, West Yorkshire Trading Standards visited the drive-thru to investigate.

On May 16 undercover officers ordered the same dessert, and again asked if the strawberry pot contained any trace of nut.

They were served a pot, which was then sent away for analysis.

This found that the sauce contained traces of hazelnut.

The court was told that “the levels of hazelnut detected were not consistent with cross contamination.”

Mrs Julian said: “It seems Nutella dessert sauce was used.”

It later emerged that the company used two types of sauce, Belgian chocolate and Nutella.

Both sauces were decanted from their packaging into unlabelled bottles.

## Training certificates created on the day

Trading Standards contacted the company, and although they had been made aware of the woman’s allergic reaction, they could provide “no evidence” that staff had been given allergy training between her hospitalisation and the test purchase 12 days later.

They were asked to provide paperwork to show the company was keeping track of its training, but most of the documents they returned were blank.

As the investigation continued, Trading Standards asked for certificates to show that staff had since been trained, and asked for these this January.

Certificates of training for three staff were sent to officers – but when they investigated the meta data, it emerged that these certificates had been created earlier that day.

One certificate said training had been completed on September 15 2026.

Mrs Julian said: “This is a date we have not yet reached.”

She pointed out that it was clear the certificates were “not authentic” adding: “it was an attempt to show training has taken place when clearly it has not.”

## Company fined

The decision was made to charge both Nabi and Cha Cha Chai Ingleby Ltd with selling food not of the nature demanded by the purchaser, and failing to comply with EU food safety provisions.

The company was also charged with knowingly furnishing information which is false or misleading.

Mr Arshad, defending, said there had been no further bad conduct involving the business following the investigation, adding: “There has not been a systematic failure in relation to the overall business.”

He said Nabi was a man with no previous convictions, adding: “This investigation has been a real valuable insight into the way in which allergens should be considered by people who are not health experts.”

He said training has since been put in place.

## Court told of £30,000 annual turnover

Before deciding to fine the defendant and the company, magistrates asked why there were no financial details of the company.

Mr Arshad said no accounts have been filed for two years.

When the court pointed out that businesses legally have to file accounts each year, Mr Arshad said the defendant’s accountant was “trying to resolve various matters with HMRC.”

He said: “The documents are not available, it is what it is.”

The court was told the company has an annual turnover of around £30,000 – although no evidence of this was provided.

## How much was the company fined?

Chair of the Bench Steven Feeley said the defendant “deliberately attempted to mislead Trading Standards” with the false certificates.

He added: “Someone could have lost their life because of this man’s actions.”

Nabi was fined £2,304 and ordered to pay a £922 surcharge and £2,909 costs.

The company was fined £4,608, ordered to pay a surcharge of £1,843 and costs of £2,909.
