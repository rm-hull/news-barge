Business confidence in the North East has dropped by 30 points, according to the latest data.

The Lloyds Business Barometer showed a significant fall to 45 per cent in August, compared to 75 per cent in July.

North East businesses reported a decline in confidence in their own trading outlook, which fell by 24 points to 56 per cent.

Optimism in the wider economy dropped by 37 points to 33 per cent.

Martyn Kendrick, regional director for the North East at Lloyds, said: "While we have seen confidence dip this month, it's encouraging to see that North East businesses haven't hit pause, and are remaining notably more positive about their own trading outlook than the wider economy.

"Economic optimism may have softened, but businesses across the region are backing themselves to grow, with many already taking practical steps to strengthen their position, from investing in technology to exploring new markets.

"We'll continue to support North East businesses as they invest, adapt and plan for the future."

Despite the drop in confidence, many businesses remain focused on growth.

A net balance of 30 per cent of firms expect to increase staff levels over the next year, although this is down 20 points from the previous month.

The main areas targeted for growth over the next six months include team investment (55 per cent), expanding offerings through new products or services (52 per cent), and entering new markets (51 per cent).

On a national level, UK business confidence rose by four points to 53 per cent in August - marking the highest reading since March.

The Lloyds Business Barometer surveys 1,200 businesses each month and has been tracking UK economic trends since 2002.

It provides early indicators of business sentiment at both regional and national levels.
