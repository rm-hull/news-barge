AS part of the Burley Summer Festival approximately 330 historic and modern classic cars and motorcycles, commercial vehicles, 4x4s, American, custom and competition cars were exhibited at the 16th Burley Classic Car Show at Burley Cricket Club.

There were displays by local and national car clubs and around 150 private exhibitors, with cars ranging from the super sporting GT Coupes of the Aston Martin Owners Club, the ever popular MG’s, and cars like the Austin 7 Chummy that were on our roads 100 years ago.

  The best in show at Burley Classic Car Show 2026 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Rosette Winners 2026Best in Show**: 1952 Daimler Barker Sport. Displayed as part of the City of Leeds MG & CCC. Owner: Jimmy Goodall

Jimmy, who has been a regular exhibitor over recent years, has owned this very elegant car for the last 49 years. There are only about 80 of these cars still in existence worldwide, about 20 of which are in the UK.

  The best American at Burley Classic Car Show 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best American**: 1970 Dodge Charger owned by Gary Walker.

One of the most powerful of the American muscle cars that became popular in the 1960’s, with high-performance V8 engines of 6 or 7 litres

  The winner of the organisers choice 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Organisers’ Choice**: 1909 Hupmobile Model 20. Private entry. Owner Dave Jones.

This vehicle attracted much attention during the day. Produced by the Hupp Motor Car Company in Detroit, it is one of only two in the UK. Dave has owned this car for 12 months and has put countless hours into getting the car running well whilst maintaining its authenticity. He went shopping for a historic two-seater tourer and came home with this rather unusual one.

  The best commercial car at Burley Classic Car Show 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best Commercial Vehicle**: 1952 Jowett Van. Private Entry. Owner Martin Pellitt.

Martin is a regular attendee at the show, exhibiting the beautifully maintained Jowett Van which was purchased from the Bradford based manufacturer by his father for use in the family business.

  The best club car at Burley Classic Car Show 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best Club Display**: Jaguar Drivers’ Club

This is the first time the Jaguar Drivers Club have attended as a group, although some local members have previously exhibited as private entries. This display showed the evolution of this famous marque, from the sports and GT XK150s and the luxurious 420 that used the 6-cylinder XK engine that also powered Le Mans winners, through the 12-cylinder Series 3 E-type, to the 8-cylinder models which power the cars of the 2000’s.

  The best custom car 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best Custom Car**: 1967 VW Beetle Convertible. Private Entry. Owner Craig Simpson.

The prototype VW Beetle, so named because of its shape, was first made in 1938 but serious production did not start until 1945, although production ceased in Europe in 1978, the Beetle continued to be produced in Mexico until 2003. Craig has only recently purchased this vehicle and was keen to exhibit it locally.

  Best competition vehicle 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best Competition Car**: 1961 E-type S1 Lightweight Race Car. Displayed as part of the Jaguar Drivers’ Club. Owner Peter House.

These cars were built to compete in International GT races in the 1960s and are now regularly competing in historic race events across the world, including recent competition at Harewood Hill Climb.

  The best Yorkshire car 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best Yorkshire Vehicle**: Jowitt Javelin. Displayed as part of Craven Old Wheels. Owner: R Meekings.

Just over 23000 Jowitt Javelins were built in Idle, Bradford, between 1947 and 1952. The 50hp cars had a maximum speed of 76 mph. Jowitt Javelins were very successful in International Motor Sport, including the Monte-Carlo Rally and RAC Rally.

  The best 4x4 at Burley Classic Car Show 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best 4 x 4**: 1998 Toyota Land Cruiser. Private entry. Owner Jeremy Twigden.

Renowned as one of the most reliable and rugged vehicles throughout all parts of the world, and a vehicle chosen for use in some of the most remote locations.

  The best motorcycle 

*(Image: submitted)*

  **Best Motorcycle**: 1972 Triumph Adventurer. Private entry. Owner Richard Keith.

Richard is a regular exhibitor at the Burley Classic Show with his collection of motorcycles. The Adventurer was an evolution of the TR5 Trophy, a bike made famous by the American film star James Dean and which was also used by the Fonz in ‘Happy Days’.

The Burley Summer Festival committee would like to thank Burley Cricket Club for the use of their ground; KT Green of Otley, who have sponsored the event for 15 years; all the car clubs and private exhibitors who were happy to chat and answer questions about their vehicles all day long, and to the spectators who showed great interest in the wide range of vehicles.
