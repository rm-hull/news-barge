A TOP York chef - whose business running one of the city’s best restaurants has entered liquidation - says it is “brutal” in hospitality right now.

The liquidation of Fish and Forest Ltd in Grape Lane follows that of top York restaurant chain Frango Eduardo Ltd, owned by Ted Hustler, which has also entered liquidation and restructured to save its constituent businesses and staff jobs in recent weeks.

Stephen Andrews stresses the Michelin-listed Fish and Forest is also trading under new owners now and he urges the government to give ‘meaningful’ support to hospitality for independent restaurants to survive.

The chef, who has appeared on TV’s Masterchef in 2024 and is ranked as one of the top 40 UK chefs, says the former company behind the restaurant has now entered liquidation, but it still trades and existing bookings are unaffected.

  


  Stephen Andrews in Fish and Forest when it was in Micklegate 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

The restaurant has been restructured and is now operated by Muddy Bears Ltd, trading as Fish & Forest, with the right to continue using the Fish & Forest name. Companies House shows Muddy Bears Ltd has Janice Robinson listed as sole director.

Janice is Stephen’s mum, and with husband Sam, Stephen’s dad, runs the Pig and Potato restaurant upstairs above Fish and Forest.

Stephen told The Press: “I’m still here, and I’m not stepping away from Fish & Forest. I continue to run the restaurant as a whole, overseeing the team, the kitchen, the food, the service and its day-to-day direction.

  Stephen Andrews of Fish and Forest 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

“Fish & Forest remains the restaurant I built. From a customer’s point of view, the things that matter remain the same: the food, the team, our standards and our ethos.

“A major priority throughout this process has been protecting the people who helped build the restaurant. All our staff have remained in employment, and our suppliers have been looked after throughout the transition. They have played a huge part in getting Fish & Forest to where it is, and I was determined they would not be left behind as a result of the restructure.”

  Stephen Andrews in Fish and Forest in Grape Lane 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

Stephen admits the restaurant’s ethos of using seasonal and sustainable ingredients might not be the easiest solution when cheaper options are available but he was proud of what he and his restaurant has achieved.

He explained: “I don’t want to dress it up: hospitality is brutal at the moment, particularly for independent businesses. Ingredients, energy, wages, business rates, insurance and almost every other overhead have risen significantly.

“At the same time, our customers are feeling the cost-of-living squeeze, so there is only so much of that increase you can put onto a menu before people simply cannot afford to come through the door.

“VAT adds to that pressure. Restaurants charge 20% VAT on sales, while there is comparatively little VAT to recover against many of the costs involved in producing and serving food.”

  Fish and Forest in Grape Lane 

*(Image: NQ)*

“I don’t think it would be fair or accurate to say that one government policy caused the liquidation. It was a combination of pressures. However, the current tax and cost environment is incredibly difficult, and the hospitality sector urgently needs meaningful support if independent restaurants are going to survive.”

Stephen says everyone worked incredibly hard to make the original Fish & Forest succeed but rising costs meant a restructure was the only way forward, to protect the restaurant and keep the staff in work.

He added: “This has been a very difficult period, personally and professionally, but Fish & Forest is still here. The restaurant remains open, bookings remain in place, the team is still here, our suppliers have been looked after and I am still cooking the seasonal food Fish & Forest is known for.

“I’m hugely grateful to our customers, our team, our suppliers and everyone in York who has supported us. We are looking forward and concentrating on the future of Fish & Forest.”

The Creditors Voluntary Liquidation took place on August 12, with Redmond Nichols Butler of Driffield appointed as liquidators.
