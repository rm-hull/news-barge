Plans for a new house in multiple occupation (HMO) have been rejected following public opposition and a petition.

Aidan Ryan Property Ltd proposed converting a property at 54 Salem Street, Jarrow, into a six-bedroom HMO, but South Tyneside Council’s planning committee voted to refuse the application on Tuesday, August 25.

A petition opposing the plan gathered around 115 signatures, including residents from Salem Street and other streets.

The scheme attracted around 14 other objections, with concerns raised about HMO concentration in the area, parking, antisocial behaviour, and waste collection.

Councillor Geraldine Kilgour, who called the application before the committee, said: "Create an overconcentration of HMOs, harm the character and cohesion of a settled residential community, exacerbate amenity, safety and parking problems."

Despite these concerns, council planning officers had recommended approval, stating that "no significant harm or adverse impacts have been identified in respect of visual and residential amenity, biodiversity, highway safety, or in respect of crime/antisocial behaviour/fear of crime and flood risk/drainage".

The planning committee ultimately voted to refuse the application, citing the cumulative impact of HMOs on the area and the potential effect on vulnerable residents.

Councillor Shirley Ford, who put forward the refusal, highlighted concerns about the character of the neighbourhood and the concentration of similar properties.

The applicant now has the option to appeal the decision through the Secretary of State.

Floor plans for the proposed HMO included a communal kitchen and dining area, cycle storage, and a mix of en-suite and off-suite bedrooms.

Work reportedly began on the property in July 2025 but remains incomplete.
