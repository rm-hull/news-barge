# Two shops linked to criminal activity including sale of nitrous oxide closed down

## Shops that have been linked to criminal activity, including the sales of nitrous oxide, have been temporarily shut down.

A court has granted the closure notices for Jimmy’s and Great Horton Road Shop on Great Horton Road.

Bradford Council has said three businesses, two shops and a storeroom, have been involved in anti-social behaviour, the sale of nitrous oxide, illegal and counterfeit alcohol, cigarettes and tobacco.

Three-month closure notices have been granted for each of the premises.

At the same time, Police have requested a review of the licence for Jimmy’s. If successful, the business will be permanently stripped of its licence to sell alcohol.

Police say they have called the review for “the prevention of crime and disorder, public safety and prevention of public nuisance objectives.”

A date for the review has not yet been set.

Bradford Council served a Closure Notice on Thursday August 20, at premises on Great Horton Road, known as Jimmys and Great Horton Road Shop.

The serving of the original Closure Notice, under Anti Social Behaviour legislation, closed the properties for 24 hours until the matter was heard in court.

Subsequent hearings have taken place at Kirklees Magistrates Court on August 21 and today (August 27).

The case was brought by Bradford Council and is a result of joint work with West Yorkshire Police and West Yorkshire Trading Standards.

In court it has been agreed that a closure order should be granted for premises 1 at 97 Great Horton Road known as Great Horton Road Shop, which will run until November 27.

Premises 2 at Under Croft, 2 Laisteridge Lane, known as JIMMYS OFF LICENSE and Premises 3 at 99 Great Horton Road were granted full Closure Orders for three months from Friday August 21.

Three months is the maximum length magistrates can give closure notices for.

Of the three premises only premises 1 at 97 Great Horton Road had a current licence to sell alcohol, although the sign referencing JIMMY’s Off Licence was displayed above premises two.

After the hearings, Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council’s Executive Member for Neighbourhoods and Communities said: “It is vital for all our communities that people are able to buy goods in shops with confidence and that we tackle anti-social behaviour.

“I want to thank our team along with police and trading standards colleagues who have supported this closure. It shows that where businesses sell illegal and counterfeit products and attract anti-social behaviour, we will take the strongest action we can.”

Sergeant Ceri Lloyd of Bradford District Neighbourhood Support Team said: “The action to close these three premises was taken following close work with Neighbourhood Policing, Anti-Social Behaviour and Licensing Officers, Bradford Council and our statutory partners.

“We have identified several issues with the premises including the illegal sale and use of nitrous oxide, drug use and the sale of illegal and counterfeit alcohol, cigarettes and tobacco.

“I hope this closure order reassures residents that we will seek to use all available powers to protect our communities.”

Police, Trading Standards and Bradford Council have had numerous dealings with the store, a short distance from the University of Bradford, in recent years.

In November 2023 Jimmys was stripped of its alcohol licence after a panel of councillors heard the business was caught selling counterfeit cigarettes on multiple occasions.

21 boxes of nitrous oxide cannisters were seized in one raid on the business and a linked flat.

A series of test purchases led to the seizure of hundreds of packs of cheap, counterfeit cigarettes, as well as a hidden chute.

Earlier this year a Licensing Committee discussed an application to transfer the licence of Jimmy’s to a man with criminal convictions for selling counterfeit cigarettes – an application that was ultimately refused.

At the meeting PC Tony Brown said: “The premises itself has an ongoing history of the sale of illegal tobacco.”

The closures follow a similar closure notice issued to a shop linked to crime last year.

The Allerton Off Licence premises was slapped with a 30 day closure notice in late 2025 after it was described as a “hub of criminality.”

But earlier this year magistrates refused to extend that closure for another 30 days.
