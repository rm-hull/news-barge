Cleveland Police deals with the highest overall crime rate in England and Wales, with Middlesbrough at the centre of a week of deadly violence and disorder.

There were 123.4 recorded crimes per 1,000 people in the Cleveland area in the year to March 2026, according to the latest Home Office figures.

The rate puts Cleveland well ahead of West Yorkshire, which recorded 107.9 crimes per 1,000 people.

Greater Manchester recorded 103.7, while the Metropolitan Police recorded 101.0. Across England and Wales, the overall crime rate was 84.7 per 1,000 people.

The figures come as Middlesbrough and surrounding areas have been rocked by a series of major incidents.

The crash involved a car travelling the wrong way on the road and a marked police vehicle.

Police have not ruled out a link between the crash and the house fire.

There have also been reports of a stabbing, properties being rammed and other incidents amid heightened tensions in the area.

On Thursday night, several cars were set alight at a vigil in County Durham for one of the men who died in the A66 crash, with police responding to multiple incidents across Teesside.

MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East, Andy McDonald said that Cleveland Police has been “underfunded for many, many years” and that the funding formula “leaves us under-resourced”.

Mr McDonald told BBC Radio 4: “We do suffer those metropolitan levels of crime, and yet comparatively, our funding looks much more like a rural area, and it simply cannot continue like that. We’ve got to put resources where the demand is most high, and that is Cleveland, and that’s the message to ministers.”

  Cleveland Police Chief Constable Victoria Fuller 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Against that backdrop, the latest crime figures show Cleveland also recorded the highest rate in several major crime categories.

The force had the highest rate of shoplifting, at 15.9 recorded offences per 1,000 people.

It also topped the table for criminal damage and arson at 15.0 per 1,000, violence with injury at 12.6, burglary at 6.1 and sexual offences at 5.0.

Cleveland had the second highest rate for all offences involving violence against the person, with 44.3 offences per 1,000 people. West Yorkshire was higher at 45.8.

For all theft offences, Cleveland was again second highest, recording 35.7 per 1,000 people. The Metropolitan Police had the highest rate at 46.4.

The force also recorded the highest homicide rate in the country.

Cleveland recorded 21.7 homicides per million people in the year to March, compared with 8.1 across England and Wales.

It also had the highest rate of knife offences, with 157.2 offences per 100,000 people.

That was up from 151.8 in the previous 12 months.

Crime rates are used to compare levels of offending across different areas because they take population size into account.

The Cleveland figures cover Hartlepool, Middlesbrough, Redcar & Cleveland and Stockton-on-Tees.

Despite recording the highest overall crime rate, Cleveland is one of the smaller forces by officer numbers. It had 1,503 full-time equivalent officers as of March 31, 2026.

That ranked Cleveland 31st out of the 43 territorial forces in England and Wales by size.

It was ahead of Northamptonshire, which had 1,488 officers, and behind Gwent, which had 1,552.

The latest figures therefore place Cleveland at the top of the national crime-rate table as the force faces intense pressure following a week of tragedy and violence across the area.
