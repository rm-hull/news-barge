One of the North East's most iconic seaside attractions will welcome visitors back after months of closures and delays.

Saltburn Cliff Tramway, a Grade II-listed landmark, has been closed for most of the year while the carriages were taken away to be refurbished.

The carriages returned to Saltburn on Friday, July 21, with Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council saying they hoped to reopen the tramway to the public within two weeks - though earlier estimates suggested that it could be the end of summer before it would be up and running again.

Over a month after the carriages returned to the seafront, the council has confirmed a reopening date for the Saltburn Cliff Tramway.

  Saltburn cliff lift will be back in operation from Friday, August 28. 

*(Image: Redcar & Cleveland Borough Council)*

Councillor Alec Brown has announced that the tramway has passed all its necessary inspections and will reopen to the public this Friday (August 28) from 10.30am.

Cllr Brown said: "Saltburn Cliff Tramway has successfully secured its LOLER inspection, and will reopen on Friday morning.

"Much later than we wanted and I apologise for the delay. On a positive, we now have carriages that will last for decades.

"Given the events of the last five days we have asked the council for no fanfares or noise and for it to just quietly reopen."

The Victorian tramway, believed to be the oldest water-balanced cliff lift in Britain, links the town with Saltburn beach and is a major draw for tourists throughout the year.

Although many had hoped the Saltburn Cliff Lift would reopen earlier in the summer - during the peak tourist season - passengers can now make the most of the final days of the holidays by taking a ride and admiring its newly refreshed carriages.

Recar and Cleveland Borough Council added: "We can't wait to welcome everyone back to see and enjoy the beautiful handcrafted carriages."
