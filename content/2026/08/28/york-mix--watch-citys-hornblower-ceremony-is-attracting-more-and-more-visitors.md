Every night of the year without fail, a horn is blown at the four corners of the Obelisk in Ripon Market Place to set the ‘watch’.

It’s attracting more and more tourists and visitors too, they are coming from all corners of the world to see the event.

This ceremony commemorates the time in the Middle Ages when Ripon’s first citizen, the Wakeman, was responsible for crime prevention from 9pm until dawn and had to compensate victims of burglary.

The Wakeman had constables to patrol the streets, and also had the right to levy a rate on houses according to the number of outside doors, 4d a year for two doors and 2d a year for one door.

*Our video shows part of the ceremony as seen on Wednesday 26th August. The Mayor of Ripon, Cllr Andrew Williams, is often there to greet the visitors who come from the USA, Europe, Asia  Australia*,* New Zealand**and all parts of the UK.* 

*The Hornblower on duty was Wayne Cobbett. On Thursday 27th August it was reported that over a 100 people turned up.* 

Wayne Cobbett, a Ripon resident, told YorkMix Radio: “So I started 12 years ago. I just wrote a letter to the Mayor at the time saying I’d be interested in becoming a Hornblower. Had a chat with him, and he said yeah, not a problem. Have a bit of training with George Pickles, who was the Hornblower at the time, and we’ll get you started.”

It is a paid position and Wayne and his colleagues receive a modest sum for their work

“What we do is turn up in the market square to perform the ancient ceremony of setting the watch every night. One of the Hornblowers stands on each corner of the obelisk in the market square and sounds a note from one of the ancient horns. After the fourth corner, we then recite the history to the gathered public that have come to see it.

“Over this summer, we’ve seen a large increase in people, which is great. It’s a case of locals bringing friends that are coming from out of town, and also local businesses like hotels are telling people about this ceremony and what’s on offer within the city centre as well, which is a great way to spread the news.”

The city has a number of blowing horns, the oldest of which is known as the Charter Horn, since according to ancient tradition it symbolised the granting of charter rights to Ripon in AD 886.

Whatever the truth of this legend, the horn is many centuries old, and is kept in the Town Hall where it can be seen on application. It hangs on a baldric, a broad belt studded with silver badges bearing the emblems of Wakemen and Mayors up to 1886.

Another horn, recently retired, was acquired in 1690. A third horn, presented by Mayor B.P. Ascough, is a beautiful example of South African oxhorn and is one of the horns normally blown today.

One was presented by Sigma Antiques on the occasion of 1100th Anniversary of the granting of the Charter and it is another horn blown today.

The council presented a horn to the city in the year of HM Queen Elizabeth II’s Platinum Jubilee, the horn was made by Duncan Grimmold, a local craftsman with silver used in its construction.
