Cars were reportedly set alight during a balloon release in County Durham held in memory of one of the five men killed in the wrong-way A66 crash.

Shocking footage has emerged online appearing to show vehicles being set ablaze in a field in Haswell, on Thursday evening (August 28).

Michael Cahill's mum had posted a message on social media asking friends to gather at a field in Peterlee, bringing a range of vehicles to mark his memory.

*(Image: ASHLEY FERGUSON/ FACEBOOK)*

Dramatic pictures in Haswell show large black plumes of smoke coming from a few vehicles on fire.

The gathering was believed to have been held in honour of 23-year-old Michael Robert Cahill, one of the five young men who died when a Volkswagen Passat travelled the wrong way along the A66 at South Bank on Saturday.

Other memorial events have been held throughout the week for the other victims, featuring balloon releases, fireworks and flares.

Seven people died in the devastating collision, including Cleveland Police officers PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough.

The other four men who died were named as: Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jacob Matusiak, 23, Makai Saddington, 18.

The incident comes as Cleveland Police responds to multiple incidents across Teesside following the fatal crash and a house fire in Grangetown which claimed the lives of 34-year-old Natalie McDonald and her seven-year-old niece Valentina Foster.

A 48-year-old woman remains in hospital in a critical condition, while three others suffered non-life-threatening injuries.

Cleveland Police is also investigating whether the A66 collision was connected to the Grangetown fire.

The force has said it is "keeping an open mind" and cannot "rule out any links to wider organised crime at this time".

A spokesperson for Cleveland Police said: "We can confirm that we have received mutual aid support through established arrangements from the other six Northern forces.

"This includes Northumbria, Durham, North Yorks, South Yorks, West Yorks and Humber and the National Crime Agency."

Alec Brown, leader of Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council, said officers were working exceptionally long hours as the force deals with the aftermath of the tragedies.

He said: "They are working 20-hour days to ensure the ongoing safety of innocent residents.

"Over 200 extra police officers from forces around the country are here and are on route, from detectives to armed response.

"I will sending a letter today to our Prime Minister asking for even more resource.

"The police really are doing an amazing job.

"If it is any reassurance at all, jobs called in to the police are actually down this week, however the seriousness of the ones that have been called in need serious and swift action.

"Everyone is heartbroken over the loss of the aunt and little girl in Grangetown yesterday.

"People need to feel safe in their homes."

Anna Turley, MP for Redcar and Cleveland, said: "I cannot thank our police enough. Officers are working incredibly long hours in extremely difficult circumstances to keep people safe, investigate what has happened and respond swiftly to incidents across our area.

"Right now, the priority is keeping people safe and making sure our police and other services have everything they need.

"I am working closely with Cleveland Police, Our Government, Redcar & Cleveland Council and our local partners to make sure there is a joined-up response across our communities.

"The additional officers coming into our area are an important and immediate response, but we will continue working together to make sure our police, public services and communities have exactly the support and resources they need, for as long as they are needed.

"I know how worried people are, and understandably people want to know what is being done. There is a significant response underway, with additional officers and resources being brought into our area and agencies working together around the clock.

"People have a right to feel safe in their homes and in their communities. That is our absolute priority."

Police have so far arrested 17 people aged between 18 and 61 as part of the investigation into the events leading up to the A66 crash.

Those arrested have been held on a range of offences including possession of a firearm and being part of an organised crime group.
