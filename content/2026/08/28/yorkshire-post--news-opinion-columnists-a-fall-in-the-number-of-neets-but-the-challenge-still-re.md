# A fall in the number of Neets but the challenge still remains

## One of the biggest challenges that Britain faces is that of youth unemployment. The high number of young people not in education, employment or training (Neets) is portentous of an economy headed for the rocks.

The picture is already bleak but unless the number of Neets is drastically reduced then the country could face a generation of economic paralysis.

The fact that the number of Neets has fallen is to be welcomed. There were an estimated 981,000 Neets, aged 16 to 24, in April to June, a fall of 30,000 compared to the first quarter of the year.

However, the figure is still 30,000 higher than a year ago.

It would be too simplistic to blame it on a lack of willingness on the part of young people to find employment and training opportunities. Especially when a lot of these opportunities are drying up.

Earlier this week, Prime Minister Andy Burnham urged UK business to create work experience opportunities for young people to tackle youth unemployment.

However, the Government also needs to look at recent employment reforms that many businesses say have made it more difficult for them to provide opportunities to young people.

Every young person in employment or training has a positive ripple effect for Britain. It is good for their wellbeing, it is good for the economy and the future of the country.

As Jon Sparkes, chief executive of learning disability charity Mencap, says “young people are not work-shy, but it is too often the systems around them that are failing to meet their ambitions”.
