Business confidence in Yorkshire has dropped, despite remaining above the regional average for the past year.

According to the latest Business Barometer from Lloyds, confidence fell by 15 points to 46 per cent in August.

This decline reflects an eight-point drop in firms’ outlook on their own trading performance, which sits at 52 per cent, and a 22-point fall in economic optimism, now at 40 per cent.

Martyn Kendrick, regional director for Yorkshire at Lloyds, said: "Despite a dip in business confidence, it’s encouraging to see businesses in Yorkshire still backing themselves to grow.

"Strong consumer demand, improving financial conditions and better supply chain conditions are providing reasons for optimism."

A net balance of 40 per cent of Yorkshire businesses expect to increase staff numbers over the next year, though this is down 19 points from the previous month.

The latest confidence figure remains above the 12-month regional average of 44 per cent.

The highest level recorded this year was 62 per cent in March.

Yorkshire firms identified their key growth priorities for the next six months as investing in their workforce (46 per cent), expanding into new markets (44 per cent), and adopting new technologies such as AI or automation (34 per cent).

The Business Barometer surveys 1,200 businesses each month and has been running since 2002.

Mr Kendrick said: "Four in ten businesses expect to grow their teams over the next year, and many are already planning to enter new markets and introduce new technology.

"We’ll continue to support Yorkshire businesses as they invest, adapt and plan for the future."

Nationally, UK business confidence rose by four points in August to 53 per cent, the highest level since March.

This increase was driven by a seven-point rise in economic optimism, bringing it to 49 per cent.
