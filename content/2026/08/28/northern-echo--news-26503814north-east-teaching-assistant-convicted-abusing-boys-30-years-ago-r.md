A paedophile teaching assistant who abused vulnerable boys has been put behind bars.

Philip Watson, now aged 60, sexually assaulted the schoolboys on separate occasions decades ago.

The abuse was said to have been committed in the Sunderland and Northumberland areas, when Watson was employed as a teaching assistant.

*(Image: Northumbria Police)*

Watson was previously jailed for six years in 2017 after committing a string of child sex offences against three other boys, linked to the same employment role.

Those charges included five counts of indecent assault on a male, one sexual assault, and one sexual assault on a child under the age of 13.

As part of his previous sentence, he was ordered to spend the rest of his life on the Sex Offenders’ Register and was made subject of a lifelong Sexual Harm Prevention Order (SHPO).

Two subsequent investigations around Watson’s offending were launched when three further victims came forward to report similar abuse.

Watson was interviewed by officers in connection with the new allegations but denied any form of wrongdoing.

In July 2024, and then again in January last year, Watson was charged with a total of four counts of indecent assault, those being the offences which took place prior to the took place before introduction of the Sexual Offences Act, in 2003.

Watson, of Beach Road, South Shields, stood trial at the court last week and was found guilty of all four offences.

He returned to the on Thursday (August 27) when he was sentenced to seven years in prison.

Watson will remain subject to registration as a sex offender and the terms of the lifelong SHPO, upon his eventual release.

Leading the investigation for Northumbria Police was PC Colin Ramshaw.

Speaking after Watson’s sentencing hearing, he said: “The effects of Philip Watson’s offending have proven to have lasted a lifetime for the victim-survivors he abused.

“Even though these offences occurred decades ago, it’s clear the damage he has caused has not diminished with time.

“Watson has consistently refused to take accountability to what he did to these boys, now men, and I’m pleased that they’ve been able to hold their abuser to account after all this time.”

PC Ramshaw added: “I hope this case acts as a reminder to those who think they can rest easy because the offences happened years ago.

“We will find you and we will put you before the courts.

“To victim-survivors who are unsure about coming forward, I want to assure you that there is no time limit on justice.

“Whether the offences happened days, weeks, months, years or even decades ago, please tell us. We’re here for you.”

Anyone who has experienced any form of sexual abuse, whether recent or non-recent, should contact police using the ‘Report’ pages on the Northumbria Police website, or send the force a direct message on social media.

For those unable to report in those ways, then they should call 101.

But in an emergency, or if a crime is ongoing, the advice is to always call 999.

Crimes can also be reported anonymously by contacting the independent charity Crimestoppers, by calling (0800) 555111, or by visiting its website.
