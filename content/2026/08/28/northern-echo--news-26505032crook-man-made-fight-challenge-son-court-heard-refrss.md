A man accused of killing his dad the first time they met was challenged to a fight by his father minutes earlier, a jury was told.

Martin Greensmith saw his biological father for the first time when Steven Shevels turned up at a pub in Willington on Christmas Eve last year, after drinking nine pints in pubs in his home town.

Mr Shevels. 61, was causing a nuisance in the bar of the Queen’s Head before he was asked to leave by the barman, Tesside Crown Court heard.

Moments later, 37-year-old Greensmith pushed over his dad in the beer garden when he claimed he tried to block his way back into the pub, the jury was told.

Teesside Crown Court heard how Mr Shevels was annoying members of his son's family, including his mother Angela, and his nanna, after sitting next to them.

Stuart Maddison told the jury how he heard Steven Shevels say that 'if Martin was a real man he would take him (Steven) outside and fight him like a man'.

He said he saw the defendant walking towards Mr Shevels but his uncle Trevor stood up to get in the way before his mother asked for Mr Shevels to be removed from the bar.

Under cross examination from Jolyon Perks, prosecuting, Mr Maddison accepted that Greensmith appeared to have been ‘wound up’ about the comment made by Mr Shevels.

During his evidence, Greensmith told the jury how Mr Shevels had tried to intimidate him and challenged him to a fight in the minutes leading up to the fatal shove.

Greensmith was in the Queen’s Head pub with his mother, nanna, uncle and brothers during the afternoon before his dad came into the bar after already downing nine pints while out in Crook.

He said he had never met his biological father before and he just tried to gently push him out of the way to go into the pub.

"There was literally no force at all - it was gentle," he said.

Paramedics spent an hour trying to revive the 61-year-old but he was declared dead at the scene.

Pathologist Jennifer Bolton told the court that Mr Shevels suffered a cardiac arrest while unconscious and never recovered.

She said the cause of death was triggered by the fall and not an underlying heart condition he had.

Greensmith, of Rockingham Road, Sunnybrow, Willington, denies a charge of manslaughter and maintains that he acted in self-defence.

The trial continues.
