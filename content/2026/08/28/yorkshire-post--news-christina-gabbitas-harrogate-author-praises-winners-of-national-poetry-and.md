# Christina Gabbitas: Harrogate author praises winners of National Poetry and Lyric initiative

## The power of one youngster’s poem inspired Harrogate author Christina Gabbitas to create a platform for children to learn and write about challenging subjects such as county lines exploitation and associated crime.

And after receiving hundreds of entries, this year’s three winners of the National Poetry and Lyric initiative have been picked.

Christina, also founder and a trustee of the Children's Literature Festivals charity, says: “I’m not able to share some names, but in second place is Chereece Bateson and in third place, Imogen Johnston.

"This year, we have had hundreds of entries from all corners of the UK and the quality of submissions was outstanding, with many reflecting genuine life experiences, all contributing to our mission to give children and young people a voice.”

![Christina Gabbitas with third-place winner Imogen Johnston and Tim Milburn, headteacher of Rossett High Harrogate.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmY1NzlhMDczLTliM2MtNDNlMS1hMmM4LTYzNzNjNjM4YmE1MzplNWZmMjNiNi0zZTU3LTQwMTYtYjAwMC1hMDc4ZDdmOGY1Nzk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



The poem and lyric submissions were shortlisted by Christina and sent to 53 judges including Keith Fraser, who was chair of the Youth Justice Board for England and Wales, and Dan Mitchell, ex-head of National County Lines Coordination Centre (now Head of the Met Flying squad), with prizes including £500, £250 and £150 in vouchers and the opportunity for youngsters to have their work published in an anthology which will showcase 40 poems, Listen Up2!

The original Listen Up! was part-funded with Proceeds of Crime monies from North Yorkshire Police to support Christina’s goal of promoting education on challenging topics. Particularly worried about lack of awareness in schools regarding county lines exploitation and the related issues of knife crime, Christina, an author of numerous children’s books, created an initiative for young people to express their thoughts through poetry and song lyrics.

She said: “The inspiration for this initiative came from a session I led with North Yorkshire Police at Rossett High School in Harrogate, after the launch of my story Trapped in County Lines.

"After the session, one student wrote a poignant poem, which motivated me to launch this programme and publish the creative work that has recently won a national book prize for its impact. “I wanted to encourage collaboration across the UK, especially since receiving messages and having conversations across various regions due to work with police forces across the UK.

"I've connected with forces along with youth and social workers, and those who have been exploited, who helped form a judging panel to select the top 45 poems for the Listen Up! poetry and lyric anthology.

"Importantly, this initiative also gave a voice to children and young people who have experienced exploitation, which I consider vital.”

North Yorkshire Police helped with the initiative as crime proceeds were used to cover the printing costs of thebooks. The ListenUp! Books is a non-profit venture that has already helped Christina raise more than £6,500 for NSPCC’s ChildLine service, which is celebrating its 40th anniversary this year.
