# Harrogate shop closes down in heart of town centre with 'shop to let' signs going up

## A shop has closed down in Harrogate town centre in a setback which highlights the growing pressures on the charity sector in Britain.

The appearance of “we are now closed” signs at 7 Beulah Street in Harrogate is the second time a Cancer Research UK shop has shut its doors in 2026.

At the beginning of the year, the national charity closed down another of of its Harrogate branches located nearby at 24-24A Oxford Street.

The closure of these two stores highlights a wider problem – the nationwide squeeze on charities created by a combination of surging operating costs, increases to the minimum wage and employer National Insurance contributions, rising demand for frontline services and declining public donations.

As it that wasn’t sufficient, charity shops now face increasing competition for used clothes and other second hand household items thanks to the growing popularity of online marketplaces such as Vinted.

At the time of its announcement in January 2026, Cancer Research UK said it was planning to shut 190 stores in total by April 2027, blaming ever rising costs and the decline of the high street.

It added that the closure programme, the first stage of which was to involve the loss of 88 of its shops this year, was expected to save significant sums, freeing up approximately £12.4 million over the next five years which the charity said would be redirected towards cancer research.

Once the plan was complete, Cancer Research UK’s core high street shop network would be reduced to approximately 320 shops nationally.

The aim was to expand into other retail formats with 12 new out of town ‘superstores’ coming in 2028.

If anything, the wider picture for the charity sector in the UK has become tougher since Cancer Research UK announced its restructure plans.

Other major organisations, including Oxfam and the British Heart Foundation, have had to downsize or close hundreds of charity shops to remain commercially viable.

Approximately 83% of charities have reported an increase in demand for their services over the past 12 months, particularly in areas like food banks, homelessness, and mental health support.
