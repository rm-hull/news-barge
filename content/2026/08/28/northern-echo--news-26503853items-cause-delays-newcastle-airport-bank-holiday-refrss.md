Newcastle and Teesside International Airports will be busy over the next few days as passengers enjoy a Bank Holiday break away.

But, before the fun begins, it's essential to know what to pack, as several items could cause you delays.

The UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) has issued a list of holiday gadgets that are often packed incorrectly, leading to delays and even fires onboard planes.

As holidaymakers make final preparations to jet off for a late summer getaway over the August Bank Holiday weekend, newly released figures show that a staggering 2.7million customers travelled through the city's airport between January and June 2026.

The statistics also show that an impressive 138,000 people flew from Teesside International Airport during that same time period.

And, with an estimated two million people expected to depart from airports across England, Scotland and Wales over the coming days, the UK Civil Aviation Authority has issued a warning for all passengers, detailing how a simple packing mistake could lead to lengthy delays and potentially catastrophic consequences.

  The UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) has issued a list of holiday gadgets which are often packed incorrectly leading to delays and even fires onboard planes ahead of a busy weekend for Newcastle International Airport. 

*(Image: North News & Pictures Ltd)*

Travellers are being encouraged to take a few moments to check they have packed their battery-powered devices correctly, and follow a few simple steps before they board.

Passengers are reminded to keep items such as mobile phones, vapes and power banks in hand luggage, and never to charge a power bank during a flight.

Lithium batteries, which are found in many of the everyday gadgets we travel with, can overheat if damaged, and faulty or damaged batteries can explode or catch fire.

When this happens in the hold while the plane is in the air, the blaze is more likely to go undetected for longer – with potentially devastating consequences.

Common holiday gadgets that should travel in hand luggage include: power banks, vapes, wireless headphones, laptops, tablets and e-readers, smartwatches, and cameras.

Jonathan Nicholson, a spokesperson at the UK Civil Aviation Authority, said: “Whether you're heading off for some late summer sun, squeezing in a last-minute family holiday or enjoying a long weekend away, a few simple checks before you travel can help your journey get off to the best possible start.

“With the average traveller now packing around four different lithium-powered devices when they fly, it is more important than ever to ‘Pack Right for a Safe Flight’.”

According to the UK Civil Aviation Authority, a staggering 36 per cent of travellers are unaware of the dangers of packing batteries into checked luggage, which suggests 100s incorrectly enter the cargo bay on a typical flight.

Figures from the authority also reveal that around two lithium battery incidents happen a week, with cases of devices being incorrectly packed rising by 91 per cent last year.

“Don't let a simple packing mistake get in the way of your well-earned break,” added Jonathan.

For more information on travelling safely with lithium batteries and to view the full guidance, visit www.caa.co.uk/packright.
