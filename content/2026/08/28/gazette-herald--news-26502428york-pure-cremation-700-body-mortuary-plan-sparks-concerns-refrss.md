PLANS for a 700-body mortuary in York have sparked concerns from a local funeral director and councillor.

Cllr Martin Rowley, who runs Rowley & Sons funeral directors, said he had concerns that the proposals would see the bodies looked after by about 22 staff at any one time.

Pure Cremation, the company behind the proposal, stated in its planning application remains would be cared for in a dignified way in the discreet facility.

The company did not respond to a request to comment on issues raised by Cllr Rowley when contacted by the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS).

It follows Pure Cremation’s submission of plans to convert a former mobile network switching centre in Nether Poppleton’s York Business Park into its main mortuary for the North East.

The facility, at Unit 1, Hudson Court, would operate all day to store bodies collected from residences, care homes and hospices before they are sent on for cremation.

Plans are awaiting a decision from council officers on whether or not to give them the go ahead.

Five objections have been lodged, including from Liberal Democrat Rural West Ward’s Cllr Anne Hook and local funeral directors who claim it would see bodies stored in an undignified, industrial facility.

Conservative Osbaldwick and Derwent’s and funeral director Cllr Rowley said his concerns had nothing to do with preserving his profession but stemmed from how the site would operate and the way the company offered direct cremations.

A direct cremation refers to people’s remains being cremated soon after they pass away without a service or mourners present, according to the National Association of Funeral Directors.

Cllr Rowley said: “If anything this would have a positive effect on the local funeral industry.

“Part of what we do as funeral directors is provide aftercare and help to bereaved families.

“There’s a place for corporate direct cremations, my business offers them at a similar price to Pure Cremation but I always ask if people and their families are aware of what they are.

“I’ve had people who’ve come to me after taking out direct cremation plans who didn’t know what they were.

“I’m also concerned that there will be 700 bodies looked after by about 20 staff on site at a time.

“When we take remains in you have some bodies that will be fine but others will putrefy or start to decompose, they require specialist care.

“How can so few staff realistically look after so many bodies?

“Would you want your loved one to be kept in a mortuary among the remains of 700 people?”

In its plans lodged with York Council, Pure Cremation said the facility would serve the needs of those who needed their loved one’s remains collecting without delay.

They added they currently ran a similar facility in Knutsford staffed by 22 people without issue.

The company’s plans stated: “As the business continues to see growth in the type of service it offers, it has become clear that a new base is required to serve the North East of country as currently bodies have to be transported to Cheshire to be stored before they are sent for cremation elsewhere.

““Pure Cremation occupy a similar premises on a business park in Knutsford, Cheshire, no complaints or adverse impact is reported from that site.

“The whole operation is dignified, quiet and discreet.”
