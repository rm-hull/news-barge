# A66 crash: multiple cars torched at vigil to one of victims as police continue to make arrests

## Police have been sent in from across the country after a series of deadly incidents, starting with the head-on crash that killed two police officers and five young men.

Multiple cars have been torched at a vigil for one of the men who died in the A66 crash, as hundreds of extra officers were drafted in to deal with the ongoing violence in Middlesbrough.

Police have been sent in from across the country after a series of deadly incidents, starting with the head-on crash that killed two police officers and five young men on Saturday and a house fire which claimed the lives of a seven-year-old girl and her aunt on Wednesday.

Yesterday, a house was rammed by a vehicle just minutes away from where the fatalities occurred, and a 39-year-old man was also stabbed.

Following these incidents, Labour MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East Andy McDonald described the ongoing violence as “terrorism”.

Mr McDonald made the comments as councillors revealed 200 extra police officers were helping under-pressure Cleveland Police staff, and before videos shared on social media appeared to show several cars on fire at a vigil in Haswell, County Durham, where balloons were released in tribute to one of the men killed in the A66 crash.

Police have not ruled out a link between the crash and the fatal house fire. This morning, a 21-year-old man arrested over the investigation into events leading up to the fatal A66 crash was charged with a drugs offence.

Mr McDonald earlier told BBC Radio 4 there appeared to be a “causal link with organised crime”.

On Thursday evening, videos shared on social media appeared to show several cars on fire in Haswell.

According to reports, the event was being held to remember A66 crash victim Michael Cahill, 23, with footage showing a large crowd gathering on grassland while cars could be seen skidding around the area.

Later on Thursday, more videos shared online appeared to show burnt-out cars being moved from the field, 20 miles north of the crash site, by a tractor.

On Friday, remnants of fireworks, smoke canisters and balloons were scattered around a field where cars had been torched, with scorch marks visible on the ground.

The cars have been removed but a section of a front bumper remained in place.

Pc Matthew Blades, 37, and Pc Tom Clough, 38, died in the head-on collision with a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way on the A66 on Saturday at South Bank.

Cole Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Mr Cahill and Jakub Matusiak were also killed in the incident.

Everyone except Mr Cahill, had several previous convictions, with Worthy, Rae and Saddington all being convicted of assaulting police officers.

Cleveland Police said it was investigating the link between the crash and organised crime.

Police have arrested 17 people after the A66 crash, which followed a police pursuit, as part of investigations into alleged organised crime.

This morning, Cleveland Police said Kai Inico, who was arrested on Monday on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group, has been charged with possession with intent to supply a class B drug.

The force said four other people arrested by Cleveland Police on Monday have also been bailed – a 16-year-old boy and 18-year-old man arrested on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group, a 55-year-old man arrested on suspicion of assisting an offender and a 23-year-old man arrested on suspicion of threats to kill.

Earlier this week, two people were charged with offences and appeared in court and 10 others were bailed.

Meanwhile, two men have been arrested on suspicion of murder in connection with the fire which killed Natalie McDonald, 34, and seven-year-old Valentina Foster and injured four others in Grangetown.
