Summary of the latest: rescuers search for hundreds of missing

Here’s a snapshot of the latest developments in the Nepal-Tibet disaster to bring you up to speed.

Rescue teams and military helicopters have been searching on Thursday for hundreds of missing people along the mountainous Nepal-Tibet border after a huge torrent of water and mud swept through communities following a glacier collapse.

More than 390 people were killed, authorities in Nepal and China said, and more than 1,400 were missing, including hundreds of foreigners and pilgrims.

Nepal and China both warned of fresh flooding risks in the Himalayas. Chinese authorities said an artificial lake formed after a mudslide hit the Tibet-Nepal border faced a “high risk of breaching and sending floodwaters downstream”, according to state media. Nepal’s disaster authority said a lake formed by debris blocking a river at the flood site was also rising and at risk of bursting.

More than 500 foreigners were among 826 people missing in Nepal, police and tourism officials said. Among those unaccounted for were 177 from India, 90 from the US, 39 from Australia, 33 from the UK and 25 from Canada.

Chinese state media said at least 558 were missing on the Tibet side of the border, of whom at least 260 were foreign nationals.

The Red Cross said about 93,000 people might have been affected, while assessments continued in hard-to-reach areas. It launched an emergency appeal for $31m to respond to the disaster.

US president Donald Trump said the flooding was terrible to see, describing how “strong, well-built buildings were wiped out like they were toothpicks”.

Nepaleseofficials said dozens of bridges were destroyed and nearly 40km of roads damaged, complicating search efforts.

The Nepalese army had rescued more than 100 people by helicopter, an army spokesperson said. Army teams also evacuated people trapped inside facilities linked to the Trishuli hydropower project, he said. Dozens of injured people were transported to Kathmandu for medical care.

Rescuers found bodies in Nepal’s Chitwan district, hundreds of kilometres downstream from the hardest-hit areas.

The Nepal Red Cross Society said it had shifted focus from rescue to large-scale relief operations in the flood-hit districts.

China’s premier, Li Qiang, arrived in Gyirong on Thursday to direct rescue efforts, alongside 500 military and paramilitary personnel.

UN humanitarian chief Tom Fletcher said he ordered the release of $2m from the UN’s central emergency response fund to help with medical care, shelter and water. With agencies

Fears rising lakes could burst and cause new floods

Nepal and China have both warned of fresh risks of flooding in the Himalayas as two lakes that have developed on either side of their border threaten to burst in a region still grappling with the disastrous flash flooding.

Both countries have blamed the flood on a glacier collapse that pushed rock, ice, mud and debris into river systems in the mountains.

Nepal’s disaster authority issued a notice on Thursday warning that a lake formed by debris blocking a river at the flood site was rising and at risk of bursting.

“Residents of the lower banks ... passengers, rescuers engaged in rescue operations, and all concerned are requested to exercise special caution and stay in safe places away from the river banks and risky areas,” the authority said, cited by Reuters.

China warned that 3m cubic metres of water – the equivalent of about 1,200 Olympic swimming pools – are expected to flow into an already dammed lake on its side of the border over the next three days, creating a high risk of a breach, with peak flow expected around Monday.

Red Cross launches $31m appeal for disaster response

The Red Cross has launched an emergency appeal for $31m to respond to the catastrophic flash floods that hit Nepal and Tibet.

The Switzerland-based International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies (IFRC) launched the drive for 25m Swiss francs a day after releasing 1m francs to support immediate lifesaving efforts.

The appeal is to cover the emergency disaster response as well as medium- and longer-term recovery over the next two years.

“While we are getting a clearer picture of the scale of devastation, we know that entire villages were washed away and needs will grow in the coming hours and days,” IFRC secretary general Jagan Chapagain said in a statement.

Communities that have lost homes and livelihoods will need sustained support as they recover and rebuild.
