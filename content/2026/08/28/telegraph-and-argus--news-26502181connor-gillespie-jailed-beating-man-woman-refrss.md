A DRUNK who carried out a brutal attack on a couple in their own home has been locked up for six years.

Connor Gillespie punched, kicked, and back-heeled a man, leaving him with a bleed on the brain, and strangled a disabled woman until she was semi-conscious.

Jailing 24-year-old Gillespie at Bradford Crown Court, His Honour Judge Colin Burn said the evidence against him was “overwhelming”.

He added that the “significant damage” that Gillespie had inflicted on his victims meant that they were “not able to give any coherent account as to what had actually happened to them.”

## Woman was 'rendered unconscious'

Prosecutor Benjamin Bell said Gillespie, formerly of Bridge Street, Slaithwaite, near Huddersfield, had been invited with others to the couple’s home in Halifax on the evening of December 2 last year.

They were all drinking in the living room, with Gillespie said to be “drinking to excess” as he was on his second bottle of Jack Daniel’s whiskey.

Mr Bell said: “He became increasingly agitated and began to smash up property in the flat.

“He went to the bedroom and demanded that [a man] give him cigarettes and then without any provocation at all attacked [him].

“He punched him and kicked him numerous times. [The man’s partner] tried to intervene, but he punched too and strangled her.

“Whether by blows or the strangulation she was rendered unconscious.”

## 'Punching, kicking, and back-heeling'

Blood spots were later found on Gillespie’s trainer and clothing that were linked to the male victim via his DNA.

Both victims were taken to hospital.

The man had a bleed to the brain, a broken nose, fractured ribs, and multiple bruises to his head, chest, and abdomen.

Mr Bell said an explanation for the scientific finding of blood spots on Gillespie’s clothing was due to him delivering “multiple blows to [the victim] when he was injured and bleeding and that could include punching, kicking, and back-heeling type actions.”

The woman suffered a fractured shoulder blade and ribs and multiple bruising to her eyes, face, chest, abdomen, and back plus a reduced level of consciousness.

## 'He has never been Public Enemy Number One'

In a police interview, Gillespie declined to answer questions.

He pleaded guilty on the day of trial to two counts of assault occasioning grievous bodily harm (GBH) with intent.

The court heard that he had previous convictions for GBH, beating, assaulting emergency workers, and threatening behaviour.

Mitigating, David Hall conceded that Gillespie had an “unattractive” record of previous convictions but told the court: “He has never been Public Enemy Number One.”

He said Gillespie had difficulty with authority and had trouble with any form of stress or challenge.

He described his upbringing as both “very, very difficult” and “complicated” but that with guidance and support there were positive qualities that he had.

He said a short letter written to the court demonstrated his remorse and his behaviour whilst in custody showed a positive approach to rehabilitation.

## Victims were 'both knocked out'

Judge Burn sentenced Gillespie to six years’ imprisonment for what he described as “very violent offending”.

He added: “There can be no excuse for the harm that you caused, and it was serious harm.

“[Your victims] were both badly traumatised, and both knocked out in the course of this assault.”

Gillespie was handed a 10-year restraining order banning him from any contact with either victim.

Judge Burn said he would serve up to two-thirds of his six-year sentence in custody, and that time spent on remand from December last year would be taken into account.
