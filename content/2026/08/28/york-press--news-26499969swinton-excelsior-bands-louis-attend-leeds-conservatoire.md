A young musician is heading to university after starting out in a training band just six years ago.

Louis, a percussionist with Swinton & District Excelsior Band, will begin a BA (Hons) in Popular Music at Leeds Conservatoire this September after earning his Grade 8 in drums.

He first joined the band’s Training Band at an open evening in January 2020 and has since gone from strength to strength, moving up to the senior band in 2022 when the previous drummer left.

Tracey Popham, band secretary and learners’ coordinator, said: "We are incredibly proud of Louis and everything he has achieved.

"To see a young musician join us as a learner, progress through the Training Band, take on the challenge of senior band at such a young age, achieve his Grade 8 in drums and then secure a place at Leeds Conservatoire is absolutely fantastic.

"Louis has worked incredibly hard and has developed into an exceptionally talented percussionist.

"He has also become a wonderful example to our younger musicians and is now helping Arlo prepare for his own next step."

Louis has been mentoring Arlo, a current member of the Training Band who hopes to join the senior band this autumn.

Ms Popham said: "This is exactly why our Training Band and our commitment to developing young musicians are so important.

"We want to give young people the opportunity to discover music, develop their skills and see where their musical journey can take them.

"We wish Louis every success as he starts his BA in Popular Music at Leeds Conservatoire.

"We are incredibly proud of him and, although we will miss having him with us every week, we know that this is just the beginning of a very exciting future."

Swinton & District Excelsior Band was formed in 1936 and is dedicated to encouraging people of all ages to discover the enjoyment of playing music.

The Training Band provides a pathway for beginners and developing musicians to build their confidence and skills before progressing to the senior band.

Louis’ journey from learner to university student in just six years is being held up as a testament to the value of that pathway.

Ms Popham said the band’s approach to nurturing young talent can have a life-changing impact.

She said: "We want to give young people the opportunity to discover music, develop their skills and see where their musical journey can take them."

The band is currently supporting Arlo as he prepares to move into the senior band, with Louis offering guidance and encouragement.

Ms Popham said: "He has become a wonderful example to our younger musicians and is now helping Arlo prepare for his own next step."

Louis’ progress has been marked by "hard work, commitment and the opportunities provided by the band," and he has become "a valued member of the senior band."

His Grade 8 in drums is described as a "fantastic accomplishment and a reflection of the dedication he has put into developing his musical skills."

Ms Popham said: "We are incredibly proud of him and, although we will miss having him with us every week, we know that this is just the beginning of a very exciting future."

Louis’ story is a wonderful example of what that journey can achieve.
