# 'The best experience' - Yorkshire sea cadet named best drum major in the country

## Evie Rowlands, 14, has been crowned best drum major in the UK – after being in the role for just four months.

A young Sea Cadet from Harrogate has been named Best Drum Major in the UK after impressing judges at the Sea Cadets National Band Competition, held at the National Maritime Museum in Greenwich last Saturday (Aug 22).

Able Cadet Evie Rowlands, representing the Eastern Area, took home the national title after competing against cadets from across the country in one of Sea Cadets' most prestigious music events.

Hundreds of young musicians took part in the competition, showcasing their musical ability, marching skills and leadership.

![Band Major Evie](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmFlYjY4YTg5LWQzZGMtNGEwYS1hMzIxLTJmMTMyNDUzNjBmNTo4ZmIyNGNjOC00OGM4LTQ2ZGItOTJjNy03YjY5ZWEzMjhhMTU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Drum Majors play a key role in leading bands on parade and are judged on their bearing, command, drill and performance.

Evie received her award from Chris Mace MVO RM, Corps Drum Major of the Royal Marines, and is looking forward to taking part in the National Sea Cadets Trafalgar Parade in October.

She joined Harrogate Sea Cadets (TS Cleopatra) in 2021.

Over almost five years she has progressed through the cadet ranks to Able Cadet, completed her Bronze Duke of Edinburgh's Award expedition and gained qualifications in both sailing and rowing.

Evie has also played an active role in her local community, taking part in Remembrance and St George's Day parades and Poppy Appeal fundraising.

A passionate musician, she has represented Harrogate Sea Cadets at both Area and National Band Competitions, culminating in her award on Saturday.

Through Sea Cadets, she has developed confidence, leadership skills and teamwork while pursuing her love of music.

Evie said: “Winning Best Drum Major was definitely my greatest achievement. I was not expecting to place, let alone win, as I had only been a drum major for four months. When they called my name, I was probably the happiest and most shocked I've ever been.

“In the lead-up to the competition I worked tirelessly to make a good impression and make my unit proud.

“I couldn't be more grateful to the staff at Harrogate Sea Cadets for helping and supporting me to get where I am today.

“I've always loved being in the band and meeting cadets from across the country at competitions. You learn new skills, make friends and are always improving.

“I joined Sea Cadets on my tenth birthday, so I've been a cadet for almost five years now, and it has been the best experience I've ever had.

“The opportunities I've been given are amazing, and I've loved every minute of it."

The history of the Sea Cadets began in the 1850s when coastal communities and sailors in ports like Whitstable, Kent, formed the Naval Lads' Brigades to support orphans and destitute youths left behind by the Crimean War.

Queen Victoria became the first royal patron of the organization in 1899, gifting £10 to the Windsor Unit to purchase uniforms – an event celebrated annually as the Sea Cadets’ official birthday on June 25.
