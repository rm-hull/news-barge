## A waste of public money and time - why did this case go to trial?

I write concerning the Crown Prosecutions Service defence of its decision to continue to prosecute (ex Councillor) Patricia Marsh even though she has now been diagnosed with dementia, and the Judge agreeing to a trial.

I don't know her personally and have never seen her "tweets" but really don't have to. Although only recently diagnosed, dementia isn't like toothache that comes on suddenly, it takes many years for it to reach a stage when doctors can make a firm diagnosis, at least two but more likely three to four years.

The prosecution should never have gone ahead and it was a total waste of public money and our legal system's time. Is that the best they can do when the country has much more serious crimes that want dealing with? She has my sympathy.

## Could you help support the Poppy Appeal?

The Royal British Legion is seeking volunteers to help run its annual Poppy Appeal in North Yorkshire to raise vital funds to help veterans and other members of the Armed Forces community, who have served and sacrificed for their country.

The charity, which organises the Poppy Appeal, relies on around 60,000 volunteers to help deliver the appeal nationwide each year. This year, the RBL is asking people in North Yorkshire if they could spare a few hours to volunteer for the charity, particularly in Skipton and Harrogate.

We would encourage anyone who is interested in volunteering for the Poppy Appeal to get in touch with us to find out more.

If you’re looking for a more active part in the Appeal, we would love to have you as a Poppy Appeal organiser in Skipton or Harrogate. This role is crucial to delivering the Appeal and organising a team of dedicated volunteers. You will be fully supported in the role, including training.

Volunteering is a great way to meet new people, support your community and build your skills.

The Poppy Appeal takes place in the run up to Armistice Day and Remembrance Sunday.

The money raised makes a vital difference to people in the Armed Forces community, providing expert advice and practical support, whatever the need, whenever it’s needed. The Royal British Legion can support with housing, debt and money advice, employment and physical and mental health.

## The pace of change is becoming frightening

I sympathise with Mr Rickaby (see Letter ‘Podcasts…’, Press August 25). I’ve a vague notion of what a podcast is, now that I know it’s not some rustic game involving throwing unshelled peas.

I have a device and use it for ‘Your Letters’ to The Press, though I prefer the quaint, period name ‘apparatus’, not encountered so much these days. A bugbear for me with this technology is its habit of what’s euphemistically called ‘updating’. I’m all at sea when that happens which it does all too often.

Technological development is accelerating at an ever-increasing rate. It leaves me standing; indeed probably going backwards. Then add ‘AI’ to the mix - frightening! I take consolation from the fact that it won’t be a problem for me for too much longer (as the crow flies)!
