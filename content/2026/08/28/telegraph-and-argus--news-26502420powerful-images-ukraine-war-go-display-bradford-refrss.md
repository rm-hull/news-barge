A POWERFUL photography exhibition is set to open in Bradford, offering visitors a glimpse into life in Ukraine during wartime.

Titled Through Their Eyes, the exhibition will be held at Darley Street Market on Sunday, August 30, from 11.30am to 6pm.

It has been organised by the Leeds Ukrainian Community Association Charity and will take place in the market’s Courtyard space.

Bernadeta Bagaslauskaite, from the Leeds Ukrainian Community Association Charity, said: "This exhibition is about giving people a glimpse into the reality of Ukraine through the eyes of those who have witnessed it firsthand.

"It also brings Ukrainian, Lithuanian and English artists and volunteers work to one place.

"Ukrainian music and stories makes it feel like it is a little piece of Ukraine, away from Ukraine, here in Yorkshire."

The exhibition features a collection of photographs from across Ukraine, capturing moments of hardship, resilience, loss, humour and humanity.

It tells the stories of people and places permanently affected by the ongoing conflict, offering a window into daily life and extraordinary experiences during war.

Among the displays are photographs by Lithuanian photographer M. Vaisnoras, who has documented Ukrainian cities deeply affected by the conflict.

His work serves as a record of places that have been forever changed.

The exhibition will also include art by Beata Kurkul, whose pieces reflect the personal and emotional toll of war on individuals, communities and landscapes.

Visitors will be able to view objects brought directly from the frontlines in Ukraine, many of which are linked to the stories and images on display.

Some of these items will be available for purchase, with proceeds supporting humanitarian work in Ukraine.

The event will also feature live Ukrainian music, traditional food, souvenirs and interactive activities throughout the day.

Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council’s executive member for neighbourhoods and communities, said: "Bradford is home to one of the largest Ukrainian communities in the UK, with connections between the district and Ukraine stretching back many decades and contributing to the district's rich cultural diversity.

"The exhibition offers a chance to bring the powerful stories of those affected by the war to a wider audience, as well as the opportunity for residents and visitors to learn more about Ukrainian culture, experiences and personal stories through photography, art and creative expression.

"Darley Street Market is much more than a place to shop and eat; it is also a community space where people can come together to experience culture, celebrate diversity and showcase artistic and creative talent."

The exhibition was previously displayed at the Corn Exchange in Leeds.
