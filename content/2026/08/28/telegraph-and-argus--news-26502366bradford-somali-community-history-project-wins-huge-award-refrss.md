A RESEARCH project exploring the legacy of a Somali community in Bradford has won a national award.

The Somali Village project, winner of the Cultural Knowledge Exchange Project of the Year at the Future Icons of Culture Knowledge Exchange Awards, was recognised for translating academic research into impactful cultural, creative or public outcomes.

Led by Somali Village in partnership with Bradford Council’s Bradford District Museums and Galleries, Bradford 2025 UK City of Culture, the University of Leeds, and the University of Bradford, the project explored the history of a Somali community that formed part of the 1904 Bradford Great Exhibition.

Professor Fozia Bora, professor of Islamic history at the University of Leeds, said: "On behalf of the Somali Village project team, we are incredibly grateful to receive this prestigious award, which recognises both the power and the magic of effective collaboration."

The 1904 exhibition, which attracted 2.4 million visitors, included a 'Somali Village' featuring 57 men, women, and children from the Horn of Africa who lived and worked in Lister Park during the event.

The project brought together Somali elders, historians, creatives, and cultural experts to combine academic research with community knowledge.

Public engagement activities included an archaeological excavation in Lister Park, a national television feature, a schools resource pack, and a public exhibition at Cartwright Hall Art Gallery, which runs until November 1.

  The award recognises projects that successfully translate academic research into impactful cultural, creative or public outcomes 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Councillor Ian Walker, executive member for neighbourhoods and communities at Bradford Council, said: "We are delighted to see the Somali Village project receive this well-deserved national recognition.

"Through innovative research, community collaboration and a powerful exhibition at Cartwright Hall Art Gallery, the project has highlighted an important part of Bradford's history.

"This award is a testament to the commitment and creativity of all the partners involved."

  The Somali Village research project explored the experiences of the Somali community who came to Bradford as part of the city's 1904 Great Exhibition which coincided with the opening of Cartwright Hall Art Gallery 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The exhibition places the experiences of those who lived in the Somali Village at its centre, using archaeological evidence from the Lister Park dig and objects loaned by the Somali community.

It also reveals that approximately two-thirds of the profits from the 1904 exhibition, including income from the Somali Village, were used to develop and expand Cartwright Hall’s civic art collection between 1906 and 1928.

Support for the research and exhibition came from Arts Council England, Culture House, Koor Archives, Everyday Muslim, Somali Village, and the Universities of Bradford and Leeds.

The Future Icons of Culture Knowledge Exchange Awards celebrate outstanding collaborations between higher education institutions and the arts and culture sectors, recognising partnerships that demonstrate the value of knowledge exchange.
