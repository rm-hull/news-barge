ONE of the finest 17th century properties in the Keighley area is East Riddlesden Hall.

It is a Grade I Listed Building and now owned by the National Trust and open to the public.

However, Riddlesden has another Grade I Listed Building – the less well known West Riddlesden Hall which is still a private house and largely hidden from view.

There has been a hall at West Riddlesden since the 15th century. It was the seat of the Mohaut, or Maud, family and would then have been a timber framed construction.

It remained in the Maud family until the death of Thomas Maud in 1632. He was the last of the male line and left one daughter, Ann, who married John Leach of Moorhouse in the neighbouring manor of Morton.

  The coat of arms of the Maud family in a stained glass window


However, after the couple were married at Bingley Parish Church in 1634, they settled at Moorhouse and West Riddlesden Hall appears to have been occupied by tenants.

It remains unclear as to why they never moved in, but they may have considered it in too poor repair or too cold and draughty for a family home.

Both John and Ann Leach died within a few weeks of each other at Moorhouse in November 1676.

West Riddlesden Hall then came into the hands of their eldest son, Thomas, and he decided to pull it down and replaced it with the present hall. The initials TL and 1687 on a beam in the principal room suggests that it had been completed by that date.

The property is south facing with a three gabled frontage and an aspect looking across the Aire valley. It was also a farm with a substantial barn, stables and coach house.

During the first half of the 18th century it passed to Thomas’s grandson, David Leach. He married Rachel Fenton, the daughter of a Leeds merchant from Hunslet.

He was probably a lawyer and in 1742 was appointed High Constable of the Lower Division of the Wapentake of Skyrack, once an administrative district in the West Riding which included Riddlesden.

He appears to have been the first of the family to exploit the coal seams in Riddlesden and Morton.

His will of 1753 refers to his coal mines and also to a stream that ran from Morton Moor to the river Aire and drained them.

He died, shortly after making his will, and left legacies to his wife and four children, Thomas, William, Mary and Rachel and land to his two sons.

Thomas inherited West Riddlesden Hall with a proviso that his widow, Rachel, could live in four rooms at the east end and have her choice of furnishings.

His youngest son, William, inherited a farm in Riddlesden called Applegarth.

Thomas Leach was a Bradford banker and during his tenure a coal mine, Leach’s mine, was sold to the Starkie family who owned East Riddlesden Hall.

It appears that the fortunes of the Leach’s were now in decline.

After his death his eldest son, also called Thomas, appears to have fallen into debt during the slump of 1807/8 when Napoleon Bonaparte closed the ports of Europe to British trade.

To extricate himself he sold the hall and remaining estate to John Greenwood, a cotton spinner of the Knowle in Keighley.

However, he retained the tenancy of the hall and was in residence there when he died aged 78 in 1841.

Although Thomas had married twice, he appears to have died without issue and the tenancy then passed to his brother, William.

He too had married but his wife, Jane, had died childless in 1822.

When William died at the age of 89 in 1854 the Leach line at West Riddlesden Hall came to an end.

The Greenwoods sold the hall in 1919 and it has since passed through several ownerships.
