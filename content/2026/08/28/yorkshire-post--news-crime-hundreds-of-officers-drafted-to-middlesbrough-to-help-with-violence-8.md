# Hundreds of additional officers drafted to Middlesbrough to help with spate of deadly incidents

## Labour MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East Andy McDonald has described the ongoing violence as “terrorism”.

Hundreds of officers from across the country have been drafted in to help Cleveland Police deal with the ongoing violence in Middlesbrough.

Police have been sent in after a series of deadly incidents, starting with the head-on crash that killed two police officers and five young men on Saturday and a house fire which claimed the lives of a seven-year-old girl and her aunt on Wednesday.

Yesterday, a house was rammed by a vehicle just minutes away from where the fatalities occurred, and a 39-year-old man was also stabbed.

Following these incidents, Labour MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East Andy McDonald described the ongoing violence as “terrorism”.

Mr McDonald made the comments as councillors revealed 200 extra police officers were helping under-pressure Cleveland Police staff, and before videos shared on social media appeared to show several cars on fire at a vigil in Haswell, County Durham, where balloons were released in tribute to one of the men killed in the A66 crash.

![Crash on A66 South Bank](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/27/16/cs-1e6d80b8-f419-4189-9f43-335bcd15ef45.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



The extra officers have come from a number of forces, including Humberside and the National Crime Agency, and are assisting as police continue to investigate the blaze in Grangetown, near Middlesbrough.

Police have not ruled out a link between the crash and the fatal house fire.

This morning, a 21-year-old man arrested over the investigation into events leading up to the fatal A66 crash was charged with a drugs offence.

Mr McDonald told Times Radio: “People are saying that they are going to their shops and not feeling safe to do so and feeling trapped in their own homes. That is just unacceptable.

“This is terrorism and we’ve got to have an appropriate response to deal with the people who are causing such mayhem in our communities and to allow people to lead their peaceful lives. That isn’t happening. We need the resources.”

![Pc Tom Clough (left) and Pc Matthew Blades, who died in the A66 collision (Cleveland Police/PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/28/09/v2-image-production-0dc8e8d970443247094cd147f5eca008Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg3OTkyMjI0-2.85666409.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Earlier in the week, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said that emergency services will get “all the support that the authorities need”.

The Home Office told The Yorkshire Post that “the Policing Minister remains in close contact with Cleveland police and local representatives to ensure the force has the support it needs during this difficult time”.

Mr McDonald earlier told BBC Radio 4 there appeared to be a “causal link with organised crime”.

Pc Matthew Blades, 37, and Pc Tom Clough, 38, died in the head-on collision with a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way on the A66 on Saturday at South Bank.

Cole Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Michael Cahill and Jakub Matusiak were also killed in the incident.

Everyone except Mr Cahill, had several previous convictions, with Worthy, Rae and Saddington all being convicted of assaulting police officers.

Police have arrested 17 people after the A66 crash, which followed a police pursuit, as part of investigations into alleged organised crime.

![The crash site on the A66. Credit: PA](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/24/09/cs-962d0d09-a666-4918-b0ca-51b615653a9b.jpg?trim=636,2663,360,0&width=640&quality=65)



Alec Brown, leader of Redcar and Cleveland Council, told Times Radio he has written to Prime Minister Andy Burnham to thank the Government for the additional resources but asking for more.

Mr Brown said: “Over the last five or 10 years, some of our estates – the most deprived in the country – were at the coal-face of austerity.

“Cleveland Police were slashed by up to 500 officers, and I think what we’re seeing now is them doing an heroic job in very difficult circumstances, with limited resource from what they had in previous years.”

Mr Brown said two fatal events within a few days was “unprecedented” and had left people “scared”.

He said: “We’ve had nine deaths in the space of six days, starting with that horrific A66 crash.

“To lose nine lives with such close proximity of each other…. that’s led to people feeling unsafe in their own homes.”

He added: “I get there is speculation whether there’s a gang turf war, these type of things, but as you can imagine, if you were to lose nine lives in your community, on your doorstep, you would feel unsafe in your own home.”

He said: “I had an update with the superintendent yesterday, and she’s working 20-hour days. She said reports to the police have been unbelievable over the last week.”
