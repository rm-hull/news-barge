A new banking hub has opened in a North Yorkshire town, giving residents and businesses access to face-to-face banking support amid concerns about a lack of banking facilities.

A temporary banking hub has opened at Stokesley Methodist Church and will be available to customers of all major banks between 9am and 5pm on weekdays.

The hub has been launched by Cash Access UK, the not-for-profit organisation set up by major banks to help protect access to cash in communities across the country.

Customers and local businesses can use the facility to carry out everyday banking transactions, including cash withdrawals and deposits, checking balances and paying bills.

They will also be able to speak privately with representatives from their own bank on designated days through a community banker service.

Nat West representatives will be available on Tuesdays, with Lloyds, Halifax and Bank of Scotland staff supporting their customers on a Wednesday.

Santander customers can visit on Thursdays, and Barclays will be represented on Fridays.


Cash Access UK said the Stokesley facility is currently operating from a temporary location while work continues to secure a permanent home for the banking hub.

Once the premises are found, the long-term hub will provide the same services for customers on a permanent basis.

The Stokesley hub was recommended by LINK, the UK's cash machine network, following an overwhelming community request.

It becomes the fifth banking hub to open in North Yorkshire, joining similar facilities in Filey, Knaresborough, Richmond and Whitby.

Gareth Oakley, chief executive of Cash Access UK, said: "I'm pleased to announce that the new banking hub in Stokesley is now open, giving local residents access to cash and in-person banking services.

"The hub is available for everyday banking transactions every weekday, and for more specific or complex enquiries, a community banker will be on-site to assist on designated days."

Cash Access UK is funded by the UK's major banking groups, including Barclays, HSBC, Lloyds Banking Group, NatWest, Santander, TSB, Virgin Money, Danske Bank, AIB and Bank of Ireland UK.

LINK decides where banking hubs are required, carrying out assessments when bank branches close or when communities request a review of local cash access services.
