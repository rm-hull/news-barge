A hair salon owner who started her business with just £1,000 and a loyal client list has been ranked among the UK's top entrepreneurs.

Alexandra Leathley, founder of Lexa Hair in BURLEY-in-Wharfedale, was named No. 39 Entrepreneur of the Year at the 2026 UK Hair and Beauty Awards, while her salon was ranked No. 53 in the Top 100 British Salons.

The awards were presented at Manchester's AO Arena, where Alexandra was accompanied by her 16-year-old son, Fin.

The achievement marks a remarkable journey for the entrepreneur, who left home at 15, experienced homelessness as a young single mother and built her business from modest beginnings nine years ago.

Today, Lexa Hair operates from an unusual underground location in Burley-in-Wharfedale and, despite having no exterior sign, regularly maintains a waiting list of between four and six weeks.

Speaking after the awards, Alexandra said: "There's something quite funny about being named among Britain's top salons when people can practically walk past mine without knowing it's there.

"We don't have a sign. I don't hand out traditional business cards. We're underground, literally, and we're still regularly booked four to six weeks ahead.

"I started with £1,000, my clients and an enormous amount of determination. Nine years later, I'm standing at the AO Arena ranked 39th for Entrepreneur of the Year, with Lexa Hair at number 53 in the British Top 100 salons. That's a moment I'll remember."

Alexandra said having Fin at her side made the occasion particularly emotional.

"He has seen every version of this journey," she said. "He was there when I was a single mum trying to build a life and a business, and now he has two little brothers, a stepdad and a mum who apparently drags him down red carpets."

Since launching in 2017, Lexa Hair has expanded beyond traditional hairdressing to include a Japanese Head Spa, luxury professional haircare products and an online bridal and occasion accessories business.

Alexandra has also established herself as an industry educator, author and product developer.

She is now preparing to launch her most ambitious project yet: an original hairbrush developed over several years and featuring technology covered by an international patent application spanning more than 150 countries.

"The awards are wonderful, but I don't feel as though I've reached the destination," she said. "If anything, I feel like I'm at the beginning of the biggest part.

"This autumn I'm launching a product I've spent years developing, and my ambition for the Lexa brand goes far beyond the four walls of my salon."

The launch will mark the beginning of Alexandra's own product brand, as she aims to take an idea developed in a small Yorkshire salon to international professional and consumer markets.
