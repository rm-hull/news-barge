# 'British wool is one of the great untold stories': How Yorkshire Dales brand Glencroft is making fashionable connections

## Yorkshire Dales-based countrywear and lifestyle brand Glencroft has cool designers looking to use its home-grown wool and tweeds, as owner Edward Sexton tells Yorkshire Post Fashion Editor Stephanie Smith.

At Glencroft HQ - a converted old stone cow barn in Clapham in the Yorkshire Dales - Edward Sexton is examining a baseball cap - a potential new accessory line for the countrywear and lifestyle brand.

“I want to put it in our own tweed and make it quite funky,” he says. “It seems to be coming back round with tweed baseball caps. I was thinking maybe we could have a fun patch on the front of it, or something.”

There was a changing of the guard earlier this month when Edward’s parents, Richard and Justina Sexton, who founded Glencroft in 1987, retired, after almost 40 years.

“Will my dad retire is the question,” says Edward. "We're changing to a limited company in my name, so the partnership has been dissolved. But he'll still be trimming sheepskin rugs.”

Named Yorkshire Post Rural Business of the Year 2025, Glencroft takes inspiration from its surroundings to produce country clothing and home wears designs including knitwear, flat caps and hats, slippers and gloves, and interiors pieces such as sheepskin rugs and woollen throws.

It sells its wares directly via its own website and at its on-site factory shop, as well as supplying national and international retailers.

Last month, the Great Yorkshire Show saw the launch of Glencroft’s collaboration with super-cool sustainable clothing brand CLÒ-FOLD to launch the Clapdale Skirt, crafted entirely from Glencroft’s Clapdale tweed cloth using its fully traceable British wool sourced directly from Dalesbred sheep from seven farms within a 10-mile radius of its Clapham base. Six are within two miles of the village.

Glencroft paid the farmers £1 per kg (more than three times the standard auction price) alongside a profit share on all finished Clapdale Wool products.

The wool is scoured, spun and dyed in Yorkshire and woven into finished tweed cloth by Mallalieus of Delph (in historic Yorkshire, just off Saddleworth Moor).

“We believe British Wool has one of the greatest untold stories — it connects landscapes to wardrobes and generations of craftsmanship to the clothes we wear every day,” says Edward.

“The Dalesbred breed is iconic to our area of the Yorkshire Dales, and by turning this coarser wool into a refined tweed, we’ve found a purposeful way to use more local fleece while directly supporting our farming community with fair pay and profit sharing.”

CLÒ-FOLD founder and designer Dee McGilvray, based in Norfolk, says: “When I first saw and felt the Clapdale tweed, I knew we had something truly special.

“Beautiful clothing should begin with the land and the people who dedicate their lives to farming it. Partnering with Glencroft allowed us to create a timeless, classic country skirt with genuine provenance - combining traditional British craftsmanship with effortless style.”

The connection came via British Wool, as both brands are licensees.

“She's a very talented designer,” says Edward of Dee, adding that he would like to collaborate with more brands.

The Clapdale wool project has been lengthy and costly, but there is another batch in the pipeline.

“We're taking wool this summer and autumn from farmers and then the process will start again over the next year or two of creating more yarn, tweed, cloth, and learning from previous years.” Edward says.

“Doing it in a sustainable way is the challenge. But also we've learned what things you can make from it and what stuff sells.People love natural undyed yarn.

“We're looking at a ‘make your own sheep’ kit - another way of using our wool.”

There will also be gilets coming soon, which will retail for £250-£300, using the same cloth as the Clo Fold skirt.

Although Richard and Justina have retired, Glencroft is still very much a family firm, with Helen Rodgers, sister of Edward’s wife Ruth, as second-in-command and marketing manager, focusing on the retail side of the business. Like Edward, she has two children and lives in Harrogate.

Edward’s parents will be close by. “They still live two minutes up from the office,” he says.

Glencroft’s British sheepskin rugs remain bestsellers, supplied to Fodder in Harrogate and to Chatsworth House, among many other prestige outlets.

“My father hand-trimmed 100 a week,” Edward says. “It's a really unique quality product that no one else is really doing - 100 per cent British. They do really well.

“In terms of clothing, we still can't believe the amount of flat caps we get through - thousands each year. There's plenty of other people making flat caps, but our thing is always British-made. We've changed our linings to organic cotton. We're trying to make sure you're getting a British-made quality product at a decent price.”

Sales are especially strong among tourists in Scotland. Edward says: “We struggle to supply shops in England because they're less bothered about where something's made and it's more on price, which is bizarre.

“Farm shops and tourism focus is where the trade side of things is going.”

Glencroft also supplies The Barn in Hawkshead in the Lake District, which also offers crafting lessons. “Shops that offer something else seem to be the ones that are surviving these days,” Edward says.

Busy season is coming up, September to December. “So we're really trying to make sure we've got all the stock we need - we've got special offers,” says Edward.

“Part of our retail focus is not just online; it is our shop in Clapham," he says. “We're making more of it. It's a good opportunity to showcase out here. And we're known now, even though there's very little footfall in Clapham, because people know we're here - we get people coming in every day.

“It's a lovely tiny little Dales village. There's a couple of nice cafes and lovely walks.”
