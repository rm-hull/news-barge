A Middlesbrough MP has described a wave of violence following the deadly A66 crash and fatal house fire as "terrorism".

Andy McDonald, Labour MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East, made the comments as around 200 extra police officers were deployed to support Cleveland Police.

His remarks came after a series of incidents across the Middlesbrough area, including reports of a stabbing, properties being rammed and cars being set alight.

Mr McDonald told Times Radio: “People are saying that they are going to their shops and not feeling safe to do so and feeling trapped in their own homes.

  Andy McDonald, MP 

*(Image: Press release)*

“That is just unacceptable.

“This is terrorism and we’ve got to have an appropriate response to deal with the people who are causing such mayhem in our communities and to allow people to lead their peaceful lives.

“That isn’t happening. We need the resources.”

On Thursday evening, videos shared on social media appeared to show several cars on fire at a vigil in Haswell, County Durham. The event was reportedly held in memory of A66 crash victim Michael Cahill, 23.

  Cars were reportedly set alight during a balloon release in Peterlee held in memory of one of the five men killed in the wrong-way A66 crash. 

*(Image: ASHLEY FERGUSON/ FACEBOOK)*

Footage showed a large crowd gathered on grassland, with cars appearing to skid around the area before several were seen on fire.

Further videos later appeared to show burnt-out cars being removed from the field, around 20 miles north of the A66 crash site, by a tractor.

By today (August 28), remnants of fireworks, smoke canisters and balloons were scattered across the field, with scorch marks visible on the ground.

The cars had been removed, but part of a front bumper remained at the scene.

  Field in Haswell where cars were torched at a memorial event for Michael Cahill, one of the men who died in the VW Passat in the A66 crash. 

*(Image: TOM WILKINSON/ PA WIRE)*

200 extra officers have been drafted in from a number of forces, including the National Crime Agency, as investigations continue into the fatal house fire in Grangetown, near Middlesbrough.

  Police at the scene of the crash on the A66. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

It follows a deadly house fire in Grangetown on Wednesday, August 26 that killed 34-year-old Natalie McDonald and seven-year-old Valentina Foster and injured four others.

Two men have also been arrested on suspicion of murder in connection with the Grangetown fire.

Police have also not ruled out a link between the A66 crash and the house fire.

  Police at the scene of the fire on Birchington Avenue in Grangetown 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38, died in the head-on collision with a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way on the A66 at South Bank.

Cole Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Mr Cahill and Jakub Matusiak also died.

  Police officers at the scene of a fatal crash involving a police car and a Volkswagen Passat on the A66 in South Bank, near Middlesbrough (Owen Humphreys/PA) 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Police have arrested 17 people following the crash, which happened after a police pursuit.

Redcar and Cleveland Council leader Alec Brown said the two fatal events within days were “unprecedented” and had left people “scared”.

He said: “We’ve had nine deaths in the space of six days, starting with that horrific A66 crash.

“To lose nine lives with such close proximity of each other…. that’s led to people feeling unsafe in their own homes.”

Cllr Brown said there was speculation about a possible gang turf war but stressed the impact the deaths had had on communities.

He said: “I get there is speculation whether there’s a gang turf war, these type of things, but as you can imagine, if you were to lose nine lives in your community, on your doorstep, you would feel unsafe in your own home.”

  Flowers at the scene on Birchington Avenue, Grangetown, near Middlesbrough, where Valentina Foster, aged seven, and Natalie McDonald, 34, died following a fire at an end-of-terrace property on Wednesday morning. 

*(Image: OWEN HUMPHRIES)*

He said police were working under huge pressure.

Cllr Brown added: “I had an update with the superintendent yesterday, and she’s working 20-hour days. She said reports to the police have been unbelievable over the last week.”

He said communities in South Bank and Grangetown were close-knit and that the deaths had had a major impact.

Cllr Brown added: “I’m from an estate very much like the make-up of South Bank and Grangetown, and I know how wonderful those people are and how close they are.

“This is why it’s having such an impact on their mentality, because these communities are close.

“Everybody knows each other, so… one loss is tragic – nine is unbelievable to deal with.”
