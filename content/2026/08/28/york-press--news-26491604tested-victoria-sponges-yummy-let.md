## I tested the best supermarket Victoria sponge cakes

Victoria sponge cake is a traditional favourite

WHO doesn’t love a slice of Victoria sponge?

The staple of afternoon tea, it graces many a cake stand in hotels and cafes across the country.

Originally known as a Victoria sandwich, the cake became popular in mid-19th-century Britain. It was named after Queen Victoria, who famously enjoyed a slice with her afternoon cuppa.

The cake's light and fluffy texture was made possible by the 1843 invention of baking powder by English manufacturer Alfred Bird.

At the risk of adding a few inches to my waistline, I sampled eight cakes to see how they shaped up.

Weights are given where mentioned.

*Morrisons The Best Hand Decorated Victoria Sponge Cake, 6 servings, £4.00

This was a neatly-shaped shaped cake, with a good dusting of icing sugar on top – was that the hand-decorated bit? Aside from that, though, the cake was scruffy on one side with a fair bit of runny strawberry jam that had seeped out. As for taste, it was suitably jammy in the middle, with a reasonable amount of buttercream, but the cake itself was quite dry. The pack boasts of ‘artisan bakery quality; and ‘only The Best will do’ - to me, this cake wasn’t great, it was quite basic.

Pros: shape.

Cons: appearance, consistency, taste.

Marks: 2/5

*Tesco finest Victoria Sponge Cake, serves 6, £4.00

An appealing concave top and nice dusting of sugar gave this cake an appealing look, but the sponge was a little dry and tasted rather bland. The strawberry jam wasn’t sufficiently sweet, but, along with the buttercream gave a reasonable centre. It was okay but not what I’d expect from the finest range.

Pros: appearance

Cons: consistency, taste

Mark: 2/5

*Sainsbury’s Taste the Difference Victoria Sponge Cake, 345g, serves 6, £3.95 (£1.97 with Nectar).

This cake was one penny short of half-price with a Nectar offer, so that’s a bonus to start with. But sadly, things went downhill fast. The cake was by far the worst in appearance with a yellow, waxy appearance on top and virtually no sugar dusting. My husband thought it was a lemon drizzle cake. The cake shape was also a little skewed, higher at one side. The sponge was moist, which was nice, but the buttercream and jam had an artificial taste to it. I certainly couldn’t ‘Taste the Difference.’

Pros: the Nectar offer price, consistency.

Cons: appearance, taste.

Mark: 1/5

*Specially Selected Victoria Sponge Cake by Aldi, 350g, serves 6, £2.99

The shape of this one was a little skewed – higher at one side than the other and the sugar dusting was uneven. The sponge was a bit dry, but and tasted okay. I thought the butter cream had an artificial taste, and there wasn’t much jam. It’s a good price, but not a great cake.

Pros: taste.

Cons: appearance.

Mark: 2/5

*Exceptional Hand Finished Victoria Sponge Cake, Asda, serves 6, £2.50

A lovely shape, but not much sugar dusting on top and quite dark in appearance – not as appealing as some others. The cake had a more solid in consistency than some, which I quite liked. It had just the right level of sweetness and I could really taste the jam and butter cream. Like Morrisons, I wasn’t sure as to what was hand finished. At £2.50, it’s extremely budge-friendly.

Pros: consistency, taste, price.

Cons: appearance.

Mark: 4/5

*Coop Irresistible Hand Finished Victoria Sponge Cake, 6 servings, £3.85

This looked slightly larger than the others. It had a nice shape and convex top. The sponge had a light, moist texture with a jammy centre. The only downside was the cream, or lack of it - there could have been more. I enjoyed it, however. Another hand finished cake - it must be the dusting.

Pros: appearance, taste.

Cons: filling.

Mark: 3/5

*M&S Food Buttery & Fruity Victoria Sponge Cake, 460g, £4.25

At first glance I could see that the cake had a very uneven filling, with lots of cream on one side and barely any on the other, so the taste varied depending upon where it was cut. The sponge itself was soft in consistency, and pleasantly buttery. The cream was tasty and the jam suitably gooey. I liked this cake the best, but marked it down due to the lopsided filling; it’s also the most expensive.

Pros: taste.

Cons: filling, cost.

Mark: 3/5

*Iceland Victoria Sponge Cake, 345g, serves 4, £2.50

This cake was different in that it needed defrosting. It says serves four, but could easily serve six.

It was a little flat in appearance with a heavy sugar dusting - it looked like one of my homemade efforts. Appearances are deceptive, however - the sponge was soft, moist and nicely sweet, and I could taste the cream. There wasn’t much jam, but overall it was okay.

Pros: consistency, taste.

Cons: appearance.

Mark: 3/5
