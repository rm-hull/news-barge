**Families are flying out to Nepal to help the ongoing search for 33 British nationals still missing after deadly floods hit the country.**

The brother of Neetu Goyal Tiwari, one of the missing Brits who has not been seen since Wednesday, has appealed to officials to locate his sister.

"Please help us find her", Naresh Goyal said as he struggled to hold back his emotions.

On Friday, Nepalese authorities named 573 tourists who are still missing in the country and despite fears of a second wave of flooding, search efforts have continued.

And as rescue efforts continue in the aftermath of the disaster which killed at least 538 people, details of the missing are slowly emerging.

Tiwari, 51, had travelled to Nepal to take part in the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra pilgrimage, which was "her dream", her brother Naresh Goyal told the BBC.

She travelled to Delhi from London before heading to Kathmandu on 14 August with a group from the spiritual organisation Isha Foundation.

Tiwari was expected to return to India on Thursday and meet her brother and parents. But now, the family is desperately searching for answers.

"We have called every helpline given by the Indian government and reached out to the British Embassy in Kathmandu as well," Goyal said.

"Please help us find her," he said, his voice breaking. "We are all very scared."

Elsewhere, Fishtail Tours and Travels confirmed Leena Bector, from Slough, was among 27 travellers linked to the tour company who remain unaccounted for.

Her husband, Bhupesh Bector, told The Times: "I know that she's in Nepal for sure, so even if they find her today or tomorrow, I need to be there."

![BBC graphic showing how a glacier collapse sent ice and rock debris into a river between Tibet and Nepal](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/532a/live/6d9c8230-a221-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

Essex nurse **Rajendra Pudasaini** said his relatives were "swept away" by the floods.

"My wife's auntie and uncle, and their granddaughter and her husband are missing, and my godson as well," he told PA Media.

"They're gone. The speed the water came, they couldn't be saved, they were just swept away."

**Sarada Simkhada**, a senior nurse at Darent Valley Hospital in Kent, is also among the missing, according to a local councillor.

The Times said Simkhada was "following in the footsteps of thousands of Hindus this month in making the spiritual journey to Mount Kailash".

Vikram Kolagatla, from London, told the BBC he has not heard from his wife, **Geetha Reddy,** since the flooding hit. 

Reddy, who has two daughters aged 12 and 14, had been in Hotel Kailash in Rasuwagadh when the flooding hit.

"We're not sure what to do. I have two little girls, and their schools are starting next week," Kolagatla added.

"The kids still don't believe it. They think she's stuck in the flood and that she'll come back. They think she's stuck somewhere and there's no mobile network."

Geetha Reddy, pictured with a tour guide, has been missing since Wednesday.

Tatajana Phillips, from Folkestone in Kent, told the BBC she has not been able to contact her sister who was near the Nepal-Tibet border when devastating flash flooding hit.

She said the last known location of her sister **Julija Jakovicka** was in the border area an hour before the flash floods struck.

"Then there is no more news," she told the BBC.

Phillips said her sister, a mother-of-two living in Germany, was in the country for a yoga and meditation retreat.

"We are terribly worried," added Phillips' husband Ben. "We just know where they were and that's really tough."

My wife is missing in Nepal - I have to go to find her

Pramod Poudel, who lives in Uckfield in East Sussex, told the BBC he has not heard from his wife **Sumitra Sapkota** and family since the flood hit on Wednesday.

"It's a very, very difficult time for me and for my family and my country at this moment, but obviously I have to go and see what's happening there," he said.

Poudel, who goes by the name Pedro, said he is travelling home to Nepal because "my family needs me".

University of Kent graduate **Nabhi Das** from Streatham in South London has also been widely reported as missing Nepal.

Several of those missing had been making a pilgrimage to the sacred Hindu site of Mount Kailash, Nepalese tour operator The Trekkers' Society said.

The operator said nine British nationals on one of its tours to the site were among those unaccounted for, including four males, aged 14, 50, 50 and 54, and five females, aged 40, 46, 50, 50 and 53.

Tour companies including Alpine Eco Trek, The Trekkers' Society, Himalayan Glacier US, Kailash Journeys and Fishtail Tours have reported missing British nationals while on trips with the companies.

Alpine Eco Trek said it has not been able to establish contact with 12 Britons who were booked on a tour to Mount Kailash and were in the region when the flash floods struck.

The company's travel logs which were shared with the Press Association, with a 13-year-old girl the youngest person missing.

Four of the firm's Nepali tour guides are also missing.

The flood hit early on Wednesday in Rasuwa, just north of Nepal's capital Kathmandu.

Preliminary information suggested it was caused by an earthquake, but the US Geological Survey said additional analysis indicated "the seismic energy was instead generated by a landslide."

It said a magnitude 5.2 seismic event caused by a "glacial collapse and debris flow" occurred near the border at 08:37 local time (03:52GMT) on Wednesday.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the UK has offered £5m to help the Nepalese government following the floods, saying it was a "very distressing and devastating situation".

He had previously urged Britons in the region "to follow guidance from the local authorities and follow our travel advice".

Speaking on Thursday, King Charles said he and the Queen were "heartbroken" and sent their "most profound sympathy" to those affected.
