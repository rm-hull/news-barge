# Fatal A66 crash and house fires in Middlesbrough 'not linked' say detectives

## Detectives have confirmed they are not linking a house fire which killed a seven-year-old girl and her aunt to a crash on the A66 last weekend which left seven people dead, including two police officers.

Hundreds of extra police officers have been drafted in to support officers after a series of recent incidents in the Middlesbrough and County Durham area – starting with the head-on crash that left two police officers and five young men dead.

On Friday, the head of local policing at Cleveland Police confirmed the force is not linking the crash to the fire.

Assistant Chief Constable Dave Sutherland: “Since the tragic incident last Saturday morning in which seven people, including two of our serving police officers, Pc Matthew Blades and Pc Tom Clough, sadly lost their lives, officers and staff have remained working around the clock to continue to serve the public of Cleveland.

“In the same week, we have also seen a tragic house fire on Wednesday in which two people lost their lives, which is also understandably impacting the force and our local communities. At this time, we are not linking this incident to the fatal collision on the A66.

![A woman and child died in a house fire in Grangetown, near Middlesbrough, on Wednesday (PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/28/15/v2-image-production-c58f9776a19792308f6b95c00d75580bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg4MDEwOTQ3-2.85667736.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



“Our thoughts remain with all those affected by these incidents as we continue our investigations into what happened.”

“Since these two horrific events, the reality of policing in Cleveland and the levels of violence suffered in our communities, has come under the spotlight on a national level.

“Brave officers face significant challenges day in day out, but I want to reassure the public that we will remain unwavering in our efforts to tackle criminals and protect our communities.

“Despite these challenges, calls for service to the force are being answered effectively, we are deploying to incidents, and investigating crime as we do every day.

“It’s important to acknowledge the support and additional resource we are receiving from a number of other forces in the northern region and beyond, and also from the National Crime Agency.

“This support not only means we can maintain our service to the public, but also continue to conduct patrols in the areas most affected and be a visible presence should anyone wish to speak to officers with any concerns.

“Lastly, I want to thank the general public. Our officers work closely with many corners of our communities on a daily basis to tackle issues in their local neighbourhoods, and whose support these past seven days has been overwhelming and very much appreciated.

“It has provided officers, staff and the families of the officers with great comfort.”

Meanwhile, Anna Turley, Redcar MP, said she had been in a briefing with the Chief Constable of Cleveland Police, Victoria Fuller, who wanted to reassure the public that the police had the necessary resources.

Ms Turley said: “People are feeling frightened in their communities, and I’ve just come from a really important briefing with the chief constable, who said that ‘I really want to reassure people that we have all the resources we need’.

“We are working day and night to keep people safe.

“We have over 200 extra officers from all across the country who’ve come in and who are doing everything they can, and we’ve seen from the number of arrests so far already that they are taking action.

“They are keeping our communities safe, and they want us to feel reassured going forward.”

Ms Turley said she personally felt reassured by the number of arrests made so far.

She said: “This is not going to be a blip. These resources will be here for some time, and then the conversation should start about what we need to do going forward.”
