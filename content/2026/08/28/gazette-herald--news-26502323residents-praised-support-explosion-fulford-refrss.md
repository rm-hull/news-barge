An MP has praised residents who rallied around to support each other after an explosion in a York street which left a man seriously injured and residents evacuated from their homes.

York Outer MP Luke Charters said the actions that followed the gas cylinder exploding in Crossfield Crescent showed the “community spirit in Fulford”.

The MP said he knew “people across Fulford will be wishing the man who was injured well and thinking of him and his family as he recovers”.

Mr Charters thanked the emergency services and nearby residents who supported each other after the explosion.

  Luke Charters, MP for York Outer 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Residents said they believed the explosion happened after the man cut into an acetylene cylinder, used for welding.

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service said the cylinder was removed from the scene by the British Oxygen Company on Thursday morning.

Lewis Abley was hailed a hero after scaling his garden fence to help the man after hearing the explosion and seeing him badly burned.

The 35-year-old landscape gardener, who previously served in the Army, said he shouted “are you OK?” to his rear neighbour before he started dampening down the area while checking on the man and reassuring the man’s wife.

  Lewis Abley who rushed to help after hearing the explosion on Wednesday (August 26) 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Meanwhile, Crossfield Crescent resident Stephanie Cressey allowed emergency services to treat the injured man in her home, located a safe distance from the scene.

Stephanie, 34, a teacher at Sutton on the Forest CE Primary School, said the emergency services “handled the situation very well”.

“They were keeping everybody calm and update with what was happening … They were really supportive,” she said.

The married mother of two, who has lived in Fulford for 10 years, said she took her 11-month-old baby to her neighbour Michelle Cutsforth’s home, while Michelle stepped in to help the paramedics.

Michelle, 62, a retired ambulance service manager, said the man had “bad burns” to his right arm and was taken to hospital for treatment.

  Police on the scene in Crossfield Crescent, Fulford, York, on Wednesday (August 27) 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Michelle and her husband Neil opened the garden of their home in Crossfield Crescent for neighbours who had been evacuated due to the explosion. The residents were allowed to return to their homes after the cordon was lifted at around 4pm on Wednesday.

Stephanie admitted it was a “scary situation” but said: “I feel like everybody came together and helped.”

Mr Charters said: “I want to say a big thank you to our emergency services, and to Lewis, Neil, Michelle, Stephanie and everyone else who helped. They saw that their neighbours needed support and simply got on with it, whether that meant helping the injured man, reassuring his wife or opening up their homes and gardens.

“Many of us know and recognise the community spirit in Fulford. People look out for each other and help however they can, and that was clear on Wednesday.”
