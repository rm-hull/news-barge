A FORMER banker is encouraging others to follow their passions after switching careers to become a social worker.

Omar Khan, 35, enrolled in the University of Bradford’s BA (Hons) Social Work programme after being made redundant from his role as a bank manager at Santander in Bradford.

He began the course in September 2025, seeing his redundancy as an opportunity to return to education.

Mr Khan said: "Sometimes you have to find your own journey.

"I do think becoming a social worker is my calling.

"I have family members who are social workers.

"If I had studied social work when I was younger, I don’t think I would be as good at it.

"Everything happened for a reason.

"I’m not confused about what I want to do now.

"Social work is exactly what I want to do."

Fifteen years earlier, Mr Khan began studying for a teaching degree but was forced to leave due to ill health.

He then spent a decade working in the banking sector before deciding to pursue social work.

Mr Khan is now encouraging others to consider programmes in social care, such as Working with Children, Young People and Families or Public Health and Population Studies, through the University of Bradford’s Clearing process.

He said: "Weigh up your options and choose something you’re passionate about, even if you’re still trying to figure out exactly what you want to do.

"It’s never too late to study.

"Do your research and understand the course requirements.

"If you do something you enjoy, you’ll really excel at it."

Alongside his studies, Mr Khan works part-time as a support worker for adults with disabilities.

During his first year on the course, he took part in video podcasts on values and ethics and performed in a theatre production related to his studies.

He said: "I just assumed I would never come back to education.

"My redundancy gave me the opportunity to plan and prepare for it.

"It’s not a decision I regret.

"I love university.

"Social work is a rewarding career.

"You can make a real difference to people’s lives.

"It’s about standing up for people."

The University of Bradford offers a range of social care programmes through Clearing.

Clearing remains open until October 9.

The university’s Clearing phone line is 0808 196 9129.

Applications can also be submitted online.
