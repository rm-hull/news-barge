Two brick bridges that hold years of memories will be demolished amid growing safety concerns.

Crawford’s Bridge and Black Squares Bridge, located across the Castle Eden Walkway, have been closed to the public due to long-standing structural defects, including cracks and movement.

Regular inspections have raised concerns about the risk of falling masonry and potential collapse.

Stockton Borough Council say the bridges, which are heavily overgrown and fenced off, "no longer serve any functional purpose".

The announcement has prompted disappointment from residents on social media.

   

*(Image: /Google Maps/Stephen Psallidas)*

One person wrote: "Another little bit of history gone again."

Another said: "These bridges have a history and should have been maintained decades ago."

Nigel Cooke, cabinet member for environment, leisure and culture at Stockton Borough Council, said: "We know these two bridges are familiar landmarks for many people who enjoy the Castle Eden Walkway, but we have to put the public’s safety first.

"The bridges are now old and have long outlived their original purpose.

"Due to the nature and severity of the failures, we know that a repair option would effectively require a full rebuild, which would have significant cost implications and would far exceed the available budget."

Others shared memories of playing near the bridges as children, riding horses under them and organising family photoshoots with the bridges in the background.

"Don't demolish our industrial heritage and history", another said.

Demolition is expected to begin this autumn and will take up to two weeks.

The walkway will be closed for the duration of the works to ensure public safety.
