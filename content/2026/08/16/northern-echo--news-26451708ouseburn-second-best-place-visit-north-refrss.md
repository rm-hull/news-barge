Tucked away beneath railway bridges and just a short stroll from Newcastle city centre, one neighbourhood has quietly become one of the north's most talked-about destinations.

The former industrial district of Ouseburn has been named the second-best place to visit in the North of England this summer by The Independent, which praised its blend of culture, food, nightlife and independent spirit.

Described as a place to 'eat, drink, see a live band, hang out and take a walk', Ouseburn has managed to retain a village-like feel despite being so close to Newcastle city centre.

Set in a valley carved by the Ouse Burn river before it meets the Tyne, the neighbourhood is characterised by steep streets, converted warehouses, stone bridges and pockets of greenery that soften its industrial past.

Once home to factories and workshops, Ouseburn has reinvented itself as Newcastle's creative quarter, drawing artists, musicians, independent businesses and visitors from across the country.

At its heart is The Biscuit Factory, a former Victorian warehouse that claims to be the UK's largest independent commercial art, craft and design gallery.

Regular exhibitions showcase the work of both established and emerging artists - cementing Ouseburn's reputation as one of Newcastle's cultural hubs.

The area's creative streak extends beyond its galleries, with independent studios, workshops and colourful street art dotted throughout.

*(Image: Living North)*

Families are also well catered for, as Ouseburn is home to Seven Stories - the National Centre for Children's Books - while Ouseburn Farm offers the unusual experience of meeting goats and pigs in the middle of a city.

Those interested in history can join one of the Ouseburn Trust's heritage walks exploring the neighbourhood's industrial history.

Visitors can also step underground on a guided tour of the Victoria Tunnel, a former 19th-century wagonway later used as an air raid shelter during the Second World War.

Food and drink are another major part of Ouseburn's appeal.

Independent cafés, bars and restaurants occupy former warehouses and railway arches, while craft breweries and live music venues help create a thriving nightlife scene.

Venues such as The Tyne Bar and The Cumberland Arms have become favourites with locals and visitors alike.

Throughout the year, the neighbourhood hosts markets and events celebrating local makers, artists and food producers.

The monthly Ouseburn Market attracts dozens of independent traders and has become a fixture in Newcastle's cultural calendar.

In naming Ouseburn the north's second-best destination for summer 2026, The Independent highlighted a neighbourhood that has successfully reinvented itself without losing its character.

Combining industrial heritage, creativity, culture and community spirit, it has become one of the North East's most distinctive places to visit.
