Premier League champions Arsenal take on FA Cup winners Manchester City in the Community Shield in the traditional curtain-raiser to the Premier League season.

This year’s edition takes place at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff, relocated from its usual home at Wembley due to a series of concerts by The Weeknd at the London venue.

Arsenal won their first league title for 22 years when they finished top of the pile in May, seven points clear of Pep Guardiola’s City, and Mikel Arteta will hope to pick up where they left off as they bid to defend their crown.

City, who beat Chelsea 1-0 in the FA Cup final, begin a new era under Enzo Maresca after a decade of success led by Guardiola – although the club begin the new season with the threat of possible sanctions still hanging over them.

## City to finish second best?

Manchester City last won the Community Shield in 2024, beating Manchester United on penalties.

However, they’ve been runners-up in the match nine times, with only their neighbours doing so more (10).

## Arsenal's Community Shield record

Only Manchester United (21) have won the Community Shield more often than Arsenal (17, including shared).

The Gunners have won each of their last five such fixtures.

They have also won all three matches as reigning Premier League champions (1998, 2002, 2004).

This is exactly the kind of occasion this stadium was made for, and we cannot wait to welcome everyone to Cardiff."

## Why is the Community Shield not being played at Wembley?

The 2026 Community Shield is being played at Cardiff’s Principality Stadium for the first time in 20 years due to the match clashing with The Weeknd’s tour dates at Wembley.

The match between the Premier League champions and FA Cup winners, which this year pits Arsenal against Manchester City, is traditionally played at Wembley, but the iconic stadium was unavailable on Sunday 16 August “due to pre-planned concerts”, as revealed by the FA back in March.

The Weeknd is playing five dates at Wembley between 14 and 19 August, with “The Home of Football” being chosen to stage his London performances.

The forced change has brought the Community Shield back to the venue that hosted six editions between 2001 and 2006 while the new-look Wembley was under construction.
