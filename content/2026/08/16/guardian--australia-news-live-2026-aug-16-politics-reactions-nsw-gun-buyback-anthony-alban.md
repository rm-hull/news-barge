Albanese says states 'have a willing partner' in commonwealth as NSW gun buyback announced

Anthony Albanese has just been speaking alongside the New South Wales premier, Chris Minns, about the state’s gun buyback scheme agreed in response to the Bondi terror attack.

The prime minister said:

Australians are quite rightly proud of our gun laws but the fact is there are more guns in Australia today than there were at the time of dealing with the Port Arthur gun buyback that occurred under the Howard government.

These arrangements are available to every state and territory, and this reform comes on top of previous reforms that have been undertaken by my government in partnership with states and territories in particular the first ever development of a national firearms register.

That’s absolutely critical. I’ll continue to work constructively with states and territories.

They will make their own decisions under our federation system. They are responsible. But what I would say and reiterate is that in the commonwealth they have a willing partner. We know this is universally supported in some quarters.

This is controversial and it will be opposed. But we’ve made a decision.

NSW opposition leader says gun buyback scheme ‘rushed’

The NSW opposition leader, Kellie Sloane, has accused the state government of rushing the buyback scheme and has called on Labor to answer seven questions.

In a statement, Sloane said the Minns government must urgently explain the total anticipated cost of the buyback and how much will be paid by NSW; how the compensation amounts were determined; whether compensation would also apply to ammunition and other accessories; how many firearms are expected to be surrendered; why NSW is proceeding ahead of a national agreement; what safeguards will protect businesses and licence holders from financial loss; and whether NSW police are adequately resourced to administer the scheme.

Sloane said:

The NSW Liberals have consistently supported practical reforms that improve public safety. We will also continue to hold Labor accountable and ensure its laws are workable, properly funded and do not unfairly punish law-abiding citizens.

The Government must correct course, engage genuinely with affected stakeholders and resolve these concerns before the buyback begins.

Defence minister headed to PNG and Solomon Islands

Tom McIlroy

Richard Marles is visiting Port Moresby and Honiara this weekend, meeting with Papua New Guinea’s prime minister, James Marape, and defence minister, Elias Kapavore, today.

Discussions will focus on strengthening defence cooperation and advancing implementation of the historic Pukpuk treaty.

Marles will then head to Solomon Islands and meet with the country’s prime minister, Matthew Wale, and join government ministers at the opening of the Western Border Outpost in the Shortland Islands.

The facility will bring together police, customs, immigration and biosecurity officials in one location, and support Solomon Islands’ Guardian-class patrol boats.

“Pacific security is most effective when it is led by Pacific nations,” Marles said.

Australia is proud to work alongside our Pacific partners to support their priorities and contribute to a secure, stable and resilient region.
