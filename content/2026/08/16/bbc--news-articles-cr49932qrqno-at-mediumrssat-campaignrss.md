Some homes have avoided complete destruction - others have burned to the ground

**People who had to flee their houses during the devastating Stourbridge fires are starting to return home.**

Many families are only just discovering the extent of damage to their homes as West Midlands Fire Service allowed some behind the cordon after three days.

Nineteen properties across five streets have been fully destroyed with another 18 severely damaged.

Aerial images of the scene appear to show some houses have remained entirely entact while those next to them have been burnt to the ground.

Residents of Brunswick Gate, Landsgate, Ounty John Lane, Quarry Park Road and Racecourse Lane were asked to gather to speak to fire chiefs one by one.

They then learned if it was safe to return or if their home needed significant repairs or had been lost entirely.

The blaze was one of several being tackled across England and Wales on Thursday, the hottest day of the year so far.

Phil Garrigan from the National Fire Chief's Council (NFCC) said on Saturday that 11 major fires in the past 48 hours and 43 new wildfires since Friday have left services under "incredible pressure".

![A fireman walks in front of a completely destroyed house](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1408/cpsprodpb/1744/live/451bfb00-9949-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.png)

Some residents are only just discovering if their homes are among those lost

Among those who have been able to return home are the Abdullah family, who fled as they were preparing to head to a local Wetherspoons pub to celebrate 18-year-old Dominic's A-level results.

Dominic told the BBC that nothing could describe the feeling of watching his neighbours houses burn.

"Seeing the houses go down, seeing neighbours we know - their rooftops just burning, the whole street covered in smoke," he said.

His mother said they smelt smoke and when they saw their neighbours leaving their homes they ran for their lives.

In Stoke-on-Trent, four homes were completely destroyed by a fire that started in a park in Sandyford and 13 other homes suffered structural, roof and window damage.

Marie Leese told how her treasured garden went up in flames after fire ripped through properties in Maureen Avenue.

When she was allowed to return home, she discovered she had also lost the summer house her husband built before he died three years ago.

Police have arrested a man and woman on suspicion of arson in connection with the Stoke-on-Trent fire.

Investigators are still working on what caused the blaze in Stourbridge.

A fundraiser created by Dudley Council to help residents "rebuild their lives" has surpassed £25,000.

Another by the Heart of England Community Foundation to help families impacted by fires across the West Midlands has hit £50,000.
