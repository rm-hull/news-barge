  **Why we must act now on climate change**

THIS year is already the hottest on record, but for those who enjoy the warmth there is a looming problem that could bring near-Arctic temperatures to the British Isles.

Researchers at Exeter University have recently completed simulations of how the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC), which includes the Gulf Stream, might behave over coming decades.

The AMOC current acts as an ocean conveyor belt, transporting warm, salty water northward from the tropics across the Atlantic at the surface.

Around Iceland the water cools, becomes more dense and sinks to return southward at the bottom. Climate change from greenhouse gas emissions has resulted in more rapid melting of Greenland ice, making the sea there less dense and slowing the AMOC.

The researchers ran 21 computer simulations with varying rates of Greenland ice melt and greenhouse gas emissions. They found there is already a ten per cent chance of the AMOC failing. If we are unsuccessful in reaching net-zero this likelihood would more than double.

Reform UK, Restore and the Conservatives are all noisily opposed to the government's net-zero target.

However, it is no coincidence they are all heavily funded by the oil and gas corporations.

The message is crucial: if we don't want future generations to suffer a near-Arctic climate we must give our support to those political parties not on the take – those that want Britain at the forefront of green development and all the new industries and jobs it will create across the country.

  *Dr Peter Williams,*
