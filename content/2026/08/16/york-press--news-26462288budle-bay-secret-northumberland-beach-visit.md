When the sun comes out in Britain, it is common for everyone to flock to the seaside to spend a day paddling in the sea and enjoying fish and chips while watching the waves.

However, if you are looking for a relaxing place to visit to soak up some sun, there are busier areas that you should avoid.

The Telegraph recently released a list of the 20 best secret and remote beaches around Britain to help you avoid the “madding crowds”.

The quiet spot, Budle Bay, made it onto the list as an alternative to the usually busy Bamburgh.


## Why should I visit Budle Bay?

On the beach, The Telegraph said: “Visitors to the popular coastal town of Bamburgh looking for a quieter seaside experience should head north, past the castle and the lighthouse at Stag Rocks and round the head to reach Budle Bay.

“Here, as well as acres of golden sands to enjoy and sea that is safe for swimming and watersports, you can see Lindisfarne – or Holy Island – in the distance.”

Budle Bay is described as being a “hidden gem” in Northumberland with “stunning scenery and rich wildlife”.

Budle Bay is made up of a narrow strip of sand that follows the bay around, with grassy dunes and Northumberland countryside backing it.

The area is great for birdwatching and spotting wildlife like wildfowl, wading, and migratory birds, and if you are lucky, you might even spot grey seals, dolphins, and otters.

There are lots of walking and hiking trails to follow around the bay, or if you prefer to relax, the area is great for swimming and sunbathing.


If you want to spend a weekend in the area, The Telegraph recommends staying at Budle Hall, which is a B&B with rooms and a camping field behind it.

There are also plenty of other campsites where you can stop and enjoy the area.
