Reform UK has unveiled plans to create a so-called ‘Turkish barber tip line’ in an effort to crack down on businesses employing illegal migrant workers.

The pledge, which was announced by Reform UK’s home office spokesperson Zia Yusuf on Thursday, will encourage members of the public to contact authorities if they are suspicious of a business.

The policy comes alongside wider measures to crack down on illegal employment practices, and would see police and trading standards be required to follow up on credible tips.

Labour accused the party of using the announcement to “distract the public” from Reform’s continued financial scandals.

Labour party chair Bridget Phillipson said: “This is Reform’s latest desperate attempt to deflect attention from the ever-growing catalogue of scandals engulfing Nigel Farage and his party.

She added: “Rather than trying to distract the public, Farage needs to come clean and explain why he did not declare his secret £5million ‘gift’ from a crypto billionaire. The public deserve better than a party who clearly think the rules don’t apply to them.”

![The pledge, which was announced by Reform UK’s home office spokesperson Zia Yusuf on Thursday, will encourage members of the public to contact authorities if they are suspicious of a business](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/06/12/01KZBCV75VM5FWHTZRBDYTF4P5.jpg)

Mr Yusuf said a Reform government would establish a central prosecution fund, managed by the Home Office to enforce action, but it is unclear how the tip line itself would be set up or operated.

Tips that lead to successful enforcement action or prosecutions will receive a direct financial reward funded from a share of the penalties recovered, Mr Yusuf said.

But the details of such rewards remain unclear, with details of the exact amount to be laid out in legislation.

Speaking at a press conference on Thursday, Mr Yusuf accused Labour and the Conservatives of having “effectively decriminalised illegal working in the United Kingdom”.

“Well, under a Reform government, this betrayal ends,” he said. “We will introduce the toughest penalties in the world for those who employ and profit from illegal mining, will restore order to our high street, protect the integrity of our border, and return fairness to the British economy.”

Under the plans announced on Wednesday, employers would be held responsible, be fined 10 per cent of their global revenues, and potentially face jail sentences, even if they were unaware of the worker’s immigration status.

![Earlier this year, security minister Dan Jarvis urged Britons to report “dodgy barbers” to the police if they were suspicious](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/04/10/22/10220317-d00609b1-138a-4fb1-8c0d-015a882f6508.jpg)

Under current legislation, businesses employing someone who does not have the right to work without carrying out the correct checks face fines of up to £60,000 for each illegal worker, and Reform’s announcement comes after a government crackdown on illegal working in the delivery sector.

Earlier this year, security minister Dan Jarvis urged Britons to report “dodgy barbers” to the police if they were suspicious.

“Where people see activity that they think is suspicious, that absolutely should be reported,” he said.

“In the end, people will have to make a judgment about where they want to get their hair cut.”

Mr Yusuf’s other announcements include making it illegal to use a vehicle for business purposes without holding a full British licence.

The measure would exclude lorry drivers delivering to the UK from other countries, he said.

![Mr Yusuf’s other announcements include making it illegal to use a vehicle for business purposes without holding a full British licence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/06/12/01KZBCH67YR2SQVYWKC1KTG8EK.jpg)

Responding to the announcement, which has been dubbed the ‘Deliveroo law’, a Deliveroo spokesperson said: “Illegal working should absolutely be addressed, and that’s why we’ve led the industry in cracking down on the sophisticated, organised criminals who attempt to abuse our platform.

“We run right to work checks on all riders, conduct multiple daily identity checks and use cutting-edge fraud-detection technology, and we’d encourage other industries affected by illegal working to do the same.”

The Home Office announced in July it had arrested 10 Evri workers who were on immigration bail, subject to strict conditions, while further inquiries took place.

A Home Office spokesperson said it was closing loopholes by “extending right to work checks to the gig economy and delivery sector”

“Employers who break the law face severe consequences, including fines of up to £60,000 per illegal worker, business closures, licence revocations and prison sentences of up to five years,” they said.

Figures published in January showed visits and arrests linked to illegal working had reached their highest levels since current records began in 2019.

Some 12,791 visits took place in 2025, up 57 per cent from 8,122 in the previous year, to businesses including nail bars, car washes, barbers and takeaway shops.
