# Sheffielders have been let down by politicians and planners: Yorkshire Post Letters

## Richard Pearson, Junction Road, Woodhouse, Sheffield.

The residents of Sheffield, in particular the Woodhouse ward, have been badly let down by politicians and strategic planners.

For decades now, with the UK population increasing by 15 million since 1970, it has become apparent that if the housing crisis is to be met in an equitable manner that new towns are required. This would mean that well designed places could be built in a sustainable way, with all the infra structure in place, rather than putting excessive strains on existing communities. Successive governments have kicked the can down the road by failing to decide where these new towns should be located. Belatedly, in 2024 a New Towns Task Force was set up, unfortunately the recommendations of possible locations for sites for new towns in the report it produced did not include one in South Yorkshire.

Is the South Yorkshire Mayoral Combined Authority responsible for doing a review of the green belt in South Yorkshire? If it is SYMCA has the opportunity to plan the site of a new town, which could remove the need for the Whitestone solar farm as solar panels would be incorporated in the building of a new town. No development should be allowed on any greenfield site in the green belt until such a review has been done so that it can be incorporated into the Spatial Development Strategy.

In their examination of Sheffield's local plan the government's inspectors stated that more homes where required and that the council might like to consider looking at suitable sites in the green belt. In their wisdom/folly the council proposed 3,948 additional homes in the green belt of which 1,638 where on two large sies in the Woodhouse ward. The landowners of these sites are Sheffield Council and Norfolk Estates which would mean that they stand to gain financially with the potential to develop the sites quickly if the sites are removed from the green belt.

In May 2025 the councillors approved these proposals, voting on party lines, Labour and Greens for, Liberal Democrats against. The stance taken by the Green councillors being particularly perplexing. Given that the proposed sites only compound the inequalities that exist in Sheffield, the main reason to approve the sites would seem to be that the local plan is needed to stop developers building wherever they want.

Presumably if a local plan is not in place developers still have to follow the National Policy Planning Framework, of which Chapter 11 is ‘Making effective use of land’. The two Woodhouse sites being both greenfield agricultural land and sited where they are, even if they weren't in the green belt, should not be developed as housing. They can contribute to carbon capture, combating climate change, enhancing bio diversity, increasing food security and reducing food miles, improving people's mental and physical well being, the green network extending into urban areas and combating urban heating.

Is Sheffield Council taking full advantage of the funding that has been allocated to SYMCA, by the government, for brownfield remediation work and building council homes for social rent? When developing brownfield sites are they being done to the maximum density permitted so as to relieve pressure to build on greenfield sites? Should the council halt its programme of selling land it owns, particularly smaller sites, until the shortfall of council homes for social rent has been met?
