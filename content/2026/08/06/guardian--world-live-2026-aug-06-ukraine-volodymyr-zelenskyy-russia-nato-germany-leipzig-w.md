Lithuanian minister warns against Russia's 'false flag' strike on the Baltics

Lithuania’s defence minister Robertas Kaunas warned that Russia could be considering possible “false flag” provocations against Nato countries in the Baltic region.

A “false flag” is a term for an event or attack which would later be blamed on someone other than the actual perpetrator.

Kaunas said there was no specific date for any potential Russian actions, but authorities were closely monitoring Russia’s plans and exchanging information with Poland and other Nato allies.

“We are assessing and monitoring the threat level,” he said, quoted by LRT.

BNS news agency reported that one scenario believed to be considered by Moscow included a “false flag” strike using intercepted Ukrainian-made drones on the region’s critical infrastructure. Such possibility was also reported by Lithuanian media outlet 15min.

The minister suggested that a potential Russian attack – which could be carried using an intercepted or a reconstructed drone that would look like it is of Ukrainian-origin – would be used to test Nato’s resolve to support Ukraine.

He stressed that the risk of a conventional attack on Lithuania remained low, LRT said.

Kaunas’s latest comments come after months of warnings from senior regional leaders from Poland, Latvia and Lithuania about the risk of escalation by Russia.

Lithuania repeatedly experienced air drone alerts in the last few months.

EU preparing for solar eclipse impact on energy production next week

Oh, that’s interesting.

Talking about energy security in the EU during the continuing heatwaves and droughts affecting multiple countries, an EU spokesperson pointed to another phenomenon likely to have an impact: next week’s solar eclipse.

“I can point you to the solar eclipse next week on the 12th, which will have an impact on energy production.

We have established a operational group that is dedicated to the preparation of this and … my understanding is that they have a meeting tomorrow, Friday, where they are preparing for possible impacts.

But given the fact that it should be taking place, the solar eclipse, towards afternoon, its impacts is not expected to be huge, but this is just to show you that we are really looking very holistically… at the situation and, and any possible, factors that might [have an] impact.”

The EU also got asked about yesterday’s Leipzig drone incident over in Germany.

But a transport spokesperson declined to speculate on what happened, pointing strongly towards the German authorities instead.

“It’s for the national authorities to investigate. We do have aviation safety legislation in place … Before making any assessments or any kind of statement … we need to first let the national authorities look into this. And obviously we are closely, closely in contact or in close contact with them on this.”

EU disbursed €8.5bn of its €90bn loan for Ukraine's defence

Over in Brussels, the European Commission is giving its regular midday briefing, with reporters’ questions focusing on Ukraine at the moment.

The EU has already disbursed €8.5bn out of its €90bn loan for Ukraine, focusing on financing Ukraine’s defence needs, including fighter jets, missiles, drones.

Asked if Ukraine could use the money to spend on missile interceptors for its air defence, an EU spokesperson confirmed that’s the case.

“Ukraine defines the needs: Ukraine looks at the situation on the battlefield, identifies the needs, submits what we call a product schedule. We examine the project schedule and we proceed on that basis. So, it is very much a possibility for Ukraine to request, for example, interceptors or related equipment, and then we can look at it.”

Ukraine looking at cut-price Patriot missiles as it looks for missile defence options

Ukraine could benefit from a Lockheed Martin project to build a new, lower-cost Patriot interceptor, the PAC-3 Adapted Capability Effector (ACE) missile, Reuters has reported.

UK expands designations under Russia sanctions regime

The UK has added 13 new designations and six new specifications to its Russia sanctions regime.

The new entries include asset freezes, director disqualifications and transport sanctions, and shipping and trade sanctions, among others.

The new listings include banks, companies involved in natural resources exploration, tankers and companies responsible for selling them into Russian ownership, and engineering companies working on maintenance of airplanes, the government’s website showed.

Head of drone manufacturer critically injured in car bomb in Russia

Pjotr Sauer

The head of a leading Russian drone manufacturer has been critically injured in a car bomb near Ekaterinburg, in what appears to be the latest in a series of assassination attempts targeting figures linked to the country’s war in Ukraine.

Russia is likely to blame Ukraine for the bombing, which comes just days after an explosion outside a restaurant in central Moscow. Photograph: Pavel Bednyakov/AP

Vladimir Tkachuk, the founder of the private drone manufacturer Uraldronzavod, was rushed to intensive care on Wednesday after a bomb planted beneath his Mercedes exploded, according to Russian state media, citing emergency services. His bodyguard was killed in the explosion.

The Russian news agency Tass reported that Tkachuk was in a critical condition and doctors were “fighting [to save] his life”.

Images circulating on social media showed the charred wreckage of a Mercedes, with blood stains visible on the pavement nearby.

Uraldronzavod is one of several private drone manufacturers that sprang up in Russia after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. The company is best known for producing the Upyr (Ghoul), a low-cost first-person-view (FPV) kamikaze drone manufactured using 3D-printing technology at its facility in Ekaterinburg and widely used by Russian forces in Ukraine.

In 2024, Vladimir Putin visited the factory, where he was given a tour by Tkachuk.

Uraldronzavod and Tkachuk have been hit with sanctions by western governments for their role in supporting Russia’s war.

Tkachuk is also the founder of the popular pro-war Telegram channel Obsessed with War, which has more than 500,000 followers and has been used to raise funds and procure military equipment for Russian soldiers fighting on the frontline.

Russia is likely to blame Ukraine for the bombing, which comes just days after an explosion outside an upmarket restaurant in central Moscow that killed five people.

Morning opening: Ukraine hits Russia's oil refineries as Russian strikes on Ukrainian cities continue

Jakub Krupa

At least six people were killed and 36 injured in another round of Russian overnight attacks on Ukraine, targeting the eastern city of Balakliia and the northern region of Sumy.

Firefighters work at the site of a private house destroyed during overnight Russian drone strikes, amid Russia's attack on Ukraine, in the town of Balakliia, in Kharkiv region, Ukraine. Photograph: State Emergency Service Of Ukraine/Reuters

But the Ukrainian president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, noted that Ukraine’s military managed to hit two Russian refineries in targeted attacks, hitting “Russia’s oil revenues, which it uses to finance the war and the killing of Ukrainians.”

“Ukraine’s Defence Forces struck the aggressor’s facilities more than 1,300 kilometres from the frontline. The Bashneft-Novoil refinery was hit in Bashkortostan. In the Yaroslavl region, nearly 700 kilometres from our border, the Slavneft-YANOS refinery was hit,” he said.

“Russia must choose peace,” he added.

The attacks come at a particularly sensitive moment as Ukraine is growing concerned about the lack of air defence missiles to protect its skies. Nato secretary general Mark Rutte pledged to “urgently” look into this after speaking to Zelenskyy last night.

Later this month, US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to make their first visit to Kyiv for direct talks with Zelenskyy, with the issue of US licensing the manufacturing of Patriot missiles to Ukraine is likely to be discussed.
