The discovery of an explosive-laden drone near a runway at Germany’s Leipzig/Halle airport reflects a "new threat" spilling over from the Ukraine war, German interior minister Alexander Dobrindt said.

Germany has opened a counterterrorism investigation after the drone was discovered by airport workers in a restricted cargo area near the southern runway.

While Dobrindt did not directly blame Russia for the incident, according to Bild the drone was found close to a Ukrainian Antonov cargo plane.

Flights were suspended and several aircraft, including some owned by German logistics firm DHL, were diverted.

After the drone sparked the part closure of one of Europe’s biggest cargo hubs, Dobrindt said: “Drone sightings and drone threats in a hybrid context are something we have known from the past, but a drone armed with explosives being present on airport grounds is a new threat scenario."

This comes as Volodymyr Zelensky issued a fresh plea for help, telling allies that ballistic missile interceptors “could have saved the lives” of 17 people killed in Kyiv on Tuesday night.

The president has urged Donald Trump and other partners to step up their support, as Russia looks to “significantly ramp up its ballistic missile production”.

## Zelensky says he spoke to Nato head Rutte about securing interceptors

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said he has spoken with Nato secretary general Mark Rutte about the possibility of securing more air defence interceptors.

"Mark is well-informed about the threats, and we have coordinated our efforts regarding countries that possess the necessary missiles and the capability to help," Zelensky wrote in English on social media platform X.

## Ukraine's weakness exploited as air forces fail to intercept a single missile in attacks

Ukraine’s depleted air defences failed to stop any of Moscow’s incoming missiles yesterday as Russia unleashed at least 115 drones and 28 hypersonic and ballistic missiles.

The barrage, which lasted about two hours, was the latest in a series of large-scale Russian missile strikes that have become almost routine this summer.

Moscow is exploiting Ukraine’s critical shortage of interceptors for the US-made Patriot systems, the sole air defence weapon in its arsenal able to shoot down ballistic missiles.

Volodymyr Zelensky said after a Russian attack Friday night that air defences shot down only one ballistic missile “solely because there are no missiles for the Patriots.”

Supplies of defensive missiles for Ukraine in the first half of this year fell to one-third of the 2025 level, Zelensky said.

“Ballistic interceptors are what could have saved the lives of those killed today,” Zelensky said.

“It is very important for our partners to understand that delays in supplying them, or a reluctance to hand over anti-ballistic systems, lead directly to such horrific casualties and destruction.”

## Explosive drone at German airport raises new danger, minister says

German interior minister Alexander Dobrindt said the explosive-laden drone found near a runway at Leipzig/Halle airport overnight was a hybrid attack which opened a new level of danger.

Germany opened a counterterrorism investigation on Wednesday after the drone prompted the closure of parts of one of Europe's biggest cargo hubs.

“Drone sightings and drone threats in a hybrid context are something we have known from the past, but a drone armed with explosives being present on airport grounds is a new threat scenario," Dobrindt said, after an emergency meeting with the heads of Germany's security services on Wednesday night.

Airport workers discovered the drone in a restricted cargo area near the southern runway overnight. Flights were suspended and several aircraft, including some owned by German logistics firm DHL, were diverted. Authorities said there had been no danger to passengers or airport staff and most operations resumed by morning after one runway was reopened.

All speculation about the explosives, the drone, its origin, and who is behind it, is now part of the investigation, the interior minister said. "It is part of the nature of hybrid threats that it is not always immediately clear where they originate," Dobrindt said.
