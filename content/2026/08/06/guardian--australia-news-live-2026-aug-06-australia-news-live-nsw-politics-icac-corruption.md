Federal parliamentary inquiry focusing on racism in sports and media

Sarah Collard

The federal parliamentary inquiry into racism, hate and violence directed at Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people is continuing this week, with hearings held in Melbourne.

The inquiry has heard harrowing evidence from sportspeople, including community-controlled sporting groups and Olympian boxer Marissa Williamson Pohlman, the first Indigenous female boxer to represent Australia and competed in the 2024 Paris Olympic Games.

Williamson Pohlman, a Ngarrindjeri woman, told the inquiry there was widespread racism, sexism and violence in the sport, saying there was a lack of support, and made allegations of inappropriate behaviour and assaults – urging an inquiry into Boxing Australia.

“I think there needs to be an independent investigation on that organisation and there’s something terrible going on there.” Williamson Pohlman told the inquiry.

“All these athletes come from the school of hard knocks. So the racism, the sexism, the misogyny, the homophobia, and just the violence is rife in those places. It’s in the organisation too.”

Disability advocates warn billions of NDIS funding is flowing overseas

A Senate inquiry examining the NDIS reforms has been told the NDIS funding model has seen billions of taxpayer dollars flow into the profit of overseas companies, while leaving disabled Australians under-resourced.

People with Disability’s Sam Connor, who got a $1 NDIS plan a few weeks ago, said:

The waste that goes towards occupational therapists is ridiculous. I was born disabled. When I was told by the NDIA I had to hire a wheelchair. That means you have to have a lease agreement; there are only two places here that do that.

She said huge companies, many based overseas, have “hoovered” up smaller players in the disability market and now dominate – sending profits overseas.

They’ve aggressively rolled up NDIS care and equipment providers …. $5 billion is being syphoned offshore as profit rather than being reinvested in care.

Analysis from Spinal Life has previously revealed $20bn of NDIS funding is flowing to providers owned by overseas private equity firms, delivering a 25% profit margin, or $5bn.

Welfare cancellations back on, but some question legality of system

There is big news for jobseekers this week, with the government announcing it will be slowly turning back on payment cancellations.

Many of you will remember that payment cancellations are currently switched off because the Targeted Compliance Framework – the automated system that runs mutual obligations – has been paused since January last year after the government found it was working illegally.

On Tuesday, Simon Duggan PSM, secretary of the Department of Employment and Workplace Relations, announced that from 26 October the system will start suspending and cancelling payments for a refusal or failure to accept an offer of suitable work and a failure to “reconnect” with a provider within four weeks.

He said:

Resuming these decisions on 26 October 2026 is contingent on successful completion of assurance activity and system testing to ensure the necessary IT changes, improved guidance for decision-makers and additional safeguards to ensure decision-making under these provisions align with the law.

The department has a responsibility to ensure the systems we administer are operating within the law as enacted by the Australian parliament, and this is an important step in returning the TCF system to lawful operation.

Two other types of cancellations, those relating to persistent MO failures and work refusal failures, will still be turned off as the government works to ensure they are overseen by a human who can make a legal decision.

It’s unclear exactly how many people had their payments illegally cancelled but, speaking at Senate estimates earlier this year, representatives for DEWR said the number of illegal cancellations, where people were not given enough time to reconnect to a job provider after missing a compulsory activity as part of their mutual obligations, was in “the vicinity” of 300,000.

SpaceX share slump leaves Gina Rinehart with $283m loss on paper

Luca Ittimani

Gina Rinehart has lost an estimated $283m on paper as Elon Musk’s SpaceX company slumps in value.

SpaceX shares fell overnight to US$108.27, 19.8% below its price of US$135 when it listed on share markets. It is now valued at US$1.4tn. Musk, its founder, was briefly the world’s first trillionaire but is now worth US$693.7bn, according to Forbes.

The company had released financial results showing strong revenue growth but capital spending on AI development was particularly high. Tony Sycamore, market analyst at IG, said the report had unsettled investors.

Rinehart’s company, Hancock Prospecting, took a stake in SpaceX at its share market debut. It has republished articles on its website reporting it bought over US$1bn worth. Wednesday’s price fall implies the company has lost about US$200m (A$283m) on paper.

A Hancock Prospecting spokesperson said the company was still invested in SpaceX but did not directly answer when asked whether it had sold or bought more shares since its initial investment. A spokesperson said Hancock was a “long-term investor” with a diverse portfolio and a particular focus on companies with “outstanding leadership”. They added:

Our investment in SpaceX reflects our confidence in Elon Musk, an exceptional and brilliant person, and SpaceX’s execution under his leadership, as well as in Elon Musk’s proven ability to use science for the betterment of mankind.

SpaceX’s share price is at risk of falling further tonight. Early investors and company insiders may rush to cash out when they gain permission to sell their shares in the company, after a lockup period expires on Thursday, US time.

Unprecedented images may explain one of greatest mysteries of the sun

Golden bands and swirling vortices have been spotted on the sun in the most detailed images ever captured of the turbulent surface of our home star.

Astronomers observed the sun with the Inouye solar telescope in Hawaii, the world’s most powerful solar telescope, and saw new features smaller than 20km (12 miles) across in a magnetically active region close to a sun spot.

The unprecedented pictures show processes on the surface that are likely to explain the decades-long mystery of why the corona, the sun’s outer atmosphere, is millions of degrees hotter than its surface.

Nearly 1,500 properties bought using first home buyers’ 5% deposit scheme converted into investments

The government’s 5% deposit scheme for first home buyers has enabled the purchase of nearly 1,500 properties that were turned into investments, new data shows.

The scheme is also helping hundreds of Australians earning more than $300,000 a year, after Labor stopped excluding high-income borrowers.

First-time buyers can borrow 95% of a property’s value and have the government guarantee the loan, waiving costly lenders’ mortgage insurance, under the scheme.

Housing Australia, which runs the program, provided the data in response to a Senate estimates question on notice in June.
