# What does being held in contempt of Congress mean?

- A Senate committee led by Committee Chairman Sen. Rand Paul voted to hold Dr. Anthony Fauci in contempt of Congress and refer him to the Justice Department for potential prosecution. The decision stems from Fauci's refusal to answer questions at a hearing last week regarding his handling of the COVID-19 pandemic.
- Contempt of Congress is a criminal misdemeanor offense for officials who obstruct an ongoing investigation. It is a federal charge punishable by a fine of up to $100,000 and a jail sentence of up to one year.
- Individuals can face charges for ignoring legal commands, congressional subpoenas, or demands to hand over documents.
- Similar to contempt of court, it occurs when an individual intentionally disobeys or interferes with a Congressional action.
- This power is considered an implied legislative authority, essential for Congress to effectively carry out its duties, as affirmed by the U.S. Supreme Court.
