# Illegal e-bike seizures more than double in South Yorkshire amid police crackdown

## Police seizures of e-bikes have more than doubled in South Yorkshire as forces across the country crack down on illegally modified machines capable of dangerous speeds.

South Yorkshire Police confiscated 267 e-bikes in the 12 months to May 19, up from 126 during the previous year – an increase of around 112%.

The force was among 11 across the UK where the number of seizures more than doubled year on year, according to data obtained by the Press Association under freedom-of-information laws.

Elsewhere in Yorkshire, West Yorkshire Police recorded 20 seizures during the same period, down from 26 the previous year.

Humberside Police recorded 190 seizures between May 20, 2025 and May 19, 2026, although the force did not provide comparable figures for the previous year and was therefore excluded from the Press Association's year-on-year analysis.

Across the UK, 7,049 e-bikes were confiscated by the 31 police forces which provided comparable data in the 12 months to May 19, compared with 3,858 during the previous year – an increase of 83%.

The figures prompted police, politicians and charities to call for new laws to clamp down on the dangerous vehicles which have caused deaths and serious injuries in some cases.

Sergeant Stuart Ford, who leads the cycle team at City of London Police, described illegal e-bikes as “a menace”.

Labour MP for Carlisle Julie Minns, who has been campaigning for a ban on sales of illegal e-bikes and conversion kits, said they were the “perfect accessory to phone theft, mugging and drug dealing” as well as a danger to motorists and pedestrians as she called on Andy Burnham’s Government to brand them an “unsafe product”.

During a recent e-bike crackdown in Cardiff, South Wales Police arrested four riders on suspicion of drug offences after a man in his 20s was found with 33 wraps of crack cocaine and heroin worth about £1,000.

Under UK law, legal e-bikes are known as electrically assisted pedal cycles (EAPCs) and must meet certain safety regulations such as their electric motor having a maximum power output of 250 watts and cutting out when a speed of 15.5mph is reached.

Many illegal e-bikes confiscated by police have been modified to travel much faster, with some exceeding 70mph.

Non-compliant machines are effectively electric mopeds or motorbikes, which must be registered, taxed and insured.

While major retailers sell thousands of legal e-bikes every year, many illegal machines are bought on the internet.

Kits which add an electronic motor to a conventional bike are available online for under £300.

As well as being used for crime, illegal e-bikes are also often used for legitimate journeys such as food deliveries and commuting.

Thirty-one police forces out of 45 across the UK provided comparable data after PA’s freedom-of-information requests.

The Metropolitan Police was the force which seized the most e-bikes in the year to May 19, with 2,367.

That was ahead of Leicestershire Police (661), Police Scotland (585) and Thames Valley Police (425).

The 11 forces where seizures more than doubled year on year were Devon and Cornwall, Dorset, Hampshire, Leicestershire, Lincolnshire, Northamptonshire, Northumbria, South Yorkshire, Suffolk, Thames Valley and the Police Service of Northern Ireland.

Wiltshire Police said half of its seizures were recorded in Swindon, the town which Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander’s constituency covers.

Last month, the Met carried out an operation to catch illegal e-bike users near Tottenham Court Road station in London’s West End – a hotspot for phone theft and other street crime.

Speaking to PA at the scene, Commander Neerav Patel said Londonders were “fed-up of the criminal and antisocial use of e-bikes”.

The force has started using portable rolling road devices so officers can test an e-bike’s maximum speed using its motor to help them spot illegal machines.

City of London Police seized four of the machines in just half an hour last month.

Mr Ford said some illegal e-bikes are three times heavier than a conventional bike.

“If that was to hit a small child, it can cause serious injuries,” he warned.

Calling for new legislation to deal with illegal e-bike users, he added: “I would like to see specific laws around e-bikes brought in.”

Pedestrian safety charity Living Streets warned “the rise in e-bike use must not come at the expense of people walking” while the Royal National Institute of Blind People said e-bikes are “heavy, fast-moving, silent vehicles” that present a danger when “ridden carelessly”.

E-bikes have been behind a string of serious incidents.

Pedestrian Gloria Stephenson, 86, was knocked down and killed by an illegal e-bike on a zebra crossing in Sunderland in May last year.

A 10-year-old child was seriously injured after being hit by an e-bike on a pavement in Grimsby on April 9.

A pregnant woman in her 30s struck by an e-bike while crossing a road in Poole, Dorset on January 26 was taken to hospital where she prematurely gave birth to a seriously ill baby.

A record 432 e-bike fires were reported in the UK last year, with brigades raising concerns about faulty batteries igniting while being charged.

A Government spokeswoman said: “Illegal e-bikes that can reach dangerous speeds have no place on our roads and pavements, and the rise in seizures shows the scale of the problem police are dealing with.

“That’s why we have strengthened police powers, changing the law so that officers no longer need to issue a warning before seizing these vehicles where they are used anti-socially.”

These tougher powers came into force on June 29 under the Crime and Policing Act 2026.
