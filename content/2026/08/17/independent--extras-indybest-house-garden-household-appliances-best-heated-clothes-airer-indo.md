The best heated clothes airers are essential when it comes to drying your clothes in the UK’s inclement weather. Even putting a load in your washing machine and being able to dry it outside in the summer months isn’t guaranteed, as rain can often still be lurking on the horizon. Once summer is over, cooler and damper days can make it hard to get anything dry indoors without turning up the radiators or even employing a dehumidifier.

Tumble dryers are one solution, but these can be noisy, expensive and not everyone has room for one in their home. With all this in mind, it’s no surprise that heated clothes airers have increased so much in popularity thanks to their ability to dry washing without sending energy bills skyrocketing. Most models cost between 6p and 8p an hour to run, with a full drying session costing up to 50p. This may sound pricey, but it’s still cheaper than the cost of running a tumble dryer.

As IndyBest’s resident home appliances expert, I’ve tested a range of the best heated clothes airers, drying everything from heavy towels and jeans to lightweight t-shirts and nightdresses. Keep my reading for my top tried and tested picks.

The Lakeland dry:soon deluxe is the Rolls Royce of heated airers, with a price tag to match. The best heated clothes airer I tested, it fits 15kg of wet washing onto 21m of drying space, which is equivalent to a couple of loads for those with an 8kg washing machine. That’s all very impressive, but what really sold me was the different ways in which you can configure it. Lay everything flat on the bars and smaller items will be dry within a couple of hours, thanks to the cover that keeps heat trapped inside.

It’s got a shorter cable attachment compared to others, and you can’t deny that it’s pricey. However it’s versatility, capacity and power makes it worth the investment.

The beauty of Beldray’s pod system is that you can, if you wish, use it with any existing three-tiered airer measuring 145cm x 65cm x 55cm or less. While you can buy the heated pod on its own, this budget-friendly combo comes with Beldray’s own airer that’s been made to measure as well as a generously sized cover that slots over with ease.

![best heated clothes airer indybest review Beldray clothes airer & heated pod drying system](<[https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/14/14/42/Beldray-clothes-airer-heated-pod-drying-system.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/14/14/42/Beldray-clothes-airer-heated-pod-drying-system.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w>)

Unlike the Lakeland above, the Beldray uses a heater that you plug in at the base of the airer on its own special rack (to protect the floor from the heat it generates), which blows hot air up the airer. I found that this dried thinner clothes such as t-shirts within an hour, with jeans taking around three hours.

However, the temperature isn’t consistent throughout the pod, with it being cooler at the top. Although there is an adjustable timer dial, I wouldn’t want to leave this system unattended while I was out due to the heat the heater generates. It’s also a little noisy but considering the price and the fact it holds up to 7kg of washing, I think it’s a great budget buy.
