Animal cruelty cases are on the rise in some parts of Yorkshire, according to the RSPCA

**South and West Yorkshire are among the worst counties across England and Wales for animal cruelty, according to the RSPCA.**  

New figures released by the animal welfare charity show a 64% annual increase nationally in cruelty reports during the summer.

In West Yorkshire, cruelty reports increased by 15%, in North Yorkshire by 6%, while in South Yorkshire they dropped by 6%.

RSPCA Supt Jo Hirst described the figures as "alarming", and said the rise in pet ownership during the pandemic and longer summer days, which could increase opportunities for people to witness and report cruelty, could be contributing to the increase in reports.

In June, July and August 2025 the RSPCA received 6,322 cruelty calls to their emergency line.

In West Yorkshire, that number stood at 268, an increase from 234 in the same period in 2024.

In North Yorkshire the number of reports increased by 6% from 86 in summer 2024 to 91 in 2025.

Meanwhile, in South Yorkshire 153 reports were made during last summer - a 6% decline from 162 the previous year.

Among the cases reported were a terrier which was found in West Yorkshire lying in its own blood with untreated injuries and missing teeth and two kittens, which were found dead in a pet carrier in a field in Rotherham.

Emily Harrison said many animals arrived in poor condition

RSPCA Sheffield animal care lead, Emily Harrison, said many rescued animals arrived in poor condition or had experienced little positive contact with humans.

"It always amazes me that we can get the most horrendous case in and you walk into that kennel and expect the dog to be sat in a corner shaking and it's just like 'oh hello', you show them that little bit of love and care and it's amazing," she said.

"I think what keeps surprising me every day of working here is actually how resilient animals are."

**Ten areas with  the highest number of incidents reported in summer 2025:**

1. London - outer and inner: 488
2. West Yorkshire: 268
3. Greater Manchester: 250
4. West Midlands: 248
5. Kent: 178
6. Lancashire: 166
7. South Yorkshire: 153
8. Essex: 151
9. Hampshire: 133
10. Nottinghamshire: 123

In response to the figures, the charity launched its Cruelty Hurts, Love Rescues campaign to highlight the work it does to save animals from abuse.

"Sadly this year we expect the upward trend will continue and we are already busier than ever rescuing animals from many awful situations.

"Our centres are full with thousands of animals being cared for in emergency kennels and catteries," Hirst said.

"Our dedicated rescue and rehabilitation teams are here to help transform their lives so they can find love and happiness in a new home."
