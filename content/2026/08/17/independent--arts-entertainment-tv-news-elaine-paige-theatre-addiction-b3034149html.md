West End legend Dame Elaine Paige has revealed she “lived on sleeping pills” for many years, revealing she kicked the habit during the pandemic.

The 78-year-old began her stage career in the 1960s and originated the role of Eva Perón in Lord Andrew Webber’s *Evita* in 1978, winning a Laurence Olivier award for best performance in a musical. 

The role rocketed Paige into the spotlight and she collaborated with Lloyd Webber on a number of other occasions, also taking the lead as Grizabella in *Cats* and starring opposite Barbara Dickinson in*Chess*. 

Speaking to *The Sunday Times Magazine*, Paige said in a new interview: “For many years in the theatre I lived on sleeping pills, otherwise you’re wired until 2am after a show. 

“I called it my Judy Garland syndrome. They’re dreadful things to become addicted to, which I was. But since the pandemic I’ve managed to get off them and will not take one again.”

Paige continued: “I say to myself that even if I don’t sleep well, I’ll be fine the next day. I’m not ready to take afternoon naps yet.”

During her decades-spanning career, Paige has also released multiple albums and performed a number of concert tours, as well as appearing in television shows including BBC comedy *Home From Home* and the Alison Steadman-led drama*Life.* Since 2004, she has presented a weekly Radio 2 show,*Elaine Paige on Sundays*. 

The singer was made a dame for services to music and charity in June 2025 and received a Special Award at the Oliviers back in April.

The prestigious accolade was presented by Lloyd Webber, who read a letter from the Queen shortly before he handed Paige the prize at London’s Royal Albert Hall.

The message read: "I was delighted to learn you were to receive this year’s special award of the Oliviers 2026. I can think of no more fitting recipient."

Camilla further praised Paige’s "talent, commitment and generosity of spirit" which have made her a "truly defining figure in musical theatre”, adding, "you have quite simply brought huge joy to untold numbers of people across the globe, and we are all in your debt."

![Paige with her Oliviers special award](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/04/12/20/09/SEI292959568.jpg)

Accepting the award, Paige remarked: "Music and theatre have been the mainstay, really, well, the major force in my life and when I look back, I really can hardly believe the journey and the longevity of it all, to be honest."

She candidly recalled the early challenges of her career, including "bit-parts and chorus lines and lots of rejection and long stretches when the phone doesn’t ring".

Offering words of wisdom to emerging talent, Paige urged: "I wanted to say to the next generation, may you find your people, your voice and your courage and make sure to ignore anyone that says it can’t be done."
