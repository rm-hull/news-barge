# How modern window design is responding to Yorkshire's traditional housing stock

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/17/6/06/Traditional_Yorkshire_Townhouses_Featuring_Classic_Sliding_Sash_Windows.jpeg?trim=0,0,0,0&crop=&width=640&quality=70&enable=upscale)



## Yorkshire's streets tell the story of its housing and give our county its unique look – but how can more traditional properties benefit from modern windows?

Stone terraces climbing Pennine hillsides, Victorian villas in Leeds and Sheffield suburbs, Georgian townhouses in York and Harrogate, and newer developments built to echo all of them. These designs present a particular challenge for modern windows: products need to respect the appearance of older properties while meeting today's expectations for comfort, ventilation, security, durability and ease of maintenance.

### **Why Yorkshire's housing stock shapes window design**

A large proportion of the region's homes were built before modern glazing became what it is now. When those properties need replacement windows, the wrong choice can diminish the appearance of an entire street. Planning departments and conservation officers in many Yorkshire towns take a close interest in proportions, sightlines and finishes, and homeowners are increasingly conscious of their homes’ kerb appeal.

Developers face a version of the same pressure. Traditional-looking new-build schemes sell partly on their resemblance to established streetscapes, which means the windows have to carry conviction rather than simply fill an opening.

### **The continuing appeal of sash windows**

Few window styles are as closely tied to that traditional look as sliding sashes. They defined Georgian, Victorian and Edwardian building across Yorkshire, and it remains the natural reference point for period refurbishment and heritage-style development alike.

What has changed is what buyers and specifiers expect alongside the appearance. Modern uPVC sash windows for traditional properties are no longer chosen purely as a low-maintenance alternative to timber. They’re chosen more and more because they can combine authentic proportions with double glazing, discreet ventilation, practical security options, a broad colour palette and consistent factory quality, without the repainting cycle that original sashes demand.

### **What modern projects now expect from traditional-style windows**

For builders and installers, predictability matters as much as appearance. A window that arrives factory-finished and consistent from one unit to the next reduces time on site and callbacks afterwards. For homeowners, the priorities are warmth, quiet operation, easy cleaning and a window that won’t become a weekend job every few years.

The result is a specification conversation that goes well beyond "timber or uPVC". It now covers ventilation options, colour and finish, hardware, security upgrades and how well the manufacturer supports the project from survey to installation.

### **Why discreet ventilation details matter**

Ventilation has become a key part of window specification, driven by building regulations and a wider awareness of indoor air quality. The issue often encountered with traditional-style windows is that a visible trickle vent can interrupt exactly the clean lines the design is trying to preserve.

Concealed head vents have emerged as a practical answer. By integrating the ventilation path into the head of the frame, the airflow requirement is met without an obvious plastic vent on show. Quickslide, the Brighouse-based company and one of the UK's leading manufacturers of uPVC sliding sash windows, has designed its concealed head vent around a straightforward fitting approach: the canopy simply clips into place on top of the frame. On a conservation-sensitive property or a period refurbishment, these small details make a noticeable difference to the finished elevation.

### **How colour choice affects traditional property character**

Colour is another area where the market has become more refined. A smooth white frame can look slightly stark against weathered Yorkshire stone or a muted render, which is why softer tones are gaining ground. Chalk White is a good example: a textured matt white that sits more sympathetically on period-style homes and heritage refurbishments than a bright modern finish. Alongside greys, creams and greens, it gives homeowners and specifiers room to match the window to the property.

### **Why smart security is entering window specification**

Security expectations are shifting too, and not only at the front door. Quickslide's Legacy Protect, a smart security capability integrated into its sliding sash windows at manufacture, is designed to work with Kubu smart sensors, which let homeowners check through the Kubu app whether a window is open or closed. It’s a monitoring convenience rather than a security guarantee, but for many households that reassurance is genuinely useful.

Notably for the trade, the sensors are designed to be bought by homeowners directly from Kubu, so builders and installers are not drawn into supplying or supporting the technology. The windows are ready to work with Kubu from mid-2026, with marked sensor fitting points integrated into the frame at the factory.

### **The role of reliable manufacturing partners**

That last detail points to a wider theme: how much of a modern window's value is built in before it reaches site. Factory preparation, consistent tolerances and dependable lead times are what allow architects, builders and developers to specify traditional-style products with confidence. It is one reason manufacturing partnerships are now recognised in their own right; Quickslide's shortlisting for Manufacturing Partner of the Year at the Architect of the Year Awards 2026 reflects how seriously the industry takes supply confidence and practical support alongside the products themselves.

### **Conclusion**

Yorkshire's traditional housing stock is an asset worth protecting, and modern window design has to respond to it carefully. For homeowners, builders, architects and developers, the challenge is the same: preserve character while meeting modern expectations. Small specification choices, from concealed ventilation and softer colour finishes to smart security readiness and factory-prepared components, are helping traditional-looking projects keep their appearance while becoming easier to live with, easier to specify and easier to deliver.
