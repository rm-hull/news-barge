A North Yorkshire heritage railway is opening up some of its fascinating behind-the-scenes areas as part of this year's Heritage Open Days celebrations.

The North Yorkshire Moors Railway (NYMR) will take part in the nationwide festival from September 11 to 20, offering free events across Pickering, Goathland, and Grosmont.

Visitors will have the chance to explore the railway’s history and operations through guided tours, exhibitions, and hands-on demonstrations.

Rhiannon Hope, learning and interpretation lead at NYMR, said: "Heritage Open Days is a wonderful opportunity to share the stories that make the North Yorkshire Moors Railway so special.

  Goathland Station 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"From the memories preserved in our archive to the incredible engineering skills that keep our locomotives running, every experience offers visitors a chance to connect with the people behind the railway.

"Whether you’re a lifelong enthusiast or discovering us for the first time, we look forward to welcoming everyone to explore, learn, and experience our heritage from a new perspective."

A key highlight of this year’s programme is the Living Histories: A Dive into the Archive exhibition, which will be held from September 14 to 16 in the Learning Classroom at Pickering Station.

  Pickering Station 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The display will feature historical materials documenting the railway from its mainline days through to its preservation era and current operations.

Visitors can also take part in guided station tours at Pickering, Goathland, and Grosmont from September 14 to 16.

Each location offers a unique glimpse into the past, including the 1930s-themed Pickering Station, the well-known Goathland Station (which appeared as Hogsmeade in the first Harry Potter film), and Grosmont Station, styled as it would have appeared in 1952.

Behind-the-scenes access continues with the Motive Power Depot Walking Shed Tour in Grosmont, running on September 12 and 13 from 11am to 3pm.

These tours will allow visitors to see the locomotive sheds, view ongoing restoration projects, and photograph the historic engines up close.

Additional events include guided talks at the Motive Power Depot from September 14 to 16, running from 1pm to 3pm, as well as signal box demonstrations at Pickering Station during the same dates.

The demonstrations, held from 10am to 12pm and 12.30pm to 2pm, will offer a hands-on introduction to railway signalling, including the chance to operate the signal and use the token machine.

Visitors can also go behind the scenes at Armstrong Oilers in Grosmont, a vital part of the heritage rail industry featured in the Channel 4 series Steam Train Diaries.

From September 13 to 16 and on September 20, attendees can meet Brian and Tammy, who help manufacture essential components for steam locomotives.

Heritage Open Days is England’s largest festival of history and culture, featuring thousands of free events organised by the National Trust and delivered by local organisations and volunteers.
