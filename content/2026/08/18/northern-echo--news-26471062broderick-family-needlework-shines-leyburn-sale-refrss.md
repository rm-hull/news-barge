A collection of historic needlework linked to a Yorkshire Dales family proved a major attraction at an auction, with a group of antique samplers selling for £1,000.

The Broderick family from Swaledale, who worked the samplers, owned the properties Spring End and Summer Lodge and were prominent in the region from the 14th to the 20th century through farming and mining.

Their family papers are now held by Richmondshire Museum.

  Detail from collection of Broderick Family Samplers – Sold for £1,000 

*(Image: Tennants Auctioneers)*

The samplers were part of Tennants Auctioneers’ Fashion, Costume and Textiles Sale, held on August 14 in Leyburn.

A spokesman for Tennants Auctioneers said: "A family collection of eleven antique samplers worked by the Broderick family of Swaledale were one of the highlights of the Fashion, Costume and Textiles Sale on 14th August at Tennants Auctioneers, after selling for £1,000 (all figures exclude buyer’s premium).

"The Broderick family owned two properties in Swaledale – Spring End and Summer Lodge, near the village of Gunnerside.

  A Circa 1865 Thomson’s Red Cage Crinoline – Sold for £700 

*(Image: Tennants Auctioneers)*

"The family had a significant impact on the dales from the 14th to 20th centuries through farming and mining activities, and their extensive collection of family papers are now held by Richmondshire Museum."

Other notable sales included a 19th-century embroidered wall hanging, which possibly originated in Russia, which sold for £1,600, and an early 19th-century patchwork bed cover with an appliqué heraldic crest to the centre incorporating the word ‘Industry’ sold for £1,300.

An early 20th-century Kalamkari block-printed silk shawl depicting a tree of life sold for £450.

Chinese textiles also performed well, with a late 19th or early 20th-century blue silk embroidered waistcoat selling for £850 and a group of 19th-century Chinese embroideries sold for £700.

In the costume section, a cream silk evening dress with a lamé train from the 1930s sold for £850.

Early 20th-century undergarments from a private collection drew strong interest, with four lots sold for a total hammer price of £2,150.

A Thomson’s red cage crinoline from around 1865 sold for £700.

The auction achieved a total hammer price of £40,030, with 91 per cent of the 181 lots sold.
