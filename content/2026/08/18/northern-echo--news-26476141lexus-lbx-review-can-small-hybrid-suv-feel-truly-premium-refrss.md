From the moment it was launched in the UK, the Lexus LBX was a small car with a big job.

The vehicle is designed to draw new buyers into the brand: people who might admire Lexus’s reputation for quality but previously viewed it as a marque reserved for larger, more expensive executive cars.

As the smallest Lexus yet, it does that by blending compact crossover practicality with an upmarket cabin and a very contemporary hybrid powertrain.

It is not a conventional mini-SUV in the mould of some of its more anonymous rivals.

  The Lexus LBX 

*(Image: Lexus)*

At 4.19 metres long, the LBX is compact enough for tight town centre car parks, yet its low, wide stance gives it a more planted and purposeful appearance.

I rather like the way the old Lexus spindle grille has been reworked into what the company calls its 'Resolute Look', helping the LBX establish a fresher identity without losing the family connection.

The result is a car that, in my eyes, looks more premium than its footprint suggests.

There are neatly sculpted body panels, pronounced wheelarches and a broad front end, but Lexus has avoided making the LBX overly aggressive.

It is smart rather than shouty, with enough visual interest to stand apart from the crowded compact-crossover market.

  The Lexus LBX 

*(Image: Lexus)*

  That feeling continues inside, where the cabin has to be among the best in class, if not *the* best, in my opinion.

Lexus has long been capable of producing cabins that feel a cut above the mainstream, and the LBX brings much of that attention to material quality and finish into a smaller package.

The dashboard wraps around the front occupants, while the driver-focused layout follows Lexus’s 'tazuna' principle: key information and controls are placed to require minimal movement of the driver’s hands and eyes.

The effect is a cockpit that feels considered rather than cluttered.

There is a light, open ambience up front and good outward visibility, both of which matter in a car likely to spend much of its time negotiating urban traffic.

I found the physical climate controls to be very user-friendly. The Takumi model adds acoustic front side windows and a crisp-sounding Mark Levinson audio system.

  The sound system 

*(Image: Lexus)*

Lexus also offers different 'Atmosphere' themes, allowing buyers to choose colour, trim and finish combinations to better suit their own tastes instead of simply climbing a predictable ladder of equipment grades.

It is perhaps not a car to buy for carrying a family of adults over long distances, however.

Rear-seat space is acceptable rather than generous, which is an inevitable compromise of the LBX’s compact dimensions.

Still, front-wheel-drive versions offer a useful 402-litre boot, while folding the rear seats expands capacity to 994 litres.

All-wheel-drive models sacrifice some luggage capacity, with 317 litres behind the rear seats.

Under the bonnet is a 1.5-litre, three-cylinder self-charging hybrid system.

It combines petrol and electric power without the need to plug in, recovering energy under braking and using the electric motor to support the engine in day-to-day driving.

  The Lexus LBX 

*(Image: Lexus)*

Total system output is 136hp, so it is not intended to be a hot hatch in crossover clothing, but it does feel responsive enough around town and on faster A-roads.

The key benefit is efficiency.

Front-wheel-drive versions officially return 61.4–62.7mpg on the WLTP combined cycle, with CO2 emissions from 102g/km. In real-world driving conditions, I found it to be very frugal.

Those figures make the LBX a potentially attractive alternative to diesel for drivers who cover mixed urban and motorway miles, especially those without the ability or inclination to charge a plug-in hybrid or EV.

  The engine 

*(Image: Lexus)*

Behind the wheel, it was clear to me that Lexus has paid particular attention to the way the LBX drives.

The GA-B platform has been strengthened and revised with a longer wheelbase and wider track, while suspension, steering and braking have been tuned to deliver the brand’s 'Lexus Driving Signature' of control, comfort and confidence.

It is a lofty ambition in this class, but the aim is clear: the LBX should feel less like a compromised entry-level model and more like a properly engineered premium car in miniature.

A new nickel-metal hydride battery is designed to be smaller, lighter and more responsive than the unit it replaces, helping the hybrid system react more promptly at lower speeds.

I found that the car’s electric assistance makes pulling away and moving through stop-start traffic smooth and quiet, while the combustion engine takes over more naturally as speeds rise.

That refined character is central to the LBX’s appeal.

Lexus has worked to reduce road noise and vibration, a worthwhile focus in a compact crossover where poor sound insulation could quickly undermine the feeling of quality.

The intention is that, even at this lower price point, the LBX retains the calmness and thoughtful hospitality associated with the larger Lexus range.

It may not be the most spacious compact SUV on sale, nor is it the quickest.

But that misses the point.

The LBX offers a distinctive alternative for buyers who want manageable dimensions, low-effort hybrid motoring and an interior that feels genuinely special.

It is a compact crossover that brings Lexus’s premium values into a more accessible part of the market — and does so without appearing to be a cut-price Lexus.

  **The lowdown**

  Price: £36,210 for Premium Plus Design; up to £39,460 for Takumi Design

  Engine: 1.5-litre three-cylinder petrol-electric self-charging hybrid

  Powertrain: Front-wheel drive or AWD-i all-wheel drive; e-CVT automatic

  Power: 136hp combined system output

  Engine power: 91hp

  Electric motor: 94hp / 185Nm

  Battery: 1.0kWh nickel-metal hydride

  0–62mph: 9.2 seconds (FWD); 9.6 seconds (AWD)

  Top speed: 106mph

  Fuel economy: 61.4–62.7mpg (FWD); 58.8mpg (AWD)

  CO2 emissions: 102–108g/km (FWD); 110–113g/km (AWD)

  Transmission: e-CVT automatic

  Warranty: Three years/60,000 miles, with Lexus Relax cover available up to 10 years/100,000 miles subject to annual servicing at a Lexus centre.
