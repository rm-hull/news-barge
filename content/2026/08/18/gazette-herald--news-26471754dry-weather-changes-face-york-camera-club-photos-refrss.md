York and the surrounding countryside are showing the effects of prolonged dry weather, with parched grass, low water levels and dusty paths transforming familiar views.

These photographs of everyday scenes by The Press Camera Club members show how the dry spell has taken its toll.

We've picked our favourite 11 photos for our gallery "Dry weather changes face of York - Camera Club photos".

  Gillian Evans captured this bird's eye view of York and its surroundings from a helicopter. 

*(Image: Gillian Evans)*

Castle Howard's usually luscious lawn, the grassy green seat of Clifford's Tower and the Museum Gardens grounds have turned yellow, while shrinking rivers, ponds and streams are also showing the strain.

  Regular visitors will remember how verdant this vista towards Castle Howard usually looks. Photo: Gillian Evans 

*(Image: Photo: Gillian Evans)*

  Beautiful blooms by St Mary's Abbey in the Museum Gardens - but the patchy grass is showing the effects of the prolonger dry spell. Photo: Lisa Young 

*(Image: Photo: Lisa Young)*

The weather has quickly reshaped the appearance of landmarks in York and its rural edges where farmers and food producers have struggled to get water on their crop.

  Steve Martin captured a farmer hard at work. 

*(Image: Steve Martin)*

  **Recommended reads:**

Parks, riverside walks and open spaces remain popular places to visit, but people are being urged to take care in the heat and to follow any local guidance on water use and fire risk.

  Garry Hornby captioned his photo: "Alpaca blending in nicely with the parched scorched field." 

*(Image: Garry Hornby)*
