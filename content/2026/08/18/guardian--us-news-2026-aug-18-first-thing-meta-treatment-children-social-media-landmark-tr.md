Good morning. More than half of the states in the US have joined together in an unprecedented lawsuit against Meta that goes to trial on Tuesday, accusing the parent company of Instagram and Facebook of deliberately designing addictive products that lured in young people and caused them harm.

The sweeping legal proceedings could have profound consequences for the company. The attorneys general say that if Meta is found liable, damages could be as high as $200bn – equivalent to the company’s 2025 annual revenue. The lawmakers are also asking that Meta be compelled to change the design of its products to make them safer for children, which may have longer-term effects than a fine.

- 
  **What is Meta accused of?** The 233-page lawsuit, first filed in October 2023, alleges that Meta regularly collects data on children under the age of 13 without parental permission. The lawmakers claim in court documents that Meta “refuses to abandon its use of known harmful features” and that its motives are based solely on profit.
- 
  **How has Meta responded?** Meta denies all allegations and says: “Rather than sticking to the facts or the law, the states have instead decided to chase an outlandish payout. The state AGs may call this a landmark case, but their limited claims are unsubstantiated and their financial demands are vastly disproportionate.”

## **Trump dismisses USS Lincoln criticism and snaps at CNN reporter to ‘be quiet’**

![Donald Trump in the Oval Office of the White House.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/b9bdaee0c357b5b66eed4b5de359a06701449b78/196_0_2560_2048/master/2560.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Donald Trump claimed he had third-hand reports that the USS Abraham Lincoln is “beautifully maintained” despite extensive accounts of deteriorating living conditions during its lengthy deployment in the strait of Hormuz.

The Navy Times and Stars and Stripes – the latter notably under increased control of the Pentagon as a defense publication – have largely confirmed reports of the Lincoln’s conditions. The publications described shortages of supplies and water and plumbing problems. Several sailors have attempted to leap from the ship according to their reports, prompting Democratic lawmakers to demand answers from the Pentagon.

At the White House the president berated CNN reporter Kristen Holmes insisting that she “be quiet” and describing the reporting as fake news. The White House also attacked Holmes in social media posts, calling her “callous and vindictive”.

- 
  **Why have conditions onboard the ship become contentious?** The Lincoln, a nuclear-powered aircraft carrier left port from San Diego on 21 November 2025 and has now set a modern record for the longest time at sea without a port call. It has been more than 250 days since it has touched land.
- 
  **What else is causing concern with US navy ships?** A US guided-missile destroyer reportedly spent four days adrift and without power in the South China Sea in July. The USS Benfold was reported to have been towed to Subic Bay in the Philippines.

## **South Korea president calls for military independence in wake of Trump call to cut joint drills**

![South Korea’s military carry out a drill.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/f761f1b172ac79ad2ceeca6e5704c67ba22ac690/649_0_3009_2409/master/3009.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

South Korea’s president has renewed his push to regain independent control of the military from the US in the event of war and urged faster progress in acquiring nuclear-powered submarines after Donald Trump’s order to cut joint drills.

Lee Jae Myung said on Tuesday that Seoul should proceed without disruption with the planned transfer, during his term, of wartime operational control of South Korean forces from the US – a goal he and the government have long held.

- 
  **Why does the US have operational control of South Korea’s forces?** The issue dates back to the Korean war, which ended without a formal peace treaty in 1953, meaning the two Koreas remain technically at war. The authority to direct South Korean forces if fighting resumes still rests with the US. Seoul regained peacetime control of its own military in 1994, but wartime authority has become a potent symbol of national sovereignty.

A New York state judge effectively postponed Luigi Mangione’s trial on murder and weapons charges over the 2024 killing of a health insurance company CEO, after Mangione pleaded guilty last week to separate federal charges.

The trial had previously been scheduled to begin on 8 September. Mangione has pleaded not guilty to the state charges. Mangione’s lawyers on Friday asked New York state justice Gregory Carro to dismiss the charges. They argued that Mangione should not have to be punished for the same conduct twice.
