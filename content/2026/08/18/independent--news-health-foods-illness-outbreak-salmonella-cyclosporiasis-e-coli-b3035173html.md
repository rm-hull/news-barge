Outbreaks of severe foodborne illnesses, causing symptoms including “explosive diarrhea,” have soared across the country this summer.

With health officials working to address continually growing outbreaks of the parasitic illness cyclosporiasis, Trump administration health officials have insisted that the U.S. food supply remains safe and that fresh produce is safe for consumers.

But the Food and Drug Administration is simultaneously responding to tens of thousands of cyclosporiasis cases, which can also cause sickness and nausea, as well as outbreaks of *E. coli* and salmonella, both caused by bacteria. 

Two deaths have been tied to outbreaks of the illness. None have been linked to *E. coli* or salmonella, according to public health officials. 

However, all of the illnesses, which are mainly caused by eating contaminated food, have forced recalls across the country.

## Brown and white eggs

![Cartons of eggs are seen at a supermarket in Houston, Texas, last month. Millions of eggs were recalled after Americans were sickened by the bacteria salmonella. Foodborne illnesses have soared this summer, forcing recalls of multiple foods](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/18/19/46/GettyImages-2289610085.jpeg)

Last month’s salmonella recall related to white and brown eggs from Midwest Poultry Services was designated a “class 1 recall” by the FDA.

Class 1 refers to the agency’s highest-risk designation meant to show “a situation in which there is a reasonable probability that the use of, or exposure to, a violative product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death.”

The eggs were sold in six states, including Texas, Arkansas, Louisiana, Oklahoma, Mississippi and New Mexico. The recalled eggs can be identified by a code number - P-1950 or 0840962 with a Julian Date between 157 and 184 - printed in date-coding ink on the left or right sides of the carton.

Texas was most impacted by the illness, with 73 of the 98 cases reported. There have been 26 hospitalizations tied to cases nationwide.

Salmonella causes about 1.35 million infections in the U.S. every year, with contaminated food as the source of most of these illnesses, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

Cases can lead to diarrhea, nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain, as well as more severe artery infections, arthritis and heart infections that can be fatal in children, the elderly and people with weakened immune systems, the FDA says.

Salmonella is treated with fluids and antibiotics, according to the Mayo Clinic.

## Jalapeños in appetizers and entrées

Eggs haven’t been the only salmonella threat this summer.

Earlier this month, Coast Citrus Distributors recalled jalapeños found in pico de gallo, stuffed mushrooms and soup sold at stores in 27 states.

Those states include Alabama, Arkansas, California, Colorado, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, North Carolina, North Dakota, Nebraska, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Virginia, Washington, Wisconsin and Wyoming, the FDA says.

Colorado and Minnesota have seen the majority of the 345 illnesses and 36 hospitalizations.

## Frozen blueberries at Publix

Last month, Publix announced a recall of GreenWise organic Frozen Whole Blueberries and Whole Mixed Berries after a dozen people were sickened in Florida and Georgia.

There have been four hospitalizations and the products were distributed in eight states, also including Alabama, Kentucky, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Virginia.

*E. coli* are bacteria found in many places like the intestines of people and animals. While most are harmless, some can make you sick, the CDC says.

This kind of E. coli produces powerful toxins known as Shiga, which can lead to kidney failure and death.

Symptoms begin between 3-4 days after consuming contaminated food or up to nine days later, including severe stomach cramps, diarrhea and vomiting.

This form of *E. coli* is treated with fluids, as well.

## Iceberg lettuce at Taco Bell

Iceberg lettuce from a Mexican Taylor Farms facility and served at multiple Taco Bell fast-food restaurants is at the heart of the nation’s largest cyclosporiasis outbreak.

The lettuce has been recalled in 27 states, including Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Iowa, Illinois, Indiana, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Maryland, Michigan, Missouri, Mississippi, North Carolina, New Hampshire, New Jersey, Ohio, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia and Wisconsin.

There have been nearly 25,000 cases of the disease, per the CDC, with close to 14,000 in Michigan alone.

Infections can cause symptoms including watery diarrhea, frequent bowel movements, stomach cramps, nausea and fatigue, with symptoms sometimes lasting for weeks.

Symptoms may not start for two weeks after eating food or drinking water contaminated with the parasite.

Treatment typically includes antibiotics.
