## Summer evenings bring people back into the streets of Samarkand, Bukhara, Khiva and Tashkent, where restaurants, parks and historic squares remain busy long after sunset.

For centuries, Silk Road cities welcomed merchants, pilgrims and travellers. While their landmarks continue to attract visitors during the day, the atmosphere changes noticeably after sunset. As temperatures cool, parks, pedestrian streets and public squares fill with people enjoying the evening outdoors. Restaurants stay open later, cultural venues remain active and families make the most of the cooler hours, particularly during the summer months when daytime temperatures are at their highest.

## Summer evenings in Samarkand

Samarkand is often associated with monuments such as Registan Square, the Gur-e-Amir Mausoleum and Shah-i-Zinda. After dark, these landmarks remain active as residents and visitors continue walking through the city's historic centre under architectural lighting introduced in recent years. Situated around 740 metres above sea level, Samarkand benefits from cooler evening temperatures during the summer. The combination of fresh air and architectural lighting has gradually made evening visits part of the city's daily routine. Historic monuments remain the main attraction, while nearby parks, cafés and pedestrian areas also become gathering places after work. Local guide Khurshid Narimov says these conditions make evening walks part of everyday life.

“During the day, summer temperatures can be difficult, so people prefer going out in the evening. Families come here to walk, children play around the illuminated monuments, and visitors can continue exploring long after sunset.” Narimov believes lighting has encouraged people to spend more time around historic sites while extending visits beyond daylight hours. He also recommends exploring “Boqiy Shahar” (Eternal City), the Imam al-Bukhari Memorial Complex and traditional craft workshops, many of which remain open in the evening. Australian tourist Jamie Mikic says one of the strongest impressions of Samarkand came from watching local families gather outdoors after dark.

“I love seeing children playing, families walking together and people enjoying the parks. It creates a welcoming atmosphere and gives visitors the opportunity to experience everyday life while exploring the city.”

## Bukhara after dark

Several hours west of Samarkand, Bukhara follows a similar rhythm. As the temperature drops, the historic centre becomes busy again with people walking through narrow streets, visiting cafes and gathering around Lyabi-Hauz, Poi Kalyan and the Ark Fortress. According to tour guide Dildora Safarova, the old city takes on a different atmosphere after sunset.

“During the day you see the natural colour of the brick architecture. At night, the monuments are illuminated and the streets become livelier. Musicians perform, dancers invite visitors to join them, and people stay outdoors until late.” Restaurants overlooking the old city have become popular evening destinations. Rooftop terraces offer panoramic views of illuminated monuments, while serving traditional green tea, Uzbek cuisine and contemporary dishes to residents and international visitors. Many venues combine local food with live music and cultural performances that continue into the evening. The compact layout of the old city also allows visitors to explore many of Bukhara's best-known landmarks on foot after dinner. Safarova says evening is also when many artisan workshops, embroidery shops and souvenir stores remain open, giving visitors the opportunity to observe traditional crafts and speak directly with local artisans.

## Khiva in evening light

Further west, Khiva offers a different experience. Much of the city's evening life is concentrated within the walls of Ichan-Kala, where monuments, museums and narrow streets are located within a relatively small area. The quieter setting encourages visitors to explore the old city on foot. Tour guide Anaposhsha Boltaeva says many people choose to visit Ichan-Kala in the evening because the weather becomes more pleasant.

“The museums stay open until the evening, and people enjoy walking through Ichan-Kala when it is cooler. Many visitors like photographing the monuments at night because the lighting highlights details that look different from daytime.” She adds that evenings are equally popular with local families, who spend time in nearby parks, recreation areas and the recently developed Arda Khiva complex before returning to the historic centre for a walk.

## Evening life in Tashkent

The final stop is Tashkent, where evening activity extends well beyond the historic centre. As Uzbekistan's largest city, the capital offers a broader range of evening activities. Parks, shopping districts, cafés, open-air terraces and pedestrian streets remain busy well into the night, particularly during weekends and the summer. Serbian tour guide Stefan Pavlović says the city's evening atmosphere surprised him.

“Many people don't expect this when they first think about Uzbekistan. After sunset the parks are full, people are walking, meeting friends and enjoying the cooler weather. There is a very relaxed atmosphere.”

Even at 11pm, cafes continue serving customers while families, young people and visitors remain outdoors. Walking has become one of the most popular ways to spend the evening, supported by extensive public spaces and comfortable night-time temperatures. Each destination offers a different evening experience. Samarkand and Khiva invite visitors to explore illuminated historic centres, Bukhara combines architecture with music and dining, while Tashkent's parks and cafés reflect the pace of a modern capital. As temperatures fall, spending time outdoors becomes part of daily life, making the evening one of the busiest times of day for residents and visitors alike.
