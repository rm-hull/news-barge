Volunteers from the Yorkshire Dales National Park have helped spruce up the surroundings of one of Britain's rare surviving AA roadside call boxes, preserving a distinctive reminder of the early days of motoring.

The Grade II listed AA Box 442, on the A684 between Swinithwaite and Aysgarth, dates back to about 1956.

It is one of the few remaining in Britain and serves as a reminder of a time when motorists depended on roadside telephones for help.

  Grade II listed AA Box 442 near Aysgarth before improvements 

*(Image: Supplied)*

David Noland, member champion for cultural heritage at the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority, said: "This is a small but much-loved feature of the Dales landscape and an important part of the country’s motoring heritage.

"It’s one of those places where, if local people and volunteers don't lend a hand, the site could easily deteriorate, but our volunteers have stepped up and made a real difference, helping ensure this unique landmark can continue to be enjoyed by residents and visitors alike."

Volunteers carried out repairs to drystone walls, cleared ivy that was encroaching on the box and maintained the retaining walls around the surrounding flower beds.

  Volunteers working on the walling 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The AA maintains the call box itself.

Sheila Simms, a nearby resident who has looked after the box and its surroundings for many years on behalf of the AA, joined the volunteers during the clean-up.

The AA Heritage Team supported the project with a £400 contribution toward the work.

Duncan Webb, a spokesperson for the AA Heritage Team, said: "We are grateful to the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority and its volunteers for helping care for the surroundings of AA Box 442.

"These historic call boxes represent an important chapter in the history of motoring in Britain, and we are pleased to support work that helps preserve them for future generations."

It stands as a piece of motoring history from the days before mobile phones.
