A new blue plaque has been unveiled to honour the legacy of a once-thriving industrial landmark.

The Glassworks Chimney in South Shields, a prominent remnant of the town’s glassmaking past, is now officially recognised for its historical importance as part of the South Tyneside blue plaque scheme.

The plaque, which recognises individuals, structures and buildings that have made a significant contribution to South Tyneside's culture and heritage, was unveiled by the mayor and mayoress of South Tyneside, councillor Stan Wildhirt and his wife Dawn, during a celebration at The Word in South Shields and later installed on the chimney.

   

*(Image: South Tyneside Council)*

Councillor Wildhirt said: "The Glassworks at Mill Dam played a significant role in the growth and development of our town.

"The surviving chimney remains one of the most visible symbols of that legacy and of the hard work, innovation and enterprise that helped shape our community.

"By commemorating the Glassworks Chimney with a blue plaque, we help preserve the memory of an industry that supported local families, contributed to the town's prosperity, and played a significant role in South Shields' industrial past."

The Cookson Glassworks, established in the 18th century, was once a major glass manufacturing site and a key part of South Shields’ economy.

   

*(Image: South Tyneside Council)*

By the early 19th century, it was considered one of the largest glass producers in the country.

The site provided employment and prosperity for generations of residents until the late 19th century, when it was acquired by the Harton Coal Company.

Later, it was demolished to make way for new industrial developments.

All that remains today is the chimney, dated 1865, which stands as a symbol of the town’s industrial heritage and the skilled workforce that once powered it.

Its survival is thanks to workers in 1919 who stopped its demolition after recognising its historic value.

   

*(Image: South Tyneside Council)*

Val Parks of the South Shields local history group, who nominated the site for the blue plaque, said: "The history of glassmaking in South Shields is a fascinating story of innovation, craftsmanship and industry."

They added: "From the pioneering work of the Cookson family to the international success of local manufacturers, the chimney is a powerful symbol of a chapter of our history that deserves to be remembered and celebrated.

"This Blue Plaque is a fitting tribute to both our glassmaking heritage and the people who helped ensure its story lives on for future generations."

The South Tyneside blue plaque scheme recognises individuals, structures and buildings that have made a significant contribution to South Tyneside's culture and heritage.

Councillor Wildhirt said: "Blue Plaques play an important role in helping us recognise the people, places and events that have shaped our history, ensuring their stories continue to be told for future generations."
