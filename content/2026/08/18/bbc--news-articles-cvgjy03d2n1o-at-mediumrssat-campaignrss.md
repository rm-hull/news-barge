![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mail reads: "Who was the White House hoaxer who duped PM?"](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/810/cpsprodpb/367d/live/2d455a80-9a7f-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

There's a mix of stories across Tuesday's front pages. "Who was the White House hoaxer who duped PM?" asks the Daily Mail as it reports that Andy Burnham exchanged messages with a person posing as the chief of staff to US President Donald. The paper says an international hunt is under way for the hoaxer responsible for the security breach during which Burnham thought he was sending messages to Susie Wiles "before becoming suspicious the contact was illegitimate". Downing Street has declined to comment on the incident.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Star reads: "Andy's faked!"](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/885d/live/40216310-9a7f-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

"Andy's faked!" is the Daily Star's headline, advising the PM to "just switch off yer mobile!"

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph reads: "Rayner paves way for more traveller sites.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1040/cpsprodpb/dbca/live/7c6c5850-9a81-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

In more news involving the PM, the Daily Telegraph says Burnham is expected to intervene to prevent water companies from raising prices during droughts. It comes after news that regulator Ofwat planned to allow water firms to include "surge pricing" into bills from April during periods of water scarcity.

![The headline on the front page of the Guardian reads: "Tice call to stop fighting climate crisis sparks fury."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/782/cpsprodpb/1a59/live/f97e9570-9a80-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

The Guardian shifts its focus to Reform UK's deputy leader, Richard Tice, who is facing a backlash after he urged people worrying about climate change to enjoy the warmth of this year's record-breaking heatwaves. Elsewhere, the paper's picture slot shows huge crowds gathered in London to pay tribute to former Cambridge professor Jason Arday who was found dead last week.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror reads: "Wine buffoon."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/d12a/live/3ab79570-9a7f-11f1-b792-a710e56a11dc.jpg)

The Daily Mirror calls Tice a "wine buffoon" in its headline, referencing his comments on the deadly heatwaves being good for English wine production. Speaking to PA, the Reform deputy leader said: "Of course climate change is real, but where is the evidence that net zero is going to stop it? Where is the evidence? There isn't any." The paper also features former Man City manager Pep Guardiola "agony" as he opens up about the strain of the job on his marriage to Cristina Serra.

![The headline on the front page of the Sun reads: "Pep's divorce rant at players."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/7fee/live/0b857d40-9a8d-11f1-b792-a710e56a11dc.jpg)

The Sun follows with more details of Guardiola's reported talk to his team about his marital problems in a new documentary that "lifts lid on his agony".

![The headline on the front page of the Times reads: "Children left 'fixated with violence' by social media."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/809/cpsprodpb/e2fb/live/fd86fe50-9a80-11f1-b792-a710e56a11dc.jpg)

The children's commissioner for England is warning that kids in the UK are "becoming intoxicated by extremism" on social media, the Times reports. Dame Rachel de Souza has called on the government to introduce restrictions on social media use for under-18s, saying safety experts were "deeply concerned" children are being "drawn into radicalisation and violence fascination online". The paper also pays tribute to actress Hayden Panettiere who has died aged 36. She was best known for her roles in popular TV series including Heroes and Nashville.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Express reads: "Smugglers use Britain as 'transit hub' for migrants."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/0201/live/1b58bb00-9a7f-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

Fears that migrant camps could grow on the UK's south coast is the Daily Express' lead story. The paper says people smugglers are using the UK as a "transit hub" for migrants hoping to reach other European countries.

![The headline on the front page of the Financial Times reads: "Ring leader Kim 'respect' disarms US."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1056/cpsprodpb/4149/live/26ea00f0-9a7f-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

North Korea leader Kim Jong Un and his daughter Kim Ju Ae are pictured on the Financial Times' front page attending a circus performance celebrating the 81st anniversary of Korea's liberation from Japanese rule. The paper's headline references Kim's "respect" for the US after Donald Trump ordered the scaling back of military exercises with South Korea, citing a "very good relationship" with the North Korean leader.

![The headline on the front page of the Metro reads: "That doesn't sound much like peace, Mr President!"](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/828/cpsprodpb/c964/live/33e2ed30-9a7f-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

Trump also takes the top story in the Metro. The paper covers the US president's threat to bomb Oman, an American ally, as the deadline to resolve the Iran war looms.

![The headline on the front page of the Independent reads: "Trump: I'll bomb Oman if it blocks our Hormuz plan."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1536/cpsprodpb/c866/live/9fd97190-9a8d-11f1-b792-a710e56a11dc.jpg)

"Trump: I'll bomb Oman if it blocks our Hormuz plan" echoes the Independent.

![The headline on the front page of the i Paper reads: "Neighbours told: don't phone 999 to report next door's barbecue."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/831/cpsprodpb/d084/live/a54df5f0-9a7f-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

Finally, the i Paper says fire chiefs are warning the public not to use 999 to report neighbours hold barbecues or bonfires after the government's use of the national alert system to millions of mobiles last Friday.
