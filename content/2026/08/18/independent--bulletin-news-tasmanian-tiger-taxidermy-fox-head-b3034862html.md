# Stuffed ‘fox head’ turns out to be extinct Tasmanian tiger worth £50k

![The taxidermy head was a rare example of a Tasmanian tiger, which went extinct in 1936](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/18/7/49/thylacine_closeup2.jpeg)

- A taxidermy head bought for £50 at an antiques fair has sold for £50,000 at auction after it was discovered to be a rare Tasmanian tiger specimen.
- The seller originally purchased the item at a West Country market as a novelty because it had googly eyes attached to it.
- Buyer James Cranfield paid £50,000 plus £10,000 in fees after competing against another bidder, who also realised the item was not a fox head.
- The Tasmanian tiger, or thylacine, was a marsupial unique to Australia and New Guinea that was hunted to extinction, with the last known living specimen dying in 1936.
- Auctioneers described the discovery as unbelievably rare, noting that complete taxidermy heads of the species are virtually non-existent outside major national museums.
