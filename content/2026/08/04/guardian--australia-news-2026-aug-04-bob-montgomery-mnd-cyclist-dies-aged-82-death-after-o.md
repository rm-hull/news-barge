Bob Montgomery, a charity cyclist who has just finished a 5,200km bike ride from Broome to Bowral, died on Monday at the age of 82.

His family confirmed his death on social media, saying he had died unexpectedly just a day after completing the cycling feat alongside his grandson, Tom, as a fundraiser for motor neurone disease.

Guardian Australia understands he died in a single-vehicle crash in Sydney’s southern suburb of Arncliffe about 7pm after a car crashed into the side of the M5 East tunnel.

Emergency services treated him at the scene before he was taken to St George hospital in a critical condition, where he later died.

A 79-year-old passenger was also treated at the scene before being taken to hospital in a stable condition.

In the post, his family said it was Montgomery’s sixth interstate fundraising ride in support of Motor Neurone Disease (MND) NSW, “raising vital funds and awareness for a cause that was deeply personal to him”.

“Fittingly, and now poignantly, the ride was named One Last Ride,” it continued. “At 82 years of age, Bob was still passionately sharing his message only hours before his passing.”

According to the MND NSW website, his epic ride raised $104,965 in donations.

MND NSW shared a tribute, saying it was “deeply saddened” by the sudden passing, calling Montgomery a “cherished friend and tireless supporter of MND NSW”.

“For many years, Bob dedicated himself to raising vital funds and awareness for people living with motor neurone disease,” the group’s CEO, Liam O’Meara, said in a statement.
