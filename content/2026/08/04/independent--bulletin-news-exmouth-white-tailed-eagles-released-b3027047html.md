Six white-tailed eagles, known for their impressive 8ft wingspan, have been released into Exmoor National Park, marking their return to the area for the first time in over 200 years.

The reintroduction is part of an ambitious conservation initiative by Forestry England and the Roy Dennis Wildlife Foundation, aiming to bolster the species' population and extend its breeding range.

This follows successful releases from the Isle of Wight since 2019, which led to the birth of the first wild chick in southern England in 2023, a testament to the project's early success.

Exmoor was chosen as an additional release location due to its historical significance as a breeding ground, ideal foraging conditions, and previous visits by eagles from the Isle of Wight.

The birds were sourced from Scotland, and up to 20 more young eagles are planned for release from Exmoor over the next three years, a move welcomed by Environment Secretary Angela Eagle.
