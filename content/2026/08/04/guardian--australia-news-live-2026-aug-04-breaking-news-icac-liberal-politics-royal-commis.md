St Kilda could lose 20-40% of its bird population to bird flu

Achol Arok

Ecologist Lana Austin, from conservation group Earthcare St Kilda, said bird flu was likely to arrive at a little penguin colony in Melbourne’s bayside area, which is home to about 1,400 of the birds.

Speaking to ABC Radio Melbourne on Tuesday, Austin said she was “pretty worried because I don’t want to lose one bird”, and that efforts to protect the colony would require “a whole ecosystem response”. She said:

We need to be concerned at St Kilda, not just for penguins, but for all the other wildlife that hang out in Port Phillip Bay generally.

So, we have dolphins, seals, rakali, which is a native water rat. It’s very likely that they will also be affected.

Austin said it was unclear how bad the impact of the virus would be on the colony, due to a lack of data for little penguins, and that authorities had “probably missed the boat” on vaccination efforts.

Arthur Rylah [Institute] did a great risk assessment early last year where they looked into vaccination possibility … It did have some good effects and birds had some resilience and immunity to the disease. However, it had to be introduced before bird flu arrived.

We have missed the vaccination boat and it’s more just about managing it once it arrives.

Senior NSW Liberal parliamentarian employed Reformers and supported them financially, inquiry hears

Senior NSW upper house Liberal Damien Tudehope has been giving evidence to the NSW Icac’s Operation Rosny.

Tudehope has described how Reformers Jean-Claude Perrottet and Christian Ellis worked as staffers in his ministerial office. He says Jean-Claude, who previously worked in his electorate office, had a “real talent” for the annual recruitment and renewal of Liberal party members, which he says is important for parliamentarians looking to keep hold of their seats.

But he said there was an incident in which his chief of staff “blew up” about staffers, who may have included Ellis and Jean-Claude Perrottet, who had spent ministerial working hours on recruitment, against the code of conduct for staffers.

He has admitted to speaking at Reformers recruitment events, defending himself as a “warrior for social issues for the party”. After being shown evidence of a bank transfer and a message in which Jean-Claude Perrottet wrote that “Damien” had paid for a venue for an event, he has conceded he may have made payments to support the Reformers work.

Tudehope has not been accused of any wrongdoing.

Victorian premier says AI portfolio will focus on ‘guardrails’ and reform

Ben Carroll held a press conference earlier after his major cabinet overhaul. He says his new AI portfolio – held by Anthony Carbines – will be working on “guardrails and regulatory reform” in the sector.

He went on:

Artificial intelligence is the biggest transformation going through society and the economy right now. My government will be focused on workers. Artificial intelligence is a great opportunity, but it also needs guardrails and regulatory reform. So I want a voice at the cabinet table on that.

Record number of complaints about financial products

Patrick Commins

Australians lodged nearly 120,000 complaints with the Australian Financial Complaints Authority in the most recent financial year, a record number that reflects the intense economic pressures being felt across the community.

That was about 20% more than the previous financial year, according to the preliminary numbers.

Personal transaction accounts, car insurance and credit cards were the most complained about products in 2025-26, AFCA said.

Complaints relating to the way banks dealt with customers in financial difficulty jumped by 17% in the year, and credit reporting complaints by 22%, AFCA said.

But AFCA said the largest growth was driven by major investment failures and rising superannuation disputes, most notably the collapses of the Shield Master Fund and First Guardian.

Complaints alleging failure to act in the client’s best interest remain the top issue with a 65 per cent increase on last year, up to 2081.

While most financial advice firms do the right thing, we are seeing a significant number of complaints stem from major financial collapses that have affected thousands of consumers. These matters are complex and can be incredibly stressful for individuals and their families.

Corruption inquiry hears about request for $50k to branch stack against federal member

Retired businessman Frits Maré has been telling the NSW Independent Commission Against Corruption (Icac) about a 2020 meeting in which Reformers Jean-Claude Perrottet and Christian Ellis asked him to contribute $50,000 to an operation to unseat the Liberal member for Mitchell, Alex Hawke.

On Monday, Reformer Robert Assaf told the commission he was concerned that the Perrottets, including former premier Dominic Perrottet and brothers Jean-Claude and Charles Perrottet, had a “vendetta” against Hawke, who had “backstabbed Dom” and become a “kingmaker” in the party.

Neither Hawke nor Dominic Perrottet is accused of any wrongdoing. Jean-Claude and Charles Perrottet have previously denied any wrongdoing.

Maré, a Liberal party member who is not named in any allegations and is not accused of any wrongdoing, described meeting Jean-Claude Perrottet at Young Liberals fundraiser in 2019. After repeated attempts by Jean-Claude Perrottet to set up a meeting, Maré met with him and Ellis at Maré’s office in May 2020.

He says the men asked him for $50,000 to fund a call centre to sign up new members at the party, so that they could “get enough people to challenge Alex Hawke in his seat and then vote him out”.

“I found it strange,” says Maré. “I thought the sitting member was doing a good job in parliament.”

Maré said he asked the men if they had budgeted how they would spend the $50,000. He says Ellis said “he hadn’t done a budget, but he’d go and do one”, but Maré declined their request for a contribution. “For the record, I never paid the money. I never caused it to be paid by anyone else or by a corporation,” he says.

Tabcorp and Sportbet are being asked to explain their offerings to so-called VIP customers, under some intense questioning from Greens senator Sarah Hanson-Young.

Tabcorp’s Julian Whealing says his company has 800,000 active customers, of which about 400 are classified as VIPs.

Whealing says he does not know exactly how many account managers are employed by the company but estimates it is around 20 people. He stresses they do not receive incentive payments or bonuses based on recruitment of customers or on customer spending.

Jules Norton Selzer, director of corporate affairs for Sportsbet, says his firm has about the same number of account managers.

Hanson-Young asks Norton Selzer if account managers ever ask customers if they want drugs.

“I don’t accept that characterisation,” he says. “We’re a legal, licensed, regulated product.”

He says Sportsbet adheres to dozens of regulatory requirements across federal and state laws.

The inquiry was told yesterday that VIP account managers for unnamed gambling companies had provided drugs, alcohol and access to sex workers to customers, to try to keep them betting. No companies were specifically identified in Monday’s evidence.
