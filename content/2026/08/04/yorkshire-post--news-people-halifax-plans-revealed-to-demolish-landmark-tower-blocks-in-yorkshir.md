# Halifax: Plans revealed to demolish landmark tower blocks in Yorkshire

## A social housing group has announced plans to demolish a pair of landmark tower blocks in Halifax.

Together Housing has written to former residents of St James Court and Albion Court in Halifax to inform them of its decision to apply for permission for the demolition of the two high-rise blocks.

Built in the 1950s, the buildings have served the people of Halifax for many decades says Together – but the group adds changing building safety requirements, introduced following the Grenfell Tower tragedy, mean that substantial investment would now be required to bring the blocks up to the standards expected for the future.

Over the last year, Together Housing says it has worked closely with residents to help them move into new homes while detailed surveys and assessments were carried out.

![Albion Court, Halifax.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjQ4ODNjOGQ5LTQ4NGYtNDZjMi1iNzAyLTkzZWUzNTU0ZTc1OTozZTJmZWJiNC0xZGYyLTQ5OWEtYjM4MC04Zjc1NTgzZmE4YmE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



All residents have now successfully relocated, with many choosing to remain in central Halifax and within their local communities, says Together.

The group argues surveys confirmed that the scale of work required to the buildings would be “significant” and would not represent the best long-term investment in providing safe, modern and comfortable homes.

“As a result, Together Housing has taken the decision to progress a demolition application and will confirm time scales once the planning process is under way,” said a spokesperson.

“We recognise that the currently vacant buildings are a prominent feature of the Halifax skyline and that local people are keen to understand what comes next.

“Together Housing remains committed to investing in Halifax and ensuring high-quality housing is available for local residents.

“The future of the site forms an important part of wider regeneration ambitions for the town.

“As plans develop, we will continue to work with partners and stakeholders to explore opportunities for new housing that meets the needs of current and future residents,” they said.

While St James and Albion Court presented unique challenges due to their age, design and investment requirements, Together Housing say the group remains “committed to maintaining, improving and modernising homes across Halifax and throughout the areas we serve.”

Patrick Berry, Executive Director of Property for Together, said: “This decision follows extensive surveys and careful consideration of the long-term future of the buildings.

“Our priority has always been the safety and well-being of residents, and we are delighted that everyone has successfully moved into new homes.

“We remain committed to investing in Halifax and creating high-quality homes that will serve local people for generations to come.”
