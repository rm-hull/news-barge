Rail passengers who are turning 18 will soon benefit from extended discounted travel, the Department for Transport (DfT) has announced.

From August 17, changes to the 16-17 Saver railcard will allow holders to continue receiving half-price train fares until the card expires, regardless of when they turn 18.

Previously, the railcard expired on a passenger’s 18th birthday.

DfT said the change will particularly help students, noting that more than 70,000 students each year lose out on an average of £175 due to the existing policy.


The new policy brings the 16-17 Saver, which costs £35 per year and gives a 50% discount on most train fares, into line with other railcards.

## What railcards are available for passengers across the UK?

National Rail has shared the railcards that you can buy to reduce your rail fares and how much they cost for a year and for three years, where applicable.

- 16-17 years old - £35 for one year
- 16-25 - £35 for one year or £80 for three years
- 26-30 - £35 for one year
- Disabled Persons Railcard - £20 per year or £54 for three years
- Family & Friends Railcard - £35 for one year or £80 for three years
- Network Railcard - £35 for one year
- Senior Railcard - £35 for one year or £80 for three years
- Two Together Railcard - £35 for one year
- Veterans Railcard - £35 for one year or £80 for three years

Rail minister Lord Hendy claimed this “common sense change” is “exactly what passengers should expect from the railway”.
