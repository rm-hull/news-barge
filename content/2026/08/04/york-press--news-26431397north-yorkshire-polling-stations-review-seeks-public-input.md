A review is underway to ensure that polling arrangements remain fit for purpose.

North Yorkshire Council has launched a consultation on polling districts and polling stations to ensure they remain accessible and fit for purpose ahead of the next full council elections in May 2027.

The review follows recent changes to parish ward boundaries and a Community Governance Review.

Councillor Heather Phillips, executive member for corporate services, said: "It’s vital that we make it as easy as possible for people to vote and ensure that everyone has an equal opportunity to participate in local democracy.

"Following recent changes to electoral and parish boundaries, we want to make sure that our polling districts and polling stations are suitable ahead of future elections and that they continue to provide good accessibility for voters.

"We would encourage residents to take part in the consultation, particularly anyone who may experience difficulties travelling to or accessing their polling station, as their feedback will help inform our final recommendations."

Around 800 polling districts and 500 polling stations across North Yorkshire will be considered as part of the review.

Between 50 and 100 stations could be affected by proposed changes.

The council is seeking input from residents—especially those who face barriers accessing their polling stations—as well as community groups, elected officials, and partner organisations.

The consultation will run for 10 weeks and will close on Monday, October 12.

Submissions can be made through the council's website at northyorks.gov.uk/PollingPlacesReview.

Paper copies are also available on request at council offices.
