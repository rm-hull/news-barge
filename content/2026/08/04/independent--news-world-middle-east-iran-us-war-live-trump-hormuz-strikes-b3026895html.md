Donald Trump has accused Iran of being “unbelievably duplicitous” and says Tehran has a “last chance” to agree a peace deal, after the Iranian side denied any discussions were taking place.

The US president said Tehran had “begged” for negotiations and whether they admitted it or not, discussions were taking place to reach “a solution” to the current stand-off.“This is a last chance for them to sign a good document,” he said. “[The talks] are going on, they're going on right now.”Trump claimed that the Strait of Hormuz could be open “literally by tomorrow”. “The first phase is the opening of the straits. The second phase will be the denuclearisation,” he said. “That’ll take a little while, but … we’re very strong on it.”

Iran flatly denied any plan for negotiators to meet with their US counterparts on Monday in the latest blow to Trump’s hopes of an imminent end to the war.

Iran’s foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said that Tehran is not currently holding any talks with Washington, and is rather in discussions with Oman about securing a temporary safe route through the Strait of Hormuz.

## US military asks for 'unconventional ideas’ to punish Iran

The US military has been crowdsourcing ideas for “creative and unconventional” ways to “punish” Iran, according to a report, after both sides reached an impasse over how to end the unpopular five-month war.

A senior officer at the US Central Command’s intelligence branch is said to have written to military analysts last Wednesday asking for new ideas to “pressure and punish Iran”, according to CNN, citing two sources with knowledge of the matter.

## Trump says Tehran has ‘last chance’ to make deal

Donald Trump has insisted that talks with Tehran are happening after the Iranian side denied any discussions were taking place.

Speaking from the Oval Office, the US president told reporters that it was Iran’s last chance to make a deal.

“This is a last chance for them to sign a good document,” he said.

“[The talks] are going on, they're going on right now. “For some reason, when they're talking, they don't like saying that they're talking.

“So I say, ‘We're in talks’. Big deal, we're in talks. And they'll sometimes deny it, even though they spend hours and hours together talking.”

He said the Iranian side had asked for talks after the US had planned the biggest attack since the Second World War.

## Syrian and Iraqi leaders discuss security concerns

Syrian interim president Ahmad al-Sharaa and Iraq's Kurdish regional president met yesterday in Damascus to discuss ways of improving mutual cooperation and contributing to regional security.

The meeting between al-Sharaa and Nechirvan Barzani came amid regional tensions as Iranian-backed Iraqi militia groups carried out multiple drone strikes targeting American interests across Iraq, including the country's Kurdish region.

Syrian state media reported last month that authorities at the country's border with Iraq thwarted an attempt in June to smuggle weapons believed to have been headed to the Iranian-backed Hezbollah militant group in Lebanon. Hezbollah denied the charges.

## Oil prices rise to $84 a barrel

Oil prices rebounded slightly this morning on concerns that Middle Eastern supply remains at risk as a diplomatic resolution to the US-Iran war that has disrupted shipments still seems unlikely.

Front-month Brent futures rose $0.62, or 0.7 per cent, to $84.39 a barrel as of 0055 GMT after dropping 7 per cent in the previous session to a three-week low.

US West Texas Intermediate crude was $0.61, or 0.7 per cent higher at $80.95 after falling over 5 per cent in the previous session to its lowest in nearly a week.

## Trump’s Iran desperation is revealed in crowd-sourcing memo seeking ‘creative’ ways to punish the enemy: report

The US military has asked combat experts to think outside the box in a desperate bid for new ideas to end President Donald Trump's war against Iran, according to a report Monday.

In an email last week, an officer in the intelligence branch of US Central Command asked a broad group of military analysts for suggestions, CNN said, citing an unidentified source.

"We are looking for new creative and unconventional ways to pressure and punish Iran,” the email reportedly read.

Military officials said the effort to crowdsource a solution to the war by email was highly unusual, CNN reported.

The move came as Trump is considering dramatic, "fireworks"-style airstrikes against Iran that could hit the same or similar sites that the US bombed last year when targeting the nation’s suspected nuclear weapons program, a source told CNN.
