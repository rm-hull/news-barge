Trump declines to call for Max Miller's resignation amid abuse allegations

Trump stopped short of calling on Rep Max Miller to resign Monday, saying he would “let the families figure that out”.

Asked whether the Ohio Republican should step down following allegations of domestic abuse from his ex-wife, Emily Moreno, Trump said he has always known Miller as “a good person” and called the situation “a very sad thing.”

The abuse allegations, which include physical abuse of Moreno and their two-year-old daughter, alleging in police reports that he threw her against a wall, previously held a gun to her head, and threw hot water on her. Miller denies all the claims and is suing his ex-wife for defamation.

The accusations have drawn calls for resignation from Emily Moreno’s father, Republican senator Bernie Moreno of Ohio, as well as several Democratic lawmakers, amid a bitter custody dispute over the couple’s young daughter.

Trump said the matter comes down to “accusations” that the families involved should resolve themselves.

Trump administration halts border wall well drilling in New Mexico amid ranchers’ concerns

Construction crews building a border wall in New Mexico were ordered to stop drilling new wells after ranchers said that pumping groundwater to produce concrete could threaten their livestock amid a severe drought.

At a public meeting in July, ranchers said they were noticing unmonitored tapping of border aquifers that threatened the water for those nearby.

“Whatever they do on that well affects everybody in this basin,” one rancher said in the meeting, adding that, from his conversation with border patrol, he believed that “they really don’t even understand what an aquifer is.”

A spokesperson for Customs and Border Protection told the AP on Monday that border wall contractors in New Mexico have been instructed to stop drilling new wells.

“CBP is committed to working collaboratively with local landowners to ensure that construction activities do not adversely impact their water needs,” said CBP in a statement.

Previously, CBP, which oversees the border wall construction, has said water is needed to produce concrete, build roads, construct barriers and control dust at construction sites. New Mexico’s Office of the State Engineer told NPR last month that it counted at least six wells under development along the border, and that none had the necessary permits required by state law.

US House ethics panel recommends censuring North Carolina’s Chuck Edwards

Chris Stein

The US House of Representatives ethics committee on Monday recommended that Chuck Edwards, a Republican congressman, face censure after determining he “fostered a hostile work environment and engaged in sexual harassment” by giving gifts and lavishing attention on two young female staffers.

The bipartisan panel began investigating the second-term North Carolina congressman earlier this year, after receiving complaints about his conduct. In their report, they determined Edwards broke House rules by singling out the two unidentified aides for special treatment that made them uncomfortable and harmed relations with other employees.

While the investigation uncovered no evidence that Edwards engaged in sexual activity with the staffers or suggested it, his behavior violated provisions in the House code of conduct that prohibit sexual harassment and unwanted advances. The congressman “acknowledged at least some of the behavior at issue could be viewed as inappropriate”, but “has not acknowledged or taken responsibility for the harm he caused”, the committee wrote in its report.

A compromise between Todd Blanche and two Republican senators that potentially cleared the way for Blanche’s confirmation as attorney general is weak, Democrats and several experts said, as it makes promises Donald Trump can easily reverse.

Blanche, the acting attorney general, posted two documents on Sunday evening showing he was withdrawing an 18 May order allowing for the creation of a $1.8bn weaponization fund and clarifying that broad tax immunity the justice department granted to Trump only applied retroactively to the president, his sons, and businesses.

The agreement stemmed from a deal worked out with senators John Cornyn of Texas and Thom Tillis of North Carolina, two Republicans who had threatened to block Blanche’s nomination. Cornyn and Tillis said on Monday they would support Blanche when his vote comes up for the Senate judiciary committee Tuesday.

“We are pleased that the Department of Justice has issued a formal order terminating the anti-weaponization fund,” Cornyn and Tillis said. “Additionally, the department has acknowledged in a binding written order that the audit settlement is limited to the plaintiffs and the scope does not extend beyond the defendants in the lawsuit, the IRS and the treasury, addressing concerns that multiple of our Republican colleagues share.”

But legal experts and Democrats said what Blanche announced Sunday was far from ironclad and left room for Trump to resurrect the fund.

“These assurances are not worth the paper they’re written on,” Brandon DeBot, policy director at the Tax Law Center at NYU, said in a statement. “They don’t pull back on the ongoing attempts to give the president, his family, and his affiliates unauthorized immunity from tax audits. They don’t do anything new to stop the president from trying to bring back the fund, as he threatened to this weekend. It’s abundantly clear we need congressional action.”

25 states sue Trump administration over latest round of tariffs

A group of 25 states sued the Trump administration over a round of tariffs that went into effect last month, arguing that the US president exceeded his legal authority to tax imports after the US supreme court struck down his previous set of global tariffs.

“President Trump is so intent on raising the cost of living for Americans that he is willing to break law after law after law to do so,” said California attorney general Rob Bonta in a statement. “Tariffs are taxes, and the American people cannot and should not shoulder the extra costs that come from the President’s failed and illegal economic policy — no matter how much the President wants them to.”

The lawsuit challenges tariffs of 10% to 12.5% imposed on dozens of countries, including the UK, Mexico, Canada, Australia, India, China and the 27 countries that make up the European Union, which took effect on 24 July.

The Trump administration said it imposed the tariffs in an effort to stop exports of goods produced with forced labor. But states argue the latest measures are an unlawful attempt to replace tariffs the courts have repeatedly rejected.

In February, the US supreme court declared many of Trump’s tariffs illegal in a 6-3 ruling, deciding that a 1977 law designed to address national emergencies did not provide the legal justification for most of the Trump administration’s tariffs. In May, the US court of international trade ruled in favor of small businesses that challenged the tariffs.

Ahead of a vote by the senate judiciary committee on whether to advance Todd Blanche’s nomination to become US attorney general, Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer attacked Blanche, saying he has “no intention of stopping Trump’s corruption,” adding that he has instead “spent his career enabling it.”

Amid threats from two Republican senators to block Blanche’s confirmation, Blanche rescinded his establishment of Trump’s “anti-weaponization fund.” John Cornyn and Thom Tillis said they would advance with their nomination.

Addressing this, Schumer said: “Instead of seeing Todd Blanche’s so-called deal for the sham that it is, Senate Republicans are once again taking Trump’s personal attorney at his word.”

“Blanche is still perfectly fine giving Trump a permission slip to commit tax fraud to the tune of millions of dollars,” Schumer added. “Is the GOP willing to go along with it?”
