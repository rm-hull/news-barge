SpaceX reported a substantial 92 per cent surge in revenue for the April-June quarter in its first earnings release since going public, driven by robust expansion in its Starlink satellite-internet and burgeoning AI businesses.

The company posted revenue of 7.8 billion, a significant increase from $4.1 billion a year earlier, and surpassing analyst expectations of $6.93 billion, according to LSEG data.

Despite the strong revenue performance, the aerospace giant recorded a net loss of $541 million attributable to shareholders for the three months ending June 30. The company’s stock has also seen an 8 per cent decline since its record−breaking initial public offering in June, which valued the company at approximately $1.75 trillion.

Further pressure could emerge with the expiry of SpaceX's post-IPO lock-up period, set to begin on Thursday, potentially releasing a wave of insider and early-investor shares onto the market.

Starlink and SpaceX's broader connectivity operations remain the company's primary financial engine, underpinning Elon Musk's ambition to build an AI-first business. This vision extends beyond merely renting compute capacity to developing frontier models, consumer and enterprise software, and eventually, data centers in space. The satellite-internet unit has continued to expand its global subscriber base, bolstered by additional satellite launches and a growing array of services for consumers, enterprises, aviation, maritime, and government sectors.

However, this rapid expansion has come with trade-offs, notably a drop in average revenue per user (ARPU) as SpaceX has entered more international markets and introduced lower-priced plans. Investors are closely monitoring whether the company can sustain its growth trajectory while simultaneously improving the economic viability of its network, especially as it invests heavily in expanding coverage, increasing capacity, and developing direct-to-device mobile services.

SpaceX's AI business, which encompasses xAI, Grok, the social-media platform X, and a rapidly expanding data center operation, represents its largest area of investment. The company has poured $18.37 billion into AI infrastructure, Starship, and Starlink expansion. While the AI unit is generating revenue from compute contracts with firms like Anthropic, Alphabet's Google, and Reflection AI, a portion of its recurring revenue is yet to be recognized. Operating losses within the AI business have mounted, and SpaceX has cautioned that the unit will require sustained investment before it can consistently generate profits.

The company's next-generation reusable rocket system, Starship, is yet to enter commercial service but is anticipated to be crucial for deploying higher-bandwidth Starlink satellites and orbital AI-computing infrastructure. Starship's ability to become a reliably reusable vehicle is central to SpaceX's long-term strategy, with investors keenly observing updates on testing progress, launch cadence, reusability milestones, and its satellite-deployment capabilities. Separately, SpaceX announced a partnership with Nvidia to utilize its chips in the Starmind AI1 orbital compute satellites.

The broader space segment, including commercial launches, government missions, and Starship development, continues to be a significant source of costs and uncertainty. While launch activity for Falcon, SpaceX's partially reusable workhorse rocket, has remained robust, revenue can fluctuate depending on the mix of internal Starlink deployments, commercial customer missions, and government contracts. In recent years, SpaceX has increasingly prioritized launches for its own satellite network over third-party payloads, all while absorbing substantial costs tied to Starship's development.

Investors will also be eager for comments from Elon Musk regarding a potential merger between SpaceX and Tesla, following a *Wall Street Journal* report last week suggesting that executives at his electric-vehicle company had been told to prepare for a separation of its China business ahead of a possible deal. Musk dismissed the report as "fake news," though he had previously declined to rule out the possibility, citing growing overlap between the two companies.
