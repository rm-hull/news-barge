Delta Air Lines is ending service to the Caribbean nation of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines next month, with no plans to resume the route.

The airline’s final flight to Argyle International Airport on St. Vincent will depart September 5 from Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport, a Delta spokesperson told *The Independent*. Delta said it is working to rebook passengers who had flights scheduled after that date.

The route was originally expected to pause for the season after the September date and resume December 19, but Delta has since confirmed it will be permanently discontinued. The decision to remove the route from Delta’s system was officially made May 23, Delta said.

“As part of Delta’s ongoing network planning process, the airline has decided not to resume seasonal service between Atlanta and St. Vincent, Delta told *The Independent* in a statement. “The final scheduled flight will operate on Sept. 5, 2026, and the route will not return as previously planned on Dec. 19, 2026. Customers with affected bookings will be contacted directly about alternate travel options, and Delta apologizes for any inconvenience this may cause.”

The airline did not provide a specific reason for canceling the St. Vincent route, but a spokesperson said it “routinely evaluates a variety of factors when making network decisions, including customer demand, seasonality, aircraft availability, operating costs and broader operational considerations.”

![Delta did not give a specific reason for ending the route, which served a destination known for its beaches, rainforests and volcanic landscapes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/05/16/17/iStock-1152140548.jpg)

Delta launched nonstop flights from Atlanta to St. Vincent in December 2025, initially offering daily service before reducing flights to five times a week during the slower fall season.

Delta will continue operating flights to other Caribbean destinations, including Nassau, Punta Cana, Curaçao and Grenada.

U.S. travelers can still reach Saint Vincent and the Grenadines through several airlines serving Argyle International. American Airlines has frequent nonstop flights from Miami and seasonal nonstop service from Charlotte. JetBlue also operates nonstop flights from New York’s John F. Kennedy International Airport multiple times a week.

Caribbean Airlines offers additional options from New York and Miami, though its flights typically require a connection through Caribbean hubs such as Trinidad or Barbados rather than nonstop service.

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is known for its beaches, rainforests and volcanic peaks. Tourism is a major part of the nation’s economy, with visitors drawn to activities such as snorkeling, diving, hiking and sailing.
