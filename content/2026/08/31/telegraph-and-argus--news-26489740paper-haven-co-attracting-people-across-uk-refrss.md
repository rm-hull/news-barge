A business owner is encouraging people to put down their phones and pick up their craft supplies.

Julie Sutcliffe, owner of The Paper Haven and Co in Idle, said she has seen rising demand for creative workshops since moving to larger premises in March.

More than 100 children attended her craft sessions over the summer holidays, up from 45 the previous year.

Mrs Sutcliffe said: "It's a privilege to see these kids come."

  Inside the space 

*(Image: Julie Sutcliffe)*

## Not just for kids

While her workshops are popular with children, she is keen to remind people that adults are welcome too.

Mrs Sutcliffe said: "I'm not just for kids.

"That's probably the biggest challenge I have, people think I just do children's classes.

"I've actually been teaching adult classes for 18 years. I only recently branched out to children."

## Why does she do it?

She believes crafting is a valuable way to support mental health and disconnect from technology.

Mrs Sutcliffe said: "A lot of adults struggle with their mental health and crafting is a really mindful activity.

"A phrase I like to use is 'get it out of your head and into your hands.'"

## Phones not allowed

Phones are not allowed during children's sessions, a rule that is well respected by attendees.

Parents are asked not to send children with devices, and older children who arrive alone only use phones when necessary.

Mrs Sutcliffe said many adults are also seeking ways to reduce screen time.

She said: "People are being mindful of wanting to do something, they want to stop the scroll."

  A class within the space 

*(Image: Julie Sutcliffe)*

## High demand

Adult classes, especially cross-stitching sessions, are in high demand and fully booked until November.

Mrs Sutcliffe also sees her business as part of a broader effort to support the Idle community.

She stressed the business is a community led effort.

“Idle village is really trying to regenerate itself. Lots of the businesses here are really trying."

She collaborates with nearby businesses such as Miss Butterfinger's Tearoom and hosts guest tutors who teach skills including arm knitting, finger knitting, and wreath making.

  A table workshop 

*(Image: Julie Sutcliffe)*

## From Manchester and Nottingham

Mrs Sutcliffe said: "We're really trying to build up the community."

It’s not just a popular feature within Bradford, “My ladies are coming from Manchester, Hull, Nottingham.

"They go get a chip butty from the fisheries in Idle village. He sold out of bread baps last time they were here.”

The Paper Haven and Co also runs an after-school craft club on Monday evenings.
