Downton Abbey star Hugh Bonneville will headline this year’s Whitby Lit Fest.

He joins a packed programme of literary events running from November 19 to 22, with tickets going on sale on 31 August.

Mr Bonneville, best known for his roles as the Earl of Grantham in Downton Abbey and Mr Brown in the Paddington films, will appear in Whitby in a different capacity – as a bestselling children’s author.

He will present his latest book Rory Sparkes and the Spy Who Loved Tea at a family event at 1pm on Saturday, November 21 at Whitby Pavilion.

Lois Kirtlan, chair of Whitby Lit Fest, said: "We were already incredibly proud of the programme we had assembled for Whitby this November, so to be able to announce Hugh Bonneville as a surprise addition just as tickets go on sale is fantastic.

"Hugh is loved by audiences of all ages, from Downton Abbey to Paddington, and we think families are going to have enormous fun discovering another side to him as the creator of Rory Sparkes."

The special event promises storytelling, spies, secret codes and, fittingly, some rather suspicious cups of tea.

The book follows young Rory Sparkes, who uncovers coded messages in a neighbour’s recipe book and suspects a mysterious family is part of a secret plot.

As the adults around him become obsessed with a particularly unpleasant new brand of tea, Rory begins to believe he has stumbled onto a genuine spy conspiracy.

Illustrated by Loretta Schauer, the book is a humorous mystery aimed at readers aged seven to 11.

Mr Bonneville will discuss the inspiration behind Rory’s world, answer questions from the audience, and bring the story to life on stage.

His appearance adds to an already impressive line-up spanning crime, fiction, history, poetry, politics, food, film, children’s literature, and stories from the Yorkshire coast.

Other headline guests include John Boyne, author of The Boy in the Striped Pyjamas, with his new novel The Weight of Angels; number one Sunday Times bestseller Ann Cleeves, marking 40 years in print and the publication of her 40th novel; and Chocolat author Joanne Harris.

The programme also features Hairy Biker Si King, crime writers Elly Griffiths and Mark Billingham, and internationally acclaimed actor Greta Scacchi.

Grammy Award-winning singer-songwriter Corinne Bailey Rae will host a special family event celebrating her picture book Put Your Records On, inspired by her well-known hit song.

Additional speakers include Peter Oborne, Channel 4’s chief correspondent Alex Thomson, broadcaster Louise Minchin, author Lucinda Hawksley, and journalist Sian Williams.

Events will be held at venues across Whitby, including Whitby Pavilion, Eighteen91, Flowergate Hall, Whitby Museum, Whitby Library, the Coliseum, and the Jet Black Jewel.

Ms Kirtlan said the festival aims to offer something for everyone.

She said: "The whole programme reflects what we want Whitby Lit Fest to be – big-name writers alongside exciting new voices, events inspired by Whitby and the Yorkshire coast, and everything from crime and history to politics, poetry, food, film and children’s books.

"We would encourage people to take a look at the full programme and book early, particularly for some of our headline events and smaller-capacity venues."

The festival opens on Thursday, November 19, with bestselling author Jess Kidd launching the event with a seaside-themed mystery.

Four days of conversations, performances, workshops, family activities, and literary encounters will follow.

Tickets go on sale on 31 August through the Whitby Lit Fest website.

Mr Bonneville’s family event at the Whitby Pavilions (Northern Lights Suite) takes place on Saturday, November 21 at 1pm.

Tickets cost £11 for adults and £5 for children.
