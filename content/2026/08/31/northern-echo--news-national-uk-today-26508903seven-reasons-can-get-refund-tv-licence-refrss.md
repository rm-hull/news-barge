If you watch any live TV or use BBC iPlayer, you will need to pay for a TV licence.

With the cost of a TV licence going up each year, many people are opting to watch streaming services instead of paying for the licence.

However, if you do have a TV licence, you might be unaware of some of the reasons you can claim back a refund on it.

Here are the seven reasons you could claim some money back on your TV licence.

  You might be able to get a refund on your TV licence if you fit any of these categories. 

*(Image: Getty)*

## Seven reasons you can claim back a refund for a TV licence

TV Licensing has a range of reasons you could get some money back for your TV licence, ranging from accidentally buying another TV licence when your property is already covered or moving to a property that will be covered by a TV licence.

You can easily apply for a refund through the TV Licensing website.

### 
  **1. Address is covered by another licence**

You can get a refund on your TV licence if you move to a different address that is already covered by another person’s licence.

### 
  **2. Blind concession refund**

You can get up to half of your licence fee back if the licence holder has a blind certificate from a local authority or ophthalmologist but did not apply for the blind concession reduction when buying their TV licence.

You can also apply for a backdated refund on expired TV licences back to April 1, 2000, or the first of the month in which they became blind.


### 
  **3. Refunds for a free TV licence application**

You can get a refund for the payments made for each of the months remaining on the current licence after applying for a free TV licence.

### 
  **4. Refunds following the death of the licence holder**

If the licence holder dies, you can get a refund for the remaining months of the licence that will be paid to the licence holder’s estate.

### 
  **5. TV receiving equipment is no longer used**

If you stop using TV receiving equipment at your property, you can apply for a refund.

You will no longer be able to watch TV channels on TV services, watch BBC iPlayer or watch live TV on streaming services.


### 
  **6. TV licence bought in error**

If you accidentally buy the wrong type of licence or buy another one when there is already one in place for the property, you can get your money back.

If the licence was bought in error owing to advice from TV Licensing, you can claim up to six years’ worth of money back.

### 
  **7. You buy a replacement licence**

You can get some money back if you need a different licence for your property, such as moving from a colour licence to a black and white one.

Other instances include moving to an address covered by an ARC concessionary TV licence or applying for a hotel and mobile units TV licence.

If the licence was purchased accidentally following advice from the Licensing Authority or its agents, you might be able to claim a refund dating back to six years.
