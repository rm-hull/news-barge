BRADLEY Lowther is the latest local boxer to turn professional and says he is “beyond excited” to make his paid debut later this year, **writes James Bovington**.

The 21-year-old, from Holmewood, has signed with Leeds-based WAW Sports Management and will be trained and managed by Wayne Woods at The Life of Boxing gym.

Lowther, a former Forster Academy student, will work alongside strength and conditioning and nutrition coach John Keenan as he prepares to campaign at lightweight or super-featherweight.

“I’ve been working with Wayne and John for a while now, and I’ve already made considerable improvements,” he said.

“Sparring more experienced professionals at the gym has been invaluable.

"They’ve been working wonders and I now feel much more comfortable about starting out at lightweight or super-featherweight in the paid ranks.”

Lowther combines his boxing commitments with a full-time job as a welder, training several evenings a week while also fitting in regular running sessions — known as roadwork in boxing.

“I’m busy, and it’s all physically demanding when you add in the running I do,” he said.

“But I love the competitive nature of the sport.

"Your results depend on how hard you work, how consistent you are and how dedicated you remain if you want to reach the elite level.

“I’ve always enjoyed going head-to-head with an athlete who has the same drive.”

  John Keenan and Bradley Lowther 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Lowther’s involvement in the sport began at the age of four through his stepfather, who later took him to Burmantofts ABC in Leeds when he was eight.

He spent eight years at the club, winning most of his 30 amateur contests, claiming Yorkshire titles and reaching a national final, where his bout was stopped because of a nosebleed.

“I gained valuable experience competing against some of the country’s top elite amateurs,” he said.

“The pandemic put my career on hold, but I’m back now and determined to succeed.

“I’ve mostly trained in Leeds because that’s where my stepdad started.

"I was at Nelson’s Boxing Academy for a while, and now I also help out at Bradford gyms such as Bradford Police Boys Club.”

A fan of the late former world champion Ricky Hatton, Lowther said he admired the Manchester fighter’s aggressive approach and the excitement he brought to the sport.

“I like watching Ricky Hatton for his front-foot style and for the beauty he brought to boxing,” he said.

“I’m keen to represent Bradford, my hometown, and Leeds, where I’ve done much of my training.

“I’ve put my heart and soul into boxing.

"I promise to remain grounded and hopefully inspire younger people across West Yorkshire who want to challenge themselves and stay disciplined and driven through sport.”

Lowther hopes his journey into the professional ranks will give supporters an insight into the work required to progress in boxing.

“I want people to see what it takes to reach the top level as I bring something special to the sport,” he said.

“As well as skill and courage in the ring, boxers need to build a fanbase to succeed.”

He has already secured sponsorship from Scarborough-based Mark of Perfection and is seeking further support from businesses in Bradford and the surrounding area.
