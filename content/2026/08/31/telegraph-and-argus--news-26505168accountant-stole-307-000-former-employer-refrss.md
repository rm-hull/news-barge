AN accountant who stole more than £300,000 from her former employer over a four-year period has been ordered to pay back just over £52,000.

Anne-Marie Dinsdale will also have to sell a caravan and a car and hand over the proceeds of the sales as part of the compensation payment.

In October 2024, Dinsdale, 59, was sentenced to 30 months’ imprisonment after pleading guilty to fraud by false representation, and to seeking to conceal, disguise, convert, transfer or remove criminal property.

The charges related to Avocet Dye & Chemical Company Ltd, based on Birds Royd Lane in Brighouse at which Dinsdale, of Priestley Grove, Taylor Hill, Huddersfield, had been employed as an accountant from 2010.

Part of her job involved having responsibility for ordering stationery.

Following this latest hearing, a spokesperson on behalf of Avocet Dye & Chemical Company said: “The company is relieved that the hearing has finally taken place and that it has received some closure for what has been a very challenging and distressing incident.”

## How did she swindle the money?

Bradford Crown Court previously heard how, between October 2017 and December 2021, Dinsdale created fake invoices in the name of a company called ACS Supplies purporting to supply stationery being purchased by Avocet.

Over a four-year-period, Dinsdale fraudulently channelled £258,522.58 into a Lloyds Bank account in her unsuspecting husband’s name.

The offences came to light in December 2021.

Dinsdale was arrested and interviewed during which she admitted the act of fraud but disputed the amount.

She said her husband had not been involved in the offences.

The court previously heard that Dinsdale had a previous conviction for theft from an employer and had been sentenced to 12 months imprisonment and handed a confiscation order for more than £39,000.

## What must she pay back?

At a Proceeds of Crime (POCA) hearing on Friday, Mr Recorder Abdul Iqbal KC heard that the benefit figure attributed to Dinsdale was £307,984.02.

The available figure was £52,612.60.

That is the amount she will pay back via a confiscation order directly to Avocet.

Dinsdale must also sell two assets: a Buccaneer Commodore caravan and a Mini Cooper Roadster.

Recorder Iqbal ordered the monies to be paid within three months and set a default period of 12 months’ imprisonment if it is not.

## 'Deeply ashamed'

During mitigation at the sentencing hearing, barrister Mollie Briggs said Dinsdale, who suffered from poor mental health, had taken full responsibility for her actions and was “deeply ashamed” of what she had done.

She said Dinsdale had a long-standing severe mental health diagnosis including unspecified personality disorder, major depressive disorder, and post-traumatic stress disorder.

She was also said to present as someone having an extremely fragile personality.

Ms Briggs said: “It is likely that her poor mental health was a precursor to the offences.

“What can be deduced from Miss Dinsdale’s psychological profile is that it was unlikely that this was a direct attempt at the time to defraud.

"It appears more likely she was creating an alternative world for herself.”
