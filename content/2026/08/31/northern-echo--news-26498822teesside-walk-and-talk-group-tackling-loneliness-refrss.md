What started with just 18 people on a Friday evening walk has grown into a community movement tackling loneliness for hundreds of people across Teesside.

In just 29 weeks, Charlie Morrison and Dan Boorsma have seen attendance at their We Do walk-and-talk events grow from 18 people to around 170 every week, with more than 2,000 people taking part since February.

Following the success of the original Friday evening walk from Tees Barrage, the pair have launched a second walk in Stockton in partnership with Stockton Council and are preparing to launch a third location in Thornaby.

Dan said: "It's absolutely mad. We're quite overwhelmed with how much it's grown."

The idea for We Do was born out of the pair's own experiences with alcohol addiction.

Charlie spent around 30 years battling alcoholism - often going on "chaotic" three-day benders- before joining Alcoholics Anonymous four years ago.

Dan's experience looked very different - describing himself as a hidden drinker who continued working in a corporate job and going about normal daily activities.


Their first project together, We Do Recover, focused primarily on alcohol recovery in Hartlepool.

However, in February this year, Charlie and Dan decided to broaden the concept and bring the walk-and-talk idea to Stockton instead.

Dan said: "Previously we targeted it with one label - mental health, recovery and things like that.

"What we're finding is that if there's no labels attached, people feel more comfortable coming along.

"It doesn't matter who you are, there's no referrals, no pressure. Bring your dog, find friends, find community."

Every Friday at 7pm, participants gather at the Talpore pub on Tees Barrage for a walk lasting around 50 minutes.

Afterwards, many stay behind to continue chatting, with the pub providing an upstairs space for the walkers.

Dan said: "It's not just the walk. You don't have to talk if you don't want to, but you often end up talking anyway without realising it.

"As soon as the walk starts, you see people's shoulders drop.

"You can see the anxiety at the start but after having a laugh and a joke people realise it's a bit like a family."

Dan says anyone considering coming along will not regret it.

He added: "We've had people come to the pub afterwards and not even get out of the car," said Dan.

"They've sat there and watched everybody for weeks. Eventually they've come in, and it's changed their life."

Among those whose lives have been transformed through the group is Hama Frank, who now manages the social media content for We Do.

Charlie said: "Hama came along and didn't feel accepted within the community.

"Now he has friends, people to talk to, and the support he's given us has helped take We Do to the next level."

The group's social media presence has helped fuel its growth, with its videos attracting millions of views online.

Following the incredible success of the project, Stockton Council approached We Do about launching a second walk at the new Waterfront Urban Park.

The newer Monday evening walk starts at 7pm from Riverside Car Park opposite the Hilton, which also provides a space for the group.


Dan confirmed that We Do's third walk will launch in Thornaby after the summer holidays - just six months after launch.

The woodland-based Trail and Talk walks will set off at 7pm on a Wednesday in partnership with Sprouts Community Centre.

An official start date has yet to be confirmed, but anyone interested in taking part is encouraged to keep an eye on We Do's social media channels for updates.

While the Friday and Monday walks are suitable for everyone, the Trail and Talk walks may not be accessible for wheelchair users due to the uneven terrain.

For Charlie and Dan, the goal is the same as it was with those first 18 walkers back in February.

Dan said: "It doesn't matter what you've had going on that week. It could be grief, loneliness, addiction, anxiety, or you've just had a bad day.

"We just want people to know there's somewhere they can come where they don't have to explain themselves."
