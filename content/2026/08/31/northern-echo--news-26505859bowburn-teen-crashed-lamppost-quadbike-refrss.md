A teenager who took a younger boy on a quad bike ride smashed into a lamppost, throwing the child to the ground and leaving him with a fractured skull.

Durham Crown Court heard the victim lost consciousness, but has thankfully gone on to make a good recovery since the incident in Bowburn, on the evening of May 28, last year.

The court was told the boy’s sister asked Kye Dymond, who was 17 at the time, if he could give her younger brother a ride on the quad, which belonged one of Dymond's friends.

He agreed, and the boy’s sister told him to ride carefully and not to speed.

Martin Towers, prosecuting, said Dymond was doing “loops” to and from Clarence Street, taking in a path and track leading from a play park near the village community centre.

The boy sat in front of Dymond when he departed.

Mr Towers said a witness saw the quad being ridden in the road at between 15 and 20mph, but he was not concerned about the manner of the driving.

The boy’s sister became concerned when they had not returned for a while and informed her parents.

Mr Towers said the boy’s mother went looking for them in the area and, near the library, heard Dymond calling her name, while carrying her son in his arms.

He told her that her son was “okay” and that the blood she could see was his and not from the boy.

Dymond claimed a deer had run out in front of him, causing him to collide with a lamppost.

Mr Towers said the boy was, however, “not okay” and looked “floppy” in Dymond’s arms.

An ambulance was called and police spoke to the boy who was in obvious pain, with slurred speech and looking “traumatised”.

He was physically sick, suffered a seizure and lapsed out of consciousness.

The boy was given a protective neck collar and taken to paediatric intensive care at hospital, where it was confirmed that he had suffered a fractured skull.

He was discharged from the consultant’s care in July last year.

Blood samples taken from the defendant, who was also taken to hospital, showed he was marginally over the drink/drive limit.

Dymond, now 18, of Durham Road, Bowburn, admitted causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

Laura Peto, in mitigation, said the defendant accepted he had alcohol in his system but said he did not feel impaired.

Miss Peto said Dymond did not have a full licence and only held a provisional licence at the time.

She said he was also injured in the collision, suffering cuts and other injuries to his face, nose and neck, while he also lost front teeth.

Miss Peto said he is “extremely remorseful”, fully aware of the harm he caused and wanted to take responsibility for the offence from an early stage in proceedings.

He has also apologised to the victim’s sister.

“It was an irresponsible decision made that day when he was only 17 and lacking maturity.

“He knows he should not have been riding around on a quad bike.

“There’s no suggestion he was driving at speed or showing off and putting anyone else in danger.”

Miss Peto added that the defendant has “positive relationships” with his family and long-term partner and is in full-time work with a delivery company, while also working part-time doing pressure washing.

Judge Richard Clews said he accepted the defendant was not swerving, speeding or performing dangerous manoeuvres, but by his plea the defendant conceded he was driving dangerously on a footpath and in a park.

He told Dymond that, given his age at the time and admission of guilt, he would not receive a prison sentence.

The judge said it was “inherently risky” to take a passenger on such a vehicle, aggravated by the fact he was over the drink/drive limit, albeit claiming not to have been impaired.

He passed a 12-month community order during which Dymond must attend 15 rehabilitation activity sessions with the Probation Service.

Banning the defendant from driving for the next two years, the judge added that he hopes the court does not see the defendant in future.
