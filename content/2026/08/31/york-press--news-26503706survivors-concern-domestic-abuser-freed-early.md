A domestic abuse survivor from North Yorkshire has said she is “deeply concerned” about the government’s early prisoner release scheme which could see her abuser freed from jail early.

Helen Duncanson said she felt her “safety and concerns have been overlooked”, after it was revealed that domestic abusers would be among the offenders released from jail early under the plans to ease overcrowding in prisons.

The Ministry of Justice said it is working to fix a prison system it inherited “on the brink of collapse” and shares “the public’s anger that these options are even being considered”.

Michael Boyes, of Ruffa Lane, Pickering, was jailed for three and a half years on his 40th birthday in January for abusing Ms Duncanson over a period of one year and eight months

  Domestic abuser Michael Boyes who was jailed for three and a half years in January 

*(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Prosecutor Michael Cahill said Boyes, who has more than 20 previous convictions including for domestic violence, attacked Ms Duncanson and tried to strangle her with her own necklace.

Ms Duncanson said Boyes, her former partner, subjected her to “mental torture” that “was worse than the physical injuries”, but she felt “safe” and has started to rebuild her life after he was jailed.

Ms Duncanson, who works for the ambulance service, has been praised by Queen Camilla for speaking out about her case. The queen wrote to Ms Duncanson saying: “I am full of admiration for the strength that you have shown as you have overcome the terrible experiences that you have endured.”

Ms Duncanson said she has now, however, been told that Boyes is due to be released from jail in September 2027.

She called on the ministers to have a rethink on including domestic abusers in the early prison release scheme, telling The Press: “I understand the prison system faces serious pressures and that there are legitimate reasons to consider early release. However, victims of domestic abuse should not become an afterthought in that process.

“If an offender is released early, victims need clear information about when that will happen, what conditions will apply, what restrictions will be placed on the offender, and what support and protection will be available to the victim.

“They should not be left feeling that the system has prioritised an offender's early release over their safety and wellbeing.”

Ms Duncanson said Boyes being jailed allowed her to feel safe, adding: “A prison sentence is not simply a number on a piece of paper to a victim. It can represent a period of safety. It can provide time to recover, to make arrangements, and to start building a life without constantly worrying about the person who harmed you.”

She questioned whether domestic abuse survivors “are being given sufficient consideration and protection when decisions about early release are made”.

## 'Victims deserve to be heard' - domestic abuse survivor

Ms Duncanson said she does not believe the “feelings of the victims have been taken into account for a second” under the proposals.

“How can a person even begin to know now what it feels like to be truly terrified of another person and to feel completely vulnerable?” she asked. “I am rebuilding my life and just feel as though it is all a constant battle. A battle to protect my mental health, but also a battle to fight for what’s right.”

  Domestic abuse survivor Helen Duncanson who has urged ministers to rethink proposals to release domestic abusers from jail early 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Duncanson said she decided to speak out “because I do not want other victims to experience the same fear and uncertainty that I am experiencing now”.

“People who have suffered domestic abuse have already had their sense of safety taken away from them," she said.

“The justice system should be helping to restore that sense of safety – not leaving victims wondering whether the person who abused them will soon be back in their community.

“I hope those responsible for implementing the early release scheme will listen to victims and recognise that behind every sentence is a human being whose life may have been profoundly affected by the offence.

“Victims deserve to be heard. They deserve to be informed. Most importantly, they deserve to feel safe.”

Ms Duncanson’s MP, Kevin Hollinrake, told The Press that he has asked the justice secretary “to look into this case, but the best thing Labour can do to protect victims is to immediately bring forward emergency legislation to stop the early release of thousands of serious criminals”.

The Conservative MP said: "Labour’s early release scheme is putting people across the country like Helen at risk with thousands of serious criminals, including domestic abusers, set to be released soon.

“Helen has shown exceptional bravery and resilience in speaking out about her case and her fear about her abuser being released early.”

A Ministry of Justice spokesperson said: “We have always stated we wanted to go further, and we are fast-tracking a plan to keep more of the most dangerous offenders behind bars. That’s on top of murderers and those serving sentences for rape, serious child sexual abuse and grooming offences who are already blocked from any changes.

“We share the public’s anger that these options are even being considered, after we inherited a prison system on the brink of collapse. We are fixing this by building 14,000 new prison places, expanding electronic tagging on an unprecedented scale, and investing in probation services at record levels.

“We are also exploring how we can go further, including faster deportation of foreign criminals and reviewing the unjust IPP sentences.”
