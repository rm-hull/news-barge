A WARD councillor is confident rough sleeping will no longer exist in Bradford city centre come the end of this year as enforcement increases.

A large encampment in the grounds of Bradford And Keighley Magistrates' Court was removed earlier this month.

A notice has also recently appeared, on behalf of the Government, by Bradford Crown Court.

The two areas have become hotspots for rough sleepers in recent months.

It comes after new Prime Minister Andy Burnham recently pledged to end rough sleeping by Christmas, backed by a £442 million funding package to support housing, healthcare, and other essential needs.

*(Image: NEWSQUEST)*

## Encampment at Magistrates cleared

The encampment at the city's magistrates court consisted of numerous tents alongside a shopping trolley filled with waste and piles of fly-tipped rubbish.

The site is managed by HM Courts and Tribunal Service (HMCTS), and the enforcement notice banned entry to the area.

  Bradford Magistrates' Court now clear 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

## Notice at Bradford Crown Court

The notice at Bradford Crown Court was issued on behalf of the Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Government.

It says anyone who is occupying the premises without permission is trespassing.

It adds that they must leave immediately or they may face court proceedings.

  The notice by Bradford Crown Court 

*(Image: T&A)*

The notes reads: "If you are experiencing homelessness and are concerned about where you will sleep, you should contact Bradford Metropolitan District Council's Housing Options Service by telephone on 01274 381 999 or attend in person at City Hall, Norfolk Gardens Entrance, Bradford, BD1 1HY."

## Praise for those tackling homelessness

City ward Councillor Nazam Azam (Labour) said he is in favour of the Government’s approach.

He said: "It gives me a lot of confidence with the new Prime Minister.

"If we could eliminate homelessness it would speak a lot of us as a nation and as a country.

"His priorities are absolutely right."

He also praised Bradford’s efforts to tackle homelessness.

The Homeless Outreach Partnership (HOP), commissioned by Bradford Council and led by team co-ordinator Clare Flannigan from Horton Housing, has supported 99 rough sleepers into accommodation since January.

The HOP team, made up of 12 professionals, includes drug and alcohol workers, complex needs specialists, and housing experts who offer tailored support to individuals based on their specific circumstances.

Cllr Azam said: "We have a brilliant homelessness team working around the clock.

"I’m confident with the new announcement the target is absolutely achievable.

"I am personally aware of the brilliant work that teams have been doing in all weather.

"Putting themselves out there, I am grateful for them.

"They are the people on the ground who are doing everything to ensure that the most vulnerable people are helped."

He added: "Over the years, Bradford has done very well when it comes to tackling homelessness.

"However, with the new motivation and dedication and commitment that the new Prime Minster has shown I am confident the target set of Christmas is realistic."

Cllr Azam also said: "When it comes to homelessness, no one should have to spend even a single minute outside without shelter.

He said: "Let’s just hope that the funding that’s been announced comes through and we can completely eradicate homelessness from Bradford.

"Which is where we need to be, it’s more than achievable absolutely."

## Councillors glad encampment has gone

Cllr Azam also commented on the encampment’s removal.

He said: "I am informed that what was proposed at the magistrates court that nobody would be left alone, and accommodation would be offered.

"This absolutely good news, and I believe it all stems from the new Prime Minister’s priorities and initiatives to tackle homelessness."

Fellow City Ward Councillor Sajawal Hussain (Labour) also supported the encampment’s removal and called for further support for those experiencing homelessness.

He said: "It’s right that the encampments were removed, I think the council should discourage people from sleeping rough and staying outside.

"The people should not be on the streets, especially in really harsh winter conditions."

Cllr Hussain also welcomed the Prime Minister’s pledge.

He said: "The Prime Minister’s initiative to ensure homelessness is eliminated by the end of this year, I welcome that and I really appreciate what the government are doing."
