A LARGE Bradford school's decision to shut a long-used entrance has sparked frustration among parents.

Dixons Allerton Academy, which is part of the Dixons Academies Trust and caters for 1775 pupils aged between three and 19, will close its rear entrance on Oaks Lane from September, forcing pupils to use the main entrance instead.

Parents say the change will add up to 20 minutes to the school run, with concerns raised about safety, accessibility, and a lack of consultation.

## What has the school said?

The trust says its decision to close the gate was taken following a "review of site access arrangements".

The result of this was that "consolidating access points would support the effective management of entry to and exit from the school site".

It adds that the "safety and wellbeing of students, staff and visitors is our priority".

  Dixon Allerton Academy has defended the decision to close the entrance

## 'A lot of inconvenience'

Aneesa Khalifa, who has three daughters at the school aged between 10 and 13, said the closure will cause significant disruption for many families.

Ms Khalifa said: "My youngest is 10 years old and all she needs to do is cross the road and she’s in school.

"If this weather gets colder, it means they’re going to have to walk around.

"The car park is busy as it is.

"It’s causing a lot of inconvenience, and not just for myself.

"So many families with two, three or four kids are having to take their little children all the way round."

She added: "Parents should have been called in to a meeting and asked about our views.

"This was told to us after the school was closed for the summer holidays.

"I never had to bring my car before.

"Nobody was consulted on this whatsoever.

"It's quite concerning.

"The gate being closed and us not being told is not right at all."

## Safety concerns

She also expressed concerns about the safety of the new route.

Ms Khalifa said: "It will take 15-20 minutes longer to walk around, which will include crossing a busy main road with a lot of cars rushing along it.

"I don’t think it’s safe for kids."

## Lack of communication

One parent of three children, who asked to remain anonymous, said: "Parents were not consulted or warned about this decision to close off the rear entrance to the school.

"We have been told nothing by the school.

"I have three children. I live adjacent to the rear entrance that has been closed.

"We got a notification telling us in a short sentence that the entrance was closed due to safeguarding concerns.

"There has been no consultation with parents.

"It now takes me about 20 minutes to get into the school via the front entrance, which means it can be 40 minutes going and coming back.

"For those with mobility issues, it’s going to take a lot longer.

"Kids are going to be impacted.

"If parents are going to have to use their cars, there will be more traffic.

"There is already tight parking.

"It just seems a bit extreme.

"This entrance has been open for the last 15 years and had no issues until now.

"They have to make sure the back entrance is guarded to make it safe.

"If there was a consultation, it would have been fine.

"But they have not even asked if we wanted it to happen."

## Full response from the trust

A spokesperson for Dixons Academies Trust said: "The decision to close the Oaks Lane gate was taken following a review of site access arrangements.

"The safety and wellbeing of students, staff and visitors is our priority.

"Following the review, it was determined that consolidating access points would support the effective management of entry to and exit from the school site.

"We recognise that this change may be less convenient for some families and appreciate their understanding.

"We will continue to keep site access arrangements under review to ensure they best support the needs of our school community.

"The closure does not affect access for extracurricular activities, with appropriate arrangements remaining in place."

The school has not provided further details about the specific safeguarding concerns that prompted the closure.
