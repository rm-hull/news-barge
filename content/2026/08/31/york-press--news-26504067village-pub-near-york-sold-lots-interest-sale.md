A village pub near York has been sold after its previous owner entered liquidation.

The Aldwark Arms was sold for an undisclosed sum after being put on the market with an asking price of £450,000.

It comes after The Press revealed that the pub had gone under after facing issues due to Covid, a cost-of-living crisis and a closed toll bridge.

Aldwark Arms Pub had an estimated deficiency of £696,323 when it was liquidated in March, according to Companies House.

Administrators FRP Advisory said the freehold land and property would raise £450,000, with £358,000 owed to Investec Bank, leaving £92,000 available to preferential creditors.

A summary of liabilities also noted HMRC was owed £350,000, leaving a deficit to preferential creditors of £258,000.

Warren and Sue Taylor took over the pub in 2021 after buying the venue in 2020, just as the UK entered lockdown during the Covid pandemic.

They praised the support they received from locals but noted that “timing was not on our side”.

In a Facebook post, the owners said taking over the pub was “a dream come true” and they “sold everything we owned and moved 12,000kms across the sea to start a new chapter in our lives”.

“We fought through, then came the Ukraine war, and the energy crisis, the cost-of-living crisis, the staffing crisis, inflation increases – but we still held firm,” the post said.

It added: “Just when we thought we were over with the troubles, came the 10-month closure of the Aldwark Bridge which was our most significant contributor for us, as we lost 40 per cent of our trade overnight.

“Even when the bridge eventually reopened, people had found new places to go, and new favourite venues to visit.”

The pub remains closed but is “immaculately presented and extremely well maintained throughout”, according to Christie and Co which announced the sale of the pub.

Christie and Co’s regional director for pubs and restaurants, David Cash, said there had been “lots of interest in the pub” and it received “multiple offers, which is reassuring but not surprising given the quality of the asset and its location”.

“This resulted in a best and final offers process and the pub going under offer within weeks of launching to the market, as well as a swift timeline between the deal being agreed and completion,” he said.

The sale included the 0.65-acre site featuring the pub as well as a car park and the owner’s first floor flat.

Planning permission has also been granted for an extension to the existing pub and the construction of a separate accommodation block, Christie and Co said.
