A County Durham NHS manager is preparing to take on an 11-mile swim across Windermere just weeks after celebrating her 60th birthday.

Christine Murphy and husband James are attempting the Chillswim Triple Crown, a Lake District challenge involving swims across Coniston Water, Ullswater and Windermere.

The couple have already completed the first two lakes, leaving the 11-mile Windermere crossing as their final and toughest test.

Christine, who manages community mental health services for older people across County Durham for Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust, said: “We wanted a proper challenge to mark my birthday, and this fitted the bill.

“Each lake swim gets progressively harder, and Windermere is 11 miles long.

"If we manage to finish the swim, it will mean spending eight hours in the water.

"Luckily, I love swimming.”

The pair are fundraising for the Alzheimer’s Society and British Heart Foundation as part of the challenge.

  Christine and James Murphy are taking on the challenge.

## From Consett swimming club to open water

Annfield Plain-born Christine competed with Consett Swimming Club as a child before returning to the sport in her 30s, when her own children joined the same club.

Her interest in open-water swimming came after two back operations for severe sciatica.

She said: “I needed a challenge to aim for after surgery, to work on my fitness, and James suggested open-water swimming.

"It proved a perfect choice.”

The couple took part in their first open-water event, Swim Serpentine in London’s Hyde Park, in 2017.

The event was held behind Imperial College, where both had studied.

Christine said: “There is unpredictability about swimming outside.

"You have currents and chop and obviously wind, but the view is usually better. It can be amazing.

"There is something magical about swimming on a day when the sun breaks through the clouds, and you get shafts of light reflecting on the water.

"There is nothing like it.”

## Long-distance training

Training has included pool sessions and endurance lake swims at Ellerton Water, Weardale Adventure’s Stanhope Quarry and Sleekburn.

Christine and James have set themselves a target of swimming at least 226 miles this year.

Completing the three Triple Crown swims will add 23 miles to their total.

Christine said the Alzheimer’s Society had been chosen because of her work with people living with dementia and their relatives.

“I’ve worked closely with the Alzheimer’s Society for years and see every day the difference they make to people living with dementia and their families,” she said.

The British Heart Foundation also has a personal link for the couple, as relatives of James have been affected by hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, a genetic heart condition.

## Next challenge awaits

After Windermere, the couple are planning to travel to Montenegro in October for UltraSwim 33.3, which covers 33.3km over four days.

Christine said: “These are personal challenges, but they’re also significant ones and we’re proud to be raising money for two charities that mean a great deal to us.

“Right now, all our attention is on Windermere.

"It’s a very long way to swim, but we’re hoping all the training will pay off.”

After that, they may try swim-run — a race format in which competitors swim in the footwear they run in and run in their wetsuits.

Christine added: “It could be the end of 39 years of marriage, or just a lot of fun.”
