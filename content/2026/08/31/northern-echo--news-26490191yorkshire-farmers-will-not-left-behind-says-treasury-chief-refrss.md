A Treasury chief has insisted rural communities will not be left behind by the Government's drive to shift investment and decision-making away from Westminster.

Chief Secretary to the Treasury Emma Reynolds said the Government's pledge to bring "power to the postcodes" would extend to farming communities and the wider rural economy as she visited Northallerton last week.

Ms Reynolds, who also toured No.11 North at the Darlington Economic Campus during her visit to the region, said she had previously discussed the challenges facing farmers with the president of the National Farmers' Union at his North Yorkshire farm.

Speaking to The Northern Echo, she highlighted increasingly unpredictable weather and pointed to a recently-announced £65m Government package designed to help farmers deal with drought conditions.

*(Image: Simon Walker / HM Treasury)*

"I've previously met with the president of the National Farmers' Union at his North Yorkshire farm and talked about the challenges of farming, including drier conditions and Government funding of £65m," she said.

"Going from one extreme to another, including drought and then prolonged rain and flooding, you can't make sustainable farming work with that.

"We want to support and provide good jobs in every postcode of the UK and that includes rural communities.

"We know how important the rural economy is."

Her comments echo Prime Minister Andy Burnham's pledge to deliver "power to the postcodes" by directing more investment and decision-making towards communities outside Westminster.

  The Chief Secretary to the Treasury, Emma Reynolds, visited Crazy Kingdom in Northallerton, a local soft play centre, where she met parents and children enjoying the facilities 

*(Image: Simon Walker / HM Treasury)*

The £65m farming package was announced earlier this month following what the Government said was the driest July on record in England and Wales.

Around three-quarters of England was in drought at the time of the announcement, although North Yorkshire was not among the areas formally listed as being in drought.

The funding includes an additional £50m for the Sustainable Farming Incentive in 2026, taking its budget to £290m, alongside up to £15m to support the construction of on-farm reservoirs.

Farmers affected by drought are also being given greater flexibility over environmental agreements, allowing them in some circumstances to graze livestock or cut land for feed without losing payments.

The Government has also pledged to make it easier for farms to gain access to water during drought conditions and to examine planning rules which can make the construction of agricultural reservoirs more difficult.

*(Image: Simon Walker / HM Treasury)*

Announcing the measures, Mr Burnham said farmers were increasingly having to deal with the consequences of a changing climate.

"This is the third drought in five years," he said.

"Farmers are at the sharp end of a changing climate, and they should not be carrying that risk alone."

Environment Secretary Angela Eagle said farmers were facing "some of the toughest conditions in decades" and said the Government wanted both to provide immediate assistance and make the farming sector more resilient to future extreme weather.

Ms Reynolds' remarks came during a wider visit to North Yorkshire in which she also backed investment in town centres and high streets.

She confirmed £40m of local growth funding for York and North Yorkshire, including investment connected to the Northallerton Town Investment Plan and High Street Growth Plan between now and 2030.

But she stressed that the Government's growth ambitions extended beyond larger towns and cities, arguing that farming and rural businesses remained an important part of the regional and national economy.
