A tiny Yorkshire Dales village sits just a short distance from the remote farm made famous by Amanda Owen and Our Yorkshire Farm.

Keld is in Upper Swaledale, close to the Owen family’s 2,000-acre Ravenseat Farm.

The village is surrounded by some of the most dramatic countryside in North Yorkshire and is also known for its waterfalls, walking routes and historic landscape.

For viewers familiar with Ravenseat, Keld is one of the closest settlements to the area seen on television.

## Where is Keld?

Keld sits near the head of Swaledale in the Yorkshire Dales National Park.

It is beyond Muker and is one of the most remote settlements in the dale.

Ravenseat lies further west in the countryside around Whitsundale.

The area becomes increasingly isolated as the road leaves Keld and heads towards the Pennines.

## How close is Keld to Ravenseat?

Keld is one of the closest villages to Ravenseat.

Walking routes in the area head out from near Keld towards Ravenseat and the surrounding moorland.

It means the village is a useful point of reference for people trying to understand just how remote Amanda Owen’s farm really is.

## Why is Keld famous?

Despite its small size, Keld is well known among walkers.

The Coast to Coast Path and the Pennine Way both pass through the area.

The village also sits close to the Herriot Way.

That means large numbers of walkers pass through Keld despite its isolated location.

Another well-known landmark nearby is Crackpot Hall.

The ruined farmhouse sits in the hills above Swaledale and can be reached on popular walking routes between Keld and Muker.

The building dates back to the 18th century and is surrounded by former lead-mining remains.

It is one of several signs of the area’s industrial past.

### Waterfalls near Keld

The area around Keld is also known for its waterfalls.

East Gill Force is one of the best known and sits close to popular walking routes.

Kisdon Force can also be reached from the surrounding area.


The River Swale and its tributaries have helped shape the landscape around the village.

## Keld and Swaledale’s mining history

Upper Swaledale was once an important lead-mining area.

Old workings, spoil heaps and ruined buildings can still be seen across the surrounding hills.

Keld was once busier during the height of the mining industry than it is today.

Now the area is better known for farming, walking and tourism.

## Can you visit Ravenseat from Keld?

Ravenseat remains a working family farm rather than a visitor attraction.

Public walking routes cross the wider countryside around the farm.

Visitors are advised to stick to recognised paths and respect private land.

Keld itself offers an easier base for exploring the scenery seen in Amanda Owen’s television programmes.

## Why Keld is worth knowing

Amanda Owen may have helped bring this remote corner of North Yorkshire to a national audience, but Keld has long had its own identity.

The village sits among waterfalls, old mining remains, stone barns and some of the best-known walking routes in Britain.

And just a few miles away is Ravenseat, the farm which made Upper Swaledale familiar to millions of television viewers.
