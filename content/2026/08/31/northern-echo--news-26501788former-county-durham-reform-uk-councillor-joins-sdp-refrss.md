A County Durham councillor who was expelled from Reform UK for his drunken behaviour at an Armed Forces Day event has joined a new party.

Councillor Jack McGlenen, who represents Dalton and Dawdon, will now sit as a member of the Social Democratic Party.

Cllr McGlenen apologised for his behaviour, saying he had learned from his mistakes, after he was kicked out of Reform UK in September last year following allegations of public drunkenness and abusive behaviour at an event to honour the armed forces in Seaham.

Cllr McGlenen joined the SDP on August 20 after months of discussions, with then leader of the party William Clouston saying he was "totally aligned to the party’s values."

"The SDP offers something that fits with my identity as a politician," Cllr McGlenen told The Northern Echo.

"Since becoming a County Councillor, I have always tried to put local people first.

"My focus has been on listening to residents, taking action on the issues that matter to them and fighting to improve our communities.


"Joining the Social Democratic Party is a new chapter for me, but my commitment to the people I represent remains exactly the same.

"I will continue to stand up for residents, challenge decisions when I believe they are wrong and work constructively to get things done.”

Cllr McGlenen was elected in May 2025 with 876 votes, alongside George Anderson of Reform UK, as the party took control of Durham County Council for the first time.

A spokesperson for the SDP said: "The allegations relate to a time before Cllr McGlenen became a member of the SDP.

"They were discussed with him before he joined.

"Cllr McGlenen has apologised and expressed sincere regret for his actions."
