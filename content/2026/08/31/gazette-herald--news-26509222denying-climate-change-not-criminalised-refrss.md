Apparently, we can now declare 2026 the hottest year on record - despite the fact that it’s still only August. This reminds me of a hapless football pundit declaring England had won the World Cup semi-final after scoring in the 55th minute . The remaining 35 minutes are apparently irrelevant.

Those who question the prevailing climate narrative apparently account for just 0.3% of the population. And their opinions come from social media . That is a remarkably lazy way of dismissing an argument. My views come from observation, intuition and extensive reading. And if anything, social media algorithms have relentlessly amplified the prevailing narrative by repeatedly feeding people the same material.

Being in a minority does not make an argument wrong. Scientific history is littered with ideas that were once dismissed because they contradicted the prevailing consensus. Bloodletting was accepted medicine; continental drift was ridiculed. Consensus can be useful, but it is not infallible - and certainly not a substitute for evidence.

Which brings me to champagne socialist, eco warrior, tea towel-wearing Dale Vince and his reported suggestion that climate “denial” should be criminalised. The Orwellian notion that ‘wrongthink ‘should become illegal ought to alarm anyone who believes in free inquiry. It is particularly important to scrutinise the views of influential advocates rather than simply accepting them because they are loudly expressed.

Science should welcome questions, not criminalise them.

As Einstein put it: “The important thing is not to stop questioning. Curiosity has its own reason for existing.”

Perhaps we should apply that principle - and wait for the final whistle.

John Hicks

Cawton
