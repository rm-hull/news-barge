Music fans braved the rain to enjoy a city centre street festival.

Bradford’s North Parade Street Party returned for its 12th edition on August 30, offering nine hours of live entertainment, street food, from 12pm to 9pm.

The event has become a staple of the August Bank Holiday weekend, drawing thousands of visitors to the city each year.

  Rain didn't deter revellers at the street party 

*(Image: Phil Lickley)*

James Brunskill, owner of Boar and Fable and part of the organising team, said: "A free music festival in Bradford city centre – who else is doing that?"

He explained that the event is completely and proudly independently run.

  Rain didn't deter revellers at the street party 

*(Image: Phil Lickley)*

Although rain dampened the early hours of the event, many festival-goers sheltered inside nearby venues.

Outdoor seating remained available for those willing to brave the weather.

This year’s line-up featured Bradford band The Fourth Act, Natural Rhythm, the Richard Harris Experience, Rubber Kazoo, Austin Haddock, and Boki.

  Rain didn't deter revellers at the street party 

*(Image: Phil Lickley)*

Food was provided by vendors including Rumshackalack, Grade 2 Sports Bar, Harvana Lounge, and L&G Bar and Lounge.

Mr Brunskill said the event "offers something different" and hopes people will still come out to enjoy the festivities despite the "dreadful weather" expected for the weekend.

He said the street party should "avoid the worst of it."

  Rain didn't deter revellers at the street party 

*(Image: Phil Lickley)*

Visitors and residents alike praised the event for its community spirit and support for local talent.

David Jared, who was visiting family in Bradford, said: "My family were coming along so I fancied coming along too to see what it's all about.

  Rain didn't deter revellers at the street party 

*(Image: Phil Lickley)*

"What beautiful architecture on this street.

"It's a shame the sun isn't shining, but there's plenty of places to go inside and nice beers.

"I think it's great to have a free event like this for a city, championing independent businesses and musicians."

A Bradford resident also highlighted the importance of such events for the city centre.

They said: "I think it's important to host these community events and get everyone talking, supporting local businesses.

"Less and less people are visiting the high street, city centres, so we need to give them a reason to come out – and this is it."
