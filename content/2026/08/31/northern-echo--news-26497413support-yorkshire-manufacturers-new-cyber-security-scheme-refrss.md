North Yorkshire manufacturers are being offered new support to help protect their businesses from cyber attacks as the risk of digital disruption continues to grow across the sector.

The fully funded programme, part of a £400,000 pilot from Made Smarter Yorkshire, aims to boost digital resilience in the manufacturing sector through improved awareness, training, and accreditation.

Dharmendra Shadija, head of school at the School of Computing and Digital Technologies at Sheffield Hallam University, said: "We are delighted to be working with Made Smarter Yorkshire to help manufacturers build the knowledge, skills and confidence they need to protect their businesses.

  Dharmendra Shadija, head of school, School Of Computing and Digital Technologies, Sheffield Hallam University 

*(Image: NIGEL BARKER PHOTOGRAPHY)*

"Our experts in cyber security, digital forensics and organisational change will help businesses understand the risks they face and, just as importantly, develop people who can champion safer working practices and make cyber awareness part of everyday working life."

The programme is designed to help manufacturers protect staff, systems, and operations as factories become more digitally connected and vulnerable to cyber threats.

The new pilot will begin in September and is being delivered in partnership with Sheffield Hallam University.

It will provide four levels of support, from general cyber awareness to internationally recognised ISO 27001 accreditation.

A key component of the scheme is the creation of Cyber Security Champions within manufacturing businesses.

These employees will be trained to identify threats, promote safe digital practices, and help build a culture of security throughout the organisation.

The pilot could also influence how cyber security support is delivered to manufacturers across the rest of England.

Jessica Armitage, programme manager for Made Smarter Yorkshire, said: "Cyber attacks are no longer something manufacturers can afford to think will happen to somebody else.

"Digital technology is creating huge opportunities, but the more connected a business becomes, the more important it is that cyber security develops alongside it.

"This isn't simply an IT problem.

"An attack can stop production, delay orders, increase costs and disrupt customers and suppliers.

"For smaller manufacturers, one of the biggest challenges can simply be knowing where to start.

"That's why we've made this support fully funded and accessible to businesses at different stages of their journey.

"We want to help manufacturers take that first step, develop people who can champion cyber security within their business, and provide a pathway to keep strengthening their resilience."

Cyber threats are a growing concern across the UK.

The National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), part of GCHQ, has reported that nationally significant cyber incidents have more than doubled. The average cost of a cyber attack to UK businesses is estimated at £190,000.

The wider economic impact is substantial, with the Government estimating total annual losses at £14.7 billion.

Research by Make UK, the manufacturers' organisation, found that nearly one in three manufacturers experienced a serious cyber incident in the past year, either directly or through their supply chain.

Affordability and lack of awareness were cited as the main barriers to improving cyber resilience, particularly among SMEs.

The first stage of the Made Smarter Yorkshire offer is a 2.5-hour Cyber Security Awareness session, delivered online and open to all SME manufacturers in Yorkshire.

The session is especially suited to businesses new to cyber security.

It will introduce the concept of Cyber Security Champions and cover common risks such as phishing, weak passwords, and cloud security.

Participants will leave with a practical action plan to begin improving their cyber defences.

The pilot’s outcomes will help inform future national support schemes for manufacturing SMEs.

Ms Armitage said improving digital resilience is not only about technology, but also about people.

She said: "While threats continue to evolve, human error remains the most common cause of security incidents, making awareness and safer working practices vital to protecting a business."
