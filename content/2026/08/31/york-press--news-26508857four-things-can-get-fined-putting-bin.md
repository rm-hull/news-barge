## Avoid doing these 4 things when putting out your bins or be issued with an £80 fine

If you block the pavement with your bins, you could be fined. *(Image: Getty)</i>*

While bin collections might seem as simple as just putting the bins out on the correct days, bin collections have gone through a lot of changes this year and you might find it difficult to keep up with.

There are lots of rules and regulations you must follow when dealing with your bins, such as making sure all of your waste is put in the correct bin.

If you are putting the wrong things in your bins, or leaving them out incorrectly, the binmen can refuse to take them or even get you fined.

Here are the four things to avoid when putting out your bins for collection so you don't get fined.

  If you avoid doing these four things, you will not be fined. 

*(Image: Getty)*

## Four things that can get you fined for your bins

The government website states: “The Government wishes to encourage a measured and balanced approach, where householders are not penalised for minor breaches of waste bin rules.

“The use of these penalties should focus on those who cause genuine harm to the local environment.

“It is good practice to try and inform the household about any issues on the presentation of their waste bins.

“For example, you could use a letter or information notice.

“You should do this before moving to the process of issuing penalty notices outlined here.

“Where you chose to do so, you must follow these rules when issuing fixed penalties under section 46A of the Environmental Protection Act 1990.”


Households can be issued with fixed penalties if they are breaking waste collection rules and this causes either a nuisance or has a negative effect on local amenities.

You can be issued with a fixed penalty if your waste causes one of the following four things:

- causes an obstruction to neighbours, such as forcing people using wheelchairs or buggies to walk on the road
- restricts access to the pavement or street, for example leaving waste receptacles (bins or bags) out for several days
- is likely to attract vermin like foxes and rats, such as leaving bags or open receptacles out days before a waste collection
- is unsightly (torn bags or overturned receptacles are left out)

As long as you are putting the correct items in the right bins and are making sure your bins are put away soon after collection, you will not be issued with a fine.

If you accidentally put something in the wrong bin by mistake or forget to close your bin lid, you will also not be fined.

The maximum penalty for breaking these rules is £80.
