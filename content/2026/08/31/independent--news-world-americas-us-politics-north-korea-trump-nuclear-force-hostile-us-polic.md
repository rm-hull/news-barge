North Korea declared on Monday that Washington’s hostile policy remains unchanged and pledged to continue strengthening its nuclear capabilities, rebuffing repeated American calls for denuclearization.

In a statement released via state media KCNA, a Foreign Ministry spokesperson accused Washington of maintaining a confrontational stance through demands for Pyongyang's "complete denuclearisation," criticism of its human rights record, and upcoming joint military exercises with South Korea and Japan.

"The Trump administration has thus fully reconfirmed its invariable hostile policy to wantonly violate the sovereignty, political system and security interests of the DPRK," the spokesperson said, referring to the Democratic People's Republic of Korea.

The official described the country's nuclear arsenal as "an absolute guarantee for defending its sovereignty," adding that U.S. demands would not weaken its nuclear status.

![A Foreign Ministry spokesperson accused Washington of maintaining a confrontational stance through its commitment to the ‘complete denuclearisation’ of North Korea](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/19/14/28/GettyImages-1159161650.jpg)

Expecting any easing of regional tensions is "pointless," the representative said. The official added that there is similarly "no change" in North Korea's policy toward the U.S.

The statement follows U.S. President Donald Trump ordering a major scaling back of the annual Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises between the U.S. and South Korea this month.

Trump cited his "very good relationship" with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, calling the drills costly and unnecessarily hostile to Pyongyang.

Trump has been seeking another summit with Kim, following three meetings in 2018 and 2019.

Despite the reduction, Kim Yo Jong, the leader's sister, noted this month that the exercises remain provocative and aggressive even at a smaller scale.

She said Trump’s reduction of the drills was “not even worth commenting on” and Pyongyang found it “completely uninteresting” and snapped that if Washington saw the scaling back of the drills “as some kind of goodwill measure,” she said, “they will not get the answer they desire.”

"I know Kim Jong Un very well, and he's going to be fine as long as we’ve a smart president," Trump later told reporters at the White House. "The fact that I get along with him, that's a good thing, not a bad thing.”

Separately, the U.S., South Korea, and Japan will hold the Freedom Edge exercise from September 7 to 11 to bolster coordination against missile and nuclear threats, per Seoul's military. North Korea has repeatedly condemned the trilateral exercise as a regional security threat.
