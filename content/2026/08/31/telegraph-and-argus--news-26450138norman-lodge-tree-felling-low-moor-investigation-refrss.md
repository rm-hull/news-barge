AN investigation has been launched by Bradford Council into tree felling and associated works linked to a proposed car park at a Bradford site.

Residents, community leaders and councillors have raised concerns after several mature trees were cleared from the former Norman Lodge care home in Low Moor - next to Sedbergh Youth & Community Centre.

The former Bradford Council-operated care home on Glenroyd Avenue closed in 2024 following a review of adult social care services.

It is understood the site's new owners are seeking to bring the building back into use as a children's home.

## Community centre says wildlife projects damaged

Lucy O'Farrell, centre manager at Sedbergh Youth & Community Centre, said residents became concerned after mature trees were felled near the centre as part of works understood to be linked to the creation of a car park.

The centre, which has invested heavily in environmental and wildlife projects, claims valuable habitats have been lost as a result of the clearance.

Ms O'Farrell said: "We've put together a nature hub and spent hundreds and thousands of pounds on wildlife projects.

"The neighbours were devastated because all the birds were screeching."

She added that volunteers and residents had spent years improving the area for wildlife and had been shocked by the scale of the clearance.

Ms O'Farrell also raised concerns about the positioning of a new boundary fence.

She said: "There's a boundary issue.

"They've taken some of our land and put a fence up, so that's taken some of our land from our charity."

## No planning permission for proposed car park

Residents believe the tree felling was undertaken to make way for a new car park connected to the former Norman Lodge site.

However, Bradford Council has confirmed that no planning permission has been granted specifically for a proposed car park at the property.

A review of planning records by the Telegraph & Argus was also unable to identify any planning applications relating to tree felling at the site or surrounding roads this year.

The council confirmed it has received complaints regarding both the tree clearance and associated development works.

## Residents 'deeply distressed'

Wyke ward councillor Ian Reed (Reform UK) said he was contacted by residents after they witnessed large-scale tree felling and changes to the site's boundaries.

He said: "Many were deeply distressed by what they had seen and were particularly concerned about the impact on local wildlife and nesting birds.

"I immediately raised these concerns with council officers and requested that the matter be investigated."

Cllr Reed said residents deserved clear answers about what had taken place and whether any regulations had been breached.

He also raised concerns regarding changes to the site's boundary.

Cllr Reed said: "It is understood that officers identified what appears to be a substantial movement of the site boundary onto council-owned land and are now considering what further action may be required."

## Council investigation ongoing

Bradford Council has confirmed officers have visited the site and opened an investigation into complaints about the works.

A council spokesperson said: "We are aware of the tree felling, and officers have visited the site.

"An investigation is taking place following the complaints and allegations that protected trees have been felled.

"Until the investigation is completed, we are unable to comment on any potential enforcement actions."

The council confirmed it is investigating concerns raised by residents and Sedbergh Youth & Community Centre.

When asked whether any of the felled trees were protected, the authority said it could not comment while enquiries remain ongoing.

Bradford Council also acknowledged wider concerns around the loss of mature trees.

The spokesperson said: "We are always mindful of the concerns raised regarding the potential impacts of extensive tree felling on wildlife habitats and the character and amenity of the local area.

"The loss of mature trees can also cause alarm among nearby residents."

## 
  **Forestry Commission contacted**

Bradford Council has referred concerns to the Forestry Commission amid questions over whether a felling licence may have been required.

The Telegraph & Argus contacted the potential owners of the site seeking a response to concerns raised by residents and the community centre.

No comment addressing the allegations was provided.
