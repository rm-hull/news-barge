A woman was left frightened to sleep after she overheard her neighbour's "screaming and shouting" partner making racist comments, a court heard.

Luke Wilson was told he needs to "need to change [his] ways" by a judge when he appeared at Durham Crown Court, having previously admitted a charge of racially aggravated harassment, alarm or distress.

Alexander Strother, prosecuting, said it arose from a heated row between Wilson, 29,and his now ex-partner, in Market Crescent, Wingate, on August 6, 2024.

Amid the “screaming and shouting” Wilson was heard saying: “Why do you live with those black c, next door?

He made further abusive comments, even when the door was opened by the neighbour who overheard.

Wilson was arrested two days later and taken to Peterlee Police Station, where he denied using racist language.

The victim said in a statement prepared for the court that Wilson’s comments made her feel “scared”, “distressed” and, “frightened to sleep”, as the defendant knows where she lives.

Wilson, of Alexander Drive, Hetton-le-Hole, was said to have 12 convictions for 28 past offences, most recently for breach of a non-molestation order, for which he received a 12-month suspended sentence in March last year, plus two assaults in August 2024, for which he received a community order.

Martin Scarborough, for Wilson, said he accepts his comments were “inappropriate” and the victim may well have found them “upsetting”.

But Mr Scarborough said they were words said to someone else and not directed at his partner’s neighbour.

Judge Richard Clews told Wilson: “I hope you understand the attitude and language you used that day are unacceptable and have no place in modern civil society.

“People on the receiving end are bound to feel violated, unsafe and insulted.

“You need to change your ways.

“We live in a multi-ethnic, multi-racial and multi-denominational society and we all should live in harmony.”

The judge told Wilson had he been dealt with nearer the time he probably would have received a different sentence.

“It happened two years ago and there has been nothing of this nature since, but he has received both suspended and community sentences in the meantime for unrelated matters.

Passing a 12-month community order, the judge ordered the defendant to complete 40 hours’ unpaid work, attend 15 rehabilitation activity days overseen by the Probation Service, and pay £250 compensation to the victim.

He was also ordered to pay £150 costs of the case, bringing the overall bill to £400.

The judge put in place a restraining order prohibiting Wilson from contacting or approaching the victim, or from going to Market Crescent, Wingate, for 12 months.
