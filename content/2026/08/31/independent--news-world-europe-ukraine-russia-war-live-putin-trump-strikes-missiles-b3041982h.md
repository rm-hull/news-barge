Russian strikes have destroyed nearly 12 million books in Ukraine, with forces targeting printing presses and publishing houses in recent attacks.

Hundreds of schools have been affected in the strikes ahead of the academic year on Tuesday as around nine per cent of new textbooks have been burned, Pravda reported.

The devastating attacks wiped out around 1.5 million books in July and 10 million in August, according to the newspaper.

Viktoria Haida, the commercial director of the Konvi publishing firm in Kyiv, which was bombed, told BBC: “I believe it is deliberate on the part of the Russian Federation.”

Meanwhile, Volodymyr Zelensky has promised to respond after Russia subjected Kyiv to relentless strikes over five consecutive days.

Overnight on Saturday, 38 people were killed after an attack on an ammunition depot in Kyiv triggered detonations and a large fire.

It comes as Russia’s defence ministry has announced it is preparing “massive strikes” on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure in retaliation for a drone campaign by Kyiv attacking warehouses and oil and gas facilities.

## Russia's jet-powered drones bring empty Kyiv supermarket shelves and rattle Ukrainians

Russian forces relentlessly battered Kyiv and cities in the region around the Ukrainian capital for a fifth straight day Monday, leaving some supermarket shelves bare after repeated strikes on warehouses as Ukraine reels from attacks with jet-powered drones.

Russia has intensified its aerial attacks in recent months, more than four years after it invaded its neighbor. Almost constant air raid alerts in the Kyiv region in recent days have severely disrupted daily routines. Two major attacks in July killed almost 50 civilians, and a strike on Saturday killed at least 38 people.

Classes were due to resume at Ukrainian schools on Tuesday, but it was unclear whether parents would send their children. Schools have air raid shelters, and classes can also be held online.

“Right now, Russia’s tactic is to wear down Ukraine and Ukrainians — first and foremost, our people,” Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on social media late Sunday.

Ukraine has also tried to make Russian civilians feel the burden of war by hammering major online retailers and oil facilities. The attacks have brought shortages for ordinary Russians, though its strikes have not been as deadly for civilians.

India’s prime minister has urged ally Vladimir Putin to end the war in Ukraine, saying his country backed every peace effort, Russian media reported.

Iranian president Masoud Pezeshkian and Chinese leader Xi Jinping are also attended the annual two-day summit, which kicked off on Monday.

## EU naval forces board sixth suspected Russian 'shadow fleet' tanker

European Union naval forces have intercepted another suspect oil tanker, marking what EU chief diplomat Kaja Kallas confirmed as the "sixth suspected shadow fleet vessel boarded by our EU operations in recent months."

Executed under maritime security missions like Operation IRINI and Operation ATALANTA, the high-seas boardings conduct "flag verification" checks on unflagged, falsely flagged, or uninsured vessels operating outside standard maritime law to skirt G7 price caps.

It is widely believed that Russia’s shadow fleet consists of hundreds of tankers, transporting crude oil off-grid to evade energy sanctions.

## US coaches Polish forces in a drone-focused drill based on lessons from Ukraine

On a moonless and rainy night, a group of soldiers emerged from a ramshackle building meant to resemble a church. The alpine terrain loomed over them as they quietly set off for the tree line of the German countryside a short distance away.

The soldiers are members of the Polish special forces and are taking part in an exercise designed only months ago that simulates moving through enemy lines, traversing miles of enemy territory and launching a drone strike on a key enemy location.

Initially designed by the Army's Green Berets for U.S. special forces, the exercise that began Friday is informed by and simulates many of the challenges that Ukrainian troops have experienced in their conflict with Russia. It is the first time a NATO ally like Poland has undertaken the training, and it comes at a time when tensions between Russian President Vladimir Putin and NATO countries have escalated.

“We’re using lessons learned from Ukraine,” an Army Green Beret officer assigned to the training complex said Thursday. “All of those effects that we’re seeing either Russia or Ukraine having to overcome — we’re doing our best to replicate.”

The Associated Press received rare access to both the training ground and the troops conducting the exercise, which has only been conducted twice before, both times this year, by two different Green Beret teams.

## Russia suffered 1,600 casualties on Sunday, Ukraine says

Russia suffered 1,600 troop losses in Ukraine on Sunday, its armed forces has claimed, bringing the total casualties to 1,488,200.

It estimates that Vladimir Putin has also lost 12,316 tanks, 25,255 armoured fighting vehicles, and 48,883 artillery systems in Ukraine since the war began.

## Another town falls on Ukraine’s ‘fortress belt’ as Russia creeps closer

Charred vehicles line empty roads littered with the debris of destroyed buildings, while the thud of artillery fire gives way to the sharp, high-pitched hum of an attack drone.

This is today's reality in the eastern Ukrainian town of Druzhkivka, where a remnant of civilians live alongside troops fighting Russian forces who are now only 15 km (nine miles) away and steadily edging closer.

Widespread ruin of this nature is mirrored along the entire 1,200-km (746-mile) front line across the east and south. As Russian forces push ahead, apartment blocks, local markets and shops are flattened, rendering whole communities virtually uninhabitable.

"I can't sleep," said Iryna Litovchenko, one of a handful of civilians who have stayed. "Some guys took us in - strangers," added the 56-year-old, walking through broken glass and debris beside badly damaged buildings.

"My home is gone, as if it never existed. There were so many good things, so much of everything. In one fell swoop – boom — it's as if nothing's left."

## Russia hit Ukraine with nearly 2,000 drones last week, says Zelensky

Russia has launched nearly 2,000 strike drones, more than 1,600 aerial bombs, and 31 missiles in Ukraine this week, its president said on Sunday.

Volodymyr Zelensky said strikes were “virtually constant”in Nikopol, Kramatorsk, Kherson, and communities in the Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Donetsk, and Sumy regions.

“We are seeking just responses to every Russian strike and crime. They will certainly come,” he said.

## Russia says it will keep Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant supplied with diesel but external power must be restored

Russia's Rosatom state nuclear corporation said on Monday that its priority was to keep the Russian-controlled Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant in southeast Ukraine supplied with diesel fuel in the absence of an external power source.

Alexei Likhachev, Rosatom's head, said in a statement that the situation at the plant was under full control as it was kept functioning on its backup diesel generators.

But he said such a situation could not go on indefinitely and that it was essential to restore external power lines.

He said he hoped that a local ceasefire could be arranged to pause fighting between Russia and Ukraine in order to carry out repairs to the Ferrosplavnaya-1 power line, which supplies electricity to the plant.
