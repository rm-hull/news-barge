A medieval burial ground in the North East, where more than half of those laid to rest were children, is set to be explored in a day of talks.

The site, located near Hartlepool, will be discussed during Tees Archaeology Day on Saturday, October 3, at Stockton Central Library.

The event will delve into recent early medieval research from across northern England.

A major focus of the day will be the Hart Manor site near Hartlepool, where a 2016 excavation by environmental consultancy Cura Terrae uncovered 365 burials.

The remains included infants, adults, and multiple graves, painting a detailed picture of life, health, and mortality in the early medieval period.

More than half of those buried were children, many of whom showed signs of childhood illness and nutritional deficiencies such as scurvy and rickets.

Some graves also reflected the risks associated with pregnancy and childbirth.

Adults who survived childhood often reached mature age, though their skeletons bore marks of a demanding life.

Evidence of manual labour, accidents, and repeated injuries was common, along with signs of violent conflict.

Thirteen adults showed injuries sustained around the time of death, including blade wounds and decapitation, suggesting a possible raid or violent event.

The findings collectively reveal a community shaped by vulnerability, hard work, disease, ageing, and violence.

Tees Archaeology Day will run from 9.30am to 4pm, with tickets priced at £25 for adults and £15 for students.

The ticket price includes tea, coffee, and a buffet lunch.

Bookings can be made through Hartlepool Art Gallery in Church Square by calling 01429 869706.

A second event, Tees Archaeology Fieldwalking, will take place at Marsh House Farm in Greatham on September 23 and 24.
