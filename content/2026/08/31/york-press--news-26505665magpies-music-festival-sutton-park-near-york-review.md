## Inside the sold-out festival near York where music makes it 'a most treasured event'

At The Magpies festival in North Yorkshire 2026. Photo by Esme Mai

  *Magpies music festival in Sutton Park near York – review by GARETH JOHN*

  *Photos by Esme Mai*

  *Date: Sutton Park, York 14-15 August 2026*

THE Magpies Festival has built its reputation on championing the best of contemporary folk music, and this year’s sold-out gathering at Sutton Park once again proved why it remains one of the most treasured events in the calendar.

Across a weekend blessed with sunshine, community spirit, outstanding musicianship and an attentive audience, this boutique festival’s programme started on Friday afternoon and concluded late on Saturday night with hundreds of campers staying the entire weekend.

I was fortunate enough to visit the festival on Saturday afternoon and early evening to take in some excellent performances by Blair Dunlop, Tarran and Ophelia’s Beard.

  At The Magpies festival in North Yorkshire 2026. Photo by Esme Mai


Blair Dunlop delivered the sort of performance that reminds you why he is regarded as one of the finest songwriters on the folk circuit and a Magpies Festival established favourite. Armed with a catalogue of strong and engaging songs including the Let's Get Out of the City, Trilobite and This Isn't Love (But It's Working), Dunlop balanced personal storytelling with wider observations on life and society as he held the audience in rapt attention from the opening number.

His guitar work was, as ever, exceptional, weaving intricate melodies beneath a warm and expressive vocal. There was a confidence and maturity to the set, but also a humility that connected effortlessly with the crowd. Whether playing a heartfelt ballad or something with a little more rhythmic drive, Dunlop made the festival audience feel close and personal.

  Blair Dunlop At the Magpies Festival in North Yorkshire 2026. Photo by Esme Mai


Tarran brought a different energy altogether, injecting youthful exuberance into the programme while never sacrificing musical sophistication. Their set was packed with inventive arrangements and beautifully delivered harmonies, creating a sound that felt both rooted in tradition and refreshingly modern. The audience response grew with every song as the trio displayed an obvious chemistry and enjoyment in performing together. There is an authenticity to Tarran’s music that resonates deeply, and their appearance at Magpies suggested a group with a very bright future ahead.

  At The Magpies festival in North Yorkshire 2026. Photo by Esme Mai


Meanwhile, Ophelia’s Beard offered one of the weekend’s most captivating performances. Their music carries echoes of folk’s rich heritage but is delivered with imagination and flair. Tight vocal harmonies reminded me of Indigo Girls and were complemented by skilled musicianship, producing a sound that was both polished and emotionally engaging. Moments of gentle humour between songs only strengthened the connection with the audience, who responded warmly throughout. Their set moved effortlessly between reflective pieces and more uplifting material, showcasing impressive versatility.

What united all these performances was their ability to celebrate folk music’s enduring power to tell stories and bring people together. In an age of increasingly disposable entertainment, Blair Dunlop, Tarran and Ophelia’s Beard demonstrated the value of craftsmanship, authenticity and live performance at its very best.

However, this was just one small section of a weekend packed with wonderful musicianship, excellent arts and crafts, dozens of traders serving lovely food and drink. As the festival drew to a close, the audience basked in the music and activities, providing a fitting reminder of why Magpies continues to be such a cherished destination for folk music lovers.
