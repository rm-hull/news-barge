House lawmakers return to Capitol Hill as Republicans hope to pass stopgap spending bill

The US House is back after a five-week recess. And this week, Republicans are focused a key legislative agenda item: passing a stopgap spending bill to stave off a possible government shutdown.

Currently, funding is guaranteed through 30 September, but after a year marked by record-breaking shutdowns, GOP House speaker Mike Johnson is hoping to advance a bill that would extend funding spending at its current levels until after the November midterms.

Johnson is expected to put a vote on the House floor this week, using a fast-track procedure that requires a two-thirds majority to pass.

Prior to the Senate heading home for their August recess, the upper chamber approved its own continuing resolution to keep the government until 11 December, this also included provisions like preventing political appointees having approvals over federal grants.

The House is set to leave Washington again at the end of this week for Labor Day recess, provided it can get through its to-do list.

Trump says 'only reason' Americans would oppose datacenters is if they 'want to end up being backwards and poor'

In another Truth Social missive today, Donald Trump has taken aim at the Americans who oppose the construction of datacenters – the power-demanding warehouses that tech companies use to house their computer servers and systems.

The president said that the “only reason” communities would not want datacenters is “if they want to end up being backwards and poor”.

“If they want to be successful and rich, with far lower taxes and jobs all over the place, let Data Reign,” Trump wrote. “The good news is that there are plenty of other places that want them. If we kill the Golden Goose, you will only have yourselves to blame.”

Across the country anti-datacenter animus has galvanized many areas – both Democratic and Republican. According to recent polling, 75% of Americans oppose a new datacenter being built near where they live.

Meanwhile, more than a dozen states have weighed temporary bans on new datacenter construction, and New York last month became the first to enact one. Progressive stalwarts Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio‑Cortez are now pushing for a national moratorium. And in Texas, even Governor Greg Abbott – a hard‑right Republican – has called for halting datacenter development in rural parts of the state.

Trump, for his part, is politically boxed in: he’s pushing to make the US a leader in artificial‑intelligence innovation – a goal that depends on rapid datacenter expansion – while also promising to bring down electricity costs for consumers, who are already facing higher bills.

“China could not be happier with this anti‑Data Center movement,” Trump wrote in a social‑media post. “Actually, they can’t believe it is happening!”

As my colleague, Dharna Noor has reported, so far the president’s answer to this bind is the “Ratepayer Protection Pledge” – a voluntary agreement he says will prevent families from paying for the AI boom. Nearly 200 companies, utilities, datacenter developers and states have signed on, the administration announced last month.
