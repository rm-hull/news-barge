## Southern Water seeks tighter crackdown on water use, warning of household curbs

Southern Water has applied for a rare non-essential-use ban drought order, which, if imposed, would mean businesses must stop using water for activities such as cleaning cars and filling non-domestic swimming pools.

In its application, the firm said an exceptional shortage of rain in the South East demonstrated “a clear threat” to supplies within the Hampshire Southampton West water resource zone, as well as the Isle of Wight (IOW).

It added that 165,000 people in the Southampton West supply area could soon be significantly affected by a restricted water supply.

The application will be considered by Environment Secretary Angela Eagle, and a decision is expected soon.

A non-essential use ban has not been imposed for 20 years. The last one was granted to Sutton and East Surrey Water in May 2006.

## Firefighters' leader says equipping crews should be national priority

Firefighters are under extra pressure due to an “unprecedented summer of dangerous and volatile wildfires”, the Fire Brigades Union says.

The union said it was a “national priority” to have enough properly equipped firefighters.

July was the busiest month on record for wildfires, statistics show, after firefighters attended 458 cases last month.

More than 120 firefighters are tackling a blaze in the New Forest in Hampshire that began on Sunday, and Nottinghamshire fire and rescue service declared a major incident on Sunday as two large fires in woodland near Mansfield put a significant strain on resources there.

Emergency calls to the London fire brigade have increased by almost a third this summer, according to a briefing seen by *The Guardian*. 

The union says there are 12,000 fewer firefighters now than in 2010.

Steve Wright, the FBU general secretary, said: “Our country is facing unpredictable environmental challenges, and it must be a national priority to ensure we have enough properly equipped firefighters ready to face these threats.”

## Climate crisis demands fundamental rethink of farming, says farming group

The UK needs a fundamental rethink of how farmland is managed if it is to cope with increasingly severe floods, droughts and heatwaves driven by continued fossil fuel use, the Nature Friendly Farming Network (NFFN) says.

After another year of record-breaking heatwaves and droughts, the farmer-led organisation warns that short-term responses to individual weather events are no longer enough. Instead, farming policy and corporate investment must focus on long-term resilience.

The NFFN says farmers need to recognise water as their most valuable resource, prioritising healthy soils that can absorb and retain rainfall, alongside creating more on-farm ponds and reservoirs to store water for prolonged dry periods.

The organisation is calling on the Government and the private sector to provide greater financial support for water infrastructure and simplify the planning process for work with clear environmental outcomes.

## Water mains bursts leaves taps dry in homes

Water stations opened in Ilford, east London, and Chigwell, Essex, after burst water mains left thousands of homes and businesses with no water or low water pressure.

The affected areas are: E10, E11, E17, E18, E4, IG10, IG2, IG4, IG5, IG6, IG7, IG8, IG9 and RM4.

Thames Water said: “Our teams continue to work in the extreme heat to fix the multiple broken pipes in the area. We’re sorry to those who are still affected.

“Whilst progress is being made, it’s taking longer than normal to fix the broken pipes as often excavations are needing to be extended.”
