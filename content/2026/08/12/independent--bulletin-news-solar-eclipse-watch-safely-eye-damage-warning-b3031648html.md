# Why you should never look at the solar eclipse directly – even for a moment

- Britons have been warned that even brief exposure to the sun during the upcoming partial solar eclipse on 12 August can cause permanent vision damage.
- Royal Museums Greenwich said that it will be the most complete solar eclipse visible in the UK since August 1999, an event that resulted in 70 reported cases of visual loss.
- Experts from the Royal College of Ophthalmologists warned that retinal damage is painless, meaning symptoms such as central blind spots or blurred vision may only appear hours later.
- Standard sunglasses, cameras and binoculars do not offer adequate protection, so experts recommend using verified ISO-certified eclipse glasses, indirect projection, or watching live broadcasts.
- The eclipse will be visible across the UK between 6.08pm and 8.10pm on 12 August, reaching its peak between 7.05pm and 7.16pm.
