Prime minister Andy Burnham will chair a meeting of the government's Cobra emergency committee this afternoon in response to the heat, the wildfires it has fuelled and the drought now covering much of the country.

It is the second Cobra meeting on the heat this year, after one held during the June heatwave. Temperatures are forecast to reach 33C today and up to 38C tomorrow.

A Downing Street spokesperson said the government understood that firefighters, farmers and NHS staff were all facing pressures connected to the weather.

On Tuesday a firefighter union chief warned the country faces a “national emergency” amid ongoing wildfires.

Southern Water is pushing for a rare drought order which would ban the filling of non-domestic swimming pools.

The firm said an “exceptional” shortage of rain in the South East “demonstrates a clear threat to supplies” in Hampshire and the Isle of Wight. 165,000 people in the Southampton West supply area could “soon be significantly affected” by a restricted water supply, it added.

Thames Water said that thousands of homes and businesses have now been left without water or with low pressure after pipes burst in London and Essex.

## Stay away from Dunwich Heath amid ongoing fire risk, warns National Trust

The National Trust has warned people to stay away from Dunwich Heath until the area has been assessed following a devastating wildfire, amid a risk of further flare-ups.

More than 100 hectares of heathland habitat have been destroyed by fire at the Suffolk reserve since it took hold almost two weeks ago, the conservation charity said.

The fire service said on Monday that the fire has been stood down as a major incident but crews remain on scene damping down the affected area and monitoring for hotspots.

Coastal winds are stirring up ash and debris, rabbit burrows and warrens have retained significant heat and trees with burnt root systems are vulnerable to collapse, the National Trust said.Parts of the landscape have been left unrecognisable, making it harder to navigate, and fires spread along cliffs putting their stability at risk and making the beach below unsafe.

Russell Clement, general manager for the National Trust, praised all of Suffolk’s 35 fire stations, the 18 fire services from around the country and others who “worked tirelessly to tackle this fire”.

The National Trust said there are still parts of the fire ground that they have not yet been able to access to map and record hazards.

## Grid operator issues warning amid solar eclipse and heatwave

The National Energy System Operator (NESO) has issued an appeal for extra electricity supply or for energy users to power down this evening as temperatures crank up today, and amid a solar eclipse this evening.

Octopus Energy has also asked customers to power down during the eclipse from 6-8pm today, when the moon will block more than 90 per cent of sunlight.

This will, in turn, lower solar-powered electricity generation just as demand for energy surges as people finish up work and are at home for the evening.

## Remains of lost village uncovered as drought lowers reservoir levels in record-breaking heatwave

The remains of a lost village demolished and flooded over 50 years ago have been resurfaced as England’s back-to-back heatwaves and dry weather continue to affect water levels.

Nether Hambleton, in the East Midlands, was submerged to create Rutland Water reservoir in the 1970s. It became Europe’s largest man-made lake at the time, with over 3,000 acres of land flooded.

Exceptionally low water levels in the reservoir have now revealed stonework and foundations of the lost buildings, around half a century after they were demolished.

The village was part of the wider parish of Hambleton, which now exists only as Upper Hambleton in the heart of the peninsula at the centre of the reservoir.

## Head of Fire Brigades Union warns UK faces 'emergency'

Steve Wright, general secretary of the Fire Brigades Union (FBU), warned the UK faces a national emergency “right now” with fires across the country.

Firefighters have responded to 966 wildfires across England and Wales this year, including 185 in the first 10 days of August alone, while July was the busiest month on record for wildfires with 458 incidents, the National Fire Chiefs Council (NFCC) said.

Crews from Hampshire and Isle of Wight Fire and Rescue Service have battled a fire in the New Forest, which started after a van caught fire on the A31 near Ringwood on Sunday.

The area hit by the blaze is protected heathland and home to a number of rare species, according to the New Forest National Park Authority.

Mr Wright told Times Radio: “In many parts of the country, firefighters do not have the new modern equipment to deal with these.

“They do not have the new modern personal protective equipment, the lightweight fire kit that we wear, the right helmets, lightweight helmets, breathable uniforms to make sure they’re better protected, their health is protected when they’re out dealing with these incidents for hours and hours on end.”

## Expert responds to Burnham's emergency Cobra meeting

In response to Andy Burnham chairing a meeting of the government’s emergency Cobra committee following another heatwave, Robert Palmer – deputy director at Uplift – says new oil and gas drilling needs to stop.

“If his government is going to properly face up to the threat of climate change it must stop major new oil and gas drilling such as the Rosebank field.

“This decision is the first major climate test for the government. They are literally playing with people’s lives if they sanction more drilling which will wreck our planet and cause this dangerous heat.

“Climate Change is clearly devastating the UK, with nearly three-quarters of the country in drought, a fifth heatwave on the way in the south of England and severe health alerts. Heartbreakingly, this is after the sweltering heat has already killed thousands already this summer.

“It is also a horrific economic burden in terms of dealing with wildfires, ruined crops and fears that food bills are set to rocket.

“We need leaders that protect the public’s health, their need for lower bills and a safe climate ahead of oil company profits, and speed up the shift to clean energy to stop the heat once and for all.”

## 'We need to make sure our national infrastructure is able to cope', minister says ahead of emergency Cobra

Today's Cobra meeting is aimed at ensuring the UK's "national resilience is one step ahead of the situation" and Britain's "national infrastructure is able to cope", a government minister has said.

Asked whether the Cobra meeting is coming too late, after a series of wildfires and drought across the country, immigration minister Anna Turley told Sky News: "No, we've kept the situation monitored closely throughout the summer, and I know that you know the cabinet have all been working closely on this.

"So it's been really important that we maintain our pressure on this."

She added: "But I think it's right that the prime minister, has called a Cobra today because we need to make sure all of our national infrastructure is able to cope...

"We've been supporting farmers. We've been ensuring the railways are working smoothly.

"But the priority, as I said, to make sure now that with Cobra, we really make sure that our national resilience is really one step ahead of the situation."

## Temperatures more than 2C above average this summer

Provisional statistics from the Met Office show that the mean temperature for the summer so far, from 1 June, stands at 16.48C – which is 1.88C above the 1991 to 2020 average.

To equal the 16.12C in 2025, the UK’s warmest summer on record, the rest of August would only need to be 0.21C below the long-term average, and any warmer than this would establish a new record for the country, the Met Office said.

And for England and Wales, which have seen temperatures more than 2C above average so far this summer, it is even more likely that June to August 2026 will be record-breaking.

And in a summer that has been marked by extreme weather, temperatures are to spike again this week.

## Temperatures could reach as high as 38C on Thursday

Temperatures could hit 33C on Wednesday before potentially reaching highs of 37C or 38C on Thursday, the Met Office say.

Met Office chief forecaster Chris Bulmer said: “While the peak heat will be more short-lived than recent *heatwaves*, temperatures widely into the mid-30s Celsius on Thursday brings the potential for impacts for some, which is why a Met Office warning has been issued.

“High pressure is now becoming dominant and temperatures will build day by day. Southerly and south-easterly winds will also tap into the hot conditions over Europe which helps develop this further notable heat in what has been a hot and dry summer so far for many in the south of the UK.”

## Back to back heatwaves raising flood risk, experts warn

Months of heat have left soil so dry and compacted that it will struggle to absorb rain when it returns, raising the risk of flash flooding within days of the drought breaking, researchers have warned.

"We can move from drought conditions to flooding in a very short space of time," said Jeff Da Costa, a researcher in hydrometeorological hazards at the University of Reading. "Flood preparedness needs to be ongoing because the risk is very real, even during a drought."

Rain returning will not end the drought either, he said. River levels recover relatively quickly while groundwater, reservoirs and soils take far longer, meaning the landscape can look green again and rivers can rise while serious water deficits remain underneath.

Yanchen Zheng, a senior research associate in hydrological modelling at the University of Bristol, also said hot weather drives water demand up at exactly the point when rivers and aquifers are lowest, and that recovery can take months or years. Current models tend to overestimate how much water is left in rivers, she said, a problem most acute in south-east England, which is densely populated, depends heavily on groundwater and is already short of water.
