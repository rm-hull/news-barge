A North East teenager whose story prompted a huge rise in organ donor registrations after it was featured on BBC’s The One Show has revealed her ambition to become a transplant nurse, a decade after The One Show reported on her heart transplant.

Scarlett Ungurs, now 15, is the focus of a special anniversary film on The One Show revisiting the impact of her story.

It also explores the legacy of donor Evey Staley, who died aged 10. Evey’s organs were donated by her parents after she died in a 2012 drink-driving crash.

  North East teen Scarlett Ungurs, whose BBC One Show story boosted organ donor registrations, has revealed her ambition to become a transplant nurse 10 years after her life-saving heart transplant. 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Scarlett, of North Tyneside, said: "I understand how big it is now, it’s always been a part of me.

"On heart anniversaries, on her anniversary of when she passed away, and we’re still in touch with the family so when we celebrate me, we celebrate her."

Ten years ago, The One Show reported on Scarlett’s story, revealing how she received a donor heart just in time to save her life.

The episode led to a spike in organ donor registrations, with a 70 per cent rise reported after it aired.

Evey’s parents, Neal and Penny Staley, made the decision to donate their daughter’s organs in the midst of unimaginable loss.

Mr Staley said at the time: "I just made the decision that yes we’ll go ahead with organ donation. It was as simple as that really."

  Kept Alive: 18-month-old Scarlett Ungurs, with dad Darren, and transplant co-ordinator Lynne Holt, pictured when she needed a new heart 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The two families later met in an emotional reunion. Mr Staley said meeting Scarlett reinforced their choice.

He said: "It’s very special and just reinforces the decision we made, we’re proud of Evey really for giving Scarlett the life she’s got now.

"She’s just fabulous."

A decade on, Scarlett remains deeply conscious of the gift she received and stays in contact with the Staleys.

She described how important those connections remain.

She said: "On heart anniversaries, on her anniversary of when she passed away, and we’re still in touch with the family so when we celebrate me, we celebrate her."

The experience has also shaped her ambitions.

She said: "This is where I had my transplant.

"I’d love to be a paediatric nurse back on Ward 23 when I’m older because they’ve just inspired me.

"All the staff here are so caring and it is like a second home.

"Just makes me want to do the same as them."

During the anniversary film, Scarlett and The One Show revisit Ward 23, where she recovered following her transplant.

They meet Julie, one of the transplant nurses who has known Scarlett since she was a baby.

Julie said: "It certainly has its challenges at times but then you see people like Scarlett who has so many wonderful things to look forward to, it’s fantastic."

Scarlett said the transplant was the 'greatest gift' she has ever received.

She said: "I just feel very fortunate because I could have had a very different outcome so I’m going to make the most of it."

Scarlett’s mother previously described the Staleys’ decision to donate Evey’s organs as the reason her daughter is alive today.

When Evey’s mother read an article about a girl from the North East receiving a heart transplant in August 2012, she instinctively knew it was her daughter’s heart.

The Staley and Ungurs families were later connected through NHS Blood and Transplant.

Their meeting and Scarlett’s story drew national attention to the life-saving impact of organ donation.

During the original broadcast of Scarlett’s story, thousands more people joined the NHS Organ Donor Register.

At the time, Mr Staley said one reason the family spoke publicly was to encourage others to talk about organ donation and consider signing up.

- The One Show’s feature on Scarlett aired on Monday August 10 on BBC One and BBC iPlayer as part of the week of celebrations of 20 Years of The One Show.
