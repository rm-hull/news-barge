To watch or record live programmes on TV, a computer, or any other device, or use BBC iPlayer, you will need to have a TV licence.

This year, the cost of a TV licence was increased to £180 per year, which is £15 per month if you decide to pay it monthly.

If you watch live TV without having a TV licence, you could be visited by a TV licence inspector and fined up to £1,000 if you get caught.

There are two instances where people can get a discounted TV licence, so it is worth checking if you qualify.

  You need a TV licence to be able to watch live TV and BBC iPlayer. 

*(Image: Getty)*

## Can I get a discounted TV licence?

You can get a TV licence for a discounted price if you are registered blind or live with someone who is.

The government website says: "The licence must be in the blind person’s name - if it’s not, you can make a new application to transfer it into their name.

"You’ll need to provide your existing TV licence number when you apply."

You can get the TV licence for 50% of its normal price if you do this.

To apply for a TV licence if you are registered blind, you should go to the TV licensing website.

You can also get a TV licence at a lower price if you live in residential care or sheltered accommodation.

If you are living in a residential care home, supported housing, or sheltered accommodation, you must all be either retired and over 60 or disabled.


You will only have to pay £7.50 per year for the TV licence.

Your housing manager can check if you are eligible and apply for you.

### 
  **What can I watch without a TV licence?**

If you do not want to pay for a TV licence, there are still several services you can watch shows on.

While you will need to pay if you want to watch something on BBC iPlayer, you do not need a TV licence to watch on-demand TV through services like All 4 and Amazon Prime Video.

You can also use streaming services like Netflix and Disney Plus as well as watching videos on YouTube.

You also can watch DVDs for free.
