Australia politics live: tech giants face law to pay for news content; Australia joins call for Iran to end death penalty

News bargaining bill expected to pass parliament after Labor and Coalition agree on terms that will force tech companies to contribute to media outlets. Follow today’s news live

Tech companies including Meta, the owner of Facebook, face new laws requiring them to contribute some of their Australian revenue to support local media. Photograph: Jenny Kane/AP

The news bargaining incentive bill will pass parliament after a deal between Labor and the Coalition, which will see tech giants forced to make more total deals with news publishers and a special deal for the Australian Associated Press newswire.

The NBI, which is designed to force tech companies such as Facebook and TikTok to pay media outlets for the value they derive from news on their platforms, will be introduced into parliament today.

After negotiations with the opposition, Labor will amend the bill to require tech giants to make a minimum of eight deals with news publishers – up from the previous six. The design of the scheme will also mean big tech platforms can only offset a maximum of 25% of their exposure to the levy with each deal, which is to incentivise deals being made with a range of outlets.

Additionally, 5% of funds will be directed to AAP, the news wire service.

“The Albanese government is delivering for journalists and ensuring they get their fair share from digital platforms that benefit from Australian news,” said the communications minister, Anika Wells.

The assistant treasurer, Daniel Mulino, called it a “significant day for the future of Australian journalism.”

“We want to see deals between digital platforms and a diverse range of media organisations to ensure fair recompense for the use of content,” he said.

Australia joins condemnation of Iran’s use of capital punishment

Australia has joined more than 30 nations, including France, the United Kingdom and Canada, in condemning Iran’s use of capital punishment, saying in a joint statement the Islamic republic was executing protesters to “silence dissent”.

AFP reports that the countries, joined by the European Union’s top diplomat, “condemn in the strongest terms the ongoing executions of protesters and use of the death penalty” by Iran.

“The use of capital punishment to silence dissent, intimidate communities and punish individuals exercising their human rights can never be justified,” the statement said.

New Zealand, Ireland, Italy, Germany and numerous other European nations were also among those calling for Iran “to immediately end the use of the death penalty and release all those arbitrarily detained”.

The statement joins growing alarm from the United Nations and rights groups over a surge in executions in recent months.

According to the UN, since 19 March, at least 56 people have been executed on national security-related charges, including 27 people in cases linked to the protests.

The UN and rights groups have warned of a lack of fair trials and forced confessions.

Thousands of students are expected to rally in Students Say No to Pauline Hanson protests across major cities and towns, AAP reports.

“I’m hoping this will be the start of a real kind of movement,” said Australian National University student Yerin Park, who is organising the Canberra protest.

“We want to make sure we lay the best basis for … an anti-racist, anti-sexist, anti-bigot response in Australia.”

Rally flyers distributed by the National Union of Students include calls to “fight against racism” and “stand up against the far right”.

Hanson, the leader of One Nation, used her June National Press Club address to call for a monocultural Australia before appearing on a podcast hosted by British far-right activist and convicted criminal Tommy Robinson during a work trip to the UK last month.

Hanson’s track record shows she has voted against pro-education policies such as free Tafe and increasing funding for public schools, the union’s general secretary, Akash Nagarajan, said.

“Pauline Hanson actually doesn’t stand for things that benefit students’ lives … [and] a lot of students are galvanised by that,” the 21-year-old said.

Rikkie-Lee Tyrrell, One Nation’s only member in Victorian parliament, said everyone had the right to protest but expressed concern about who was behind the movement.

“Students really should be focusing on academics and I think the Socialist party getting involved with students is the wrong way to go,” she told reporters.
