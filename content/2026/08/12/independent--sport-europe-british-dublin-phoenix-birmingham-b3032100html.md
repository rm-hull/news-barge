Cricket fans at Edgbaston were among sporting spectators given a stunning view of Wednesday evening’s partial solar eclipse.

The sky took on an eerie darkness as the Moon obscured more than 90 per cent of the Sun, leaving only a slim crescent of light visible.

Spectators watching Birmingham Phoenix face MI London in The Hundred were able to take in the spectacle from the stands, with many wearing special glasses.

![Spectators in the stands uses special eclipse glasses to watch the solar eclipse (Jacob King/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/12/20/e6c1c75afb5c4a14b5956b3f3435e4ddY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg2NjQ3NTMx-2.85546293.jpg)

The eclipse was the fullest above British skies since the total eclipse in 1999.

Other sporting events also took place against the unusual backdrop.

Floodlights were turned on in anticipation at the European Athletics Championships in Birmingham.

Small groups, including Swedish athletes Erika Warff and Tilde Bjerager, gathered outside Alexander Stadium to observe the phenomenon, congregating on both sides of the terrace below the west stand.

![Stewards wearing welding helmets at Croke Park (Niall Carson/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/12/21/bdfd243476d30d64202bc884066a2184Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg2NjUxNDcx-2.85547245.jpg)

Some were prepared with protective paper glasses while others watched through their phone cameras, although the rare occurrence was already obscured by clouds when it peaked at around 7.15pm.

Stewards in Dublin, where Manchester United were facing Leeds at Croke Park in a pre-season friendly, were pictured watching the eclipse through welding helmets.

In Salzburg, football fans attending the UEFA Super Cup between Aston Villa and European champions Paris St Germain also gazed skywards.

The eclipse took place around half an hour before kick-off in the Austrian city.
