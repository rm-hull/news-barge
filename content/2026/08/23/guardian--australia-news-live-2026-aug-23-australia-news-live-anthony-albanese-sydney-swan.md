Ben Carroll rejects Victorian Liberals’ proposal to split court of appealas ‘thought bubble’

Victoria’s premier Ben Carroll was asked this morning about the Liberal party’s proposal to split the court of appeal in two and appoint new judges if they win the election.

He says the Labor government has already reformed bail laws and is reviewing sentencing, leaving the Liberals “floundering”. Carroll went on:

The Liberal party are upset that we’ve captured the law and order agenda. And now they’re saying we’re going to skip that process and go to the court of appeal. That’s why they’ve had this thought bubble overnight, and they will not be able to explain it. That’s the simple truth … Let’s wait and see what all the legal stakeholders say about Mr Newbury’s new policy.

Carroll, who is trained as a lawyer, questioned how the Liberal party would reform a court that “has been around [for] 200 years, and rewrite case law overnight”. He said:

It shows you the Liberal party today have no legal expertise, but [are] totally at sea on having a policy agenda for the next election, and are floundering. We’ve seen today the Liberal party searching for a policy idea and not doing any homework.

Victorian Labor expands on free dental care election promise

The Victorian premier, Ben Carroll, and health minister, Ingrid Stitt, have held a press conference this morning off the back of their announcement yesterday that the Labor government would open 10 free public dental clinics if re-elected in November.

Today, they’ve announced $12.9m to grow and retain the workforce required to operate the 10 clinics. The government estimates it will need 180 full-time equivalent workers to keep the 10 clinics running from 9am to 9pm on weekdays and 9am to 5pm on weekends.

Stitt said the funds will include $2.1m for relocation support for 20 dental clinicians for the three regional clinics, $3.8m to fund 30 dental assistant traineeships and 20 dental graduate placements for the next two years and $4.4m to attract and retain existing staff.

Carroll said that while some Victorians can access public dental care, the group is limited:

You can’t use a Medicare card for dental unless you’ve got a healthcare card or you are a category of a particular citizen, it is very difficult ... I’ve been visiting a lot of dentists over the past few weeks. There [are] waiting lists. People are in enormous, unbearable pain.

This will be a cost of living measure but it will also make sure that we deal with the healthcare of some of the most vulnerable Victorians. This is really going to fill the gap that’s been there for decades.

Seal confirmed first mammal death from bird flu in Australia

Lisa Cox

A long-nosed fur seal has died from H5 bird flu in South Australia, marking the first confirmed case in a mammal on the Australian mainland.

The fur seal was found dead at Beachport on Wednesday on the state’s Limestone Coast.

It marks a fresh escalation in the spread of the virus days after the South Australian premier, Peter Malinauskas, announced three mass mortality events on offshore islands involving more than 1,000 greater crested terns.

The highly pathogenic strain of bird flu has killed millions of birds globally and crossed over into mammal species, including elephant seals.

Long-nosed fur seals and the endangered Australian sea lion are among the mammal species the federal government has assessed as facing very high risk from the virus.

The SA government has also confirmed a little penguin found at Kangaroo Island this week also tested positive for H5 bird flu.

A 46-year-old Melton man driving a stolen car has died following a fatal collision in East Melbourne overnight, according to Victoria police.

The man was seen in the Richmond area of Melbourne on Saturday night “erratically” driving a stolen white Subaru, which was bearing false plates and had been stolen from Glen Iris on 20 August, police said in a statement early this morning.

The man, reportedly travelling on the wrong side of the road at the time, then collided with two vehicles, one of which was a Mustang, near Punt Road and Boulton Parade at about 9pm.

He got out of the crashed vehicle and jumped into the Mustang before driving off, police said. The driver of the Mustang wasn’t injured and wasn’t in the vehicle at the time.

The man continued driving erratically in the stolen Mustang onto Barassi Way where he collided head on with a Range Rover and then into a Toyota Camry. He was ejected from the Mustang and died at the scene, police said.

The driver of the Range Rover received an injury to her arm and was taken to hospital. The other motorist was not injured.

Police are also following up reports the vehicle was seen driving erratically in Clarendon and Whiteman streets prior to the first collision, and have appealed for any witnesses to the collision or with dashcam vision to contact Crime Stoppers.

First exhibitions on display at new Powerhouse Parramatta museum in Sydney

Powerhouse Parramatta – a museum and gallery project 12 years and $915m in the making – has unveiled its inaugural exhibition program. The five exhibitions focus on “immersive” experiences, including 3D cityscape, a crystal tunnel and historic aeroplanes.

Powerhouse describes the forthcoming exhibitions as follows:

The Dark: an immersive exhibition created for children aged 4 – 12 years old. Developed in collaboration with creatives Kip Williams, Elizabeth Gadsby and Marg Horwell, The Dark is a bedroom, a crystal tunnel, a forest, and a gateway to a land of giants. It is magical with many things to be discovered one by one.

Es Devlin: The Turning is an exhibition of 5 major works by British artist, Es Devlin whose genre-defying work ranges from sculpture to film, architecture, language and performance. Her work immerses audiences into atmospheric environments that draw on Earth’s vast biodiversity, maths and our collective imaginations.

Overflow: A First Nations-led exhibition grounded in the living principles of Water Country, working with seven Aboriginal organisations to consider the importance and care of salt and freshwater systems throughout NSW. These stories are brought to life through the cinematic lens of acclaimed film-maker and Kamilaroi man Ivan Sen.

Task Eternal: will explore the intersection of design, innovation and technology and trace humanity’s quest to defy gravity, take flight and journey into the sky.

The Mall: across two storeys, the exhibition examines the history, evolution and cultural significance of the shopping mall, exploring Australian and global innovation across architecture, design, technology, engineering, and fashion.

The institution isn’t scheduled to open its doors until 7 November. Admission will be free, but visitors aged 16 or over will be charged a fee to see Task Eternal.

Lisa Havilah, Powerhouse’s chief executive said in a statement this morning:

Powerhouse Parramatta will redefine how museums connect with community, industry and learning. As Western Sydney’s first NSW cultural institution, it will be a place that celebrates the creativity, ambition and diversity of our communities, connecting local stories and knowledge with international innovation.

These five foundational exhibitions place audiences at the heart of the museum, bringing to life stories of technology, creativity and excellence. Together, they reflect our ambition to create a museum that amplifies community, creates new opportunities and helps define the future. We are deeply grateful to our patrons for their visionary support and for joining us on the journey to create one of the world’s great new museums. We can’t wait to open the doors this November.

Victorian Liberals announce plan for new appeals court to ensure tougher sentences

Benita Kolovos

The Victorian Liberals have announced they will split the court of appeal in two and appoint new judges if they win the election, saying a separate one for criminal matters would match “community expectations”.

The opposition leader, Jess Wilson, will today announce a plan for two separate appeal courts – criminal and civil – and appoint additional judges specialising in criminal matters to the former.

Jess Wilson. Photograph: Joel Carrett/AAP

The Coalition said the new court of criminal appeal would have “broad oversight across Victoria’s courts” to ensure “sentences for serious crime match community expectations” and give effect to new bail and sentencing laws they would enact before Christmas.

They argue the decisions of this court would then flow down through the entire system, pushing up sentences handed out in courts across the state.

Wilson said:

Our new criminal court will empower specialist criminal law judges to ensure sentencing of criminals meet community expectations. Each sentence they hand down will form part of case law going forward, helping to establish appropriate consequences for criminal actions.
