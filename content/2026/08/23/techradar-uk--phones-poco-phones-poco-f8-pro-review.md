### TechRadar Verdict

The Poco F8 Pro is a masterclass in making the right compromises. It's the best affordable flagship smartphone I’ve used in recent memory, and one that sets a benchmark for the upper-mid-range category. It delivers top-tier performance at an affordable price, while boasting a stunning 6.59-inch display, a massive battery, and incredibly fast charging. The everyday value here is extraordinary. There are compromises — namely with the ultra-wide, selfie, and telephoto cameras, the USB data speeds, and the lack of wireless charging — but the pros far outweigh the cons. For most people, this is all the phone they'll ever need.

#### Pros

- +Outstanding value for money
- +Vibrant, bright, and colorful display
- +Flagship-grade performance
- +Excellent battery and blistering 100W charging
- +Bose-tuned speakers are loud and crisp

#### Cons

- -HyperOS is still bogged down by ads and bloatware
- -No wireless charging or USB 3.0 fast speeds
- -Selfie camera maxes out at 1080p video

## Poco F8 Pro review: Two-minute review

 ![Poco F8 Pro in the hand in silver](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Fu7yemGvmJQncNu7ai3GZR.jpg) 


Over the past two decades, I’ve been lucky enough to test hundreds of smartphones from a range of brands around the world. Some of these brands receive the credit they’re due, others are criticized fairly or unfairly, and some fail to get the global recognition they deserve.

Xiaomi's sub-brand, Poco, falls into the latter category, and the Poco F8 Pro is another example of an extraordinary phone that should be talked about more than it is.

While its pricier sibling, the Poco F8 Ultra, packs true flagship specs and a stunning denim material finish, the Poco F8 Pro more closely follows Poco’s model: take the core features that every smartphone user needs, build a great overall experience, and offer it at a truly affordable price.

The Poco F8 Pro marries the first-gen flagship Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset with 12GB of RAM and either 256GB or 512GB of storage. Yes, this is not the second-gen Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 that powers many of the current best Android phones, but you’d be hard-pressed to notice the difference. The Poco F8 Pro flies through day-to-day tasks with flagship-level ease, and its Bose-tuned stereo speakers deliver the same great audio experience as its pricier sibling.

There’s also an excellent 6.59-inch 120Hz OLED display, which is my favorite size for a smartphone screen, and it’s wonderful to use, with a 3,500-nit peak brightness and excellent color reproduction. As is customary with Poco phones, there’s no skimping on battery life, either, with the F8 Pro packing a 6,210mAh battery that can last for multiple days, and 100W charging that’s among the fastest on any phone.

There’s also a 2.5x telephoto camera, a first for Poco’s Pro lineup, although it does lack OIS. That’s indicative of the Poco F8 Pro as a whole; yes, there are compromises — including a lack of wireless charging, occasional slowdowns under heavy gaming loads, sluggish USB speeds, and some bloatware in apps — but if you can live with them, this phone offers the best value-for-money in its price bracket.

## Poco F8 Pro review: price and availability

- **Launched for £549**
- **The current retail price in the UK is ~£449**
- **Not available in the US or Australia**

In terms of availability, the Poco F8 Pro falls into the "widely available outside the US" category, and if you’re in the UK, you’ll find it’s priced extremely aggressively (the phone *can* be imported to the US, but its network compatibility will be limited).

## Poco F8 Pro review: design

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ZyZMEkoX2sTwdmeMnpNGTj.jpg) 


- **Three colors**
- **Premium aluminum and glass design**
- **6.59-inch display is ergonomically friendly**

I mentioned earlier that 6.59 inches is my favorite smartphone screen size, and that’s because it's at the top end of the perfect 6.5-6.6-inch range. With these sizes, I’ve found you get the comfort benefits of a smaller phone like the Pixel 10 Pro while also getting the viewing benefits of larger flagships like the Galaxy 26 Ultra.

The F8 Pro strikes the perfect balance between those two phone sizes, and it pays off: it’s very comfortable to hold. Yes, it lacks the added grip of the denim leather finish on the Poco F8 Ultra, but the F8 Pro's is a more mature approach that appeals to everyone. Instead of a denim back, you get a flat aluminum frame paired with a frosted glass rear panel. It’s adult and functional, but deliberately less interesting than its flashier sibling.

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/pzBkFy3mfAYV2bD29J4ZSj.jpg) 


What makes this design more comfortable is the overall lack of heft: weighing 199g and measuring 8.1mm thick, this is a phone that’s lighter than most, and that pays dividends in the overall experience. What's more, despite being thinner and lighter than many other phones, the F8 Pro still offers full IP68 dust and water resistance; it's one of only a few devices to do so at this price point.

At the top of the phone, you’ll find the IR blaster, which is a common feature of Chinese-made phones, and it’s brilliant. I think every phone maker should bring back an IR blaster, as it’s a feature you don’t know you need until you lose your TV remote or need to bypass a locked-down hotel TV remote. Yes, the IR blaster has solved both of those problems for me.

- **Design score: 4.5 / 5**

## Poco F8 Pro review: display

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/EiTxLT9w8bFQLsFZywCbTj.jpg) 


- **6.59-inch 120Hz OLED display**
- **Flagship-grade color accuracy**

The F8 Pro's 6.59-inch display is not only the perfect size but also an excellent all-around panel. Poco has rightly recognized that the display is arguably the most important part of any phone, and here you get a vibrant, rich, and colorful display that’s a joy to use.

Poco claims the display can reach a peak brightness of 3,500 nits. In day-to-day use, it is apparent that this display isn’t just bright; it’s extremely colorful, with testing showing it covers 99.2% of the sRGB gamut and 96.5% of the DCI-P3 color space. This is flagship-level display performance, and it makes the Poco F8 Pro a champion for consuming content.

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/jUQfgnffqA7Y7MwW4DcnTj.jpg) 


Beyond that, there’s a 120Hz dynamic refresh rate. While it lacks the expensive LTPO technology found on £1,000+ flagships, the phone's software automatically cycles between 60Hz and 120Hz depending on the content you’re using. The higher refresh rate is perfect for super-smooth scrolling and gaming, but drops to save battery when you’re just reading a book or an article online.

The display is protected by Corning Gorilla Glass 7i, which offers strong durability against general wear and tear. Across my testing, I have had no concerns about the toughness of the F8 Pro's screen.

- **Display score: 4.5 / 5**

## Poco F8 Pro review: cameras

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/SZzvZaWY6EXgjpyXLfcYSj.jpg) 


- **50MP main sensor**
- **First Poco Pro with 2.5x optical telephoto**
- **8MP ultra-wide camera**

Poco’s rivals should take note of the Poco F8 Pro for several reasons — most of which are positive. Poco has traditionally ignored telephotos on its more affordable devices, but the Poco F8 Pro marks a shift in this area, likely driven by increased competition from triple-camera-toting rivals.

Although Poco has added a 50MP telephoto lens with 2.5x optical zoom, it lacks OIS, and the ultra-wide camera is an 8MP snapper that just about scrapes by. The 50MP Light Fusion 800 main camera sensor, meanwhile, takes excellent daylight photos with dynamic range and natural colors that look fantastic on the Poco F8 Pro screen.

The new telephoto lens does a decent job overall, but it’s nothing to write home about, and somewhat ironically, the 50MP main sensor enables a 2x in-sensor crop that can take better photos than the telephoto lens. The lack of OIS means you’ll get some blurry photos unless your hands are perfectly stable, so while it’s great that a telephoto lens exists, it won’t be as capable as those you'll find on a true flagship smartphone camera.

While the main rear camera can handle up to 4K video at 60 fps (and even 8K at 30 fps), the 20MP selfie camera is noticeably compromised, with Poco limiting selfie video capture to 1080p. These aren’t necessarily dealbreakers, but they are compromises you need to be conscious of.

## Poco F8 Pro review: performance

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qxC5fuF8RiavfsFfkMdToi.jpg) 


- **Flagship Snapdragon 8 Elite chipset**
- **12GB RAM + 256GB/512GB storage**
- **Excellent in-display fingerprint sensor**

If speedy performance is your jam, look no further: the Poco F8 Pro has the best processor you’ll find in a smartphone at this price.

The Poco F8 Pro uses the first-generation Snapdragon 8 Elite processor, which is paired with 12GB of RAM and either 256GB or 512GB of storage. This processor powered many of the best Android phones last year (including the Galaxy S25 Ultra), making it extremely powerful and far superior to the mid-range chipsets used by other phones at this price point.

During day-to-day use, the chipset absolutely shines. It’s fast to respond, shows no signs of slowing down, and often reminds me of the Duracell bunny in that it just keeps on going. If you want a phone that can handle most things you throw at it, this is the best package at this price point. It’s great for everything from everyday multitasking and social media to emulating Nintendo Switch games, though it struggles under the heaviest gaming loads.

 ![Poco F8 Pro in the hand in silver](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/9xedFtrU59C5N6VVvyheYR.jpg) 


Many phones aren’t limited by their internals as much as by how effectively phone makers manage thermals, and Poco firmly took the approach of raw power over cool operation in the F8 Pro. This means the aluminum frame and camera island can get a tad warm to the touch, but not to the point where it’s dangerous.

Where Poco does deserve credit is for the excellent in-display fingerprint sensor, which is fantastic even with wet or sweaty fingers. This is among the best in-display fingerprint sensors on any phone, and it’s wonderful that Poco chose not to skimp on this feature like many rivals in this category.

However, the F8 Pro is an exercise in acceptable compromise, so you do have to live with sluggish USB 2.0 wired speeds. Mind you, this is a trade-off I find acceptable, especially since you can use wireless transfer apps like LocalSend to easily send files to other devices or to your Mac or PC.

- **Performance score: 4.5 / 5**

## Poco F8 Pro review: software

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/jUQfgnffqA7Y7MwW4DcnTj.jpg) 


- **HyperOS 3.0 with Android 16**
- **4 Android updates and 5 years of security updates**
- **Bogged down by bloatware and ads**

Like all current Xiaomi phones, the Poco F8 Pro runs HyperOS 3.0 on top of Android 16. Depending on what you are used to, HyperOS 3.0 will either be comfortable or polarizing.

All phone makers are now forced to focus on longevity as well as features, especially as phones cost more and customers upgrade less frequently. For Poco, this presents an interesting challenge: it offers a respectable four years of Android version updates and five years of security updates, which provides some longevity, but it is known to be slow in pushing these updates out to users.

Look beyond this (and the final point below), and you’ll find that Xiaomi actually has some neat software features. Take Hyper Island, which looks and feels exactly like Apple’s Dynamic Island. It was inevitable that Xiaomi would be among the first to adopt (read: copy) this feature, and while the UX is unabashedly inspired by Apple, Xiaomi has executed it extremely well. Timers, voice recordings, and music playback all minimize into a dynamic bubble centered around the selfie camera.

Then there’s Hyper Connect, which makes even more sense in light of the above. Like many Android phone makers, Xiaomi is targeting Apple users, and Hyper Connect enables file sharing, clipboard syncing, and interoperability with Apple devices, as well as other Android phones and PCs.

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/noaoKGsiMBQXwbwW94imSj.jpg) 


Throughout this review, I’ve talked about compromises, and the biggest one comes in the software department. But first, some context: simply put, there isn’t much margin in making affordable smartphones, **especially** given the current AI-fueled RAM and storage shortage, which has increased the cost of these components by up to 10x.

What this actually means for you is that Poco had to either raise prices or offset higher costs with higher income from alternative revenue sources, such as ads. In native apps like Themes, Files, and Security, you’ll find annoying system-level ads that slow down the experience. There are also many preloaded apps, although you can at least remove these.

All of this serves as a reminder that this phone has compromises, which detract from the overall experience. However, the pragmatist in me also recognizes that companies like Poco already had tight margins before the current RAM crisis, which will have been eroded further, and if ads like these mean it’s possible to keep making phones like these, then this is a trade-off that becomes slightly more acceptable. I’d rather they didn’t exist, but I also recognize that the raw cost of tech has exploded in a very short amount of time.

- **Software score: 3.5 / 5**

## Poco F8 Pro review: battery life

 ![The Poco F8 Pro in Silver in the hand against a garden backdrop](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/xmo7ewHoLoLyTwSKH5tEWi.jpg) 


- **Huge 6,210mAh battery**
- **100W charging gets you to full in 42 minutes**
- **No wireless charging**

What happens when you put a humongous battery in a phone with a power-efficient display and flagship performance? You get phenomenal battery life, and when you need to top it up, one of the fastest charging speeds of any smartphone.

The 6,210mAh battery in the Poco F8 Pro is massive, and far larger than batteries offered by the mid-range competition. It features Poco’s latest silicon-carbon technology, which offers much higher energy density, allowing Poco to pack a larger battery into the same size or a smaller phone.

In practical use, it offers around 7 to 9 hours of screen time over up to two days, depending on your settings. This puts the F8 Pro on par with most flagship phones and makes it superior to others in this category.

Then there’s 100W charging, and it’s ridiculously fast, charging your phone to full in around 42 minutes. This gives it a charging rate of around 148mAh per minute (mAh/min), which is considerably faster than its rivals, and almost three times the speed of the 30W charging on the Google Pixel 10a (57 mAh/min).

Again, like any phone at this price, there are compromises, but in this case, it’s the lack of wireless charging or an included charger. The former is a dealbreaker for some, while the latter is common practice among flagship smartphone makers but a shift for Poco, which traditionally included a charger in the box. Regardless, for most people, this is the best battery life you can get at this price.

- **Battery score: 4 / 5**

## Buy it if...

**You want great bang for your buck**

This phone offers an incredible flagship-like experience at a price tag that won’t hit your wallet as hard.

**You don’t need the best camera**

The camera is fine, but it's an area where Poco had to compromise slightly. It’s worth noting that the F8 Pro takes good enough photos for many people.

**You want phenomenal battery life and fast charging**

The Poco F8 Pro is the Energizer Bunny of smartphones. If battery life is important to you, look no further: it excels in day-to-day use and offers ridiculously fast charging.

## Don't buy it if...

**You want the best phone that money can buy**

The Poco F8 Pro's specs are definitely impressive, but more expensive phones do offer a more complete experience overall.

**You need the best camera**

If photography is important to you, this camera, especially the zoom, may be a disappointment. The main camera is usually good enough, but the same can’t be said for the ultra-wide, telephoto, or selfie cameras.

**You want software longevity**

HyperOS has ads, questionable update timeliness, and an inferior integration of AI services like Gemini. This will be a dealbreaker for some, but not for others, and the Poco F8 Pro is definitely for the latter group.

The Google Pixel 10a starts at £449 or $499 with 128GB of storage. It offers the best of Google's software, and although it has only one camera, it’s proven extremely capable. The problem? It feels like a considerable compromise in flagship specs for the benefit of AI features and better updates.
