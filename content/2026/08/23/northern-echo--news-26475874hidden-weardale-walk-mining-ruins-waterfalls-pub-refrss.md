Some adventurous walks offer a picturesque view at the end, while others seem to throw in a little bit of everything along the way.

Slitt Wood, near the Weardale village of Westgate, offers a gentle woodland path followed by a tumbling burn past a series of small waterfalls, before leading walkers into one of the most fascinating old mining landscapes in the Durham Dales.

Finish back in Westgate and there is the prospect of a cold drink and some well-earned pub grub at the Hare and Hounds.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

The route follows Middlehope Burn through an ancient woodland, featuring clear pools, limestone cascades and small falls along the way.

It's the perfect setting the scene for a calming stroll with the family, friends or just you and the dog.

The wood sits in the North Pennines National Landscape and UNESCO Global Geopark, an area known for some dramatic geology as well as its industrial past.

The usual circular walk from Westgate is around three miles, although longer routes can be created by linking it to the West Rigg Geotrail and the higher moorland beyond.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

One favoured route begins from the lay-by opposite the caravan park in Westgate, where a path heads towards the old High Mill and into the trees.

It is a fairly manageable at around 4.4 miles, with 111 metres of ascent, while an alternative Slitt Wood Waterfalls and Low Slitt Lead Mine loop is just under five kilometres.

The waterfalls are a big part of the draw.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

Rather than one giant cascade, Slitt Wood has a succession of smaller falls and rapids where Middlehope Burn runs over limestone and through the narrow wooded valley.

In online reviews, walkers have described “small tumbling waterfalls”, a “fabulous waterfall” and a “delightful tumbling stream” that rises steadily beside the path.

On a warm day, it is the sort of place where you will find yourself stopping more often than planned, whether to take photographs, listen to the water or simply admire the way the woodland wraps around the burn with a picnic.

This quiet woodland was once a busy industrial landscape, and the remains of Slitt Mine and the wider West Rigg workings are still scattered through the route.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

Slitt Mine worked the Slitt Vein, described as the largest mineral vein in the North Pennines, while the area also saw iron ore extraction.

Visitors can spot old shafts, brickwork, wheel pits, washing floors, spoil heaps and the remains of structures once used to process lead ore.

Interpretation boards around the old lead-mining area help bring the landscape into focus, explaining what would otherwise look like a collection of weathered stonework and embankments being slowly reclaimed by nature.

Reviewers say it takes around 15 to 20 minutes of steady uphill walking to reach the mine site, where the ruins of old buildings, a dam and views across the River Wear valley add another layer to the walk.

While the lower route through Slitt Wood has been described as an easy walk with a good surface, the path beyond the mine workings towards Middlehope Shield can be badly eroded and is not suitable for pushchairs or anyone unsteady on their feet.


Westgate also offers exactly the kind of finish a North East walk deserves.

The Hare and Hounds is regularly recommended as the place to head after exploring Slitt Wood, whether you are after a drink or a sit-down pub dinner.

For anyone looking for a walk that feels more rewarding than its length suggests, Slitt Wood is a strong contender with its waterfalls, proper local history without a museum ticket, views when the route rises above the valley and a pub waiting at the end.
