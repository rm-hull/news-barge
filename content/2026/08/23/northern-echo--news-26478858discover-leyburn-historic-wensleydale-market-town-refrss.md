A North Yorkshire market town known for its countryside views, independent shops and traditional market continues to prove popular with visitors to the Yorkshire Dales.

Leyburn sits on the edge of Wensleydale, close to the Yorkshire Dales National Park, and is often used as a base by walkers, day-trippers and tourists exploring the area.

  Leyburn, North Yorkshire 

*(Image: Richard Doughty)*

Despite its relatively small size, the town has a busy centre, a long history and several well-known attractions nearby.

Visitors regularly praise Leyburn for combining the feel of a traditional Yorkshire market town with easy access to some of the Dales’ best-known scenery.

## A traditional Yorkshire market town

Leyburn has a history stretching back centuries and was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086.

Leyburn was granted a market charter by Charles II in 1686 and went on to become an important commercial centre for Wensleydale.

That market tradition continues today, with a weekly market held in the town square on Fridays.

  Leyburn, North Yorkshire 

*(Image: Richard Doughty)*

The square is surrounded by shops, cafés and pubs and remains one of the busiest parts of the town.

Visitors have described Leyburn as a “proper town” and praised its “brilliant market”, with reviews regularly highlighting the friendly traders and traditional atmosphere.

## Independent shops and long-standing businesses

Leyburn is also known for its independent retailers.

Among its best-known businesses is Milners of Leyburn, a family-run department store which first opened in 1882.

Campbell’s of Leyburn is another long-established name in the town, having traded as a grocer since the 19th century.

  Leyburn, North Yorkshire 

*(Image: Richard Doughty)*

The mix of traditional businesses, cafés, pubs and independent shops has helped Leyburn retain its character despite its popularity with tourists.

One visitor praised the town for its “old-fashioned service”, while another said it felt “authentic, not overly commercial”.

## Views across Wensleydale

For many visitors, Leyburn’s biggest attraction is its location.

Leyburn Shawl, an escarpment to the west of the town, provides far-reaching views across Wensleydale and towards villages including Preston-under-Scar and Castle Bolton.

The popular walking route is around 1.5 miles long and is frequently recommended by visitors looking for an accessible walk with impressive views.


Local legend also links the Shawl with Mary, Queen of Scots, who is said to have dropped her shawl there while escaping from nearby Bolton Castle.

Walkers have described the route as having “breathtaking views”, with some calling it one of the best short walks in the area.

## A base for exploring the Yorkshire Dales

Leyburn’s location also puts several major attractions within easy reach.

Bolton Castle, Middleham Castle and the wider Yorkshire Dales National Park are all nearby, while the surrounding countryside offers numerous walking and cycling routes.

Tennants Auctioneers, on Harmby Road, is another significant attraction in the town and draws buyers and sellers from across the country and further afield.

The neighbouring Garden Rooms also hosts exhibitions, events and other activities throughout the year.

For visitors, Leyburn offers a combination of history, shopping and countryside without losing its traditional market-town feel.

One traveller described it as “the perfect base for exploring Wensleydale”, while another said the views from Leyburn Shawl were worth the visit alone.

With its weekly market, independent businesses and position at the edge of the Yorkshire Dales, Leyburn continues to be one of Wensleydale’s most popular stopping points.
