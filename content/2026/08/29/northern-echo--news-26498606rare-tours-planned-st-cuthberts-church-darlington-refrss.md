Visitors are invited to explore the heritage of one of Darlington’s oldest and most remarkable buildings through music, history, craft and conservation.

St Cuthbert’s Church in Darlington will open its doors to the public on Saturday, September 19, as part of Heritage Open Days, England’s largest festival of history and culture.

The free event, running from 10am to 4pm at St Cuthbert’s Church Centre in Market Place, promises a full day of live music, heritage exhibitions, hands-on workshops, family activities and exclusive guided tours.

  St Cuthbert's church restoration work pictured in March 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

A spokesperson for St Cuthbert’s said: "Heritage Open Days is about bringing history to life.

"This year visitors won't simply learn about the past—they'll experience the craftsmanship that is preserving this remarkable building and discover the stories that have made it such an important part of Darlington's life.

"Whether you come for the music, the exhibitions, the stained glass demonstrations, the Gallery and Belfry Tours or to share your own memories through the Listening Project, we hope people will leave with a deeper appreciation of St Cuthbert's and its place at the heart of the town."

Although the church itself remains closed due to ongoing construction, it will be the centre of one of Darlington’s most significant restoration projects.

Visitors will have a rare opportunity to witness the transformation underway through guided gallery and belfry tours led by St Cuthbert’s bell ringers.

  St Cuthbert's church restoration work pictured in March 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

These tours will offer a behind-the-scenes look at the restoration work and a chance to view the building from rarely seen vantage points.

A highlight of the day will be the live stained glass conservation studio hosted by Northern Lights Stained Glass.

Conservators will demonstrate traditional methods of cutting glass, leading panels and painting decorative details.

Adults can try their hand at cutting and assembling glass, while children can create their own colourful stained glass-inspired crafts.

  St Cuthbert's church restoration work pictured in March 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

One of the directors of Northern Lights, a stained glass specialist featured on BBC’s The Repair Shop, will also be in attendance.

Visitors will be among the first to discover the St Cuthbert’s Listening Project, which aims to capture memories and stories from people connected to the church and the broader community.

Throughout the day, there will be live performances from Cockerton Prize Silver Band and Soul Samba.

A photography heritage display will showcase the history of St Cuthbert’s, while a special pews exhibition will explore the story of the church’s historic seating and its role in the current redevelopment.

Heritage Open Days is England’s largest festival of history and culture, celebrating the places, people and traditions that shape communities across the country.

The event offers the public a chance to explore hidden places and try new experiences, all free of charge.

St Cuthbert’s Church has played a central role in the life of Darlington for generations, serving as a place of worship, community gathering and historical significance.

The Opening Doors Project, which began in January, marks the next phase of long-term restoration, conservation and improvement.

The aim is to ensure the church remains safe, accessible and welcoming for future generations.

During construction, the church building is closed but worship and community activities continue at the St Cuthbert’s Church Centre.

Weekly Sunday Parish Communion services are held at 11am, while Wednesday Holy Communion takes place at 10.30am.

The monthly Choral Evensong is held at Holy Trinity Church on Woodlands Road.
