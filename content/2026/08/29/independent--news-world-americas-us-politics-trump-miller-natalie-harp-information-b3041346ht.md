Stephen Miller, the architect of Donald Trump’s hardline deportation policy, puts anti-immigration content in front of Trump aide Natalie Harp to ensure that it gets seen by the president, a report says.

Harp, Trump’s 35-year-old personal assistant, has been a fixture by his side since 2022 and the subject of recent media scrutiny over her White House role.

Miller, 41, has used Harp’s position with the president “at least a couple” of times to get his message to Trump, 80, without speaking to him directly, a source told Zeteo.

The insider revealed that after Miller showed Harp videos, they quickly appeared on Trump’s Truth Social account.

“I wanted the president to post something about crypto, so I [asked Natalie], and it worked,” another source told the outlet.

Her role for Trump hit the headlines after she was one of only a few staffers the president took with him during a secret plane swap in Turkey after Iran made a threat against his life and Air Force One.

Harp, Dan Scavino and Walt Nauta all rode with Trump, while Secretary of State Marco Rubio and the White House press corps were left aboard Air Force One as an apparent decoy.

The nature of the former One America News anchor’s relationship with Trump also attracted attention after author Michael Wolff obtained a pair of letters in which Harp expresses her devotion to the Republican, writing: “You are all that matters to me.”

After the emergence of the letters, in which she also told Trump she felt “unworthy” of serving him, MS NOW reported that Harp had gone more than a year without securing the routine security clearance West Wing staffers are usually expected to obtain, raising concerns about what classified material she might have seen.

White House spokesman Davis Ingle has told *The Independent* that Harp “is one of the most loyal and hardest-working aides on President Trump’s team.”

*The Independent* has contacted the White House for comment.

Harp has reportedly been nicknamed the “human printer” for the way in which she prints out information, such as supportive social media posts or op-eds, on a portable machine and hands it to the president.

Earlier this month, one person who wanted to give a physical message to Trump told *Politico* they texted the material to Harp, who replied, “printed.”

Trump reportedly welcomes her work, treating Harp as a “Binkie” or “his comfort blanket,” according to *New York Times* reporter Maggie Haberman.
