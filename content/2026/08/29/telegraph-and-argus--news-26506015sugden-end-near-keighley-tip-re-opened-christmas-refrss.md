A BRADFORD district tip could be re-opened by Christmas – a new report has revealed.

At a meeting on September 8, Bradford Council’s Reform led Executive are expected to approve plans to re-open the Sugden End household waste recycling centre in Cross Roads.

The report says the re-opening will be funded by pulling £400,000 of funding for cultural events across the district that aim to “drive increased footfall in district centres".

## 3 tips in Bradford district shut

The tips were closed amid a swathe of cost cutting measures by Bradford Council’s former Labour leadership.

The closure of Sugden End, Golden Butts in Ilkley and Ford Hill in Queensbury saved £900,000 a year – but has been heavily criticised.

Golden Butts and Ford Hill have since been sold, but Sugden End remains in the council’s possession.

## How much will tip reopening cost?

A report to the Executive says re-opening Sugden End will cost £95,000.

It will then cost the authority £315,000 a year to run.

The Executive will also consider plans to have all tips open on Bank Holidays from 10:15am to 15:45pm excluding Christmas Day, Boxing Day, and New Years Day.

That would cost £33,000 a year.

A report to the Executive says the closure of the three tips managed to achieve the required savings – adding: “The 2024 HWRC rationalisation programme successfully delivered the approved annual savings target of approximately £0.9m and enabled the council to maintain a statutory HWRC network at a time of significant financial challenge.”

But a statement from the council said: “During peak periods, several of the (remaining) sites get extremely busy with long queues stretching beyond site boundaries and causing congestion on neighbouring roads.

“It is hoped that by reopening Sugden End, it will ease the pressure on some of the other sites, such as Royd Ings in Keighley and Dowley Gap in Bingley.”

## Where will the funding come from?

Referring to where the money for the re-opening will come from, the report highlights that £400,000 was recently allocated by the council for cultural events across the district.

It says the funding was to “Support the delivery of events across the district to promote local heritage, culture and arts and drive increased footfall in district centres.”

The report suggests this be “de-prioritised” to allow the Cross Roads tip to re-open.

Councillor Ian Walker, Portfolio Holder for Neighbourhoods and Communities, said: “It is clear that we have a strong case for reopening this site which would serve residents on the Worth Valley side of Keighley and the surrounding villages which are some distance from Keighley’s existing HWRC at Royd Ings.

“With the current national changes to waste and recycling, this move, as well as extending openings to bank holidays, will increase our recycling rates and give residents more options when wanting to deposit waste.”

## Tories have their say

Councillor Rebecca Poulsen, Leader of the Conservative Group said, “My Conservative Group colleagues and I are delighted that it looks likely that the closure of Sugden End Waste Recycling Centre will be reversed at the meeting of the Council’s Executive Committee on 8 September, some two and a half years after the then Labour Council closed it.

“As soon as the closure of the site was proposed, we began a campaign to keep it open, in support of the many thousands of local residents who would be affected by the closure.

"Despite the scale and strength of public opinion on the issue, the Labour majority on the council in 2024 forced the closure upon us.

“There has been much inconvenience, with residents having to travel further and queue longer to do the responsible thing with their waste and I would like to thank all of those who did so, rather than fly tip.

“Conservative councillors have continued to push for Sugden End HWRC to reopen and now that Labour no longer have a stranglehold on the council, my colleagues and I are looking forward to the upcoming meeting of the Executive Committee with a sense optimism.

“We are also calling for further work to be done by the waste team to look at options for Ilkley where the Golden Butts site has been sold as well as the sold site in Queensbury.

"There are other possible locations for a Household Waste Recycling Centre in Ilkley, and the options need to be properly considered.

“Currently residents in Ilkley have a long journey to Keighley to dispose of waste where queues can be extremely long.”
