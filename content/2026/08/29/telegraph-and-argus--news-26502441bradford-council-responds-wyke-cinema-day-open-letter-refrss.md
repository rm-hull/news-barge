Bradford Council has responded to an open letter from the organisers of an event that was cancelled at short notice.

Wyke Cinema Day, which was due to take place at Wyke Sports Village on August 22, did not go ahead due to a scheduling conflict with a British Cycling competition.

Big TV technician Jodie Cottrell, who organises the event, visited the site on the day and claims it was "extremely quiet and the car park was not even half full".

Bradford Council says the decision was based on information provided by organisers which meant, due to "a number of health and safety and operational concerns", it was not possible to host both events at the same time.

## What does the open letter say?

In her letter, she said: "I attended the Sports Village on August 22 to see how the site was being used and was extremely disappointed by what I found, as it appeared inconsistent with several of the reasons we were given for why both events could not take place.

"We were told the site could not accommodate the anticipated numbers from both events.

"However, the site was extremely quiet and the car park was not even half full.

"We were also told the fields were required for camping, yet I observed no camping on the fields that day."

## Safety concerns

Ms Cottrell also raised safety concerns, saying: "Most concerningly, we were told the lower fields needed to remain clear for emergency access.

"However, when I attended, the bottom entrance gates and overhead height-restriction barrier were locked.

"I would like an explanation as to how this was consistent with the emergency-access requirements given to us."

In response, Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council's executive member for neighbourhoods and communities, said: "When bookings are made, we have to rely on the information provided by the event organisers on the numbers expected to attend the event, how they will use the space, etc.

"Unfortunately, based on the information provided by both potential bookings it was not possible for two events to take place simultaneously due to a number of health and safety and operational concerns.

"These included restricted access to toilet facilities, emergency access routes being obstructed by the screen, and the use of a tannoy system at the cycling event, which would have significantly impacted the outdoor cinema experience."

## £5 parking charge

Ms Cottrell asked the council to clarify who authorised a £5 parking charge on the day, which areas were officially allocated for camping and parking, and why the lower entrance was locked if it was intended for emergency use.

She said: "Big TV committed significant time, resources and money to Wyke Cinema Day based on a confirmed council booking.

"Having now personally seen how the Sports Village was used on August 22, I am even more disappointed that our free community event was cancelled and believe we deserve a clear explanation of how and why this happened."

Councillor Walker clarified that the parking charge was set by the cycling club and that proceeds went to them.

He said: "The charge for the car park was made by the cycling club, with proceeds going to them.

"The emergency access is not left open until it is required and is managed through the event plan and stewards.

"Keeping the access clear in case of emergency is paramount, which was explained to the organisers."

## Alternative days offer

He also said that alternative arrangements had been offered.

Councillor Walker said: "We did offer the organisers from Big TV an alternative date at the Sports Village on the following day, Sunday, which was declined.

"An alternative venue at Wyke Recreation Ground for the original date and time requested was also provided.

"This offer was made in partnership with the local football and rugby clubs, which had agreed to support the event by providing stewarding, toilet facilities, and other practical assistance.

"Big TV also declined this option.

"The alternative arrangements offered were both practical and viable solutions to enable the cinema event to take place."

The Telegraph & Argus approached British Cycling for a comment.
