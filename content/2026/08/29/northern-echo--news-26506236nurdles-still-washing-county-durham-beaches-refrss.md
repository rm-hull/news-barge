Nurdles are continuing to wash up on County Durham beaches more than a month after one billion plastic pellets are believed to have spilt into the River Tyne following a crash between two vessels.

Durham County Council has warned beachgoers about what to do if they spot the nurdles, used in the manufacturing of plastic products, which have been seen at Crimdon, Seaham Hall beach and the Slope beach at Seaham Marina.

  Nurldes on beaches in County Durham 

*(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

The environmental incident was triggered when the Wind Orca vessel struck the berthed container ship BG Orange, causing a dockside gantry crane to collapse at the Port of Tyne on Sunday, July 19.

A spokesperson for Durham County Council said: "Durham County Council are continuing to find nurdles, small plastic pellets, washing up on our beaches.


"While the council are not seeing the high numbers experienced in other local authority areas, council teams have found nurdles in the last week at Crimdon, Seaham Hall beach and the Slope beach at Seaham Marina.

"Staff have carried out safe, clear and co-ordinated clean-ups when they have found nurdles; counting, weighing and recording them; and continue to monitor beaches.

  Nurldes on beaches in County Durham 

*(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

"The public health risk associated with nurdles is still considered to be very low. They are not toxic, but can pose a risk to wildlife and other animals if ingested.

Previously, North East Mayor Kim McGuinness has called on the owners of the two vessels involved in the collision to "put their hands in their pocket" and help pay for the environmental response.

  **Durham County Council has given this advice:**

- Do not handle nurdles as a precaution and to support any safe, co-ordinated clean-up that might be needed. If you do inadvertently touch them, wash your hands afterwards.
- Avoid stepping on nurdles or pushing them deeper into the sand.
- In locations where nurdles are present, dogs should be kept on leads.
- Check the latest Environment Agency bathing water advice before entering the water, at the bathing water quality web page.
- If you see nurdles on County Durham beaches, please email [nurdles@durham.gov.uk](mailto:nurdles@durham.gov.uk) and include: the location (using what3words if possible); date and time seen; a photo where possible and approximate amount of pellets seen.
- Do not collect nurdles independently as it is really important that all pellets picked up are recorded and weighed. This is to allow authorities in the region to understand how many have been recovered following the initial spill last month, and to assess environmental impact.
- For anyone who does still collect nurdles, please do not put them in general rubbish or leave them loose on the beach. Designated collection points will be in place at Seaham Hall Beach car park, Seaham Marina and Crimdon for people to use.
