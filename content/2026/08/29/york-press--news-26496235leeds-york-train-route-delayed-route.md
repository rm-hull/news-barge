## New data shows the most delayed route for York train passengers

Passengers wait for a train at York's station. *(Image: Dylan Connell/Newsquest)*

Direct services between York and Leeds recorded more delay minutes than any other York-linked route in Network Rail data covering the last two full financial years.

Figures obtained under the Freedom of Information Act show trains between York and Leeds accounted for 14,877 delay minutes across 2024/25 and 2025/26.

The York-Leeds service is one of the most heavily used routes in the area, linking the cities for commuters, students and onward connections across West Yorkshire.

The regularity of arrivals and departures goes some way to explaining why the route experiences such high delays compared to other routes such as services between York and Manchester Piccadilly, which recorded 3,295 delay minutes and York-London Kings Cross recording 1,976.

Across 2024/25 and 2025/26 there were 942 incidents in which direct trains operating between York and Leeds recorded delays of more than two minutes.

Despite topping the list overall, delay minutes on York-Leeds services fell from 9,066 minutes in 2024/25 to 5,811 in 2025/26.

While York-Leeds delay minutes fell year on year, some other York routes experienced higher delay times over the same period, including services to London Kings Cross and Manchester Piccadilly.

Across 2024/25 and 2025/26 there were 267 incidents in which direct trains operating between York and London Kings Cross recorded delays of more than two minutes.

A Network Rail spokesperson said: "Services between York and Leeds are part of a busy and interconnected railway, meaning disruption elsewhere can have a knock-on effect on services here.

"We work closely with train operators to monitor the causes of delays and deliver joint performance improvement plans, taking locally targeted action and focusing on collaborative work.

"We’re also delivering the Transpennine Route Upgrade, which is modernising the railway between Manchester, Huddersfield, Leeds and York to provide more reliable journeys and greater capacity."

According to the official website, the Transpennine Route Upgrade "is a transformative, multi-billion pound railway programme that will better connect passengers in the North between Manchester, Huddersfield, Leeds and York."

TRU aims to transform large parts of the northern rail network and improve connections between York, Leeds, Manchester and Huddersfield, however, admit that disruption will be caused as work progresses.

The plans include the electrification of parts of the Transpennine route, which includes the recent installation of 50 overhead line foundation bases between Huddersfield and Leeds.

James Richardson, TRU’s managing director, said: "While our teams work around the clock to deliver faster, more frequent trains across the Pennines, the benefits go well beyond the railway itself – shaping opportunities for the people these services will support for generations to come, and boosting our local economies.

We’re proud to be working closely with our partners in business and local government to deliver a railway that will help the North reach its full potential."

The TRU project has installed electrified wires as "the first step towards a cleaner, greener, fully electrified railway between York, Leeds, Huddersfield and Manchester".
