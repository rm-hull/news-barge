Middlesbrough residents and businesses say they are 'living in fear' as a spate of violent incidents continue to grip the town, claiming the lives of nine.

More than 200 extra police officers from forces across the country have been drafted in to help Cleveland Police tackle the ongoing disorder and bring a higher police presence to the streets.

Teessiders told The Northern Echo they feel unsafe walking the streets, are closing up businesses and worry about letting their children grow up in the area.

  A large police presence is set to be seen across the town 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Jenna Coates, 34, from Middlesbrough, said: “I just think it is going really downhill, it’s quite rough, there are a lot of serious crimes happening and I just think something needs to be improved.”

She said she would worry about bringing children up in some parts of the town.

“I wouldn’t want my kids to be growing up in some of these areas,” she said.

Jenna said more focus should be placed on serious crime.

“We have the Matrix [police team] but maybe if more effort was focused on these serious crimes rather than petty crimes we could free up some prison space and put the people who are doing these serious things away for a very long time, then hopefully the area can improve.”

One 71-year-old Middlesbrough resident, who did not want to be named, said the town felt very different from the past.

“Well, it’s disgusting. Taking lives for no reason but that seems to be the society we live in today.”

“You can’t even walk the streets at night and you can’t have your doors unlocked.”

The resident said more visible policing was needed.

“You don’t have police patrolling the streets like they used to.”

And they said they feared for younger generations.

“I have grandkids and I hate it for them, what life are they going to have when they get older? There is no appeal for anything around here anymore.”

  Brenda Hickson, who works for Connections, the community deli 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

For Brenda Hickson, who works for Connections, the community deli, the atmosphere in the town has become frightening.

Brenda said: “Working in the town can be scary as we are hearing that things could escalate or ‘kick off’ and it is worrying.

“I do feel a little bit intimidated when I walk into work in the morning or if I lock up late at night, when I am putting the shutters down I feel I need to just keep aware and look around a bit.

"There just seems to be a little bit of unrest in the area.”

She added: “I don’t know what more can be done for the area but just know something has to be done.”

  Shaaz, who works at Johns News 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

At Johns News on Linthorpe Road, worker Shaaz said the problems were being felt by businesses every day.

Shaaz said: “Nowadays we have even ten, maybe 12 year olds coming in wanting vapes. It is shocking to me.

“Crime is a problem in Middlesbrough, with all this happening with the A66 crash I feel it has brought it all more to light.

“These underaged kids know that not much will be done to sanction them and they will more than likely get away with it so I think they are not deterred away from crime like they should be.

“Most people we see and serve are really decent people.”

A worker at Not Just Vape, who did not want to be named, said the problems were becoming increasingly entrenched among younger people.

She said: “See kids have grown up these days watching their parents struggle and grow up around the violence and drugs and everything so they think it is normal.

“Then they grow up to do the same thing and nothing is changing.

“I do think things are getting worse and people are getting younger.”

  Ionela, who lives in Middlesbrough 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Ionela, who lives in Middlesbrough, said she was worried about her son going to college in the area.

She said: “I am worried, my son is about to go to college round here and it’s a worry.

“We are afraid to come out at night because if we want to go out to a pub or restaurant we cannot because it is dangerous.

“I am glad to see more policemen but more needs to be done.”

But not everyone believes the violence is a reflection of Middlesbrough as a whole.

  Billy and Marjorie Bell, who live in the centre of Middlesbrough 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Billy and Marjorie Bell, who live in the centre of Middlesbrough, said most people remained decent and law-abiding.

“I think most people in Middlesbrough are decent, hard-working, law-abiding people.”

They said they had lived in the town centre for 33 years and did not believe the recent violence represented the community.

“All of this going on is not a reflection on Middlesbrough as a town at all.”

“We live around great people and everyone is lovely and cares for each other.”

   

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

The car carrying the young men drove down the wrong side of the A66 and crashed head-on into the police car. All seven were pronounced dead at the scene.

Police have arrested 17 people following the A66 crash, which followed a police pursuit, as part of investigations into alleged organised crime.

Then there was a fatal house fire in Grangetown then claimed the lives of seven-year-old Valentina Foster and her aunt Natalie McDonald, 34.

  Emergency services at the scene of a major residential fire on Birchington Avenue, in Grangetown, Middlesbrough. 

*(Image: North News & Pictures Ltd)*


Police have since dealt with further incidents across the Middlesbrough area, amid concerns over organised crime and further unrest.

Homes on Ruskin Avenue, Hadfield Court and Steele Crescent across Middlesbrough have also had cars driven into them, believed to be linked to the A66 crash.

As well as five cars torched at a vigil for one of the men who died in the crash, Assistant Chief Constable Jon McAdam of Durham Police said: “The behaviour we saw at last night’s gathering was completely unacceptable.”

Anna Turley, MP for Redcar, described the scenes as “completely unacceptable” and said she would not accept the behaviour becoming normalised.
