A Ferryhill takeaway has been fined £40,000 after illegal workers were found to be employed by the business.

Pizza Hot NE Ltd, which operates Pizza Hot on Main Street in Ferryhill, appears on the Home Office’s latest quarterly list of employers issued with civil penalties for illegal working.

The company was handed a £40,000 penalty in the latest release, which covers the period from January 1 to March 31, 2026.

According to the Home Office, civil penalties can be issued where businesses employ workers who do not have permission to work in the UK or are working in breach of restrictions attached to their immigration status.

Pizza Hot NE Ltd was contacted for a comment.

Pizza Hot is based at 16 Main Street in Ferryhill.

The business is one of several across the North East to appear in the latest quarterly figures.

Another County Durham business, KLW NE Ltd, which operates Hollathan’s on Ashfield Terrace in Chester-le-Street, was also handed a £40,000 penalty.

Style Barbers Birtley Ltd, based at Windsor Road Stores on Durham Road in Birtley, was also listed with a £40,000 penalty.

The largest fine among the North East businesses in the latest figures was £135,000, issued to Jana Shawarma Ltd, linked to Shawarma & Grill House on Gallowgate in Newcastle.

Mr Bubbles Mini Valet Centre NE Ltd in Newcastle was fined £90,000, while Sunderland Hand Car Wash Ltd, linked to Fence Houses Hand Car Wash, received an £80,000 penalty.

Employers in the UK have a legal responsibility to prevent illegal working by carrying out right-to-work checks on people before they employ them.

The Home Office says businesses should also carry out follow-up checks where a worker has time-limited permission to remain and work in the country.

Those who knowingly employ somebody they know, or have reasonable cause to believe, does not have the right to work can face criminal prosecution as well as financial penalties.

The Home Office publishes details of employers subject to illegal-working civil penalties four times a year.

Its published list covers employers whose cases have reached the relevant stage of the objection and appeal process.
