## Closed church could soon offer affordable accommodation on the North York Moors

St Michaell's Church, Cold Kirby

A historic church could soon offer a warm welcome to travellers on the North York Moors.

Saint Michael’s Church in Cold Kirby, a Grade II-listed building, is set to be transformed into a low-cost hostel under plans developed by the Saint Aelred’s Pilgrim Trail Society.

The project has been awarded a £122,760 grant from The National Lottery Heritage Fund to explore converting the closed church into Saint Michael’s Lodge, offering affordable accommodation for walkers, riders, and pilgrims.

The grant will enable the Society to develop its plans and undertake the specialist work needed to explore how Saint Michael’s can be conserved and adapted for its proposed new use. The funding will help the project investigate its conservation, design, access, planning, environmental, business and operational requirements and prepare its proposals for the next stage.

Recently listed Grade II by Historic England, Saint Michael’s is a remarkable historic building in the North York Moors. It stands on both the Saint Aelred’s Pilgrim Trail and the Cleveland Way National Trail, and is just over a mile from the North York Moors National Park’s Sutton Bank Centre.

Reverend Ian Robinson, Project Lead for Saint Michael’s Lodge, said: “We are delighted that The National Lottery Heritage Fund has recognised the potential of our vision for Saint Michael’s. Thanks to National Lottery players, this £122,760 award enables us to take the next important steps towards giving this historic church a sustainable future.

“Our hope is to create Saint Michael’s Lodge as a place of welcome and affordable accommodation for pilgrims, walkers, riders and visitors to the North York Moors. It would give this closed church an opportunity to serve people once again, while protecting and celebrating its heritage for future generations.”

Reverend Mel Burnside, Chair of The Saint Aelred’s Pilgrim Trail Society CIO, said: "Pilgrimage is about journeying, meeting people and finding welcome along the way. Our hope for Saint Michael's Lodge is that this beautiful church can once again become a place of welcome — somewhere people can find shelter, friendship and a deeper connection with the heritage and landscape of the North York Moors. This award gives us a wonderful opportunity to explore how we can make that vision a reality."

Councillor George Jabbour, Cold Kirby's representative on North Yorkshire Council, said: “Having worshipped at Saint Michael’s in the past, I know how important this community asset is to the area. Now that the church has closed, it is essential that its legacy is maintained. This is why I wholeheartedly support the Saint Michael’s Lodge Project. On behalf of Cold Kirby, the surrounding villages and local residents, I am grateful to the National Lottery Heritage Fund for their generous grant.”

Helen Featherstone, Director of England - North at The National Lottery Heritage Fund, said: "Saint Michael's Church is an important part of the North York Moors' heritage and has the potential to continue serving people in new and meaningful ways. Thanks to money raised by National Lottery players, this project will help explore how this historic building can be conserved and adapted to create a welcoming place for pilgrims, walkers, riders and visitors, while safeguarding its heritage for future generations.”
