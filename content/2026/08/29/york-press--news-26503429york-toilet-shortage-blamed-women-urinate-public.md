## Why two middle-aged women ended up urinating in York streets

Signs telling people not to publicly urinate or defecate have gone up across York city centre (Image - LDR)

  **York toilet shortage blamed after women urinate in public**

THE recent reports of people relieving themselves in public implies that the activities happen in the evening and as such are often alcohol related with excessive consumption being the main cause.

However, on a couple of occasions in the early afternoon I witnessed two unfortunate middle-aged women having to take advantage of the coverage of bushes (near St Wilfrid's school) and behind a low wall in the moat (on Lord Mayor's Walk).

  Signs telling people not to publicly urinate or defecate have gone up across York city centre (Image - LDR)


Neither of these women fit the profile of people over indulging with alcohol.

Rather, it was due to the complete lack of toilets in a so-called tourist city which is disgraceful and I'm sure brings a negative appeal to visitors and locals alike.

  **Tea hits the spot other brews just can't reach**

MR Shepherdson’s ‘My Earl Grey tea held a poignancy that evening’ letter in The Press, August 24, chimed with me.

Not because I’m a tea connoisseur though I regularly sup the ‘leaf’. I often can’t tell the difference between various brands and lack a sophisticated tea palate (as opposed to tea plate which lives with the crockery).

However, I vividly remember when my mum, and her mother before her, drank tea.

I observed them quite closely and was intrigued by the way in which ‘the shutters seemed to come down’ or partway down, as the tea reached the right parts.

They’d gaze into the middle distance, where there seemed to be a lot ‘going on’ and be lost in a kind of reverie in their semi switched-off state.

That scenario stays vividly etched in my mind. I was fascinated by it as a youngster and thought to myself, ‘She’s off again!’

Is there such a therapy as ‘tea hypnosis’? Is tea a possible topic for Helen Mead’s food and drink reviews; a future forthcoming feature on top ten teas, perhaps? Apologies for outbreak of alliteration there.

As a lifelong dabbler in cosmology, what particularly rang multiple bells with me about Mr Shepherdson’s letter was the second half.

The late Sir Bernard Lovell borrowed from an earlier source this all-encompassing expression as the title of his book, ‘The Centre of Immensities’. That says it for me, as much as any words can.

  *Derek Reed,*

  *Middlethorpe Drive,*

  *York*
