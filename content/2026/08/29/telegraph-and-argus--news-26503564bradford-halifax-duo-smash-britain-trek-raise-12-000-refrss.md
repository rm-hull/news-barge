TWO men who completed a 1,000-mile trek across Britain have raised £12,000 for Marie Curie.

Shaun Casey, 56, from Bradford, and Grant McConnell, 62, from Halifax, set off from John O’Groats on June 17 and reached Land’s End 48 days later, covering 1,192 miles on foot and carrying all their gear, including camping equipment.

The challenge was inspired by the care Marie Curie provided to their loved ones.

Mr Casey and Mr McConnell said: "We’re raising money for Marie Curie, a charity that has supported close friends of ours during the most difficult times of their lives."

During the journey, Mr McConnell was forced to pause due to injury and later completed the route by mountain bike, rejoining the challenge in Carlisle after recovering at home.

Mr Casey said: "Crossing the finish line at Land's End was a huge personal achievement, but the lasting memory won't be the miles or the blisters.

"It will be the incredible generosity of people and the countless stories of compassion that they shared with me.

"I finished the journey believing that I had travelled the length of Britain, but what I really discovered was the extraordinary kindness of the people who live here and the remarkable difference Marie Curie makes to so many families."

Mr McConnell admitted the challenge was physically tough but deeply rewarding.

He said: "It was a great adventure that has not only raised significant funds for Marie Curie, but one that we hope has raised awareness of the work they do, too."

Marie Curie provides end-of-life care for people with terminal illnesses, supporting patients and families at home and in hospices.

Claire Macina, community fundraiser at Marie Curie Yorkshire, said: "We are extremely grateful to Shaun and Grant for completing this incredible challenge to raise vital funds for end of life care."
