THE two faces of Middlesbrough, as seen from the air by our regular contributor, Northern Perspectives.

In the foreground, shrouded in scaffolding, is the Old Town Hall, built in 1846, with its clocktower added in 1858, as the heart of the new docks boomtown that was being created by the Stockton & Darlington Railway.

  Northern Perspectives' view of Middlesbrough Old Town Hall with the Transporter Bridge behind 

*(Image: Northern Perspectives)*

Over the decades, the town of Middlesbrough grew away from this riverside area, known as St Hilda’s, and in 1996, the Old Town Hall was abandoned and dereliction took hold.

But now, 30 years later, £6.3m is being invested in restoring and reinvigorating the area with the historic, atmospheric old buildings central to the future.

Then, in the background of the picture, is the Transporter Bridge, the defining symbol of Teesside’s industrial heritage and one of the most recognisable landmarks in the North East.

Constructed in 1911, it was an ingenious Edwardian answer to the challenge of crossing a busy industrial river.

It stopped operating in 2019 when structural defects were discovered and, seven years on, there is no viable plan for raising the millions of pounds to get it going or, indeed, to stabilise it.

As it took three decades to get the old heart of Middlesbrough beating again, it’ll probably be 2049 until the icon of Teesside is moving again – by which time, of course, the cost will have risen enormously.

The Transporter Bridge was once a symbol of Teesside’s engineering brilliance; now it looks as if it is a monument to modern inertia.

Northern Perspectives is a Durham-based cameraman who uses the latest drone technology to shed new light on our beautiful and historic landscapes – and never can one of his frames have captured a more contrasting story.
