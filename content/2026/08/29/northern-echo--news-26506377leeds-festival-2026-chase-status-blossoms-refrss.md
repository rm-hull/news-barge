On Friday afternoon, the first full day of programming got underway at Bramham Park.

This was also the first opportunity for the festival punters to experience the new stages on offer this year at Leeds Festival.

In the run-up to the event, the organisers announced somewhat of a shake-up to the four-day event’s many stages.

And having experienced all of the stages and tents that the festival has to offer on Friday, we can say that there has been a lot of thought put into the look and aesthetic of this year’s festival.

When it comes to the main stage, there’s not too much change, other than the name of the stage, which is now known as The Grid. However, gone this year are The Chevron stage, the Festival Republic stage and the BBC Introducing stage.

If you’ve been following social media commentary around the latter, it’s caused a bit of a stir online. Despite this, its replacement, The Canopy, is still showcasing emerging acts and is situated in the same spot in the arena just like its predecessor, albeit with a roof over the top to keep the crowd covered from being exposed to the elements.

On Friday afternoon, a smorgasbord of emerging talent performed on The Canopy. One of the highlights of their programming was Violet Grohl, daughter of Foo Fighters and Nirvana legend Dave Grohl.

Whilst boyband December 10, who formed on Simon Cowell’s Netflix series The Next Act in 2025, packed out The Canopy and proved to be a big hit at Bramham Park.

Outside of The Grid, new stage The Gallery is one of the largest arenas on site.

Throughout the day, thousands of music fans descended upon the big top tent to witness the likes of new artist Mulaa Jones, US-based alt/rock star Julia Wolf and chart-topping star Holly Humberstone grace the stage.

This year’s Leeds Festival lineup is somewhat eclectic, and there really is something here for everyone. For the dance music community, new stage The Warehouse provides a huge indoor arena with some of the scene’s biggest names.

Hybrid Minds topped the bill on Friday evening.

From early in the day, thousands of dance music fans descended upon The Warehouse’s dance floor, as the DJs delivered an arsenal of floor fillers to the Bramham Park dance music fraternity.

Perhaps one of the most interesting new stages on site is The Ballroom. Draped in burgundy and with chandeliers suspended from the ceiling, the stage created a wonderful aesthetic for both those watching the bands and the acts performing themselves.

One of the biggest draws of the day in The Ballroom was current indie/rock buzz band Westside Cowboy.

Like the rest of the stages around the site, The Grid delivered an eclectic lineup on Friday, with country/pop superstar Role Model, Stockport’s indie darlings The Blossoms, Florence and the Machine and dance music legends Chase and Status gracing the stage.

Chase and Status were the perfect closers to Friday’s programming, delivering a pyro-fuelled set, groundbreaking production and an arsenal of dance hits that had the Bramham Park crowd moving from the off.

Much like Kasabian, who performed on Thursday evening, Chase and Status were the perfect band to close out the day at Bramham Park.

Leeds Festival returns to Bramham Park on Saturday, with headliners Fontaines DC and Charli XCX topping the bill.
