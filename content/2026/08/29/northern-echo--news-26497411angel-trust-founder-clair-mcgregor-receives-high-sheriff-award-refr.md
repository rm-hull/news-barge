A Bishop Auckland woman who founded a charity from a hotel bedroom with a borrowed laptop has been honoured for her outstanding voluntary service to communities across County Durham.

Clair McGregor, founder of Angel Trust, received a High Sheriff’s Award for Voluntary Service from Dr Arnab Basu, the High Sheriff of the County Palatine of Durham.

The charity has grown to employ about 30 people and delivers a wide range of support to people facing hardship, including help for the homeless, counselling services, and foodbanks.

It also runs a Safe Night Hub, offering individuals access to hot showers, warm beds, home-cooked meals, clothing, and washing facilities, along with counselling and help with online forms and benefits paperwork.

Dr Basu said: "Clair’s commitment to Angel Trust and the people it supports is remarkable.

"What began with a borrowed laptop has grown into an organisation which is there for countless families and individuals when they need help.

"She has worked tirelessly to build the charity, earn the trust of the community and make sure that support is there day after day.

"Recognising people like Clair is exactly what the High Sheriff’s awards are about, and it was a pleasure to present her with this award for voluntary service."

Despite the charity's expansion, Ms McGregor continues to volunteer her time every day and remains personally involved in supporting those who turn to Angel Trust for help.

She said: "I’m absolutely delighted to receive this award.

"To see how far we’ve come is something I’m incredibly proud of.

"This award is also a reflection of our brilliant staff, trustees and volunteers because none of what we do would be possible without them."

Angel Trust has made a significant impact in the community, including its 2025 toy appeal, which saw more than 15,000 toys donated to children across County Durham.

The High Sheriff’s awards recognise individuals and organisations whose voluntary service makes a significant contribution to communities across the county.
