A SURGE in need is putting growing strain on a charity’s frontline services.

The InTouch Foundation, based in Bradford, has reported a 30 per cent rise in people seeking support over the past six months.

This comes as demand in the district rises faster than the national average.

The volunteer-led charity offers hot meals, outreach, and practical support to people experiencing hardship.

Osman Gondal, founder and CEO, said: "Seeing the number of people using our services increase is both humbling and deeply concerning.

"Every number represents a person, a family or an individual facing circumstances many of us will never experience.

"Our message has always been simple: if someone is in need, we want to be there.

"The growth we are seeing shows that our support is reaching further, but it also highlights how many people are struggling.

"I want to pay tribute to our volunteers, donors and supporters.

"They are the heartbeat of this organisation.

"Their dedication ensures we can respond when people need us most.

"We are proud of the difference we are making, but we cannot ignore the reality.

"Demand is rising, and so must the support available.

"Greater investment and stronger community action are essential if we are to meet the growing need."

The rise is believed to reflect both the charity’s expanding reach and increased hardship across Bradford.

According to Bradford’s Food Insecurity Health Needs Assessment 2026, 16.3 per cent of underserved groups in the district experience food insecurity—more than twice the national average of 6.9 per cent.

The InTouch Foundation relies on volunteers and donations to continue its work.

Its volunteer programme supports both the hot food kitchen and street outreach activities across the district.

Andrea Dean, a volunteer with the charity, said: "I get a lot out of volunteering here; it's rewarding to be part of something that brings people together.

"We don't know what challenges people are facing in their daily lives, so being able to offer a warm welcome, a meal and a friendly place to spend some time means a lot to me."

The charity remains committed to reaching even more people in need but warns that meeting rising demand will require collective action from funders, partners, and the wider community.
