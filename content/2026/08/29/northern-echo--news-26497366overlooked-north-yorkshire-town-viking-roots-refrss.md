Perched between the North York Moors and the coast is a town where Viking heritage, Victorian ambition and East Cleveland's industrial past still leave their mark today.

Today, Loftus serves as a gateway to some of the region's most spectacular scenery, with many walkers starting their journey to Staithes along the Cleveland Way.

First recorded in the Domesday Book in 1086, Loftus grew from a small settlement with Viking roots into a thriving market town and a centre for the region's ironstone industry.

Its name comes from the Old Norse word lofthus, meaning a house with an upper storey, a nod to the Scandinavian influence that helped shape much of the North Yorkshire and Teesside coastline.

*(Image: Getty Images)*

By the 1800s, Loftus had established itself as a busy market town, with traders, inns and a bustling high street serving nearby villages and workers from the alum industry.

A weekly Thursday market proved so popular that it attracted people from across the surrounding countryside.

Much of that heritage can still be seen today, with St Leonard's Church, the Town Hall, Oddfellows Hall and the Angel Inn all standing as proud reminders of Loftus' industrial past.

The discovery of ironstone in Cleveland transformed Loftus during the Victorian era, with the opening of Loftus Iron Mine in 1865 bringing jobs, investment and new residents to the town.

While nearby coastal destinations often attracted a lot of local attention, Loftus has quietly remained at the heart of East Cleveland life since the end of mining in 1958, and the closure of its railway station.

Skinningrove, Cattersty Sands, the Cleveland Way and the North York Moors are all within easy reach for walkers and visitors exploring the wider area - but the town itself should not be overlooked.

  Loftus sits just 10 minutes away from some of the North East and North Yorkshire's most picturesque beaches including Skinningrove and Staithes. 

*(Image: Yorkshire)*

To top it off, Loftus has also produced several notable figures including Stanley Hollis - the only soldier awarded the Victoria Cross on D-Day - as well as Game of Thrones actress Faye Marsay and Commonwealth Games gold medallist Paul Drinkhall.

With Viking origins, industrial heritage, famous former residents and almost a millennium of history behind it, Loftus remains one of East Cleveland's most fascinating and often underrated towns.
