# LNER and EMR warn of 'significant disruption' on trains between Yorkshire and London

## Two of the main train operators operating routes between Yorkshire and London have warned of “significant disruption” this bank holiday weekend.

The company said it will run a “significantly reduced” timetable between Sheffield and London on both days, advising passengers to only travel if their journey is essential on these dates and to check before setting out.

Compounding the disruption is that from 10pm on Saturday to 8am on Monday there will be no LNER trains between London King’s Cross and Peterborough as part of digital signalling upgrades being carried out by Network Rail.

LNER said: “This will significantly disrupt local journeys, and also longer distance journeys on the East Coast Main Line between Edinburgh, Newcastle, Leeds, Hull, Doncaster and London Kings Cross.”

As a result, EMR services to and from London St Pancras are expected to be extremely busy, with increased demand coinciding with a reduced service because of the strike action. Sheffield station will also be very busy and queues to board services will be in place.

EMR advised people travelling from Yorkshire to London on LNER to continue to use its services and get a replacement bus between Peterborough and London.

On top of this, the operator was recently forced to reduce its timetable on services between Sheffield and London. Four more weekday services are set to be added from next week, taking the total number of pre-planned cancellations to five.

The RMT has voiced concerns about safety issues and faults on some trains.

EMR said it continues to have “constructive discussions” with the RMT and has met with the union six times as it works towards resolving the issues.

Philippa Cresswell, customer experience director at EMR, said: “We recognise how frustrating this disruption will be for customers, particularly following the three Saturdays affected by RMT strike action earlier this month, and we are very sorry for the impact this will have on August bank holiday plans.

“We continue to have constructive discussions with the RMT and have met with the union six times as we work towards resolving the issues at the heart of this dispute.

“We strongly advise customers to only travel to or from London if their journey is essential on Sunday 30 and Monday August 31, and to check their full journey, including first and last trains, before setting out.”

The union said its members are instructed to refuse to work a Class 810 train if a list of faults it has drawn up, including lack of air-con, door faults and non-working toilets, continue.

RMT general secretary Eddie Dempsey said the union would ensure representatives will be permanently involved in discussions with both EMR and manufacturer Hitachi to try to improve the situation.

“RMT is demanding that whilst these faults exist and until these trains are shown to be operating properly, they should be taken out of service,” he said.

“It is now time for EMR management to act on the safety concerns of our members.”

Ms Cresswell added: “Services will be significantly reduced and are expected to be extremely busy, with queuing systems in place at London St Pancras and Sheffield and the possibility of standing room only.

“Customers should allow extra time to make their journey and consider travelling on Saturday or Tuesday where possible.

“Customers in Scotland, the North East, and Yorkshire travelling to or from London, and are yet to purchase a ticket and would normally travel with LNER, are strongly encouraged to follow LNER’s travel advice and use their replacement bus service.”
