Violent disorder across Teesside has sparked urgent calls for action from politicians and police leaders after days of unrest left communities living in fear.

Redcar and Cleveland MP Anna Turley has warned that she “will not accept this kind of behaviour becoming normalised”, with communities across Teesside continuing to witness disorder and violence following the fatal A66 crash last weekend which claimed the lives of seven people.

  PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough died when their marked police vehicle was involved in a crash with a Volkswagen Passat in South Bank, **__Middlesbrough__**, at 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

The five men who died in the Passat were named as Michael Robert Cahill, 23, Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jacob Matusiak, 23, and Makai Saddington, 18.

  A further two people, **__seven-year-old Valentina Foster and Natalie McDonald, 34,__** lost their lives in a house fire in Grangetown on Wednesday and five vehicles were torched during a __gathering for one of the seven who died in the fatal A66 wrong-way crash Thursday evening.__

The spate of incidents - which include numerous arrests for offences including murder - has been condemned across the region with Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen’s calling for an increase in policing presence over the bank holiday weekend.

The disorder has reignited the political row over police funding, with regional policing figures and Stockton West MP Matt Vickers arguing forces have been left under-resourced after years of cuts.

Durham Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen said there is no escaping the fact that Durham Police and Cleveland Police have more than 300 fewer police officers than they had in 2010, while the Government insists funding for Cleveland Police is increasing and support remains in place.

The developments come after police confirmed there is no link between the Grangetown house fire that claimed the lives of a seven-year-old girl and her aunt and the fatal A66 crash in which seven people, including two police officers, lost their lives.

In a letter to Cleveland’s Police and Crime Commissioner Matt Storey and Redcar and Cleveland Council leader Alec Brown, MP Anna Turley has warned that lawlessness cannot be allowed to take hold and is urging local authorities and police leaders to use every available measure to prevent further outbreaks of violence.

In a post on Facebook, Home Office minister Ms Turley said: “After everything our area has endured over the past week, I will not accept this kind of behaviour becoming normalised.

"I am writing publicly today to Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council and the Police and Crime Commissioner asking for a zero-tolerance approach to dangerous and antisocial behaviour, and for every appropriate power available to be used to protect our communities."

The MP has called for consideration of further measures, including dispersal orders and public space protection orders.

The call comes as politicians and policing figures warn of the lasting impact the violence has had on communities across Teesside.

Middlesborough and Thornaby East MP Andy McDonald went as far as describing the incidents as ‘terrorism’, saying many residents no longer felt safe leaving their homes or visiting shops.

Speaking on Times Radio, Mr McDonald said: “This is terrorism and we’ve got to have an appropriate response to deal with the people who are causing such mayhem in our communities and to allow people to lead their peaceful lives.”

Around 200 additional officers have been deployed to support Cleveland Police as investigations continue into the string of incidents.


Durham Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen also condemned the disorder, describing the behaviour as ‘contemptible’ and warning that organised crime continues to pose a serious threat to communities.

While stopping short on commenting on ongoing investigations, she said recent events had highlighted the harm criminal groups can inflict and stressed the need for those responsible to be brought before the courts.

Ms Allen also renewed calls for greater investment in policing, claiming Durham and Cleveland have more than 300 fewer officers than they had in 2010.

It’s a sentiment shared by Stockton West MP Matt Vickers, who said Cleveland Police had been operating under severe financial pressures for years, while also pointing to a £1.3m funding shortfall facing Cleveland Fire Brigade.

Mr Vickers said he had repeatedly raised concerns about police funding in Parliament and called for an urgent review of the funding formula to better reflect demand in areas such as Teesside.

   

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

He said: “The loss of two Cleveland Police officers this week is a devastating reminder of the risks our officers face every night to keep our communities safe; they were heroes who did not return home.”

Tees Valley Combined Authority Mayor Ben Houchen joined the shouts for further police resources, saying he was receiving a full briefing from Cleveland police about the ongoing investigations.

He said: “While the police are doing all they can under immense pressure, I have urged Cleveland Police to bring in the additional resources needed over the bank holiday weekend, so residents across our region can have the reassurance they deserve.”

Their comments came as the Government insisted Cleveland Police would continue to receive support.

A Home Office spokesperson said ministers remained in close contact with Cleveland Police and local representatives following the recent incidents.

The spokesperson said total police funding across England and Wales would increase to £18.5bn this year and confirmed Cleveland Police is set to receive up to £197.8m, an increase of up to £7m on the previous year.

The Government said it was also reviewing the police funding formula as part of wider plans to reform policing and ensure forces have the resources, they need to keep communities safe.

But as political leaders debate funding and powers, the message from those representing affected communities remains focused on restoring public confidence.

For many residents living through days of uncertainty and unrest, the overriding demand is simple: an end to the violence and a return to safety.
