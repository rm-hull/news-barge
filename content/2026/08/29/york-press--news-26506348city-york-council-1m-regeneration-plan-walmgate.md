ALMOST £1m of regeneration is earmarked for a York neighbourhood, the city council revealed.

A neighbourhood improvement plan (NIP) for an area of Walmgate has been formulated and funded and is set to be put in front of City of York Council bosses early in September.

The news follows a recent decision to build new social housing on the former Willow House care home site in Long Close Lane in the area, as reported by The Press last month.

  


The homes are at the heart of the South Walmgate NIP, which also includes new landscaping and estate upgrades.

Dave Merrett, executive member for housing at City of York Council, said: "The masterplan’s projects and ambitions prioritise community involvement and multi-agency collaboration alongside investment in local spaces and services.

"The new genuinely affordable homes, for social rent and shared ownership, will be set in an attractive position offering sustainable living in a vibrant city centre location."

  An architect drawing of the proposals for social housing on the site of Willow House in Long Close Lane off Walmgate 

*(Image: City of York Council)*

A council spokesperson said the homes form the next phase of its housing delivery programme and have been shaped with input from residents.

City of York Council has appointed a contractor to demolish the former care home.

The spokesperson said the scheme is supported by almost £1 million in funding, including £500,000 from the council’s local transport grant, £250,000 from the council housing budget, and £217,000 from York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority via the Mayor's high street funding.

The NIP has been shaped through consultations with residents and proposes improvements to the high street, enhanced security, a community-managed garden, and a heritage trail.

It also aims to make the area more accessible for pedestrians and cyclists.

  Cllrs Merrett and Ravilious at an area in the Walmgate estate set to be regenerated under City of York Council plans 

*(Image: City of York Council)*

Kate Ravilious, executive member for transport at City of York Council, said: "We are developing options to improve accessibility and make walking, wheeling and cycling safer and easier in the Walmgate area.

"These include considering improved level access to the shopping parade, additional Blue Badge parking, and improvements to the cycle route linking Fishergate with Navigation Road.

"The proposals will be developed further through stakeholder and public engagement."

Broader improvements are also planned for the surrounding estate and high street, with the goal of supporting businesses and encouraging footfall in the area.

Pete Kilbane, the council's executive member for economy and culture, said: "We’ve sourced significant funding for these projects to bring them together into a coherent single plan.

"They’ve been developed to improve the economic success of Walmgate’s local high street, set in a historically rich area close to the internationally important city walls and Walmgate Barbican."

The council spokesperson added: "Work is also underway to secure ownership of a small number of brick stores and garages required to deliver the Willow House development safetly and efficiently.

"The council's executive will be asked to approve the preparatory work needed to support the potential use of compulsory purchase powers, should they be required.

"Any decision to make a compulsory purchase order would be considered separately by the Executive later."

The NIP will be presented to the council’s executive for approval in early September.
