# Two sentenced over £266m Bradford money laundering operation

## Two men have been sentenced for their roles in a £266 million money laundering operation centred on a Bradford business and believed by police to be the largest investigation of its kind in UK legal history.

The operation involved criminal cash being funnelled through Bradford-based scrap jewellery business Fowler Oldfield Ltd before being used to buy high-purity gold which was shipped to Dubai using fake invoices.

West Yorkshire Police's investigation established that more than £200 million in criminal cash was laundered through the company's NatWest bank account between 2014 and 2016.

Most of the money being directed to Fowler Oldfield by 2016 was shown to have been sent there by Shahid Qadar, who was described in court as an international money launderer based in Dubai.

![Baqa Haider](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI0NjAxZWRlLWQyZWItNGFiZi04YjA1LTM0ZjQwNDdkYzAzMzoyZjUwYjAyMy0wMmMyLTRkMzYtYjQ2MS01ODc2YjdjMDYzODM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Pure Nines Ltd, a company in London's Hatton Garden set up by its owner and director Samir Jagirdar, was used as a front for delivering cash to Fowler Oldfield and purchasing and exporting the "clean" gold.

Jagirdar's brother-in-law, Baqa Haider, was also involved, with the two men creating fake shipping invoices and travelling to Dubai to export and deliver the gold.

The investigation into Fowler Oldfield began in 2016 after a security company raised concerns about a huge increase in the amount of cash its staff were collecting from the premises.

The long-established company had legitimately bought scrap jewellery, including gold, for cash, but police said its business model had changed dramatically to processing cash on a large scale.

![West Yorkshire Police's investigation established that more than £200 million in criminal cash was laundered through the company's NatWest bank account between 2014 and 2016](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI0NzY0M2I0LTBjNTgtNDIzMi04YTIxLTRiNzczOGQ3OWRhYjo1ZmIwMmU5Yi05ZGRhLTRmYWItYjNlZi1hMjA4OTQ1NTlhNTk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Banking enquiries showed cash coming into the company's accounts had risen from £11,000 in 2012 to more than £650,000 the following year.

By 2015, payments into the company had risen to £95 million.

Surveillance by investigators showed couriers regularly arriving at Fowler Oldfield's premises on Hall Lane in Bradford carrying large bags of cash.

At times, the money was being processed at a rate of £1.7 million a day.

![The cash was banked by Fowler Oldfield and used to purchase high-purity gold grain, which was then shipped to Dubai under fake shipping invoices](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBmMzRjMWEwLThlNTMtNDZkZi05ZTRhLWM1NjBmNWRlZjI3NDpmZDgyMjVlNC1lZTgzLTQ2MDctODg4Ni0yY2ZlMTNjZDc2ODM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



The cash was banked by Fowler Oldfield and used to purchase high-purity gold grain, which was then shipped to Dubai under fake shipping invoices.

Police said the process gave apparent legitimacy to criminal proceeds while helping those behind the cash avoid checks they would have faced if they had attempted to bank the money or buy gold themselves.

In September 2016, officers executed search warrants at Fowler Oldfield and two businesses in London, including Pure Nines.

A total of 16 people were arrested and cash and gold worth more than £4 million were recovered.

![A total of 16 people were arrested and cash and gold worth more than £4 million were recovered](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjUwZDcwM2YzLTk2OGEtNDE5MS1hNjFmLTQ5ZTFiZTc2YTI2YzoxZmI4Y2Y3Zi1iMTViLTRiYWYtYTg4MC00NDYwNTg2MmI5NjI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Internal CCTV from Fowler Oldfield showed staff processing large amounts of bank notes through automated counting machines before packing the cash for collection by legitimate cash-in-transit companies.

At Pure Nines, investigators found evidence the premises were mainly being used to count cash rather than for genuine gold trading.

Officers discovered a holdall filled with cash, an automated counting machine and bundles of money in a safe and on a worktop.

They also found what appeared to be 147 gold bars in the safe.

On closer examination, however, police found only one was actually gold, while the remaining bars were less valuable silver which had been gold-plated.

West Yorkshire Police's Economic Crime Unit went on to conduct years of detailed analysis, including examining invoices, emails and paperwork and studying more than 8,000 hours of CCTV footage.

Investigators calculated that more than £266 million had been paid into Fowler Oldfield's accounts between January 2014 and September 2016.

In December 2021, NatWest was fined £264,772,619.95 following a Financial Conduct Authority prosecution for failing to comply with money laundering regulations in relation to its monitoring of Fowler Oldfield's banking activities between November 2012 and June 2016.

Money laundering charges were subsequently brought against a number of defendants following work between West Yorkshire Police and the Crown Prosecution Service.

In March 2025, four men were handed prison sentences after being convicted of money laundering following a trial at Leeds Crown Court.

Fowler Oldfield director Daniel Rawson, 49, of Syke Green, Scarcroft, was sentenced to ten years and ten months in prison.

Fellow director Gregory Frankel, 48, from Shipley, was sentenced in his absence to 11 years and eight months.

Haroon Rashid, 56, of Toller Lane, Bradford, was sentenced in his absence to ten years, while Arjun Babber, 34, of Trevor Place, Westminster, was handed an 11-year sentence in his absence.

Frankel, Rashid and Babber are believed to have left the country. Police are continuing efforts to locate them and secure their extradition to the UK.

Following a further trial which concluded in July this year, Haider and Fowler Oldfield staff member Nathan Rivers were convicted of money laundering.

Jagirdar had pleaded guilty to the money laundering offence before the trial and is due to be sentenced at a later date.

Haider, 51, of Bob Marley Way, Lambeth, and Rivers, 45, of Rathmell Road, Leeds, have now been sentenced at Leeds Crown Court.

Haider was jailed for six years.

Rivers was given a 27-month prison sentence, suspended for two years, with a curfew requirement.

Proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act remain ongoing against all those convicted.

Gary Smith, 62, of Waterfront Mews, Apperley Bridge, Bradford, is also due to stand trial at Leeds Crown Court from February 1, 2027, charged with failing to report suspicious activity regarding money laundering while working in the regulated sector.

Ramona Senior, who was head of West Yorkshire Police Economic Crime Unit at the time and now leads the Yorkshire and Humber Regional Organised Crime Unit, said: “Those convicted of involvement in this criminal enterprise played their part in an operation that was laundering criminal cash on a truly industrial scale.

“Behind every bank note that went through their fingers is the harm that serious and organised crime causes in our communities, whether that be from the supply of controlled drugs and the serious violence that accompanies it, or the cruelty and exploitation of human trafficking and modern slavery.

“Money laundering is not a victimless crime and those who involve themselves in it are helping criminals to prosper from the misery of others.

“This was the largest ever investigation undertaken by West Yorkshire Police’s Economic Crime Unit.

“It was a hugely challenging and complex investigation which has required an enormous amount of tenacity and skill by our specialist investigators. Their diligence and commitment, alongside our colleagues in the Crown Prosecution Service, over a lengthy and protracted investigation and court process has resulted in these convictions and sentences.

“West Yorkshire Police is firmly committed to making sure criminals have nowhere to hide their activities or the money they make from them, and we hope the public will take some reassurance from the successful outcome of this landmark case.”

Officers are continuing to appeal for information that could help locate Frankel, Rashid and Babber.

Ms Senior added: “We remain absolutely determined to do everything we can to ensure these men are found and returned to the UK to serve their sentences.

“They will never be forgotten about and the longer they stay at large only delays them starting to serve the sentences rightly imposed on them for this serious and significant crime.

“I would urge anyone who has any information on the current whereabouts of any of the three or who knows about anyone they may be in contact with, no matter how slight or insignificant that information might seem, to contact us immediately.”
