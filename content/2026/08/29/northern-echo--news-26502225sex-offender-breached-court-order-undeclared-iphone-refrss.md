A convicted paedophile ran the gauntlet of having a suspended prison sentence activated as he was unaware the Republic of Ireland is not part of the UK.

Darron Wilkinson was said to have "put his job before a court order", by making four work trips to Dublin without informing police, putting him in contravention of the terms of a Sexual Harm Prevention Order.

It meant the now 51-year-old delivery driver was at risk of activation of a two-year suspended prison sentence, imposed at Newcastle Crown Court, in August last year.

The sentence was imposed following Wilkinson’s guilty pleas to three offences arising from sexualised communication with what he believed to be a 12-year-old girl who he exchanged messages with, on an online chat site.

Wilkinson, who was living in Murton, County Durham, at the time, suggested travelling to Coventry for a sexual liaison with the ‘girl’.

But the court was told Wilkinson had, actually, been chatting to a decoy undercover police officer, posing as the girl.

As part of the sentence, Wilkinson was made subject of a Sexual Harm Prevention Order and registration as a sex offender, both for ten years.

Wilkinson, by then of Victoria Terrace, Trimdon Colliery, appeared at Durham Crown Court in May after admitting failing to comply with the notification requirements as a sex offender.

The court heard that it related to him failing to give police seven days’ notice when he made long-haul delivery trips to Ireland.

Wilkinson told the court all were work-related visits, but he had since lost his job due to strict bail conditions, leaving him unable to make the trips to Ireland.

Recorder Mark McKone told Wilkinson: “It seems to me you put your work before this court order.”

He said he would put off passing sentence for six months on condition the defendant avoids any further breaches.

Deferring sentence until November 27, he lifted the bail conditions, to enable the defendant to try to get back into work, in the meantime.

Wilkinson, now of Portmeads Rise, Birtley, appeared back before the court on Thursday (August 28) for a further breach of the SHPO, as an iPhone he had not declared having was found in his possession upon his arrest for the breaches.

But the court heard that offence pre-dated his previous court appearance, in May.

Recorder McKone told Wilkinson he would give him, “the benefit of the doubt”, as he had when, “you went to the Republic of Ireland, believing it was still part of the UK.”

Dan Wilberforce, for Wilkinson, said part of the problem was the defendant's “understanding of the order”, not helped by the fact he has only partial literacy and, “struggles with documentation.”

Recorder McKone told Wilkinson: “Between January 21 and April 13 this year you went to Dublin four times and, when you left, you were required to give seven days’ notice to the police.

“It’s your case you didn’t realise the Republic of Ireland is not part of the UK.”

The Recorder said it now emerges that upon his arrest on April 26 the defendant had an iPhone which had been in his possession for six weeks.

He said that should have been brought to the court’s attention when Wilkinson appeared on May 28.

So, he, again, gave the defendant the “benefit of the doubt”, and fined him £50 for the breach.

He reminded Wilkinson that the deferment of the sentence remains in place until November 27.

Recorder McKone told him: “My advice to you is, if in doubt, ask your probation officer to check you are not further breaching the order.”
