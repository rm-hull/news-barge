# Linton-on-Ouse: asylum camp could make North Yorkshire villagers 'prisoners in their own homes'

## Sir Alec Shelbrooke MP has said the Government’s plans are already impacting people in the village, with children being pulled out of the primary school.

North Yorkshire villagers fear they could become “prisoners in our own homes” should the Government turn a former military base into an asylum camp.

The Home Office has pledged to close asylum hotels and is looking to move migrants into former Ministry of Defence sites across the country.

Wendy Hood, proprietor of the Linton Lock Inn, which has a cosy restaurant, marina and campsite on the banks of the Ouse, told The Yorkshire Post: "It isn't fair.

![RAF Linton-On-Ouse in North Yorkshire. PIC: Owen Humphreys/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBjYTBkNjEzLTZiODUtNGJkYy1iOTA1LTJkZDZhNDBiNTg5MDo1NTQ0NzI5Zi0wZWVlLTQxZGItOGI5MS01MjQ2ODEwMzEzMDM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



“Can you imagine being a parent of one of the children attending our primary school?

“And who would camp on my campsite, knowing there are more than a thousand asylum-seeking men in the village?

"I feel like I may have to employ additional security, and improve the boundaries, fencing and even consider barbed-wire where necessary.

“That would effectively make us prisoners in our own village and homes."

Tory MP Sir Alec also raised concerns that people in the village are now unable to sell their houses, as they could be pushed into negative equity.

There are at least eight properties currently listed for sale or let along the high street in Linton-on-Ouse.

![Wetherby and Easingwold MP Sir Alec Shelbrooke. Credit: Sir Alec Shelbrooke](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/28/13/06/Sir-Alec-Shelbrooke.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)



“Nobody can sell their house at this moment in time, it would potentially put people in negative equity,” the Wetherby and Easingwold MP has said.

“We have heard reports that people who were going to send their children to the primary school have now withdrawn that application.

“All of these points are very understandable from people, so already it is having a hugely detrimental effect on the village.”

Sir Alec also revealed that Yorkshire Water had informed him it would have to undertake “detailed review” of the supply to RAF Linton-on-Ouse should it be turned into a migrant camp.

“One of the things it hasn’t got is a strong enough water supply for that base, so there will be a lot of disruption with getting that sorted out,” he said.

“That water system is derelict, effectively. Yorkshire Water sent me an email saying they would not be confident the system could take the increase in pressure.”

A spokesperson for the firm said: “At this stage, we can confirm that a water supply exists to this site, but we would need to undertake a more detailed review of requirements and network capacity should any substantive redevelopment or change of use be progressed.

“Should this go ahead, then Yorkshire Water would carry out the relevant surveys and investigations necessary to accommodate an increase in supply request, in the same way that we would for any other new development or change of use request.”

The plans have attracted cross-party condemnation from the Labour mayor and Lib Dem MPs, as well as the Conservatives.

Andy Burnham recently told this paper that he would make sure concerns raised by York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith were taken on board by the Home Office.

A Home Office spokesperson said: “We are closing every asylum hotel and moving asylum seekers into alternative accommodation, including former military sites.

“We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, engaging with local authorities to reduce the impact on communities.

“And we are reducing the burden on taxpayers, with asylum costs down by over £1bn since the general election.”
