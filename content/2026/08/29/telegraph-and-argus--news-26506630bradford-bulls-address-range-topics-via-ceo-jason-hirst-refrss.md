BRADFORD Bulls have sunk to the bottom of Betfred Super League having lost 10 of their last 11 games.

Injuries have bitten hard, but performances have often been far below the required standard too this summer.

Bulls’ CEO Jason Hirst has addressed the fans in a lengthy statement this week ahead of the first-team’s trip to Toulouse, discussing a number of topics, including what the future holds.

  **A THANK YOU**

He said: “I write to you to say a heartfelt ‘thank you’ to each and every fan who has supported our club through the turnstiles, this year, upon our return to Super League after a long and quite often painful 12-year absence.

“Please trust me when I say, your support is greatly appreciated, needed and never taken for granted.

“I also write to offer a sense of perspective on this season and offer some insight into what we’re doing to improve and kick-on, next season.

“As a long-term fan myself, I understand and feel the joy of victory and particularly the pain of defeat, as much as anyone, as is the case with all my colleagues.

“A win or loss can and does make or break our week in many respects.”

  **SPENDING WISELY**

Hirst added: “As one of, if not the lowest spenders in Super League, I remain incredibly proud of our first season back among the elite, especially our start to the season, which saw us win three of our opening five Super League fixtures and defeat London Broncos, away in the Challenge Cup.

“It’s worth us all remembering and reminding ourselves, that unlike a lot of our competitors, we don’t have a rich benefactor.

“Instead, as is often the Bradford way, we have rolled our sleeves up and worked incredibly hard to return to the top level.

“As someone reminded me recently, having to do it that way will make any future success all the more meaningful and sweeter.”

  **INJURY CRISIS**

Hirst conceded: “Since those opening six matches, it’s got considerably more difficult to win as regularly.

“Our horrendous and unprecedented injury list has, without doubt, contributed to that significantly – that’s a fact.

  Esan Marsters badly injured his Achilles in April and is unlikely to return again this season. 

*(Image: Alex Whitehead/SWpix.com)*

“We have never offered it up as an excuse and that’s testament to our club and in particular Kurt Haggerty and our players.

“It is worth remembering those early season performances and results when we had a near to fully fit squad and close to our strongest matchday 17.

“Because you saw what we could achieve despite us only having circa six weeks to assemble a squad.

“That gives me and hopefully you, real confidence as to what Kurt and our 2027 squad can achieve, as we began our ongoing recruitment planning months ago.”

  **SHORT-TERM FUTURE**

But Hirst added: “Before 2027, however, have three vitally important matches left this season.

“This season where we can still finish anywhere between ninth and 14th, with between six to nine wins.

“Our team and club aim, as always, is to perform to the best of our ability, play with pride and win.

“The season isn’t over, the players are all conscientious, meaning that they all want to win as many matches as possible, regardless of whether they’ll be with us next season, or not”.

"That’s the culture we’ve installed these last four years, on and off the field.

"That’s the foundation as to why we’re back in Super League.

"Neither Kurt, nor me, will tolerate anything other than total commitment to the season’s end, no matter how ravaged we are with injuries.

"You, the fans and all the club’s staff and volunteers deserve nothing less, no matter how difficult it’s likely to be.

“I, for one, am proud of how our depleted squad of players have competed throughout the season, save for one or two exceptions.

“Exceptions that all teams, in all sports, have throughout a season, included more talented ones than ours.

"With the greatest respect to all our other squads in my tenure as CEO, this is, in my opinion, the most professional, unified, hardworking and determined one we’ve had.

“What we sometimes lack in quality, we compensate for through sheer hard work and pride in our badge.”

  **QUESTIONS OVER APPROACH**

Addressing absences and planning for this year and next, Hirst said: “With regard to injuries, we have, for a number of reasons, tried to avoid the loan market, as much as possible, this season.

“Those reasons include building a culture of progression and pathways, not loans, as well as practical and financial reasons.

“Unless it’s an absolute necessity, why would we spend valuable money in bringing in loan players who do not improve our team or results?

“Much better we save the money to spend on our 2027 squad and backroom staff.

“The fact is that quality Super League loan signings are few and far between.

“More significantly, it’s essential we run our club sustainably, that’s the bigger picture that we all need to be conscious of.

“The reality is, we’re damned if we do and damned if we don’t.

"In my first year as CEO, we had little choice financially, but to rely on the loan market and dual registration.

“That, somewhat, understandably drew criticism from lots of our fans, but it was borne out of necessity.

“Fast forward four years and some fans now question why we don’t use the loan market more.

“But when we do, others bemoan the fact that we’re not giving our own youngsters a go.

“I can appreciate and understand both viewpoints, but we cannot please everybody all of the time.

“It’s all a fine balance and I ask that you trust us to make these decisions in the best interests of our team and club.

“The vast, vast majority of our decisions these last four years have proven to be the correct ones.

“Personally, I’m extremely proud to see a whole host of young players, including various homegrown ones, make their Super League and, in some cases, first team debuts this season.”

  Young homegrown prop Jamie Gill has been handed opportunities this season that he might never have expected some six to eight months ago.

  **WHY SO MANY OUT?**

Hirst added: “Rightly so, there’s been lots of fan conjecture as to why we’ve suffered so many injuries.

“The answer is that there are lots of reasons, one being that a lot of our squad having stepped up from part-time to full-time, something that will not be the case next year.

“The topic of injuries and conditioning is no different to many, many others within our club in that we constantly review them with the aim of learning lessons and implementing improvement actions.

“As such, we will be adding to and strengthening our Medical, Physio and Strength and Conditioning Department, as can be evidenced by the appointment of Andrew Dixon.

“That appointment alone would not have been possible 12 months ago given how much we had to spend on players and how much we spent on increasing our vitally important IMG grading score that ensured our return to Super League.”

  **GOODBYES AND HELLOS**

And looking to the short and longer-term future, Hirst said: “Please help us in that quest (to end the season on a high) by collectively and unequivocally backing the team, not least in our home fixture versus Castleford, next Thursday.

“That night, you’ll have the unique opportunity to wave and say goodbye to numerous players who have served our club so well over recent seasons.

“I know, full well, that the players would really appreciate that and what better send off for them?

“I make no apology for again saying, the more people through the turnstiles and the more you spend inside the ground, the more money we have to spend in improving our squad for 2027 and beyond.

“Talking of 2027, we’ve already made and announced four great signings in Sam Eseh, Cam Scott, John Bateman and James Bentley (with released Hull FC forward Ligi Sao reported to be the next to join, by Love Rugby League), all impressive players who will significantly improve our squad and results.

“There are also more signings to be made, with various talks ongoing, so expect more signing announcements very soon.”

  **SEASON TICKETS**

And on one final point that will directly affect the fans, Hirst said: “You can also expect season tickets to go on sale next month, with some attractive reasons to buy early which again, only strengthens our ability to improve our on-field squad.

“Season ticket prices are always an emotive subject.

“What I can assure you of is that we’ve balanced the need to make them as affordable as possible against the need to generate income to add quality to our 2027 playing squad.

“So, I politely ask that if you can afford to, please buy a season ticket and encourage others, who might be undecided, to take the plunge.”
