## I ate breakfast, lunch, and tea at Greggs and one item stole the show

Maxine tucks into the PM's Greggs choice: "the sausage, bean and cheese thing" at Greggs in York. Photo - NQ

  *Our reporter MAXINE GORDON ate every meal at Greggs for one day – here's her verdict*

HIGH street fast-food favourite Greggs rarely misses a publicity opportunity.

So when our new Prime Minister Andy Burnham revealed in an interview that his favourite menu item was "the sausage, bean and cheese thing ", the bakery chain was quick to respond. A digital advert soon appeared promoting "The sausage, bean and cheese thing – as eaten by the Prime Minister".

Burnham himself shared the post on Instagram, joking: "It has a nice ring to it tbf" followed by the crying laughing emoji.

You can't buy publicity like that.

  Andy Burnham's post on social media


Greggs – a proud British brand founded in Newcastle in 1939 – has always had a canny knack for tapping into the public mood, whether it's the now-famous vegan sausage roll or its unlikely but hugely successful fashion collaboration with Primark, which produced the must-have Greggs bucket hat.

In fact, it was the vegan sausage roll that last tempted me through Greggs' doors. I remember joining the national craze when it launched, popping into the St Sampson's Square branch in York and hoping they hadn't sold out. I was sceptical, but it turned out to be an impressively convincing version of the original.

Since then, though, Greggs has largely disappeared from my food radar. Not because I don't like it. Quite the opposite. Pastries are simply too delicious. If I could eat sausage rolls every day with no health and waistline consequences, I'd happily do so.

I put it down to my Scottish heritage – and a childhood diet of chip-shop dinners where everything was deep fried (you can take the lassie out of Scotland but not the Scotland out of the lassie).

So, inspired by the Prime Minister, and with the blessing of my editor and The Press's expenses account, I decided to return to try not only the “sausage, beans and cheese thing” but spend a full day eating breakfast, lunch and tea at Greggs.

Before setting out, I took a look at the menu online.

The biggest surprise was the sheer choice. While Greggs is still synonymous with bakes and sausage rolls, there are also porridge pots, yoghurts, salads, sandwiches, fruit pots and wraps.

The menu includes calorie information and traffic-light nutrition labels. Frankly, some of that makes sobering reading. There's nothing quite like discovering that a pair of pepperoni pizza slices contains more than half of your daily calorie allowance to make you wonder whether you should look for a healthier option.

But that is easy at Greggs too thanks to its Balanced Choice range, which includes items with fewer than 400 calories and no red traffic lights on the nutrition guide.

Armed with this knowledge, my Greggs marathon began at 8.30am.

I headed to the branch in York's Pavement, just a short walk from our office in Walmgate. The cafe area was busy but I managed to find a table after a short wait in the queue.

  Greggs in Pavement, York. Photo - NQ


The breakfast meal deal looked good and I ordered a bacon roll, a flat white and a yoghurt with strawberry compote for £4.25. What a bargain. In some cafes, you almost pay that for a frothy coffee alone.

I was asked if I wanted sauce on my sarnie – brown please – but noticed there was no option for the bread (it's white or nowt; definitely no ciabatta or sourdough here!).

  Breakfast at Greggs in York. Photo - NQ


The bacon buttie was good – three generous pieces of back bacon, nicely cooked. The roll was soft and fluffy. It would be the perfect thing to eat after a boozy night out.

The flat white came from a machine but it was surprisingly good. Strong enough that you could actually taste the coffee rather than just warm milk.

Although quite full, I ate the yoghurt, hoping that the advertised 7g of protein might help keep hunger at bay until lunch. It did the trick, but by 12.30pm I was ready for Greggs round two.

This time I invited a colleague to join me.

Back at the Pavement branch the queue was much longer and we were lucky to secure a table.

For lunch I ordered another meal deal – the tuna crunch baguette, an apple and strawberry fruit pot, a second flat white, and, of course, the Prime Minister's favourite: "the sausage, bean and cheese thing".

  Bakes at Greggs in York. Photo - NQ


My colleague also chose a meal deal and the total price was £14.55. It's worth noting that you pay a bit extra for eating in.

The bake had to be eaten immediately while it was still warm. It was love at first bite. Imagine a square sausage roll stuffed with cheesy beans, what's not to like?

Could I have eaten another one? Absolutely. Maybe even three (hence the reason I have to resist going into bakeries like Greggs!).

Despite being super tasty, the bake wasn't particularly filling. Luckily, I had the tuna crunch baguette too. Given the 10/10 deliciousness of the "sausage, bean and cheese thing", the tuna crunch baguette had big boots to fill.

And it didn't disappoint. Generously filled and not drowned in mayonnaise, you could actually taste the tuna. The red onion added texture and crunch, while the baguette itself was decent.

The fruit pot was good too: the apple pieces still had bite and tasted fresh.

Given my whopper of a lunch, it was perhaps not surprising that when teatime arrived I wasn't ravenous.

For this final meal I recruited my husband and we headed to Greggs at the Foss Islands Road retail park shortly after 6pm.

  Meal deal advertised at Greggs in Foss Islands Road, York. Photo - NQ


Unfortunately, I quickly discovered this was not the ideal time to visit Greggs.

Stock levels were running low and some hot items had cooled down and under the cafe's rules could not be reheated at this time.

My original plan had been to try a pepperoni pizza slice, but that was no longer an option.

Instead, I did a complete 180 and ordered the chicken Caesar salad, along with a bottle of water and an iced vanilla matcha.

Despite it being cold, my husband chose the chicken katsu bake, an apple juice and a fruity flapjack and we picked up a portion of the southern fried potato wedges which were among the final hot items still on display.

  Chicken katsu bake and southern fries at Greggs in York. Photo - NQ


The katsu bake received an immediate thumbs up. "Pretty tasty," was the verdict. Some praise given it was not warm.

The potato wedges weren't at their absolute best, having clearly spent some time under the heated display, but they were still warm enough and disappeared quickly.

As for my salad, it was much better than I expected. Cooked chicken, parmesan shavings and a whole boiled egg came with a packet of croutons and a generous amount of salad leaves. The dressing was really excellent and brought the whole thing together. It was fresh and surprisingly filling and a good healthy option to offset my earlier meal choices.

  Chicken Caesar salad at Greggs in York. Photo - NQ


The iced vanilla matcha was pleasant enough, although the vanilla flavour was difficult to detect.

Our bill for two was £11.80, again a meal-deal discount had been applied.

And with that, my day of Greggs dining was complete.

Regular readers will know that we normally review local independent food outlets – but a bit like indulging in the occasional pastry, it's worthwhile visiting popular chains also to see if they live up to their reputations.

Besides the prices at Greggs, what surprised me most was the variety. Yes, you can still treat yourself to sausage rolls, steak bakes and doughnuts. But if you are looking after your waistline as much as your wallet, there are plenty of lighter options too.

And if I had to pick my favourite item, one product clearly stood out.

Whatever your view of the new Prime Minister, I have to agree with him on one thing: the sausage, bean and cheese melt is the Greggs item worth ordering.
