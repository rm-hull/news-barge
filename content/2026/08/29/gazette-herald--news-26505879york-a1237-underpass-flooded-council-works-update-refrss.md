WALKERS, cyclists and others using a York underpass have had to traverse a large pool of stagnant water, with works to planned to address it.

Liberal Democrat Cllr Anne Hook, of Rural West York ward, said the underpass under the A1237 near Knapton could become totally unpassable with rain forecast in the coming days.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, City of York Council’s Labour transport spokesperson, said a temporary raised walkway had been installed to maintain the important link ahead of works to fully reopen it.

A council spokesperson said a water pump had been removed and sent off for repairs, with works set to be finished by the end of September.

It comes as the council put an emergency temporary traffic restriction in place in the underpass on Friday, August 28.

The restriction is set to remain in place until 7pm on Thursday, September 17.

Liberal Democrat ward members Cllr Hook and Cllr Emilie Knight and Labour Transport Executive Member Cllr Ravilious have all reported the issue and called on the council to fix it.

The opposition Liberal Democrat group said the underpass provided a safe route for people to get to the York Community Woodland without having to cross the busy A1237.

The group said following the flooding: “Cyclists can just about get through as long as they don’t mind having wet feet, but pedestrians have to walk the plank which is part of a rickety makeshift walkway.

“Wheelchair users and people with buggies risk being partially submerged if they try to use the route.”

  Cllr Kate Ravilious, Labour transport executive member on City of York Council 

*(Image: City of York Council)*

Cllr Hook said the situation highlighted the need for the council to be properly prepared for breakdowns of water pumps so they could be fixed promptly.

She added it raised questions about whether the flooding was linked to wider drainage issues in the area.

The Liberal Democrat said: “With more heavy rain in the forecast for the coming days, I am concerned that the underpass will become totally impassable, which would be a real blow.”

Labour Transport Executive Member Cllr Ravilious said she first reported the issue after cycling through the area a couple of weeks ago.

The executive member said: “I know this route is an important link for residents of Rufforth and Knapton, as well as for people accessing York Community Woodland, which is why we have temporarily installed the raised walkway.

“As the pump is a council maintained asset, our highways team have worked with specialist contractors to investigate and aim to fully reopen the underpass as soon as possible.”

The council’s spokesperson said they appreciated concerns which have been raised about the underpass.

The spokesperson said: “To minimise disruption we are installing a raised walk way to allow for continued use by walkers and cyclists who are asked to dismount.

“Due to size and weight limitations horse riders are asked to use the diversion route, using the crossing point on the A1237 to Moor Lane Bridleway.”
