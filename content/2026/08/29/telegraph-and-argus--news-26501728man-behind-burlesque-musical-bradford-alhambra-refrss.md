YOU might never have heard of Steven Antin, but you’re probably familiar with his work.

He was Troy, the preppie bad boy in The Goonies movie. He was the teen heartbreaker in 80s cult classic The Last American Virgin. He was Jessie in MTV video favourite Jessie’s Girl. He helped his sister, Robin, create The Pussycat Dolls. And he was the brains behind 2010 movie Burlesque, starring Cher and Christina Aguilera.

Now Steven is the writer, producer, creative director and co-songwriter of stage show Burlesque The Musical, which arrives in Bradford next month on a UK tour direct from a hit West End run.

Step inside a club where sequins shimmer, seduction sparkles and a single song can change your life forever.

When Ali heads to New York searching for her mother, Tess Richarde, she’s swept into a dazzling underground world of ambition, temptation, music and desire - discovering not only her extraordinary voice, but the family she never knew she needed. Featuring songs by the likes of Christina Aguilera, Sia and Todrick Hall, Burlesque the Musical is “a scintillating celebration of self-discovery, empowerment and unapologetic fabulousness”.

It’s a glittering, high-voltage theatrical event packed with powerhouse vocals, jaw-dropping choreography and glamour. Produced by Christina Aguilera, the show’s creative team is led by Steven, director Racky Plews, whose credits include I’m Every Woman The Chaka Khan Musical and Green Day’s American Idiot musical, and choreographer Robin Antin.

Starring as Tess Richarde is Faye Brooks, who played Kate Connor in Coronation Street and was runner-up on Dancing On Ice in 2021. Faye’s many musical theatre lead roles include Roxie Hart in Chicago, Elle Woods in Legally Blonde, Princess Fiona in Shrek and Fran in Strictly Ballroom.

Steven Antin grew up in a creative household in Los Angeles, by his British mother and American father. His elder brother is a cameraman and producer, his sister a famous choreographer and younger brother a celebrity hairstylist.

“When I was a kid, my mom said to me one day - and she meant it in the nicest possible way - that I was good at so many things, but I didn’t master any of them, and she said ‘that’s going to serve you very well in life’,” recalls Steven. “She was a tiger mom. Fearlessly protective and very tough. ‘You can do better...everyone can’ was a phrase I heard my whole life.”

Steven’s late mother, Brenda, was a manager in the music business before becoming a successful interior designer with her own shops and line of furniture. His artist father, Mike, began his career designing posters for Broadway shows.

In the early 90s, Steve’s sister Robin put together a group of young go-go dancers. Says Steven: “We both knew Johnny Depp - I knew him through acting roles and he knew actress Christina Applegate, who shared an apartment with my sister.”

Depp co-owned The Viper Room, notorious West Hollywood nightclub, and one day in 1995 he called Robin asking if her dance troupe would do a 20-minute turn before the headliner. Says Steven: “Johnny needed a name to put outside on Sunset Strip. Robin called me and said ‘Quick, I need a name for the group now!’ We liked the film Faster, Pussycat! and I loved the name of glam rock group New York Dolls. In a flash of inspiration we combined the two and the Pussycat Dolls were born.”

Robin morphed the Dolls into a burlesque group. “I shot videos of everything they did and made a 40-minute movie of them, to help Robin get a record deal,” says Steven, who was assisted by his friend, film producer, record executive and media proprietor David Geffen. In 2003 Steven called Geffen for advice on what record label Robin should go with. Geffen contacted Interscope and the deal was done. The Dolls transformed into a fully-fledged pop group. Robin took herself out of the group to become the creator/owner, and the group was joined by Nicole Scherzinger.

“And that was when everything changed,” says Steve. “Nicole became the star of the show. Her staggering talent was clear to Robin, Interscope and everyone. Nicole is a rare breed.”

Broadly inspired by the Pussycat Dolls’ club act, Steven wrote Burlesque, developing it closely with Sony/Screen Gems President Clint Culpepper who pushed him to write the movie.

Steven directed the film, which cost $55 million to make. It took more than double that at the box office worldwide, plus a further $42 million in domestic video sales. He co-wrote a song on the soundtrack, But I Am A Good Girl, sung by Christina Aguilera.

Badgered by theatre companies to turn it into a stage show, Steven teamed up with British producer Adam Paulden, who urged him to write a new script. Workshops in LA and London were followed by hit runs in Manchester and Glasgow in 2024. “There were standing ovations every night, but I still wasn’t happy with it. I heard my mom’s words: ‘You can do better’,” says Steven. “I knew it had potential, there was lightning in the bottle, but the show wasn’t there yet.”

By the time it reached the West End, it was better, but not quite best. “I had long conversations with Culpepper and Christina and Adam. We agreed the whole vehicle needed to be leaner, meaner, tighter. There are now more songs but they’ve been edited down; in the original, some songs lasted seven, eight minutes. Scenes were too long.”

Eventually it became the show Steven wanted it to be. “I’m determined to make every production spectacular. I’m creating a template so that, wherever and whenever you see Burlesque, it’s perfection,” he says. “Every sequin, every Swarovski crystal, every eyelash, is important to me.”
