This week’s review comes to you courtesy of the ladies of Updale Women’s Institute.

By which I mean it was the hearty recommendation of this WI in the North York Moors which lured us to the subject of this week’s review.

After almost 25 years of writing this column, entirely sane and respectable organisations like WIs, rather amazingly, invite me to come and speak to them about the stories that visiting over 1,000 pub, restaurants, cafes, etc., over 25 years and writing about the experiences have thrown up.

  Inviting bar 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

And so it was that I made the journey across the North York Moors earlier this summer, to the Rosedale Reading Room, to speak to the Updale Institute about Eating Out.

As I always do, in the questions bit at the end I asked for places they would recommend the column should visit. The Blacksmith’s Arms at Lastingham was where I should go, they reckoned.

It had actually been on my ‘to do’ list for a while – perhaps a couple of years or more – but always got passed over for somewhere a bit closer to home. It is over an hour from chez nous near Northallerton.

  A classic North York Moors pub recommended by Updale Women’s Institute 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

It just needed a perfect summer’s day – of which we have had a few this year – to persuade us to make the trip across the top of the Moors.

Descending from the bleak and blasted (by the sun) high Moors, Lastingham is picture postcard pretty and the Blacksmith’s Arms is the picture postcard pretty pub at its heart, just opposite the mightily impressive Church of St Mary.

The village was dozing in the midday heat as the temperature headed towards 30C, but the interior of the Blacksmith’s was mercifully cool. There was also an inviting rear garden where we found some dappled shade and looked at menus.

  Cap 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

These were extensive and the options covered all bases – from a simple cheese and chutney roll with salad and coleslaw for £7.95 up to the Chef’s seafood salad at £19.95. There were lots of things billed as starters that would probably qualify as small plates (roast belly pork with black pudding bon bons and a goat cheese, field mushroom and Parma ham stack, for example) which could be combined to create ‘Yorkshire Tapas’ at £30 for two to include a portion of chips.

There were also some tempting pie options – lamb and mint (£18.50) and steak and ale (£17.95) – and we went for the latter and the chef’s seafood salad.

  A classic North York Moors pub recommended by Updale Women’s Institute 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

What can one say about a steak pie? By that I mean it’s just meat, gravy and pastry, so how good or bad can it be?

This was, however, something a bit special. Firstly, it was a proper slice of pie, not a little dish of filling topped with a puff-pastry lid.

The golden shortcrust pastry was superb, short enough not to go irretrievably soggy and not too thick. The steak pieces were substantial, tender and bathed in a deeply satisfying, rich gravy.

We had ordered some hand-cut chips to go with the pie, which were £3.75, but it did also come with Savoy cabbage and creamed leaks, which hadn’t been listed on the menu description. A pleasant surprise.

  Dappled shade in the Blacksmith’s Arms’ inviting rear garden 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

The seafood salad was a mélange of cod goujons, crayfish tails, smoked salmon, trout pâté and prawn cocktail with salad and coleslaw.

For less than £20 it was excellent value and special mention is merited for the batter enveloping the goujons. I can’t recall a lighter, more golden or crispier casing. It surely must have been calorie-free, I mused wistfully.

The trout pâté had that lovely, clean, freshwater taste and was plumped on a chunk of soft white sourdough. The salmon was subtly, mildly cured and the prawns in the cocktail were plump and juicy. It was all crowned by one giant, whole king prawn. The plentiful dressed salad and coleslaw were good too.

Given the size of this platter of piscine plenty, the portion of skinny fries (£3.75) we had ordered to go with it was probably unnecessary, but even so our total bill for a very satisfactory lunch with halves of lager and local bitter (from a great selection of Yorkshire brews) was only just over £40.

Service was swift and friendly.

On our way out we spotted a framed newspaper cutting featuring the Blacksmith’s chef/owner Alastair Moran, a local lad who also has the White Horse Inn at Ampleforth, which we recall being the location of a very wonderful Sunday lunch a couple of years ago.

All credit to him for managing to run two classic country pubs worthy of anybody’s time and money. And thanks to the good ladies of Updale Women’s Institute for reminding me of this moorland gem.
