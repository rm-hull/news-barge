Stars of the now classic Yorkshire-set film, Brassed Off, joined a 30th anniversary celebration of the film at Shibden Park, Halifax.

It's a film that always packs an emotional - and political - punch - but played in the grounds of Shibden Park, Halifax, with a 'local' world class Yorkshire brass band, it brought crowds to their feet for a pride-packed standing ovation.

Before the screening, cast members Tara Fitzgerald, Sue Johnston, Stephen Tompkinson, and Peter Gunn with the film's writer and director Mark Herman took to the stage to reflect on filming in Calderdale and the film's enduring legacy; its story of the human impact of pit closures on mining communities.

But arguably this night, the true stars were the musicians of Grimethorpe Colliery Band - which had played the fictional Grimley Colliery Band in the original film. Also sharing memories was original band member David Arnold who told the audience before the film; 'The band were all ex-miners and several are no longer with us - so apart from the emotion of the film, which is amazing, seeing old friends who are no longer with us is emotional.'

The cast reflected on filming in Yorkshire. Stephen Tompkinson said, 'It was an absolute privilege and something I will treasure for the rest of my life. '

Speaking about the brass band, he added; 'They are as good as any classical band in the world. They are amazing. They played Danny Boy and nobody could look each other in the eye - we were all in floods. And they played 'Orange Juice' and it was just breath-taking.

'We knew from that sound that if we could put a film around that - we might have a chance.'

Asked about filming in Yorkshire, Tara Fitzgerald added; 'I got such a massive Yorkshire welcome - and for a pound I got a lifetime's membership of the working men's club!

She added; 'These films really don't come along that often and as had been proven, it's so authentic and so full of heart.

'It became apparent that people care deeply about this subject and about the people and about this place and so that's been a testament.'

As the film was shown, the audience was invited to cheer and applaud the live band's playing which accompanied the film. There were huge cheers for the scene where the Grimley Colliery Band play at Halifax Piece Hall - just a mile or so from the outdoor venue.

And movie and reality merged when the band played to the finale filmed at the Albert Hall. The crowd might as well have been in the hallowed venue as the film hit its pitch.

Absolute credit to this fine band - they have surely seen Brassed Off plenty of times but executing the timing of the soundtrack for a live performance requires precision - with just eight hours of rehearsal, according to conductor Ben Dixon.

The event was the finale of the four-day Stage at the Lake festival—marking Shibden Park's centenary as a public space. A fitting performance full of heart.
