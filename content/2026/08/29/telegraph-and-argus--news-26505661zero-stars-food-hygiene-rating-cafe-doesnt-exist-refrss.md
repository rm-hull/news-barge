Anyone looking at recent food hygiene standards will see a community organisation has received a zero stars rating – but it doesn’t tell the whole story.

According to the Food Standards Agency, food hygiene ratings are scores from 0 to 5 awarded by local authority food safety officers after inspecting food businesses, with the aim of giving the public clear information about hygiene standards so they can make informed choices about where to eat or buy food.

The ratings are published so people can do this, but one recent case in Halifax is unusual.

Community Support Services (CSS) was awarded zero stars – meaning “urgent improvement necessary” – by Calderdale Council on July 23, 2026.

The inspection site, at Aachen Hall, Skircoat Moor Road, Halifax, is listed as a restaurant/cafe/canteen.

But the organisation, which is one of Calderdale’s leading providers of support services for people with learning disabilities, says that does not tell the whole story.

A CSS spokesperson said there were very particular circumstances behind the inspection.

“The food hygiene rating relates to exploratory discussions we initiated with Environmental Health regarding the possibility of opening a public kitchen as a training opportunity.

“We have never operated a public kitchen at this location, and we invited Environmental Health to visit as part of those discussions.

“Following consideration and the work required to open a kitchen, we have decided not to proceed with the proposal.

“This matter has no connection to the care and support services we provide, which are independently regulated by the Care Quality Commission and are currently rated Outstanding,” explained the spokesperson.
