A grant from a housing association has helped to create a wildflower and wellness area which aims to restore the balance between the need for new homes and the benefits of biodiversity.

Romanby Parish Councillor Nigel Jones has taken the lead in transforming an overgrown part of Ainderby Park into a space which supports flora, birds and small mammals and in which people can relax to enjoy the tranquillity of nature.

“Everyone appreciates the need for new housing though this can come with a loss of greenspace,” said Cllr Jones.

“We also wanted to do our bit to preserve and hopefully increase biodiversity.”

Using a £500 grant from Broadacres, via its Community and Sustainability Fund, Cllr Jones and other volunteers have created a wildflower meadow and pathway to a public seating area where people can relax and enjoy the sights, sounds and scents of nature.

Cllr Jennie Town has overseen the creation of a wildlife garden.

As well as being a space for community use, local schools can use the newly created haven to educate children on nature and the environment.

“The flowers are starting to come through and the birdboxes are in place so it’s looking good,” said Cllr Jones.

“The upfront work has been done and this is a project which will come to fruition over the next couple of years with people able to see the transformation taking place.”

With public finances increasingly tight, Cllr Jones said communities were having to look elsewhere for funding.

“It’s unlikely that council funds could have been found for a project of this nature so we are very grateful to Broadacres for this funding,” said Cllr Jones.

“It allows us to make our own small contribution to increasing bio-diversity and many local people and wildlife will benefit from it over the years,” he said.

The Broadacres Community and Sustainability Fund supports projects that deliver social, environmental or shared benefits for local people with grants of up to £500 or £1,000 in exceptional cases.

To find out more about the Fund, including how to apply, visit www.broadacres.org.uk.
