Cauldron Snout is one of County Durham’s most dramatic natural landmarks, where the River Tees thunders over a long stretch of rocky cascades beneath Cow Green Reservoir in Upper Teesdale.

Rather than falling in one sheer drop, the waterfall races down a rugged natural staircase, plunging around 61 metres over approximately 180 metres.

It is often described as one of England’s longest waterfalls, with the sound and scale of the cascade making it a striking alternative to the more familiar High Force.

The waterfall, dubbed a "best-kept secret", is set within Upper Teesdale National Nature Reserve, an upland landscape of moorland, rare plants and well-used public footpaths.

Visitors can reach it from Cow Green Reservoir car park, following a route alongside the water towards the dam and the falls.

The area is also crossed by the Pennine Way and includes two other well-known River Tees waterfalls, High Force and Low Force.

Cauldron Snout has now been named one of Northern England’s top hidden gems, placing third in a new ranking by car hire firm SIXT.

The River Tees attraction was given an overall score of 55.10 out of 100 by the company and was the highest-ranked location in County Durham.

It placed behind Wasdale Emerald Pool in Cumbria and Howick Bathing House in Northumberland.

SIXT said it reviewed attractions across northern counties and council areas using measures including online search interest, social media coverage, visitor reviews, weather, pollution, proximity to parking and public transport connections.

The ranking placed the County Durham waterfall above Aysgarth Falls’ upper pools in North Yorkshire, St Mary’s Lighthouse in Tyne and Wear and Hardcastle Crags in West Yorkshire.

For those planning a visit, the walk from Cow Green Reservoir is around 1.9 miles each way, with paths that can be uneven, steep and slippery, particularly after rain.

Visitors are advised to wear suitable footwear, allow time for the return journey and keep well away from the water, which can be especially powerful below the reservoir dam.
