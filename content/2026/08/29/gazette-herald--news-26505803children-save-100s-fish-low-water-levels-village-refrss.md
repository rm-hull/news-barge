A AMAZING effort by a group of young children has saved hundreds of stranded fish and averted a 'near tragedy'.

The recent dry weather led the water level in Sinnington to dramatically fall, leaving no water flow in the village and the fish stranded.

However, the children, led by Verity Healey, a keen conservationist, stepped in with a huge rescue effort.

Working all day Saturday and Sunday a group of around nine or more children from the village, supported by parents and friends, some of whom were on holiday, moved hundreds of fish.

Sinnington Angling Club’s secretary, John Scoble, said the Environment Agency, who were informed immediately, were very supportive and a team arrived on Tuesday with the intention of capturing any remaining fish and moving them higher up stream into deeper water.

"Fortunately, long awaited rain arrived over night and the river re-established itself and Sean McGinty, from the Environment Agency highly praised the work of the volunteers.

John said he was delighted by the outstanding effort of the children and Verity Healey and, in appreciation of their superb rescue mission, presented them all with book tokens.

He said: "In this day and age it is great to hear of such a brilliant community action. It was a true rescue mission. Very well done to the children and Verity."

A spokesperson for the Environment Agency said the river level at Sinnington was low due to the low rainfall.

"We monitored the low river level for several weeks and when the river became fragmented the local Environment Agency fisheries team planned a fish rescue.

"When the Team got to site the rainfall that occurred for two nights running provided enough water for the river flow, so the fish rescue was no longer needed.

"We have continued to monitor the site this week with no signs of the river drying up."
