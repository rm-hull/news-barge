# You’d think Manchester was where the North begins and ends for Andy Burnham - Sarah Todd

## Earlier in the week your correspondent cringed as our Prime Minister arrived in Ukraine wearing a polo shirt. Before the ink was dry on those words, he was pictured plucking at a guitar with one of the war-torn country’s soldiers.

Yes, this is a ‘me problem’ but this musical moment felt so staged. The news was watched through hands held over eyes, like witnessing drunk dancing at a wedding - but multiplied by a million on the awkwardometer.

Look, there is no doubting Andy Burnham would be better company on a night out than the previous incumbent of Number 10.

But he needs to start showing statesmanship and stop trying to be everybody’s mate.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham plays the guitar in Ukraine. PIC: Henry Nicholls/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmNhNTM0OGE1LTViMjAtNGE2Zi1iY2MxLTFjM2QwOWZlYmRlNzozY2E1YjJkZS1jMzAwLTQ5ZGYtOWZjMS1iZmU2YWRjODNjZTA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Turning up to represent Great Britain wearing the sort of top Luke Littler would sashay up to the oche in to play darts doesn’t cut the mustard. Same goes for - like a labrador puppy - the way he’s so desperate for people to like him.

Being a good leader isn’t a popularity contest and nobody knew this better than the late Margaret Thatcher. The lady in blue believed that true leadership meant standing by strict principles, not trying to be liked by everyone; warning that if a leader’s main goal is to be universally liked, they ‘end up compromising on moral and economic standards.’

It’s been a joy for our grown-up children’s friends to enjoy descending on our home. Turning up for food, free taxi rides and overnight accommodation with, of course, breakfast in the morning. Then, with one too many beds to make and half-drunk cups of coffee left on the side, there was a realisation of blurred boundaries. On the back foot, after being too soft, it’s so much harder to command a little respect. Or at the very least the gift of a bottle or bunch of flowers.

A basic example, but our Andy needs to learn that he can’t be bezzy mates with everyone. Not everybody will like him and being a leader is about much more than popularity. Taxpayers aren’t fools; we won’t hand over our money any less begrudgingly because of some TikTok video.

Talking of social media clips, footage from housing estates in Middlesborough in the wake of the deplorable deaths of the pair of police officers, has been an absolute revelation. Windows smashed in, burnt out cars, boarded up doors and law-abiding residents little more than prisoners in their own homes. It’s no exaggeration to say some streets looked straight out of Ukraine; a stark reminder that there is plenty to put right on our own doorstep.

It made this misery muse that since Mr Burnham has taken over the reins it feels as if the narrative has changed about where the north actually is. To anybody that doesn’t know, they could be forgiven for thinking it begins and ends in Manchester.

Trying to get to grips with modern technology, AI was asked to describe living in Manchester. It came back that it ‘is a dynamic, fast-paced, and cosmopolitan industrial city.’ That’s what this country bumpkin thought.

Middlesborough, it informs, has ‘localised challenges with crime and deprivation.’

That’s putting it politely. It was heartbreaking to hear older residents talk about the area they had brought their own children up in. Scrubbed doorsteps, tidy gardens, youngsters playing in the street and a real sense of community. Everybody knew everyone.

What has gone wrong?

No woe-is-me British born oik - tired of talking about illegal migrants as nothing ever gets done about them - could have had a humbler start in life than the late great country singer and phenomenal philanthropist Dolly Parton.

Her Tennessee mountain home, with one bedroom that she shared with her parents and 11 brothers and sisters, still stands. No electricity or running water.

She spoke about how they never thought of themselves as poor as they were rich in things that money couldn’t buy, symbolised by the love that her mother put into stitching from rags that coat of many colours.

Her work ethic was inspired by her father, a farmer.

She thrived on graft, certainly putting in longer hours than nine to five. Politicians please take heed of her line that ‘We can’t just hope for a brighter day, we have to work for a brighter day.’

Work is what huge swathes of the population are short of.

Those that can should do some, from apple picking at A to (this is harder than first thought) zebra crossing cleaning at Z, their benefits converted into a wage. Getting owt for nowt should be the exception rather than the norm.

It might not be a popular decision, the sort a guitar-strumming man of the people would want to make, but the legacy of instilling a work ethic would last much longer than any song (apart from those of dear Dolly).

Housing plays its part. Rather than - another one from that new centre of the universe called Manchester - Angela Rayner’s build, build, build, should we not be looking at rethinking residential areas rather than gobbling greenbelt to create yet more soulless streets?

As a country that is around £3 trillion in debt, with the double whammy of looking woefully weak when it comes to defending itself, British people don’t need a new best friend.

Aristotle nailed it with, "A friend to all is a friend to none."

Sarah Todd is a journalist specialising in farming and country life. Read her weekly column in Wednesday’s edition of The Yorkshire Post.
