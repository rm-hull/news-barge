TV presenter Steph McGovern has said Middlesbrough has been “woefully neglected” for decades and warned that the problems facing the town go far deeper than policing following a devastating week on Teesside.

The Middlesbrough-born broadcaster spoke out after seven people, including two Cleveland Police officers, were killed in a head-on crash on the A66 at South Bank last Saturday.

Pc Matthew Blades, 37, and Pc Tom Clough, 38, died when their marked police vehicle was struck by a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way along the dual carriageway.

Five people in the Passat, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, Jakub Matusiak, 23, and Michael Robert Cahill, 23, also died.

Since then, Cleveland Police has launched a major operation targeting suspected serious and organised crime, with 24 people arrested by Friday night.

Hundreds of additional officers have also been drafted into the area, while the past week has seen a string of incidents including vehicles being set alight, a caravan fire and a vehicle crashing into a wall at an address in Steele Crescent, South Bank.

On Friday (August 28), McGovern, who grew up in Middlesbrough and has maintained close links with schools, colleges, charities and community groups in the town, shared a lengthy and emotional message about the place she still considers home.

She wrote: “I grew up in this town and am still part of it through various schools/colleges, charities and community groups.


“Middlesbrough is a town that’s been woefully neglected by successive governments and politicians, of all flavours, for decades.

“Some of them, past and present, will tell you now about how lawless it has become... and yes our underfunded police service is part of the problem... but the real issue goes much deeper than this.”

McGovern stressed that those responsible for crimes should face punishment, writing: “The criminals need to be punished. There is no doubt about that.

“But don’t think that solves what is at the heart of this.”

  PC Clough (left) and PC Blades (right) 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

She pointed to deprivation, unemployment and the long-term effects of Middlesbrough’s industrial decline as factors which she believes have helped create the conditions in which some young people can be drawn into crime.

“This is a place that has been hit hard by huge industrial decline,” she wrote.

McGovern said workers affected by the disappearance of traditional industries should have been given better opportunities to retrain and move into new jobs.

  From left to right: Makai Saddington, 18, Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jakub Matusiak, 23, Michael Robert Cahill, 23 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

Instead, she argued, Middlesbrough had experienced underfunding of further education colleges, job centres, libraries, youth clubs and other public services.

“We have all been let down,” she said.

Her comments come as the events in Middlesbrough have attracted increasing national attention, with questions being raised about organised crime, deprivation and the resources available to Cleveland Police.

The force confirmed on Friday that seven more men had been arrested as part of its ongoing operation, taking the total to 24.

  Scene of the fatal crash on the A66 at in South Bank, Middlesbrough 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Four men, aged 21, 25, 35 and 40, were being questioned on suspicion of criminal damage with intent to endanger life, arson and possession with intent to supply Class A drugs after police stopped a Mercedes on Kingsley Road in Grangetown.

The suspected arson relates to a caravan set alight on Atlee Road on Wednesday, while the criminal damage investigation relates to Steele Crescent. Police said Class A drugs had also been seized.

The A66 crash itself remains under investigation.

The Independent Office for Police Conduct has said the Passat had been pursued by an armed response vehicle for just under seven minutes before it entered the A66 against the flow of traffic.

Officers abandoned the pursuit at that point and remained on the correct carriageway before the Passat later struck another police vehicle.

The week also saw another tragedy when seven-year-old Valentina Foster and her aunt Natalie McDonald, 34, died following a house fire in Birchington Avenue, Grangetown, on Wednesday.

  At 11am police officers held a minutes silence for the two officers killed on the A66 while guarding the scene of a house fire on Birchington Avenue where two people were killed 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Two men, aged 23 and 31, were arrested on suspicion of murder. Cleveland Police have since said they are not linking the fire to the fatal A66 collision.

A 48-year-old woman remained in hospital in a critical but stable condition on Friday, while a 27-year-old man was also receiving treatment.

McGovern said the deprivation some children in the town experienced was stark.

She said one youth charity she works with operates in the community where recent trouble had taken place and claimed some children there do not have adequate clothes or shoes, lack access to basic sanitary products and regularly go hungry.

“For some in these communities, crime is all they know,” she said.

“It is the only place they get validation, self worth and a seemingly sustainable income. Criminals exploit this and groom vulnerable young people into gangs.

“Then social media validates this for them.

“None of this excuses their behaviour but it is important context to help explain why it is so prolific.”

McGovern also criticised an education system which she said places too much emphasis on academic achievement, arguing that greater access to vocational training, work experience and routes into real jobs could help young people on Teesside.

  Natalie McDonald and Valentina Foster 

*(Image: UGC)*

She said investment, rather than charity, was ultimately needed.

“We don’t need charity,” she wrote.

“Places like Middlesbrough need meaningful investment, decent reskilling/training, jobs and an education system fit for purpose.”

She added that the town also had successful businesses, charities, teachers, care workers, probation officers and volunteers battling to improve their communities.

McGovern was particularly keen to push back against the idea that the criminality seen over recent days represents Middlesbrough as a whole.

“There are also lots of hardworking people in Middlesbrough who are appalled by the crimes of some in their community,” she said.

“They are no doubt sick of the terrible headlines about the place they call home.

“Wishing we could instead show some of the good things going on, or the beauty of the place, not the pockets of bad.”

Her message ended with an appeal for people to come together.

“Middlesbrough made me, as the steel used all over the world,” she wrote.

“Let’s get back to building each other up, not tearing each other down.”
