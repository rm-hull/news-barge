# Cleveland Police need more officers amid escalating violence, mayor Ben Houchen says

## Hundreds of additional officers have been deployed to help Cleveland Police, however Lord Ben Houchen urged the force to request more to provide greater reassurance to residents.

The Tees Valley Mayor has called for further police officers to be mobilised into his region over the bank holiday weekend to tackle the wave of violence engulfing Middlesbrough.

Hundreds of additional officers have been deployed to help Cleveland Police, however Lord Ben Houchen urged the force to request more to provide greater reassurance to residents.

Police have been sent in from across the country after a series of deadly incidents, starting with the head-on crash on the A66 that killed two police officers and five young men on Saturday and a house fire which claimed the lives of a seven-year-old girl and her aunt on Wednesday.

On Thursday, a house was rammed by a vehicle just minutes away from where the fatalities occurred, and a 39-year-old man was also stabbed.

Police are investigating whether the incidents are linked and also the influence of organised crime.

Lord Houchen met with Cleveland Police Chief Constable Victoria Fuller yesterday afternoon after several cars were torched in an apparent tribute to one of the young men killed in the A66 crash.

![Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmZhZWE2ZWQyLWZlYWMtNGYyOS1iOWNmLWNlMDFmMmI5ZGNmZTo3ZjIxODhhNy1kNWE4LTQ3ZTQtOGVmMC0yMzM0NGZkNDM1NWU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



“The events we’ve seen across our region over the past week are deeply concerning and people across Teesside deserve reassurance,” he said.

“I am receiving a full briefing from Cleveland Police on the ongoing investigations and the work being done to keep our communities safe.

“While the police are doing all they can under immense pressure, I have urged Cleveland Police to bring in the additional resources needed over the bank holiday weekend, so residents across our region can have the reassurance they deserve.

“My thoughts remain particularly with the families, friends and colleagues of Pcs Matthew Blades and Tom Clough, and with the innocent victims and families caught up in the terrible events which have followed.

“I want to thank the police and all our emergency services for everything they are doing in incredibly difficult circumstances.”

Pc Blades, 37, and Pc Clough, 38, died in the head-on collision with a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way on the A66 on Saturday at South Bank.

![Pc Tom Clough, left, and Pc Matthew Blades were killed in the A66 crash last weekend (Cleveland Police/PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/28/15/v2-image-production-edfbdc464fbfd463088688f5d5432f85Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg4MDEwODU2-2.85666409.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Cole Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Michael Cahill and Jakub Matusiak were also killed in the incident.

Everyone except Mr Cahill, had several previous convictions, with Worthy, Rae and Saddington all being convicted of assaulting police officers.

Cleveland Police said it was investigating the link between the crash and organised crime.

Police have arrested 17 people after the A66 crash, which followed a police pursuit, as part of investigations into alleged organised crime.

On Thursday evening, videos shared on social media appeared to show several cars on fire in Haswell, County Durham.

![A woman and child died in a house fire in Grangetown, near Middlesbrough, on Wednesday (PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/08/28/15/v2-image-production-c58f9776a19792308f6b95c00d75580bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg4MDEwOTQ3-2.85667736.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



According to reports, the event was being held to remember A66 crash victim Mr Cahill, 23, with footage showing a large crowd gathering on grassland while cars could be seen skidding around the area.

Later on Thursday, more videos shared online appeared to show burnt-out cars being moved from the field, 20 miles north of the crash site, by a tractor.

On Friday, remnants of fireworks, smoke canisters and balloons were scattered around a field where cars had been torched, with scorch marks visible on the ground.

The cars have been removed but a section of a front bumper remained in place.

Durham Constabulary Assistant Chief Constable Jon McAdam said yesterday: “The behaviour we saw at last night’s gathering was completely unacceptable.

“Our officers and the communities of County Durham and Darlington were understandably horrified by the tragedy which unfolded on the A66 last week which resulted in multiple deaths, including two serving police officers.

“We utterly condemn anyone who would use that tragedy as an excuse for disorder and expect the overwhelming majority of the public would join us in doing so.”
