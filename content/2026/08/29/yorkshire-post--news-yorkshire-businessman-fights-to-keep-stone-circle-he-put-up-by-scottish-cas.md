# Yorkshire businessman fights to keep stone circle he put up by Scottish castle

## A businessman who put up a stone circle by a Scottish castle claims council officials have taken a dislike to it because the stones were brought in from Yorkshire.

David Whitehead, from Brighouse, bought Barcaldine Castle, on Loch Creran, nine miles from Oban, from the Campbell family in 2009, and now runs it as a luxury B&B.

He said he'd always wanted to build a stone circle and chose natural-looking rocks from a quarry at Rastrick, to make the circle of 13 stones, with two inside, to commemorate the date of the Glencoe Massacre on February 13, 1692 (13/2). However he is now fighting for them to stay after officials refused a part retrospective application for planning permission.

Mr Whitehead says the circle commemorates the desperate winter journey of Alasdair Ruadh MacIain, the 12th Chief of the Glencoe MacDonalds, whose attempt to swear allegiance to King William III came too late to prevent the destruction of his clan.

He said: "I feel like I had a calling to build one. It's worth any battle to get them kept there.

"I'm convinced in 200 years time they will become part of the landscape. When you think of the Eiffel Tower, the London Eye, the Angel of the North, many things are controversial when they are put up, but after a while they become part of the landscape.”

He points out that stones for Stonehenge came from Wales, apart from one from Scotland, adding: "It would be an awful shame if they had to come down because they are from Yorkshire, which is what it boils down to."

Work on the circle started last November and was well underway when officials from Argyll and Bute Council got in touch to advise that planning permission might be required.

![David Whitehead, from Calderdale, put up the stones on land near Barcaldine Castle, which stands on the south shore of Loch Creran, and which he runs as a luxury B&B.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjE0NjNiZmYzLWI0M2UtNDE3Ni1hYjEyLWMwNmVhMDFjN2NmMDplYjcwN2QzYy04YjlhLTRiYmQtYWE3Yi0zMGQ0YTJhODk0NTk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Mr Whitehead claims that early discussions were encouraging, but the case later took a different turn.

His architects were surprised, he said, when the stones were allegedly described by the planners as “naff” in correspondence.

The circle was refused by officers earlier this month as it “detracts from the cultural and historical importance of the site and introduces a replica structure that conflicts with the established character and historical understanding of the castle”.

There were three objections – including one from a member of the Campbell family, and another from a neighbouring landowner, who keeps cows on the field, Mr Whitehead said.

He claims there is “overwhelming support” for the stone circle shown in response to a Facebook post, and says the public are welcome to come and take a look round.

He is now hoping people will write in support of his Save Our Stones campaign at saveglencoememorial.co.uk, ahead of an appeal.

Argyll and Bute Council said: “Planning permission was refused because the design, siting and materials used were considered inappropriate for the sensitive historical and cultural setting and fail to respect, preserve, or enhance the character of the category A-Listed Barcaldine Castle, contrary to national and local planning policy.”
