# Yorkshire MP leading role in crackdown on ‘cowboy builders’

## Halifax’s MP has had a key role developing new regulations to crack down on “cowboy builders” and rogue traders.

Kate Dearden is the Minister of State responsible for consumer protection, helping develop a new government-supported scheme to help residents identify and hire traders with confidence, their money protected in a trusted place until key building milestones have been hit.

This new clampdown on cowboy builders will help to close loopholes that have let unscrupulous operators fleece local families, giving residents more confidence that they are hiring trusted traders and that their money will be protected whilst building works are ongoing, she said.

From September, the new government supported scheme will deliver an Approved Code so that customers can be confident they are not hiring a rogue builder.

![Kate Dearden, Halifax MP](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjhkMjY3MjNlLTg2NmYtNDIwYi1hZmExLTg5YWZjZjQ0OTFiMToyZmU1NGZhMC0xMzQ0LTRkZTMtYTA5OC0xNjZjMzFiNDE4N2M=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Businesses who sign up to the approved code will be able to demonstrate that they meet higher standards of customer service, transparency and dispute resolution, said Ms Dearden.

The scheme will also include a new system to protect people’s money during building projects with payments directly linked to key project milestones.

Issues raised have ranged from projects abandoned halfway through, undue pressure on elderly people to overpay for shoddy work, or even builders disappearing on holiday and taking up-front payments with them despite little to no work being completed.

This announcement will also back the many builders who do the right thing by their customers, preventing them being undercut by rogue operators and helping residents support those playing by the rules, she said.

Alongside this announcement, tougher regulation will also be introduced to tackle bailiffs who act aggressively, ensuring those who fall behind on a bill are treated fairly.

Private bailiffs will be held to consistent professional standards whilst those facing enforcement action will have access to an independent complaints process – strengthening the rights of the most vulnerable.

Families in Halifax have been pressured into repayment plans that leave them unable to afford day-to-day essentials, whilst they repay what they owe, said Ms Dearden.

“When you hand a builder thousands of pounds, you’re not just trusting them with your money.

“You’re trusting them with your home.

“Yet too many people in Halifax have been left with work half-finished, thousands of pounds down the drain and homes that aren’t safe to live in.

“As the Minister responsible for consumer protection, I’m proud to now be delivering changes that will make it easier to identify reputable traders and better protect people’s hard-earned money while work is being done.

“Your home is probably the biggest investment you’ll ever make, and you deserve to know that when you put your savings and your trust in someone, you’re properly protected,” she said.
