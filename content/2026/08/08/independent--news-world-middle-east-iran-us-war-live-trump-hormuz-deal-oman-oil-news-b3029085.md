A furious Donald Trump has hit out at “leakers” as he condemned “treasonous” reports that the US has almost depleted its stock of precision long-range missiles in its war with Iran.

The president claimed the US still has “massive amounts” of the expensive munitions, adding that “large amounts are being manufactured and shipped to the US as needed”.

“The ‘leakers’ of these treasonous statements are being hunted down. Long term jail sentences will be sought!” he added in the Truth Social tirade.

Trump also denied US media reports that he had reprimanded defence secretary Pete Hegseth during a Cabinet meeting in which low ammunition stockpiles were discussed.

Two sources told Reuters news agency that the military’s surface-to-surface weapons, known as Army Tactical Missile Systems (ATACMS) and Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM) had been "virtually all" used in its war against Tehran.

Meanwhile, the US president claimed the Strait of Hormuz is “sort of open” and predicted that the war with Iran will “end pretty soon” as negotiations continue between Tehran and Oman.

The US president said although the shipping route was under US control, there was a possibility that Tehran could still “shoot things” or “drop a mine.”

## US military leader is looking for off-ramp from Iran war: report

Chairman of the Joint Chiefs General Dan Caine has privately told other top US officials that the country needs to find an off-ramp from the Iran war, according to CNN, which cited three sources familiar with the matter.

Caine’s talks with advisers to US President Donald Trump are just to make clear the limitations and pitfalls of different military options, according to the sources.

Joseph Holstead, a spokesperson for Caine, told CNN, “We do not discuss the Chairman’s confidential conversations with the President, the Secretary, or other senior leaders, nor do we comment on anonymous, agenda-driven characterizations of those conversations by sources who were not privy to them”.

A White House official praised Caine, telling the outlet: “The General always provides accurate, unbiased information and a host of options to the Commander-in-Chief, who ultimately makes decisions based on what he feels is best for US national security”.

*The Independent* has reached out to the White House and Pentagon for comment. 

## American prisoner in Iran has spoken out about 'poor' conditions

An American prisoner in Iran has spoken out about what he describes as “poor” conditions.

Reza Valizadeh, a US citizen and journalist, said in a voice memo sent to CBS News this week, “Because of the poor quality of prison food, most prisoners refuse to eat the meals provided by the prison”.

Valizadeh is reportedly one of five US citizens detained in Iran. Two of these Americans, including Valizadeh, are inside the infamous Evin Prison.

“Medical care in the prison is almost non-existent”, Valizadeh said. “The prison's doctors are rude, unmotivated and lack professional competence. As a result, most prisoners avoid seeking medical treatment unless they have no other choice”.

The other American in Evin Prison is Kamran Hekmati, who is believed to have recurring cancer, CBS News reported, citing his US-based advocate Kieran Ramsey from the nonprofit Global Reach.

“Kamran Hekmati, an Iranian American prisoner, was guaranteed medical leave for cancer treatment. However, after only one month and before the end of his approved 45-day medical furlough, he was ordered back to prison.

“This happened, even though his leave should have been extended so he could begin chemotherapy”, Valizadeh said.

## Amnesty says Gaza mass funeral highlights 'chilling' destruction of entire families

Amnesty International says a mass funeral for 112 members of the al-Hasayna and Abu Sharia families in Gaza City underscores a pattern of Israeli attacks that have wiped out entire multi-generational families.

“Israel must urgently allow access to Gaza for UN bodies and independent human rights investigators to preserve evidence, in line with the ICJ’s orders,” she added, using the acronym for the International Court of Justice.

## Ships stuck in the Strait of Hormuz risk creating global biosecurity ‘super-spreader’ event

The economic ramifications of the closure of the Strait of Hormuz are much discussed. But so far, the risk of ecological damage following a long blockade of ships has been overlooked.

Our recent research shows that the more than 1,500 commercial vessels that have been laid up in the Persian/Arabian Gulf since February pose a significant environmental threat because they have been accumulating marine biofouling species on their hulls.

As the strait reopens, these ships may spread these organisms to ports they visit, potentially causing an international biosecurity “super-spreader” event.

Such incursions of alien marine plants, animals, parasites and diseases affect native ecosystems and wild fisheries as well as aquaculture stocks and coastal industries. They can lead to significant environmental damage and economic losses.

## US Treasury targets crypto exchanges in new Iran sanctions

The US treasury has targeted two major cryptocurrency exchanges with sanctions on Friday as it seeks to dismantle illegal financial networks funding Iran's IRGC and other groups.

It said it was also sanctioning the ringleader of a network of front companies operating across multiple jurisdictions, facilitating illicit cryptocurrency activity and sanctions evasion.

The treasury said Iranian actors “exploited unlicensed or lightly regulated digital currency exchange platforms to transfer large volumes of digital assets.”

Secretary of the treasury Scott Bessent said: “The Iranian regime’s reliance on digital assets and shadow banking networks is further evidence that Economic Fury is working.

“We will continue to increase the economic pressure. Whether in dollars, rials, or crypto, Treasury will hunt down and dismantle the illicit financial networks that keep the regime afloat.”
