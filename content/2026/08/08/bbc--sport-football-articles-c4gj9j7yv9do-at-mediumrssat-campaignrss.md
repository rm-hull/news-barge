**WhenChelseaarrive in Malaysia for the final leg of their pre-season tour, new manager Xabi Alonso will be reunited with formerLiverpoolteam-mate Luis Garcia.**

Garcia is now the chief executive of Johor Darul Ta'zim, who welcome Chelsea to their 40,000-capacity Sultan Ibrahim Stadium on Sunday.

The two Spaniards go way back to 2004, when they both signed for Rafael Benitez's Liverpool on the same day.

Despite facing each other multiple times in the Spanish football ecosystem, that day at Anfield was the first time they met properly.

"He told me he didn't speak English too well before our unveiling press conference," Garcia told BBC Sport.

"I answered the first question in Spanish, as we had a translator present, and he answered in perfect English and I thought: 'Why you little?!'"

Both started in Liverpool's dramatic Champions League final win over AC Milan at the end of that season and they went on to play together for Spain.

Garcia says they remain "really good friends" and have stayed in touch, but insists Alonso is facing a difficult final overseas friendly.

After matches in Australia and other parts of Asia against elite European football opposition such as Tottenham, Juventus and AC Milan, Chelsea's tour ends in Johor.

The match will be a monumental moment in the JDT's history and for Malaysian football as a whole.

"Chelsea is the biggest test we will face this season," Garcia admits. "I'll be so happy to welcome them in Johor. The stadium is going to be packed.

"We can't wait to challenge ourselves against a top club, top players and it is great to face Alonso. I can assure you on Sunday we will compete.

"I wish him all the best [at Chelsea], but even though I love him, hopefully he ends up second to Liverpool next season."

## 'We want Malaysia to reach the World Cup some day'

![Xabi Alonso and Luis Garcia celebrate Liverpool's Champions League win in 2005](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2048/cpsprodpb/81c3/live/cd17c030-9254-11f1-b4dc-f3d0b9f07ec5.jpg)

Xabi Alonso (second from right) scored Liverpool's third goal in the 2005 Champions League final

JDT drew with Rangers and Spanish second-division side Elche before beating Cartagena, as they took on European opposition in a training camp in Alicante.

They have not lost a domestic match since 2021 and are currently on a world record-equalling 108-game unbeaten run, which could soon be extended when the Malaysian season restarts in August.

Success on the Asian continent is also becoming a realistic prospect after reaching the quarter-finals of the AFC Champions League last season.

"Hopefully in the first game of the season we'll break one of the most incredible records in football, that is the domestic unbeaten run," Garcia said.

"The owner is the Crown Prince, His Royal Highness Ismail Tunku. He has taken the club on an amazing journey. Top-class stadium and training facilities that people will be talking about.

"We don't have a limit, and that motto of 'the sky is never the limit' is something that we repeat quite often in the dressing room."

Those facilities are based on Real Madrid's aptly nicknamed White House training base.

Garcia, who splits his time between Spain and Malaysia, gained his Uefa Pro Licence and sporting director licence after retiring.

However, his work also extends to commercial activities and wider planning behind the scenes, although he leaves the coaching duties to compatriot Xisco Munoz.

There are also Spanish coaches throughout the backroom staff and academy, delivering best-in-class coaching to Malaysian talent.

"We want to increase the level of Malaysian football and have a fantastic academy. We brought Spanish coaches to the academy so we can get closer to the best, professional style of work," Garcia added.

"Spain winning the World Cup shows you the value of having a coaching programme and a football philosophy that lasts 25-30 years.

"We got to the quarter-finals of the Asian Champions League and would like more Malaysian teams in the competition.

"The next step is to compete in the Asian Champions League and last year was our main introduction. We arrived there, and we kind of caught up with the situation. It was difficult against teams that [have] got budgets that pass over $150m (£111.2m) and we only have $15m, so it's quite a difference, a big gap between.

"But we want to contribute to the development of Malaysian football to reach the World Cup some day."
