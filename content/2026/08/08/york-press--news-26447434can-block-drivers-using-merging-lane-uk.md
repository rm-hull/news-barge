Drivers in the UK are being warned of the dangers and repercussions of a common manoeuvre that stops drivers 'cutting queues'.

A merge in turn, or 'zip merge,' is a system where drivers use both lanes until a closure point, then alternate merging.

It is common on UK motorways and dual carriageways and is designed to ease congestion and keep traffic moving.

However, the rise in drivers blocking the empty lane and preventing cars from 'pushing in' is frustrating other motorists, and it can lead to a fine.

  Merging in turn takes place before an upcoming lane closure

## What does the Highway Code say about the merge in turn?

The Highway Code is not explicit on whether blocking is allowed, but Rule 134 does shed some light on the manoeuvre.

It says: "You should follow the signs and road markings and get into the lane as directed.

"In congested road conditions, do not change lanes unnecessarily.

"Merging in turn is recommended but only if safe and appropriate when vehicles are travelling at a very low speed, e.g. when approaching road works or a road traffic incident.

"It is not recommended at high speed."

A video was recently shared on Reddit of a van blocking an upcoming empty lane due to an impending lane closure, and drivers shared their anger at the situation.

One said: "There needs to be a public information advert on this, and it's so easy to show.

"You turn a 1-mile queue into a 2-mile queue by refusing to use both lanes."

Another wrote: "We're allergic to the zipper merge in the UK and I don't understand it."

## What are the dangers of blocking a merge lane?

Other experts have warned that blocking an upcoming merge in turn can lead to accidents.

Ben Welham, a motoring expert at Marshall Motor Group, said: "Blocking another vehicle can force sudden braking or risky manoeuvres in already hazardous conditions, increasing the likelihood of a collision.

"Drivers in both lanes should keep speeds low and allow traffic to merge smoothly and safely."


John Muckian, managing director of Refused Car Finance, also warned that blocking merging turns is not recommended.

Mr Muckian said: "Drivers that are using the closing lane near to the end are actually following correct instructions, not attempting to cut the queue as many people think.

"Straddling lanes or aggressively blocking other drivers can cause sudden stops and increase the risk of collisions.

"Moving over too early when driving can cut the usable space on the road in half, which turns a balanced queue into a single line that can quickly back up and block traffic at roundabouts or junctions."

## Is it illegal to block a merge lane in the UK?

Despite the Highway Code not having a specific rule against blocking the lane, it can lead to punishment if drivers are not careful.
