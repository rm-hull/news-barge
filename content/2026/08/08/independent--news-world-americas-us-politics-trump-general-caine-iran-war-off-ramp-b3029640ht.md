President Donald Trump’s Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, General Dan Caine, is seeking an “off-ramp “ from the ongoing war in Iran, according to a new report.

Caine has privately told other top administration officials that the country needs to find a way to exit the war, according to CNN, which cited three sources familiar with the matter.

Despite Trump claiming throughout the war that a peace deal, which would include his demand that Iran never obtain a nuclear weapon, was within reach, fighting has continued for more than five months.

“Caine is looking for an off ramp,” one of CNN’s sources said.

The general’s meetings with Trump officials, including Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, are just to make clear the limitations and pitfalls of different military options, according to the sources.

![President Donald Trump’s Chairman of the Joint Chiefs, General Dan Caine, is seeking an 'off-ramp' from the ongoing war in Iran, according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/08/0/44/Gen-caine.jpeg)

Joseph Holstead, a spokesperson for Caine, told CNN, “We do not discuss the Chairman’s confidential conversations with the President, the Secretary, or other senior leaders, nor do we comment on anonymous, agenda-driven characterizations of those conversations by sources who were not privy to them”.

A White House official praised Caine’s know-how, calling him an “incredible asset to President Trump’s national security team.”

“The General always provides accurate, unbiased information and a host of options to the Commander-in-Chief, who ultimately makes decisions based on what he feels is best for US national security”, the official said.

*The Independent* has reached out to the White House, the Office of the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff and the Department of State for comment. 

Trump’s war has primarily been an air campaign, relying on fighters to launch strikes against Iran instead of deploying ground troops. Caine and other Trump officials privately think it's unlikely that an air campaign alone can put enough pressure on the Iranians to agree to a deal that’s favorable to the president, according to multiple people familiar with recent discussions who spoke with CNN.

![The Iran war, which is both costly and unpopular with Americans, has dragged on for more than five months](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/08/1/50/GettyImages-2288066153.jpg)

Meanwhile, Trump has denied recent reports that the US military’s weapons stockpile is dwindling.

“The U.S. has massive amounts of ‘munitions,’ especially of certain types. Additionally, large amounts are being manufactured and shipped to the U.S. as needed. Defense companies are building the largest number of plants and factories in our country’s history.

“The ‘leakers’ of these treasonous statements are being hunted down. Long term jail sentences will be sought!” Trump wrote on Truth Social Thursday.

Trump’s threats came after Reuters reported Tuesday, citing two unnamed sources, that the Army has used “virtually all” of its long-range Army Tactical Missile Systems and Precision Strike Missiles.

A recent report has also found that the Pentagon is running low on missile interceptors.

The US military had 2,330 Patriot systems and 452 Terminal High Altitude Area Defense systems before the war, according to the report from the Center for Strategic and International Studies. As of July 27, an estimated 759 to 827 Patriot systems and 234 to 278 THAAD systems remain, the report said.

![Despite Trump claiming throughout the war that a peace deal was within reach, fighting has dragged on](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/08/1/18/GettyImages-2289377473.jpg)

During a Senate Appropriations Committee hearing last month, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said US military operations in Iran have cost an estimated $37.5 billion. He also asked Congress for an additional $67 billion to, in part, replenish weapons stockpiles, blaming the Biden administration for not properly funding the Pentagon.

The price of gas has also surged during the war, sitting above $4 a gallon Friday, according to motor club AAA. The price of a gallon of gas was just below $3 at the start of the war, according to the Associated Press.

Just 28 percent of Americans said they approved of Trump’s handling of Iran in a new Reuters/Ipsos poll.
