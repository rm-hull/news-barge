Argentina and South Africa continue a busy period of international action with an intriguing clash in Buenos Aires.

This encounter serves as something of a warm-up for the Springboks with four Tests to come against New Zealand as the Rugby’s Greatest Rivalry series launches, a mouthwatering new venture. Rassie Erasmus and his side dare not overlook a Pumas team with the ability to exploit even the very best – though the hosts are heavily depleted by injury and unavailability.

It makes the task tall for Felipe Contepomi’s side, though they will count on fervent home support as ever. Back in the Springboks ranks are Siya Kolisi, Eben Etzebeth and Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu and Erasmus is again able to examine his depth – which will be required as the world champions look to keep their outstanding recent record intact over the next six weeks.

South Africa welcome back Siya Kolisi, Eben Etzebeth and Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu after injury, a handy trio of returnees to strengthen a fearsome-looking side. Cameron Hanekom and Elrigh Louw join captain Kolisi in the back row, with six more forwards readied on the bench.

## Team news – Argentina

Argentina are without a host of regulars for this clash, including key leaders like Julian Montoya, Marcos Kremer and Santiago Chocobares. Tomas Albornoz is suspended so Santiago Carreras moves forward to fly half, with Geronimo Priscinatelli stationed at full-back.

Tighthead prop Franciso Moreno starts on debut with Leonel Oviedo, Juan Penoucos and Juan Martin Scelzo all awaiting their first caps from the bench.

## Springboks out for revenge

Back rower Elrigh Louw was part of the last South Africa side to visit this particular locale, and still carries the tough memories a one-point defeat that showed how Argentina were flourishing as a Rugby Championship force.

“Argentina is a real quality side, and we learned our lessons from the last time we played here,” said Louw.

“We lost that game in 2024, so the focus for me is on doing my job, and to ensure that we match their physicality, so we certainly took our lessons out of that game and we have been preparing well this week.”

## Argentina's odd year

The pausing of the Rugby Championship to allow South Africa and New Zealand’s series to go ahead has left Argentina with a slightly strange year. The Pumas have already played three times at home in the Nations Championship and are still to welcome Australia to South America for a two-Test tour straddling the end of August. To be fair, they are spreading the love, too, with six different venues for their six home matches – today, we’re in the Estadio Jose Amalfitani, the usual home of Velez Sarsfield in Buenos Aires.
