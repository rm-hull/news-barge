# Pinewoods Revealed sets out 'ambitious future' for cherished part of Harrogate

## One of the potentially most transformative projects for how Harrogate views and utilises its spa heritage has been unveiled.

Pinewoods Revealed, a project led by Fontis for Pinewoods Conservation Group, uncovers the forgotten role of Harrogate’s Pinewoods as Britain’s Kurwald, or cure forest, reconnecting one of the town’s most familiar landscapes to its wider spa history.

This year marks 230 years since Scots pines were first planted on the open moorland above Harrogate.

Today, the 96-acre historic woodland stretches between Valley Gardens and RHS Garden Harlow Carr, forming one of Harrogate’s most important natural connections.

Pinewoods Revealed sets out an ambitious future for this cherished natural landscape.

Cultural strategist George Eglese, Founder and Practice Director of Fontis, said: “What is exciting is the story Pinewoods allows us to tell.

"We began with a woodland that most people in Harrogate know and love, but whose deeper origins were barely visible.

"The more we researched it, the clearer it became that the pines were not an isolated planting but closely tied to Harrogate’s spa anatomy, alongside the mineral springs, gardens, promenades and the practice of taking the air."

Combining historical research with a new identity and website, the project brings heritage, conservation, nature and wellbeing into one defining idea: Pinewoods is Britain’s Kurwald, Harrogate’s living cure forest.

At the centre of Pinewoods Revealed is a new website, creating a ‘digital home’ for the woodland.

Visitors can explore its history, understand the idea of the forest cure, discover its habitats and seasonal character, plan a visit, find upcoming events and follow the continuing conservation work of Pinewoods Conservation Group.

Neil Hind, Chair of Pinewoods Conservation Group, said: “Pinewoods means something different to everyone who uses it.

"For some it is part of a daily walk. For others it is a place of childhood memories, wildlife, quiet or connection.

"Beneath all of that is a remarkable history that deserves to be much better known.

“The Pinewoods Conservation Group has cared for and championed Pinewoods for more than 20 years.

"This renewed image and website allow us to tell its full story, explain the work involved in looking after it and invite more people to become part of its future.”

The history of Harrogate's Pinewoods began in 1796 with the six-acre King’s Plantation.

By the 1890s, the Corporation had acquired the plantation and surrounding moorland, with ambitions to create a pinewood inspired by the great Kurwälder of continental spa towns.

A further 15,000 pines were planted in 1907.

In 1934, the Corporation acquired the remaining adjacent land to complete the core footprint connecting Low Harrogate to Harlow Carr.

This new chapter of Harrogate’s Pinewoods is supported by two leading scholars of European spa culture.

Professor Astrid Köhler, formerly Professor of German Literature and Comparative Cultural Studies at Queen Mary University of London, has researched Harrogate within the wider European spa tradition.

Professor Ian Bradley, Emeritus Professor of Cultural and Spiritual History at the University of St Andrews and author of The Last Enchanted Places: Spa Towns of Europe, said Harrogate was closely modelled on German spas, something still visible in the word Kursaal on the façade of the Royal Hall.

“Pine trees, with their distinctive scent and perceived healing properties, are particularly associated with Kurparks,” he said.
