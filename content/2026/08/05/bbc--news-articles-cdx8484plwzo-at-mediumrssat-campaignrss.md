!["Oil giants make $90b from price surge as climate crisis engulfs Europe" reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/780/cpsprodpb/8495/live/936f3560-9048-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

"Profits of war" leads the Guardian as it says oil companies have made $90bn from the price surge over the Iran war. An environmental NGO quoted in the paper accuses them of "cashing in on human misery" as a "climate crisis engulfs Europe." Elsewhere, the paper's home affairs editor points out that the electronic tags that will be used to monitor offenders' early release from prison are "failing to alert the authorities of potential breaches", according to a watchdog.

!["Super-rain" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/b283/live/6515c7c0-9047-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

"Is it a bird? Is it a plane? No it's.. super-rain" heralds the Daily Star after the driest ever July for England and Wales, according to the Met Office. The downpour is marked with a picture of Brits under umbrellas.

!["Private firms may face ban on profiteering to make care homes cheaper" reads the headline on the front page of The i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/830/cpsprodpb/a4a3/live/a4b48340-9046-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

The i Paper reports that private companies could face a ban on "profiteering to make care homes cheaper". Also on the front page, "migrants face new Channel peril" after 173 people were rescued from a fire aboard a "mega dinghy".

!["How many more lives will you risk by doing nothing?" reads the headline](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/8c03/live/9f809d40-9047-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

Following the "mega-dinghy" rescue in the channel, the Daily Express asks "how many more lives will you risk by doing nothing?"

!["Jailed just last year... but my son's killer already set for freedom" reads the headline on the front page of Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/827/cpsprodpb/2fc0/live/d86d53b0-9046-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

Several of Wednesday's papers continue their coverage of prisoners being released early. The Metro highlights one mother's story: that of Jodian Taylor, who's "son's killer" is "already set for freedom" after he was "jailed just last year".

!["See you in court, PM" reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/6d75/live/2467bf20-9048-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

Meanwhile, the Sun leads with "see you in court, PM". The killers of "hero" PC Andrew Harper are due to be released and the red-top reports that Prime Minister Andy Burnham could be taken to court by "furious cops". It says the family of PC Harper are understood to be backing the legal action. It also quotes Justice Secretary Alex Norris saying: "Were we not to make these changes, then we would run out of prison places in October or November."

!["Setting free PC Harper's killers is 'not justice'," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1040/cpsprodpb/7c5e/live/2a6cc4e0-9054-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

The Telegraph also leads with the story of PC Harper's killers being set free, quoting the chair of Thames Valley Police Federation, who says this is "not justice". It also highlights an accusation by Reform UK's Robert Jenrick that Bank of England officials "work from beach", as it is revealed more than 1,000 of its staff worked abroad last year.

!["9,300 domestic abusers set to be released" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/810/cpsprodpb/ed67/live/56a2acb0-9049-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

The Daily Mail reports that "9,300 domestic abusers are set to be released" under the scheme to tackle prison overcrowding, citing Ministry of Justice (MoJ) data collated by the Conservatives. The paper says charity bosses and the Conservatives have accused Burnham of "putting vulnerable women at new risk". An MoJ spokesman told the paper the figure was "speculative".

!["Google assembles $200bn Wall Street machine to supply chips for Anthropic" reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1056/cpsprodpb/8943/live/5b00fa90-9045-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

In the top slot for the Financial Times, Google has assembled a $200bn infrastructure financing programme to supply microchips for Anthropic. Also on its front page, the founder of Revolut is being sued €17.5m by a superyacht broker "going behind its back" and allegedly failing to pay commission for a €350m boat. A spokesperson for Storonsky's family office told the paper the claim was "without merit and will be defended".

!["40 knives a day off our streets" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/e7e0/live/4d943680-9048-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

The Daily Mirror meanwhile celebrates that knife amnesties mean there are "40 knives a day off our streets". Parents have told the paper each one "is a life saved".

!["Borrowing splurge in bid to boost economy" reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/809/cpsprodpb/aca6/live/1c636920-904b-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

The Times reports that the government will conduct a "borrowing splurge in bid to boost economy". It also writes that Burnham's wife, Marie-France van Heel, told him "not again", when he said he would attempt to become prime minister. The PM shared the story in an appearance on the How to Fail podcast.
