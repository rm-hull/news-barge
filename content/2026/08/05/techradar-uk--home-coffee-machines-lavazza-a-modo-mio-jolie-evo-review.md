### TechRadar Verdict

The Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo is a tiny capsule coffee machine that brews delicious espresso using Lavazza's A Modo Mio pods. It's very affordable, as are the pods, though it's worth being aware that their plastic shells are harder to recycle than aluminum, and can only be dropped off at designated collection points. If you happen to live near to one though, the Jolie Evo is a top-notch, budget-friendly espresso machine that's ideal for compact spaces.

## Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo: one-minute review

The Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo is an exceptionally small capsule coffee maker, which brews espresso using the company's own A Modo Mio pods. These vacuum-packed capsules are available in a wide variety of coffee types, roasts, and blends, but unlike Nespresso capsules, there are no elaborate brews with added flavors to choose from.

That's no bad thing in my opinion, because the Jolie Evo produces some of the best espresso I've had from a pod machine, and flavored coffees often tend to taste artificial.

The Jolie Evo is very reasonably priced too (considerably less than the A Modo Mio Smeg and A Modo Mio Deséa), though you'll need to pick up a standalone milk frother too (I recommend either the Smeg Mini Milk Frother or Philips Baristina Milk Frother) if you want to make lattes and cappuccinos.

The controls are extremely simple, with just a single button and a large lever. However, bear in mind that there's no timer, so you'll need to manually tap the button to stop the flow once the machine has dispensed the right amount of coffee.

Overall, it's an excellent compact espresso machine. For me, the only real drawback is that the plastic pods aren't particularly easy to recycle. They can only be dropped off at designated collection points, which can be scarce outside big cities.

## Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo: design

- **Ideal for small kitchens**
- **Only two controls (lever and button)**
- **Lever is ergonomic and easy to use**

The Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo is easily one of the smallest coffee makers I’ve tested, measuring a mere 4.9 x 8.3 x 13 inches / 124 x 210 x 330mm (W x H x D). That’s even tinier than the diminutive Nespresso Vertuo Pop, which is 5.3 x 9.8 x 16.7 inches / 136 x 250 x 426mm. It’s ideal for slotting into small spaces, and you can even route the power cable right or left underneath the machine if you need it to sit against a wall.

The Lavazza machine looks classier than its Nespresso counterpart as well, with a smart matte finish and a large chrome handle on top. Pulling this handle up opens a slot on top of the machine. so you can slide a coffee pod inside.

 ![Person inserting coffee pod into Lavazza Jolie Evo coffee maker](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/xApffFE58t3e9HSwsUzYPf.jpg) 


Pushing it back down closes the drawer and pierces the pod, and pulling it up a second time will drop the used capsule into a small bin behind the Jolie Evo’s drip tray. The bin has a grille at the bottom that allows the pods to drain so they’re dry when you remove them for recycling, which is a thoughtful touch.

The large lever is easy to grip and requires barely any force to move, which makes this a particularly good option for people with limited strength or mobility in their hands.

The drip tray has a plastic grate that can be removed for easy cleaning, and is just the right height for an espresso cup. A standard size cup or mug will fit neatly under the dispenser with the drip tray detached.

 ![Person raising handle of Lavazza Jolie Evo coffee maker](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/uFHK5jztwjgRvoVWPqYp2g.jpg) 


Other than the handle, there is just one control on the Jolie Evi: a button on top of the machine, with a white light to indicate the machine’s current status: blinking means heating, and white means ready to brew.

It’s available in black and white in Australia, with a gray option also offered in the UK. It was sold in green and red too when it launched in the US.

- **Design score: 5/5**

### Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo: performance

- **Extremely simple to use, but there's no timer for the coffee dispenser**
- **It makes delicious espresso in moments**
- **Its pods can be hard to recycle compared to Nespresso aluminum ones**

What makes the Jolie Evo different from all other capsule coffee makers I’ve tested to date is its lack of a timer for brewing different sized drinks. Once you’ve pressed its single button, the little machine will continue dispensing coffee (or hot water, if there’s no pod inside) until you either tap the button again or the tank is empty.

This isn’t ideal if you want to leave your coffee maker to handle itself while you’re preparing the rest of your breakfast, but making a double espresso takes less than a minute so it’s no real hardship.

I would recommend using a measuring cup as your recepticle, though, at least until you’ve got your eye in, because it’s easy to under or over-estimate the output. You could use a coffee scale to a perfect measure, but it would be overkill; this entry-level machine makes a very good espresso, but it’s not intended for real aficionados.

 ![Lavazza Jolie Evo coffee maker dispensing espresso](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/w5x2JMQZ8eYBMY4K7YMB5g.jpg) 


It uses Lavazza’s own A Modo Mio capsules, which are excellent value compared to most coffee pods. Different types command different prices, but you can pick up 80 Qualità Rossa pods direct from Lavazza for £24.50 (about $30 / AU$50). That’s just over 30p per drink, compared to 49p for the cheapest Nespresso Vertuo pods.

Unlike Nespresso, the Modo Mio selection doesn’t include elaborate flavored drinks; there are various beans, blends and roasts to choose from in Lavazza’s store, but each pod contains only ground coffee with no extras. That's no bad thing, in my opinion — coffee capsules with added flavors can taste unpleasantly artificial.

 ![Lavazza Jolie Evo coffee maker dispensing water to make americano](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/AKr84iYqPqoNuJDcKE2yjf.jpg) 


I tested the Jolie Evo with three of Lavazza’s coffees: Qualità Rossa, Passionale, and Qualità Oro. Each comes in a box of 16, and is marked with an intensity score out of 12 to give you an idea of what to expect. The first is a blend of robusta and arabica beans, while the other two are 100% arabica.

My personal favorite was the Qualità Oro, which produced one of the best-tasting espressos I’ve had from a capsule machine and is described as “Sweet and delicate taste, with an intense and fragrant aroma”.

It goes particularly well with milk in a white americano or (if you also pick up a milk frother) a creamy latte.

The problem with these pods, delicious though they are, is that they can be difficult to recycle. Nespresso pods are made entirely from aluminum, which is relatively easy to process once the coffee grounds are removed, and there’s almost certain to be a location nearby where you can drop off the used capsules. If not, you can mail them off for recycling with a pre-paid bag.

 ![Used coffee pods in Lavazza Jolie Evo coffee maker](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ByynCvLFmK4T5yRhzFRz2f.jpg) 


By contrast, Lavazza A Modo Mio pods are mostly plastic and can be recycled in the UK through the company’s partnership with Terracycle, but collection points are few and far between.

If there’s nowhere to drop off your pods, your only option is to start your own collection point, accepting pods from other people and sending them for processing yourself. Individuals can’t send their pods in the mail.

South England is relatively well-served, but at the time of writing there are only four drop-off points in Scotland, and none at all in Wales or Northern Ireland.

- **Performance score: 4/5**

## Buy it if

**You have a small kitchen**

This is the most compact coffee maker I've tested to date, and the fact its cable can be routed to the right or left means you can push it right up against a wall if you need to.

**You have limited dexterity in your hands**

The large lever handle requires virtually no effort to push and pull, and there's just one button to worry about. All removable pieces are easy to detach for cleaning as well.

## Don't buy it if

**You want elaborate flavored coffees**

A Modo Mio capsules are full of nothing but unadulterated ground coffee, so if you want something fancier you'll need to add your own syrup.

**You want easily recyclable coffee pods**

A Modo Mio capsules are plastic and can only be recycled if you drop them at a designated collection point. You can't send them in the mail for processing.

### How I tested the Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo

I used the Lavazza A Modo Mio Jolie Evo in place of my usual espresso machine for two weeks. I used three different types of A Modo Mio capsules (Qualità Rossa, Passionale, and Qualità Oro), drinking it neat as an espresso, and using the Jolie Evo to add extra hot water to make an americano.

I set up and cleaned the machine following the directions in the instruction manual, and allowed the components to air-dry before reassembly.
