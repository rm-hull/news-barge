# What we know about the deadly protests in Pakistan-administered Kashmir

- An election in Pakistan-administered Kashmir has led to deadly violence, an internet blackout, and allegations of a severe state crackdown, plunging the disputed Himalayan territory into its most serious internal political crisis in years.
- The unrest is driven by the Jammu Kashmir Joint Awami Action Committee (JAAC), a grassroots movement that began protesting rising electricity prices and the cost of living, evolving into a broader challenge to the region's constitutional structure, including a boycott of the elections and demands to abolish 12 reserved legislative seats.
- Authorities banned the JAAC under anti-terrorism laws, arrested leaders, and launched security operations, while protesters accuse the state of using excessive force, with Amnesty International reporting at least 40 deaths, including 34 civilians, and calling for an independent investigation.
- The Pakistani government rejects the JAAC's demands, asserting the constitutional protection of the reserved seats, which it deems essential for maintaining its stance on the disputed status of the former princely state of Jammu and Kashmir.
- Extended internet shutdowns and restrictions on movement have severely hampered independent verification of events, disrupted daily life for residents and businesses, and complicated election campaigning, drawing criticism from rights groups.
