South Korea to restore Gyeongwon Line railway connecting with North Korea

A photo taken in May 2018 shows a general view of the northernmost limit of the Gyeongwon railway line at Baengmagoji station in Cheorwon near the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) separating North and South Korea (AFP/Getty)

South Korea is set to resume work on restoring a section of the Gyeongwon Line railway near the Demilitarised Zone, a project initially launched in 2015 but suspended in 2016.

The railway aims to eventually connect to North Korea's eastern city of Wonsan and improve access to peace and ecological tourism sites in the DMZ.

This decision follows North Korea's 2024 demolition of cross-border road and rail links, as Pyongyang redefines South Korea as a hostile state.

South Korean President Lee Jae Myung acknowledged the challenges of engaging with an unresponsive North Korea but emphasised the continued importance of pursuing peace and coexistence.

The Unification Ministry also plans to broaden the use of the Inter-Korean Cooperation Fund to support domestic border-area projects, peace economy initiatives, and reunification activities, given the low annual expenditure since 2016.
