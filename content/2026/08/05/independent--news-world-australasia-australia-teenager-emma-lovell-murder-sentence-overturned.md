An Australian teenager who fatally stabbed a British woman during a home invasion has had his reduced prison sentence overturned by the country’s highest court, restoring the original term imposed over the murder that shocked Queensland.

The High Court of Australia unanimously ruled on Wednesday that the Queensland court of appeal should not have reduced the amount of time the offender was required to spend in detention before release, finding it was “not open” for the lower court to interfere with the original sentencing judge’s decision.

The teenager, who cannot be named because he was 17 at the time of the offence, will now serve the original sentence handed down in 2024, according to the ABC. He is now 21.

Emma Lovell, 41, who had moved from Suffolk to Australia with her family in 2011, was killed after confronting two intruders who broke into her home in North Lakes, north of Brisbane, on Boxing Day in 2022.

She and her husband, Lee Lovell, forced the pair out of the house before a struggle continued on the front lawn, where she was stabbed in the heart. Mr Lovell was also stabbed, while the couple’s two teenage daughters watched as paramedics carried out emergency open-heart surgery before their mother was taken to hospital, where she died.

The teenager pleaded guilty to murder, armed burglary, and other offences and was sentenced to 14 years in detention in 2024.

Justice Tom Sullivan described the killing as “particularly heinous” and ordered that the offender serve 70 per cent of his sentence before he was considered for supervised release, rejecting arguments that his upbringing, guilty plea, and remorse amounted to “special circumstances” under Queensland’s Youth Justice Act.

According to Queensland legislation, juveniles can typically be ordered to serve a maximum of 70 per cent of their custodial sentence, unless special circumstances warrant otherwise.

![Emma Lovell, 41, lived in Brisbane with her husband and two children](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/03/11/07/newFile-2.jpg)

Last year, however, the Queensland court of appeal ruled that those factors did justify a reduction, cutting the minimum period the offender would spend in detention from 70 per cent to 60 per cent of his sentence, a reduction of about 17 months.

The court found his early guilty plea had spared the Lovell family the ordeal of a trial and should have been given greater weight alongside his prospects of rehabilitation.

Queensland attorney general Deb Frecklington appealed the decision in the High Court, arguing that the appeal court had wrongly substituted its own assessment for that of the sentencing judge’s.

In a unanimous judgment, the High Court agreed, saying there was “nothing” about the offender’s guilty pleas, deprived upbringing, or prospects of rehabilitation that made it “unreasonable or plainly unjust” for Justice Sullivan to conclude there were no special circumstances, according to *7News*. 

The judges also noted that the guilty pleas had been entered against an “overwhelmingly strong Crown case” and that the sentencing judge had found the teenager had shown only prospects of rehabilitation “over time” while displaying, “at least to a certain extent”, an unwillingness “to confront the consequences of own actions”.

Outside court, Mr Lovell said the decision brought “some validation that Emma’s life did matter”. After nearly four years of legal proceedings, he said he hoped the family could begin returning to “some sort of normality”.

“Fourteen years just didn’t seem like enough for me. And you know to then have that 70 per cent reduced was just heartbreaking, I suppose,” he said.

Ms Frecklington said she had pursued “all available legal avenues” to preserve “the small amount of justice afforded to the Lovell family”.

The decision, she said, “will never bring Emma back. It will never give those two girls their mum back. It will never give Lee his wife back”.

The other offender in the case, also a juvenile, was found not guilty of murder in October 2024. He was found guilty of burglary and assault and sentenced to 18 months in jail.
