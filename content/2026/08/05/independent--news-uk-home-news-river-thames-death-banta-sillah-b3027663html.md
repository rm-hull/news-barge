A 14-year-old boy who died in the River Thames on Monday has been named as a “talented young footballer”.

The boy died after going into the water near Millwall Outer Dock, on London’s Isle of Dogs.

Emergency services were alerted about 6.50pm on Monday, and his body was recovered around 10.30pm.

He was pronounced dead at the scene.

Tower Hamlets Mayor Lutfur Rahman has named the teenager as local boy Banta Sillah and said he was “heartbroken” over the “tragic” death.

“Banta was a talented young footballer who played for Frontline Community FC and was instrumental in helping the team win the Tower Hamlets Cup last week,” Mr Rahman said.

![A bunch of flowers left by River Thames at Millwall Outer Dock](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/04/15/4e9836dae7f54d96c925c07d94e92a02Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1OTQwNDM2-2.85486454.jpg)

“He had immense potential and was dearly loved by his peers.

“My deepest condolences, thoughts and prayers are with his family, friends, teammates and all those affected by this devastating loss.”

Mr Rahman said that the council is supporting Banta’s family.

“As we mourn Banta, I urge families to speak with children and young people, we should all be aware of the dangers swimming in open water.

“Swimming should only take place in officially designated areas with lifeguards present.”

The Met Police have not officially named the boy.

His death is being treated as unexpected, but not suspicious. Police are continuing to make inquiries to prepare a report for the coroner.

![A sign that warns against unauthorised swimming at Millwall Outer Dock](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/04/15/34b32dc5ac8307087fd4658108a645cdY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1OTQwNDA3-2.85486480.jpg)

There have been more than 100 accidental drownings in London in the past six years.

Dozens of people have died in open water this summer during prolonged hot weather across the UK.

The Royal Life Saving Society earlier urged people to “stop and think” before getting into the water in response to the deaths, most of whom were children.

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) has warned of the increased risk of cold water shock, which is the body’s natural physical reaction to sudden immersion in cold water.

The response is immediate and can lead to drowning, the National Fire Chiefs Council (NFCC) said.

Anyone who suddenly enters cold water is at risk, especially if they accidentally fall in or jump in. Experts warn that cold water shock can affect anyone, regardless of swimming ability.
