Nato confirms drone sighting near Ukrainian plane at Leipzig airport after 'security incident'

Nato has confirmed to the German press agency DPA that a drone was seen near a Ukrainian aircraft at Germany’s Leipzig/Halle airport, the German press agency DPA reported.

The confirmation comes after flight operations were briefly suspended overnight following a security incident (11:02).

Separately, Bild reported that early tests appeared to confirm the drone was carrying explosive materials that failed to ignite due to a defective detonator.

For what it’s worth, DHL Group CEO Tobias Meyer said the German logistics firm was studying the overnight incident which also involved an object clashing with one of its planes near Leipzig.

“We’ve got plans for cases like this, and we’ll try to make sure that the influence on our customers will be as low as possible,” Meyer said during a quarterly results call earlier this morning, Reuters reported.

While the Leipzig incident primarily affected various cargo planes coming into the airport, it also caused some disruptions for passengers as a Marabu Airlines plane coming from Mallorca had to be diverted to Nuremberg.

As we are waiting for more clarity on the incident, the German media are stressing the point (11:02) that Leipzig is a major hub for deliveries for Ukraine, and the drone was reported near a Ukrainian transport plane.

Russia is 'a terrorist state,' Lithuanian minister says after overnight attacks on Kyiv

Lithuanian foreign minister Kęstutis Budrys called Russia “a terrorist state” when responding to the latest round of attacks on Kyiv.

“At least 17 people were killed and 44 injured. Pure terror. Ukraine needs more air defence – now. Every intercepted missile means lives saved. The only language the Kremlin understands is strength.

The world must increase pressure on Russia and force it to the negotiating table.”

Russian strikes hit warehouses, logistics for large Ukrainian home improvement retail chain

Overnight Russian attacks on Kyiv also struck Epicentr, Ukraine’s largest home improvement and DIY retail chain.

Rescuers work at the site of a Russian missile strike on a goods storage in Kyiv, Ukraine. Photograph: Sergey Dolzhenko/EPA

Two key logistics facilities and a factory were destroyed within minutes during the attacks, Reuters reported.

It was too early to assess the full scale of the damage, it said.

“This is a deliberate attack on Ukrainian manufacturing, logistics, jobs and the country’s ability to recover,” Epicentr said in a statement.

Iryna Chechotkina, co-founder of leading online retailer Rozetka, said she watched overnight as the work of her entire life went up in flames after strikes by three ballistic missiles.

German police investigating 'unidentified flying object' disrupting flights in Leipzig

Overnight, “an unidentified flying object” disrupted operations at Leipzig/Halle airport in Germany, with local media speculating over the severity of the incident.

A brief police statement offered no details on the “object,” but stated that “a bomb disposal robot was deployed as part of the investigation.” The south runway remained closed this morning.

But Süddeutsche Zeitung reportedthe object may have been“a drone with a detonator,” targeting “a Ukrainian transport plane” at the airport. “Police are not ruling out a deliberate act of sabotage,” it said.

Meanwhile, German tabloid Bild reported that the device containing “an unknown substance” was found “in close proximity to a Ukrainian Antonov cargo plane.”

There is no reported danger to the public.

Separately, a DHL transport plane reportedly collided with another unidentified object near the airport, according to Reuters. It later safely landed in Hanover with “minor damage,” SZ said. There are conflicting reports on the timing of this incident.

SZ noted that Leipzig is considered a key hub for arm deliveries to Ukraine. In 2024, an incendiary device plot due to be sent by air to the UK was discovered at the airport as part of a suspected Russian sabotage plan. Russia has denied any link.

EU's von der Leyen condemns 'horrible atrocities' after deadly strikes on Kyiv

European Commission chief Ursula von der Leyen condemned “horrible atrocities” by Russia after another night of attacks on Kyiv, which killed at least 17 people.

“Once again we wake up to the news of horrible atrocities by Russia through its aerial attacks on Ukraine. Russia must pay for the destruction it has caused.”

She said the commission was planning to “use the proceeds from the immobilised Russian assets to make sure it does.”

“We are making a further €1.4bn of them available to Ukraine. This will support Ukraine’s continued resistance against Russia’s illegal war.”

'Russian strikes take no summer vacation,' Ukraine's foreign minister says

Ukraine’s foreign minister Andrii Sybiha responded to the latest overnight attacks on Kyiv, saying that “Russian strikes take no summer vacation.”

Rescuers working outside a warehouse premises damaged following massive Russian ballistic missile and drones strikes in the Ukrainian capital of Kyiv. Photograph: Ukrainian State Emergency Service/AFP/Getty Images

“Support for Ukraine and pressure on the aggressor must not pause either. Every interceptor counts,” he said.

Sybiha continued:

“They are life-saving tools, and I urge all partners to expedite all relevant decisions. Russia’s ability to produce these tools of ballistic terror must be disrupted. Additional, tough sanctions are critical and urgent.”

Ukraine shot down 98 of the 115 drones fired by Russia overnight, the country’s air force said. But it was unable to shoot down any of the missiles, preliminary statistics from the air force showed.

A firefighter at work near a fragment of a Russian missile after airstrikes at a food warehouse near Kyiv, Ukraine. Photograph: Efrem Lukatsky/AP

Kyiv can intercept the vast majority of drones, but still relies on supplies from allies to counter missiles, AFP noted.

At least 17 people were killed and more than 40 injured after another horrific night of Russian attacks on Kyiv, with Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy saying that “ballistic missile interceptors could have saved the lives of those killed today”.

A fire breaks out following massive Russian ballistic missile strikes in the Ukrainian capital of Kyiv. Photograph: Evgen Kotenko/AFP/Getty Images

“It is very important for partners to realise that delays in their deliveries or failure to provide anti-ballistic measures lead to such terrible casualties and destruction,” he said on social media.

Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko earlier warned that “there may still be people under the rubble”.

Ukraine has for months been warning about its shortages of anti-ballistic measures, urging allies to increase their deliveries after the US president, Donald Trump, backtracked on his promise to let Ukraine manufacture Patriot missiles locally.

“Partners who are not yet ready to play a more active role in supplying interceptors can help by implementing new sanctions. A significant share of Russia’s ballistic missile production is still not under sanctions. Even now. New steps are needed – from the G7, the European Union, and everyone who supports the protection of life,” Zelenskyy said.

Elsewhere, large parts of Europe will face another day of extreme temperatures after multiple records were broken in central and eastern part of the continent yesterday.

The Czech city of Plzeň reported the all-time high of 41.5C yesterday, with the Slovak capital of Bratislava recording an August high of 40.8C.

Austria, Croatia, Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland, Serbia, Slovakia and Slovenia will all see temperatures in high 30s and possibly into low 40s today, with the extreme temperatures worsening the already tricky drought and energy crunch situation.
