Russian strikes have killed at least 37 people in Kyiv, most of them from a care home for the elderly, according to local Ukrainian officials.

It marks the deadliest attack on Ukraine’s capital this year, the Associated Press reports.

The strikes hit an ammunition depot, sparking a fire in the Bucha district overnight that spread to surrounding areas.

Dozens were injured with nearby infrastructure including residential buildings and the care facility for the elderly and people with disabilities suffering “significant damage”.

Volodymyr Zelensky has vowed to punish those responsible and said criminal cases had already been opened on charges of official negligence.

He described it as a “terrible situation and terrible negligence on the part of those who stored explosives right next to people”.

Meanwhile, Russia’s defence ministry said it had successfully tested a nuclear-capable intercontinental ballistic missile that could reach the US.

The missile, which has a range of more than 6,700km, was fired from the Arkhangelsk region and reached its designated target at the Kura test site in Kamchatka.

It comes just days after CIA Director John Ratcliffe travelled to Moscow to warn Russia off any attack on a Nato attack according to reports. The Kremlin denied the claims and accused western media of “scare-stories”.

## Two killed, 27 wounded in Russian strikes on Ukraine's Kherson region

Russian strikes hit homes and apartment blocks in the southern Ukrainian region of Kherson on Saturday, killing two people and wounding 27 others, including a child, regional head Oleksandr Prokudin said in a statement.

Mr Prokudin had said earlier in the week that Russia had intensified its attacks on critical infrastructure in the region, about 70 per cent of which is under Russian control.

He urged tens of thousands of residents to move to safer regions, citing the possibility of prolonged power and heating outages this winter.

## Russia claims Myla warehouse held drone components

Russia's defence ministry said it struck a warehouse in Myla, a village in Ukraine's Bucha district near Kyiv, that was storing components and launch boosters for long-range drones, conflicting with Ukraine's description of the site as holding shells, mines and other ammunition.

The warehouse was hit in a Russian drone strike on Friday night that sparked explosions and fires, killing 37 people, most of them residents of a nearby care home, in the deadliest attack of the war this year.

Ukrainian officials have said the site was a Defence Forces storage facility and have opened criminal proceedings on charges of official negligence over the storage of ammunition near homes.

The ministry also said it had attacked the ports of Mykolaiv and Izmail, along with a logistics centre and warehouse facilities in the Kyiv region that it said stored parts for Ukraine's Flamingo cruise missiles

## Myla residents recall panic as drone strike sparked fires that raced through homes

Residents of Myla described scenes of panic as a Russian drone strike hit a warehouse in the village on Friday night, sparking massive explosions and fires that swept through dozens of nearby homes and buildings and killed 37 people.

The Friday night attack on the warehouse in Myla, a village in the Bucha district, was the war's deadliest attack this year and came on the second of three days of nearly round-the-clock Russian strikes that have severely disrupted life in and around the Ukrainian capital.

The warehouse strike sparked fires that raced through homes and damaged at least 50 residential buildings, said Tymur Tkachenko, who heads the local regional military administration. He was later cited by local media as saying 34 of the people killed were residents of a care home for elderly and disabled people. More than 370 people were evacuated.

The blasts rocked Myla, causing people to frantically seek cover.

"At about 8pm, the drone hit a warehouse somewhere and then the detonation began," Oleksandr Vodolaz, a village resident, told the Associated Press. "No one knew that weapons were stored there."

Another local, Zhanna Kravchuk, said she had so many questions about what happened.

"Why are weapons like this ... in warehouses and not on the front lines where they would help?" she said.

## Ukraine PM says Myla warehouse tragedy repeats 'criminal negligence' seen in July's Vyshneve strike

Ukrainian prime minister Serhii Koretskyi said the Myla warehouse strike echoed a July attack on an ammunition depot in the Kyiv suburb of Vyshneve, which killed 22 people and set off similar detonations and fires in nearby homes.

"Unfortunately, we can already see that the terrible tragedy in Vyshneve did not become a lesson," Mr Koretskyi said. "In the fifth year of a full-scale invasion, making the same mistakes is criminal negligence."

"And all those guilty, without exception, must be severely punished so that such situations do not happen again," he said.

The 37 killed in Friday's strike surpassed the 31 who died in a 2 July attack on Kyiv, which had been the deadliest single attack of the war so far this year.

## Zelensky opens probe over stored explosives

President Volodymyr Zelensky said criminal proceedings had been opened on charges of official negligence, suggesting local officials may have been responsible for storing explosives near homes in the Kyiv region village of Myla.

"There are regulations in place - ammunition cannot be stored near homes or other residential areas," Mr Zelensky said in an online post.

"Those who allowed this to happen must be held accountable for their decisions," he said, adding that "all those guilty, without exception, must be severely punished so that such situations do not happen again."

A drone strike on the warehouse, which officials say may have illegally stored munitions, sparked explosions and fires that damaged dozens of nearby homes and buildings in Myla, a village in the Bucha district.

## Navy tracks Russian warships in English Channel during ‘intense’ operation

A task group was shadowed by Type 45 destroyer HMS Duncan, Type 23 frigate HMS St Albans and patrol vessel HMS Severn as it made its way from the North Sea through the English Channel.

Russian destroyer Admiral Levchenko was watched escorting sanctioned cargo vessels General Skobelev and Sparta, with a Merlin helicopter extending the UK’s surveillance as the ships approached the Strait of Dover.

In response to the tracking, Russia deployed other military resources including the Neustrashimy frigate to guard the illegal shipment, UK officials said.

First Sea Lord General Sir Gwyn Jenkins said there was “no room for complacency” as shadow fleet vessels continue to pose “an unacceptable risk” to maritime security and the environment.

“Alongside our allies, this approach is forcing Moscow to divert Russian warships away from their core purpose. That is because Russia knows we stand ready to act again, should it be required,” he said.
