THE streets of West Yorkshire shook as hundreds of Harley-Davidson motorcycles rumbled into Shipley over the weekend.

The annual Shipley Harley Rally, believed to be the UK’s largest Harley-Davidson rally, takes place this weekend, at Baildon Rugby Club, with the main ride-out taking place yesterday.

The rally had a distinctly family-friendly atmosphere, attracting young families, grandparents, dog-walkers and biking enthusiasts of all ages.

At 1pm sharp, the bikes, in their droves, set off, circling around the roundabout and then down the hill.

A spokesperson for Shipley Neighbourhood Policing Team had previously said: “This event is the highlight of the Shipley Harley Rally."

  Crowds gather in anticipation at the annual Harley Davidson motorbike rally in Baildon. Image: T&A


Organisers estimated that as many as 400 Harleys, famous for their made-in-America V-twin engines which give them a distinctive rumbled, took part.

The rally attracts Harley enthusiasts from across the country, with riders and spectators gathering to admire models ranging from Road Kings to Sportsters.

  Riders in the Shipley Harley Rally ride-out in 2024 Image: Newsquest


Harley-Davidson began in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, in 1903, when William S. Harley and Arthur Davidson built and sold a motorised bicycle from the Davidson family’s backyard.

Arthur’s brothers Walter and William soon joined them, and the company was incorporated in 1907.

  Crowds gather in anticipation at the annual Harley Davidson motorbike rally in Baildon. Image: T&A


Its rugged V-twin motorcycles helped establish the firm, while military contracts during both world wars expanded its reach.

By 1920 it was the world’s largest motorcycle manufacturer. Despite periods of financial difficulty and overseas competition, it remains one of motorcycling’s most recognisable brands.

  Shipley Harley Davidson 40th rally at Baildon.


Last year, the 46th edition of the rally saw hundreds of bikes depart from the Northgate roundabout, weaving through Baildon, Guiseley, Otley and Ilkley in a procession that drew large crowds.

A keen motorcycle enthusiast who had travelled to the rally with his dog from Wakefield told the T&A: “I love to see all the bikes, the engineering, the beauty.

“I’m not from Baildon but I think things like this are great for the town, not least for the charities who directly benefit from the kindness of these riders."

The event is organised by The Wrecking Crew, a UK-based Harley-Davidson club formed in 1969.

Lee Boyes, of The Wrecking Crew, said: “I have been here every year since it started.

“It has definitely grown over the years.

“You always see some new faces.”
