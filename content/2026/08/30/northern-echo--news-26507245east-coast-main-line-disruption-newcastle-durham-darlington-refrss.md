Rail passengers from Newcastle, Durham and Darlington face severe disruption getting to London today as major engineering work shuts a key section of the East Coast Main Line.

There are no direct LNER trains between the North East and London King’s Cross throughout Sunday (August 30), with journeys expected to take considerably longer than normal.

Passengers travelling from stations including Newcastle, Durham, Darlington, Northallerton and York are being urged to check their journeys before setting off.

Major work as part of the East Coast Digital Programme means long-distance trains cannot run between Peterborough and London King’s Cross.

The closure began late on Saturday evening and will continue until around 8am on Bank Holiday Monday.

LNER is operating a heavily revised timetable, with most trains from the North terminating at Peterborough rather than continuing into London.

Passengers can then use a replacement coach between Peterborough and Bedford before transferring to Thameslink trains for the remainder of the journey to London St Pancras.

There are also just two special southbound LNER trains running directly from Newcastle to Bedford today.

They are scheduled to leave Newcastle at 8.26am and 12.27pm and call at Durham, Darlington, Northallerton, York and Doncaster before travelling to Bedford.

Passengers can then connect with Thameslink services into London St Pancras.

Two trains will make the reverse journey from Bedford to Newcastle at 12.49pm and 4.57pm.

LNER has warned passengers against attempting to reach London by travelling via Sheffield or Chesterfield instead, with alternative services expected to be extremely busy.

The situation is complicated further by industrial action affecting East Midlands Railway, which is running a reduced Sunday service to and from London St Pancras.

There has also been additional disruption for North East passengers this morning.

The 8.42am LNER service from Newcastle to Edinburgh, due to arrive at 10.25am, has been cancelled because of a shortage of train crew.

LNER said passengers affected by that cancellation can use alternative LNER, Northern and TransPennine Express services for up to two hours afterwards, including the 9.24am TransPennine Express service from Newcastle to Edinburgh.

Meanwhile, the 8.43am Doncaster to Edinburgh service is making additional stops at Morpeth and Berwick-upon-Tweed because of a shortage of train managers.

Other operators using the East Coast Main Line are also badly affected.

Grand Central, which normally provides direct trains between the North East and London from stations including Sunderland, Hartlepool, Eaglescliffe, Northallerton, Thirsk and York, will run no services at all today.

Lumo is operating only a reduced and amended service between Newcastle and Edinburgh or Glasgow Queen Street and is not running through to London.

Hull Trains is operating a reduced service to and from London St Pancras, rather than King’s Cross.

The widespread disruption is being caused by work on the East Coast Digital Programme, which will eventually introduce digital in-cab signalling on the southern section of the East Coast Main Line.

National Rail says the technology will provide drivers with continuous real-time information and is intended to improve reliability and reduce emissions.

Passengers have been warned replacement buses could be particularly busy and are being advised to allow significantly more time for their journeys.

The railway is expected to begin returning towards its normal timetable after the engineering work finishes at around 8am on Monday.
