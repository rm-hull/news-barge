The CIA director floated a summit with Vladimir Putin, Volodymyr Zelensky, and Donald Trump during his mysterious meeting in Moscow, it is understood.

Two sources briefed on the matter told Axios that John Ratcliffe had spoken to Russian officials this week about a trilateral meeting.

Talks between Ukraine and Russia were paused in February when the US invaded Iran, and little progress has been made since.

Meanwhile, Russia has continued to strike Kyiv with drones overnight after at least 38 people were killed in the deadliest attack on Ukraine’s capital this year.

The Ukrainian president vowed to punish those responsible for the deadly attacks, which killed 34 people in a care home for the elderly.

The strikes hit an ammunition depot, sparking a fire in the Bucha district overnight that spread to surrounding areas.

Dozens were injured with nearby infrastructure including residential buildings and the care facility for the elderly and people with disabilities suffering “significant damage”.

President Zelensky said cases had already been opened on charges of official negligence.

## Ukraine defence chief calls for bringing forward part of EU loan to cover deficit

Ukraine wants to bring forward part of a European Union loan earmarked for next year to help cover a $27 billion budget deficit this year, defence minister Yevhenii Khmara said.

Speaking to reporters, he added that Kyiv's war plan must be clear to Ukraine's partners as well as across the military chain of command.

Khmara also said Ukraine has tested four potential interceptors to shoot down the jet-powered drones Russia is increasingly firing at Ukraine.

## How Ukraine is trying to beat Russia at its own game with massive drone strike campaign

*Ukraine is striking deeper into the heart of Russia than ever before with swarms of drones that have led Russians into a panic. Experts say that Kyiv’s resourcefulness is a result of studying Moscow, Maira Butt reports:*

In 2023, a year into Russia’s unprecedented invasion of Ukraine, Kyiv was producing around 800,000 drones a year. By 2024, that number had more than doubled to two million.

Fast forward to last month – when President Volodymyr Zelensky announced that the country was now churning out more than 10 million units annually with plans to double that number to 20 million by this time next year.

The rapid rate and intensity of production is having an impact. Just this week, Russian president Vladimir Putin was forced to acknowledge that attacks on the country’s refineries and energy infrastructure were having an effect.

“There are no critical consequences from such attacks, there have not been and cannot be,” he told a conference of business and economy leaders, trying to downplay their scale.

However, he added: “But they certainly cause us harm, that is obvious, and we understand, see and know this.”

## Comment: What is the real reason the CIA boss John Ratcliffe made a secret trip to Moscow?

*Peter Frankopan writes:* 

Earlier this week, a C17 Globemaster descended into Moscow’s Vnukovo airport. Although it was hopping in from Riga, Latvia, the plane had originated at Andrews Air Force Base, close to Washington DC. On board was John Ratcliffe, the director of the CIA. After being whisked into the Russian capital in a motorcade, a few hours later, Ratcliffe was up in the air again and on his way home.

Who Ratcliffe met was not clear. The visit had not been announced in advance in the US, while Kremlin spokesman Dimitry Peskov claimed to have no knowledge or information about the arrival of a US Air Force plane.

Not surprisingly, there has been much speculation as to why the most senior figure in US intelligence had gone to Moscow in person. After all, if Ratcliffe had an important message to share or communicate, there were other, easier and safer ways to do so than spending hours in a plane travelling to Russia.

Some speculated that Ratcliffe’s mission involved Iran, perhaps a request for Moscow’s help in focusing Tehran’s mind about a workable settlement that might lead to the full re-opening of the Strait of Hormuz, or perhaps a warning that Putin’s government should be careful in its dealings with a country that is the focus not only of US military but economic pressure: ties between Russia and Iran have been strong for years, with the latter a source of prodigious numbers of Shahed drones that have been used in the war against Ukraine.

## Death toll rises to 38 from Russia's deadliest attack on Ukraine this year

The death toll from Russia’s deadly attack on Kyiv rose to 38 with four people still missing, Volodymyr Zelensky said on Sunday.

The strike on Friday night on the ammunition storage facility sparked massive explosions and fires that swept through dozens of nearby homes and buildings.

The Ukrainian president said twenty people were injured, including two children.

Meanwhile, Russia's defence ministry said on Sunday it is preparing to launch “massive strikes” against Ukraine’s energy infrastructure, following a Ukrainian drone campaign against a retailer dubbed “Russia's Amazon” as well as the country's oil and gas sector, which Kyiv says both funds and directly fuels Moscow's invasion of Ukraine.

## CIA director proposed Trump, Putin, Zelensky summit - report

The director of the CIA had proposed a meeting between the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and the US when he visited Moscow, sources say.

During his secret meeting with Russian officials, John Ratcliffe had floated a summit with Vladimir Putin, Volodymyr Zelensky, and Donald Trump, Axios reported.

It comes after talks between Ukraine and Russia were paused in February, when the US invaded Iran.

The US has held talks with negotiators from both sides in recent months, but little progress has been made.

Kyrylo Budanov, Ukraine’s head of office, said this week that talks with Russia could resume in September.

Ratcliffe’s visit to Moscow on Tuesday, which was not announced in advance, has sparked much speculation.

President Trump played down the visit, saying it was “semi-routine”.

## Drone attack on Russia's Leningrad region hits residential buildings, sparks fire

Several residential buildings in Russia's Leningrad region were damaged after an attack, governor Alexander Drozdenko has said.

He added that 62 drones had been downed and a fire broke out in an industrial area of the town of Kirishi, but has now been put out.

“The consequences of the fire are currently being assessed,” Mr Drozdenko wrote on Telegram.
