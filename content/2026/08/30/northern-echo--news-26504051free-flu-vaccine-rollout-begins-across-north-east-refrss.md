Thousands of people are being invited to get their free flu vaccine as part of the annual winter rollout.

The North East and North Cumbria Integrated Care Board (ICB) is urging everyone who is eligible to take up the offer in order to protect themselves and others during the colder months.

This year’s flu vaccine programme begins on September 1 for pregnant women, children aged two and three, and school pupils from Reception to Year 11.

From October 1, the vaccine will also be available to people aged 65 and over, those with certain long-term health conditions, main carers, care home residents, and frontline health and social care workers.

Ben Anderson, director of population health at the ICB, said: "Flu is not just a nasty cold.

"It can be a very serious illness.

"Every winter we see people becoming seriously ill with flu, particularly older and more vulnerable people and those with long-term health conditions.

"Some will need hospital treatment and, sadly, flu can be fatal."

People living with someone who has a weakened immune system and people experiencing homelessness are also eligible.

Mr Anderson added: "The flu vaccine is our best defence.

"It only takes a few minutes to book, and I would strongly encourage everyone who is eligible to do so – not only to protect themselves, but also to help protect those around them."

Flu vaccines for schoolchildren will be delivered by school immunisation teams.

Parents of children who have not received their vaccine through school by December 1 can book an appointment at a participating community pharmacy through the NHS website.

Children aged two and three can receive the nasal spray vaccine at their GP practice or, from October 1, at participating community pharmacies.

Pregnant women can get their vaccine through their GP practice, local pharmacy, or during routine antenatal appointments.
