Standing high above the surrounding countryside, St John the Evangelist Church at Kirk Merrington is one of County Durham’s most striking village landmarks — with a history stretching back to the Norman period and an extraordinary connection to a medieval siege.

The church, close to Spennymoor, occupies a ridge more than 600 ft above sea level and is described by its congregation as the highest church in the Diocese of Durham.

Its prominent position means it can be seen for miles across the Durham landscape.

Although the present building dates largely from 1850-51, St John’s stands on the site of a much older Norman church.

It was rebuilt in the Victorian era by architect George Pickering, retaining part of the original north wall and recreating the earlier church’s Norman style.

## A church with a dramatic past

The original church was at the centre of one of County Durham’s most unusual medieval episodes.

In 1143, William Cumin — a former chancellor to Scotland’s King David I — attempted to seize control of the powerful bishopric of Durham.

When William de St Barbara was elected bishop, Cumin resisted and used Merrington Church as a fortress.

A defensive ditch was dug around the church as Cumin’s men tried to hold it against forces loyal to the rightful bishop-elect.

The stronghold was captured by local barons, although no trace of the temporary earthworks is believed to remain.

It gives the peaceful churchyard an unexpected place in the turbulent politics of 12th-century County Durham, when the Prince Bishops held major religious, political and military power across the region.


## Rebuilt in the Victorian era

The Norman church was largely demolished in 1850, with the replacement designed to echo the structure that had stood there for centuries.

The Grade II listed church has a sandstone rubble exterior, slate roof, nave, chancel, north transept and a substantial central tower.

The tower rises from the intersection of the nave and transept and reaches around 72 ft.

The building was also used as an observation post during the Second World War, with the elevated tower offering views across the surrounding area in case of enemy aircraft.

## Visiting St John’s Church

St John the Evangelist Church stands on Rockcliffe Terrace, Kirk Merrington, near Spennymoor, postcode DL16 7HP.

The church remains an active place of worship, holding a mixture of traditional Holy Communion, café-style services and family worship.

The congregation says the church is level-access throughout and welcomes visitors.

It is normally open on the second Tuesday of each month from 10am until 2pm, with visits at other times available by appointment.

Opening arrangements can change, so visitors should check directly with the church before making a special journey.
