Paul Collingwood became one of England cricket’s most determined and decorated players, but his story began in Shotley Bridge — the County Durham village where he first picked up a bat and learned the values that would define his career.

Born in Shotley Bridge on May 26, 1976, Collingwood grew up in a cricketing family and spent his childhood around the village club.

His father, David, was an opening batsman at Shotley Bridge Cricket Club, his mother Janet made teas, and summers for the family were spent at its ground beside the River Derwent.

For a player later known for relentless work-rate, athletic fielding and stubbornness under pressure, the club gave him an early foothold in the game.

He forced his way into Shotley Bridge’s under-13s side aged just nine, having been introduced to cricket on the playing fields of Blackfyne Comprehensive School in Consett.

## Where ‘Colly’ began

Shotley Bridge sits just outside Consett, on the edge of the Derwent Valley.

It is a place with a strong sporting identity, and its cricket club proved to be the setting for the first chapter of Collingwood’s rise.

In its tribute to the former all-rounder, Wisden described Collingwood’s father as a “stalwart opening batsman” at the village club and said the family spent much of their summers there.

The cricket magazine added that the young Collingwood was exactly the sort of local player Durham needed when it became a first-class county.

His early sporting talent was not restricted to cricket.

Collingwood also played football as a teenager, but his father persuaded him to focus on cricket — a decision that set him on course for an exceptional career.

He attended Blackfyne Comprehensive School, now Consett Academy, before breaking into Durham’s system.

He made his first-class debut for Durham in 1996, beginning an association with his home county that would last more than two decades.

## From Shotley Bridge to England

Collingwood’s route from village cricket to international sport is one of County Durham’s great sporting stories.

He made his one-day international debut for England in 2001 and his Test debut against Sri Lanka in Galle two years later.

In all, he played 68 Tests, scoring 4,259 runs at an average of more than 40, while his 197 one-day international appearances remain an England record.

He was a three-time Ashes winner and scored a memorable double century — 206 — in the 2006-07 Ashes series in Australia.

But for many supporters, the defining moment was his role as captain when England won the ICC World Twenty20 in 2010, the men’s team’s first global limited-overs title.

At Durham, he became the club’s leading first-class run scorer and most successful player.

Across 304 first-class appearances, he scored 16,844 runs and took 164 wickets, while he captained the county to the 2013 County Championship title.

## A lasting village connection

Collingwood retired from professional cricket at the end of the 2018 season, having represented Durham in 23 of the county’s first 26 years as a first-class side.

But his connection to Shotley Bridge has endured.

His success continues to offer a powerful example to young players at the club where it started: a local boy who traded football for cricket, developed at a village ground and went on to captain England to a world title.

For Shotley Bridge, Collingwood is more than a famous name on a scorecard.

He is proof that a child’s first innings at a County Durham cricket club can lead all the way to the international stage.
