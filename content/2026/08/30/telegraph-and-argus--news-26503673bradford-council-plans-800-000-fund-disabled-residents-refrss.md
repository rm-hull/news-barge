EXTRA funding could soon be available to help disabled residents live more independently at home.

Bradford Council’s executive is being asked to approve a dedicated capital fund for Discretionary Disabled Facilities Assistance (DDFA), which would provide additional funding for essential home adaptations where costs exceed the current national Disabled Facilities Grant (DFG).

The proposed funding is designed to support people with complex needs that require adaptations beyond the standard DFG provisions, ensuring they can remain safely in their own homes for longer.

Councillor Jayne Morgan, Bradford Council’s executive member for healthy people, said: "This investment demonstrates our commitment to supporting disabled residents to live independently, safely and with dignity in their own homes.

"Home adaptations can be life-changing and help people maintain their independence, reduce pressure on family carers and enable children and adults with disabilities to remain active members of their communities.

"By creating a dedicated funding allocation, we can provide greater certainty for families, improve access to essential adaptations and ensure our core Disabled Facilities Grant programme continues to support residents who need standard adaptations."

The proposal includes an £800,000 allocation for 2026/27, rising to £1.2 million per year from 2027/28, subject to annual review through the council’s capital programme.

This funding would cover major adaptations such as extensions, specialist facilities, and significant accessibility improvements not supported by the standard DFG.

The scheme aims to reduce the need for more restrictive and expensive care options by enabling people to live safely and independently at home.

The council believes that the DDFA will also help prevent hospital admissions, support timely hospital discharge, and reduce demand for residential care placements.

By addressing these needs in the community, the council hopes to deliver long-term savings and better outcomes for residents.

The new fund is intended to create a clear and transparent route for high-cost cases, helping to protect the mandatory DFG budget and improve delivery times across the broader adaptations service.

If approved, discretionary funding awards will continue to be assessed through a multidisciplinary process involving occupational therapy and housing professionals.

Regular monitoring and reporting will ensure value for money and track outcomes for residents.

The proposal aligns with the council’s broader priorities around prevention, reducing inequalities, and helping people remain connected to their communities.

It is expected to benefit residents across the entire Bradford district, offering greater security and support for those with the most complex needs.
