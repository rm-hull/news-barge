A corner of North Yorkshire known for its rolling countryside, woodland and historic estates also helped shape one of Britain’s best-known actors, Happy Valley and Grantchester star James Norton.

Although Norton was born in south London, his family moved north when he was young and settled near Malton, on the edge of the Howardian Hills.

It was here, amid the hills and farmland of rural North Yorkshire, that Norton spent much of his childhood, an upbringing he has since described as “idyllic”.

The Howardian Hills stretch across around 79 square miles between the North York Moors, Yorkshire Wolds and Vale of York.

Now designated a National Landscape, the area is characterised by wooded ridges, valleys, rolling farmland and traditional villages, with Castle Howard among its most famous landmarks.

For Norton, those surroundings appear to have left a lasting impression.

Speaking about his upbringing, he has recalled a childhood spent in the countryside which felt “very innocent”, while his earliest interest in acting was already beginning to show.

He remembered being the child who persuaded friends to put on plays in the bay window of the family home, making costumes and programmes while also directing and performing.

The North Yorkshire landscape has remained important to him long after his career took him elsewhere.

Asked about his favourite view in an interview with Condé Nast Traveller, Norton chose one close to home, looking towards Castle Howard from a hill near his parents’ house, describing it as a place that felt both peaceful and familiar.

That view sits within a landscape recognised nationally for its distinctive character.

The Howardian Hills include a patchwork of woods, pasture and farmland, along with historic estates and villages, while its network of footpaths and quiet roads makes it a popular destination for walkers and cyclists.


Norton’s childhood in North Yorkshire also took him beyond the hills.

He attended Bramcote Prep School in Scarborough before going to Ampleforth College, the Benedictine boarding school on the northern edge of the Howardian Hills.

At the age of 15, he completed work experience at Scarborough’s Stephen Joseph Theatre, an early glimpse of the profession that would later make him a household name.

He later studied theology at Fitzwilliam College, Cambridge, graduating with first-class honours before attending the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art.

His career soon gathered momentum, with appearances in films including An Education, Rush and Belle before television roles brought him widespread recognition.

Among his most memorable performances was Tommy Lee Royce, the violent and deeply unsettling criminal at the centre of BBC drama Happy Valley.

The role earned Norton a BAFTA nomination and proved a dramatic contrast to Sidney Chambers, the crime-solving clergyman he played in ITV’s Grantchester.

He has since appeared in productions including War & Peace, McMafia, Little Women, Bob Marley: One Love and King & Conqueror, while also appearing in Doctor Who early in his television career.

Despite a career that has taken him from major television dramas to Hollywood films and the West End, Norton has continued to speak fondly of the landscape in which he grew up.

Some of his best-known characters have been violent, troubled or morally complicated, yet his own formative years were spent amid one of North Yorkshire’s most tranquil landscapes.

The Howardian Hills were designated an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty in 1987 and became known as a National Landscape in 2023, recognising an area of wooded hills, secluded valleys, farmland and historic parkland that has remained largely rural.

Castle Howard, Nunnington Hall, Hovingham Hall and Newburgh Priory are among the grand estates that have helped shape the landscape over centuries, while Malton sits just outside its south-eastern boundary.
