A top Treasury boss has vowed the Government will provide the funding to back growth in North Yorkshire after revealing a £40m investment connected to high streets.

Chief Secretary to the Treasury Emma Reynolds toured No.11 North at the Darlington Economic Campus and a soft play centre in Northallerton last week, where she backed efforts to shift power away from Westminster and support struggling town centres.

Speaking to The Northern Echo, Ms Reynolds confirmed local growth funding for York and North Yorkshire that will total £40m worth of investment to support the Northallerton Town Investment Plan and High Street Growth Plan.

This funding will be spaced out between this year and 2030 and has been backed by the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority and North Yorkshire Council.

  The Chief Secretary to the Treasury Emma Reynolds at work in No.11 North at the Darlington Economic Campus 

*(Image: Simon Walker / HM Treasury)*

As part of the funding, projects including business, tourism and events, environment, community and health, culture, heritage and leisure, transport and movement, alongside education and skills will be supported.

During her visit to North Yorkshire, Ms Reynolds backed the Government to invest in high streets, saying that "plans were already in place" to ensure that "North Yorkshire was backed to its full potential".

"We want businesses to thrive on the high street, and people to spend more and more time on the high streets," she said, speaking during a visit to Crazy Kingdom in Northallerton.

  The Chief Secretary to the Treasury, Emma Reynolds, visited Crazy Kingdom in Northallerton, a local soft play centre, where she met parents and children enjoying the facilities 

*(Image: Simon Walker / HM Treasury)*

"The business rates we give to places that hurt our high streets, we want to increase these, so independent businesses and the positive influences can thrive."

The Treasury secretary added that this includes the announcement of a 20 per cent cut to business rates for pubs, social clubs, and live music venues in England, starting in April 2027 and the potential to increase tax rates on vape shops, which the secretary called a "blight on high streets".

On devolution, Ms Reynolds noted the Government's creation of No10 North, No11 North and continued pledges of devolution for other parts of the UK, outside of Whitehall.

*(Image: Simon Walker / HM Treasury)*

"Today, I also visited Darlington and saw a vision of what devolution can be for the North East and the North of England," she added.

"We want to be backing independent shops, we don't want empty shops on the high street, and we want footfall to increase more and more in high streets in this region.

"Hopefully the funding announced will introduce plenty more reasons for people to visit their high streets."

Ms Reynolds was visiting North Yorkshire to promote the government's "Great British Summer Savings" scheme, which drastically cuts the cost of family outings and children's meals by lowering VAT from 20 per cent to five per cent.

Though initially introduced for the summer holidays, she recently championed these ongoing measures as vital support to give families "breathing space" during school breaks and half-terms.
