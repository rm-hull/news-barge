Lucy Worsley, BBC presenter, historian and former chief curator at Historic Royal Palaces, will be exploring Britain's monarchy in front of a York audience as she brings her touring show to this city next month.

Speaking ahead of her upcoming theatre tour ‘My Royal Life’, Lucy told this newspaper about her passion for her subject and what history enthusiasts can expect from her show at Grand Opera House York on Friday, September 18.

The popular historian is familiar all over the nation for her effervescent style of presenting on TV. She has become a staple of the BBC, a knowledgeable and engaging personality capable of talking about a vast range of topics.

“The real pleasure is not just acquiring the knowledge but talking about it so people listen,” she told us.

“I am in a position of talking to people about history and I do not mind which form that takes whether it is on stage, on television or presenting digital guides to visitors at the palace in the past.

“I am a very niche historian, some call it public history.

"It is not for people who are interested in history at universities. It is about dipping your toe into the water but I want people to jump in.”

Her upcoming tour, described by Lucy herself as “a bit cheeky”, is based on her two decades working at Historic Royal Palaces where she would write down copious notes before they escaped from her memory.

To coincide with a book she has written, the show is a history of the monarchy with a particular focus on three Queens, namely Queen Victoria, Queen Caroline and Queen Catherine Howard.

Regarding Queen Victoria, who reigned as the monarch of Great Britain and Ireland from 1837 to 1901 and the empress of India from 1876, Lucy says she is keen to change the narrative surrounding her.

“I believe she has been unfortunately put in the same box as Prince Albert but she only lived with him for 21 years of her life and lived for three quarters of her life without him,” Lucy explained.

“To my mind he tried to diminish her and undermined her. Queen Victoria is very much the heroine of the story and I try to pull apart her love affairs in some way.”

As for Catherine Howard, the fifth wife of Henry VIII who was beheaded in 1541 on the grounds of treason for adultery, Lucy described her life as a “sad story”.

The historian told this newspaper: “20 years ago a royal historian described her as a sex pot, unfaithful to her husband, ditzy, lightweight and slutty. What I and lots of other historians think is that she was a victim.”

I asked Lucy about her thoughts on the concept of monarchy as a whole, pondering whether it is still as relevant in today’s society amid scandals and family fallouts.

She responded: “The royals are woven into national life partly because of the long history of the institution and also because they are celebrities. They provide a purpose for all of us.

“Scandals, triumphs and events are in our lives and we all follow this story which is something to talk about over the passage of time. They remind us of our own mortality and they are very good for the tourism industry.”

Lucy revealed her favourite project she has worked on as being the Bafta winning series on the Suffragettes in 2018.

She has become a celebrity figure herself given her ubiquitous presence on our screens.

“A lot of people do come up to me when I am on a set but if I go to Lidl I put on a hoodie and sneak through,” she said. “Nobody bats an eyelid.

“Sometimes people will look at me and will say ‘I feel like I know you from somewhere’. One guy once said to me in the street ‘It’s the history person’.”

She believes history is as popular as ever in world of social media and instant gratification.

“Whatever form, people will always be interested in history,” she stated.

“I do see that particularly in the point of life where kids are really into the history of dinosaurs then life gets in the way. Then years later they become interested in family history.”

For herself as a historian, the presenter is a sponge soaking up information as she embarks on her latest project.

With a series on the American Revolution airing at the moment, and another secret adventure in the works, Lucy really is at the top of her game.

“I can’t believe people pay me to learn things,” she exclaimed.

“The work that goes into any of our TV series is extraordinary. One of the pleasures is getting a bit of a tutorial on a subject like the American Revolution.

"I work with amazing teams at the BBC delving into a subject and distilling it into something fun for TV.”

  **My Royal Life: An Audience With Lucy Worsley runs across the UK from Tuesday, September 1, 2026 with the show at Grand Opera House York taking place on Friday, September 18. Tickets are available via the Fane website at www.fane.co.uk/lucy-worsley.**
