A JUNCTION improvement scheme that was first proposed over seven years ago is expected to get a £6.6 million boost next week.

The junction where Toller Lane, Whetley Hill, Whetley Lane and Carlisle Road come together was first earmarked for major improvements in 2019 as part of a city wide scheme to reduce congestion on the city’s worst junctions.

At a meeting of West Yorkshire Combined Authority’s Investment Committee next week, members will be asked to approve more money for the scheme, taking its total budget to £8.56m.

The funding will come from the Authority’s Transport Capital Programme, and the committee will hear that the costs for the scheme have risen by 20 per cent since the outline business case for the junction was drawn up.

If funding is approved, work could start in October, and be completed by November 2027.

## What is happening?

The works will see the creation of new bus priority signals, eight pedestrian crossings, the closure of Cross Road to traffic and the planting of 54 trees.

It is hoped the changes will lead to a 25 per cent reduction in journey times for vehicles passing through the junction.

In the past five years, there have been 31 road traffic casualties on this stretch of road.

A number of derelict properties on the junction were demolished years ago in anticipation of the scheme.

## What does the report say?

The report says “eight small plots of land” will need to be purchased for the scheme to continue.

Negotiations with the owners are ongoing, but Compulsory Purchase Orders may be used if these negotiations are not resolved soon.

A report going to Thursday’s committee meeting says: “Given the high volume of traffic, the Toller Lane junction experiences significant congestion, with traffic queueing during the morning and evening peak periods, leading to unreliable and longer journey times.

“Congestion also impacts bus services, resulting in long journey times and poor journey time reliability.

“Services 617/618 pass through the junction at approximately 15- minute intervals, serving communities from Allerton in the northwest of Bradford to Holme Wood in the southeast.

“Traffic journey time data from 2024 shows congestion in the evening peak period results in average journey times of approximately 12 minutes, double those of the morning peak period (approximately six minutes) and nearly three times as long as at off-peak times (approximately four minutes).

“The Toller Lane junction is also a key route to Bradford Royal Infirmary from the city centre, therefore increasing levels of congestion risks impacting ambulances and patients seeking to access the Hospital.

“There are several accident clusters at the junction, and the number and severity of collisions has increased in recent years, with 31 casualties in the last five-year period. Of these incidents, five (16 per cent) have involved cyclist or pedestrian casualties.”

## The proposed changes will introduce:

- Two new bus priority signals
- Segregated left-turn slip lanes onto Toller Lane from Whetley Lane and Carlisle Road approaches.
- Eight new signal-controlled pedestrian crossings and one new Toucan crossing.
- Permanent closure of Cross Road to simplify junction layout and minimise traffic conflicts, contributing to efficient signal staging.
- Footway widening and enhancements along Church Street and Whetley Lane.
- Fifty metres of new cycle lane between Cross Road and Church Street.
- One bus stop shelter upgrade.
- Planting 54 trees and 3,345 square metres of soft landscaping.
- 8,509 square metres of highway resurfacing.

## Why the lengthy delay?

Explaining the lengthy delay between the works first being proposed and the final funding being approved, the report says: “The Toller Lane scheme previously formed part of the wider Thornton Road/Toller Lane business case, which secured outline business case approval in 2019.

“Due to funding constraints, the two schemes were decoupled via a change request in 2024.

"Full business case development funding was allocated to both schemes, but delivery funding only allocated to the Thornton Road scheme because it was further developed than Toller Lane at that time and because it was designed to integrate with the West Bradford Cycle Superhighway scheme that was also being delivered in parallel.

“The Thornton Road scheme is due to start delivery in summer 2026.

“A review of schemes within the existing capital programme has been undertaken to determine which schemes are at the most advanced state of readiness and have the greatest ability to maximise delivery of outcomes with remaining legacy funding.

“The delivery of Toller Lane has been prioritised on that basis.”

Referring to the rising costs, the report says: “costs have increased by approximately 20 per cent since outline business case due to delays, re-design and re-modelling work being required.

“However, the scheme is affordable within the current Transport Capital Programme, with funding in place to support delivery. It also demonstrates a high value for money case, supported by a strong strategic narrative.”
