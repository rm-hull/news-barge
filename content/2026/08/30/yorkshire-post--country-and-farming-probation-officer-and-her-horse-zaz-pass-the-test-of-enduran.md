# Probation officer and her horse Zaz pass the test of endurance

## A probation officer from Barnsley has spoken of her pride at achieving a lifelong dream after being selected to represent England at this year’s Endurance GB Home Internationals.

Emma Martin will ride her eight-year-old part-bred Arab gelding, Vlacq Mr Pizzaz, known at home as Zaz, at the landmark event taking place in Scotland from Friday to next Sunday.

The selection comes on the back of a performance at Thetford heralded by onlookers as “sensational”, where the partnership successfully completed the 80km Competitive Endurance Ride (CER).

Zaz finished the distance with an exemplary veterinary record, proving his peak fitness and readiness to step up to the national stage. In Scotland, Emma and Zaz aim to secure their second successful CER completion, with a focus on quick veterinary presentation times and maintaining the horse's superb condition.

![Emma Martin and Zaz, who will compete at the Endurance GB Home Internationals.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU3Mjk2ZDgzLWM1NDAtNGFlZC1iYjc3LTcyODVjZDRhODQyMDphOTM2NmEyYS02NzU3LTRhY2MtYTAwMi01ZTZmZmM0ZGUwYjU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Endurance riding is regarded by some as the ultimate test of equine fitness, preparation, and the partnership between horse and rider. Competitors navigate marked routes across challenging terrain, with strict veterinary inspections – known as vet gates – taking place before, during, and after the ride. To successfully complete a ride, a horse must pass these checks, with vets monitoring heart rate recovery, hydration, soundness, and overall well-being. The motto of the sport remains: “To complete is to win.”

Emma said: “I am incredibly proud of Zaz. Bringing him through the ranks from a young horse to Advanced, and now getting to represent England, is a feeling I can’t quite put into words.

“I actually bought Zaz during Covid as a three-year-old after spotting him on a Facebook photo. Because of lockdown restrictions, I had to wait nine long months just to go and see him, but it was absolutely love at sight. He is the most affectionate boy—he even stretches his head over my shoulder for pre-ride cuddles to delay having his bridle put on! I know I can never deny him his pre-exercise hugs.”

Emma’s journey in endurance started when she was just eight years old, inspired by her mother, Julie Martin, while riding with the Endurance Horse and Pony Society (EHPS). Though Emma has successfully competed in showjumping, eventing, and riding club activities over the years, the unique spirit of endurance always called her back.

Preparing a national-level endurance horse requires dedicated local infrastructure and a strong support network. Emma is based at Crabtree Grange Farm equestrian centre in Great Houghton, where the short, steep hills provide the perfect training ground without the need for road work. For essential speed and fitness work, Emma utilises the specialist gallops at the Northern Racing College, near Doncaster.

Looking ahead to Scotland, Emma remains focused on the welfare of her partner.

“The biggest challenge will be the unknown terrain, but we will adapt our speed to how Zaz feels on the day,” Emma said. “Endurance is a big, welcoming family where horse welfare is always paramount. For any local riders thinking about trying it, I’d say don’t hesitate – the community will support you every single step of the way.”
