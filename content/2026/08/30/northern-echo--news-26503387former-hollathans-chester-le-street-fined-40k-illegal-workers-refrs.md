The company behind a now-closed Chester-le-Street restaurant was given a £40,000 fine after employing illegal workers.

KLW NE Ltd, trading as the former Hollathan's restaurant on Ashfield Terrace, was featured in the latest Home Office list of firms fined.

Hollathan’s has since closed, with its long-time owners having stepped away from the venue after around 20 years.

In a statement last August owners Keith and Linda, said: "This decision was not made lightly, but after 20 years at the fabulous, and 53 years of working in the hospitality industry, 47 of which running our own businesses, the time has come to take a well-earned rest and to spend quality time with my wife and family.

*(Image: GOOGLE)*

The £40,000 penalty appears in the Home Office’s latest list of employers issued with penalties for illegal working, covering the period from January 1 to March 31, 2026.

The business name recorded on the Home Office list is KLW NE Ltd, with Hollathan’s listed alongside the company at the Ashfield Terrace address.

According to the Home Office, civil penalties can be issued to employers found to have employed people who did not have permission to work in the UK or who were working in breach of restrictions attached to their immigration status.

The £40,000 fine is one of several issued to businesses across the North East in the latest quarterly figures.

Another County Durham business, Pizza Hot NE Ltd, linked to Pizza Hot on Main Street in Ferryhill, was also handed a £40,000 penalty.

Style Barbers Birtley Ltd received the same penalty.

The largest fine among the North East businesses in the latest release was £135,000, issued to Jana Shawarma Ltd, linked to Shawarma & Grill House in Newcastle.

Mr Bubbles Mini Valet Centre NE Ltd in Newcastle received a £90,000 penalty, while Sunderland Hand Car Wash Ltd, linked to Fence Houses Hand Car Wash, was fined £80,000.

Employers are required to check that anyone they recruit has the legal right to work in the UK.

The Home Office says prescribed right-to-work checks must be carried out before employment begins and further checks may be required where somebody has time-limited permission to work.

The quarterly report lists employers whose civil penalty cases have reached the relevant stage of the objection and appeal process.
