A York-based construction company has seen an increase in profit margins despite a fall in turnover.

William Birch & Sons reported that its pre-tax profits for the 2025 financial year had increased from £1.2m to £1.6m, despite a decrease in turnover from £24.1m to £18.2m.

This was achieved through an increase in operational efficiency, reported the Group of Companies' accounts published this week.

The operating profit margin increased from 4.8 per cent to 5.7 per cent, maintaining profitability in cash terms.

The company said it experienced increased competition in tendering throughout the year, along with client hesitancy to commit to projects.

This resulted in fewer tender awards and, consequently, a reduction in turnover.

However, this was offset by building a stronger enquiry pipeline and securing more negotiated tender opportunities.

The company delivered over £5.5m of negotiated work on-site, a 30 per cent increase compared with the previous year.

It also maintained visibility of a further £4.5m of negotiated opportunities within the pipeline.

The company reported a slight fall in employee numbers, aligning with the reduced turnover.

However, it expressed concerns about the availability of suitable candidates in the industry, should business levels increase.

The company's property division performed well, with increases in rental levels due to strong demand in the small-sized industrial property sector.

However, some properties proved difficult to re-let due to market weakness in certain areas.

A significant development for the company was the scheduling of another 19 acres of ex-airfield land for employment use within the York Local Plan.

This land, which the company said it had been working on for decades, will require significant resources to develop efficiently.

The uplift in property value from this development has been recognised and adds to the company's existing bank of land retained for future development purposes.

The directors of William Birch & Sons say they spent considerable time throughout the year working on a detailed strategy to ensure the long-term sustainability, culture, and reputation of the construction business, in line with shareholders' aims.

The company's pre-construction team successfully strengthened the opportunity pipeline, increasing its value from an average of £68 million at the beginning of the financial year to £100 million by year-end.

Production teams continued to improve project delivery through detailed programming, proactive resource planning/allocation, quality control, and health and safety management.

The company's robust risk management processes, from pre-construction through delivery, continue to increase margin opportunities and minimise potential risks, it said.
