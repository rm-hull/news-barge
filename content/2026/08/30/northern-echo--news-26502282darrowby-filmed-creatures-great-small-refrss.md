All Creatures Great and Small fans have spent decades watching James Herriot walk the streets of Darrowby, but the famous Yorkshire town does not actually exist.

Instead, two beautiful Yorkshire Dales communities have taken turns bringing the fictional home of Skeldale House to life.

Today, viewers of the Channel 5 version will recognise Grassington as Darrowby.

But anyone who remembers the classic BBC adaptation starring Christopher Timothy, Robert Hardy and Peter Davison may picture somewhere very different.

For them, Darrowby is Askrigg.

  The cast of All Creatures Great and Small 

*(Image: CHANNEL 5)*

The two Yorkshire villages sit around 25 miles apart as the crow flies and belong to different parts of the Dales, yet both have become synonymous with James Herriot's stories.

The latest incarnation of All Creatures Great and Small has used Grassington since arriving on Channel 5 in 2020.

The historic market square and traditional stone buildings are regularly transformed for filming, taking the village back to the 1930s and 1940s.

The Devonshire becomes the exterior of The Drovers Arms, while The Stripey Badger has appeared as G F Endleby's grocers.

Other businesses become everything from bakeries and bookshops to ironmongers, while a private property is used for exterior shots of Skeldale House.

When filming ends, the fictional signs disappear and modern Grassington returns.

However, the buildings themselves remain, meaning fans walking through the village can recognise many of the places they have seen Nicholas Ralph, Samuel West, Rachel Shenton and the rest of the cast visit on screen.

Long before Grassington became Darrowby, though, another Yorkshire village had the job.


Askrigg, in Wensleydale, was used extensively during the original BBC series of All Creatures Great and Small, which began in 1978 and eventually ran until 1990.

Exterior scenes were filmed around Askrigg and elsewhere in North Yorkshire, with the village becoming closely associated with the fictional Darrowby for a generation of television viewers.

The BBC series starred Christopher Timothy as James Herriot, Robert Hardy as Siegfried Farnon and Peter Davison as Tristan Farnon.

Askrigg's streets and surrounding Wensleydale countryside became so familiar that for many fans it remains the definitive Darrowby.

That makes All Creatures Great and Small unusual: two generations can have entirely different pictures in their heads when someone mentions the same fictional Yorkshire town.

Neither is the place where the real James Herriot actually worked.

Author Alf Wight, who wrote under the name James Herriot, practised as a vet in Thirsk.

His former surgery at 23 Kirkgate later became The World of James Herriot visitor attraction.

Wight fictionalised his surroundings in his writing, with Darrowby drawing inspiration from Yorkshire rather than corresponding exactly to one real town.

Now Grassington is preparing to take centre stage once again.

Channel 5 has released the first images from Series 7 of the current adaptation, welcoming viewers “back to Darrowby” ahead of its return in September.

The story moves into 1946, with Nicholas Ralph returning as James Herriot alongside Samuel West, Rachel Shenton, Callum Woodhouse and Anna Madeley.

A new rival vet, Granville Bennett, played by Caolan McCarthy, will also arrive in the Dales as James embraces developments in post-war veterinary medicine.

Series 8 has already been commissioned, meaning Grassington's time as Darrowby is far from over.
