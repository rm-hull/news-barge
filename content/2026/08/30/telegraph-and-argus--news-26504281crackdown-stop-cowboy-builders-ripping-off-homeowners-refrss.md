COWBUY builders and rogue bailiffs are being targeted under new government plans to protect households.

Prime minister Andy Burnham said the changes would tackle unfair practices in home improvement and debt collection, as part of his “everyday fixes” agenda to give families room to breathe and make Britain fairer.

He said: "Cowboy builders don't just rip people off.

"They leave families with months of stress, the disruption of unfinished work, and that horrible feeling of being taken advantage of.

"Not only that, but they give the vast majority of decent, hardworking tradespeople a bad name.

"So we're putting the cowboys out of business."

A government-backed scheme launching in September will help consumers find reputable tradespeople, with businesses required to meet high standards for customer service and transparency.

Payments will be released in stages as work progresses, protecting homeowners’ money.

Jonathan Reynolds, business secretary, said: "Your home is the biggest investment you make.

"While improving it can be stressful, we are helping families to avoid it turning into a nightmare.

"We're taking action to protect consumers, support reputable traders and make sure people can spend their money with greater confidence and security."

The measures also include tougher regulation of private bailiffs in England and Wales.
