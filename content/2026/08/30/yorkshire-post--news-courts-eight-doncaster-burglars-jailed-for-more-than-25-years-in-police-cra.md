# Eight Doncaster burglars jailed for more than 25 years in police crackdown

## Eight burglars have been jailed for a combined total of more than 25 years following a series of investigations into break-ins across Doncaster.

The sentences were secured during August following investigations by South Yorkshire Police's dedicated Operation Castle team, which targets residential burglary in the city.

Russell Riley, 46, and Peter Coulthread, 47, both of Cardigan Road, Doncaster, were among the first to be sentenced this month after attempting to break into a home in Bawtry Road.

The pair were spotted by the homeowner, who called police, and were arrested less than 20 minutes later.

Both admitted attempted burglary, going equipped to steal and attempted theft, while Riley also admitted possessing a knife or blade in a public place.

Riley was jailed for two years and ten months and Coulthread for two years and eight months.

A father and son were then jailed after breaking into a property in the middle of the night and stealing the victim's car in a so-called '2-in-1 burglary'.

Detectives trawled CCTV from the area and spotted two suspects dressed in dark clothing around the time of the break-in.

An Operation Castle detective identified the pair as Ben and Lee MacManus and they were arrested.

A subsequent search recovered items of clothing matching those seen on CCTV.

Ben MacManus, 20, and his father Lee, 48, both of no fixed abode, admitted burglary and theft.

At Sheffield Crown Court on Wednesday (Aug 19), Ben was sentenced to three years in a young offenders' institution, while Lee was jailed for three years and four months.

Michael Scott, 47, of Maple Road, was also jailed following an Operation Castle investigation into a burglary at a home in Mexborough.

He was sentenced to two years and five months in prison.

Andrew Stewart was caught after forensic evidence linked him to two burglaries.

A stone used to smash a window during a burglary at a property in Bessacarr was analysed and provided a DNA match to Stewart.

His fingerprint was also recovered from a cat flap at the scene of another burglary.

Stewart, 42, of no fixed abode, pleaded guilty to two burglaries and was jailed for two years and five months at Sheffield Crown Court on Tuesday (Aug 25).

Daniel Finigan was sentenced the same day after breaking into a house in Bentley and stealing car keys, cash and other expensive items.

Police arrived at the property and, while officers were examining the scene, Finigan jumped from a window and attempted to flee.

A response officer pursued him and managed to detain him despite what police described as violent attempts to escape, holding on to him until colleagues arrived.

Finigan, 39, of no fixed abode, admitted two counts of burglary and assault by beating of an emergency worker.

He was jailed for two years and five months.

Mackenzie-dean Wilson was the final Operation Castle offender to be sentenced in August.

Police linked him to an offence after fingerprints were recovered from a window frame, while CCTV corroborated his movements around the time of the burglary.

A vehicle he stole was later recovered and returned to its owner.

Wilson, 24, of Davis Road, Instoneville, was sentenced to two years in prison for burglary and theft of a motor vehicle at Sheffield Crown Court on Thursday (Aug 27).

Police said the eight burglars jailed during August received combined sentences totalling more than 25 years.

Operation Castle detectives investigate patterns of offending and build intelligence around prolific residential burglars operating in Doncaster.

The team also works alongside neighbourhood officers on intelligence-led patrols in burglary hotspots and with officers who monitor convicted burglars following their release from prison.

Police say residential burglary rates in Doncaster have recently fallen to their lowest level in five years, while Operation Castle investigations have resulted in more than 70 years of prison sentences being handed out during 2026.

Detective Sergeant Milner, who heads the Operation Castle team, said: "August has been a really pivotal month in securing justice for victims of residential burglary in Doncaster. That mission drives our work and after working with countless victims of this type of crime, we know just how devastating and impactful it is to have your home broken into.

"The team's knowledge of the cohort of people committing these offences and the tactics they are using continues to grow day by day and it's leading to dangerous offenders being brought before the courts and sent to prison.

"I want to thank all victims of these offences for their cooperation and support with our investigations. Without this and without these reports to police, we wouldn't be able to bring offenders to justice.

"Anyone who decides to break into someone's home should be in no doubt that we will find you, we will gather the evidence needed to put you before the courts and we will keep working to make our communities safer."
