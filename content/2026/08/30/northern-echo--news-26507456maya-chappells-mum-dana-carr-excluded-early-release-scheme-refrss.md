The family of murdered County Durham tot Maya Chappell have spoken of their relief at changes to the Government's early release scheme which will keep her mum behind bars.

Dana Carr, is serving a nine-year prison sentence for allowing the death of her two-year-old daughter Maya, murdered by her new partner Michael Daymond in September 2022.

Last month Maya's family were told Carr could be released early under plans to help free up space in prisons.

Great-aunt Gemma Chappell called on the Government to have a rethink, saying the plans made a "complete mockery" of the justice system, and it was "heartbreaking" to think Carr could be walking the streets having served less than three years of a nine-year sentence.

  Dana Carr 

*(Image: DURHAM CONSTABULARY)*

Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced on Sunday (August 30) morning that prisoners convicted of causing or allowing the death of a child will now be excluded from changes being introduced to ease pressure on overcrowded jails.

Gemma said the family was "incredibly relieved" after weeks of uncertainty over how the changes could affect Carr’s sentence.

  Maya Chappell 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

She added that this decision from the PM is "for all of those families across the UK in the same position as us".

"It's a weight lifted off our shoulders," Gemma said.

"This decision means that victims and families can build a relationship and trust with the new Prime Minister.

"It will mean that our family don't have to come face to face with Dana Carr in the street, and she will still have to serve another three years.

  Gemma Chappell 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

“This shows that the Prime Minister has come in and listened to victims and their families.

"The fact that the PM has listened to the victims, and to the families as secondary victims of these crimes, has shown that he is willing to have that conversation with the public and people like us."

The Northern Echo has campaigned alongside Maya’s family against the proposed changes.

Carr was jailed at Teesside Crown Court in December 2023 after being convicted of allowing the death of Maya, and child cruelty by neglect.

  Prime Minister Andy Burnham 

*(Image: Cathal McNaughton/PA Wire)*

The judge said Carr would be released from custody no later than two-thirds of the way through her nine-year term, after six years, with the remainder served on licence.

Changes under the Sentencing Act 2026 had caused fresh anguish for Maya’s relatives amid fears Carr could become eligible to leave prison substantially earlier than they had been told when she was sentenced.

  Maya Chappell 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

But the Government confirmed on Sunday, August 30, that causing or allowing the death of a child will now be specifically excluded from those changes, alongside offences including manslaughter and causing death by dangerous driving.

Maya was violently shaken by Carr’s then-boyfriend Michael Daymond at their home in Shotton Colliery in September 2022.

  Michael Daymond 

*(Image: DURHAM CONSTABULARY)*

She suffered a severe brain injury and died at Newcastle’s Royal Victoria Infirmary two days later.

Daymond was convicted of Maya’s murder and sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term of 20 years.

  Gemma Chappell, Great Aunt of Maya Chappell 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Gemma added: "We will never forgive Dana Carr or Daymond for these awful crimes, and it's good that the Ministry of Justice have backed this up and not released these people early.

"What gives us the reassurances is that it's written down on paper; the PM has backed this.

"We've been waiting for him to back this since he came into the role and he's finally done what we've been asking for."

Maya’s family, who are fighting for child safeguarding laws alongside The Northern Echo, had previously described being left in “limbo” by the proposed release changes and feared they or younger relatives could unexpectedly come face-to-face with Carr after she left prison.

They had called on Mr Burnham to intervene and end what they described as an “emotional rollercoaster” for families affected by serious crimes.

The PM said “hundreds” of extra people will remain in prison following the changes, which will be laid in Parliament through secondary legislation at the beginning of next week.

  Rachael Walls (left) and Gemma Chappell (right), Maya's great aunts 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

He added: “I know how strongly people feel about this issue. I understand the concern and anger because I share it.

“That’s why I took action during my first weeks as Prime Minister to block some of the very worst in society being released before they should.

“We moved quickly to block vile criminals who commit rape, sexually abuse children and groom vulnerable young people.

“But I always said I wanted to do more. We will now not only be keeping offenders serving time for indecent assault locked up, but also those in prison for manslaughter and death by dangerous driving."

The Government's rethink followed a separate campaign over the sentences handed to two of the men responsible for the death of PC Andrew Harper.

Albert Bowers and Jessie Cole were convicted of manslaughter after PC Harper, 28, was dragged behind a car while responding to the theft of a quad bike in Berkshire in August 2019.

They had faced becoming eligible for release earlier under the changes, despite receiving 13-year sentences, prompting widespread criticism and a campaign backed by PC Harper's family and police representatives.

Sunday's announcement means all forms of manslaughter will now be excluded from the new arrangements, preventing Bowers and Cole from having their sentences shortened. The Government said offenders responsible for killing an emergency worker in the line of duty will also not be released any earlier.

Mr Burnham said he understood the "concern and anger" surrounding the proposals and had always wanted to go further in excluding serious offenders.

He said: “We will now not only be keeping offenders serving time for indecent assault locked up, but also those in prison for manslaughter and death by dangerous driving.”
