If you've ever driven past the sign for Fylingthorpe on the road into Robin Hood's Bay and mentally stumbled over how to say it, you're far from alone.

This North Yorkshire village name trips up visitors more than almost any other on the Yorkshire coast.

## How to pronounce Fylingthorpe

The correct pronunciation is FY-ling-thorp, with the stress on the first syllable, which rhymes with 'eye' rather than 'fill'.

Many first-time visitors default to 'FILL-ing-thorp' or break it into an awkward 'Fy-LING-thorpe,' but locals in this pocket of the North York Moors keep it simple: think 'filing' cabinet, but swap the 'i' for a long 'y' sound, followed by 'thorp'.

## Why the name is so unusual

The tricky spelling has deep Scandinavian roots.

Fylingthorpe sits within the ancient Fylingdales parish, and the 'thorpe' element is a classic Old Norse suffix meaning a small farmstead or hamlet, brought to this stretch of coast by Viking settlers who arrived around 1,000 years ago.

The village itself was originally known simply as 'Thorpe,' before being recorded as 'Prestethorpe' in the 13th century, and only later took on the 'Fyling' prefix from the surrounding parish to distinguish it from other Yorkshire villages sharing the same common Norse name.


## A village with Domesday roots

Fylingthorpe's history stretches back further than its current name.

It appears in the Domesday Book of 1086, when it was recorded as 'waste' and unpopulated, before coming under the control of William de Percy, who granted the land to Whitby Abbey between 1091 and 1096.

Today the village sits inland from Robin Hood's Bay, less than a mile from the coast, and is home to notable buildings including the 17th-century Thorpe Hall and Fyling Hall, the Georgian house that now serves as an independent school founded in 1923.

## Not the only tricky name nearby

Fylingthorpe isn't the only local place name that catches people out — nearby Fylingdales itself, home to the famous RAF early warning station, is another regularly mispronounced North Yorkshire name, generally rendered 'FY-ling-dales' rather than 'FILL-ing-dales'.

The Northern Echo has previously explored several other commonly mispronounced spots across North Yorkshire, including Mewker (Muker) and Chop Gate (Chop-Yat), both of which defy how they look on the page.
