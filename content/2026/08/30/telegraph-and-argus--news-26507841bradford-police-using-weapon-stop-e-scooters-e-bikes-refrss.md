West Yorkshire Police has been using an invisible DNA spray to catch masked yobs riding illegal e-bikes and electric motorcycles.

Following a successful trial, the force introduced SelectaDNA Defence Spray across all five of its policing districts late last year.

Officers and police community support officers have been trained to use the device against anti-social behaviour, retail crime and neighbourhood disorder.

## Successful trials

The move follows trials in Leeds and Wakefield, which police described as highly successful. Funding has come from the Home Office’s Safer Streets fund, which supports new approaches to preventing crime.

The handheld canister can fire a fine mist over distances of up to 10 metres. The spray coats riders, their clothing and vehicles with an invisible marker containing synthetic DNA.

Each canister carries a unique forensic code. This allows investigators to connect a person or vehicle with a particular incident, even weeks or months after the spray has been used.

The marker can be seen under ultraviolet light and is designed to stay on skin, clothing and vehicles. According to the information provided by the force, it cannot be washed off.

Police said the spray is safe to use, harmless to people and animals, and does not damage clothing or property. It is intended to give officers evidence that can link suspects to offences and support prosecutions.

West Yorkshire Police has used SelectaDNA defence sprays for several years. Previous operations focused on rogue riders, moped-enabled offenders and persistent anti-social behaviour.

The force said the technology had contributed to arrests and successful prosecutions. Evidence from the spray has also been used in court, where its forensic code can help show that a suspect was connected to a specific incident.

Superintendent Andy Loftus said: “We have seen in previous trials here how effective the use of DNA tagging spray can be in tackling issues such as anti-social behaviour and retail crime.”

Superintendent Andy Loftus said: “These are matters which cause a lot of concern to our communities, and I am pleased that all our officers and staff now have access to use this useful product, which will help them to target individuals and the vehicles they use to commit crime and cause disruption to neighbourhoods."

The spray is being used in operations involving the anti-social use of motorbikes and e-bikes. It can also support work against retail and commercial theft, public disorder and disruption on streets.

## The fightback starts now

The wider move comes amid growing complaints about powered two-wheelers being ridden on pavements and in public spaces. Police forces across the UK, including Bradford, have also unveiled DNA tagging spray and DeterTech’s SmartTag as tools for dealing with offences involving e-bikes, e-scooters and motorcycles.

West Yorkshire Police is also a major supporter of BikeRegister, the UK’s national cycle database. The force has helped deliver a 15 per cent reduction in cycle thefts in areas including Leeds and across West Yorkshire, according to the information provided.

## Tackling the issue head-on

Alison Lowe OBE, West Yorkshire’s Deputy Mayor for Policing and Crime, said: “We know that anti-social behaviour can blight communities - that's why innovative tools like this are crucial to tackling this issue head on.”

Alison Lowe OBE, West Yorkshire’s Deputy Mayor for Policing and Crime, said last year: “This investment from the Home Office will help us deliver on our Police and Crime Plan, working together to prevent crime and create a safer, fairer West Yorkshire.”

The force-wide rollout means the technology is now available to officers and staff across West Yorkshire. Its use is part of a wider policing approach that combines enforcement with efforts to prevent theft, disorder and nuisance in local communities.

SelectaDNA’s system works by leaving a trace that is invisible without ultraviolet equipment. Investigators can match that trace to the canister used, helping them connect a suspect with an offence after the event.

Police said the deployment would continue to support operations against riders and vehicles linked to crime and disruption. The Home Office funding has allowed the force to extend the system beyond the earlier trials in Leeds and Wakefield.

The latest rollout places forensic tagging technology in routine use across the county. It also adds to West Yorkshire Police’s existing work with cycle registration and other measures aimed at reducing theft and anti-social behaviour.
