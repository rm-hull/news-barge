Thousands of rural homes and businesses in County Durham are set to benefit from a major broadband upgrade as work gets under way to bring gigabit-capable internet to some of the county's hardest-to-reach communities.

The improvements are part of the UK Government’s Project Gigabit scheme, which aims to deliver fast and reliable full fibre broadband to areas that might otherwise be left behind due to their remote locations.

Openreach engineers are now working to connect properties in and around Dalton, Newsham, Gayles, Beamish, Edmundbyers, New Brancepeth, and Rushyford.

  Openreach engineers at work 

*(Image: Martin Avery)*

Ian Murray, UK Telecoms Minister, said: "Too many people in rural areas have had to put up with patchy, unreliable broadband for too long – and we’re changing that.

"Through our government-funded Project Gigabit programme, we’re bringing faster, more reliable connections to homes and businesses that might otherwise miss out.

"That means less buffering, smoother video calls, children able to do their online homework without battling for bandwidth, and rural businesses better placed to grow, create jobs and reach customers."

The full fibre upgrade promises gigabit-capable speeds, enabling households to connect multiple devices with ease and helping businesses compete in an increasingly digital marketplace.

  Openreach engineers at work 

*(Image: Martin Avery)*

To date, the UK Government has signed contracts worth £850 million with Openreach as part of Project Gigabit, set to benefit more than 350,000 premises across the UK.

Full fibre technology offers more reliable, resilient, and future-proof connectivity with fewer faults, consistent speeds, and the capacity to handle growing data demands.

Kasam Hussain, Openreach partnership director for the North and Midlands, said: "We’re bringing faster, more reliable broadband to some of the most rural properties in the region and letting local people know what to expect.

"This is a major infrastructure upgrade, so there will be more engineering teams, equipment, and vans around town, and we're working hard to keep disruption to a minimum.

"Wherever possible, we’ll use our existing network of ducts and poles to avoid roadworks, new street furniture, and disturbance.

"But there may be places where we need to install new poles, underground ducts, and fibre cables because it’s the only way to make sure households get included in the upgrade."

Mr Hussain also emphasised the company’s commitment to environmental responsibility.

He said: "Openreach is committed to building the best full fibre network, and doing it sustainably is crucial for our business, the communities we serve, and the environment we all share.

"As part of our ‘Let’s Reach Zero’ strategy, we aim to lower our carbon emissions, use less and waste less material, and protect nature wherever we operate."
