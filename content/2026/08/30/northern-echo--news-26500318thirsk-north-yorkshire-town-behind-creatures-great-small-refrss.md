A traditional North Yorkshire market town surrounded by some of the county’s most celebrated countryside continues to attract visitors thanks to its strong connection with one of Britain’s best-loved literary and television stories.

Thirsk, which sits between the North York Moors and the Yorkshire Dales, is closely associated with author and vet Alf Wight, better known by his pen name James Herriot.

His experiences working as a country vet in and around the town inspired the stories that became All Creatures Great and Small, bringing the people, landscapes, and everyday life of rural Yorkshire to readers and television audiences around the world.

  The bridge coming into Thirsk 

*(Image: CHRIS MADDISON/CAMERA CLUB)*

Although the current Channel 5 adaptation is filmed elsewhere, with Grassington used as the fictional Darrowby, Thirsk remains at the heart of the real story behind Herriot’s work.

Wight lived and worked in the town, and his former home and veterinary surgery on Kirkgate is now the World of James Herriot museum.

The attraction lets visitors step back into the world that inspired the books, with recreated consulting rooms, period veterinary equipment, and exhibits linked to both Wight’s life and the television adaptations of his work.

It also includes material connected to the original BBC version of All Creatures Great and Small, which first aired in the 1970s.

For many visitors, though, the appeal of Thirsk stretches well beyond its famous literary connection.

The town has retained a traditional market-town feel, with its historic market square, independent businesses, pubs and older buildings all contributing to its character.


Visit Thirsk has previously described the town as having “a charm of its own”, highlighting the sense of tradition that continues to attract visitors.

Its location also makes it a popular base for exploring the surrounding countryside.

Walkers can enjoy routes along Cod Beck and towards the Hambleton Hills, while the wider area offers easy access to both the North York Moors and Yorkshire Dales.

Another long-established attraction is Thirsk Racecourse, which dates back to 1855 and continues to host regular race meetings and events.

The racecourse has described its setting as offering “idyllic country charm”, adding another element to a town already known for its historic streets and rural surroundings.

But it is the James Herriot connection that continues to give Thirsk an international profile.

The fictional Darrowby may have been a creation, but much of its spirit came from the places, people and experiences Wight encountered while working in North Yorkshire.

That connection means visitors still travel from across the world to Thirsk to see where the story began.

Visit Thirsk has said that people are often struck by the welcome they receive in the town, describing it as a “gem” that continues to impress those who come to explore.

With its literary history, traditional market square, countryside walks and enduring link to All Creatures Great and Small, Thirsk remains one of North Yorkshire’s most distinctive market towns.
