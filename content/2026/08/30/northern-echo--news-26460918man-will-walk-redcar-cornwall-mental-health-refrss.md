A Teesside man is turning his own struggles with depression, anxiety and bipolar disorder into a 500-mile challenge to break mental health stigma.

Joe Chaytor will set off from Redcar on Monday, August 31 and head towards Cornwall - covering around 20 miles a day while raising money for Mental Health Matters.

Joe said mental health is "not an abstract issue" to him, having lived with depression, anxiety and BPD for much of his life.

  Joe Chaytor training for the walk, which will start on August 31. 

*(Image: Joe Chaytor)*

Joe said: "Mental health can affect absolutely anyone, and I know from personal experience how difficult it can be to ask for help or even admit that you're struggling.

"I'm hoping that by putting myself through a challenge that is going to be physically and mentally demanding.

"I can raise money while also encouraging people to talk more openly about mental health."

"There shouldn't be any shame in admitting that you're struggling," he added.

The 500-mile journey will take Joe through towns, villages and countryside as he makes his way from Teesside to Cornwall, carrying his equipment and sleeping in either a tent or B&B accommodation along the way.

But he said the challenge is about far more than the miles.

Joe continued: "I've spent years battling things that people couldn't necessarily see, and I wanted to take that experience and turn it into something positive.

"Most importantly, I'd like people who are struggling themselves to know that they aren't alone.

"If sharing my story encourages even one person to speak to somebody rather than suffering in silence, then the challenge will have been worthwhile."

Joe will document the journey on social media, sharing the highs and lows of life on the road as he makes his way from Redcar to Cornwall.

Donations can be made for Joe's fundraiser through his JustGiving page.
