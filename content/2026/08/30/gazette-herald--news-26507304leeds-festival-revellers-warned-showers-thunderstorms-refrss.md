REVELLERS at one of Yorkshire's largest music festivals will face a mix of sunshine, showers, along with potential hail and thunder today, the Met Office said.

Leeds Festival, taking place at Bramham Park in Wetherby until Monday (August 31), is set to see scattered showers starting in Scotland moving southwards into England, possibly becoming heavy and thundery.

  Grey skies and rain didn't put off these Leeds Festival ticket holders yesterday (Saturday, August 29) 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Visitors to yesterday's festival had to contend with grey skies and rain during performances which built towards headline sets from Charli XCX and Fontaines DC.

  


As reported by The Press, road users in the area should be prepared for some delays until the end of Leeds Festival as the A64 and local roads will be partially closed.

The eastbound A64 is closed from Kiddal Lane to junction 44 of the A1(M) until 2pm on Monday, August 31.

Artists Raye and Dave are set to round off a long weekend of headline performers which have also included Florence and The Machine, Chase & Status, Blossoms and Skepta on The Grid, Leeds Festival's main arena.

  Charli XCX on The Grid stage at Leeds Festival on Saturday 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Monday's weather is expected to be drier and brighter, though some showers may persist, the UK weather agency said.

With many festivalgoers enduring muddy conditions over the weekend, Met Office meteorologist Honor Criswick provided a detailed forecast for Sunday and Monday.

She said: "On Sunday there will be some scattered showers around to start, mostly across Scotland, and Northern Ireland might see some.

"Quite a few areas are going to be relatively dry to start, then it’s going to gradually turn more showery.

"Early afternoon Leeds Festival could see showers, could potentially turn quite heavy and thundery."

  The crowd watch Geese perform on the Gallery stage during the Leeds Festival on Saturday 

*(Image: Annabel Lee-Ellis / PA Wire)*

For the partnering Reading Festival and Notting Hill Carnival in London, the weather is expected to remain mostly dry until the evening, when showers may appear.

These showers, affecting the South East as well, might become heavy and thundery.

Honor Criswick said: "It will probably be quite dry first thing on Monday, some showers across western areas, some heavy showers mostly across north Scotland.

"For many areas it’s going to be turning drier and brighter, a better end to the day compared to the start."

Festival attendees are advised to remain prepared for rain, as showers are still possible.

Monday's temperatures will also peak in the early 20s.

This weather update follows continued hot weather and extremely dry conditions across the UK, with the Met Office saying the UK is on course for its hottest summer on record after five heatwaves.
