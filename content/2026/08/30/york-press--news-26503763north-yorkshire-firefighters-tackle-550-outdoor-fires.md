## Sharp rise in outdoor fires puts pressure on North Yorkshire firefighters

Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, taking part in a Drill night at Boroughbridge Fire Station *(Image: York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority)*

A sharp rise in outdoor fires has put extra pressure on emergency services.

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service attended 850 fires between June and August, with 550 of those in open areas.

This marks a 21 per cent increase compared to last year and a 55 per cent rise from 2024.

Jo Coles, deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime, said: "Our firefighters do an incredible job every day, many of them alongside their substantive jobs.

"North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service has seen a significant rise in the number of emergency call-outs to outdoor fires this summer.

"For on-call teams, that means more call-outs, more flexibility being asked of employers and more time away from their families.

"Thank you to employers and families for the support they give to our firefighting community every day."

The surge comes amid dry conditions and a national increase in wildfire incidents.

Earlier this summer, the mayor and deputy mayor called for a temporary ban - which the government has since introduced - on selling disposable barbecues.

This followed last year’s major wildfire at Langdale/Flyingdales Moor.

Both officials continue to support the ‘Don’t Spark Disaster’ campaign, which warns of the dangers and impacts of wildfires.
