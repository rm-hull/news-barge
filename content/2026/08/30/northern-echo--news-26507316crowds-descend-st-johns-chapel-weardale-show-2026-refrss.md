Crowds descended on St John’s Chapel in County Durham on Saturday as the Weardale Show returned for its 156th year.

The annual agricultural show, also widely known as the Chapel Show, saw visitors make the most of a packed programme despite changeable weather.

  Weardale Show 2026 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS/NORTHERN ECHO)*

Organised by the Weardale Agricultural Society, the event featured traditional livestock classes alongside countryside displays, competitions, children’s activities, food, drink and live entertainment.

Cattle and sheep judging formed a major part of the day, while horses and ponies, a dog show and gundog scurry also drew competitors and spectators.

  Weardale Show 2026 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The programme additionally included dry stone walling, quoits, a fell run and the ever-popular bale stacking competitions.

Elsewhere around the showfield, visitors could browse craft, farmers’ market and countryside tents, see vintage farm tractors and motorbikes, and watch displays from Walworth Birds of Prey and the Go Go Dog Agility team.

  Weardale Show 2026 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS/NORTHERN ECHO)*

Families were also well catered for, with children’s entertainment including an obstacle course, inflatables, face painting and archery.

The day continued into the evening with music from Jason Arnup and Nicola Galvin, bringing another busy Chapel Show to a close.

The Weardale Agricultural Society has confirmed next year’s show will take place on Saturday, August 28, 2027.
