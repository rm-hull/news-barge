A BRADFORD woman has spoken of her narrow escape from deadly flash flooding in Nepal.

Shashi Sharma, a 61-year-old from Bradford, was on the Kailash Mansarovar Yatra pilgrimage, a 10-day spiritual journey from India to Tibet, when disaster struck in Nepal.

More than 790 people have died and thousands more have been injured, according to Nepalese police.

Ms Sharma told BBC Radio Leeds: "The whole group is very, very sad at the moment, and we are praying for the people – those who have lost their life, and we're praying for the families, those who are looking for their loved ones."

Her group of 43 pilgrims, which included six people from the UK, crossed through Rasuwagadhi on the Nepal-Tibet border just one day before the flood hit.

She said: "We're still thinking about the lady who served us food in this beautiful place.

"We had a lovely walk there.

"There was a small Buddhist temple, we went there.

"I still remember everyone there.

"The whole journey is like it happened yesterday.

"And we were all in our groups sharing our videos.

"It is really heartbreaking."

  People stand on remnants of a bridge which spanned across Trishuli river and was washed away in flash floods, in Dhading District, Nepal, Sunday, Aug. 30, 2026. (AP Photo/Niranjan Shrestha) 

*(Image: AP)*

The group completed their pilgrimage safely and are now in China awaiting their return home, though their route has been disrupted.

Ms Sharma said: "We all feel blessed that we are still here.

"It could have been us, but we are really, really praying for the people who have lost their life.

"I have no words to express."

She described the devastation at the border crossing.

She said: "Whole villages and all the bridges, everything, the immigration office, everything is now gone."

Nepal's National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority said 1,924 people are still missing.

Among the missing are 517 foreign nationals, 33 of them British.

15,000 security personnel have been deployed to assist with search and rescue operations.
