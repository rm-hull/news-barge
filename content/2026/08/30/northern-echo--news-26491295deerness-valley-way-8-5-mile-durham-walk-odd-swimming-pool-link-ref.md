Stretching for around 8.5 miles, this North East walk is much more than a countryside route: it follows an old coal railway and has a surprisingly odd link to a Victorian swimming pool.

Deerness Valley Way follows a former railway linking Broompark, near Durham City, with Crook.

The route follows the old railway through the Deerness Valley, passing the likes of Broompark, Ushaw Moor, Deerness View, and Stanley Crook before reaching Crook.

The Deerness Valley Railway opened in 1858 and passenger services began later.

  There are plenty of signs of the former railway line along the route. 

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

As you are following the old line, there is no need to tackle steep terrain, wrestle with muddy fields or work out a complicated route.

It also forms part of the Walney to Wear (W2W) long-distance cycling route, which means it is a useful piece of a much bigger adventure for riders, but it also works perfectly well as a standalone there-and-back walk or ride.

There's plenty to see, too, with woodland stretches at Holburn and Woodhouses Wood, waterside sections alongside the River Deerness and regular views out across the valley.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

Starting from Broompark Picnic Site gives walkers and cyclists a particularly good launch point.

There is accessible parking here, and Broompark is also a key junction in County Durham’s network of old railway paths, with the Deerness Valley Way linking to routes towards Consett, Bishop Auckland and Durham, opening up longer routes - if you fancy it.

On the ridge north of the route stands Ushaw College, once a Roman Catholic seminary.

One of its swimming pools was reportedly filled using water raised hydraulically by the power of the River Deerness itself, but the system was reportedly abandoned after mining pollution turned the water black.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

For anyone who likes a walking or cycling route with regular opportunities to refuel, the trail passes close to villages with shops, cafés and toilets, including Bearpark, Ushaw Moor and Esh Winning, so it is easy to tailor the outing around a coffee stop, a picnic or a pub lunch rather than carrying everything with you.

Near the eastern end, The Duke of Wellington near Bearpark is a useful post-walk pub stop not far off the route, while The New Board Inn in Ushaw Moor is another practical option close to the railway path for a pint or a bite to eat.

Around Esh Winning, The Cross Keys is another village pub that offers a refreshment stop on a longer ride or walk.

These are the sort of places that turn the route into a day out rather than simply an exercise session - particularly if you are tackling the full length from Broompark to Crook and back.

   

*(Image: Gerald Hirst)*

At the western end, Crook offers the most obvious finish-line reward.

The town centre has its own selection of pubs, cafés and food options, as well as car parks for anyone who would rather begin or end their journey there.

The Deerness Valley Way is not trying to be a wild, remote hike.

Its strength is that it offers a proper slice of Durham’s countryside without making the day difficult.

You get the history of the old railway, quiet woodland, riverbank sections and views through the valley, with enough nearby villages to keep the coffee, pub and lunch options within reach.
