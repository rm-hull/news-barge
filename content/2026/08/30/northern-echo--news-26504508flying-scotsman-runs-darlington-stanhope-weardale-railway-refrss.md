One of the world’s most famous steam engines is coming to the North East and for the first time, it will thunder through the Durham Dales.

From November 13 to 15, Flying Scotsman will run a 60-mile return journey from Darlington along the Weardale Railway, with a four-hour round trip including a photo stop in Stanhope.

Two daily departures from Darlington are scheduled at 8.40am and 1.40pm.

Built in 1923, the Scotsman is an LNER Class A1 Pacific steam locomotive.

In 1928 it became the first express service from London King’s Cross to Edinburgh, and reached a recorded speed of 100mph in 1934.

It was retired from regular commercial service in 1963.

The November event is part of a wider return of the engine to the mainline, led by promoter Fox & Edwards Events.

The company is working with Northern Steam, who care for the locomotive, and the National Railway Museum, Flying Scotsman’s custodians.

Fox & Edwards also runs partnerships with 22 different heritage lines.

It will not be the first time the locomotive has been seen in Darlington in recent months.

In December 2025, Flying Scotsman passed through the town twice in one day as part of a UK Railtours excursion to Newcastle.

  The Flying Scotsman, alongside Tornado, at the Titans of Steam Gala at Hopetown, Darlington, last year 

*(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

Earlier in the year, it was a key attraction at the Titans of Steam event at Hopetown Darlington, drawing crowds during the Railway 200 bicentenary celebrations of the Stockton and Darlington Railway.

The engine’s history is deeply tied to the North East.

  The Flying Scotsman leaves Darlington on March 9, 1965, after an overhaul at the North Road works, on a trial run to Newcastle, Sunderland and York 

*(Image: NEWSQUEST)*

It later toured the United States, set a world non-stop steam record in Australia, and in 2004 was bought for the nation after a public campaign raised £2.2m.

Following a £2m refit, it returned to service in 2016 and has since divided its time between mainline tours and static display at the National Railway Museum in York and its sister site, Locomotion in Shildon.

Claire Gibbons, General Manager at Weardale Railway, said: “We’re beyond thrilled to be welcoming the iconic Flying Scotsman to Weardale for the very first time.

“This will be a truly special occasion for our railway, our local communities and the wider region, providing a boost for tourism and the local economy and giving people a fantastic opportunity to experience one of the most famous locomotives in railway history right here in the Durham Dales.
