A bereaved mum who lost her son to a tragic road crash has said there is a "glimmer of hope" to introduce a "vital" driving law in England after Northern Ireland moved ahead with proposals for the changes.

Nicola Bell-Percy, from Jervaulx, North Yorkshire, lost her son, Aaron Bell, 18, in 2022 after he was the passenger of a vehicle travelling on the B6268, between Bedale and Masham, with three friends, when the driver of the vehicle hit a tree.

  Nicola Bell-Percy in Masham 

*(Image: PATRICK GOULDSBROUGH)*

Aaron and two of his friends died at the scene of the incident, while the driver of the vehicle, Joshua Chapman, was jailed after admitting death by dangerous driving and other offences

Since Aaron's devastating road death four years ago, Nicola has campaigned to bring in Graduated Driving Licences (GDLs) across the UK for young people who have just passed their test.

  Aaron Bell 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

“It's bittersweet news that a country near to ours is adopting these laws. It gives us a glimmer of hope that it could one day be brought in England," she said.

"However, it's also tough because we need these changes now, and before we see results of how this has changed young drivers in Northern Ireland, it could be two or three years yet.

"We need politicians brave enough to upset young drivers and to protect them from incidents.

"So far, no one from the government has come up with a justified reason why they are against it; surely protecting our young drivers is a no-brainer."

  Aaron Bell 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

After seeing success in the USA, Canada, and New Zealand in certain forms, GDLs could see measures, including a minimum supervised learning period before a practical test can be taken, limits on the number of passengers, a ban on night driving, and rules when it comes to passenger age.

The licences usually involve a series of stages allowing new drivers to progressively gain more freedom and responsibility on the roads and reduce the risk of accidents and fatalities.

  Nicola Bell-Percy (third right) and the other grieving mothers 

*(Image: Jordan Pettitt/PA)*

From October 1, the GDL's will also be introduced in Northern Ireland.

Nicola has been pushing for the introduction of GDLs, and previously went to Parliament in London to deliver a petition with other bereaved mums, who had lost their children to crashes.

And she has now set out "brand new hopes" of getting GDLs brought into law in England after a change in Prime Minister.

"We hope we can work with Andy Burnham on this issue and hopefully he will take our concerns seriously," she added.

The campaign has previously attracted support from politicians, councillors and road safety organisations, although the UK Government had stopped short of backing the proposals.

  Floral tributes to Aaron and the other two teenagers killed in the crash 

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Last year, councillors in North Yorkshire and the North East publicly supported calls for Graduated Driving Licences, while Thirsk and Malton MP Kevin Hollinrake said ministers should explore measures including GDLs to prevent further tragedies.

Road safety charity Brake also described the system as a potential “literal lifesaver”, while the AA said such measures could save at least 58 lives each year.

  A memorial to the three teenagers killed on the B6268 

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

More recently, County Durham and Darlington Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen is renewing her call for the Government to introduce Graduated Driver Licensing (GDL) across England and Wales.

PCC Allen has been at the forefront of a national campaign to improve the safety of young and newly qualified drivers.

Joy Allen said Northern Ireland’s move to introduce Graduated Driver Licensing was an “evidence-led” decision that would save lives, and questioned why similar protections were not being introduced in England and Wales.

She said newly qualified drivers were particularly vulnerable, with one in five involved in a collision within a year of passing their test, and argued GDL was about helping young motorists gain experience rather than restricting their independence.

However, the Department for Transport said at the time that there were “no plans” to introduce Graduated Driving Licences, despite acknowledging that young people were disproportionately affected by serious and fatal crashes.
