A LONG-RUNNING Bradford restaurant is deciding to look forward to its next chapter rather than the end of the story.

It comes after the owner admitted there was an opportunity to sell the business.

Classic Persian Restaurant, based on Little Horton Lane, has certainly established itself as a core part of the city's food scene.

Arash Imani, the 43-year-old co-owner from Leeds, said: "Classic Persian Restaurant has been part of Bradford since 2015, so we are now celebrating 11 years in the city.

  The Classic Persian restaurant team 

*(Image: T&A)*

"It has been a very important part of our family and our lives.

"Over those 11 years, we have built relationships with many customers who have supported us and come back to the restaurant time and time again."

  Classic Persian restaurant 

*(Image: T&A)*

## Next steps

Mr Imani has joined up with his brother to continue to run the business following the departure of a manager.

He said: "We want to make it completely clear that Classic Persian Restaurant is an independent, family-run business.

"We have only one restaurant in Bradford, and we have no branches or connection with any other restaurant or business.

"Our former manager is no longer involved in Classic Persian, and the restaurant has not been sold.

"There is no new outside management.

"The family is continuing to run the business, and I have joined with my brother as we look towards the future."

  The Classic Persian restaurant team 

*(Image: T&A)*

## What is Persian food?

The restaurant, which serves halal Persian cuisine, has built its reputation on traditional flavours, fresh ingredients, and food cooked to order.

Its menu features a range of Persian kebabs including koobideh and chicken, alongside saffron rice, salads, and classic Persian sides.

Kabab koobideh is a classic Iranian kebab made from ground meat - usually lamb, beef, or a mix of both - seasoned simply with grated onion, salt, and black pepper, then shaped onto flat skewers and grilled over charcoal.

## Hospitality is at the heart of Persian culture

Mr Imani added: "We want people who already know Persian food to feel at home, but also want people trying Persian food for the first time to have a really good experience.

"For us, Persian food is not just about the food itself.

"Hospitality is a very important part of Persian culture, so we want our customers to feel welcome and looked after when they come to Classic Persian."

  The Classic Persian restaurant team 

*(Image: T&A)*

## An exciting next chapter is opening up

Despite briefly considering selling the business, the family ultimately chose to continue running it together.

Mr Imani said: "I have now joined the business with my brother, and we are working together with the family to take Classic Persian into its next chapter.

"We had the opportunity to sell the business, but we decided that we wanted to keep it in the family instead.

"We see this as the next chapter of Classic Persian rather than the end of the story."

  The Classic Persian restaurant team 

*(Image: T&A)*

## But what are the challenges Mr Imani faces?

Mr Imani told the T&A that running an independent restaurant comes with its inherent challenges, from managing staff and suppliers to maintaining consistent standards without the backing of a large company.

Mr Imani explained: "Every decision matters, and you are personally invested in the success of the business.

"But that is also what makes it rewarding.

"When customers come back, recommend us to their friends or tell us that they have enjoyed their meal, it means a lot because we know how much work goes into the restaurant."

## Bradford is a perfect spot for Classic Persian restaurant

Classic Persian’s roots in Bradford have strengthened over the years, and the family is proud to contribute to the city’s diverse food landscape.

Mr Imani said: "Bradford is a very diverse city, and that makes it a great place for a restaurant serving Persian food.

"We are proud to contribute to the city’s food scene and to share a part of Persian culture with people from Bradford and beyond.

"We also think the fact that people in Bradford are willing to try different cuisines is one of the reasons independent restaurants can thrive here."

  The Classic Persian restaurant team 

*(Image: T&A)*

As the restaurant enters its 12th year, the family is focused on building on its established reputation while bringing fresh ideas to the table.

Mr Imani said: "After 11 years, we are proud of the reputation Classic Persian has built in Bradford.

"Our aim now is to continue that legacy, keep providing the food and hospitality our customers know, and bring new energy and ideas into the business.

"We are excited about the future and hope Classic Persian will remain part of Bradford’s food scene for many more years."
