# Man attacked with bladed weapon two miles from fatal A66 crash scene

## A 39-year-old man is receiving treatment after being attacked with a bladed weapon in Eston, around two miles from the scene of the A66 crash which killed seven people.

Police were called to an address on Sedgemoor Road in Eston at around midday on Thursday (Aug 27) following reports of an assault involving a bladed weapon.

Cleveland Police said the 39-year-old man is receiving treatment following the attack.

No arrests have been made and enquiries into the incident are ongoing.

Police have not ruled out a link between the assault and the fatal A66 crash on Saturday (Aug 22), which claimed the lives of two police officers and five young men.

The latest incident comes amid a series of serious incidents in the area which police are investigating.

A resident living near the scene of the Eston attack said: “A bloke got stabbed and the street was full of armed police, the helicopter came, the lot.”

Referring to the TS6 postcode area, which includes Eston, Grangetown and South Bank, he added: “TS6 has gone mad, it’s brutal.

“We are just trying to keep our heads down.”

On Saturday, PCs Matthew Blades, 37, and Tom Clough, 38, died when their marked police vehicle was involved in a head-on crash with a Volkswagen Passat on the A66.

The five occupants of the Passat – Cole Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, Michael Robert Cahill, 23, and Jakub Matusiak, 23, who was named by police as Jacob – were also pronounced dead at the scene.

Inquests into all seven deaths were opened at Teesside Coroners’ Court on Thursday.

Coroner Paul Appleton said it was understood there had been a police pursuit of the Passat before it entered the A66 against the flow of traffic.

The pursuit was then aborted.

Mr Appleton said: “A short time after… the Volkswagen Passat collided with a police vehicle, which had not been directly involved in the prior pursuit.”

Police have arrested 17 people following the crash, with two charged and the others bailed, as part of investigations into alleged organised crime.

The Passat had been pursued following an earlier incident in which a house in the Acklam area of Middlesbrough, linked to Worthy, was rammed by a vehicle.

Police have previously said the five young men in the Passat may have had links to “serious and organised criminal activity” and that four had previous convictions.

Further tragedy struck on Wednesday (Aug 26), when 34-year-old Natalie McDonald and seven-year-old Valentina Foster died following a house fire in Birchington Avenue, Grangetown, around a mile from the A66 crash scene.

Four other people were injured and two men have been arrested on suspicion of murder.

Cleveland Police is investigating whether there is any connection between the A66 crash and the fire.

Ms McDonald's brother, Josh Kirton, said his sister was not associated with gangs or the A66 crash.
