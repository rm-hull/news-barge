# Yorkshire Water told by National Drought Group to do 'everything to minimise leaks'

## The National Drought Group - formed of the Government, Met Office, water companies and other stakeholders - met again today to discuss the situation.

Yorkshire Water has been told by the Government’s National Drought Group “to take every available step to minimise leakage” as water resources continue to remain scarce.

Despite thunderstorms rolling across the region earlier today, the UK has seen one of the driest summers on record due to the hottest July in history.

Some parts of Yorkshire had less than one millimetre of rain that month, increasing pressure on water resources.

The National Drought Group - formed of the Government, Met Office, water companies and other stakeholders - met again today to discuss the situation.

Yorkshire Water, alongside other firms, was told to continue to work hard to reduce leaks, which increase in hot weather as pipes move in dry soil.

The meeting was chaired by Water Minister and Hull West and Haltemprice MP Emma Hardy, who said: “We know this summer's hot and dry weather has been challenging, and the recent rainfall, while welcome, is nowhere near enough to end the current drought.

“Our climate has changed and we will continue to take all action necessary to support communities facing these pressures of both drought and flash flooding.

"As we approach autumn, we expect water companies to take every available step to minimise leakage, follow their drought plans, and reduce consumption to help avoid any further restrictions.”

Yorkshire Water has made significant strides in recent years cracking down on leaks, however the hot weather has caused issues in recent months.

The small North Yorkshire village of Marton-cum-Grafton has been beset by supply issues over the last two months, which has seen shops and pubs closed.

Families have even been forced to take their children to wash at the local swimming pool.

Meanwhile, in the Kirkgate area of Leeds recently residents were left without water for over 24 hours.

![The leak in Marton-cum-Grafton. Credit: North Yorkshire Greens](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/07/29/17/55/WhatsApp-Image-2026-07-29-at-15-35-31.jpeg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)



This was due to a burst main, likely as a result of a lack of soil moisture because of the sweltering weather.

At the National Drought Group meeting, Environment Agency chief executive Philip Duffy also warned of the threat of flooding with rainfall on the parched ground.

He said: “A few days or even weeks of wetter weather cannot reverse the impacts of several months of exceptionally dry conditions.

“Today’s yellow thunderstorm warnings show it’s possible to experience simultaneous drought and flood as heavy rain falls on compacted soils.

“Drought conditions will continue to worsen until we receive sustained rainfall across the country and we still all have a role to play in conserving precious water supplies.

“We ask people to carry on following the restrictions, where in place, and continue to save water such as by turning off taps when brushing teeth and using an eco-setting on household appliances.

“Small steps will leave more in the environment for our wildlife, our local rivers, and our farmers who are still struggling with the drought impacts.”

While Yorkshire Water has not imposed a hosepipe ban like many other water companies, it has still urged customers to try and use watering cans and preserve water where they can.

Debbie Feldhaus, head of strategic water resources and regional planning at Yorkshire Water, said: “Small changes can make a big difference.

“Whether it’s using a watering can instead of a hosepipe, taking shorter showers or only putting the washing machine and dishwasher on when they’re full, every drop saved helps us protect our water resources now and for the future.”

A Yorkshire Water spokesperson added: “The prolonged warm weather and little or no rain exacerbates leakage as the ground dries out, shrinks, moves and pipes can split, leak and burst and we have dedicated even more resource to reducing leaks as we look to protect our resources.

“We’ve recruited 100 new leakage inspectors, increasing their number to a 400-person strong team and we’re also working on a £406m mains replacement scheme to renew over 1,000km of robust and reliable pipework across the region to make burst, leaks and supply interruptions less likely. As of this week, we had replaced 275km of mains.

“On average we fix 314 leaks per week on our network. We have a £38m leakage reduction plan over the next five years to improve water conservation and efficiency.

“We’ve hit our target to reduce leakage by 15 per cent between 2020 and 2025, but we are already looking beyond that and want to reduce by a further 12.7 per cent by 2030.”
