The County Durham PCC has backed Government calls for social media companies to remove videos showing dangerous driving, following the fatal A66 crash.

County Durham and Darlington Police and Crime Commissioner (PCC) Joy Allen has renewed her call for tougher action against drivers who film and share reckless behaviour online.

It comes as pressure mounts over a series of devastating incidents across Teesside.

  County Durham and Darlington police and crime commissioner Joy Allen 

*(Image: PROVIDED)*

Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey described events in and around Middlesbrough over the last week as "deeply worrying" and called for action against organised criminals.

Former Middlesbrough mayor Andy Preston also warned of the impact of the incidents, describing the situation as "heartbreaking to see – not just the loss of life but the further scarring of once proud communities".

Joy Allen said dangerous driving content should not be rewarded with online attention.

She said: "No innocent person should lose their life because someone chose to drive dangerously in pursuit of a few likes, views or moments of online attention.

"I have campaigned tirelessly on behalf of the families of Karlene Warner and eight-month-old Zackary Blades, who were killed when Darryl Anderson got behind the wheel while nearly three times legal alcohol limit. Anderson slammed into the back of their Peugeot while taking a picture of himself driving at more than double the 70mph speed limit.

  Eight-month-old Zackary Blades and 30-year-old Karlene Warner. 

*(Image: Picture: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

"I have also highlighted the tragic case of Abbie Devaney, who was killed on her 18th birthday by a dangerous driver who had previously shared multiple videos of himself speeding, carrying out reckless overtakes and narrowly avoiding head-on collisions."

Mrs Allen also highlighted videos showing drivers using nitrous oxide behind the wheel.

She said: "Having successfully campaigned for the possession of nitrous oxide to be outlawed, I am now urging members of the public to report evidence of its use in our communities to help keep Durham's roads safe.

"It is as stupid as it is illegal for drivers to film and share videos of themselves inhaling balloons filled with nitrous oxide while behind the wheel."

She is calling for platforms to remove content which promotes or glorifies dangerous, impaired or unlawful driving.

Mrs Allen said: "If those who engage in this reckless behaviour refuse to see sense, then the incentive must be removed.

"Social media should not provide a platform for dangerous driving to be glorified, promoted or celebrated.

"Social media companies have a vital role to play.

"Dangerous driving is not entertainment, and algorithms should not reward reckless behaviour with greater visibility and reach.

"Platforms should be required to remove content that promotes or glorifies dangerous, impaired or unlawful driving and thereby puts lives at risk."

She also warned drivers that their own social media posts could be used against them.

She said: "I also want dangerous drivers to understand that the videos, messages and posts they share online can become evidence.

"What is uploaded for attention today could be used to secure a conviction tomorrow, leading to the loss of liberty for many years."

Her comments come as the Prime Minister said the Government was providing support to authorities following the recent events in and around Middlesbrough.

  Prime Minister Andy Burnham during a visit to homelessness charity Changing Lives in Newcastle 

*(Image: Scott Heppell/PA Wire)*

Asked about the incidents, the Prime Minister said: "Obviously we're offering all of the support that the authorities need.

"The important thing, it's a terribly tragic event that's happened with the loss of a young woman and her seven-year-old niece."

He said a full investigation was under way and warned it was too early to conclude whether the incidents were connected.

He added: "We do need to understand the extent to which they may be connected, but they may not be. So I think it's too early to jump to conclusions."

Cleveland's Labour MPs have also met with the Government's Policing and Crime Minister to seek further support for Cleveland Police and Cleveland Fire Brigade.

  MPs Jonathan Brash, Luke Myer and Andy McDonald 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

MPs Jonathan Brash, Luke Myer and Andy McDonald said emergency services deserved the "strongest possible backing from Government".

"They said the pressures facing Cleveland's emergency services had "built up over many years and were made significantly worse by the savage cuts during the austerity period".

  Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey said Reform is making a "complete mess" of Durham County 

*(Image: Lucy North/PA Wire)*

Sir Ed said: "There's clearly organised criminals there who need to be cracked down on, and the tragic death of those young policemen I think has really touched the country, obviously the community up in Middlesbrough.

"We need to really crack down on these criminals and harm."

Mr Preston said: "More incidents are happening and it looks like they're related.

"Everyone's praying that this peters out. If it doesn't more innocent lives could be at risk.

"Cleveland Police is a troubled force but it has first-class men and women on the ground.

"Policing everywhere became too full of empathy and tolerance – Middlesbrough needs solid and unflinching intolerance of crime. Not just this week but always."

  Andy Preston.


Mrs Allen said decisive action was needed to stop dangerous driving content being shared.

She said: "We owe it to all those who have lost their lives, and to the emergency service personnel who respond to these devastating incidents, to take decisive action.

"That means tackling this dangerous trend and removing opportunities for those involved to publish such content on social media platforms.

"I sincerely hope social media companies act swiftly and effectively to prevent such content from being published in the first place and to remove any videos that are already online.

"I am also pleased that, where appropriate, the Government intends to use powers available through the Online Safety Act to require platforms to take down this content.

"This is quite literally a life-saving measure. I have often said that, in the wrong hands, a vehicle can become a lethal weapon. Never has that been more apparent."
