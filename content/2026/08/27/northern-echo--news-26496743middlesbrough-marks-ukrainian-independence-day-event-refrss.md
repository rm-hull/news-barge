Supporters have gathered in a North East town to show solidarity with Ukraine as the country marked 35 years of independence while continuing to defend itself against Russia's ongoing invasion.

About 100 people attended a Ukrainian Independence Day event in Centre Square, Middlesbrough, on Monday, August 24.

Members of the Ukrainian community joined together for the occasion, which was organised by Middlesbrough councillor Luke Henman in co-ordination with the social inclusion charity Investing in People and Culture.

  Petro Rewko MBE, chair of the Association of Ukrainians in Great Britain 

*(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

Luke Henman, executive member for children's services at Middlesbrough Council, said: "I’m proud to stand by Ukraine and show solidarity with our Ukrainian community – their security and democracy is our security and democracy.

"Yesterday was the 35th Ukrainian Independence Day, and after four years of war, celebrating the country’s freedom and democracy and self-determination is more important than ever.

"What was meant to be over in days has turned into a grinding conflict with the Ukrainian armed forces and civilians standing firm against aggression and appalling brutality.

"This illegal invasion doesn’t just impact Ukrainians – it poses a serious threat to the UK’s prosperity and security too, and that’s why it’s vital that we continue to stand with Ukraine and show our support."

The event featured speeches from Petro Rewko MBE, chair of the Association of Ukrainians in Great Britain, and Middlesbrough South and East Cleveland MP Luke Myer.

Ukrainian children performed a song as part of the programme, and the Ukrainian flag was raised above Middlesbrough Town Hall while the national anthem played.
