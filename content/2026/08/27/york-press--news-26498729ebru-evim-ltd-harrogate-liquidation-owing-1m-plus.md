A well-known Harrogate pilates company has gone into liquidation with debts of more than £1 million.

Ebru Evim Ltd was incorporated in 2019 and opened a studio in James Street in 2022, where Laura Ashley used to be.

The business covered three storeys and offered pilates, hot yoga and other classes, earning rave reviews on social media, with google awarding 4.6 stars out of five, based on 54 Google reviews.

Its owner and sole director is listed on the Companies House website as Ebru Evim Roberts.

However, the 46-year-old closed the studio in April blaming high costs for premises, according to Harrogate-based media.

Ellis Breese of Leeds were appointed as liquidators on June 30 and a report known as a Statement of Affairs details the debts that Ebru Evim Ltd had run up.

The report says an estimated £1,458.34 was available to preferential creditors - those who are paid first.

The company also owed HMRC £112,252.01, giving a total deficiency of £110,793.63 as regards to preferential creditors.

Furthermore, Ebru Evim Ltd also had a ‘floating charge’ debt of £28,427.03, giving a total estimated deficiency of £139,220.70. A floating charge is a security interest or lien that a lender holds over a company's fluctuating or changing pool of assets, such as inventory or accounts receivable.

The Statement of Affairs says there were unsecured creditors – those who are paid last- owed £937,503.61.

These included fixed charge deficits (where borrowing is secured on a specific asset or assets) of £47,661.95, 15 trade and expense creditors owed £898,016,60, an employee owed £6,696.81, a bank/ institution owed £14,141.00 and HMRC owed a further £6,987.

Altogether, these unsecured creditors gave an estimated deficiency or shortfall of £1,084,297.18. With issued and called up capital of £201.30, this gave a total deficiency or shortfall of £1,084,498.48.

A related list of company creditors reveals 24 different companies or organisations owed a total £1,120,142.50.

The largest debt listed was of £585, 801.91 owed to Guernsey Investments Ltd, a Harrogate-based trading company that provides asset-backed loans.

The second-largest debt is of nearly £257,962.66, which is owed to Anthony and Vanessa Roberts, who own the Kilnsey Park Estate near Skipton.

HMRC is owed almost £120,000 in PAYE, VAT and corporation tax.

Further debts include Business Finance Cumbria owed £28,427.03, Aldermore Bank £16,715.71 and Barclays Bank owed 14,141.00.

After she closed the James Street studio, Ms Evrim in July launched a new venture, the Evrimotion pilates academy. It offers teacher training and studio space at its venue at Leeds Dock.

She recently posted on Facebook: “From September, we’ll be offering 33 classes a week, including Reformer Pilates, Apparatus Pilates, Mat Pilates, Barre and Yoga.”
