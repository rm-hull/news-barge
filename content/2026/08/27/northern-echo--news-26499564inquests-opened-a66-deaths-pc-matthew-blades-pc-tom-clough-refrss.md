Two police officers killed in the devastating A66 crash over the weekend died from head and neck injuries, a coroner has confirmed.

PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Thomas Clough, 38, died when their marked Cleveland Police vehicle was involved in a collision with a Volkswagen Passat on the A66 at South Bank, near Middlesbrough, at around 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

Inquests into the deaths of both officers were opened at Teesside Magistrates' Court this morning (August 27).

Area Coroner for Teesside and Hartlepool Paul Appleton confirmed brief details of the officers and the incident.

  PC Matthew Blades (left) and PC Tom Clough (right) 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE/CARMEL COLLEGE)*

Mr Appleton told the hearing that PC Blades, born in Stockton, and living in Hartlepool, and PC Clough, born in Loughborough and living in Marske, were pronounced dead at the scene, along with five other people.

He added: "In the early hours of Saturday, September 22, there was a fatal road traffic collision on A66 - involved was one police vehicle - PC Matthew Blades and PC Thomas were inside - and a VW Passat".

"There was a police pursuit; the Passat entered onto the carriageway, the pursuit was aborted, and then the Passat collided with the police vehicle - that police vehicle had not been involved in a prior pursuit. "

The provisional cause of death given to both men was head and neck injuries.

Mr Appleton said that the inquest opening had concluded and a full inquest would be held after a final post-mortem report was completed, an IOPC report was concluded, and a police force report was concluded.

As previously reported by The Northern Echo, PC Blades and PC Clough were travelling in a marked police vehicle on the correct side of the carriageway when it collided with the Passat, which Cleveland Police has said was travelling in the wrong direction.

Five people inside the Passat also died in the collision.

They have been named as Michael Robert Cahill, 23, Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jacob Matusiak, 23, and Makai Saddington, 18.

  PC Tom Clough 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

The deaths of PC Blades and PC Clough have prompted an outpouring of grief across Teesside, with both officers remembered by their families as heroes.

PC Blades' wife described him as her "beloved husband", "best friend" and a devoted father to their two young sons.

She said: "No words will ever feel like the right words to honour our Matt - my beloved husband, my best friend, and the most devoted father to our two young boys.

"He gave his life in the line of duty, protecting and serving others with courage, dedication and selflessness.

"The pain of losing him is beyond words, and our lives will never be the same. Neither will the love he gave us ever fade."

She added: "Forever loved. Forever missed. Forever our superhero. Our Matt and Dad."

Before joining Cleveland Police, PC Blades worked as an English teacher at Carmel College in Darlington.

The school described him as an "intelligent, kind and approachable teacher" who was a much-loved and highly valued member of staff.

  PC Blades 

*(Image: CARMEL COLLEGE)*

PC Clough's family also paid tribute to him, describing him as "our hero with a fantastic sense of humour".

They said: "Jumped into action whenever needed, and willing to help anyone for any reason.

"Proudly fought for his country to make the world a better place. Always on public duty throughout his short life."

They also thanked friends, colleagues and the wider community for the "overwhelming support and condolences" they had received.

Cleveland Police Chief Constable Victoria Fuller previously described PC Blades and PC Clough as "brave officers".

She said: "These were brave officers protecting our communities and serving our residents who didn't return home to their families at the end of their shift."

Flowers, balloons and handwritten messages have been left close to the scene of the collision as communities across Teesside remember all seven people who died.

A fundraiser established by Cleveland Police Federation to support the families of PC Blades and PC Clough has also attracted over £1m in donations. The money raised will be divided equally between the two families.

Investigations into the circumstances surrounding the collision remain ongoing.
