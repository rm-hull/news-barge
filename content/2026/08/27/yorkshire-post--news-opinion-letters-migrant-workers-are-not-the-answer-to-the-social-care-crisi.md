# Migrant workers are not the answer to the social care crisis - Yorkshire Post Letters

## From: John Riseley, Harrogate.

Who does Unison General Secretary Andrea Egan (The Yorkshire Post front page, August 20) think she is supposed to be representing? She tells us “Terrible pay and working conditions are creating shortages in the social care sector”.

So, is she advocating here an improvement in pay and working conditions for that sector? Bizarrely, no. She is calling for the rate of migrant worker arrivals to be restored to its recent very high level, making such improvements unnecessary.

How is this to be achieved? By bribing migrants with a renewed right to early citizenship and to bring in many dependents. If such bribery is needed, why are others willing to pay so much to come by small boat?

![A care home resident holds hands with her daughter. PIC: Andrew Matthews/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmNkYWUxNTliLTZkZmUtNDg5OS04NTdlLTFlYjBjNzE5NmQ5MzoxMTIxMzc3OC1jZWY4LTQ4YWYtYTRhNi0zNjBmYjA4MGYyYzk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



She refers to a “massive drop in overseas workers” when the relevant figures indicate only a drop in the annual number of workers arriving. This could only correspond with a massive drop in the number working if previous workers were leaving the sector at a rapid rate. In that case, migration would hardly be an effective solution to the problem.

Some might argue that the improved pay and conditions needed are unaffordable, though that is hardly a line one would expect a union leader to take. But the problem is one of the attractiveness, or unattractiveness, of care work relative to other employment.

Better pay for care workers could be funded by increased taxation upon a broader group which includes those we would wish to coax into care work. These can hardly complain they are already at rock bottom when they have clearly, until now, been better off than care workers.
