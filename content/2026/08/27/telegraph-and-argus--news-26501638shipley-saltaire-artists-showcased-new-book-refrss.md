THE work of Shipley artists from the past - whose art ranges from a military memorial to a set of miniatures - is the focus of a new book.

Past Shipley Artists describes and illustrates the lives and artwork of 18 artists from the Shipley and Saltaire areas, including sculptors, painters, designers, illustrators and art teachers.

The author, Colin Neville, who curates the Not Just Hockney website, said: “Some very talented artists have emerged from the Shipley area over the years, and it’s been fascinating to unearth their life stories and examples of the work.

“The Shipley-born sculptor Joseph Cawthra, for example, who died in 1971, is not a familiar name to most people but his sculptures and memorial stonework can be found all over the country. These include the Burns statue on his mausoleum at Dumfries cemetery, a group of four figures at Leeds Civic Hall and the statue of Britannia, at Piccadilly Circus, London. “However, Joseph considered the airman on the war memorial, unveiled in 1922 at Bootle, Lancashire, as one of his best works - and the depiction of a World War One airman on a war memorial was uncommon at that time.

“A number of women artists featured in the book did not always fulfil their full potential, or did so only later in life.

  A charming set of miniatures by May Stead.


The painter, Margaret Firth, from Saltaire, for example, found that her art career was curtailed by the caring responsibilities she had for her ailing parents. It was only in her latter years that she could establish herself as an artist. “Artist May Stead (nee Greening ) also found that her child-rearing responsibilities restricted and limited the range of art work she could undertake.

“Other interesting characters in the book include William Rushton, born at Windhill in 1860, who left school aged nine, worked in a local mill, became a poet, then a travelling actor, before establishing himself a talented painter of landscapes. Given that he was most likely self-taught as an artist, his success as an artist was quite remarkable.

“The art of Jack Coulthard, who died in 2016, defies easy generalisation and I never tire of looking at it - his paintings are full of riddles, striking forms, and glorious colours.

  Stone Flowers for Rosa by Jack Coulthard. Middlesbrough Institute of Modern Art. 

*(Image: Middlesbrough Institute of Art)*

“Another talented artist is Govinder Nazran, who gained a following for his quirky and abstract paintings and sculptures of animals, particularly cats and dogs. In 2004 Govinder became the Best Selling Published Artist at the Fine Art Trade Guild Awards and he enjoyed two sell-out tours in Japan, where his work was revered. Unfortunately he died in 2008, aged just 44, after a seizure and a fall at his Saltaire home.

  Cat Club by Govinder Nazran, one of his quirky animal sculptures.


“A chapter is also included on the history of the Saltaire School of Art, which opened in 1870 and quickly built a reputation for the excellence of its teaching, particularly under the leadership of Edward Renard and Fred Stead - both inspirational teachers, as well as very successful artists.

  Colin's book profiles a range of artists from the Shipley area. 

*(Image: Colin Neville)*

“The first art school was located, along with a science department, in The Saltaire Institute on Victoria Road.

“And although the art classes were popular from the start, the teaching area soon began to draw criticism for its lack of space and poor lighting.

“So in 1885 work started on a new purpose-built School of Science and Art on Exhibition Road. This was an initiative linked to a proposed Queen Victoria’s Golden Jubilee in 1887, and the new School was opened by Princess Beatrice in May 1887.

“There was now more space for the art students, including an area for modelling in clay and stone, an exhibition area, and studios and classrooms with much improved lighting.”

l Past Shipley Artists, priced £8, including postage, includes more than 80 mainly colour illustrations.
