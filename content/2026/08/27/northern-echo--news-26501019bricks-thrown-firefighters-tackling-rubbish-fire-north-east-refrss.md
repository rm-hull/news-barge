Firefighters were attacked with bricks and other items in an "unprovoked attack" while tackling a rubbish fire yesterday.

A Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue crew was targeted in the Blakelaw area of Newcastle at around 9.41pm on Wednesday, August 26, while responding to a rubbish fire.

The service has described the incident as an "unprovoked attack" and "unacceptable and dangerous assault".

No one was injured, but a fire engine was badly damaged and had to be taken off the road temporarily, the service said.

   

*(Image: Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service)*

Chief Fire Officer Stewart Nicholson said: "An attack on our firefighters is an attack on the communities we serve.

"Our crews responded to what was reported as a rubbish fire with the sole intention of protecting people and keeping our community safe.

"Instead, they were subjected to an unacceptable and dangerous assault where bricks were thrown at them and their appliance."

The loss of the fire engine reduced the service’s emergency response capacity and placed additional pressure on neighbouring crews.

Mr Nicholson said: "While I am relieved that no one was injured, the impact of these incidents cannot be underestimated.

"The damage caused meant a fire appliance had to be taken off the road, leaving us with one less resource available to respond to emergencies.

"This can result in delays to attending other incidents where people may be in urgent need of our help."

   

*(Image: Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service)*

He added: "Our firefighters are ordinary people doing an extraordinary job.

"They are mothers, fathers, brothers, sisters, sons and daughters who should never face violence simply for carrying out their duties."

He urged anyone with information to come forward.

Mr Nicholson said: "Anyone with information about this incident should come forward.

"Attacks on emergency service workers are completely unacceptable, and those responsible must be held accountable."

He also encouraged residents to help prevent similar incidents by securing wheelie bins and combustible materials that could be used in anti-social behaviour.

Residents are urged to report any fires, anti-social behaviour, or safety concerns in their area as soon as possible to help keep communities safe.

Information about the incident, or any related activity, can be reported anonymously through FireStoppers by calling 0800 169 5558.

Collaborative efforts between the community and emergency services are seen as vital to reducing these types of incidents and ensuring the safety of both the public and frontline workers.
