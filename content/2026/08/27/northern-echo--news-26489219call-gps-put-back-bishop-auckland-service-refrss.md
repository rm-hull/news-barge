A County Durham health boss has called for GPs to return to a service based at Bishop Auckland Hospital after funding cuts led to changes in how it is staffed.

Dr Stewart Findlay, chief executive of Durham Dales Health Federation (DDHF), says GPs have been replaced by Advanced Nurse Practitioners in the Federation’s extended access service at the hospital.

However, this service is separate from Bishop Auckland Hospital’s 24-hour urgent treatment centre, which is run by County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust and has not been cut.

The Federation’s extended access service provides extra GP appointments on top of those available at local practices. It is an appointment-only service rather than a walk-in department.

  Dr Stewart Findlay, chief executive of Durham Dales Health Federation (DDHF) 

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Dr Findlay said GP federations across County Durham had seen funding for services cut by 15 per cent in 2026/27.

He said DDHF would be willing to bring GPs back into the Bishop Auckland service if funding was restored.

Dr Findlay said: “As a GP Federation, DDHF we would be happy to re-provide and even extend a doctor (GP) led urgent care service in BAH as long as funding was made available by the ICB.”

He said Advanced Nurse Practitioners provide a valuable service but believes having GPs physically based at the hospital gives patients access to a wider range of care.

He added: “Our ANPs are excellent but can not offer the range of services that GPs can and the service to patients will very likely not be as good as it has been to date.”

Dr Findlay previously raised his concerns with then Health Secretary Wes Streeting, MPs and NHS leaders in an attempt to have the cuts reversed.

  Bishop Auckland Hospital 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

He said: “The severity of the austerity measures being implemented across County Durham is extreme and one that we have not ever witnessed before in the NHS. Certainly not in the last 42 years.”

But health organisations have stressed that patients can still access urgent treatment at Bishop Auckland Hospital around the clock.

The hospital’s urgent care provision is run by County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust.

From 8am to 8pm, it operates as a minor injuries service. From 8pm to 8am on weekdays it operates as an urgent treatment centre for minor injuries and illnesses, while the urgent treatment centre runs 24 hours a day at weekends and on bank holidays.

The trust says it has made no changes to its own GP cover.

Dr Eliot Sykes, chief medical officer at the trust, said: “There continues to be a full 24/7 urgent care service at Bishop Auckland General Hospital, and we have made no changes to our GP cover.”

  Bishop Auckland Hospital 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

He said GPs work in the trust’s urgent care service from 8pm to 8am each day, and from 1pm to 8am at weekends and on bank holidays.

Dr Sykes added: “This has been the case since 2015 and following our workforce review, we have no immediate plans to change this.”

Outside those hours, the separate Durham Dales Health Federation extended access service operates from the hospital.

The trust said GPs in that service are now working remotely and are available to provide advice.

The North East and North Cumbria Integrated Care Board (ICB), which commissions services, said savings had been required across the NHS to keep services financially sustainable.

It said the federation’s extended access service was now “led by highly trained nurses, and overseen by GPs”.

An ICB spokesperson added: “The urgent treatment centre run by County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust is unchanged.”

The ICB said patients still have access to GP practices, pharmacies, NHS 111, the extended access service and the urgent treatment centre.

## What has changed at Bishop Auckland Hospital?

There are two separate services operating from Bishop Auckland Hospital, and the changes only affect one of them.

  **Durham Dales Health Federation’s** extended access service provides extra, pre-booked GP appointments on top of those available at local surgeries. Following a 15 per cent funding cut, the service is now led by Advanced Nurse Practitioners, with GPs working remotely and available for advice rather than being based on site.

  The hospital’s urgent care service has not been cut. It is run separately by **County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust** and remains available 24 hours a day.

It operates as a minor injuries service from 8am to 8pm on weekdays, with urgent treatment available overnight. At weekends and on bank holidays, urgent treatment is available around the clock.

The Trust says its own GP cover has not changed, with doctors working overnight and for longer hours at weekends and on bank holidays.
