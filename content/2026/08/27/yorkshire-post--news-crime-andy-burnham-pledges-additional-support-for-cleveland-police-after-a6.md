# Andy Burnham pledges additional support for Cleveland Police after A66 crash and fatal fire

## Five Teesside MPs met with Policing Minister Sarah Jones to say: “Our communities deserve to feel safe, and our emergency services deserve the resources and support they need to do their jobs."

Andy Burnham has said Teesside emergency services will get “all the support that the authorities need”, after nine people have died in recent days in two deadly incidents.

A seven-year-old girl and her aunt died in a house fire yesterday less than a mile from the site of a crash on the A66 which killed seven people.

In the early hours of Saturday, a car with five young men crashed into two police officers while travelling the wrong way down the dual carriageway near South Bank.

All seven people were pronounced dead at the scene, including Pc Matthew Blades, 37, and Pc Tom Clough, 38.

Cleveland Police would not rule out linking the two incidents, and is now investigating a car ramming a property in Steele Crescent, South Bank, in the early hours of this morning, just minutes away from the two fatal scenes.

Local policing Supt Emily Harrison said: “Our investigation is in its early stages and it has not yet been confirmed whether this incident is linked to wider organised crime.”

Redcar Labour MP Anna Turley said in a statement: “My deepest condolences are with the families, friends and loved ones of those who have died, and my thoughts are also with those injured and receiving treatment.

“Our community has faced a profound amount of heartbreak over the past few weeks.

“Please keep everyone affected in your thoughts, give the emergency services the space they need, and avoid speculation as Cleveland Police and emergency services continue their investigation.”

Five Teesside Labour MPs, including Ms Turley who is also a Home Office minister, called for greater support for emergency services following the incidents.

Speaking on a visit to South Wales today, Mr Burnham said that the Government is offering “all of the support that the authorities need” after the “terribly tragic” house fire.

The Prime Minister said: “Utterly devastating, and I’m sure there’s a whole community that is grieving, not just what happened at the weekend, and particularly we should stress our deepest respect for the police officers lost in the line of duty and sending our sympathies to their families.

“And we will obviously make sure that the support is there for them going forward.

“The only thing I can say this morning is there is an investigation underway. It will be the fullest possible investigation. The authorities will have all of the support that they need.

“We do need to understand the extent to which they may be connected, but they may not be. So I think it’s too early to jump to conclusions.

“But what we can say at this point in time is that all of the support will be provided to get to the heart of what has happened here.”

Ms Turley arranged a meeting between herself and four other Teesside MPs - Luke Myer, Andy McDonald, Jonathan Brash and Chris McDonald - and Policing Minister Sarah Jones yesterday.

In a joint statement, the five MPs said: "Our brave emergency services deserve the utmost support, not only in responding to the immediate situation, but in tackling the wider community safety concerns that people across our area have felt for far too long.”

Earlier in the month, there were further incidents of vehicles ramming into houses in Middlesbrough.

The Labour MPs added: “These pressures did not emerge overnight; they have built up over many years and were made significantly worse by the savage cuts during the austerity period.

“Our communities deserve to feel safe, and our emergency services deserve the resources and support they need to do their jobs."

They welcomed the support Cleveland Police has received from other forces during the current situation, alongside the new PCSOs who have taken up post over the past year.

But the MPs warned that a return to "business as usual" would not be enough, and said they would continue to press the Government for the strongest possible support for the region’s emergency services.

It comes amid the outpouring of public support for the families of Pc Blades and Pc Clough has now raised more than £1m.
