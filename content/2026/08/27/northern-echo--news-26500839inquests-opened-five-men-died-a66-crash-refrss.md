Five young men who died after driving the wrong way up the A66 and smashing into a police car died of head, neck and body injuries, inquests heard.

Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, Michael Robert Cahill, 23, and Jacob Matusiak, 23, all died after the Volkswagen Passat they were travelling in crashed with a marked Cleveland Police vehicle.

The crash happened on the A66 at South Bank, near Middlesbrough, at around 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

Inquests into the deaths of all five young men were opened at Teesside Coroner's Court on Thursday (August 27) by area coroner for Teesside and Hartlepool, Paul Appleton.

The provisional cause of death for Jacob Matusiak, from South Bank, was given as neck injuries, while the provisional cause of death of Michael Cahill, from Peterlee, was given as head and torso injuries.

Mr Appleton also gave the provisional causes of death for Makai Saddington, from Middlesbrough, as blunt head injury; Cole Worthy, from Middlesbrough, as head and chest injuries; and Theo Rae, from Middlesbrough, as blunt head injury.

Mr Appleton said: "In the early hours of August 22, there was a fatal collision on the A66 carriageway at South Bank. Involved in the collision was a police vehicle with the occupants, PC Matthew Blades and PC Thomas Clough, and a VW Passat carrying the following occupants - Cole Worthy, Theo Rae, Makai Saddington, Michael Cahill and Jacob Matusiak.

"All occupants of the vehicles were sadly pronounced deceased at the scene. The circumstances known at this time was that the VW Passat entered the A66 against the flow of traffic while a police pursuit was aborted.

"A short time after the pursuit was aborted, the VW Passat then collided with the police vehicle that wasn't previously involved in the pursuit."

All bar Cahill had previous criminal convictions.

Mr Appleton said full inquests would be held after a post-mortem, IOPC and police investigation had concluded.

Cleveland Police officers PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38, who were travelling in the marked police vehicle, also died in the collision. Inquests into their deaths were opened earlier on Thursday, with a provisional cause of death of head and neck injuries given for both officers.

The pursuit lasted for just under seven minutes before the Passat entered the A66 against the flow of traffic, the IOPC said previously. Officers ended the pursuit and continued travelling on the correct side of the carriageway, with the fatal crash around 10 seconds later.

PC Blades and PC Clough had not been involved in the original pursuit but had been called to the area to assist with the search for the Passat.

Police investigations into the events surrounding the collision are continuing, with more than 17 people arrested.

Cleveland Police previously said that, on the morning of the collision, it received several reports of two vehicles being driven dangerously.

A Volvo XC60 was reported to have been used to ram properties in the Middlesbrough area, while the Passat was believed to have been linked to the Volvo's activities.

Both vehicles were said to have been operating with cloned number plates and were detected by automatic number plate recognition cameras.

The Volvo was later traced and recovered in Middlesbrough.

  PC Matthew Blades 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

Police have said there are "significant links to serious and organised criminal activity" in the events being investigated in connection with the collision.

Assistant Chief Constable Wayne Fox previously described it as a "fast-moving and complex investigation".

He said police had a duty to thoroughly investigate the serious criminal activity surrounding the incident and bring those responsible to justice.

   

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

Four of the five occupants of the Passat had previous criminal convictions.

Tributes have been paid to all seven people who died, with flowers, balloons and messages left close to the scene at South Bank.

More than £1m has been raised for the families of PC Blades and PC Clough.

Family members described former Civil Nuclear Constabulary officer PC Clough as "our hero", while PC Blades' wife described him as her "beloved husband", "best friend" and a devoted father to their two young boys.
