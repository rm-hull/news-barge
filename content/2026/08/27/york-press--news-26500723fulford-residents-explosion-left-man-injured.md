Residents have spoken of the moment emergency services swooped on their quiet York street after a “massive explosion” which left a man seriously injured in hospital.

One man was taken to hospital after a gas cylinder exploded in the garden of a home in Crossfield Crescent, off Fordlands Road, Fulford, at about 12pm on Wednesday (August 26).

The man was left badly burned after the explosion, witnesses told The Press.

They said the man was treated in a Crossfield Crescent home a safe distance from the scene before being taken to hospital by ambulance.

Residents said they believed the explosion happened after the man cut into an acetylene cylinder, used for welding.

  Emergency services in Crossfield Crescent, York, on Thursday (August 26) after the explosion 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service said the cylinder was removed from the scene by the British Oxygen Company on Thursday morning.

Crossfield Crescent residents near the explosion site were evacuated from their homes and offered shade and refreshments at Fulford School on Wednesday.

They were allowed to return to their homes after the cordon was lifted by emergency services after 4pm on Wednesday.

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service said its crews had been “cooling the cylinder” on Wednesday afternoon and remained in the area into the night. The crews had left the area by Thursday.

  Crossfield Crescent on Thursday (August 27) after emergency services left the scene 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Neil and Michelle Cutsforth welcomed evacuated neighbours into the garden of their house in Crossfield Crescent after the explosion.

Michelle, 62, a retired ambulance service manager, said the man had “bad burns” to his right arm and was treated in the home next door.

She said she was given medical training during her time in the ambulance service so “fetched towels and jugs and things while [the emergency services] treated him in the kitchen sink [of her neighbour’s home]”.

Michelle said the man was “walking and talking” when he was being treated but was “obviously in shock”.

Michelle and Neil were out for a walk at around 12.15pm on Wednesday when they saw fire engines heading towards Crossfield Crescent before the area was cordoned off.

“We’ve lived her for 27 years and that’s the most that’s ever happened in this street,” Michelle said, adding: "Everyone was shocked because it’s such a quiet street."

## Residents praise hero neighbour who helped man after explosion

She said the police and firefighters “were really good at giving information – trying to update us as best as they could”.

The couple also praised Fordlands Road resident Lewis Abley who told The Press he scaled his back garden fence to help the man after hearing the explosion and seeing him badly burned.

Lewis, 35, a landscape gardener who previously served in the Army, said he shouted “are you OK?” to his rear neighbour before he started dampening down the area while checking on the man and reassuring the man’s wife.

Neil called Lewis a hero, while Michelle said: “It would have been completely different without him.”

  Lewis Abley who rushed to help after hearing the explosion on Wednesday (August 26) 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Meanwhile, Fordlands Road resident Nicola Daniel, 53, was at home with her 10-year-old grandson when she said she heard a “massive explosion”.

She then heard water being sprayed followed by a knock at the door.

“There was a fireman at the door and I was like, oh my God,” Nicola said. “He [the fireman] said I had to get out of the house as quickly as possible.

“He said someone had drilled into a welding gas cannister … and set fire to [the man’s] arm and [the cannister] had exploded.”

Ms Daniel said she was stood outside her home of 20 years for an hour and a half before leaving and going to her daughter’s house.

She said she returned home on Wednesday afternoon and a fireman visited her property at 5pm and told her not to light any named flames.
