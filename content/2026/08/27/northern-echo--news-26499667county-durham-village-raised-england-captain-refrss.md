Steph Houghton captained England, won major trophies with Manchester City and became one of the defining figures in women’s football.

But her journey began on the playgrounds and football pitches of South Hetton.

The former Lionesses captain grew up in the County Durham mining village, between Durham and Sunderland, where she first kicked a ball around with her father, Len, and developed the competitiveness that helped take her to the top of the game.

Speaking to Palatinate in 2021, Houghton said: “I love the fact I’m from the North East.

"A lot of my career, the foundations of who I am today, are because of how I’ve grown up and the people I’ve been surrounded by.”

That local grounding gave South Hetton a lasting connection with one of the region’s greatest sporting success stories.

## Where Steph Houghton began

Houghton began playing football as a toddler in the family yard and at the village park, with her father — an electrician and former semi-professional footballer.

She later played against boys and girls at South Hetton Primary School, where she became the first girl to represent the school football team.

Her schoolyard determination was clear early on.

BBC Sport reported that playing with boys helped Houghton develop as a footballer, and she told the broadcaster: “Playing with the boys made me a better footballer; it made me more competitive and made me want to win even more.

"You were playing against the boys and wanted to prove how good you were.”

   

*(Image: Zac Goodwin / PA)*

She remained connected with South Hetton Primary School after reaching the international stage, returning to speak with pupils when her schedule allowed.

Former headteacher Lucy Griffiths told the BBC that Houghton gave children someone tangible to aspire to because she had “sat in their classrooms” and “played on their pitch”.

## From South Hetton to Sunderland

A summer coaching school organised by Sunderland led to Houghton being selected for the club’s Centre of Excellence.

She had already shown promise with grassroots side Boldon Girls, the club listed by England Football as her first grassroots team.

Houghton went on to break into Sunderland’s first team as a teenager, initially as a striker.

BBC Sport reported that she began her senior career at her hometown club in 2002, before later adapting through midfield and into the defensive position in which she became internationally renowned.

She left Sunderland for Leeds United in 2007, later played for Arsenal and joined Manchester City in 2014.

Her career included WSL titles, FA Cups and League Cups, while her leadership became central to Manchester City’s rise in the women’s game.


## England captain and role model

Houghton made her England debut against Russia in March 2007 and finished her international career with 121 caps and 13 goals.

She was England captain for eight years, led the Lionesses at the 2015 and 2019 World Cups, and played for Team GB at the London 2012 Olympics.

Her three goals at the tournament included the winner against Brazil at Wembley — a moment which propelled her into the national spotlight.

The National Football Museum said she became a “dependable defender and resolute leader” for club and country, while her career helped inspire a generation of players in County Durham and beyond.

## A South Hetton sporting story

Houghton retired from professional football in 2024, ending a career that turned a South Hetton schoolgirl with a ball at her feet into one of England’s most respected captains.

Her connection to the village remains an important part of that story.

For young players in South Hetton, the path from the primary-school pitch to Wembley, World Cups and international captaincy is no longer simply a dream — it is a journey already made by one of their own.
