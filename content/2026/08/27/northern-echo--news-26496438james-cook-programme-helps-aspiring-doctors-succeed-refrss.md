A North East hospital programme designed to help talented young people from disadvantaged backgrounds pursue careers in medicine is continuing to inspire the next generation of doctors.

The week-long 'I Want to Be a Doctor' residential course returned to The James Cook University Hospital in July, bringing together more than 30 students from across the UK.

Delivered in partnership by South Tees Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust and the Social Mobility Foundation, the scheme supports high-achieving 16 to 17-year-olds who aspire to study medicine but face barriers due to their background.

  Students in one of the workshops 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Louise Campbell, group head of medical education, said: "Through our partnership with the Social Mobility Foundation, we are helping to shape the future workforce by providing valuable work experience opportunities.

"A high-quality placement can be a transformative experience for students, allowing them to explore career pathways, gain confidence in their abilities, and develop vital professional skills.

"By offering these opportunities, we are empowering young people to make informed career choices and equipping them with the knowledge, experience, and confidence needed to succeed in the future."

Since its launch at James Cook in 2017, the programme has seen 45 per cent of participants go on to medical school.

  Students interacting with each other in an anatomical body painting workshop 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The course offers an immersive look into life as a doctor, with students working alongside clinicians in a range of specialties and taking part in hands-on clinical training.

They developed skills in areas such as paediatric resuscitation, trauma assessment, cardiac arrest management, and radiology.

The programme also included anatomical body painting and interactive sessions to help bring medical concepts to life.

In addition to clinical experience, students received tailored guidance on medical school applications, including interview techniques and communication skills.

Many attendees praised the opportunity to gain practical insight into the profession and said the experience had reinforced their ambition to pursue a career in medicine.
