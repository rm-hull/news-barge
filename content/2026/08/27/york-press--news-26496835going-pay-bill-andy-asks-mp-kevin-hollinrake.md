*Who's going to pay the bill, Andy? asks Conservative MP for Thirsk and Malton Kevin Hollinrake*

A MONTH ago in these pages I asked the new Prime Minister a simple question: where is the money coming from, Andy?

I am still awaiting an answer. In the meantime the bills have only got bigger, so let ask him and you the readers even more clearly. Who is going to pay?

Here are the numbers, and I make no apology for setting them clearly, because they are not discussed enough.

Our national debt now stands at around £3 trillion, up by some £500 billion in this Parliament alone. This year the Government will borrow another £120 billion or so just to make ends meet, piling yet more onto the tab. The interest on all of that borrowing now costs us around £110 billion every single year. That is shocking. We are spending more on debt interest than on many of the public services people care about and rely on, and none of it buys a single nurse, teacher or soldier.

I am afraid, it gets worse because lenders have started to doubt whether we can get a grip, the interest rate on our long-term debt has climbed to around 5.75 per cent, the highest in the G7. Not the highest in Europe. The highest among all the world’s leading economies. People often say "Oh it is an external shock… it is largely out of our hands". Rather it is the price the markets charge a country they are no longer sure will live within its means, and every family with a mortgage ends up paying a share of it.

Who foots the bill in the end? You, your neighbour and your children whether now or in the future. Taxes are already at their highest level in our history, and on the current path the tax burden is set to reach 38.5 per cent of everything our economy produces by 2031. That is money taken out of the pockets of people who work hard and out of the tills of the small businesses on our high streets.

This is the inheritance Andy Burnham has stepped into. A mayor spends money. A Prime Minister has to balance the books. Now, I have said before that I do not underestimate him as a communicator. However, being a good talker is not the same as having a safe pair of hands.

Running the country’s finances is not a speech or a slogan. It is hard arithmetic and the early signs are not encouraging. In his first days in office we have had a cut to VAT on energy bills, a cap on bus fares, and now another £10 billion for housing with little or no credible account of how it will actually be paid for. One was said to be funded by scrapping a scheme that had no money set aside in the first place; another by quietly turning overseas grants into loans and hoping nobody asked the interest rate. Yet another swipe of the nation’s credit card, and the bill left for our children.

That is not to say the issues don’t need addressing, take homes for example. Let me be clear, we badly need more homes, and I want to see them built. But you do not fix a housing shortage simply by announcing money you do not have, while at the same time making it harder to build and to employ.

The Government is choking off the very growth that would pay for its promises, loading businesses with taxes on jobs and employment not to mention the red tape, and then wondering why the economy will not grow. You cannot spend your way out of a hole if other parts of the Government are digging.

There is a better way, and it is not particularly complicated. We must get a grip on what we spend, reform a state which has become greedy. Cut the welfare bill while supporting those in genuine need, bring down the cost of energy that is crippling our industry, cut the size of the civil service and fund our defences properly at a genuinely dangerous moment in the world. We have told the Government we will work with them to do exactly that. The offer still stands.

The question has not changed since last month. It has only become more urgent. Who is going to pay the bill, Andy?
