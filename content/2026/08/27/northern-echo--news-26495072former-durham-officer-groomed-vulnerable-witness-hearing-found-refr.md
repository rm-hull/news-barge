A police officer claimed he was a "catch" as he 'groomed' a vulnerable woman in a bid to strike up a relationship, bombarding her with hundreds of messages.

The former Durham Police officer, referred to only as PC D, was investigating an assault involving the woman's partner, to which she was a witness, when he abused his position to pursue her, a misconduct hearing ruled.

PC D, who cannot be named for legal reasons, sent the woman with hundreds of messages, discussed meeting her privately and even tried to hide their conversations by deleting them from his police phone.

A report from the misconduct hearing said his behaviour involved a “determined and persistent pursuit of an emotional and sexual relationship” and that “he knew she was vulnerable."

"He abused his position to establish and maintain the relationship," it said.

The pair exchanged 636 messages between October 7 and 27 in 2025, averaging around 30 messages a day.

On some days, the messaging lasted more than two hours, and some continued into the early hours.

The officer sent flirtatious messages, describing himself as "a catch" and telling her she was "stunning".

He also told her she had "a beautiful face" and that he had "checked her out".

He discussed starting a casual relationship with her and tried to arrange a meeting.

But in what the hearing described as an attempt to conceal his behaviour, he discussed deleting their messages from his police device.

He “suggested he would delete the thread of messages every few days”, the report stated.

It added: "He clearly knew the contact was wrong and inappropriate.

"The messages suggest he was grooming [her] for sexual purposes."

It found he had been "attempting to cover up his behaviour" and was trying to manage the messaging "without getting caught".

The former officer was also the investigating officer in an assault case in which the woman was a witness.

The hearing found there was a "clear imbalance of power" because he was involved in the investigation and had control over its progression.

The report concluded that his messages amounted to grooming. "The messages suggest he was grooming A for sexual purposes," it states.

The hearing found he was contacting the woman "in order to pursue an emotional relationship with her and to develop a sexual relationship".

It also found his actions had created a conflict of interest and impacted on his ability to carry out his police work.

The officer had previously received training on abuse of position for a sexual purpose in November 2022.

His messages were later deleted from his police-issued Samsung mobile phone.

The former officer accepted "some of this [communication] was excessive" and "may be viewed as flirtatious".

He also accepted the messages came across as "over familiar".

However, he denied that the contact was for sexual purposes and said he did not intend to start a relationship with the woman.

A misconduct panel did not accept his explanation. It found his conduct was "intentional, deliberate, targeted and planned".

"He sought to exploit a witness in a case who was vulnerable," the panel said, describing abuse of position for a sexual purpose as "a form of serious corruption".

The panel said the former officer's conduct risked "serious damage to the integrity of the criminal justice system".

The woman was also found to have suffered harm as a result of his actions.
