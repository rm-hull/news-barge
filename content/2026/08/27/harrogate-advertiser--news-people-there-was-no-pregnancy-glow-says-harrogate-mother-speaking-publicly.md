# 'There was no ‘pregnancy glow’ says Harrogate mother speaking publicly for first time about her birth experiences

## A Harrogate mum has bravely opened up about her own very personal story of the “hidden reality” of neurodivergent pregnancy and motherhood.

Ella Smith-Dunn, 29, who lives in Harrogate with her family, is speaking publicly for the first time about spending nine months in “permanent anxiety”, feeling trapped and overstimulated inside her own changing body and terrified something would happen to her unborn baby.

Ella, whose daughter Gianna is now one, is speaking out alongside Kate Mortimer, Lead Midwife at Harrogate-based Cocoon Healthcare, to highlight why neurodivergent women can experience pregnancy, birth and early motherhood very differently.

The aim is to illustrate from her personal experience why maternity care needs to recognise individual sensory, communication and emotional needs.

**What early pregnancy was like for Ella**

"Before I became pregnant with my daughter Gianna, I thought I had a reasonable idea of what pregnancy would be like.

"I expected sickness and exhaustion, but also some of the excitement and happiness we are so used to associating with pregnancy.

"Instead, I spent much of those nine months feeling trapped inside a body I suddenly didn’t recognise, overwhelmed by physical sensations I couldn’t escape, and terrified that something might happen to my baby.

"There was no pregnancy glow for me, I lived with what felt like permanent anxiety, then became anxious about being anxious because I had heard so often that stress wasn’t good during pregnancy.”

**How her neurodivergence affected Ella**

"I had already begun recognising my own neurodivergence after a family member was diagnosed as autistic in adulthood.

"I started looking more closely at patterns throughout my own life and completed a Do-IT Profiler, which identified traits associated with ADHD alongside dyscalculia.

(Picture contributed)](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmE5YWFmOGRlLTljZjMtNGFhMC04MzhlLTc2ODI4NDNlNzI0ZjoxZTQ2M2Q2ZC1iODczLTQ2NDYtOWEzNy1jMWM1NWFlNjAwNDQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



"Things that had previously seemed like quirks or personal failings suddenly made much more sense.

"I entered the pathway for formal assessment around two years ago but was told I could be waiting approximately 15 years, and decided I couldn’t spend all that time waiting for somebody else to give me permission to understand how my own brain worked.”

**How pregnancy magnified everything.**

"I also have PCOS, and found the uncertainty incredibly difficult because my instinct when frightened is to research, prepare and understand what might happen, while pregnancy simply doesn’t give you that certainty.

"During my first trimester, before I could feel Gianna moving, I became so frightened that I repeatedly took pregnancy tests just to have something tangible telling me I was still pregnant.

"I constantly questioned whether my baby was okay.”

**Making it more manageable**

"Although I felt looked after within NHS maternity care, I realised I needed additional support which recognised how my neurodivergence was affecting my experience.

"I initially found Cocoon Healthcare in Harrogate because I couldn’t face waiting until my routine 12-week scan for reassurance, and later began one-to-one sessions with Lead Midwife, Kate Mortimer.

"Kate was the first person who spoke to me specifically about neurodivergence, pregnancy and labour without making me feel I was simply overthinking everything.

"We found practical ways to make things more manageable, including replacing the conventional one-to-ten pain scale, which made little sense to me, with facial expressions I could actually relate to.”

**Months of anxiety**

“We also prepared for different ways the birth might unfold because I was frightened I wouldn’t be able to advocate for myself if something unexpected happened.

"That became incredibly important when I experienced reduced fetal movement.

"After months of questioning whether my anxiety was rational, when something genuinely felt wrong, I still worried I might be imagining it or wasting somebody’s time.

"Thankfully, I sought help and Gianna ultimately had to be delivered by emergency C-section.

"Our birth plan never came out of the bag, but preparing for that possibility meant my husband Joe and I weren’t suddenly facing something completely unknown.”

**After giving birth: New fears**

“I imagined that once Gianna was safely here, all that fear would disappear and be replaced by the overwhelming rush of love people describe.

"That wasn’t what happened. I struggled to bond with her immediately and felt horribly guilty because I knew how I thought a new mother was supposed to feel.

"About two weeks after she was born, I remember looking at her crying in her Moses basket and asking Joe, “What have I done?".

"I felt ashamed that motherhood hadn’t instantly transformed me into the person I thought I was meant to become.”

**Why I now love being a mum**

"At around six weeks, things began to change when I stopped measuring myself against an imagined version of motherhood and started paying attention to what actually worked for me and Gianna.

"Gianna is now one and I absolutely love being her mum.

"Sometimes I still need quiet or time alone to decompress, but I no longer see that as evidence that I am failing her.

"I want another baby and would willingly do it again, although next time I would understand far more.”

**Sharing my experiences to help other women**

"I wish somebody had told me that you can desperately want your baby while finding pregnancy incredibly difficult, that love does not always arrive as one enormous rush when your child is born, and that needing space does not make you a bad mother.

"That is why I wanted to share something so personal.”

**More information about neurodivergent pregnancy**

Ella is sharing her story as part of a Cocoon Healthcare awareness drive, highlighting neurodivergent pregnancy and motherhood and the importance of more individualised maternity care.

Cocoon Healthcare provides pregnancy and midwifery support from its clinic in Harrogate and, virtually, across the UK.
