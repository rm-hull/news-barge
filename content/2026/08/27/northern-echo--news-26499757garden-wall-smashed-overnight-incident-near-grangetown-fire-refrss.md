A garden wall has been smashed at a property in South Bank, just streets away from the devastating blaze which killed a child and her aunt.

The damage was discovered on Steele Crescent at 12.30am today (August 27), just a short distance from the scene of the devastating house fire in Grangetown where seven-year-old Valentina Foster and her aunt Natalie McDonald, 34, died.

The overnight incident comes as the South Bank, Grangetown and Middlesbrough areas continue to be plagued by crime following the A66 crash which claimed seven lives last weekend.

Cleveland Police said it has not been confirmed whether there is any link to the latest incident, as the investigation is in its early stages.

Superintendent Emily Harrison said: “The property was empty at the time and we have not currently received any reports of any injuries.

“Officers are conducting enquiries to establish the circumstances surrounding this incident.

“Our investigation is in its early stages and it has not yet been confirmed whether this incident is linked to wider organised crime.”

The incident happened just streets from Birchington Avenue, where emergency services were called to a house fire at 5.23am on Wednesday (August 26).

Two people, including a child, died at the scene.

The victims have been named locally as Valentina Foster, seven, and her aunt Natalie McDonald, 34.

A 48-year-old woman remains in a critical condition at the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle, while three other people suffered injuries believed to be non life-threatening.

Two men, aged 23 and 31, have been arrested on suspicion of murder in connection with the fire. Both remain in police custody while enquiries continue.

The incident is being jointly investigated by Cleveland Fire Brigade and Cleveland Police.

  Emergency services at the scene of a major residential fire on Birchington Avenue, in Grangetown, Middlesbrough 

*(Image: North News & Pictures Ltd)*

Redcar and Cleveland Local Policing Commander Superintendent Emily Harrison said the force was keeping an open mind over the circumstances surrounding the blaze.

"We continue to work to establish the circumstances of exactly what has happened, but we are keeping an open mind and cannot rule out any links to wider organised crime at this time."

She added: "We will have a high visibility presence in the Grangetown area and encourage anyone who has concerns, or who may have information, to approach our officers or alternatively call us on 101."

  Police officers at the scene of a fatal crash involving a police car and a Volkswagen Passat on the A66 in South Bank, near Middlesbrough (Owen Humphreys/PA)


The fire happened about two miles from the A66 crash that killed five young men and two police officers on Saturday morning (August 22).

Police said investigations remain ongoing and have not ruled out a link between the incidents.

Cleveland Fire Brigade sent six fire engines and the Incident Command Unit to the blaze, which was confirmed extinguished at 1.30pm.

A fundraiser has also been launched to support Valentina's family and has already raised £1,000.

Natalie Taylor, who set up the fundraiser, said: "I'm fundraising to help my friend with costs for her for losing her husband and daughter. A funeral and a holiday for her and her children."
