A much-loved television vet is set to share memories of his North Yorkshire childhood as part of celebrations marking a milestone anniversary for the village church that helped shape his early years.

Peter Wright, star of The Yorkshire Vet, will speak at an event commemorating 175 years of All Saints’ Church in Great Thirkleby, taking place over the weekend of September 19 and 20.

Peter said: "I have very, very vivid memories, as a child, coming here.

"My granddad was churchwarden for 32 years, and my granny used to come every Friday, and clean up the church, and it was immaculate.

  Yorkshire Vet star Peter Wright at All Saints’ Church in Great Thirkleby 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"My brother and me used to come with her on a Friday afternoon because it was a place of good fun, it was a perfect place for hide and seek.

"We knew every little nook and cranny in the church, and we used to thoroughly enjoy our times here in the church with granny."

The TV vet will reflect on his childhood in the village and the influence of the church on his early life.

Peter said: "I don't know whether we've got any naughty stories.

  Yorkshire Vet star Peter Wright at All Saints’ Church in Great Thirkleby 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"I mean, this is the house of God after all, but we did have one or two characters going back in time.

"There's a gentleman here in the churchyard now, and he wasn't known for his generosity, and I’ll have to mention him, because one of his stories has gone down in folklore with Alf, James Herriot.

"When I told Alf what had happened involving this gentleman, I thought he would never stop laughing.

"And I think that's very important because I think laughing and being happy and joyful, I think, is very therapeutic.

"And being part of a community is what the good Lord would want us to be."

Organisers are inviting the public to share their own memories of All Saints’ Church as part of the weekend’s events.

Memories can be sent to Gill Walmsley at [gillwalmsley@tiscali.co.uk](mailto:gillwalmsley@tiscali.co.uk) or by call or WhatsApp 07969 571002.

Peter also spoke about the uncertain future facing many rural churches.

He said: "I find it very sad, and I do worry for the future of our churches.

"And I think, if it came to a point where the churches were going to have to close, as I know quite a number have done in North Yorkshire, then you get to a point where people will then say, well, what happens when we want a funeral?

"What happens when we want to christen a child? Where are we going to go?

"And it's no good waiting until we get to that point and then trying to do something about it.

"We've got to support our churches now."

Peter’s talk is part of the Saturday celebrations which start at 2pm Saturday September 19 and which will be followed by a performance by Sessay Songbirds and a community choir which everyone is welcome to join in.

A service of thanksgiving will take place at 2pm on Sunday, September 20, followed by Victorian afternoon tea in the village hall.

The weekend aims to bring together the community in celebration, reflection, and support for the future of the church.
