YORKSHIRE Water has issued a fresh safety warning to visitors.

The warning comes as the company urges people to stay out of its reservoirs and open water sites during the bank holiday weekend, following incidents this year in Yorkshire where people have lost their lives in reservoirs and other open water.

Alastair Harvey, lead countryside and woodland advisor at Yorkshire Water, said: "Sadly, we’ve seen incidents this year in Yorkshire where people have lost their lives in reservoirs and other open water.

"We’d advise sticking to the walking routes on public footpaths and don’t be tempted to go for a dip in the shallows or walk on the exposed embankment and structures, as there can be risks of sinking mud."

Reservoirs may appear calm, but they can be extremely hazardous due to cold water shock, hidden operational equipment, sudden drops, and hidden currents.

Lower water levels this year have created additional dangers, including risks of sinking mud.

Yorkshire Water is asking visitors to follow the Countryside Code and consider the wider impact of their actions, not just on themselves, but also on wildlife and the environment.

Mr Harvey said: "We want people across Yorkshire to enjoy our reservoirs and surrounding countryside, but it’s important that people consider their behaviours and do not take risks that could impact the local natural environment and wildlife, or that puts them in harm's way.

"Following the Countryside Code and keeping out of the water will help to make trips to our reservoirs safe and enjoyable for everyone."

The company also reminds the public that disposable barbecues and fires are strictly prohibited at all of its sites.

Mr Harvey said: "Refraining from using disposable barbecues or starting fires, taking litter home, and keeping dogs on leads, especially around livestock, can all help to keep wildlife, the natural environment, and nearby farm animals safe.

"Disposable barbecues and fires are not permitted on any of our sites."

Yorkshire Water’s ranger teams will be present at reservoir sites to talk to visitors about water safety and responsible behaviour.

A full list of reservoirs and information on authorised water sports or fishing clubs is available at yorkshirewater.com/things-to-do/reservoirs.

The company stresses that no water sports are permitted at its reservoirs unless organised through a tenanted club.

In emergencies where someone is in danger in the water, visitors should call 999 and ask for the fire service, which is trained to respond to such incidents.
