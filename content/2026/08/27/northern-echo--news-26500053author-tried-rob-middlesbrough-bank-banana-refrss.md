An author who wrote ‘The Man who Robbed a Bank with a Banana’ was arrested when he tried to fulfil his fantasy by holding a piece of fruit disguised to look like a gun.

Christopher North walked into a Lloyds bank wearing a balaclava before pulling out an item from his pocket covered with cloth and demanding they hand over cash.

Teesside Crown Court heard how some people in the bank shouted ‘it’s a gun while others shouted it’s a banana’ before he was ushered out of the bank by two members of the public.

Elisha Marsay, prosecuting, said: “The defendant executed a plan that he created to rob Lloyds bank.

“He approached the bank and pulled a black balaclava over his face leaving only his eyes showing.

“As he has entered, he pulled an item from his pocket which he held out in front of him, it was wrapped in a piece of white fabric and the true nature of the item to those present was unknown.

“Some people in the bank shouted - ‘it’s a gun while others shouted it’s a banana’ while he pointed the item at the cashier.”

The court heard that the 51-year-old was actually carrying a banana and a search of his home resulted in number of items being seized including three bananas and his laptop which contained his plan to rob the bank.

Miss Marsay said: “Also recovered, were five copies of a book authored by the defendant called ‘The Man who Robbed a Bank with a Banana’.”

She said the defendant has five previous convictions for ten offences including stalking and causing a bomb hoax.

North, of Marton Road, Middlesbrough, was initially charged with attempted robbery but pleaded guilty to causing a public nuisance due to his mental health condition.

Judge Jonathan Carroll sentenced the defendant 30 months in custody suspended for three years.

“This is an exceptional and unusual case,” he said.

“To plan this bank robbery with the detail you did, taking with you an item which was incapable of causing physical harm but capable of causing psychological harm, but for your mental illness you would be looking at a sentence of seven to eight years for attempted robbery.”

North was also ordered to attend 35 rehabilitation activity requirement days and was banned from entering banks within the area he lives for ten years.
