TWO drug dealers from Bradford have been jailed after running a heroin and crack cocaine operation.

Known as "Curly", Shammus Mahmood, 29, of Rushton Road in Thornbury, and Amaad Zahoor, 28, of Bridge Street in the city centre, pleaded guilty to the charges at Sheffield Crown Court on June 8.

Earlier this week (Monday), the pair were sentenced after admitting their roles in a county lines drugs network operating across South and West Yorkshire.

## Pair went to Barnsley

The pair travelled from West Yorkshire into Barnsley, exploiting county lines routes to supply class A drugs while attempting to evade police detection.

Barnsley Criminal Investigation Department (CID) identified their movements and gathered intelligence linking them to the supply network.

Zahoor was arrested in December 2024 after been circulated as wanted.

Officers recovered three phones which were in his possession, one of which is believed to be the "line" phone.

Mahmood was arrested in November 2025 after he was detained for unrelated matters in West Yorkshire.

Full analysis of the phones recovered, and the intelligence gathered led to the charge of both men being concerned in the supply of heroin and crack cocaine.

## Men were making over £400,000 a year

Detective Constable Chris Shirt, who led the investigation, said: "Mahmood and Zahoor thought they were going unnoticed, avoiding police detection by moving between South and West Yorkshire to deal drugs.

"Analysis has estimated that the drugs line Zahoor was involved in was making over £30,000 per month.

"Combining the income of both the line Zahoor was involved in and that of Mahmood, the estimated income would be over £400,000 a year.

"This is a vast amount of money that would most likely make them feel invincible, but they are now facing the consequences of their illegal actions behind bars."

## Men jailed

Mahmood was sentenced to five years and nine months in prison.

He was also sentenced at the same time for possession with intent to supply class A drugs, in relation to a separate investigation.

Zahoor was sentenced to four years and six months in prison.

Both men were also charged with modern slavery and assault-related offences but were found not guilty on those counts.

DC Shirt added: "Our work is continuing around the dismantling of organised criminality such as drug supply across the district, but we cannot do it alone.

"A lot of our investigations rely on information that is fed in by you, our residents.

"You know your local community the best.

"If you see something suspicious, report it."
