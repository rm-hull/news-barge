CIA director John Ratcliffe warned Russia against an attack on a Nato member, sources familiar with the matter told the *Wall Street Journal* and*Politico*. 

Earlier this month, the *Wall Street Journal* reported that US intelligence assessments had indicated that Putin could test Nato’s resolve with a limited assault on an allied country over the coming years.

Several possible scenarios had been posited from cyber-attacks to a small-scale land incursion, according to the report.

President Donald Trump said “something may come out of” Ratcliffe’s visit to Moscow on Tuesday.

Trump said the US is “working very hard” to end the war in Ukraine, calling the CIA chief a “fantastic guy”.

Answering a question on Ratcliffe’s visit to Russia, Trump said that the CIA director made a visit to Russia on a “very semi-routine” basis and “something may come out of it”.

The Kremlin revealed yesterday that CIA director Ratcliffe held talks with Russian intelligence officials during a mysterious visit this week but did not meet president Vladimir Putin.

Spokesperson Dmitry Peskov framed communication between the two countries’ intelligence agencies as positive.

## Kremlin says Moscow would go to court to fight any new 'illegal' EU plan to seize its frozen assets

The Kremlin said on Thursday that Russia would go to court to try to stop any new and "illegal" European Union plan to use its frozen sovereign assets to aid Ukraine.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov was responding to a report in the *Financial Times* which said that some EU states, including Sweden, the Netherlands, Spain and Poland, were urging Brussels to revive efforts to use frozen Russian assets to fund Ukraine

## Pentagon's Colby pushes Europe to do more on defence

Europe has made progress in taking over the responsibility for its conventional defence but more needs to be done, US Pentagon policy chief Elbridge Colby told allies in Brussels ahead of talks about potential US troops cuts in Europe.

"Let's look at some of things like access, basing, overflight. Well, there is a lot of good, but there's some things that we need to work out to make sense going forward," Colby said at Nato headquarters, adding Europe would remain a critical interest of the US and he had come in a spirit of consultation.

Nato allies worry that a six-month review of troops in Europe, announced in June and due by December, will lead to large-scale cuts to the roughly 80,000 US forces across Europe and punish countries that Donald Trump believes failed to support the war against Iran.

## Ukraine's president coos over a puppy, starting a new volley in his war of visuals against Russia

Wait. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, Ukraine's war chief, holding a puppy?

Yes, and it's no accident. In a collision of dog lovers, sports and ongoing war with Russia, the Ukranian president on Tuesday was captured on video as never before — cuddling and cooing over a floppy, three-month-old American pit bull terrier, handed to him by U.S. former boxer Terence Crawford.

The cute and fluffy scene in Zelenskyy's office in Kyiv was the latest volley in a deadly serious battle of visuals that runs in parallel to Ukraine's fight against Russia's brutal four-year invasion.

Crawford, a longtime supporter of Ukraine and a devotee of the American pit bull terrier, came forward and handed a reddish-yellow puppy to Zelenskyy.

## Kremlin dismisses US media reports that Russia may attack Nato as 'scare-stories'

The Kremlin has dismissed US media reports that Russia could attack a Nato member state and that the purpose of CIA Director John Ratcliffe's visit to Moscow this week had been to warn Moscow against such a move.

Asked to comment on the reports, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said: “A lot of this kind of scare-story material is being published right now. Of course, it has nothing to do with reality and nothing to do with the intentions of the Russian Federation.”

## Full story: Russia threatens to bomb UK military bases over support for Ukraine

Russia has warned it could strike British military targets both inside and outside Ukraine over the UK’s support for Kyiv.

Russia’s foreign ministry spokeswoman told Britain to back off on what she called London's hostile stance on Ukraine, which she said risked taking the conflict to an entirely new level.

She told the same Moscow news briefing earlier that Britain and France were "playing with fire" by supplying sensitive missile technology to Ukraine.

Britain announced on Monday it would allow Kyiv access to classified technology to start its own production of Storm Shadow long-range cruise missiles, capable of carrying out strikes deep within Russia.

## Russia threatens to attack British military targets over 'hostile line' on Ukraine

Russia has said on Thursday that it could retaliate against London over its help for Kyiv by striking British military targets both inside and outside Ukraine.

The country called on Britain to back off its “hostile line” on Ukraine, which is creating risks of taking the conflict to a new level.

Earlier this week prime minister Andy Burnham doubled down on the UK’s support for Ukraine and in response to Kremlin comments that Britain was “fuelling the fire”, he responded: “Who started the fire?”

## Why did Trump’s CIA director make a mysterious short trip to Moscow?

The White House is facing scrutiny after the CIA director made a mysterious visit to Moscow on Tuesday, the first visit by a chief of the intelligence agency in nearly five years.

The US Air Force C-17A Globemaster III flew into Moscow from Latvia before landing at the capital’s Vnukovo International Airport on Tuesday, according to Flightradar24.

Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said he did not have any information about the visit, while President Donald Trump stayed uncharacteristically silent on the matter.

## Russia's top commander says his troops are 4 kilometres from Ukrainian fortress city of Sloviansk

Russia's top military officer Valery Gerasimov has said that his forces are just four kilometres (2.49 miles) from the eastern outskirts of the Ukrainian fortress city of Sloviansk.

The chief of Russia's General Staff, made the comments in a video released by the Defence Ministry on Thursday.

President Vladimir Putin has made clear Moscow wants to control the fortress.

*The Independent* could not immediately verify the claim.
