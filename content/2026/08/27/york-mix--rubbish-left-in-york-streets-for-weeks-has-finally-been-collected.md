Household waste left in streets in York for weeks has now been collected following anger over bin bags being ripped open and becoming fly-infested.

Recycling and general waste was left in The Groves after gas works cut off access for bin collection crews, City of York Council has confirmed.

Council environmental services lead, Ian Hoult, apologised to residents affected by the delays.

It comes after Stephanie Holdsworth, who lives in Diamond Street, accused the council of ignoring a growing health hazard after first missing a recycling collection on August 11th.

General waste went uncollected the following week on Tuesday, August 18.

Mrs Holdsworth said Diamond Street, Emerald Street and Earle Street were among those affected and the council had not attempted to collect the rubbish despite several calls from residents.

Speaking before the council’s confirmation on Thursday, August 27 that waste had now been collected, the resident said: “The big bags and bins are starting to smell as they are opened by animals and they’re getting infested with flies, even though the residents have all tried their very best to keep things safe and tidy.

“The council are quite happy to ignore a health hazard in our streets as it’s not a very prestigious area of York.

Another Groves resident, who asked not to be named, said people who get assisted collections and only use bin bags were particularly affected by bad smells.

They said: “They are being ripped apart by vermin and are littering the streets.

For The City of York Council, Mr Hoult said the gas works had made it difficult for crews to access the streets.

He said: “We apologise for any inconvenience that residents in the area have experienced.”

Separately, the council has said waste collections across York will be unaffected by the bank holiday on Monday, August 31.

People have been told to be aware that the council’s Hazel Court and Towthorpe tips are likely to be busier than usual over the weekend.

The council has said people should remember to pre-book a slot to visit Hazel Court if they are planning on disposing of waste there.
