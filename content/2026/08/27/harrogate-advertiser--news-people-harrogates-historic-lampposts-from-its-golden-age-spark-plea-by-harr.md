# Harrogate's historic lampposts from its golden age spark plea by Harrogate Advertiser reader

## A Harrogate Advertiser reader has taken his concern over the state of historic lampposts and street signs in the town to North Yorkshire Council.

Regarded by the town as important historic artefacts, the lampposts in the Valley Gardens from the town’s golden age of aristocracy and spa tourism were formerly the responsibility of Harrogate Borough Council and North Yorkshire County Council.

With the abolition of Harrogate Borough Council in 2023 and the launch of the new more powerful unitary authority of North Yorkshire Council, there has been worry that these symbols of Harrogate’s distinguished heritage may be accidentally falling into disrepair.

One reader, Bernard Higgins, was so alarmed he got in touch with North Yorkshire Council.

"Walking through Valley Gardens recently made my heart drop at the sight and state of the lamp posts and lanterns,” said Mr Higgins.

“Over the past few years they have deteriorated to their present sorry and unattractive state.

“The lamp posts and lanterns need replacing urgently. Many of the lanterns are actually held together with black tape.

“The state they are in certainly does not help sell our historic Valley Gardens to the many visitors and existing residents.”

Harrogate’s oldest surviving historic lampposts date back to 1848, when the town's first gas street lighting was introduced by the Harrogate Gas Company.

According to Harrogate Civic Society, some of the lampposts in the Valley Gardens date back to at least the 1920s.

There are six cast iron lampposts on the footpath between Valley Drive and Cornwall Road.

In the past, suggestions of replacing Harrogate’s remaining vintage lampposts with modern street lighting have led to a public backlash.

But the Harrogate Advertiser reader is hoping North Yorkshire Council will step in to help, as it has done before.

"Several years ago the lamp posts and lanterns around St John’s Church and Vicarage Lane in Knaresborough were in a similar dilapidated state,” said Mr Higgins.

“Then Harrogate Council and North Yorkshire County Council. rose to the occasion by replacing the ‘broken’ lamp posts and lanterns with modern, taller lamp posts and lanterns along the lanes and path up to the railway station.”

Since taking on its new powers and responsibilities in 2023, North Yorkshire Council has introduced a replacement programme for infrastructure such as lampposts which it inherited from Harrogate Borough Council and the other district councils dissolved as part of a major local government reorganisation.

Mr Higgins says North Yorkshire County Council deserves praise for its action in a replacing a tired-looking warning sign for drivers in the Harrogate High School area before the new term starts.

“The new school sign leading to St Robert Primary School and Harrogate High School looks fantastic,” he said.

"The pupils, parents and staff at St Robert’s and Harrogate High, not to mention the hockey coaches and players at Harrogate Hockey Club, will be impressed when they pass by.”
