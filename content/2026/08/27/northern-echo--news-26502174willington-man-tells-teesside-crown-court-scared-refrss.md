A man accused of killing his biological dad within hours of seeing him for the first time has told a court he only pushed him gently.

Steven Shevels died after hitting his head on the concrete surface in the beer garden of a Willington pub on Christmas Eve.

Teesside Crown Court heard how Martin Greensmith learned who his father was while he was in his 20s but had never come face to face with him until that day.

The 37-year-old told jurors how Mr Shevels had tried to intimidate him and challenged him to a fight in the minutes leading up to the fatal shove.

Greensmith was in the Queen’s Head pub with his mother, nanna, uncle and brothers during the afternoon before his dad came into the bar after already downing nine pints while out in Crook.

The defendant said he didn’t really care when he was told that the loud drunken person was his actual father and told the court that he didn’t want anything to do with him.

Dealing with the fatal push, Greensmith told jurors that he didn't say anything to Mr Shevels as he approached him as he made his way back into the pub.

He said Mr Shevels put his arm out to try to stop him getting past while his right arm was slightly raised but not in a clenched fist.

"I felt intimidated and scared," he said. "He could have leaned in towards me to headbutt me or slap me."

He said he had never met his biological father before and he just tried to gently push him out of the way to go into the pub.

"There was literally no force at all - it was gentle," he said.

Greensmith accepted that he had left the scene about ten minutes after the shove and stayed at his brother’s home after falling asleep.

Under cross-examination from Jolyon Perks, prosecuting, Greensmith denied that he was angry to learn his biological father was the loud and drunken man in the bar.

The jury was re-shown the CCTV from outside the pub which captured the incident.

Mr Perks said there was an awful sound as Mr Shevels hit the ground.

"What were you thinking at that moment?" he asked.

"I didn't expect him to fall and I didn't know what to do," Greensmith replied.

When asked what was said between the pair, he replied: "I said you're my dad and I don't want to know you."

The prosecutor asked Greensmith about his actions when he left the premises and why he left.

"Did you leave because you had realised what you had done to him?" he asked. Greensmith replied: "No."

Trevor Greensmith, the defendant’s uncle, told the jury that he heard Mr Shevels say "35-year-old and look at the f** state of you" but he didn't know whether his nephew heard him.

Under cross-examination, Mr Greensmith denied that he had to step in because his nephew was angry about learning about his father.

Pathologist Jennifer Bolton told the court that Mr Shevels suffered a cardiac arrest while unconscious and never recovered.

She said the cause of death was triggered by the fall and not an underlying heart condition he had.

Greensmith, of Rockingham Road, Sunnybrow, Willington, denies a charge of manslaughter and maintains that he acted in self-defence.

The trial continues.
