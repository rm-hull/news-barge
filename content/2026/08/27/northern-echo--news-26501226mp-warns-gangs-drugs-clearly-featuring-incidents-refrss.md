A Middlesbrough MP has warned gangs and the drugs trade are “clearly featuring” in a series of incidents across Teesside which have rocked the area.

Labour MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East Andy McDonald said it appeared there was a link to organised crime as he raised concerns over police funding.

Speaking to BBC Radio 4, Mr McDonald said: “It does look, to all intents and purposes, as though there is a causal link with organised crime.

"But whatever it is, this is a series of appalling incidents.

"It’s absolutely devastated our community, and it’s imperative that our people feel safe and secure in their homes and in their neighbourhoods.”

  Middlesbrough MP Andy McDonald 

*(Image: Press release)*

Asked whether gangs are involved, he said: “It’s hard for me to be particular, but I think gangs are clearly featuring in this, as is the drugs trade, but I’ll leave Cleveland Police to be more particular about that because they’re across it.”

Mr McDonald also said he understood concerns that people may be too frightened to report crimes because of fears of retribution.

“That’s an observation that many people make, and and I understand that,” he said.

He said Cleveland Police had been “underfunded for many, many years” and claimed the funding formula “leaves us under-resourced”.

Mr McDonald said: “We do suffer those metropolitan levels of crime, and yet comparatively, our funding looks much more like a rural area, and it simply cannot continue like that.

"We’ve got to put resources where the demand is most high, and that is Cleveland, and that’s the message to ministers.”

His comments come after a series of devastating incidents across the Middlesbrough area.

A vehicle crashed into a wall outside a house on Steele Crescent, South Bank, shortly after 12.30am on Thursday.

The property was close to the scenes of the A66 crash which killed seven people on Saturday and the Grangetown house fire which claimed the lives of seven-year-old Valentina Foster and her aunt Natalie McDonald, 34, on Wednesday.

  Valentina Foster, seven, and Natalie McDonald, 34, have been named locally as the two victims who died following a house fire in Grangetown. 

*(Image: Provided)*

Cleveland Police said it had not been confirmed whether the latest incident was linked to wider organised crime.

  Police at the scene of the fire on Birchington Avenue in Grangetown 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

The latest developments came as inquests were opened into the deaths of two police officers killed in the A66 crash.

PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38, died when their marked police vehicle was hit by a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way along the road.

Teesside Coroners’ Court heard that “all occupants of both vehicles were sadly pronounced dead at the scene”.

  PC Blades and PC Clough 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

Coroner Paul Appleton said: “At this stage it is understood that there had been a police pursuit of the Volkswagen Passat.

“It entered the A66 carriageway against the flow of traffic, at which stage the pursuit was aborted.

“A short time after… the Volkswagen Passat collided with a police vehicle, which had not been directly involved in the prior pursuit.”

Cole Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Michael Robert Cahill and Jakub Matusiak, named by police as Jacob, died in the crash.

  From left to right: Makai Saddington, 18, Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jakub Matusiak, 23, Michael Robert Cahill, 23. 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

The hearings came as detectives continued to question two men arrested on suspicion of murder following the Grangetown house fire.

Police have also arrested 17 people following the A66 crash, charging two and bailing the others, as part of investigations into alleged organised crime.

The Passat had been pursued by police following an earlier incident in which a house in the Acklam area of Middlesbrough, linked to Worthy, was rammed by a vehicle.

  Police officers at the scene of a fatal crash involving a police car and a Volkswagen Passat on the A66 in South Bank, near Middlesbrough (Owen Humphreys/PA) 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Police have previously said the five young men in the Passat may have had links to “serious and organised criminal activity” and four had previous convictions.

Former Middlesbrough mayor Andy Preston said the string of incidents was “heartbreaking to see – not just the loss of life, but the further scarring of once proud communities”.

He said: “More incidents are happening and it looks like they’re related.

“Everyone’s praying that this peters out. If it doesn’t, more innocent lives could be at risk.”

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the Government was offering “all of the support that the authorities need” following the Grangetown fire.
