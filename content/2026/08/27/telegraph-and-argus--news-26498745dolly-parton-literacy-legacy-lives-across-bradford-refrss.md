ALMOST half a million free books have been handed out to children across the Bradford district through a scheme created by legendary music star Dolly Parton .

Organisers from across Bradford’s Imagination Library have been paying tribute to the country icon following her passing on Tuesday.

Dolly Parton's Imagination Library gives free books to children from birth until age five.

A child who is enrolled receives an age-appropriate book in the post every month at no cost to their family.

Parton started the programme in 1995 in her home county of Sevier County, Tennessee, inspired by her father's inability to read and write.

It has since expanded to several countries, including the UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia, and the United States.

## Dolly Parton's Imagination Library in Bradford

The scheme has been running in Bradford for 14 years and has given out 492,051 books to children across the district, with 15,994 youngsters benefiting thanks to the support of community partners.

Jan Winter, who launched the first Imagination Library in West Yorkshire, began the Canterbury scheme in Bradford in 2012 after watching a documentary about child poverty that featured a family from the area.

Ms Winter said: "It really stuck with me; I thought I wonder if I could get an Imagine Library in Canterbury.

"It was a big leap of faith."

## 'A way out of poverty'

In the Canterbury area alone, which covers parts of Little Horton and Manningham, more than 124,000 books have been distributed so far, with around 500 children currently receiving books every month.

Ms Winters said: "The book is addressed to the child so every month they're getting a free gift through the post.

"You don't need to be able to read English or even speak English to be able to share a book.

"Literacy is a way out of poverty.

"That was Dolly Parton's inspiration comes from, her dad couldn't read or write, and she could see how it impacted his life.

"It's a gift and a great scheme. You can make better decisions your whole life if you have better levels of literacy.

"It's only a small thing but lots of small things can make a big difference.

"It would be fabulous to see it across Bradford, I think of all places it really needs it."

## How much does the scheme cost?

Books are selected by the Dollywood Foundation, helping to keep costs low.

It costs just £26 per child per year for 12 books delivered through the post.

The Canterbury organisation recently received a £10,000 boost from JCT600's Driving Brighter Futures fund, which will help expand the programme in Little Horton, introduce storytelling sessions, and establish a new scheme in Holme Wood.

Ms Winters said: "Things like that really keep us going for a year."

## Scheme expands into Keighley

The Canterbury project has inspired similar schemes across the district.

In 2020, Strong Close Nursery School launched a pilot in Keighley, and by 2021, a district-wide scheme was underway with support from Councillor Caroline Firth (Labour, Keighley East), Helen Jones, and other community leaders.

More than £120,000 has since been raised to secure the scheme’s future.

Cllr Firth said: "I was absolutely hooked, I really do believe in the power of reading."

After launching in July 2021, the Keighley scheme secured £90,000 in funding, including £50,000 from a local grant and £40,000 from community fundraising.

The Dollywood Foundation also donated £25,000 to support the effort.

Cllr Firth said: "I think they appreciated our success and respected the impact our organisation has had on a generation."

## 'It's nice to have that special time with them'

As of early 2026, more than 2,000 children were registered with Imagination Library schemes across Bradford, including over 1,100 in Keighley, making it the 12th largest programme in the UK.

One Keighley parent said the scheme had become a valued part of family life.

The parent said: "I feel like the programme is increasing our bond.

"As a family with 3 children, you don't get to spend one on one time with all of them, reading books gives us that, it's nice to have that special time with them."

## Imagination Library in Baildon

The Baildon Imagination Library, organised by Councillor Debbie Davies (Conservative, Baildon), launched in 2015 and now reaches around 300 children under five in the BD17 area, with more than 35,000 books delivered to date.

Cllr Davies said: "It is really lovely, their first book is always Peter Rabbit and it has a special message from Dolly.

"Then when they reach the age of 'five' they graduate and their final book also has a special message from Dolly."

Following Dolly Parton’s death, representatives from the Imagination Library sent a message to partners around the world.

They said: "Dolly may have dreamed the Imagination Library, but all of you helped her build it.

"The greatest tribute we can give her is to keep it going — together."
