**Manager Derek McInnes says he takes "full responsibility" for Rangers' "embarrassing" Conference League exit at the hands of Jablonec.** 

Rangers, who held a 1-0 lead going into the second leg of the play-off in Czech Republic, lost 4-3 on penalties on Thursday to crash out of Europe completely.

Derek McInnes' side actually led early in the shootout, only for Findlay Curtis and James Penrice to miss their final two penalties.

It leaves them without European football beyond August for the first time since the 2-1 aggregate defeat by Luxembourg's Progres Niederkorn in the Europa League first qualifying round in 2017.

And it ratchets up the pressure on McInnes after a hapless start to his reign at Ibrox, which included a Europa League exit against Jagiellonia Bialystok and a draw and defeat in their opening two Premiership games.

"I'm embarrassed with the performance," the Rangers manager said.

"The only people who represented the club well tonight were my staff and the supporters who were outstanding, getting behind the team despite watching what we all watched.

"I'm not going to make any excuses. Yes there's been a big turnover of players, yes we looked inexperienced at times but the responsibility is mine to make sure we're better than what we served up tonight.

"We're elite in everything we do in terms of preparation and the performance lacked in everything."

McInnes 'mortified' at Rangers' European exit

## 'We got what we deserved'

The stats back up McInnes' anger. Rangers had just over 46% possession during the 120 minutes in the Czech Republic, and just one shot on target.

They won fewer than 70% of their 16 tackles and committed 17 fouls.

"I can't accept, and we shouldn't accept, losing individual battles, duels, second balls, not winning enough tackles and just getting the momentum of the game going our way," the former Hearts manager added.

"I thought we looked young, inexperienced at times. We're trying to make some signings to help with that side of it but we need to do so much more as a team.

"We got what we deserved. If we'd won on penalties we would have taken it, of course, but it would only have covered over the cracks.

"I'm mortified with the performance. I know you can lose games of football but we should have enough in the building to take care of that tonight."

Rangers had won their two previous games - a 5-1 win over St Mirren in the League Cup and the 1-0 victory against Jablonec at Ibrox - after drawing at Dundee United and losing at home to Hibernian in their opening two Premiership matches.

But McInnes - who confirmed that no bid has yet been received for the absent Nico Raskin - feels this latest result may set them back.

"We went into this game scrambling for some sort of confidence, some positivity," he said. "But I just thought the team that deserved to go through went through tonight and that's hard for me to take."

## 'One of worst results in recent history' - the pundits' view

'That wasn't football!' - Halliday reacts as Rangers crash out of Europe

**Former Rangers midfielder Andy Halliday:** "Rangers had no style, no desire, no movement, no tackles, no shots, no control. They offered absolutely nothing over 120 minutes. I'm not that shocked given what I've watched in the first few games.

"The questions are going to start coming Derek McInnes' way. Rangers played five different formations tonight and they offered nothing.

"They had a week to prepare for this game. I don't know what they did. I didn't see how they were going to score. They got played through far too easy.

"I think of Kaunas, Progres Niederkorn - that's up there with those defeats. This is one of the worst results in Rangers' recent history."

**Former Rangers striker Steven Thompson:** "One shot on target in 120 minutes. That tells a story about Rangers' threat.

"When players are on the pitch and things aren't going well and they look around them for someone to grab the game by the scruff of the neck, to lead, there's no answers.

"I could see Derek's frustration. He couldn't believe what he was watching. He tried numerous different things to bring about a better level of performance. But they need better quality on the pitch - and they need a gameplan."

**BBC Scotland chief sportswriter Tom English:** "They were passive and there was no get up and go about any of them. In a properly-functioning team, which this clearly is not, there would be one or two in there who, out of pure anger at their haplessness, would try and get something going but there was nobody. 

"There was nobody out there berating, cajoling, encouraging, there was none of that. They all just went down with the ship. It didn't matter what Derek McInnes did, nobody responded.

"McInnes will be looking at this thinking they've put a lot of work into it, spent a lot of money, tried a lot of formations and nothing is working. What does he do now?"
