# No ban at fishing lake, but remains on the table to tackle ‘poor angling practices’, council says

## The City of Doncaster Council has confirmed there is no ban on fishing at a boating lake in Askern, but options remain open to tackle “poor angling practices”.

Askern Town Council reportedly requested fishing at Askern Lake be suspended after reports of birds being caught and trapped in hooks and fishing lines.

However, local anglers have been organising this week in attempts to convince the local authority not to stop the practice.

A Doncaster Council spokesperson said: “There is currently not a fishing ban in place around Askern Lake but, in response to various ecological and environmental concerns that have been raised by the local Askern Town Council, we are exploring steps that can be taken to address poor angling practices in the area.

![Lake](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU4NWNhNWMzLTNlYzQtNDJmMy1iZTZkLWFkMDQ4ZTY3Y2MzNjo1NTdkNmEyNy1jM2EzLTQ0MzQtOGNiYS1kYmUwZjJhYTFhMmU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



“Irresponsible fishing on the lake has recently been linked to antisocial behaviour problems, to littering offences, and to wildlife being injured, with multiple instances of birds being tangled up in hooks.

“We are taking these issues very seriously and are working with partners, including our colleagues at Askern Town Council, to review what actions can be taken in the near future.”

It is reported that, in the last two weeks of July 2026, the Yorkshire Swan & Wildlife Rescue Hospital visited Askern Lake on two occasions. Both visits were reportedly in response to incidents of birds being injured or tangled in hooks.

Askern Town Council staff are reportedly to have intervened in seven other cases of birds being tangled in hooks and weighed down by discarded weights in recent weeks.

A temporary or permanent ban would give fish stocks and the wider ecosystem an opportunity to recover and would help prevent further incidents of poor angling practices and anti-social behaviour, Doncaster Council suggested.

Earlier in the week, town councillor John Rowley, who also runs Askern Angling Supplies, said a ban would be a “knee-jerk reaction” to the issue and stated he and other volunteers had proposed a local fishing club.

“We’ve got to protect the wildlife, that’s a huge thing,” the business owner said.

“There’s a dozen or so local people who want to help police the place and make it safe, keep control,” he added.

Councillors Vicky Lawson and Gerald Squire, who represent the area on the city council, said anti-social behaviour and wildlife harm at the lake must stop to prevent a ban.

“It’s been going on for months. Wildlife has been injured with fishing lines and a number of geese taken away by bird sanctuaries,” Coun Squire said.

“Nobody’s wanting it stopped but it can’t carry on as it is… we’re caught between a rock and a hard place,” he added.
