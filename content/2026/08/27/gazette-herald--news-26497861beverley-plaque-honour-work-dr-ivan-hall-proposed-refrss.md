A PLAQUE to commemorate the work of a man who was "a critical force" in saving historic parts of a Yorkshire town for three decades could be installed outside a building he acquired.

A planning application has been submitted for listed building consent to install a metal plaque on the external wall of 11 Ladygate in Beverley, in commemoration of Dr Ivan Hall.

The planning application has already received support from the town council and Beverley Civic Society.

  


The application said: "Dr Hall saved 11 Ladygate from demolition in the 1970s and restored it.

"He was a critical force in saving parts of historic Beverley from destruction in the 1960s, 1970s and 1980s and was formally thanked by order of the council for his considerable efforts."

A 2005 citation read "The thanks of Beverley Town Council and the People of Beverley are formally given to Dr Ivan Hall, formerly of Hengate in this town, who with his wife Elisabeth did so much to preserve the historic buildings and streets of Beverley when they were most under threat of destruction", documents added with the plans said.

According to documents issued with the proposal, 11 Ladygate is a grade II listed medieval, timber-framed structure with brick cladding.

It is one of the few remaining Beverley buildings that retains its original plot width, laid out when the Norman extension of the Saxon town was developed at the close of the twelfth century.

It is also one of the few surviving buildings in which a "worthwhile part" of the timber framework remains visible.

The property stands on the east side of Ladygate, an ancient street running through the central markets conservation area near Saturday Market.

The application documents say the plaque would be of an appropriate design and scale.

The supporting heritage statement within the application sets out the building’s history and Dr Hall’s role in its restoration during the late 20th century.

He acquired No. 11 after it was put up for sale by tender, with an obligation to restore it.

In 1978, its structural condition was described as a matter of great concern.

During the work, Dr Hall salvaged materials from other buildings in Beverley and elsewhere.

Oak posts came from Beverley Guildhall, which had itself received material from demolished galleries in Beverley Minster, according to the planning documents.

Beverley Town Council has supported the application, with a spokesperson saying: "Dr Hall's dedication to recording and championing Beverley's history made a significant and lasting contribution to the towns unique character and the preservation of its built heritage.”

Conservation officers said that the plaque would "enhance the understanding of the heritage asset."

A decision from East Riding of Yorkshire Council on the application is expected by September 30.
