  **In the aftermath of tragedy, Redcar MP Anna Turley pays tribute to the emergency workers, volunteers and community champions whose courage, compassion and commitment continue to bring hope.**

A good friend of mine is a paramedic in Redcar.

Every day he puts on his uniform to go out to serve the community and help those in need, never knowing what the day, or night, will bring.

We talked last night about the horrific incident on the A66. The tragic loss of the lives of the bravest who were out that night to protect us and who should have come home to their families.

The senseless loss of too many young people to lives of crime, drugs and violence. Our communities struggling under the weight of this crime and antisocial behaviour, making so many neighbourhoods feel unbearable to live in. The hideous social media feeding frenzy before people have even been laid to rest.

So much despair and shock, anger and sadness. My friend and I came to the same conclusion. In the darkest of times, you have to look to the light.

And that light is to be found there, amongst those who went out that night to serve. And those that get up every morning, put on their uniforms to protect, serve and save people they don’t even know.

Those two police officers, Matthew Blades and Tom Clough, were the light. They were the brave people who served their country in the armed forces, who volunteered in their local community, and who drove towards danger on our behalf that night, as they will have done before, time and again.

Fathers, brothers, husbands, sons. They should have come home to their families at the end of their shifts. We were so lucky to have them on our side. Their loss is devastating. Their light lives on.

The light is to be found, too in those who rushed to the most horrendous of scenes.

Paramedics who I know worked double and triple shifts. The fire service who had to deal with unimaginable human tragedy this week. The Great North Air Ambulance team - there for us that night as they always are. Doctors and other health professionals who worked tirelessly to save lives in the most awful of circumstances.

It is to be found in the Cleveland police officers who put on their uniforms once more and were back doing their duty the next day despite the loss of two of their own police family.

And it is to be found in the police and blue light networks who have come to the aid of Cleveland Police with support and resources and solidarity.

That light is found in many others too - people who work and fight every day to try and build better communities and give young people life chances.

Youth workers who never give up on their kids, and people who get up early to open community centres to provide safe spaces. School teachers and assistants keeping kids safe, protecting them from harm and helping them to live their best lives.

The light is found in those helping others tackle alcohol or drug harm, in those who are carers or volunteering to coach kids in boxing or football or netball, keeping them active and healthy and safe.

These are the heroes. They are the ones bringing hope to our communities. These are the people we need to support, invest in and give the tools they need to do their jobs. They are the light we need to look to as we rebuild our communities from this senseless tragedy.
