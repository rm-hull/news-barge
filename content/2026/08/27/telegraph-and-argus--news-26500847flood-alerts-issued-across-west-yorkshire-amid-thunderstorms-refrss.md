FLOOD alerts have been issued across West Yorkshire following a Met Office yellow weather warning for thunderstorms.

The warning, which covers today, brings a risk of sudden flooding, damage to buildings, and disruption to travel, with flood alerts already in place for parts of the region.

West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service (WYFRS) said: "Flood alerts have been put in place across areas of West Yorkshire.

"Check your local risk, prepare in advance, and stay safe if flooding occurs."

## Upper River Calder area at risk

A flood alert remained in place for the Upper River Calder catchment area as of 10.48am.

The Upper River Calder catchment covers the River Calder and its tributaries from Todmorden to Brighouse.

The alert stated: "Flooding is possible on 27 August 2026 due to the heavy and thundery downpours expected today.

"Locations most affected will be low lying land and roads near rivers."

River levels are being closely monitored, as sudden rises could lead to surface water flooding.

## Bradford has low risk

The Government’s flood forecast for Bradford indicated a low risk of flooding for Thursday, with a further drop to very low risk on Friday.

WYFRS shared further advice from West Yorkshire Prepared, a multi-agency emergency response partnership, which said: "The Met Office has issued a yellow weather warning for thunderstorms, affecting West Yorkshire on Wednesday and Thursday. This means there's a possibility of:

- Homes and businesses flooding quickly.
- Damage of buildings from floodwater, lightning strikes, hail or strong winds.
- Fast flowing or deep floodwater.
- Delays and cancellations to public transport.
- Spray and sudden flooding leading to difficult driving conditions and road closures.
- Power cuts affecting services to homes and businesses. Be prepared and stay safe by:
- Checking travel plans before setting off.
- Never driving through floodwater.
- Keeping up to date with weather and travel information."

WYFRS also issued practical advice for residents in flood-risk areas, including registering for flood warnings, preparing an emergency kit, obtaining flood-specific home insurance, and purchasing sandbags in advance.

They advised residents to move vehicles to higher ground, turn off mains power before leaving properties, and inform neighbours, especially those who are elderly or vulnerable.

During flooding, WYFRS warned against walking, swimming, or driving through floodwater, staying off riverbanks and canal paths, and avoiding contact with potentially contaminated water.

They also reminded residents not to let children play near floodwater.

After flooding, the service advised boiling tap water or using bottled water until supplies are declared safe and disposing of contaminated items.
