**Ireland's Kate O'Connor moved into pole position for heptathlon gold with one event remaining at the European Athletics Championships in Birmingham, where Great Britain's Katarina Johnson-Thompson is out of medal contention.**

Newry-born O'Connor entered Saturday morning in third place after four events, just 42 points behind overnight leader Adrianna Sulek-Schubert, and her personal best form continued in the long jump with a leap of 6.55m.

The 25-year-old Commonwealth Games gold medallist followed up with 50.16m in the javelin to take her overall total to 5,812 points, 51 ahead of Netherlands' Emma Oosterwegel (5,761) with another Dutch athlete, Sofie Dokter, in third (5,741).

Poland's Sulek-Schubert dropped to seventh after a disappointing start to Saturday.

Johnson-Thompson's struggles continued following an injury-hit build-up to the championships.

The 33-year-old entered Saturday 324 points off the pace in 16th and managed just 5.77m in the long jump before throwing 36.92m in the javelin.

That meant the two-time world champion drifted to 19th place, 780 points behind O'Connor.

Her GB team-mate Jade O'Dowda sat 13th, 491 points in arrears of the pace-setting Ireland athlete.

The heptathlon concludes on Saturday evening with the 800m at 19:45 BST.

## Hunter Bell through, GB sprinters eye history, walking records fall

Hunter Bell reaches 1500m final with heat win

Great Britain's Georgia Hunter Bell finished first in heat two of the women's 1500m to qualify for Sunday's final.

The 32-year-old finished in 4:08.97, with Poland's Klaudia Kazimierska second and Portugal's Salome Afonso third.

Ireland's Jodie McCann finished 13th in the heat to miss out on a place in the final, but her team-mates Sarah Healy and Sophie O'Sullivan made it through from heat one, finishing second [4:08.12] and fifth [4:08.88] respectively.

Both GB and Ireland will feature in Saturday's 4x100m men's and women's finals.

In the women's final, Britain's Amy Hunt and Dina Asher-Smith will seek record-equalling medals in the women's race at 21:33 BST.

Hunt is bidding for a third gold medal in Birmingham following victories in the 100m and 200m, while Asher-Smith is seeking a seventh European gold overall.

Also on Saturday, race walking world records were broken with Italy's Sofia Fiorini finishing the marathon in 3:15:11, while Spain's Maria Perez Garcia came home in the half-marathon in 1:30:06.
