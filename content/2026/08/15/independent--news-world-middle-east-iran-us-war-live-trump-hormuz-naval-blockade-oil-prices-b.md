Donald Trump has said he will soon declare the Strait of Hormuz a US territory.

The US president escalated his rhetoric over the vital waterway that carries a significant share of global oil supplies, as he spoke to the media on Friday.

He also told Fox News that the US will hit Iran hard economically as Washington and Tehran struggle to reach a deal to end a nearly six-month conflict.

His comments come as the US prepares to deploy the USS George Washington to the Middle East to relieve military personnel stationed on the USS Abraham Lincoln for more than 200 days amid growing concerns about living conditions on the aircraft carrier, according to reports.

The Lincoln has been deployed for more than 250 days and has not made a port call in 200 days, setting a record for consecutive days at sea, according to US senator Richard Blumenthal, who wrote to Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth about concerns for those on board.

Blumenthal cited reports of “shortages of basic supplies, water contamination, plumbing issues, deteriorating mental health, deck safety concerns, and disruptions in the mail system”.

Trump officials have dismissed concerns about the conditions of the USS Lincoln.

## Trump says he will declare Strait of Hormuz US territory

Donald Trump has said that he will declare the Strait of Hormuz a US territory.

“After we finish defeating Iran, which is being very badly defeated, pretty soon I'll be declaring the Hormuz Strait a territory of the United States”, Trump said to cheers at an event in New York Friday.

Washington and Tehran both claim to have control over the vital oil waterway, which has been effectively closed amid the war.

## US special forces ready to step up combat drills in Philippines

American special operations forces are ready to step up warfighting-readiness exercises with counterparts in the Philippines and other countries in Asia to further strengthen a security alliance and help deter a major conflict in the region, a U.S. military official said Thursday.

Adm. Frank Bradley, commander of the U.S. Special Operations Command, is in the Philippines after a trip to South Korea. He will then head to Japan for talks with military officials.

He is the latest American military top brass to visit key Asian allies in recent weeks to reassure them of Washington’s commitment to the region given the U.S. preoccupation with the war in Iran.

## Top US official to step down as war with Iran escalates

One of Donald Trump’s top national security advisors involved in talks with Iran is expected to resign in the coming weeks as tensions with the Islamic Republic re-escalate, according to a report.

Andy Baker, the White House deputy national security advisor, will leave the administration in the next few weeks, Axios reported on Friday.

A source close to Mr Baker told the outlet he wanted to spend more time with his family and had stayed on longer than planned to ensure a smooth transition.
