A group of fundraising cyclists have raised £3,000 for Hartlepool RNLI lifeboat station after completing a challenging coast-to-coast ride.

Known as the Steelies, the group rode 156 miles in just three days, starting at Silloth RNLI lifeboat station and finishing in Hartlepool with overnight stops in Haltwhistle and Tynemouth.

Dwayne Douglas, one of the cyclists and organisers, said: "It was a lot of hard work with a lot of steep hills along the route, but we kept going and it was great to finish the ride with a fantastic reception back at the Steelworks Club after a quick stop at Hartlepool RNLI lifeboat station where we met some of the volunteer crew and had a look at their boats.

  Hartlepool RNLI coxswain Matt Adams(far right) pictured with the coast to coast cyclists during their visit to the Ferry Road lifeboat station.

"We chose the local RNLI charity for the good job they do, which is even more relevant with recent events, and with amazing support from our sponsors and supporters, we were also able to make donations to local representatives of the Motor Neurone Disease Society and Community Link."

Malcolm Cook, chairman of Hartlepool RNLI, said: "On behalf of all the officials and crewmembers at the Ferry Road lifeboat station I would like to thank Dwayne and everyone who helped make the event such a great success."
