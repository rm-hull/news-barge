Recent heatwaves and severe droughts across Europe have caused river levels to fall sharply, bringing long-hidden traces of the continent’s past to the surface.

On the Danube in Serbia, one of the latest is the wreck of the *Slovenac*, a 112-year-old tugboat whose hull and interior are now visible above the water. Local residents say they have never seen so much of the vessel revealed at once.

Built in Budapest in 1913, when the city was part of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, the 54-metre steam-powered tug was originally named *Vag*, after the Váh River in present-day Slovakia. It carried heavy cargo between Budapest and other towns along the Danube.

After the First World War and the collapse of Austria-Hungary, the vessel became part of the newly formed Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes and was renamed *Slovenac*. During the Second World War, it transported equipment and wounded fighters after Yugoslav anti-fascist forces retook Belgrade. In April 1945, it struck a German mine while carrying wounded fighters and sank. It has remained at the same location ever since.

The wreck offers a rare, tangible link to the region’s wartime past and is part of a wider pattern across Europe, where receding waters have exposed submerged boats, military equipment and other archaeological remains. With droughts becoming more severe and river levels increasingly erratic, more of these hidden relics could emerge in the years ahead.
