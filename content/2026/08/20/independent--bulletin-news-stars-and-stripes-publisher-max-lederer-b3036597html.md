# Stars and Stripes publisher quits amid ‘fundamental’ reporting differences with Pete Hegseth

- Max D. Lederer Jr., the long-standing publisher of military newspaper Stars and Stripes, announced he will retire at the end of September due to fundamental differences with Pentagon leadership.
- The departure comes amid efforts by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's Department of Defense to overhaul the publication to align with official messaging and eliminate what officials termed woke distractions.
- In the memo, seen by The Associated Press, Lederer wrote that it had "become clear that my philosophy of leadership, and my understanding of the value and mission of Stars and Stripes, differ in fundamental ways from the direction the leadership of the Department of Defense has for the organization."
- In addition to editorial disputes, Lederer expressed concern over Pentagon plans to transition the paper from print to digital platforms, warning it could reduce news access for deployed troops.
- Although Stars and Stripes receives roughly half of its funding from the Pentagon, the historic publication is legally protected by First Amendment principles to maintain editorial autonomy.
