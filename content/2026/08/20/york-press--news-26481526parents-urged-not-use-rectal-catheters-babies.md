Medical bosses have issued a 'do not use' warning on a medical item claimed to relieve babies' gas and digestion issues.

The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency [MHRA] issued a Device Safety Information [DSI] warning on Tuesday, August 18, to parents about the use of rectal catheters on their babies.

The devices, sold online and in shops without a prescription, are used by some parents and caregivers to ease colic, constipation, and trapped wind in infants.

Dr Alison Cave, MHRA chief safety officer, said: "We understand that digestive discomfort and colic can be distressing for babies and their families.


"We're advising against the use of rectal catheters in babies because there is not enough clinical evidence to support their safety and effectiveness.

"Parents and caregivers should seek advice from a healthcare professional and follow the NHS guidance if they're concerned about their baby."

## Why are parents being urged not to use rectal catheters on babies?

Following a review and expert clinical advice, the MHRA has concluded that there is insufficient evidence to support the effectiveness of these products.

The agency is now advising parents, caregivers, and healthcare professionals to stop using rectal catheters on infants and to dispose of any already purchased.

While there have been no reported cases of serious injury, the MHRA warns that using these devices could mask underlying health conditions that need medical attention.


Medical experts have also raised concerns about the risk of injury to infants, including tearing or perforation of the rectum, which could have serious consequences.

The MHRA is encouraging anyone who has experienced a problem with these devices, or who suspects their child has had a side effect, to report it through the MHRA Yellow Card scheme.

This system safeguards the quality and efficacy of medical products in the UK.
