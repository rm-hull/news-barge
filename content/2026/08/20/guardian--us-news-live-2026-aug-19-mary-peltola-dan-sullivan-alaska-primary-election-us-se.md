Republicans reportedly fear datacenter backlash could cost them Ohio Senate seat

Amid signs that the Ohio Republican senator Jon Husted’s support for the AI datacenters Donald Trump touts at every opportunity could cost him his seat, and imperil the Republican majority in the Senate, the National Republican Senatorial Committee (NRSC) has reportedly warned leading AI companies that they need to arrest the public backlash to the massive warehouses springing up across the state.

Husted trailed his Democratic challenger, former senator Sherrod Brown, by eight percentage points in a recent poll for Fox News, and the Republican campaign arm suggested in a memo obtained by Axios and then the Washington Post that Brown is successfully channeling grassroots outrage over the noisy, energy-sucking centers.

“More than any other thing in this race, data centers are the anchor hanging around Husted’s neck,” the NRSC wrote. “If he loses and data centers get the blame, politicians across the country will take notice – and they will not go near the next one.”

“This has become a sleeper issue for the entire election cycle,” the campaign committee added.

A recent ad from Brown’s campaign blames Husted for encouraging the rapid expansion of datacenters in Ohio.

A campaign ad from Ohio Democrat Sherrod Brown blames Republican senator Jon Husted for the growth of unpopular AI datacenters in the state.

Charlie Blaettler, political director of theGuardrails Alliance, a Democratic Super PAC formed to combat “AI billionaire Trump donors trying to buy our elections and prevent common sense regulation”, said in a statement that the memo proves that the Republican party “is panicking because they sold out to the AI industry – a while ago – and they know it”.

Republicans, Blaettler said, “are realizing how wildly unpopular Big AI’s agenda is with the American people. Now the GOP and AI’s unaccountable billionaires are scrambling to backtrack and find a new way to pull the wool over the eyes of the public so they can get what they want: A Congress that will do their bidding and an AI future that benefits them, not the many.”

“It is time for the Democratic party to make it clear who they stand with”, he added. “A handful of Big Tech billionaires or the majority of people in the country who want commonsense regulation for this unprecedented technology.”

When he was asked by a Maga influencer about the public unease with AI datacenters on Wednesday, Trump argued they generate tax revenue and construction jobs, “and many of them are designed in a very beautiful way”.

Republican chair of Senate health committee calls Trump's FDA nominee's role in his 'nonsensical' vaccine order 'almost disqualifying'

Senator Bill Cassidy, the Republican chairman of the Senate health committee, said that he has “strong concerns” about Donald Trump’s nomination of Dr Heidi Overton to be commissioner of the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

Writing on social media, Cassidy, a medical doctor who was outraged by Trump’s executive order last week calling the MMR vaccine “lethal”, said that he respects Dr Overton’s “experience as a physician,” he is concerned her “lack of managerial experience does not prepare her well for leading a large organization that is already dealing with staffing and morale issues.”

“Additionally, Dr. Overton’s active role in last week’s nonsensical vaccine executive order calls into question her commitment to standing up for sound science and protecting children’s health. That alone is almost disqualifying”, Cassidy wrote.

The US debt reached $40tn for the first time on Wednesday, the US treasury department said, after the government deficit doubled over the last decade.

The treasury’s latest debt balance showed $40.047tn on Tuesday afternoon, the highest in US history.

The milestone marks years of government spending that grew under both Donald Trump and Joe Biden. During his first term, Trump approved $8.4tn worth of debt, with a huge chunk going to Covid-19 relief spending, while Biden approved $4.3tn worth of debt, according to the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget.

In fiscal year 2026, which started in October 2026, $1.8tn has been added to the debt so far, with a big chunk of new government spending dedicated to tariff refunds, which have totaled $100bn so far, according to the Bipartisan Policy Center.

Government spending watchdog groups have anticipated for weeks the US surpassing the $40tn threshold after debt reached $39tn in March.

“For perspective, it took nearly 200 years for America’s gross debt to reach $1 trillion for the first time in 1981,” said Maya MacGuineas, president of the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget in a statement. “$40 trillion of debt doesn’t exist solely on the government’s ledgers; it is felt throughout the economy and finds its way to the pocketbooks of people one way or another. The more we borrow, the more we exacerbate inflation, squeeze out other priorities in the budget, and leave ourselves vulnerable to emergencies at home and turmoil abroad.”

The threshold was breached as Congress, currently out of session, remains at an impasse over its latest spending bill. The US House and Senate passed two different bills that would fund the federal government, which is scheduled to shut down on 30 September if a bill isn’t adopted by both chambers and signed by the president.

The US Department of Justice has defended its prosecution of former FBI director James Comey, claiming in new court documents there is “no serious dispute” that his social media post last year featuring a photo of seashells arranged to read “8647” could be interpreted as a threat against Donald Trump.

“There is no serious dispute that an objective viewer of Comey’s post could read it to mean ‘Kill President Trump’,” prosecutors wrote in a document filed on Tuesday in response to efforts by Comey’s efforts to have the case dismissed.

In the photo, the seashells were arranged to read “8647”, which the president’s allies and supporters quickly denounced as a threat against Trump. The number “86” is often used as shorthand to mean to get rid of or remove something, while Trump is the 47th US president.

Comey deleted the photo after the uproar, saying he didn’t know that it could be interpreted as a violent threat. “It never occurred to me, but I oppose violence of any kind so I took the post down,” he said.

In the court documents filed earlier this week, prosecutors with the eastern district of North Carolina continued to argue the post was a “coded message to his followers meaning ‘kill’ or ‘get rid of’ President Trump”.

Comey has denied the charges against him and his lawyers have asked the judge to dismiss the case, arguing that the post was protected speech and did not amount to a “true threat” against the president.

Kim Jong-un’s sister casts doubt on Trump’s claims of secret US-North Korea talks

Andrew Roth

On this subject, the reclusive North Korean leader’s younger sister has cast doubt on communications with DonaldTrump and dampened hopes of a diplomatic breakthrough between them.

Yesterday, Trump suggested he and Kim Jong-un were holding secret negotiations after announcing the US would downgrade its participation in military exercises held by South Korea. Asked whether Kim had responded to his request for a conversation, Trump said: “Yeah, he has.”

As I said earlier, the Wall Street Journal reported yesterday that Trump was pushing aides to set up an in-person meeting with Kim, potentially during a trip to Asia in November for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China.

But today, Kim Yo-jong, the powerful sister of North Korea’s supreme leader, said she knew “absolutely nothing” about the supposed communications, and that while Trump and Kim had an excellent relationship, the US remained an enemy of Pyongyang.

“The United States’ hostile policy toward the DPRK has not changed, and the United States and its followers are vigorously carrying out hostile military activities against the DPRK even at this very moment,” she said in a statement carried by North Korean state media.

She lauded the relationship between her brother and Trump, saying he had “personally good memories and feelings” toward the US president.

But she cast doubt on whether her brother had secretly been in communication with Trump. “Perhaps it is the only matter I do not know about in relation to our supreme leadership’s foreign policy,” she said.
