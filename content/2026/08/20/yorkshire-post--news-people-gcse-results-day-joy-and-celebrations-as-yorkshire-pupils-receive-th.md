Smiles, celebrations and some nervous moments marked GCSE results day as pupils across Yorkshire discovered how they had performed.

Pictures from schools capture students opening their envelopes, comparing grades with friends and celebrating their achievements as they prepare to take their next steps in education, training or employment.

The celebrations come as figures reveal significant regional differences in GCSE performance across England.

In North East England, 17.7 per cent of GCSE entries were awarded grade 7 or above this year, down slightly from 17.8 per cent in 2025 and the lowest proportion of any English region.

However, the North East recorded an increase in entries receiving grade 4 or above, rising from 64.9 per cent last year to 65.2 per cent.

Chris Zarraga, director of Schools North East, said results day should reflect young people's "potential and hard work" rather than the advantages or disadvantages associated with where they live.
