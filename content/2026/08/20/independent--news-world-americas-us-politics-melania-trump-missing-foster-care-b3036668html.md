First Lady Melania Trump made her first public appearance in more than a month as she delivered remarks from the White House to tout her foster care initiative.

“I heard you missed me,” she said as she addressed a crowd at the Rose Garden on Thursday. “Here I am.”

President Donald Trump held her hand as she walked her towards a lectern to deliver her remarks and then patted her on the back.

The first lady has remained largely out of the spotlight with her husband’s return to the White House. She has made even fewer public appearances in the president’s second term than his first; her last public appearance was at the 2026 World Cup final on July 19.

Melania Trump did not join the president to attend the dignified transfer of the remains of four dead U.S. service members, nor did she sit beside the president at the White House Correspondents’ Dinner two days later on July 24. She also did not attend Senator Lindsey Graham’s funeral on July 28.

An analysis from *The Daily Beast* determined Melania Trump, until her appearance at the White House on Thursday, had appeared in public only 38 days this year.

Trump aide Natalie Harp, meanwhile, has seldom strayed far from the president’s side.

The ubiquitous presence of the 35-year-old executive assistant within the president’s inner circle has come under intense scrutiny following reports that she was among a small group of top aides who secretly joined Trump aboard a military aircraft departing a NATO summit in Turkey this summer.

The former One America News Network personality reportedly declined to obtain security clearance for more than a year after she started working at the White House and repeatedly received extensions to complete the paperwork that would require her to disclose her financial history, prior residences and foreign contacts.

Recent remarks from Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff — who said Trump “doesn’t want to do the job” of president but would rather build a highly contentious White House ballroom and “travel with Natalie” — sparked a fierce defense of Harp’s presence from the president’s allies while amplifying questions about her role at the White House.

Harp, who earned her “printer” nickname after routinely handing Trump large-format printouts of news articles and social media posts, has has also reportedly provided the president with deeply personal “devotional letters.”

The first lady — in partnership with IndyCar and the Fox Corporation as the president launches a Grand Prix race in Washington, D.C. — announced the launch of a new scholarship program for young adults transitioning out of foster care.

Her “Fostering the Future” initiative is being supported by a $2 million donation from the companies to fund scholarships at Indiana University and Purdue University, according to the White House. The partnership expands the program’s network to 26 academic institutions.

“America's greatest competitive advantage remains in its own people,” she said in prepared remarks. “Tomorrow’s leaders are sitting in classrooms today, including those growing up in foster care. Their capability is no less remarkable.”
