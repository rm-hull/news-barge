Dallas Cowboys owner Jerry Jones said he is considering a significant change to AT&T Stadium, contemplating the tinting of its west-facing windows.

The potential change follows a directive from FIFA over the summer which mandated the closure of curtains for a World Cup match between Japan and Sweden that was held at the venue, which was renamed Dallas Stadium for the tournament.

Jones had previously rejected any suggestions of using curtains during NFL games despite complaints about the glare from the sun hitting massive glass panels in the west part of the $1.2 billion stadium.

But speaking to *KDFW* this weekend, Jones said, "Well, do you know something? Thinking about it and doing something about it are two different things. ... But I'll think about it.”

![Players and match officials line up before the FIFA World Cup 2026 Semi Final match between France and Spain at Dallas Stadium on July 14, 2026 in Arlington, Texas](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/03/12/53/GettyImages-2286097249.jpg)

"It's hard to tell that time of the year, because the sun's not the same,” he continued. “This time of the year that it is, in the fall. And that is, you get on into — sun changes, and the angle of that sun changes from September through year-round.

“But as we look at it, I like some of the effects of the tinting. And so we're giving that a thought."

The stadium's design, while aesthetically striking, has long presented challenges during late-afternoon games, with sunlight streaming through the end zones and occasionally impairing visibility for players.

A notable instance occurred on November 10, 2024, when Cowboys star wide receiver CeeDee Lamb dropped a crucial touchdown pass during a 34-6 loss to the Philadelphia Eagles, directly attributing the mishap to the glare.

"I couldn't see the ball. Couldn't see the ball, at all. The sun," Lamb recounted at the time.

And at a game last Thanksgiving, Cowboys receiver George Pickens was seen shading his eyes after failing to see a pass thrown his way.

"The sun was beating in my eyes, so I couldn't see," he said after the game.

"He already knew it. Welcome to Dallas, bro," Lamb said of the play.
