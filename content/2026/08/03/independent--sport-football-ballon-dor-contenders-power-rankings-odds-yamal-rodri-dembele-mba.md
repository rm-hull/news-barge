The race for the Ballon d’Or is entering its final stretch after the world’s best players made significant statements during the 2026 World Cup.

Eight-time winner Lionel Messi showed that, at 39, he could still force himself into the conversation, firing eight goals to propel Argentina to the final and briefly take the crown as the competition’s all-time top goalscorer - until he was eclipsed by Kylian Mbappe.

However, Spain’s triumph over Argentina in the finale will surely bode well for the likes of 2024 winner Rodri and Lamine Yamal in their pursuit of the individual accolade, who will look to start the new club season with some spellbinding performances to complement their part in winning football’s most coveted trophy.

Harry Kane was the favourite for the Ballon d’Or before this summer after his sublime goalscoring campaign with Bayern Munich - can he replicate that unparalleled form in the early months of 2026/27 to make team achievements irrelevant come October’s ceremony?

*Here’s how the top 10 front-runners stack up in The Independent’s Ballon d’Or 2026 power rankings:*

**1. Rodri**

You might not like it, but the weight of the World Cup in the eyes of the Ballon d’Or jury is undeniable. Just ask Luka Modric.

Rodri had a sensational World Cup at the heart of Spain’s midfield, shielding their rock-solid defence while acting as the metronome that took play forward. His influence took him to the Golden Ball ahead of top goalscorers Messi and Mbappe - and we feel like that could be the precursor to him bringing home a second Ballon d’Or in October.

That would feel like a bit of an injustice, considering he didn’t have the most spectacular domestic season. Returning from injury with Manchester City, it took him a while to properly find his feet in a campaign that saw Pep Guardiola’s side win the FA Cup and League Cup, but miss out on the Premier League title. But the World Cup is the World Cup - and whether you agree with the importance of team achievements in an individual award, the reality is that it does matter a hell of a lot.

His pursuit of a second crown in three years could hinge on his recovery from minor back surgery, and then a possible move to Real Madrid. If he carries his World Cup form and inspires a lightning start to the season at the Santiago Bernabeu, that spotlight could be what swings the jury in his favour.

**2. Harry Kane**

England captain Kane is still the odds-on favourite to win the Ballon d’Or despite failing to set the World Cup alight quite like he managed the Bundesliga in 2025/26. That’s a testament to just how prolific he was last term.

Kane was the top goalscorer in Europe, hitting 61 goals in 51 games across all competitions as he spearheaded Bayern towards the league and cup double; he’s added silverware to his ever-present individual numbers. He was then hardly irrelevant at the World Cup, scoring six goals - including a crucial late brace against DR Congo to spare his country serious embarrassment - as England secured their best World Cup finish since 1966.

If his club form continues into the new campaign, the jury may have to put their emphasis on the World Cup and wider team achievement to one side.

## 3. Lamine Yamal

![Lamine Yamal has now won both a European Championship and World Cup before turning 20](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/02/13/01KXYC37X5R83GVMHFEEGYD74G.jpg)

And back to the World Cup superiority complex we go, sort of. Yamal will surely win his fair share of Ballon d’Ors across his career, but the 19-year-old could very well end up taking home his first this year, going one better than his runners-up finish in 2025.

Yamal became the first player under 20 to appear at both a European Championship final and a World Cup final - he won both. While his output was pretty modest for his standards, he was influential throughout the tournament for Spain - and that’s after coming into it carrying a hamstring injury.

He also bagged 24 goals and 18 assists across all competitions for Barcelona in another LaLiga-winning season - hardly a mediocre year for the teenager. He’s more than in with a shout.

## 4. Kylian Mbappe

It feels pretty crazy to think that, at 27, Mbappe still doesn’t have a Ballon d’Or to his name, given he’s been at the top of the game for what feels like forever now.

Once again, he’s very much in the mix this time around. His Golden Boot at the World Cup will do most of the heavy lifting, with his 10 goals in North America seeing him pass first Miroslav Klose and then Lionel Messi as the tournament’s all-time record goalscorer. Both he and Messi would have felt hard done by not to go home with the Golden Ball.

But lest we forget, despite the toxicity that surrounded his club season with Real Madrid, he didn’t exactly experience a drop-off in performance. He bagged 42 goals in 44 games, all while being hounded at the Bernabeu - and while it wasn’t enough to prevent another trophyless season for *Los Blancos*, you can’t knock his output.

If he can get along with Jose Mourinho and get 2026/27 off to a flyer, he could well shoot up to the pinnacle of these rankings.

## 5. Lionel Messi

![Lionel Messi, 39, is still in the mix after a stunning World Cup](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/16/09/6135ee5aa6a5ef276b09a937d8424c7fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg0MjcwMjUw-2.85316749.jpg)

He couldn’t again, could he? Messi’s inclusion on this list encapsulates the significance of the World Cup in deciding the Ballon d’Or winner; he’s not going to take the accolade for his MLS exploits, is he. But fair play to the eight-time winner; at 39, he showed he very much still had it.

Messi’s eight goals propelled Argentina to the World Cup final, where they were rightly defeated by Spain - had he lifted back-to-back titles, he’d probably be towards the top two of this list. After all, he claimed the 2023 award after *La Albiceleste’s* triumph 10 months earlier, by which time he was on the books at Inter Miami.

Even with his runners-up medal,***Indy Sport* writers had him as the player of the tournament. That, combined with the jury’s historic love for the Argentinian, means you can’t exactly write him off.

## 6. Ousmane Dembele

![Ousmane Dembele won back-to-back Champions Leagues with PSG](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/02/13/01KSX9AFHZWBPC3M5E1QEV9S6H.jpg)

Had France won the World Cup, as many expected them to do leading up to that drab semi-final exit, Dembele would have been heavily favoured to secure a second successive Ballon d’Or. The combination of World Cup and Champions League winners’ medals is a bit of a cheat code.

That would’ve been quite the gimme for a player who, let’s be honest, was France’s fourth best attacker at the World Cup. And while, like last year, he ended the season as Paris Saint-Germain’s top scorer with a Ligue 1 and Champions League to boot, he only managed about half of his goalscoring output.

His trophy haul still carries enough weight to be comfortably in the top 10 contenders, but the failure to lift that World Cup title gives him only an outside chance to go back-to-back - and we think that’s fair.

Sorry Ousmane, but this year shouldn’t be your year.

## 7. Erling Haaland

He had us all doing the row.

Their first World Cup since 1998, Haaland ensured Norway lived up to their dark-horse status by powering them to the last-eight. On the way, his unrelenting goalscoring exploits humbled Brazil, sending the five-time winners packing. Away from the big-hitting nations, Haaland was far and away the tournament’s standout player, to little surprise.

Add that to a pretty bog-standard Haaland season for City - just the 38 goals last year, the fourth season he’s hit 30+ at the Etihad - which saw him lift a domestic double. Legacy-defining stats for nearly anyone else, but normal service for the big Norwegian.

As prolific as he may be, he’s currently a definite outsider in the Ballon d’Or race. That may change if he emulates the start to the season he had last year, which saw him smack home 13 in his first 10 league outings. Recency bias can be a powerful thing...

## 8. Jude Bellingham

How was this man’s place on the England plane ever in question?! Thomas Tuchel’s player of the tournament, Bellingham served his critics a serious helping of humble pie by guiding the Three Lions to the *elusive* World Cup bronze. 

That beautiful redemption arc, on its own, has Bellingham on this list. That’s because the season that preceded the World Cup wasn’t spectacular. Injuries limited him to just 13 goal contributions for Real Madrid in another trophyless campaign.

It’s why his chances of winning the Ballon d’Or are still low compared to the competition. But as was proved in North America - don’t count him out.

## 9. Michael Olise

Crystal Palace could only keep him in Croydon for so long.

With Bayern Munich and France, Olise has established himself as one of the world’s best attackers, a talent so bright that the German giants are doing all they can to ward off interest from the likes of Real Madrid - who no doubt saw the World Cup partnership he formed with Mbappe and want to reunite the pair in the Spanish capital.

Will his electrifying performances be enough to win the Ballon d’Or? Probably not, this year. But he’s in the conversation, which is a statement in itself.

## 10. Vinicius Jr

![Vinicius Jr could be on the move to Arsenal this summer](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/25/16/0b588d89ca4fe81454704aeecfb234fdY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1MDc3MTY3-2.84806170.jpg)

Vinicius squeezes into our Ballon d’Or power rankings, mainly to spare myself some angry emails from those affiliated to Real Madrid, Brazil, and now maybe Arsenal. I joke, kind of.

The 2024 runner-up had another strong season at the Bernabeu, netting 22 goals in 36 appearances, though like Mbappe was unable to inspire the club to a trophy.

Similar could be said about his World Cup; he was one of the few bright lights in a wooden Brazil side under Carlo Ancelotti, which eventually succumbed to Haaland and co.

Could a move to the Emirates spark life into his Ballon d’Or bid?
