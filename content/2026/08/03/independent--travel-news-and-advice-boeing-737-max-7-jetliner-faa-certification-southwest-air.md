Boeing has finally gotten FAA approval for the smallest variant of its Max jetliner, the 737 Max 7.

The plane can now enter commercial service, a decision that comes nearly a decade after the aircraft's initial unveiling.

The certification marks a significant turning point for Boeing, which has grappled with extensive safety, quality, and production issues for years. The 737 Max 7, a long-awaited addition to Boeing's best-selling 737 family, offers 135 to 160 seats.

The FAA confirmed that its clearance followed years of rigorous testing, design modifications, and a comprehensive evaluation of the aircraft's systems.

The approval process faced considerable delays as Boeing contended with technical problems and intensified regulatory oversight in the wake of two fatal crashes involving the larger Max 8 which claimed 346 lives in 2018 and 2019.

![The first Boeing 737 MAX 7 aircraft sits on the tarmac outside of the Boeing factory on February 5, 2018 in Renton, Washington](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/03/19/47/GettyImages-914592916.jpg)

These tragedies, occurring off the coast of Indonesia and in Ethiopia, prompted the FAA to completely revise its aircraft certification procedures for Boeing.

The agency stated that its scrutiny of the company would persist even after the Max 7's approval. Prior to granting its final endorsement, the FAA mandated several critical safety enhancements from Boeing. These included updates to flight-control software, improved cockpit alerts for pilots, and a redesign of the engine anti-ice system to mitigate overheating risks.

Stephanie Pope, president and CEO of Boeing Commercial Airplanes, acknowledged the arduous journey, stating: "Our team of dedicated engineers and test experts worked through challenges, an extended pandemic, and the transition to new certification processes."

The agency further indicated that safety inspectors would maintain a presence at Boeing's production facilities to oversee manufacturing processes, including the company’s Safety Management System and its broader safety culture.

Boeing has built about 30 Max 7s and nine Max 10s, which are awaiting delivery, according to aviation analytics firm Cirium.

The Max 7 is anticipated to serve primarily short- and medium-haul routes, with several airlines, notably Southwest Airlines, holding outstanding orders for the aircraft.

Southwest Airlines celebrated the approval and said it looks forward to the plane entering service in the coming months, though it does not have the Max 7 on its current schedule, which is through March 2027.
