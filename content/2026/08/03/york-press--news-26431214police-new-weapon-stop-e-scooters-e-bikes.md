Police forces across the UK are using new 'SmartTag' technology as part of a crackdown on e-bikes, e-scooters and motorcycles.

SmartTag – a traceable solution developed by DeterTech UK – can be fired from a handheld canister over distances of up to 10 metres, coating riders, clothing and bikes with an invisible marker.

The spray is only detectable under ultraviolet light and carries a unique forensic code linked to the specific canister used, allowing investigators to match a suspect to a particular incident weeks or even months later.

In Southampton, Hampshire and Isle of Wight Constabulary says the technology gives officers a safer tactical option when dealing with motorcycles, e‑bikes and e‑scooters being used in crime or anti‑social behaviour, reducing the need for risky pursuits or close confrontation.

The move comes amid growing complaints about riders using powered two‑wheelers on pavements and in public spaces, and follows similar trials by Dorset Police and other forces to tag offenders rather than chasing them through busy streets.

North Yorkshire Police has been contacted for comment.

A BBC report in April said Thames Valley Police is now deploying SmartTag spray as part of its own response to the rising criminal use of e‑bikes and e-scooters.

The force has already seized more than 100 illegal machines in recent operations and warned that privately owned e‑scooters used on public roads risk fines, penalty points and confiscation.

According to the report, Thames Valley officers use SmartTag to mark suspects and their vehicles during incidents, with anyone stopped later subject to UV checks to see if they carry the forensic code.

A briefing to the Thames Valley Police and Crime Panel states that the spray remains on skin, clothing and other surfaces for extended periods, strengthening the evidential link between offenders and specific crime scenes.
