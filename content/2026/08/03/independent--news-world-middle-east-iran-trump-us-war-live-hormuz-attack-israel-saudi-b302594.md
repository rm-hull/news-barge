President Donald Trump said that peace talks with Iran will take place on Monday after calling off what he described as World War II-level strikes.

“Obviously they don’t want to be attacked. They knew the extent of the attack because they saw it forming,” Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One on Sunday. “Now what we’re doing is we’re talking to them in the form of a negotiation. It begins tomorrow afternoon.”

His comments followed a Truth Social post on Saturday in which Trump wrote that the U.S. military was “locked and loaded” for what he cast as an unprecedented campaign against Iran. But, he said, he decided to hold back after appeals from Iran and several Middle Eastern allies, who suggested a deal was within reach.

Trump said the agreement would include reopening the Strait of Hormuz and address Iran’s nuclear program.

After the GOP president called off the strikes, Iran’s acting defense minister accused the U.S. of “psychological and cognitive warfare.”

The latest round of fighting erupted after a brief ceasefire collapsed last month, with the passage of ships through the Strait of Hormuz — a vital artery of global trade — emerging as a major sticking point.

Negotiations between Iran and Oman over the critical waterway are in their “final stages”, Iran's foreign ministersaid on Sunday, according to official news agency IRNA.

## Iranian president says MOU will remain 'center of gravity'

On Sunday, before President Trump announced negotiations would take place on Monday, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said the June memorandum of understanding would be the focal point of Iran’s foreign policy.

“I believe this memorandum will be the center of gravity of our foreign relations in the future,” Pezeshkian wrote on X on Sunday, according to a translation. “We must strive to compel the enemy to remain committed to what it has signed. The security of the country, the region, and our allies will be enhanced by this memorandum.”

## Pete Hegseth says US military remains 'ready to go'

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the U.S. military remains primed for action against Iran after President Trump called off this weekend’s planned attacks.

Writing on X on Sunday night, Hegseth said the Pentagon “was ready to go — and remains ready — at levels not seen since World War 2.”

On Sunday, President Donald Trump said that peace talks with Iran would take place on Monday after calling off planned strikes this weekend.

## What has Trump said about the potential deal?

Donald Trump says he will order American forces to hold off on new strikes against Iran, claiming Mideast allies have reached the parameters of a deal to end the 5-month old war.

The emerging deal "would include the Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat," Trump wrote in a Saturday evening social media post.

"Based on this request, I have agreed, for the future benefit of the WORLD and, likewise, the survival of a successful and prosperous Iran, to cancel the attack, subject to being able to rapidly make a DEAL," Trump said.

## Inside Iran’s Pickaxe Mountain, the secret nuclear site Trump has threatened to bomb

Donald Trump has issued threats to bomb a secretive under-construction Iranian nuclear site buried deep under a mountain.

The site, known as Kuh-e Kolang Gaz La, or “Pickaxe Mountain,” lies on the south side of Iran's Natanz nuclear enrichment site, which was bombed by the United States during Operation Midnight Hammer in 2025's 12-day war between Iran and Israel. But Pickaxe Mountain has remained untouched as Iran continues to dig at the site.

It remains unclear just what has been placed at Pickaxe Mountain, as the United Nations' nuclear watchdog has never gained access to the site.

## Palestinians in Gaza hope Hamas will disarm but fear Israeli forces won't withdraw

For Palestinians in Gaza, President Donald Trump's announcement that Hamas had agreed to disarm was a small sign of hope that Israel's attacks might end, allowing them to rebuild their lives and what remains of their homes.

But it also clashed with the reality on the ground, where Israeli strikes have continued since Trump first touted the deal late Thursday in Washington, and where few steps have been taken to advance a broader ceasefire agreement that went into effect in October.

## Saudis concerned about Iran retaliation

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, the kingdom's de facto leader, spoke with Trump by phone Saturday and raised concerns about the US potentially escalating the conflict with Iran, according to the Saudi press agency.

The Saudis, according to a person briefed on the substance of the call but not authorized to comment publicly, are concerned that Tehran could respond with attacks on the energy infrastructure of the kingdom and other Gulf countries.

The call happened before Trump's social media announcement, the person said.The crown prince sought clarity from Trump on what potential new action he was weighing to take against Iran, the person said.
