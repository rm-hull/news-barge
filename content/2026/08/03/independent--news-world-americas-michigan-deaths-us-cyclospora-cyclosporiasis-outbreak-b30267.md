Michigan health officials just reported the first two deaths from the state’s ongoing outbreak of the explosive diarrhea-causing foodborne illness cyclosporiasis, marking the first fatalities reported in the U.S. since the surge of cases began earlier this year.

The deceased patients had “significant underlying health conditions that may have been impacted by cyclosporiasis and dehydration,” according to the state’s Department of Health and Human Services.

“No additional information will be provided on these two cases,” it said Monday.

Michigan is the state with the largest number of cases nationwide, and officials reported there were 11,234 sickened and nearly 200 people hospitalized so far.

Cases in the Great Lakes state and eight others - including West Virginia, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Kentucky, Kansas, Indiana and Illinois - have been tied to contaminated lettuce.

That outbreak is the largest in the nation and led to salad-maker Taylor Farms’ recall of iceberg lettuce sourced from Central Mexico. The lettuce had been served at several locations of the fast-food chain Taco Bell and the recall also included lettuce that was sold at retail stores.

There are now several outbreaks reported nationwide, but questions still swirl surrounding sources of exposure.

Some cases in North Carolina have been linked to parsley and cilantro, state health officials said.

There is also a lag in the number of cases confirmed by federal health officials.

While the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has received 6,707 lab-confirmed cases of cyclosporiasis since May 1 and is aware of more than 11.500 additional cases that are not lab-confirmed, it only shows Michigan with 1,000-4,000 cases on its surveillance webpage.

The agency says there is a six-week reporting lag between illness onset and case reporting, and it anticipates that case counts will continue to rise.
