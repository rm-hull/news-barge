Reformers member may have allegedly tried to ‘hit up’ MPs for public money, inquiry hears

Penry Buckley

Returning to the NSW Icac’s Operation Rosny: Reformers member Robert Assaf has been giving evidence about the breakdown of his friendship with fellow Reformer Christian Ellis.

Two blockbuster exhibitions dedicated to the late David Hockney are opening in Australia later this year, months after the British artist’s death at the age of 88.

CSIRO confirms first mass mortality event from H5 bird flu

Lisa Cox

Tests by the CSIRO have confirmed Australia’s first mass mortality event from the deadly H5 bird flu and the environment minister, Murray Watt, has warned more mass wildlife deaths will occur as the disease continues to spread.

Watt told a media conference in Canberra:

I want to level with people: this is the beginning of what is likely to be a long, difficult journey in terms of the impacts on wildlife.

The mass deaths of greater crested terns were announced in South Australia over the weekend, with 49 dead terns and 35 unwell terns detected by aerial surveillance.

Samples from some of those animals were sent to the CSIRO for verification and have come back positive.

Watt said:

The reality is, you can’t stop native and wild birds flying around, and you can’t stop wild animals moving around, and that will mean more mass mortality events like the one we have now seen.

What we can do, and what we have been doing, is build up the resilience of the populations of our native birds and mammals.

Agriculture minister, Julie Collins, said Australia now had 74 confirmed H5N1 cases – 54 in SA, 10 in Western Australia, seven in Victoria, two in New South Wales and one in Queensland.

Angus [McCaffrey] wasn’t part of it. NSW’s new toll deal to cost taxpayers now, but not the toll company

Luca Ittimani

New South Wales taxpayers will foot a $143m bill for one-off cuts to tollways while the multinational operator faces no net cost.

The operator, Transurban, said the reforms were neutral for its prospects and its share price held steady in early trading. Transurban expects the state to pay $600m in equalisation payments and cover the cost of the M7-M2 widening, while it and its co-investors in the M2 and the eastern distributor will repay the state $600m earlier than previously agreed.

The NSW transport minister, John Graham, said the reforms will cost the state a net $143m over the next five year, even as the companies say they face no net cost. When asked what that included, Graham told reporters:

The accounting is complex here, but that’s the budgeting.

He also acknowledged the one-off 10% reduction to tolls on a few motorways “will eventually” be offset by the operators’ regular increases to tolls.

Chris Minns, the premier, denied this implied taxpayers were footing the bill for the deal. He told reporters:

We need toll relief now and it’s better to have it at a lower base given the multiple increases that they have through the year.

The government already spends over $100m a year to foot the bill when a motorist spends more than $60 each week on tolls.

Refugee advocates call for reforms as Bikram Lama’s body returned to Nepal

Refugee advocates are calling for reforms to protect non-residents trapped in homelessness as the body of Bikram Lama returns to his family in Nepal.

Lama, a young Nepalese student, died in Hyde Park in December while sleeping rough, prompting a widespread outpouring of support and calls for change to systemic barriers preventing non-residents from accessing key housing services.

The Refugee Council of Australia says temporary visa holders, including those seeking asylum, were making up a growing proportion of those sleeping rough. The council is calling for practical solutions such as expanding access to rent assistance, bond loans and other housing support.

Senior policy officer Dr Shaheen Whyte said Lama’s death highlighted the devastating consequences of leaving people without an adequate safety net.

Whyte said:

Like anyone else, non-residents and people seeking refugee protection can experience job loss, illness, family violence and housing stress. A fair homelessness system responds to those in need in times of crisis, regardless of where they are from or what visa they are on.

For too long community organisations have been leading the fight on this. It’s time for state and federal governments to step up and take action. Community organisations are doing everything they can by providing food, clothing, blankets and referrals to healthcare,but they cannot provide long-term accommodation, or the financial support people need to get back on their feet.

Lama’s body has been stuck in Australia for almost eight months after his death. But his family were expecting it to arrive back in Kathmandu over the weekend, allowing them to perform last rites.

Sydney road tolls to fall under state government deal

Tolls are set to fall on some Sydney motorways after the operator, Transurban, reached a long-awaited deal with the New South Wales government.

Motorbike tolls will be halved on all motorways gradually, starting from July 2027.

Tolls will fall 10% on 1 July 2027 for the Lane Cove Tunnel and “longer journeys” on the M2. The cross city tunnel’s toll will fall 20% when the new western harbour tunnel opens, scheduled for 2028.

M7 trip fees at present stop rising after 20km. That cap will fall to 18km from 1 January 2028, pending a deal to widen the motorway.

The eastern distributor motorway is set to bring in tolls in both directions from when the western harbour tunnel opens, but with north and south tolls at just over half of the current northbound level.

The government has previously promised to bring in two-way tolling on to the harbour bridge as well. The promised 4% toll hike from 1 July 2027 for the Sydney Harbour Bridge and the harbour tunnel has been trimmed to 3.25%.
