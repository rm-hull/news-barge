Myspace could be making a comeback in the latest grab at Millennial nostalgia.

Millennials, born between 1981 and 1996, are well into adulthood now, and many yearn to go back to simpler times, when their biggest problem was picking who would be in their Top 8 friends list on the once-popular Myspace site.

As the internet was first becoming a household commodity, teens would sit at boxy computers, updating their profiles with their latest music obsession on Myspace – which was then styled as MySpace. The platform, created by Tom Anderson and Chris DeWolfe in 2003, was once the hottest social media platform, reaching over 22 million members just two years later as its rapid growth continued. But it was overtaken by Mark Zuckerberg’s Facebook before 2010.

For those wishing to rewind the clock and get back on Myspace, preferably with an updated profile photo, the current owners of the social media platform, Viant Technology’s Chris and Tim Vanderhook, have announced a planned site revival.

“We still own Myspace. We are stewards of the Myspace brand at this point, and we are going to relaunch Myspace,” Tim said in filmmaker Tommy Avallone’s latest documentary, *Myspace*, according to* People*. “We’re just waiting for the right time to do it. And if that one doesn’t work, we’ll do it again.”

![Myspace could be coming back —again — in the latest grab at Millennial nostalgia](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/03/18/39/GettyImages-74208581.jpg)

A possible relaunch date has yet to be announced. *The Independent* has reached out to Viant Technology for comment. 

Myspace’s ownership has changed hands a couple of times since it was created. Viant, then called Specific Media, bought the platform from News Corp in 2011 for $35 million, with singer Justin Timberlake as a key investor, according to reports.

“For us, it was all about the artist community that was the heartbeat that we saw, that we wanted to resuscitate,” Tim said in the documentary.

But the Vanderhook brothers then ran into multi-million-dollar problems.

“Everything that could go wrong, goes wrong. Every advertiser cancels. They like Facebook,” Tim said.

Chris told Avallone, “We lost a little over $150 million.”

![Viant Technology’s Chris, left, and Tim Vanderhook, the current owners of the popular early 2000s social media platform, announced the revival, teasing, ‘We’re just waiting for the right time to do it’](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/03/18/30/GettyImages-170435767.jpg)

In 2019, Myspace also suffered from a public blunder when an estimated 50 million songs vanished from the platform after a server migration project went awry.

“As a result of a server migration project, any photos, videos, and audio files you uploaded more than three years ago may no longer be available on or from Myspace,” a statement on the platform read.

Myspace was known for helping artists such as Arctic Monkeys and Adele get their start.

Whether the Vanderhook brothers will lean into music again or take Myspace in a totally new direction is unclear. But a revival is sure to spark fond memories in former members and may even bring in new generations looking to kick it old school.
