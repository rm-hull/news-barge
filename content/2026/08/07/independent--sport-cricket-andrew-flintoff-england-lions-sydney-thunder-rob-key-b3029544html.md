Andrew Flintoff has stepped down as head coach of the England Lions as the coaching upheaval at the ECB continues.

Flintoff, one of England’s greatest all-rounders, had spent nearly two years in charge of the second-string side after being persuaded into coaching by Rob Key, the ECB’s managing director of men’s cricket.

Well liked by the players, Flintoff had drawn plaudits for his work in developing some of the brightest young talents in England, but is due to take up a new role with the Sydney Thunder in Australia’s Big Bash this winter.

“Unfortunately I’ve made the decision to step away from my role with the Lions. Not only have I enjoyed my time having the opportunity to work with the best young cricketers in the country, but have taken great pride in seeing them develop.

“I’d like to thank the coaches and staff for all their passion, tireless work and support they give everyday. In particular, Ed Barney who I’ve loved working with and was so lucky to have as a performance director, and obviously Rob (Key) for trusting me with one of the most important roles in English cricket.

“I can’t wait to get started with the Thunder later in the year and see where coaching takes me.”

![Andrew Flintoff (centre) has proved a popular coach](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/01/13/36ffe2358f020a6c770b14af5a52a980Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzUxNDU2NjAw-2.80862554.jpg)

His exit follows the removal of Brendon McCullum as Test head coach, with fellow New Zealander Stephen Fleming appointed. McCullum continues to oversee England’s white-ball teams, while Marcus Trescothick is in interim charge of the Test team for the upcoming Pakistan series.

While no replacement for Flintoff has yet been confirmed, Mike Yardy – the former Sussex and England all-rounder – will step in for the four-day Professional County Club Select XI match against Pakistan, which begins at Beckenham on Wednesday 12 August.

Flintoff’s departure comes just a week after Key had praised his coaching talent highly, and suggested he could well be a contender to be England coach in the future.

"It [the Test vacancy] just came too soon, but he should be someone that goes on to become an outstanding head coach,” Key said of his former teammate. “When you talk about who could be the best person, Andrew Flintoff - in the future, with a bit more experience under him - could do it, and him not being there at times in the Lions gives opportunity for other coaches, and it's for us to choose who comes in to do that.

"I think he's an unsung hero. A lot of these young players that are coming through, you think how hard it is when they've got T10s, T20s, ILT20, SA20. We've got to go to a player and say, 'We want you to come and commit to a red-ball tour of Australia, we want you to come and play 50-over cricket in Abu Dhabi'.

"Having Andrew Flintoff there has made a massive, massive difference in being able to do that, and I think he deserves the respect and the thanks for doing it."

Flintoff, known as “Freddie”, has forged a successful career as a television host and personality since his retirement from playing. He reached a settlement with the BBC after suffering serious injuries following a crash while filming *Top Gear* in 2022.
