An East Yorkshire pub that is famous for being the first pub artist David Hockney got drunk in has gone on the market.

Christie & Co is seeking £850,000 for the freehold of the Wolds Inn at Huggate, between Stamford Bridge and Driffield.

The agents report “strong trade” from walkers and cyclists, 80 covers inside, four en-suite letting rooms, generating a turnover in 2025 of £700,000.

The well-regarded venue, built in 1592, receives 4.6 stars out of five on Google, based on 521 Google Reviews. Tripadvisor awards 4.0 stars based on 326 reviews.

The sales particulars said: “The Wolds Inn is a traditional, characterful 16th‑century public house offering a warm atmosphere, home‑cooked food and quality accommodation.

  The exterior of the Wolds Inn at Huggate, East Yorkshire. 

*(Image: Righmove)*

“Known for its locally sourced pub classics, rotating specials, and friendly service, it is a central social hub within the village. Trading is supported by strong food demand, traveller stays, destination diners, and a consistent flow of walkers and cyclists.”

The inn is situated in the highest village in the Wolds and “occupies a prominent roadside position, attracting locals, tourists, walkers and cyclists year‑round.”

There is a public bar, dining areas and four en-suite guest bedrooms. A spacious beer garden has patio seating and a lawned area. A new pizza oven is boosting trade and there is a large car park.

  One of the four en-suite bedrooms. 

*(Image: Rightmove)*

Christie & Co said: “The Wolds Inn presents an excellent opportunity to acquire a thriving food‑led country inn with rooms in a highly desirable walking and cycling destination.

“Future operators could increase revenue through making even greater use of the land and maybe implementing other accommodation methods (subject to planning).”

  The interior of the Wolds Inn, Huggate, East Yorkshire. 

*(Image: Rightmove)*

The agents also suggested future owners could boost the inn’s social media reach and stage more events.

They added: “The inn trades with strong food and beverage income, supported by accommodation revenue from its four en‑suite rooms.

“Reviews across major platforms reflect consistently positive guest feedback. Detailed trading information can be made available to serious parties following a formal viewing.”
