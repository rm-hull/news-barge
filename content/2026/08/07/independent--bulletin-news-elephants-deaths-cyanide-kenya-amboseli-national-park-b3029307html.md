Fifteen elephants have died in Kenya's Amboseli National Park from suspected cyanide poisoning after consuming tomatoes from nearby farms.

The Kenya Wildlife Service is investigating the source of the cyanide, with preliminary tests confirming its presence and post-mortem examinations finding tomatoes in the elephants' stomachs.

Local farmers in the Kimana area, bordering Amboseli, dispute claims that their produce is responsible, stating they have never encountered cyanide among regulated agricultural chemicals.

The deaths highlight escalating human-wildlife conflict in the region due to expanding agricultural activities, despite farmers coexisting with elephants and suffering significant crop losses without compensation.

The International Fund for Animal Welfare (IFAW) has called for an urgent investigation and the implementation of coexistence solutions, emphasising the need for strict control of toxic substances.
