Assistant referee Sian Massey-Ellis, a trailblazer in English football officiating, has announced her retirement from the touchline after a career she described as "beyond anything I could ever have imagined".

Her departure marks the end of a distinguished Premier League tenure that began in 2010, encompassing over 150 matches.

Massey-Ellis is set to transition into a new role with the Football Association, taking up the position of senior manager for women referees.

Her career has been marked by numerous pioneering achievements; she was only the second female official to work in English top-flight matches and remains the sole woman to have been part of an FA Cup final officiating team, serving as assistant VAR in 2021 and again slated for 2025.

Her significant contributions to football officiating and commitment to representation were recognised with an MBE in 2016. She also officiated at the FIFA Women’s World Cups in 2019 and 2023, further cementing her international standing.

Reflecting on her remarkable journey, Massey-Ellis stated: "From standing beneath the Premier League sign before my very first match to officiating at Fifa World Cups, Uefa European Championships, Champions League finals and across domestic fixtures and competitions in England, football has taken me on a journey beyond anything I could ever have imagined. Every opportunity has been a privilege and one I have never taken for granted."

She continued: "Throughout my career, football has challenged me, tested my resilience and pushed me to become a better professional and person. While there have been unforgettable highs and difficult moments, what I will treasure most are the people and experiences that have defined my journey.

“I am incredibly grateful to Pro Ref, The FA, Fifa and Uefa for the opportunities they gave me, as well as every coach, mentor, colleague and match official I have had the privilege of working alongside. Above all, I owe an enormous debt of thanks to my family, whose unwavering support, sacrifices and belief made every achievement possible."

Looking ahead to her new chapter, Massey-Ellis expressed her desire to inspire the next generation. "Although I am stepping away from the touchline, I am not stepping away from football. If my journey has helped change perceptions, open doors or inspire even one young person to believe they belong in the game, then I will consider that one of my greatest achievements. I’m excited by the opportunity to help support, develop and inspire the next generation of match officials, giving something back to a sport that has done so much for me."

She concluded with a poignant message: "As I prepare for one final walk onto the pitch, I leave with nothing but gratitude. It has been the greatest honour to serve the game I love and represent English officiating. To the young girl who simply loved refereeing and dreamed of reaching the very top – we did it. Thank you, football."
