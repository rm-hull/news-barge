Salmon DNA skincare treatments are still trending in the beauty world, with polynucleotides (or PDRN, also referred to as polydeoxyribonucleotide) now available for topical use as well as in-clinic aesthetic procedures. PDRN skincare claims to be regenerative, helping to improve hyperpigmentation and fine lines – and it’s appearing in everything from serums to creams.

As a 42-year-old beauty journalist, I closely track the latest innovations. But above all else, I favour affordable products that offer tangible results. So, I was excited to hear about The Ordinary’s lacto-PDRN 4% + B9 eye cream launch, particularly as its price is less than £9.

The Lacto-PDRN component is vegan, and made by fermentation of a probiotic bacterium. The Ordinary says its DNA is broken down into fragments sharing structural components with salmon DNA, so the skin also responds in a similar way. It’s billed as promoting smoother-looking skin, and improving the appearance of fine lines, wrinkles and elasticity.

I don’t tend to use eye cream, and instead stick to a dab of intensely hydrating moisturiser for my sensitive, dry (and often dull) skin. So, could The Ordinary’s PDRN eye cream impress me? Officially launching on 28 August, IndyBest has scooped the exclusive to be the first to share all about it with you. I’ve spent several weeks putting the affordable eye cream to the test – keep reading for my honest verdict.

Presented in a small 15ml tube, the eye cream squeezes out easily. It’s a rich formula that can be dotted onto the delicate under-eye area and glides across skin with a glossy effect to begin with. It’s a gentle layer that creates an even effect and I noted this made my eye area look fresher and smoother.

I found that a little bit goes a long way with this eye cream and was heavy-handed with application at first, so it took quite a bit of rubbing in. Because of that, I’d recommend applying a small amount at a time. It’s buildable if you need to add more, too.

As well as the lacto-PDRN, this eye cream contains folic acid (or vitamin B9), which is known for its moisturising properties and supporting the skin’s natural barrier. Eye creams often cause irritation to my sensitive skin, with reactions ranging from redness to sore patches meaning I’ve discontinued use. So, I was pleased to note the skin around my eyes tolerated this formula well. It feels comforting to apply, and is gently nourishing.

I have dry skin, and that can mean fine lines are more visible when my face is particularly parched. This moisturising product smoothed my fine lines, temporarily improving their appearance with the film-like layer it left. This thin invisible layer is thanks to the inclusion of a polysaccharide-based element (or ‘film former technology’ as The Ordinary calls it) made up of caesalpinia spinosa gum and kappaphycus alvarezil extract. Polysaccharides promote hydration and skin barrier support, further adding to the eye cream’s smoothing benefits too.

On bare-face days, I saw an awakened, rejuvenated look, with the eye cream blending seamlessly with the moisturiser and SPF I added on top. When wearing make-up, I noticed that the eye cream acted like a primer, providing a smooth base underneath concealer and eyeshadow.

It’s hard to gauge long-term results after just three weeks of testing, but I’m pleased with the initial skincare benefits. The radiance and hydration I’ve seen so far are what I always look for when trying new skincare buys. With so many products from The Ordinary a staple in my daily routine – I swear by the moisturising factors + phytoceramides moisturiser – I will definitely keep using this eye cream. Plus, at £8.70, this is an affordable way to try PDRN if the ingredient is currently piquing your interest.
