At least six people, including a teacher and pupils, have been confirmed dead in a school shooting in Thailand on Friday, authorities said.

The shooting happened at around 10am local time at the Debsirin Nonthaburi School in the Bang Kruai district of Nonthaburi province, located just outside Bangkok.

As the shooting unfolded, police urged people to hide and wait for the authorities, saying an active shooting incident was underway and that the attacker was believed to be inside a school computer room.

Thai police later said six people had died and at least 15 were injured, including two who remain in critical condition. The suspected gunman died by suicide, Lt Col Dechrapee Kongdee, Nonthaburi Provincial Police Commander, told Reuters. It was not immediately clear whether the shooter was included in the overall death toll.

"Who is still stuck there? Hide securely," the police wrote on their Facebook page as the attack took place.

A video, also shared to the police’s Facebook handle, showed students crouching under desks inside a classroom as gunfire rang out in the background.

Pictures showed students running out of the school in panic as ambulances arrived on the scene. A picture also showed one person lying on a stretcher outside an ambulance while a medic attended to another person.

An 18-year-old student said he first thought firecrackers were going off or someone was banging an object.

"I didn't think it was a gun at first," he told Reuters. "There were many shots: bang bang bang. Then it went quiet. Then it started again."

The suspect, reportedly a ninth-grade student, barricaded himself on the third floor of the school's building, according to a local media report.

As the incident unfolded, police urged the public and media to stop livestreaming the incident and evacuated the teachers and students.

Friday’s incident is the second major school shooting in Thailand this year. In February, a 17-year-old entered Patongprathankiriwat School in Hat Yai district, Songkhla province after stealing a firearm from a police officer.

The student shot at the school director and a female student, briefly taking several teachers and students hostage. The school director died, and the shooter was eventually detained by police.

Thailand has strict gun laws, with illegal possession of firearms punishable with up to 10 years in jail. However, the country is believed to have more than 10 million privately-owned guns in circulation, according to data by the University of Sydney’s School of Public Health – one of the highest gun ownership rates in the region.
