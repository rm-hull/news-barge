The police watchdog has announced that it is investigating “alleged failures of Met Police officers in their management” of serial sex attacker Simon Levy after he was convicted of murdering two women and raping a third.

Levy’s crime spree spanned from 2018 to 2025 and included a number of sex attacks on women in public spaces and two killings, as the Metropolitan Police, the British Transport Police (BTP) and the courts failed to stop him.

The Met, BTP and Crown Prosecution Service have all admitted failings in how they handled Levy, who was on bail as his attacks on women escalated to double murder.

Deputy Assistant Commissioner Kevin Southworth, from the Met, said failures to remand Levy in custody at hearings at Highbury Corner Magistrates’ Court in May and June 2025 left him free to kill a second time.

The Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) confirmed on Friday that an investigation into alleged failures of Met Police officers in their management of Levy was “ongoing”.

The IOPC spokesperson said: “Our independent investigation began in January following a voluntary conduct referral from the force, relating to alleged failures by two officers in his management following his prison release on licence in February 2023.

“We are investigating the management of Simon Levy from his first conviction in September 2021 up to his arrest in September 2025.

“We are examining whether action could or should have been taken to prevent his reoffending, and if all officers involved in his management followed policies, procedures, and training.

“We are also assessing whether any learning, whether individual, force-wide, or nationally or other changes should be implemented in light of the events under investigation.

“At this stage, we have served a gross misconduct notice on a police constable and a misconduct notice on a detective sergeant, which means that the actions of those officers are under investigation.

![Sheryl Wilkins, 39, was killed in High Road, Tottenham, on August 24 last year (Family handout/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/07/15/04f33e8b4bf58700ecebeba04fdba2b8Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1NTEwNjMy-2.82544702.jpg)

“The serving of conduct notices does not necessarily mean disciplinary proceedings will follow.”

Levy, 40, of Beaufoy Road, Tottenham, north London, was found guilty at the Old Bailey on Friday of murdering Carmenza Valencia-Trujillo, 53, in March 2025 and Sheryl Wilkins, 39, in August that year.

Levy was also found guilty of two counts of rape, grievous bodily harm with intent and the non-fatal smothering of a third woman whose collarbone was broken during a brutal sex assault in January 2025 in the same car park where Ms Wilkins was later found dead.
