# Trump hosts mining CEOs amid worries over depleting weapons stockpiles

- U.S. President Donald Trump is convening executives from leading mining companies at the State Department to strengthen domestic and allied supplies of critical minerals, a key component of the nation's security and industrial strategy.
- The administration seeks to replenish weapons stockpiles, depleted during the Iran conflict, and reduce U.S. dependence on Chinese supply chains for essential minerals like rare earths, tungsten, and germanium, which are vital for advanced military systems.
- The gathering will include approximately 200 participants, featuring executives from companies such as Resolution Copper, MP Materials, and Century Aluminum, alongside high-ranking officials including Interior Secretary Doug Burgum and Secretary of State Marco Rubio.
- To address a shortage of skilled labor in the mining sector, the Department of Energy will host representatives from U.S. mining schools and announce $180 million in grants to boost educational programs and encourage more students to pursue mining careers.
- The administration has previously established a $12 billion strategic minerals stockpile, supported investments in U.S. mines and processing facilities, and aimed to limit defense contractors' reliance on Chinese supplies to counter Beijing's extensive dominance in global mineral production.
