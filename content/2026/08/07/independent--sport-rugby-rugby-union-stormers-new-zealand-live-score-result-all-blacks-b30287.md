New Zealand embark on their tour of South Africa as the All Blacks take on the Stormers in Cape Town.

Dave Rennie’s side will play eight matches across the next few weeks, including four Tests against the Springboks, as part of the new “Rugby’s Greatest Rivalry” reciprocal touring agreement. These tour games offer an opportunity for the new head coach to examine his depth after New Zealand showed promising signs during the Nations Championship.

They will know there is a mighty challenge awaiting them against the world champions – and perhaps tonight, too. The Stormers possess a good blend of youth and experience, and reached the semi-finals of last year’s United Rugby Championship. Can they strike an early blow on the tourists to start the landmark series in style? Or will the All Blacks show their class?

## All Blacks forced into late change

Ah – a real blow for Wallace Sititi, scratched late from the All Blacks team. In comes Anton Segner to the openside, with Peter Lakai moving across to No 8.

## Experienced heads

Longer in the tooth than Sititi but nonetheless looking to stake a claim to starting shirts might be Beauden Barrett, Anton Lienert-Brown and Rieko Ioane in the All Blacks backline. Ioane was not part of the Nations Championship squad after his year at Leinster, but joins his fellow veterans in getting a chance to fire in a New Zealand side that will be backed again to play with ball in hand.

Barrett’s role over the next year or so will be fascinating as Dave Rennie builds towards a World Cup. Ruben Love was rock solid, but perhaps not spectacular, in July, and you can’t buy 144 Tests worth of experience. A start at some stage in the Springboks series feels a certainty, but Barrett will still have designs on the No 10 shirt in the longer term.

## Wallace Sititi hoping to force his way back into Test mix

New Zealand back rower Wallace Sititi said it had been tough to be dropped from the team for their last Test match – but is aware that he had to be at his best to get back his place in the side.

The 23-year-old was a surprise omission from the All Blacks side who hammered Ireland 40-21 in Auckland in the last of their three Nations Championshipmatches last month. Sititi had started the previous week against Italy but, despite a comfortable 47-17 victory in Wellington, made too many mistakes for coach Dave Rennie’s liking.

The new All Blacks coach said he had made it clear toSititi what he needed to fix, but when asked on Monday if he had a clear idea of what needed to be done to get back in the side, the Chiefs forward evaded the question. “Look, I'm just trying to enjoy the footing; playing at my best is the focus,” Sititi said – though this feels a real opportunity to impress before Ardie Savea arrives on tour.

## Team news – New Zealand

Patrick Tuipulotu captains New Zealand from the second row with Ardie Savea rested from the start of this tour and Codie Taylor not involved. Tuipulotu partners Fabian Holland, a welcome returnee at lock, while Simon Parker, Peter Lakai and Wallace Sititi form a mobile loose forward trio.

Beauden Barrett gets an opportunity to impress having been out of favour during the Nations Championship, while the uncapped Siale Lauaki and Josh Jacomb will wear an All Blacks shirt for the first time as tighthead and fly-half cover respectively.
