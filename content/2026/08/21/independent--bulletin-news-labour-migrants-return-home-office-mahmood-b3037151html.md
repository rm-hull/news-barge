# Millions spent by UK government to get migrants to return home

- Labour has spent almost £40m on an assisted voluntary returns scheme that pays migrants up to £3,000 to leave the UK.
- Since taking power in July 2024, Labour has significantly ramped up the programme, with 17,346 migrants accepting the offer by March.
- The Home Office defended the scheme, noting that an average voluntary return costs £4,000 compared to £48,000 for a forced return and £158,000 a year to house a family in an asylum hotel.
- Shadow Home Secretary Chris Philp criticised the government, arguing Labour should focus on stopping illegal arrivals rather than paying migrants to leave.
- The Refugee Council supported the voluntary returns process, saying it treats individuals with dignity while offering a far cheaper alternative to forced deportation.
