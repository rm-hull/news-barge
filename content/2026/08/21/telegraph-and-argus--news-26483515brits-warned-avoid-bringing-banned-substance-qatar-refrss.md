Brits travelling to Qatar risk jail if they bring banned medication into the country.

The Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office (FCDO) updated its travel advice on Wednesday, August 19, reminding travellers that some prescription and over-the-counter medicines are considered controlled substances under Qatari law.

This includes codeine, tramadol, diazepam and pregabalin.

Qatar will host the penultimate round of the Formula 1 Championship in November, with tens of thousands of visitors expected.

The FCDO advice states: "Some prescribed and over-the-counter medicines may be controlled substances in Qatar."

Travellers can bring these medications into the country, but only if they follow Qatar’s legal requirements.

## How to bring medication into Qatar legally

The FCDO now advises holidaymakers to carry a prescription in English or Arabic, issued within the past six months.

Visitors must apply for a permit through the Qatar Ministry of Public Health’s electronic portal before travelling.

Applicants can track their application online and receive a digital permit to present at the border.


Failing to follow these rules could result in up to five years in prison and a fine of between 50,000 and 100,000 Qatari riyals (approximately £10,000 to £19,000).

This applies even if the drugs are intended solely for personal medical use.

Qatari law states: "NDDPS [Narcotic Drugs and Dangerous Psychotropic Substances] may only be imported, exported or transported, by a written permission from the Minister of the Public Health.

"Whoever imports NDDPS with the intention of illicit consumption or personal use, unless he proves to be permitted to do so pursuant to the provisions of this Law, shall be liable to imprisonment for a term of up to five years and not less than two years.
