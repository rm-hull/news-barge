Jaafar Jackson has said he hopes the proposed *Michael* sequel will address the sexual abuse allegations against Michael Jackson from the singer’s “point of view.”

Jackson, 30, portrayed his uncle in the divisive biopic about the “King of Pop,” which was released in April and proved a hit at the box office as it grossed over a billion dollars worldwide.

The film ends in 1987, years before the accusations that dogged the later years of the musician’s life were made public. A sequel dealing with the rest of his story is reportedly currently in development.

In a new interview with *GQ Middle East*, Jackson said that while he is not personally involved in writing the screenplay he does hope that “the second film will give even more insight from his [Michael’s] point of view, because at the end of the day, [the allegations] are always from someone else’s.”

He continued: “It’s someone else’s story. It’s someone else’s idea of what it is, and it’s mainly driven by an agenda. And that’s really the issue we’ve been having as a family – not having Michael’s voice being heard. I think that’s very, very important, especially in the late years.”

![Jaafar Jackson as Michael Jackson in 'Michael'](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/04/20/12/34/MICHAEL_KM2_0106R10C5.jpeg)

Jackson went on to say that he believes splitting his uncle’s story into two films will allow for a deeper understanding of his character, arguing: “This first film focuses on Michael from his roots. So you get that idea of who he was before he had all the success and became the icon that people are familiar with, you understand who he was as a human being first, because at the end of the day, that’s who he was in the ’90s until he passed.”

He continued: “He never changed that purity, that sense of always wanting to find the good in other people. And no matter all the stuff he went through, how he was treated, what things were said about him, he never stopped treating people with kindness. He never stopped believing in and wanting to look for the good in people… even though he was broken, his heart still stayed the same… I hope that this is felt in the second movie.”

Michael Jackson stood trial over the allegations made against him in 2005, but was eventually acquitted of multiple counts of child molestation.

During the court case, the prosecution called several boys who accused Jackson of molesting them to take the stand. For the defense, stars including Macaulay Culkin, Jay Leno and George Lopez testified to Jackson’s character. The jury deliberated for more than 30 hours across seven days.

The “Thriller” singer faced more than 20 years in prison before he was found not guilty of all 10 felony charges against him, including four counts of child molestation, four counts of giving alcohol to a minor in order to molest him, one count of attempted child molestation and one count of conspiring to kidnap a child and hold him hostage at Neverland Ranch.
