Plans have been unveiled for a new 59 bedroom boutique hotel next to Acklam Hall in Middlesbrough.

Designs have been drawn up for the “major new investment opportunity” on the land adjacent to the town’s only Grade I-listed building, off Hall Drive.

The project is hoped to boost investment, jobs, and footfall in the area while bringing more customers to Acklam Hall’s wedding and events business.

It comes just two years after a previous bid for a 55-bedroom hotel and spa were rejected after councillors argued it was “not in keeping with the historic building”.

Landowner James Watson, who runs Over Ice Business Consultants and Teesside Restaurant Week, said he believes there is a gap in the market for a new boutique hotel.

The 47-year-old said the venue, which would boast its own Victorian-style garden, has been designed to feature glass and local steel and to have its own identity away from Acklam Hall.

## 
  **Acklam Hall history** 

Acklam Hall’s history began when William Hustler, a cloth merchant from Bridlington, wanted a country estate close to the River Tees from where he could trade.

It was owned by Sir Francis Boynton at the time - and while Hustler initially rented the site, he decided to buy the whole estate in 1637.

  IMPRESSIVE: The exterior of Middlesbrough's only Grade I-listed building


At the time, this included the whole of Acklam as well as parts of Ayresome, Middlesbrough and Linthorpe, all of which were owned by the Boynton family.

In 1683, Hustler built the two-storey Acklam Hall - and for the next two centuries, it was occupied by generations of his family.

The hall retains many of the architectural features created when it was built in 1683, as well as those added in 1912 by architect Walter Brierley.

The Hustlers eventually left the hall and it was sold to the Middlesbrough Corporation in 1928, which opened it as a grammar school for boys in 1935.

By 1967, the grammar school the Grammar School merged with Kirby Girls' Grammar School to form the co-educational Acklam High School.

More recently, Acklam Hall was a sixth form and latterly Middlesbrough College until it moved to a new purpose-built campus in Middlehaven in 2008.

The land was then sold by the council in 2014 for £907,000

From 2008 until to 2015, the venue stood empty, with Acklam Hall Ltd finally starting work on its restoration in 2014.
