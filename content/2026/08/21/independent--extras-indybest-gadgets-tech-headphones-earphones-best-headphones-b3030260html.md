If smartphones and laptops rule the roost in the tech hierarchy, then it’s headphones and earbuds that come in just behind them. Whether you’re listening to music, hearing directions on the move or even just zoning out with some noise-cancelling as you try and stay productive, headphones are an essential addition to anyone’s everyday carry in 2026. What’s more, there’s never been a better time to buy.

Thanks to continued innovation from big brands including Sony and Bose, alongside impressive newcomers like Nothing, headphones (and their more compact earbud alternatives) have never been more powerful or feature-packed. While they might have once been deemed inferior to full-on speakers, headphones are now a more preferable choice for many, and I include myself in that mix.

As a tech journalist who’s been testing the latest gadgets for almost a decade, I’ve never left the house without some means of enjoying my favourite albums in a pinch, and it means I know exactly which of the latest cans are worth buying and why. Whether you’re thinking about picking up your first-ever pair of headphones or are hoping to upgrade from your current pair, I’ve put the most recent contenders through their paces, using them in real-world scenarios to find out exactly which of them are best suited for your needs. Off the back of that testing, here are my recommendations for the best headphones you can buy in 2026.

When I originally put this list together, the first-generation model of Bose’s premium headphones shot straight to the top as my favourite pick of the bunch, with noise cancelling that felt like it was in another league to everything else out there. It seems only fitting then that those cans have been superseded by their direct successor, the Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones 2nd Gen.

![Bose QuietComfort Ultra Headphones 2nd Gen IndyBest review](<[https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/8/25/Bose-QuietComfort-Ultra-Headphones-2nd-Gen-IndyBest-review.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/8/25/Bose-QuietComfort-Ultra-Headphones-2nd-Gen-IndyBest-review.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w>)

Even though I didn’t think it could be possible, Bose has found a way to improve the ANC performance yet again, and the best way I can convey this is that, when trying to work in a busy London cafe, with additional noise pouring in from outside and the constant hum of a fan pointed directly at my face as I tried my best to battle the day’s heatwave, putting on the new QuietComfort Ultra Headphones made it all go away, giving me the chance to recentre my focus and be productive once more. No other pair of headphones can match that kind of power.

It’s not just about the ANC however, as there’s a very enjoyable soundscape at play here with a seriously thumping bassline. I am slightly more partial to the sound of Sony and Apple’s headphones, but with external noise kept at bay, you have more of a chance to enjoy your tracks here. Plus, there’s now lossless playback with a USB-C cable in tow, and the battery life is longer at up to 30 hours in total. I didn’t think that Bose could do it, but the best just got better.

While big-brand headphones cost a fair amount of money, the Anker soundcore space one pro prove that there are great budget alternatives for those who decide to shop around.

Already known for great value devices in the world of portable chargers and Bluetooth speakers, Anker’s sense for bringing a top-tier experience down to the budget level is obvious here, as the soundcore space one pro aim for the same high-energy sound profile as the Sony WH-1000XM6 (£399, Amazon.co.uk). The sound isn’t as detailed as Sony’s offering, but it works really well given the £149.99 asking price.

![indybest best noise-cancelling headphones Anker space one pro](<[https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/08/15/57/anker-space-one-pro-2.png?quality=75&width=608&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/08/15/57/anker-space-one-pro-2.png?quality=75&width=608&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp) 608w>)

There’s a nice amount of weight afforded to the bassline, so you can really bop along whilst studying or trying to get some work done. Both the headband and the ear cushions are wonderfully comfortable when in use, and much like the Marshall monitor III ANC, these headphones can be folded up into a very compact state, so if you need to chuck them in a backpack filled with books and a laptop, they’ll barely take up much space at all.

I don’t recommend trying to change the sound profile in the soundcore app – trying to amp up the bass even further only made it worse, so it’s best to stick with the default sound profile. When you also consider the 40 hour battery (60 hours with ANC off), it’s hard to argue with Anker’s value proposition here.

![Best headphones IndyBest review AirPods Max 2](<[https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/9/44/Best-headphones-IndyBest-review-AirPods-Max-2.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/9/44/Best-headphones-IndyBest-review-AirPods-Max-2.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w>)

On the former, I could hear the difference the moment I switched the AirPods Max 2 on. I’ve been on a real Fall Out Boy kick throughout the course of my testing, and when returning to *Take This to Your Grave*, an album I’ve listened to countless times, I was amazed when I started picking up on parts of the rhythm guitar that I simply hadn’t heard before. It was an absolute delight as it felt like I was hearing some of my favourite songs for the first time, something that felt exhilarating yet wonderfully nostalgic.

Even though the AirPods Max 2 aren’t the best buy for Android fans, because I am an iPhone user, I found myself reaching for these headphones more than any other in this list. Their ability to work so seamlessly with Apple’s ecosystem never gets old, and I adore the fact that they now include Conversational Awareness, something which has been on the more compact AirPods for ages and dips the volume when you’re having a chat. For moments when I’m working from home but my wife needs to get my attention, it’s been a huge help. It also doesn’t hurt that, despite being a tad bulkier than Bose’s headphones, they are actually more comfortable to wear with ear cushions that never agitate the skin.

The soundscape is now much wider than before, creating a very immersive experience that completely belies your expectations of what’s possible with a pair of buds. On the design front, the stem is now shorter than before but the head has been reconstructed to provide a tighter seal, making them better suited for when you’re running for a bus or beating a personal best on the treadmill as they’re less likely to become dislodged. Speaking of which, Apple has made these AirPods more workout-focused than before with a tougher IP57 rating and a built-in heart rate monitor so those without an Apple Watch can still keep tabs on their exertion.

Noise-cancelling has been given an uptick as well, and I was impressed to see how much more equipped these buds are at dimming the noise of traffic. It’s still not a touch on what Bose’s buds are capable of however, so if you are looking to buy based purely on ANC prowess, the AirPods Pro 3 just miss the mark. For everything else however, they’re right on target.

They’re nowhere near as svelte as the AirPods Pro 2 (£199, Amazon.co.uk), but the larger build and the wing-tip feature make them well-suited for workouts, as they stay in place without worry.

The biggest problem with the ultra earbuds is their price. At £299, they are among the most expensive earbuds on the market, and you can find options with great ANC for half the price – the problem is, none of them can withhold ambient noise like Bose can.

Ever been on a long-haul flight and seen the dreaded ‘low battery’ alert come up for your headphones? If the answer is a resounding yes, then the Marshall monitor III ANC headphones should probably be your next upgrade. They can last for a whopping 70 hours with noise-cancelling, and 100 hours with the feature switched off.

Several days of battery longevity runs rings around every pair of headphones on this list. Even the Dyson ontrac (£499, Amazon.co.uk), which can go for 55 hours, are a full 15 hours short compared to what Marshall is offering.

![indybest best noise-cancelling review Marshall monitor III A.N.C](<[https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/08/15/20/marshall.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/08/15/20/marshall.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w>)

Because this is Marshall, after all, I’ve found the headphones really come alive when listening to rock of any sub-genre. Drums are given lots of weight, so when it all kicks in with a song like *Welcome to the Jungle* by Guns N’ Roses, you really feel the energy. When you do need to pack them away, the Monitor III contorts unlike any pair of headphones I’ve seen, aside from the Anker soundcore space one pro, so they won’t be taking up much space in a carry-on bag.

The only things working against the headphones are the design, which may be a bit old-school for some, and the noise cancelling, which is good but isn’t as powerful as you’ll find elsewhere. However, if you travel a lot and need something compact and long-lasting, these are the headphones.

They also aren’t helped by their higher-than-average price point, so you may want to wait until they’re on offer before nabbing a pair.

Still, the one thing you can’t criticise the Dyson ontrac for is being boring, and the ANC is powerful enough to put the headphones somewhere between Bose and Sony, which is quite impressive.
