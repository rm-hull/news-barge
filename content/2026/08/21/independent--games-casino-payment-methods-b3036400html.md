Casinos are constantly battling for customers as the online gambling industry continues to grow in the UK, and offering a range of casino payment methods is a surefire way for a casino to stand out.

Online casino payment methods are simply options by which a player can send money to – or receive it from – a casino online.

The most popular casinos online are starting to offer more and more methods in a battle to enhance convenience and land more customers.

While debit cards and bank transfers once dominated casino payments, improved technology means e-wallets, prepaid vouchers and pay by mobile casino deposits are all possible now, alongside common payment methods such as Google Pay or Apple Pay.

With so much on offer it can be difficult to decide what is best for the customer when using a casino online, so we’ve produced this guide to the best online casino payment methods on the UK market, giving readers more details on each of the methods mentioned above as well as a guide on how to make deposits, common mistakes to avoid and key points to look out for.

## **What are casino payment methods?**

A casino payment method is the way a player sends money to, and receives money from, a casino account.

Most casinos online will offer a wide range of payment methods.

The most common types of casino payment methods can be grouped into five rough categories: debit cards, e-wallets, prepaid vouchers, pay by mobile and bank transfers.

## **Types of casino payment methods**

### **Debit cards**

Visa and Mastercard debit cards are probably the most commonly accepted payment methods at casinos online in the UK, and they almost always qualify for welcome bonuses too.

While Visa casinos and Mastercard casinos are common, it is important to note that a UKGC ban that came into force in 2020 means that UK casinos cannot accept credit cards.

### E-Wallets

E-wallets are online platforms that securely store your payment card details on a device (often via an app, such as PayPal).

PayPal, Skrill and Neteller are the most commonly seen e-wallets when using casinos online, though Neteller casinos and Skrill casinos are not quite as common as PayPal casinos.

If you do decide to use an e-wallet to make a casino payment online, remember that some e-wallets can be excluded from welcome bonuses.

### **Prepaid Vouchers**

Paysafecard is a commonly seen prepaid voucher on UK casinos. It runs via a one-time PIN code that it gives to the user, and suits players who want to keep card details private.

Note that prepaid vouchers usually cannot be used for withdrawals and won’t qualify for welcome bonuses. If you’re keen to learn more, read our full guide to Paysafecard casinos.

### **Pay by Mobile**

With a pay-by-mobile or pay by phone casino deposit, the money is added to your casino account via the player's phone bill or mobile balance.

The usual deposit limit is around £30 to encourage responsible gambling, but withdrawals are not possible with this method.

As such, you’ll need to have an alternative way of cashing out any winnings should you be so lucky.

### Apple Pay and Google Pay

Apple Pay casinos and Google Pay casinos offer convenient deposit methods that are safe and accessible, and function in the same way that they do for other in-person or online payments.

Withdrawals can also be processed within as little as four hours with these methods, though it depends on the casino itself as not all casinos permit withdrawals via Apple and Google Pay.

### **Bank Transfers**

Casinos can facilitate both deposits and withdrawals via bank transfer, though deposits often come with higher minimum amounts, and it is the slowest withdrawal times of the five types.

Nevertheless, there are dozens of UK options if you’re interested in signing up to a bank transfer casino.

The best casino payment methods for you personally will depend on various factors, and what you want from your casino experience. However, the following factors are worth considering:

Debit cards use the bank's own fraud protection for security, and while these are a common method to use, e-wallets and prepaid vouchers keep bank details away from the casino, with e-wallets having the added bonus that you can also store your debit card details within them.

Privacy is a concern when using casinos online, and while cards and bank transfers are popular, they do require the user to input details.

Conversely, prepaid vouchers and some e-wallets can store your card details while also hiding all information from the casino itself.

All trustworthy UK casinos will be licensed and regulated by the UK Gambling Commission. This ensures that customers and their data are kept safe and secure. Readers can check a licence number on the UKGC website.

### **KYC checks**

When you’re signing up for a new UK casino, all regulated sites must carry out KYC – ‘Know Your Customer’ – checks before the customer can make a first withdrawal. This only happens before the first withdrawal, and is used to verify a customer’s identity.

### **Responsible gambling** 

Every licensed gambling site in the UK must offer the full suite of safer gambling tools, including self-assessment calculators, deposit limits, loss limits, max-bet limits, reality checks and time-outs.

In addition to the usual safer gambling tools, sites and casino apps that are licensed by the UKGC must also be signed up to GamStop, which is an initiative that allows users to exclude themselves from gambling sites across the entirety of the UK. Regulated UK casinos must offer GamStop, and cannot accept credit cards.

UK-licensed casinos cannot accept cryptocurrency deposits, as per UKGC rules, so there are no crypto casino payment methods legally available to UK players. While some casinos out there may claim to accept cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, these sites are not licensed by the UKGC and should be avoided.
