Catholic Schools NSW chief executive sacked after Icac appearance

Penry Buckley

The chief executive of Catholic Schools NSW, Dallas McInerney, has been sacked from his position following his appearance at the NSW Icac’s inquiry into allegations of corruption in the state’s Liberal party.

In a statement shared today, the schools governing body said its board had “unanimously determined that there is a loss of confidence in Dallas McInerney’s ability to lead the organisation into the future”.

As such, the Board has now terminated Mr McInerney’s employment agreement with Catholic Schools NSW.

Interim CEO, Peter Yates, will remain in the role while a search process is conducted.The schools governing body has said it will not make any statement “regarding the inquiry or key witnesses” until Icac’s initial examination has been conducted, but says it will support an independent review of Catholic Schools NSW’s operations. It said:

In the opening statement, counsel assisting the commission stated that there was no suggestion that CSNSW is responsible for the alleged conduct being investigated by the commission, nor that any CSNSW Board or executive were aware of the alleged actions of Mr McInerney. The matters that are the subject of the Inquiry are, though, of deep concern to CSNSW and to its key stakeholders.

The Board will work constructively with them to ensure there is an externally conducted review of CSNSW’s operations.

Former NSW Liberal minister David Elliott appears before Icac

A former NSW Liberal minister and MP, David Elliott, is appearing before the NSW Independent Commission Against Corruption (Icac) this morning.

The inquiry is investigating the allegation that members of conservative factional group the Reformers took donations from Jean Nassif, a fugitive property developer, in exchange for the pursuit of outcomes including damage to Elliott’s career.

The inquiry has previously heard the Reformers created a “shit sheet” on Elliott, which included an alleged affair. Elliott has put out a statement strongly rejecting any affair had taken place, while witness Jeremy Greenwood, who commissioned the document, admitted to knowing the allegation of an affair to be untrue.

‘Flies in the face of evidence’: Brethren spokesperson rejects coordinated campaign by church, inquiry hears

Plymouth Brethren Church spokesperson, Lloyd Grimshaw, has also denied there was coordination by the church for its members to campaign for Liberal candidates during the 2025 election.

He tells the parliamentary inquiry into the 2025 election that the volunteers would have “coordinated amongst themselves”.

Volunteers of the Brethren church were seen at polling booths across the country including across Sydney, Adelaide, Melbourne and Perth.

Grimshaw says:

If members of a religious group decide to do something in their private capacity as private citizens, that they don’t speak on behalf of the church when they do that … I’m not saying that members didn’t participate, and clearly they did, and they must have been some coordination, but they would have coordinated amongst themselves, self-coordinated, and whoever they were.

But Labor MP Corinne Mulholland says Grimshaw’s claim “flies in the face of evidence” the committee has heard during previous hearings.

Spontaneous 3000 kilometres away from each other just [coordinating] amongst themselves? … I think it is a difficult pill for us to swallow on that one, unfortunately, Mr Grimshaw, and it flies in the face of evidence the committee has heard from ex members of the church who have said to us in extreme detail in their evidence before us about the level of control and direction from the church.

Icac is investigating the allegation that McInerney, who gave evidence to the inquiry this week and last week, arranged and approved illegal political donations to lobbying companies controlled by conservative factional group the Reformers, under the guise of consultancy services. McInerney has insisted payments totalling $265,900 over four years were for genuine consulting work.

On Wednesday, McInerney denied he performed a factory reset on a work phone and iPad over the weekend to wipe critical evidence. He said he performed the wipe on his phone after his bank contacted him last Friday to tell him his account had been “compromised by an unknown merchant”. He said he performed a factory reset on an iPad ahead of it being surrendered to Catholic Schools NSW, “in the realisation I’m not likely returning to the company”.

He previously stood aside from his role as CEO of the governing body for 600 schools in NSW before the Icac hearings began “to allow the work of Catholic Schools NSW to continue its important mission”, he said in a statement.

The Plymouth Brethren church claims it was subject to “extraordinary attacks by the media and even by members of parliament” during and after the 2025 federal election, rejecting newspaper reports today that it had engaged with far-right influencers to stage stunts targeting Anthony Albanese and other politicians.

The parliament’s inquiry into the 2025 election continues today, with the Brethren church and rightwing lobby group Advance appearing at a committee hearing. Lloyd Grimshaw, a director at the Plymouth Brethren Christian church, was the first witness, conceding members of the church volunteered for various parties at the election but denying the church participated itself or coordinated the actions of members.

Some media reporting has alleged there was a systematic and coordinated effort from Plymouth Brethren members to support the Liberal party at the election, including on polling booths.

Grimshaw told the inquiry the church had been targeted with “unsubstantiated” allegations about its activities, claiming some members did volunteer at the election but for “pretty normal reasons” like backing their local candidate or being worried about the economy.

The Nine newspapers reported allegations on Friday that members of the church had paid far-right activists to disrupt Labor’s election events. Grimshaw strongly denied this reporting, calling it a “complete lie” and saying the church did not make any donations or give any support to political parties or “third parties”.

Wong calls Israeli foreign minister over Zomi Frankcom

Josh Butler

Foreign minister Penny Wong phoned her Israeli counterpart overnight to discuss the decision not to pursue a criminal investigation into Gaza strikes that killed Australian aid worker Zomi Frankcom and six of her colleagues.

“Minister Wong spoke with Israel’s foreign minister Gideon Sa’ar last night,” a spokesperson for Wong said this morning.

The minister conveyed Australia’s view and expectations as she did with the Israeli ambassador.

Australian government ministers have been scathing of Israel’s decision not to open a criminal case. Anthony Albanese told the ABC last night “it clearly is an outrage and there’s a need for a far more transparent and clear explanation from the Israeli government other than what we’ve received”.

He didn’t rule out Australia pursuing some form of independent investigation. Albanese said Australia would “press the Israeli government” and “then make an announcement in coming days” about what his government would do in response.

Israel can ‘check’ reparations for aid worker’s death, ambassador says

Israel is open to reparations for the family of killed Australian aid worker Zomi Frankcom, as the federal government backs an independent investigation into her death, AAP reports.

The Israeli military on Thursday announced it had found there was no suspicion of criminal misconduct surrounding the Australian’s death, despite a previous finding there had been failures in how it was carried out.

Israeli ambassador Hillel Newman was summoned to Parliament House on Thursday morning to explain the outcome of the two-year investigation.

“Israel is open to discussions of a legal aspect, we can share information with legal teams of Australia,” he told ABC’s RN on Friday.

And the aspect of reparations can be checked definitely.

Newman said a request needed to be made before Israel could investigate potential reparations.

Sydney air traffic controllers have ‘grave concerns’ about collision risk after airspace redesign

Air traffic controllers at Sydney airport have warned federal safety regulators they have “grave concerns” about changes to airspace rules introduced last month, likening the situation to the months leading up to a crash in Washington DC in which 67 people died.

Guardian Australia can reveal the federal government was notified on Thursday of a confidential complaint considered by the Australian Transport Safety Bureau (ATSB), in which an experienced air traffic controller warned in June that changes related to the new Western Sydney International airport had been rushed, with “inadequate training for controllers, of all experience levels”.

The complainant told the regulator a large group of controllers in Sydney “hold grave concerns about the project”, referring to the air traffic redesign to accommodate the new airport.

Brisbane woman allegedly attacked and raped on morning walk

A man has been charged with several counts of rape after an alleged random attack on a woman who was out on a morning walk, AAP reports.

The woman, aged in her 20s, was walking in the leafy, semi-rural Brisbane suburb of Brookfield around 6.30am on Thursday when she was allegedly stopped by an unknown man, police said in a statement.

The man allegedly sexually assaulted the victim multiple times before he fled the scene on foot. Police later arrested a 32-year-old man a short distance from the scene.

He has been charged with six counts of rape, stalking, assault with intent to commit rape, assault occasioning bodily harm and deprivation of liberty. He is due to appear in court later today.

Police said while the attack was being treated as an isolated incident, police patrols would be increased in the Brookfield area over the coming days.
