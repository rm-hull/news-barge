A rare £2 coin has sold for more than 67 times its face value due to an error it has, which sparked a bidding war.

The 2015 coin, featuring Queen Elizabeth II and the Standing on the Shoulders of Giants design, fetched £134.08 on July 23 due to a minting error known as the fried egg error.

This error is a type of minting error that affects bi-metallic coins, including the UK £1 and £2s, and it occurs when the silver-coloured inner disc of the coin spreads outwards, resembling the yolk of a runny egg.

The coin attracted 19 bids, starting at just £3.95, before selling for more than 67 times its original value.
