# What is China’s moon mission and why is it so interesting?

- China's space programme is preparing to launch its Chang'e-7 mission to explore permanently shadowed craters near the lunar South Pole to locate and analyse accessible water ice.
- The mission features an orbiter, a lander, a rover, and an innovative hopper vehicle designed to leap over broken terrain and descend into deep craters where temperatures drop below minus 200 degrees Celsius.
- Communication between Earth and the spacecraft will be maintained via the Queqiao-2 relay satellite, which was launched in March 2024 and previously supported the Chang'e-6 mission.
- China's state-led approach to lunar resource evaluation contrasts with NASA's strategy, which utilises private commercial partners through its Commercial Lunar Payload Services programme to support the Artemis initiative.
- The Chang'e-7 mission marks a key step towards utilising lunar resources, setting the stage for the Chang'e-8 mission in 2028 and the joint development of an International Lunar Research Station with Russia.
