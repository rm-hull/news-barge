Ukraine heeded a special request from the Donald Trump administration not to attack major Russian cities during a visit by CIA director John Ratcliffe.

The US contacted Kyiv last week to request that it not strike Moscow, St Petersburg and a stretch of northern Russia while a plane carrying the senior US official was flying in and out of the Russian capital, sources told Reuters.

The request covered Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday and was only revealed hours after Ratcliffe left Russia late on Tuesday.

Subsequently, a large US military aircraft was confirmed to have travelled to Russia from the US via Latvia on Tuesday, flight tracking data confirmed.

The purpose of Ratcliffe’s visit and the nature of his meetings in Russia were not immediately clear. It was the first known trip to Moscow by a US spy chief since his predecessor, William Burns, held talks with Vladimir Putin in November 2021.

Ukraine resumed strikes on Russia early on Wednesday after Ratcliffe's departure, with Russia saying it downed at least 426 drones overnight.

## Repairs at Russia's Novorossiysk grain terminal may take up to four months, company says

It could take up to four months to restore damaged facilities at NKHP, one of the largest grain terminals at Russia's port of Novorossiysk, where operations have been halted since August, the company said on Wednesday in its financial report.

Local officials said earlier in August that a Ukrainian drone and missile strike on the port damaged two major grain export terminals and hit the last big Russian naval base on the Black Sea.

## Burnham warned Russia plotting retaliation against Britain over support for Ukraine - report

Andy Burnham was reportedly warned that Russia is plotting retaliation against Britain for its continued support for Ukraine.

Security experts were said to have told the prime minister to be ready for a ‘direct response’, according to the *Daily Mail*.

Mr Burnham travelled to Kyiv on Monday for his first overseas trip since taking office last month and said London and Paris would provide long-range missile technology to Ukraine.

A unnamed senior security source told the Mail: “An attack on the UK’s defence and security infrastructure is inevitable, based on our high profile support for Ukraine.

“Ourselves and the French are front and centre, and Burnham's visit to Kyiv and his forthright stance will have been noticed at the Kremlin,” they assessed.

“There had been some optimism in Moscow that he would be softer than Starmer on Ukraine. That was not the case.”

## Release of Marine veteran from Russian custody turns focus to other detainees with health concerns

Robert Gilman’s release from Russian custody this month heralded a long-awaited homecoming for the Marine veteran after more than four years behind bars.

But as Harold Jezler followed the media coverage from the United States, he couldn’t help but notice Gilman’s gaunt and sickly appearance and think of the implications for his wife, Olga, who has been detained in Russia since 2022 and faces her own health problems.

“You just look at the pictures and the guy is not in good shape. It’s concerning for me and for all the families, I would imagine," Jezler said in an interview with The Associated Press. "Is this what it takes for the government to really make a move and prioritize this — that the person is basically in dire straits? It was a very bittersweet thing to see that."

## Russia downs 426 drones overnight, says Russian media citing defence ministry

Russia downed 426 drones overnight, Russian news agencies quoted the defence ministry as saying. The figure includes Russia-controlled parts of Ukraine.

## Russia says it struck five vessels in Ukraine's Black Sea ports

The Russian defence ministry said that its forces had struck three cargo vessels carrying supplies for the Ukrainian military and a tanker at the Black Sea port of Pivdennyi yesterday.

The ministry also said it had hit another cargo vessel in the nearby port of Odesa, as well as port infrastructure in Pivdennyi and Odesa and fuel storage facilities at the port of Chornomorsk.

## Wildberries warehouse in Russia's Tambov region on fire after drone attack

A warehouse of Russian e-commerce giant Wildberries in Russia's Tambov region caught fire after a drone attack, local governor Evgeniy Pervyshov said on the Telegram messaging app.

Pervyshov said one person was injured in the incident.

## Ex-US special envoy for Ukraine calls for mobilisation of women

Ukraine should mobilise women in its fight against Russia as they “fight no worse than men,” a former US special envoy has urged.

Keith Kellogg told the Naspravdi MAX YouTube channel: “Do I believe women should be mobilised into the army?

“My answer is yes, because they fight no worse than men.”

This comes just days after former defence minister Oleksii Reznikov also said that the government should consider a universal conscription model.

Pointing to Israel as an example where women serve alongside men, he said: “That immediately would double our mobilisation resource.

“Then you can clearly see which military positions and specialties can easily be filled by women - they don’t have to go to the battlefield.

“They fill many rear positions, logistics and support, and men can be moved from rear positions closer to the front.

“The question is whether society is ready to say, like Israel, that we can survive only if everyone accepts that they must defend Ukraine.”

## Burnham says he is planning US trip in September to lobby for Ukraine’s defence

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he is planning on travelling to the US in September to join European leaders in lobbying President Donald Trump on support for Ukraine’s defence.

In an interview with Bloomberg, Mr Burnham said he was “looking forward to going to New York” next month, which is when the UN General Assembly will meet.

He said details of the trip are not yet “fully confirmed” but it is “likely” to go ahead. The trip would mark Mr Burnham’s first US visit since becoming Prime Minister in July.

## Two employees of Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant injured in drone attack

At least two employees at the Russian-controlled Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant were injured in a Ukrainian drone attack, the plant's Russian-installed management said.

The strike occurred near the plant's hydraulic structures and wounded two maintenance and construction workers.

Management did not provide details on any damage caused by the attack.

## Ukraine hands Britain ‘goldmine’ of secret battlefield intelligence to protect bases from Russian spies

Ukraine has handed Britain a “goldmine” of secret battlefield intelligence to help the UK protect its bases from Russian spies.

The agreement was signed as prime minister Andy Burnham made his first visit to Kyiv on Monday to chair his first meeting of the so-called “coalition of the willing” and pledge “continuity” in the UK’s approach to Ukraine.

Volodymyr Zelensky handed the Ministry of Defence (MoD) access to Ukraine’s “Avengers” AI database, which is expected to help the UK learn from Ukraine’s experience on the battlefield and develop AI-powered solutions to national security challenges in both countries, making it the first outside of Ukraine to receive the intelligence, Downing Street said.
