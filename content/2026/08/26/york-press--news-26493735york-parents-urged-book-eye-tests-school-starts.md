Parents are being reminded to add eye tests to their back-to-school checklists.

Specsavers York and Specsavers Vangarde are encouraging families to book appointments for their children ahead of the new school term.

Eye tests are free for children under 16 with an NHS optical voucher, and some glasses are also provided at no cost.

Charlotte Turner, ophthalmic director at both locations, said: "We know that children can fall behind in school if they are struggling to see clearly, and a pair of glasses can sometimes be such an easy fix.

"You might not know that your child is struggling to see if they don’t tell you, but there are some signs you might be able to spot.

"These include sensitivity to light, complaining of headaches or tired eyes, sitting too close to the TV, or frequently rubbing their eyes, amongst other things.

"Even if you haven’t spotted any of these signs, Specsavers recommends that children have an eye test at least every two years.

"Eye tests can also spark a great sense of wonder in many children, so they could learn something while you check their eyes are in good health."
