President Donald Trump intends to put himself at the front and center of this fall’s Republican midterms campaigning, despite not being on the ballot and in spite of his dire approval ratings, according to a report.

*The Atlantic* reports that Trump met with his political advisers in the Oval Office earlier this month to discuss how best to deploy his MAGA Inc super PAC’s $400 million war chest to boost GOP candidates.

The advisers reportedly warned the president about his tanking ratings and pointed to a lack of awareness among voters about his accomplishments, which they identified as a series of stock market highs, a reduction in fentanyl deaths, a “sealed” U.S.-Mexico border blocking illegal entries and an increase in domestic manufacturing.

Per *The Atlantic*, the president ultimately decided against a more tailored approach to individual candidates and local races, as some advisers recommended, in favor of making the midterms a referendum on his own record since returning to Washington, leaning into a strategy also likely to be adopted by Democrats – albeit for opposing reasons.

Trump duly ordered a series of TV spots highlighting his achievements, as he sees them, to run nationwide in support of House of Representatives and Senate candidates, effectively nationalizing the contest.

![President Donald Trump reportedly plans to make himself and his record in office the focal point of the Republican midterms strategy, rather than focusing on individual candidates and local races](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/00/2026-08-24T202227Z_1102145854_RC275NA3BY3Q_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG)

“Go look at the clips,” he reportedly instructed his team, advising them to mine his back catalogue of media appearances for material. “I cite accomplishments all the time.”

A person familiar with the meeting told *The Atlantic*: “We are going to unload the guns on both chambers real soon.”

Another said they felt the strategy made sense: “I think everybody does understand that the president and his agenda are on the ballot to some degree.”

“When the issues that you typically campaign on don’t move voters – or voters are p***ed off at you about those issues – the question becomes: What do you campaign on?” said another.

MAGA Inc told the outlet it did not discuss its ad spending plans but White House spokesperson Olivia Wales said: “President Trump is cleaning up Joe Biden’s mess.

“There is a great story to tell because the United States of America has never been stronger than under his leadership.”

*The Independent* has also reached out to the White House for comment.

With narrow conservative majorities in both chambers of Congress in jeopardy and the party of the incumbent president almost always losing seats at the midterms (Bill Clinton in 1998 and George W Bush in 2002 are two recent exceptions), there seems no question the strategy is risky.

Trump’s popularity hit the lowest level of his presidency last week when a Reuters/Ipsos survey put him on just 33 percent.

Voters have consistently told pollsters they do not feel the president has done enough to address inflation and the cost of living crisis they face, which has been exacerbated by his unpopular war with Iran driving up gas prices as a consequence of the shuttering of the Strait of Hormuz.

![Trump has already roadtested his messaging strategy while campaigning for Darline Graham in South Carolina](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/22/01/2026-08-21T235958Z_1745098741_RC2B3NAWI7GR_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG)

Trump campaigned in 2024 on the explicit promise of delivering a new American “golden age” that few believe has materialized and on ending “forever war” commitments in the Middle East, only to find himself embroiled in precisely that having overruled his own intelligence experts who warned him against it.

Clearly undaunted by doubts about his brand, the president has already roadtested the messaging in his recent stumping on behalf of South Carolina Sen. Darline Graham.

“You have record manufacturing and auto plants being built right now in the United States and in South Carolina,” he told a rally crowd in Myrtle Beach last week.

“We have the greatest economy in our history. Now, some people haven’t seen it yet, but they will.”

He added: “What I really want you to do is pretend, please, that I’m on the ballot. Just come and vote. It’s so important. Just say that I’m on the ballot one more time.”

Former Barack Obama senior strategist David Axelrod pointed out the high-wire nature of the approach, telling *The Atlantic*: “If you’re the average person out there, you’re seeing that and thinking: ‘What planet is he living on?’

“You have to acknowledge that people are struggling and this is a work in progress.”
