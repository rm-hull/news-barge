A BLUE plaque has been unveiled at one of Halifax’s most iconic landmarks.

The plaque, at Halifax’s Wainhouse Tower, commemorates John Edward Wainhouse, whose vision led to the creation of the Grade II*-listed tower.

The ceremony took place on Friday, August 7, and was attended by the Mayor of Calderdale, Councillor Andrew Tagg, members of the Halifax Civic Trust, heritage supporters, and other guests.

Councillor Tagg said: "Wainhouse Tower is one of Calderdale's most loved and most recognisable landmarks.

"For many, a glimpse of the iconic tower means that they're close to home and the visitors who climb to the viewing platform are rewarded with spectacular views of the borough and wider region.

"The tower also has a fascinating history, and I was delighted to unveil a blue plaque form the Halifax Civic Trust celebrating our local heritage and raising awareness of John Edward Wainhouse’s story and this very special building."

Wainhouse Tower was originally commissioned by Wainhouse as a chimney for a dye works but was never used for its intended purpose.

Instead, it became known as the tallest folly in the world and remains a striking example of Victorian ambition and design.

The new blue plaque will serve as a lasting tribute to Wainhouse’s contribution to Halifax’s industrial and architectural legacy.
