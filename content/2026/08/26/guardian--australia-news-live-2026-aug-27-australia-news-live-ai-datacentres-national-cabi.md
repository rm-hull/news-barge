The prime minister, Anthony Albanese, says Dfat officials are ready to provide consular assistance to Australians in Nepal, after the devastating flooding in the country.

A local tourism official said they believed 34 Australians were missing in the area, among hundreds of foreign nationals. Australian officials were not confirming numbers at this stage, and were working to check on the welfare of people they know were in the affected region.

In a statement this morning Albanese urged Australians to listen to Nepalese authorities and said the government was aware of “a number” of people in the area.

“We extend our deepest sympathies to those affected by the significant flooding in Nepal and Tibet, China,” he said.

Australians in the affected area should follow the advice of local authorities. We are aware of a number of Australians in the area and Australian officials are urgently working to confirm their welfare. We are liaising with local authorities as well as tour operators.

We stand ready to provide consular assistance.

The PM said Australians in need of emergency consular assistance should contact the Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade’s 24-hour Consular Emergency Centre on 1300 555 135, or +61 2 6261 3305 (if calling from overseas).

'This system is going to break': Ken Henry says GST deal benefiting WA is ‘stupid’

The former Treasury secretary Ken Henry says the GST deal benefiting Western Australia is “stupid” and undermines the “integrity of the system of horizontal fiscal equalisation”.

Henry spoke to RN Breakfast this morning amid an ongoing stoush after the Productivity Commission said in an interim report the Morrison-era GST deal was a mistake that should be reversed. Western Australia has responded with fury, and Anthony Albanese said this week there would be no changes to the agreement while he is in power.

Henry, however, said he agreed the GST carve-out was hurting the nation, telling RN:

If a state, the wealthiest state in the nation, can come to Canberra with a begging bowl and get the rest of the country to put money into that begging bowl being held by the wealthiest people in the country, if that’s how this system is going to work, then this system is going to fail. And one day this system is going to break.

Without a high-integrity system of fiscal equalisation, the Australian federation is going to face some pretty serious challenges.

Home buyers have more power but new listings down as owners hold fire

Australia’s spring property season is shaping up to be unusually sluggish, as sellers retreat from a weak market and unsold homes pile up, AAP reports.

New property listings in the four weeks leading up to August 23 were 8.2% below the five-year average, property analytics firm Cotality data shows.

Advertised stock levels remain elevated overall, suggesting homeowners are holding back from adding to an already crowded market.

Sydney is leading the downturn, where new listings were 14% below the five-year average in the four-week period. Similar pullbacks were recorded in all capitals except Adelaide.

‘Reluctant’ approval for Tasmanian datacentre sets marker for national battle

The day before Anthony Albanese secured agreement with states and territories to develop national datacentre standards, a small council in Tasmania was grappling with the existing regulations in the face of strong community opposition to a new AI factory planned for its town.

The George Town council voted 6-1 to approve the plans of Australian developer Firmus to build a 288 megawatt (MW) datacentre at Long Reach, north of Launceston, but said it did so reluctantly.

The AI factory is planned on the former Gunns Pulp Mill site near the Tamar River, and has faced strong opposition from locals. A petition calling to stop the centre going ahead had nearly 6,000 signatures. More than 300 people wrote submissions on the application to the council.

Malinauskas defends national cabinet carveouts for AI data centres

Ima Caldwell

South Australian premier Peter Malinauskas has defended national cabinet’s decision to drop strict mandates requiring new AI data centres to be powered entirely by renewable energy.

After a meeting of national cabinet, prime minister Anthony Albanese flagged exemptions for jurisdictions including Queensland and the Northern Territory.

Speaking on ABC’s 7.30 on Wednesday evening, Malinauskas denied the PM had been rolled by state leaders, adding there was a “genuine desire” to manage the national rollout together.

“It’s pretty clear... to any objective observer that the way we’re going to see these datacentres roll out in an Australian context will be powered by renewables, because that is where the generation can come from,” he told host Sarah Ferguson.

That’s where the very large scale of energy is able to be procured at relatively quick speed... we can get renewables online sooner rather than later...

What you saw today was a nationally consistent position, that gas will have a role to play when it comes to the firming of renewables, which has always been South Australia’s view.

Malinauskas noted that different state energy profiles, such as Queensland’s state-owned generation, meant a federal mandate was unnecessary. However, he said all jurisdictions agreed that operators must construct new generation capacity.

We cannot have datacentres connecting to the grid without bringing their own new generation. Water is also clearly a consideration, but, frankly, energy is the main game. And that is accepted by all states and territories.
