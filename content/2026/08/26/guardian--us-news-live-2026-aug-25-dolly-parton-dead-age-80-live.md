JadAbumrad, journalist and creator of the Dolly Parton’s America podcast has written an insightful opinion piece for the New York Times detailing his interactions with the country music queen. He starts:

I was lucky enough to interview Dolly Parton a dozen times, on two different continents, and she began nearly every interview with some version of this: ‘Ask me anything you want to ask me, and I’ll tell you what I want you to hear!’”

Beyond her musical talent, Parton was also lauded for her extensive philanthropic work on literacy through organizations such as the Dollywood Foundation and Dolly Parton’s Imagination Library.

The Imagination Library, which sends free books to children from birth to age five, has gifted more than 270m books since it was founded by Parton in 1995, inspired by her father, who could not read or write.

In 2024, one in seven children aged under five in the US received a book in the post each month from the program.

Dolly Parton displays a pin on her jacket. Photograph: Mark Humphrey/AP

The Imagination Library was launched in the UK in 2007 on the back of the success of the project in the US.

The program’s UK outfit, which began in Rotherham, South Yorkshire, has since gifted 7m books.

The Imagination Library X account posted that their team were “heartbroken” at the loss of their founder, who was proud that the program “grew into what it is today”.

About 15,000km (9,300 miles) away from Dolly Parton’s beloved Smoky Mountains home in Tennessee, a small town in rural Australia will always love her.

Narromine, a farming community of 4,000 people in central NSW, is home to the DollyPartonfestival, which fills the wide country streets with her music every year.

Organiser Susie Rae said the town would hold a minute of silence to honour the queen of country music at the festival on 10 October, after Parton’s death.

Remembering Dolly Parton, one of the most beloved personalities in music and beyond

Country music icon and celebrated philanthropist Dolly Parton has died in Nashville at the age of 80, her family announced. “She never saw a door she couldn’t open, and the doors she opened made and changed history,” her nephew Bryan Seaver said in a video statement, adding: “Dolly has lived in the light and is in the arms of Jesus.” Her funeral will be small and private, per her wishes, her family said.

In an interview last week, Parton had shared that she had been “dealing with some health issues” that had gone unaddressed while she cared for her husband of almost 60 years, Carl Dean, who died in March last year. “I still have so many things I want to achieve, so I’m gonna keep on working as long as I can to see everything come to life,” she told People on Friday.

Parton rose from grinding poverty in Tennessee to become a rhinestone-studded cultural icon, writing 3,000 songs including hits like Jolene and 9 to 5, acting in major Hollywood films and donating millions of dollars to charities in her home state and beyond. She was an accomplished businesswoman, with interests spanning from her Dollywood theme park to cosmetics, pet apparel, baking mixes and even a truck stop. Among her celebrated philanthropic work, the Dollywood Foundation distributes about 25m books each year across the US, Canada, Australia, the UK and Ireland, while her $1m donated for research into Covid-19 formed the basis for the eventual Moderna vaccine.

One of the country’s most unifying personalities, Parton was paid tributes from figures across music, film, politics and beyond. In a lengthy and emotional Instagram post, her 9 to 5 co-star Jane Fonda wrote: “I know that millions are mourning and I want to say this – more than anyone I have ever known, Dolly will always be with us because she was so present, so powerful, so generous and playful that she is woven into all our lives and hearts forever.” She was “the voluptuous embodiment of empathy”, she added. Fellow country star Reba McEntire shared a picture of her and Parton on Instagram, adding: “Dolly, thank you for all the wonderful things you did for us here on earth. Your love, your music and you gave so much with your huge huge heart.”
