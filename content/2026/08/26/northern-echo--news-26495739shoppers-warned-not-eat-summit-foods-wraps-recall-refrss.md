Popular wraps sold in major UK supermarkets such as Sainsbury's and Morrisons are being recalled due to possible contamination.

Summit Foods is recalling various filled wraps, including its Chicken Fajita Wrap and Snacksters Hoi Sin Wrap.

Shoppers are being warned not to eat the products listed and instead to return them to stores immediately.

The recall includes various wraps that shoppers may have purchased, all of which have use-by dates up to and including September 5, 2026.

They are:

- Snacksters Chicken Fajita Wrap
- Snacksters Hoi Sin Wrap
- East Street Deli Sweet Chilli Chicken Wrap
- Target a Snack Chicken Fajita Wrap

Summit Foods has said the affected products may possibly be contaminated with E.coli.

Symptoms include severe diarrhoea, abdominal pain and occasionally haemolytic uremic syndrome, which can lead to kidney failure and can be fatal.

If you are experiencing these symptoms, it is advised to stay away from work, school or nursery until you have stopped having symptoms for at least 48 hours to avoid passing it on to others.

Those who have already purchased any of the wraps have been urged to return the product.


The Food Standards Agency (FSA) said: "If you have bought the above product, do not consume it.

"Instead, dispose of the product and provide proof of purchase on [sales@summitfoods.co.uk](mailto:sales@summitfoods.co.uk), stating when and where it was purchased, for a refund."

## What is a product recall?

If there is a problem with a food product that means it should not be sold, then it might be 'withdrawn' (taken off the shelves) or 'recalled' (when customers are asked to return the product).

The FSA issues Product Withdrawal Information Notices and Product Recall Information Notices to let consumers and local authorities know about problems associated with food.

In some cases, a 'Food Alert for Action' is issued, which provides local authorities with details of specific actions to be taken on behalf of consumers.
