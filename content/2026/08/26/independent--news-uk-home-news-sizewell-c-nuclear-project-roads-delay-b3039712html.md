The new Sizewell C nuclear project that forms part of the government’s strategy to reduce gas imports could be delayed after a falling-out over road building.

The contractor that had been due to build a link road and bypass has been replaced after Sizewell bosses reportedly wanted changes that pushed up the bill.

The two sides failed to agree on costs, and it is feared the resulting delay will set back work on the £38bn nuclear plant, as well as prolonging misery for nearby villagers putting up with construction traffic going past their homes.

Sizewell chiefs deny the change in contractor will cause delays, insisting there is no disruption to the project.

Last year, when work on Sizewell C began, construction firm Galliford Try was awarded a contract thought to have been worth up to £200m to build the four-mile Sizewell Link Road and mile-long Two Village Bypass to carry heavy vehicles to the site, away from residential streets.

But a source told the *FT* that several design changes had led to heavy cost overruns. “They asked for changes that added £30m,” they said, referring to Sizewell C.

When it is due to open in 2028, the link road will connect the nuclear power station to the A12, bypassing the villages of Theberton and Middleton Moor. Building giant Kier will now take charge of it.

Local firm Breheny Civil Engineering will provide the Two Village Bypass, which will route traffic around Farnham and Stratford St Andrew.

The change of contractor was likely to delay completion of the roads by between six months and one year, the source told the *FT*.

Sizewell bosses had promised to minimise delays and avoid the cost overruns that have hit Hinkley Point C in Somerset by learning from that scheme.

“The link road and bypass were mitigation to reduce the significant impacts of a decade of HGVs and worker buses travelling through multiple small villages,” said Alison Downes of campaign group Stop Sizewell C.

Sizewell C said last year it expected construction of both roads to be completed this year, and the link road was due to open next year. But Sizewell C’s website states that the road will be completed next year.

![Sizewell C said last year it expected construction of both roads to be completed this year but that has now been put back](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/26/14/26/18_02_25-Sheet-Piling-on-the-TCA-17.jpg)

Last year, then-chancellor Rachel Reeves announced £14.2 billion worth of investment in Sizewell C, as the government took a 44.9 per cent stake in the project. The consortium of investors also includes Centrica and EDF.

Sizewell C is due to start generating power in the mid- to late 2030s, after a 10-12 year construction programme, and have a lifespan of 60 years.

Energy secretary Ed Miliband said new nuclear power capacity was needed to deliver a “golden age of clean energy abundance”.

The government wants to decarbonise Britain’s energy supplies by 2030, replacing fossil fuels with green power.

Sizewell C is expected to deliver clean power to the equivalent of six million homes.

Environmentalists, including the RSPB, have argued the scheme would harm wildlife.

A Galliford Try spokesperson said: “Our highways business has been unable to reach a mutual agreement with Sizewell C to construct the transport infrastructure that the project requires, following an intensive 20-month period of early contractor involvement.

“Our robust risk-management process has helped us achieve five consecutive years of growth and it protects our interests and those of our stakeholders, by ensuring an appropriate balance between risk and reward. We remain committed to only taking on work when these criteria are met.”

A Sizewell C spokesperson said: “While we did not agree commercial terms with Galliford Try for the construction phase of the Sizewell Link Road and Two Village Bypass, we’d like to thank them for their work in helping to set up and ready the sites.”

They said Kier and family-owned Suffolk company Breheny already worked on the project and were well placed to deliver the roads.
