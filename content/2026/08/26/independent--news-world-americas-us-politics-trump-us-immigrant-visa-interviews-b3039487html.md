Immigrant visa interviews at U.S. embassies and consulates worldwide have been temporarily suspended as President Donald Trump's administration continues a broad immigration enforcement push during his second term.

A U.S. State Department representative said Tuesday that the pause stems from a newly launched global training program for diplomatic missions worldwide, requiring adjustments to scheduled visa service appointments.

The measure forms part of a wider immigration policy overhaul under Trump, who maintains that stricter measures are required to enhance national security.

![A U.S. State Department spokesperson said on Tuesday that it launched a global training initiative at all U.S. embassies and consulates worldwide](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/09/22/18/26/Illustration-shows-U-S--flag-and-U-S--H-1B-Visa-application-form-8htiua7w.jpeg)

The administration's efforts have included canceling visas and permanent residency status, as well as turning down applications linked to political views, such as participation in pro-Palestinian demonstrations targeting Israel's military actions in Gaza.

These initiatives have encountered legal hurdles in federal court. On Friday, a U.S. judge blocked a policy that would halt immigrant visas for individuals from 75 nations, ruling that Secretary of State Marco Rubio had exceeded his legal authority by issuing the suspension.

While the State Department provided few details on the schedule or full scope of the staff training, it said the program is designed to teach consular staff to identify individuals who might rely on U.S. public assistance, while ensuring applicants are assessed "comprehensively and consistently."

Applicants affected by the decision have already begun receiving notices that their interview dates have been canceled and will be rescheduled later, according to an initial report by the *Financial Times.*

![Trump has pursued an aggressive deportation drive and immigration crackdown that includes revocations of visas and green cards and rejection of applications](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/00/2026-08-24T202227Z_1102145854_RC275NA3BY3Q_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG)

Human rights organizations have strongly criticized the administration's actions, calling the measures discriminatory and alleging violations of constitutional guarantees to free expression and due process.

Critics further warn that the enforcement push fosters an unsafe climate within the country, raising particular concerns among ethnic minorities regarding potential racial profiling.

Although Trump's 2024 presidential campaign focused primarily on curbing unauthorized border crossings, his administration has simultaneously introduced hurdles for lawful entry, including steep fee increases for specific employment-based visa categories.
