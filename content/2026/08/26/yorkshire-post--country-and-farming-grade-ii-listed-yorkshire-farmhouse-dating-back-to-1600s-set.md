# Grade II-listed Yorkshire farmhouse dating back to 1600s set to be restored

## Proposals to restore part of a historic Yorkshire farmhouse and convert two barns into new homes have been submitted to planners.

Described in supporting papers with the application as being “of some notable importance in the national context for its evidential and historic interest,” applicant Mr Gibson wants to make the changes at Woolrow Farm at Woolrow Lane in Brighouse.

The farmhouse is Grade II-listed according to the application submitted to Calderdale Council planners.

Agents PWA Planning say the proposed works seek to secure the long-term preservation, repair and viable reuse of heritage assets.

![Woolrow Lane, Brighouse.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjE0Yjc3ZjVhLTgwMDEtNGI0MS05MmEyLTU1OTc4YjExZDNkMzplYzM1ZTllYS1kZWJiLTQ4ZDAtOGZlOC1jZGMzYWYyYTQ2Njk=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



“The proposals adopt a conservation-led approach which seeks to retain and repair historic fabric wherever practicable, preserve the architectural and historic character of the buildings and secure their long-term future through beneficial use,” they argue.

In another supporting statement, heritage consultant Ian Rowan said the farmhouse dates to the 1600s.

The complex consists of the farmhouse, which retains residential use, and a later 18th century cross-wing which is vacant, in a poor state, but would see repair through the proposals.

To the north east is a barn which has undergone alteration, whilst to the south west is a modern barn, said Mr Rowan.

“The proposal is to redevelop the listed building to provide residential accommodation and to convert the two barns for the same purpose.

“The proposals have been developed giving due consideration to the building’s listed status and its setting.

“The new buildings have been designed to complement the heritage assets and sit comfortably with local character,” his report said.

The proposed works will not impart any undue harm upon the significance of the buildings and their primary heritage values are conserved.

Rather, “alteration to the heritage assets offers the opportunity to bring them back into viable use, whilst also taking the opportunity to enhance significance where possible,” said Mr Rowan.

“Whilst there are some components that will cause low levels of harm, there are also positive impacts from the proposals which will offset this harm.

“However, any low levels of harm accrued should be considered in the light of bringing these degraded assets back into active use,” argues his report.
