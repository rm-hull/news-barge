A man has been convicted of murder after arranging the fatal shooting of a Microsoft manager who was killed in front of his daughter in Florida.

Jared Bridegan was shot in 2022 after getting out of his SUV to remove a tire from a road in Jacksonville Beach. The attack stumped police for months as they struggled to figure out why someone with no apparent enemies would be killed while his daughter sat in her car seat.

Prosecutors believe Bridegan was killed because of an ongoing dispute with his ex-wife, Shanna Gardner, over the parenting of their twins.

Mario Fernandez Saldana, 38, was convicted Wednesday of first-degree murder and solicitation of murder and faces an automatic sentence of life in prison. He’s married to Gardner, 39, whose trial is set to begin in September.

![Mario Fernandez Saldana, who is married to Jared Bridegan’s ex-wife, has been convicted of arranging for Jared Bridegan to be killed](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/26/20/38/AP26238711775978.jpg)

“They wanted a majority of the decision making. They wanted more custody, even 60-40," Assistant State Attorney Christina Stifler told the jury during closing arguments Tuesday in Duval County.

“It doesn’t matter if they were completely right about the situation or completely wrong. What matters is how they fixed it,” Stifler said. “They fixed it by executing a man when they had other alternatives available to them. That’s why we’re here.”

Bridegan was shot twice when he stopped his Volkswagen Atlas to move the tire out of the way. He had just dropped off his two children at Gardner's home after a meal with the twins.

At the time of his death, he was married to Kirsten Bridegan and they had two children. Kirsten Bridegan told the jury that her husband and Gardner had a “pretty terrible” post-divorce relationship and that there was “absolutely no trust.” She said it interfered with the children's medical appointments and meetings at school.

![Jared Bridegan, pictured with his wife Kirsten, was shot dead in Jacksonville Beach in February 2022](https://static.the-independent.com/2023/01/24/23/IMG_4683.jpg)

Fernandez Saldana hired a handyman named Henry Tenon to carry out the fatal ambush, prosecutors say. He had no personal connection to Bridegan but was a former tenant at Fernandez Saldana’s rental property in Jacksonville.

The prosecutor described Tenon as “basically homeless, squatting on people’s couches with overdrawn bank accounts.” Jurors were shown three checks, totaling $10,000, written to Tenon after the murder, marked as payments by Fernandez Saldana for handyman work and a business idea with “good luck!!” written on one.

Tenon’s DNA was found on the rim of the tire that was intentionally placed in the road, police said. He initially pleaded guilty and agreed to testify against Fernandez Saldana but backed out and will stand trial in 2027.

![Kirsten Bridegan reacts after the guilty verdict on Wednesday](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/26/20/39/AP26238711777749.jpg)

At Fernandez Saldana’s trial, his defense attorney Jesse Dreicer tried to argue that he had no motive to arrange to have Bridegan killed. Fernandez Saldana and Gardner are estranged and Gardner moved to Washington state after the killing. She was arrested in 2023.

“He certainly gained no benefit from the death of Jared Bridegan,” Dreicer said, noting there was no testimony that Fernandez Saldana told anyone that Bridegan “deserves to die.”

Gardner is the daughter of the co-founder of Stampin’ Up!, an arts-and-crafts company based in Utah.

Bridegan worked at Microsoft for less than a year before his death after serving as chief technology officer at Utah-based Clean Simple Eats.
