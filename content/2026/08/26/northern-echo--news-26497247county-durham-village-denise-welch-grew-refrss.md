Denise Welch is one of the North East’s best-known television personalities, but before Coronation Street, Waterloo Road and Loose Women, her teenage years were spent in the County Durham.

Welch was born in Tynemouth in 1958, but moved to the village of Ebchester with her family at the age of 13.

The relocation placed her close to Consett, where she attended Blackfyne Grammar School and began to find the confidence and direction that would lead her towards a career on stage and screen.

Set above the River Derwent between Consett and Shotley Bridge, Ebchester is a village with a much older story than its celebrity connection.

Its name is linked to the Roman fort of Vindomora, whose remains lie nearby, while St Ebba’s Church stands at the heart of a settlement that has long been shaped by its rural setting, the Derwent Valley and its proximity to the former steel town.

For Welch, arriving in the village came during a defining period.

She had previously attended schools in Whitley Bay and Newcastle, but after the move to County Durham, she joined Blackfyne Grammar School in Consett, where she gained five GCEs.

## Where Denise Welch found drama

Welch developed an interest in acting while at school after being cast in a production of the musical Finian’s Rainbow at the age of 14 - an experience that was a turning point.

She considered training as a teacher after leaving school, but her father and drama teacher encouraged her to apply for the Mountview Academy of Theatre Arts in London instead.

She was accepted, graduated in 1979 and went on to gain her Equity card while teaching dance at Watford Palace Theatre.

A later interview with i-D Magazine also shows how formative those school years were.

Looking back on her teenage years, Welch said she transferred to a local grammar school at 15 and met “a life-changing drama teacher”, who guided her towards drama school in London.

Although accounts differ slightly on the age at which she began at the Consett school, they agree that her County Durham education was central to the decision to pursue acting.


## From County Durham to TV fame

Welch began her professional career in the North East, working with Newcastle’s Live Theatre after training at Mountview.

Her early screen credits included Byker Grove and Spender, before she became a household name in ITV drama Soldier Soldier in 1993.

She later played Natalie Barnes in Coronation Street, appeared as Steph Haydock in BBC school drama Waterloo Road, and became a long-standing panellist on ITV’s Loose Women.

Her career has also included theatre work, memoirs and a public campaign to raise awareness of depression and addiction.

Welch’s family connection to the performing world extended to her sons: Matty Healy became frontman of The 1975, while Louis Healy has built an acting career.
