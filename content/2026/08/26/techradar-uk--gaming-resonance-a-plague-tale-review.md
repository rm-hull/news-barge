### TechRadar Verdict

As a prequel to Asobo Studio's pair of popular A Plague Tale games, Resonance takes the risk of not only introducing a new protagonist, but significantly switching up the franchise's enduring gameplay formula. Forgoing the stealth, survival, and relentless rat swarms the series is known for, Sophia's story is a swashbuckling one, packed with fast, action-focused combat and plenty of puzzles to solve and ancient artifacts to uncover. Aside from an occasionally clunky combat camera, this fresh approach works brilliantly, although faithful fans craving another foreboding trek through the Black Death might find its new formula a bit too safe and familiar.

#### Pros

- +Lightning-quick, layered, dynamic combat is a blast
- +Focus on action is nicely balanced with plenty of puzzles and exploration
- +Visually stunning world drenched in immersion-ratcheting details

#### Cons

- -Camera sometimes has trouble keeping up with the combat
- -Lighter tone and familiar action-adventure elements abandon franchise's defining elements

Why you can trust TechRadar

Several hours into *Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy*, my protagonist confidently strides into a battle arena and soaks in the cheers of an adoring audience. Wielding a sizable blade and sporting gleaming gold armor, the hero then unleashes a devastating, tornado-like attack, making short work of dozens of aggressive gladiators.

Needless to say, this third installment in Asobo Studio's *A Plague Tale* series is a far cry from the franchise's previous stealth-focused, rodent-infested entries. But I'm getting ahead of myself.

**Platform reviewed:** PS5**Available on:** PS5, Xbox Series X|S, PC**Release date:** August 27, 2026

*Resonance* is the latest chapter in the decidedly dark, Black Death-inspired series that previously put players behind the stealthy skills of siblings Amicia and Hugo as they faced relentless Inquisition soldiers and swarms of plague-spreading rats. But*Resonance* is also a prequel, set 15 years before the events of*A Plague Tale: Requiem* and starring Sophia, a returning, fan-favorite supporting character from that same entry.

Sophia's a smuggler and skilled swordsman, far more capable in battle than the franchise's previous brother and sister duo. As such, *Resonance* is a completely different type of game, a third-person action-adventure – with some light role-playing game (RPG) elements – that favors slitting throats and spilling entrails over sneaking in the shadows. It's a pretty drastic pivot, one that might be initially off-putting to longtime*A Plague Tale* players, but it generally works, allowing Asobo's beloved universe to successfully branch off into a different genre.

## Like Assassin's Creed…condensed

 ![Puzzle in Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qkigYBFRxRzrkevqYHS2FD.jpg) 


What *Resonance* recalls most, especially in its first few chapters, is a more streamlined, story-driven*Assassin's Creed* game. Like Ubisoft's sprawling action-RPG series, it sports stunning environmental visuals and plenty of crunchy, dynamic combat – complemented by lots of brutal finishers – as well as a good deal of exploration, parkour, and stealth. Heck, the latter even allows you to deliver death from above à la*Assassin's Creed*'s hidden-blade executions.

There's also a combat-enhancing skill tree to progress through, passive perk-granting relics to equip, and enough lore-expanding collectibles to make any member of the Assassin's Brotherhood envious. But on the plus side – especially for those burnt out on Ubisoft's bloated open-worlds – *Resonance* layers all these familiar elements into a far more straightforward, cinematic adventure that respects your time.

## Borrowing from the best

 ![Wide shot in Resonance: Plague Tale Legacy](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ufmRVaRQYUnG2KfK9dVfJN.png) 


But while it delivers, arguably, some of the *AC* series' best bits in a more digestible package, it also borrows inspiration from some other favorite franchises. Its prevalence of engaging spelunking and clever puzzle-solving gameplay, for example, recalls Lara Croft's greatest adventures, while its adrenaline-pumping set pieces could give Nathan Drake and Indiana Jones a run for their fortune and glory.

None of this is to say that Asobo is merely mimicking successful series' formulas that have come before. While its inspirations are obvious, they're also incredibly well realized and organically remixed into a much wider experience that feels fresh, allowing the *A**Plague Tale* franchise to reinvent itself for this prequel installment.

And while you won't find hordes of plague-ridden rats waiting around every corner of *Resonance*'s breathtaking Venice and Crete locations, its story is still very much embedded in the series' overarching narrative. Without spoiling too much, Sophia – it turns out – lived quite a life before taking to the high seas and helping out Amicia and Hugo in* Requiem*.

And like Hugo, she too is affected by something quite curious, supernatural even, that informs and shapes not just the storytelling, but the gameplay. Her mysterious ties to something quite ancient touch nearly everything in the game, from her puzzle-cracking prowess to that aforementioned gladiatorial arena battle.

But also like Hugo's blood curse, her affliction comes with more than its fair share of terrors. Sophia spends most of her time cracking skulls and conundrums in equal measure, but her swashbuckling sometimes gives way to the sort of nightmare-conjuring stretches that'd set the previous games' sibling duo desperately running for their lives.

Thankfully, Sophia also has some friends to lean on. In fact, *Resonance* does a fantastic job leveraging side characters to organically flesh out the world and Sophia. Rather than relying on forced exposition, the exchanges between the protagonist and her friends – and sometimes foes – flow naturally to deliver both fun banter and integral information.

## Less plague rats, more plundering

 ![Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy's main character, Sophia, standing inside an ancient cave.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/BZFoiA6ShMHimfTQLaFtLd.jpg) 


Whether with a buddy or on her own, Sophia does indeed occasionally find herself in frightening situations evocative of the first two games' more nail-biting moments. These scarier, often survival-focused moments are some of the game's best, but they also take a backseat to what more often feels like a lighter, blade-clashing, plundering romp.

Again, this isn't so much a knock against the game, as *Resonance* does a brilliant job blending inspirations from the aforementioned properties while also layering in plenty of its own personality. But passionate fans of the previous*A Plague Tale* titles, craving more of their suffocating atmosphere and killer rodents, may find*Resonance* a tad too lighthearted, familiar, and formulaic. While it's not without its scares, surprise betrayals, and emotional gut punches, it's still very much a spirited action-adventure that draws more from*Black Flag* than the Black Plague.

**WhileResonance's combat starts out simple enough, with a couple of death-dealing combos delivered via Sophia's sword and dagger, it quickly ramps up. The game's at its thumb-blistering best when you're leveraging everything in her growing arsenal – from unlocked offensive and defensive maneuvers to devastating special attacks, throwable environmental items, and even her trusty grappling hook – to clear a room full of goons with style to spare.** 

Beyond that gripe specific to those hoping for a return to rat-infested Middle Ages France, *Resonance*'s battle camera could have benefited from a bit more fine-tuning. Sophia's sword- and dagger-based melees are generally quite rewarding, especially once new weapons, moves, specials, and perks are unlocked and layered into her blood-spilling repertoire; there’s also some welcome nuance and depth, like stun-inflicting parries and a risk-reward system that sees you replenish a health segment with each henchman slain. But the camera can have some difficulty keeping up with all this lightning-quick action. Toss in the lack of a target lock-on, and you're occasionally left wondering what cool thing just happened off-screen.

Nitpicks aside, *Resonance* makes a compelling case for expanding the*A Plague Tale* universe, while also serving up 15 or so hours of solid third-person action-adventuring. You'll likely complete Sophia's campaign wondering what exactly the future holds for the series' other characters and narrative threads, but you also won't hold any regrets having conquered a swashbuckling romp as rewarding as a treasure chest brimming with shiny trinkets.

## Should you play Resonance: A Plague Tale?

## Play it if...

**You're a fan of the previousA Plague Talestories***Resonance* significantly changes up the gameplay of the previous two series' installments, but its narrative unfolds within the same universe. Protagonist Sophia is a supporting character from*A Plague Tale: Requiem*, and her prequel yarn is still connected to the larger series' story, albeit loosely, as well as to its existing characters, Amicia and Hugo.

**You're craving a swashbucking action-adventure in the vein ofAssassin's CreedandTomb Raider***Resonance* trades much of the moody atmosphere and stealth- and survival-fueled gameplay of the franchise's first two games for a more traditional third-person action-adventure. Expect quick, dynamic combat, plenty of puzzle-solving gameplay, a bit of exploration, and some epic set-piece moments that favor cinematic style over slow-burn storytelling. 

## Don't play it if...

**You're expecting an experience similar to the series' previous installments**

As an original prequel story, *Resonance* doesn't just introduce a new protagonist in Sophia, but also significantly switches up the series' gameplay and atmosphere. The former feels far more like familiar action-adventure fare, while its world, characters, and storytelling all support a lighter, less foreboding tone. 

## Accessibility features

*Resonance* significantly expands on the accessibility options of the series' previous two installments, offering a number of visual assistance options, including UI, HUD, and subtitle customizations, as well as high contrast and colorblind modes.

Blood effects can be toggled on and off, and five different difficulty settings allow you to tailor the experience to your playstyle and skill level. These options range from a story-focused mode – that puts parrying and dodging on auto – to a deep difficulty mode for those craving the steepest challenge. Difficulty options can also be changed at any time.

## How I reviewed Resonance: A Plague Tale

I completed *Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy's* campaign in about 15 hours, but clocked in another 5 or so revisiting chapters to gather missed collectibles.

I ran the game on a PS5 Pro using my budget TCL 4K 65" TV, with HDR enabled. For audio, I tested with both the display's onboard stereo speakers and Sony's PlayStation Pulse Elite headset.
