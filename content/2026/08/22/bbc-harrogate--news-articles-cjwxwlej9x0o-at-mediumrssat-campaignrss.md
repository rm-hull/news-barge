The mural on the Grade II listed building in Scarborough will be extended

**Plans to extend a prominent outdoor mural on a Grade II listed building in a North Yorkshire town centre have been approved by councillors.**

They voted in favour of the application by North Yorkshire Council for the artwork at 3 West Square in Scarborough.

The council said the aim was to enhance the town centre by fostering a "stronger sense of civic pride", but some residents argued the design lacked "sufficient artistic quality".

However, during last week's meeting, councillors were reminded that the application was for listed building consent and not for judgment on the design.

The mural faces Westborough and will be extended towards the gable-end elevation, according to the Local Democracy Service Reporting Service.

It is currently 26ft (8m) wide and 36ft (11m) high, while the extension will be 21ft (6.5m) wide and about 13ft (4m) high.

The proposed extension of the mural

Speaking at the meeting, resident Susan Millar objected to the plans, arguing that the mural made "poor use of the available wall space".

"The entire project should be reconsidered and opened to tender for alternative artistic proposals," she said.

"Public art should benefit both residents and visitors, foster civic pride and contribute positively to the appearance of a prominent gateway location opposite the railway station."

Members of the Scarborough and Whitby area planning committee debated the artistic merits of the mural, with several members stating that it would be "a positive addition that enhanced the appearance of West Square".

However, council planners told the committee it was not "acting as an arbiter of artistic merit", and any wider concerns regarding the process through which the mural was commissioned were "beyond the remit of the committee".

Councillors voted to approve the application, with five votes in favour and one against.
