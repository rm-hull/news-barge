## 7. Amazon ruined the Kindle Oasis?

 ![Amazon Kindle](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Js7EBjhu2jTkfATfjvBYji.jpeg) 


A Kindle Oasis update has ruined the ereader for many, thanks to a change that has frustrated users.

The first change applies stricter DRM (Digital Rights Management) to books on the device, meaning most DRM-removal tools won’t work anymore. While DRM can help fight piracy, it also makes it harder to share the book with non-Kindle ereaders (or ereaders without the Kindle app) if you ever want to take your library with you when you change devices. This update isn’t new, but the Oasis was one of the last few to not have it.

The other change means you can’t keep reading a Libby book beyond its due date by turning airplane mode on. The workaround let users hold onto borrowed books, but now the app requires an internet connection to read your titles.

## 6. The UK got ready for plug-in solar

 ![Balcony solar panel](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/AkTJa79sawhiUsP3NqsAKA.jpg) 


Plug-in solar kits are a hot topic in both the US and the UK, with the latter planning to legalize the low-cost home energy helpers on August 27. Ahead of that date next week, we’ve started to see the first kits get the regulatory green light.

## 5. Tesla’s Robotaxi had a bad day

 ![The view from the rear passenger seats of a ride in a Tesla Robotaxi](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/JYHq2wQHiozy684Uz3oCa.jpg) 


With impressive comic timing, Tesla’s Robotaxi was this week filmed plowing through some plastic traffic posts in Texas before carrying on with its journey — just weeks after Tesla had been crowing about its “impeccable safety record” with “zero notable incidents”.

Okay, the video shot by the Robotaxi’s distressed passenger doesn’t exactly show a *GTA 6*-style pile-up, but the timing is poor, with Tesla also reportedly planning to trial its fully autonomous Cybercab on public roads this month. We might carry on taking those human-driven cabs for a little while longer.

## 4. Grand Theft Auto 6 leaked

 ![A Grand Theft Auto 6 screenshot provided by Rockstar Games.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/hoLh5qUFpk5hv8txQ5BNCA.jpg) 


The most hotly anticipated game of 2026 was due to get a major reveal via a Netflix special next week, but Christmas has come early, so to speak, as several major leaks came out this week — including GTA 6’s map, new gameplay, and cutscenes.

Hacker group Cyberleek has claimed responsibility for the breach as a protest against Rockstar’s digital-only release strategy. Even physical disc cases will include only a digital download code.

However, this data dump might not completely spoil Rockstar and Netflix’s announcement plans. It seems at least some of the leaks are over a year old, according to people with information on the subject, though the polish was quite high, so it’s unclear how different the end result will actually be.

## 3. Meta’s smart glasses bans continued

 ![Orange RayBan Meta Smart Glasses in front of a wall of colorful lenses including green, blue, yellow and pink](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/f2ErG2ZjSDQM6Bsj7MgT9A.jpg) 


While governments are weighing up how they’ll deal with the rise of smart glasses and the privacy concerns they’re raising (as pervs and creeps use them to spy on people), it seems their action might not be required.

That’s because we’re seeing more and more private businesses announce bans on the glasses across various spaces, including pubs, cafes, and restaurants — UK cinemas and regular theatres are even considering banning the camera glasses, though their concerns are also focused on piracy (where folks could record or even livestream a movie).

It seems Meta’s Ray-Bans have a tough road ahead. Perhaps things will eventually turn around at Connect in September.

## 2. Meta might owe $1.4 trillion

 ![Mark Zuckerberg](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/jfWGLZMm6MuZWbZj7VUHJ5.jpg) 


We’re used to company fines often pales in comparison to the profits and growth the business made by taking the punished action — they’re often just a slap on the wrist; it’s merely ‘the cost of doing business.’ That’s not so true about a lawsuit Meta finds itself in.

Attorneys General across 32 US states argue that Meta has used its technology to get teens (and younger) users hooked on its platforms — and that the company ignored the harms this action could cause. If Meta loses the suit, it could be on the hook for $1.4 trillion, roughly the value of its entire market capitalization (i.e., the combined value of all its shares).

Meta calls the AGs' claims "unsubstantiated" and the potential penalties "vastly disproportionate."

We don’t yet know how things will turn out, but be prepared for both sides to seriously fight their case in court.
