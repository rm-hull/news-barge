A County Durham market town has been praised by visitors for its peaceful atmosphere, historic streets and picturesque countryside setting.

Stanhope, in Weardale, sits at the heart of the Durham Dales and is surrounded by rolling hills, open moorland and the River Wear.

  Stanhope Castle 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The town is a popular base for walkers and visitors exploring the North Pennines, while its traditional stone buildings and riverside setting have also earned it a reputation as one of the area’s most scenic places.

Some visitors have described spending time in Stanhope as being “like stepping back in time”.

## Riverside walks and stepping stones

One of the town’s best-known attractions is the set of stepping stones across the River Wear.

The large stones allow walkers to cross the river when conditions are suitable and have become a popular spot for photographs.


Riverside paths also run through the area, giving visitors views of the surrounding countryside and easy access to the centre of Stanhope.

The town’s historic buildings, stone bridges and traditional streets add to its old-fashioned feel.

## Gateway to the Durham Dales

Stanhope is well placed for exploring Weardale and the wider North Pennines.

The surrounding countryside includes moorland, wooded valleys and walking and cycling routes, attracting visitors throughout the year.

  Stanhope sign 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The Durham Dales Centre is also based in the town and provides tourist information as well as shops, local crafts and refreshments.

Stanhope is often used as a starting point for trips further into the dale, with several scenic villages and countryside routes nearby.

## Historic landmarks

The town has several notable historic attractions.

St Thomas the Apostle Church is one of Stanhope’s most recognisable buildings and has origins dating back to the medieval period.

  Weardale Railway, Stanhope 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Inside the church is a font made from Frosterley marble, a local limestone known for containing fossilised coral.

Another unusual landmark is Stanhope’s fossil tree, which is around 320 million years old.

The fossilised remains were discovered in a nearby quarry and are now displayed in the town, providing a reminder of the area’s geological history.

Stanhope Castle is another prominent building and forms part of the town’s historic centre.

## Weardale Railway

Visitors can also explore the area on the Weardale Railway, which runs through the valley.

The heritage railway offers journeys through the County Durham countryside and links several communities along the route.

## Popular with visitors

Stanhope has also gained attention on social media, with visitors sharing photographs and videos of the stepping stones, river and surrounding hills.

Its combination of countryside, history and traditional architecture has helped make it a popular destination for day trips.

The name Stanhope is believed to come from Old English words relating to stone and a valley, and is commonly interpreted as meaning “stony valley”.

For visitors looking for a quieter day out in County Durham, the town offers riverside walks, historic landmarks and easy access to some of the region’s best-known countryside.

With its traditional streets and peaceful setting, it is easy to see why some visitors have compared Stanhope to “stepping back in time”.
