# What you need to know about the upcoming lunar eclipse

![A reddish-orange shadow will be cast across the moon’s surface during the eclipse](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/12/16/09/GettyImages-1320155777.jpeg)

- A deep partial lunar eclipse will shroud approximately 96 per cent of the moon's surface from Thursday 27 August into Friday 28 August.
- Nearly one billion people across North, Central, and South America will have a complete view, with over three billion people worldwide able to see at least part of the event.
- Earth will align between the sun and moon, casting a reddish-orange shadow across the lunar surface during a partial stage lasting over three hours.
- The event forms the second part of a celestial pair, taking place two weeks after a total solar eclipse swept across Greenland, Iceland, and Spain.
- This is the fourth eclipse of the year, with the next total lunar eclipse not expected until New Year's Eve 2028.
