ChatGPT has accumulated a surprisingly large collection of education tools, including Study Mode and new plugins designed specifically for college students and educators.

The trick is using them to learn rather than outsourcing the learning itself. OpenAI explicitly describes its approach as helping students understand material rather than simply shortcutting it, which is a useful rule to keep in mind before handing ChatGPT the first assignment of the semester.

Here are five ways I would set up ChatGPT before classes get properly underway.

## 1. Study Mode for all

 ![Study mode in ChatGPT](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/v7rJ4f7ZrdF6UPdwndzYZS.jpg) 


ChatGPT's aptly named Study Mode is the obvious place to start with using the AI for learning, and it doesn't even require a paid subscription. You simply start a normal conversation with ChatGPT and type **@study**, or click the**+** button and search for**Study**.

Once activated, Study Mode changes ChatGPT's responses from direct answers to more Socratic behavior. It asks questions, provides hints, and checks whether you understand each step before moving forward.

Try prompts like, *"Teach me derivatives, asking one question at a time, and give me a hint when I'm stuck."*

You can also upload a photo of a problem or, where file uploads are available, add your class notes and ask ChatGPT to work from those. For instance, you could upload what you wrote down in class and tell ChatGPT to *"Use these notes to teach me today's lesson. Start by identifying and explaining concepts I need to understand, then quiz me before moving to the next."*

Study Mode can work with uploaded files and images, making it much more useful when it knows what your actual class is covering rather than delivering a generic miniature lecture.

## 2. ChatGPT College Plugin

 ![ChatGPT Study](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Ns59HnXpxmNDF4niSHc8A6.png) 


A newer and more focused option is ChatGPT's College Student plugin. It's not universally available like Study Mode, only through ChatGPT Edu, so your university needs to offer it and make it available. Students who can access it get a collection of learning tools.

Of particular interest is its interactive learning site creation. You can share your course material to make interactive visual explanations, while also getting guided tutoring on concepts you find difficult. The AI can take uploaded lecture slides or notes and make a unique website full of study questions and able to provide feedback on your answers.

Depending on how confident you feel, you could ask ChatGPT for something as elaborate as, *"Build me a study plan for the next two weeks using these course materials and my upcoming deadlines, and spend extra time on the topics I struggled with in the quiz."*

## 3. Syllabus study

 ![ChatGPT College Student Plugin](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/RaFoXRp5pbhUv9PEFNJHtW.png) 


My memory of student life is that the syllabus traditionally enjoys about 20 minutes of intense attention before beginning its long journey toward the bottom of a backpack. ChatGPT can make it considerably more useful. Upload the syllabus to ChatGPT, and it can have a much more productive lifespan.

I'd suggest a prompt like, *"Read this syllabus and create a week-by-week study plan. Include every test, paper, and deadline. Suggest what I should begin preparing for at least a week ahead of time."*

You can ask about the extra busy weeks and ideas on making them as easy as possible too. If you let ChatGPT send you alerts, you might avoid the end-of-semester crunch when every deadline hits at once and you still only have 24 hours a day to study.

## 4. Flashcards

 ![ChatGPT College Student Plugin](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/AunXZc8Cy4YcTGUT5P7ioW.png) 


ChatGPT can also save you from spending an entire evening making flashcards instead of studying them. You can ask it to make flashcards from your notes, telling it to *"Focus on particulalry confusing concepts and terms."*

If you ask for one card at a time, it turns the exercise into an actual study session instead of generating another giant page of material to stare at, hopefully.

With the College Student plugin, flashcards can become part of the interactive learning experience rather than simply appearing as text in the conversation. You could ask: *"Turn this week's lecture material into flashcards and organize them by topic."*

## 5. Quiz time

 ![College student wearing a grey hoodie falls asleep in class](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/rzczu4ge76semQoX5oDhFU.jpg) 


Quizzing you on whether you actually have learned anything from studying is vital, and ChatGPT can be a big help in that regard. With its Study Mode, you can create practice questions, quiz yourself one question at a time, get it to explain mistakes and change the difficulty as you progress.

Upload your course material and try asking for a *"10-question practice quiz based only on these notes. Ask one question at a time, don't reveal the answer until I respond, explain anything I get wrong, and make the next question harder when I answer correctly."* 

The College Student plugin can turn that same process into a more structured interactive quiz built around the course sources you provide. You can ask for more complex practice exams, and set up interactive tests that analyze your weakest areas for further study and quizzing.

With or without the plug-in, having ChatGPT close the metaphorical book and start asking questions is a much faster way to discover whether anything actually stuck.
