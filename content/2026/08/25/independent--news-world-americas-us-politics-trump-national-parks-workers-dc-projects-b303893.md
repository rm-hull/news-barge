National Park Service emergency responders have been recruited to work on President Donald Trump’s beautification projects in Washington, D.C., including fountain renovations and repairs to the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, according to a new report.

Nearly 300 employees from the Park Service’s “incident management team” — which responds to wildfires and other emergencies — have been deployed to the capital, CNN reported, citing internal documents and agency workers.

“It just shows how much DC operations are getting priority above all other park units and staff,” an unnamed Park Service employee told the outlet.

The employees reassigned to Washington, D.C., previously worked at parks across the country, including in Alaska and California. Their duties have since included cleaning up the East Potomac golf course, according to CNN.

The Trump administration pushed back on the CNN report with an Interior Department spokesperson telling *The Independent* that "NPS sets up incident management teams for all sorts of things, from major events to major projects" and that "people were not pulled from the front lines of wildfires." 

![Nearly 300 employees from the Park Service’s “incident management team” have been deployed to the capital](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/14/47/GettyImages-2282945980.jpeg)

“President Donald J. Trump has done more to make our nation's capital a shining beacon than any other president in the history of this country,” a spokesperson told *The Independent*. “The Department of the Interior, the National Park Service and our U.S. Park Police had the honor of being part of this effort to Make D.C. Safe and Beautiful.”

Since returning to office, Trump has devoted significant resources to revitalizing Washington’s public spaces, including reviving defunct fountains, gilding statues and repairing the Reflecting Pool — a multimillion-dollar effort that has produced mixed results.

The administration has also pursued grand projects like a massive ballroom at the White House, which the president says is critical for national security, and plans for a 250-foot victory arch along the Potomac River.

![Since returning to office, Trump and Interior Secretary Doug Burgum (pictured left) have devoted significant time and resources to revitalizing Washington’s public spaces](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/14/48/GettyImages-2286830742.jpeg)

Incident management employees work across the country’s 63 national parks, responding to planned events such as Fourth of July fireworks displays as well as “unplanned emergencies,” including wildfires, floods, hurricanes and search-and-rescue operations, according to the Park Service. The team includes firefighters, radio operators and liaison officers.

“The immediate NPS response for parks significantly impacted by severe weather and other natural disasters is often coordinated by an NPS incident management team,” the agency’s website states.

One Park Service employee described the deployment of nearly 300 team members to Washington for Trump’s “safe and beautiful” initiative — a federal effort to clean up and improve public spaces — as “crazy abnormal.” The staffer noted that only about 60 personnel were brought in for D.C.’s National Cherry Blossom Festival.

Incident management team members have also been tasked with preparing “highly detailed status reports” for Interior Secretary Doug Burgum’s office, CNN reported. One employee said these “operational details” are typically overseen by less senior officials.

“It’s highly unusual,” the staffer said. “Political folks have wanted to know even the tiniest details, and it’s always about schedule. Will it be on time?”

![Incident management employees also assist with other unplanned events, such as floods and search-and-rescue operations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/16/27/Screenshot-2026-08-25-at-12-30-51-PM.png)

The employee said the Parks Service is also operating with reduced resources because of staffing cuts spearheaded by former special government employee Elon Musk’s DOGE initiative, as well as a hiring freeze.

The Park Service workforce has been cut by nearly 25 per cent since 2025 through terminations, early retirements and federal buyouts. Trump’s proposed 2027 budget would eliminate an additional 3,967 full-time positions, reducing staffing by 31 percent from 2025 levels.

The Interior Department’s reliance on the incident management team in Washington appears likely to continue. According to internal documents, the department is requesting “continued support through September ” in order to “sustain the great work underway”  by relocated staff.

![Incident management employees respond to both ‘planned events’ and ‘unplanned emergencies,’ such as wildfires](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/25/16/36/GettyImages-1410656363.jpeg)

Exactly what work the employees might be assigned in D.C. remains unclear. Trump, fresh off hosting his Freedom 250 IndyCar race this past weekend, has expressed interest in refurbishing a golf course in the capital.

“The wholistic approach which is common practice across the entire Parks system, from team members across the district and country, to celebrate our nation's 250 demonstrates what makes this country great,” the Interior Department spokesperson also told *The Independent*. “Across D.C., residents and visitors are excited to see the streets are safe and the city revitalized to the grand destination it was meant to be.”

*The Associated Press* reported earlier this week that the Park Service received a federal directive earlier this year to deprioritize maintenance projects nationwide in favor of other priorities, including work on the Reflecting Pool and initiatives connected to America’s 250th anniversary.

*The Atlantic* also reported that Trump recently used nearly $700,000 in taxpayer funds allocated to the Park Service to replace a White House walkway, despite claiming that he had paid for the project himself.
