Dozens of new homes could be created under plans to transform a city centre building into a 63-room co-living space.

Arngrove House, located in Sunderland's Sunniside district, is the focus of the proposal submitted by Nova Co-Living.

Developers said the scheme provides an “alternative to both conventional self-contained flats and a traditional sui generis HMO.”

The new proposal would shift the building away from its previous mixture of office and café uses.

   

*(Image: Nova Co-Living)*

The scheme would offer 63 studio suites with private bathrooms and kitchenettes.

There would also be communal facilities including a lounge, dining room, fitness suite, co-working area, games lounge, rooftop terrace, and cycle storage.

A design and access statement submitted with the application described the scheme as a “catalyst for the regeneration of Sunniside.”

Developers said the project is aimed at young professionals, graduates, key workers, and contract workers seeking “flexible, high-quality city-centre living designed to support social interaction and community.”

   

*(Image: Nova Co-Living)*

It would also include on-site management, concierge services, CCTV, and a programme of resident events.

The project would combine the modern commercial building and adjacent townhouses at 3–6 Frederick Street into a “single managed co-living residence.”

Proposed changes to Arngrove House include a two-storey extension and a new, set-back ground floor entrance.

The design and access statement said: “The scheme will be operated by Nova Co-Living under a structured management framework combining daily on-site attendance with a dedicated digital platform providing transparent all-inclusive billing, proactive maintenance reporting, and a curated programme of resident events

"This approach directly addresses the common shortcomings of traditional shared housing and delivers a stable, well-managed residential environment.”

The proposal includes 40 single-occupancy and 23 double-occupancy suites, allowing for up to 86 residents.

Although primarily car-free, the plan includes one parking space for a receptionist or cleaner and a short-term space for deliveries and drop-offs.

A decision on the proposal will be made once the public consultation process has concluded.
