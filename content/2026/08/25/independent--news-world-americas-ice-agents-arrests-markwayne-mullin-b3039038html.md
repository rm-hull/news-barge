Newly released government figures show that U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement arrested nearly 50,000 individuals in July, the highest single-month arrest total recorded so far under the second Trump administration.

This sharp rise shows the administration is continuing to push its mass-deportation agenda.

Earlier this year, federal officials shifted away from high-profile operations in major American cities that had provoked widespread public backlash, turning instead toward lower-profile enforcement actions that remain deeply disruptive despite drawing less attention.

Data ICE provided to the Deportation Data Project at the University of California, Berkeley, and later analyzed by The Associated Press shows that 49,571 arrests took place in July.

That tally represents a 15 percent climb from the 43,021 arrests made in June and a 70 percent surge from the 29,241 recorded in February following an enforcement push in Minnesota.

In the month prior to Trump taking office, monthly immigration arrests hovered just above 8,000, consisting largely of individuals transferred to ICE custody from city or state correctional facilities for deportation.

During Trump’s first year in office, totals expanded as the administration relaxed constraints on where and whom agents could arrest while directing billions of dollars into the agency.

By December, total monthly arrests had risen to more than 40,177, according to data secured through a Freedom of Information Act lawsuit.

![The Department of Homeland Security, which oversees U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, has hit a new record with some 51,000 migrant arrests in July](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/23/24/GettyImages-2287296268.jpg)

Arrest totals dropped to roughly 30,000 in February following public outcry and criticism from Democratic lawmakers over two fatal shootings in Minnesota in January.

After holding relatively flat for several months, the number of individuals arrested surged to over 43,000 in June and then to approximately 49,500 in July.

During his confirmation hearing earlier this year, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin pledged to keep ICE out of the news, hinting at a potentially softer approach to enforcement.

Yet his tenure has been shadowed by multiple fatal shootings of immigrants during encounters with ICE officers, and the latest figures show Mullin has not steered the agency away from President Donald Trump’s vision of mass deportations.

Highlighting their central importance to the administration's goals, Texas and Florida accounted for nearly 20,000 of the arrests in July.

Both states have made extensive use of 287g cooperation agreements with ICE, effectively enabling state and local police to act as arms of federal immigration enforcement.

This latest surge in arrests coincides with the agency expanding its workforce by hiring 12,000 new deportation officers and investigative agents, backed by billions of dollars provided by Congress last summer.
