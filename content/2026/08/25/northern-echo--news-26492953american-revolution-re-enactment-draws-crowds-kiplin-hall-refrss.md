Revolutionary history came to life in North Yorkshire as visitors stepped back in time to explore the events that shaped the birth of the United States and the region's links to its founding.

The event took place at Kiplin Hall and Gardens, a Jacobean estate with deep historical ties to early American history through its first owner, George Calvert.

Calvert, who became the First Baron Baltimore, founded the colony that would become the state of Maryland.

James Etherington, director of Kiplin Hall, said: "We are delighted to host re-enactment events of this sort.

"Last year visitors discovered what life was like in 1625, when Kiplin Hall was built.

"This year, in line with the 250th anniversary of American independence, we took visitors back to 1776.

  The fearsome effects of musket fire caught on camera 

*(Image: DL10 Media)*

"Re-enactors help us to bring our historic spaces to life in a very real way.

"Creating conversations with visitors about areas they are interested in.

"From how ladies of the period dressed to the bangs of the big guns, the weekend brought the past to life for over 600 visitors.

  Dressing the Georgian Lady – a talk took place set to the backdrop of historic Kiplin Hall 

*(Image: DL10 Media)*

"We look forward to revealing the period for next year’s re-enactment in the spring."

The event, held on August 22 and 23, transported visitors to the era of the American Revolution, which lasted from 1765 to 1783.

Set against the stately backdrop of Kiplin Hall, the re-enactment included demonstrations of 18th-century cooking, cannon and musket fire, and the encampment.

  Modest fashions of the period also provided projection from the sun for ladies 

*(Image: DL10 Media)*

Visitors engaged with “loyalists” who supported British rule and “patriots” fighting for independence.

The American Revolution saw 13 British colonies reject imperial control and declare independence, ultimately forming the United States of America.

This year marks the 250th anniversary of the Declaration of Independence, signed on July 4, 1776.

  Naval officers of the period charging into battle as well 

*(Image: DL10 Media)*

Lord Baltimore's legacy lives on in the state of Maryland, including the city of Baltimore and Calvert County, all named after him and his descendants.

Organisers also acknowledged the longer history of the continent, noting that Native American people lived in North America long before European colonisation.

Today, around 2 per cent of the US population—about 6.6 million people—identify as Native American.

Kiplin Hall and Gardens, which is open six days a week and closed on Thursdays, regularly hosts historical events and educational programmes.

The estate’s connection to transatlantic history continues to draw visitors interested in both British and American heritage.

More information about upcoming events and the history of Kiplin Hall can be found at www.kiplinhall.co.uk.
