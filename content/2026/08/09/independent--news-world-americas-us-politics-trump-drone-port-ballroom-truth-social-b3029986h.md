President Donald Trump spent his Saturday evening on Truth Social, releasing a series of seemingly AI-generated posts mocking up the planned White House ballroom and its rooftop “drone port.”

In three different images all titled “DronePort”, the president depicted the roof of the ballroom structure covered by numerous military drone aircraft, armed soldiers and refueling trucks, turning the placid grounds of the White House into a major military installation. The images were the same ones he originally posted in May, as he attacked a federal judge for delaying the project by ruling that the administration lacked congressional authorization.

“This great and very important military asset sits atop the heavily protected Ballroom at the White House. It provides National Security for Washington, D.C., and will protect future Presidents!!!” Trump wrote on Truth Social.

The president’s focus on his ballroom is not new. His fixation on the military center and “drone port” pictured in the three images he released late Saturday evening comes, however, as he has shifted his rhetoric about the construction project to focus on its planned security features in an attempt to win further congressional support for funding and battle a court order stopping construction. Money for the ballroom was originally set to be approved by Congress as part of legislation passed earlier this year, but it was stripped out due to lack of support in the Senate.

Like his first post back in May, this latest posting by the president follows his defeat in court over the project.

![One of three AI-generated images depicting the planned White House "drone port"](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/09/13/08/f99e14baee2ea1bf.png)

A federal appeals court delivered the second blow to the project on Friday that the administration must cease construction of the project until congressional approval is gained, leaving the building process in limbo and putting another awkward test to the Republican-controlled House and Senate with just a few months to go before the midterm elections.

The president frequently veers off onto tangents about the ballroom and its construction process during unrelated events and planned remarks as the project (and the totality of his D.C. beautification efforts) have become a clear, sustained priority that at time often trumps matters that would seemingly be more important to Republican voters, to say nothing of the officials and dignitaries often in the room listening to him wander off topic and derail his own speeches.

His efforts to beautify D.C. include projects to restore fountains and other park features around the District of Columbia as well as the now heavily scrutinized effort to restore the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, fixing leaks in the structure’s base and repainting the surface with an aqua-blue swimming pool coating.

That project has also consumed his attention in recent days as his U.S. attorney for the District of Columbia, Jeanine Pirro, dropped charges against an Olympian falsely accused by the Trump administration of vandalizing the pool’s new coating with a knife or other sharp object. Pirro admitted in court filings that the damage to the coating appeared to be the result of a faulty installation process.

Apart from the chunks of pool coating that were visible in the waters as the new surface failed, the waters of the pool turned lime-green this summer as park workers struggled to deal with a massive algae bloom which the president also previously attempted to blame on vandalism. Like the failed installation of the coating, the algae was actually thought to be the result of contractor error during the addition of new micro-bubbler devices to the structure.

The president has pivoted to describing the ballroom and “drone port” project as an issue of national security as his popularity and subsequently the appeal of his D.C. renovation projects has waned on Capitol Hill. On Friday, he attacked the appeals court that ruled against his adminsistration in court on the project for supposedly endangering the lives of current and future occupants of the White House, with his rationale being that the planned structure’s anti-drone capabilities were the only thing that could guard from such attacks.

“The Circuit Court’s decision, which is totally wrong on the vital subject of STANDING, of which the plaintiff has none, severely jeopardizes the lives and welfare of the people who work, and will be working, at the White House — including all future Presidents of the United States, and their families, as well as the lives of all those visiting,” wrote Trump.
