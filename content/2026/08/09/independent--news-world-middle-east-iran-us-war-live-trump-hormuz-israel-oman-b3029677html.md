## Two Israeli soldiers killed in Lebanon explosion as military announces first deaths since fragile Hezbollah truce

Two Israeli soldiers were killed in an explosion in southern Lebanon on Thursday, marking the first Israeli fatalities since a fragile truce between Israel and the Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah was implemented in June.

Four additional soldiers sustained serious injuries, the military said. The deaths had been reported by Israeli media the previous day.

An Israeli military official, speaking anonymously in line with army policy, stated that "an explosion was detonated" as the soldiers entered a site in the village of Majdal Zoun, adding that the nature of the explosive device was under investigation.

## UAE oil tanker attacked by missile in Strait of Hormuz

The United Arab Emirates condemned an Iranian missile attack on its carrier as it passed through the Strait of Hormuz today, accusing Tehran of "acts of piracy".

State news agency WAM initially reported ADNOC as saying one of its vessels had been targeted by a missile early on Saturday and that the situation was under control.

The UAE Foreign Ministry later condemned what it called "the hostile Iranian attack" on an ADNOC vessel.

Neither statement gave details on the tanker, its cargo or possible damage. No injuries were reported.

The ministry said the attack violated a U.N. Security Council resolution on freedom of navigation and accused Iran's Revolutionary Guards of "acts of piracy" by targeting commercial shipping and using the waterway as a tool of economic pressure.

It called on Iran to halt the attacks and reopen the strait fully and unconditionally.

## Fallout from war in Iran is leaving millions vulnerable to hunger

As the US, Iran and Oman edge towards an interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, months of insecurity and disruption are leaving millions more vulnerable to hunger in Yemen.

The economic fallout from the regional conflict has driven up food and fuel prices, while more than four months of conflict with Iran has driven renewed escalation in Yemen, compounding one of the world's worst humanitarian crises.

More than half the population were already facing hunger, but slow progress from world leaders to re-open the Strait has caused months of disruption to global food and energy supplies, disrupting humanitarian aid operations.

## Pentagon urges defence industry to hurry up making weapons

The Pentagon has asked the US defence industry to ramp up production of weapons amid reports of depleted stockpiles due to the war in Iran, according to a memo.

The *Washington Post* reports that secretary Steve Feinberg told industry leaders on Wednesday that they had 3 weeks to bring together plants to “drive significantly faster, more aggressive delivery schedules and/or increased production for critical capabilities”.

“We must dramatically accelerate our program schedules and expand our production capacity now,” he was reported to have said.

## Iran says Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until US meets six demands

Iran’s top security official has said the Strait of Hormuz would remain shut until the US meets six demands, including compensation for the war.

Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, said on Saturday that the US must end all of its military interventions across the region before the strait can reopen fully to traffic.

He also demanded that all US forces pull back from around Iran, that Washington should compensate Tehran for “the two wars of aggression”, and that it should lift its sanctions.

## Iran's president says now is best time for an agreement- Iranian media

Iran president Masoud Pezeshkian said now is the best time for an agreement because Iran is “strong and united and seen as victorious in war,” Iranian media reported.

## What compensation does Iran want from the US to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

The head of Iran’s supreme national security council, Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, has said that the Strait of Hormuz will remain shut until the US meets six conditions.

In comments carried by Iran’s IRNA news agency on Saturday, Zolghadr laid out his terms.

Included in full, in his words, these were:

1 - Never threaten Iran with any language or insult the sanctities of this nation.

2- End the war and aggression against Iran and its allies in Lebanon, Palestine, Yemen, and Iraq forever.

3- Lift the naval blockade and withdraw its military forces (naval and air) from around Iran.

4 - Pay the damages of the two wars of aggression and imposition on Iran without any reduction or reduction.

5 - Lift the cruel and illegal sanctions against the Iranian nation.

6- Unconditionally release the frozen and stolen assets of the Iranian people.
