A surge in abandoned kittens is putting pressure on animal rescue services in North Yorkshire.

Blue Cross in Thirsk has described the rising numbers as a "hidden pet welfare crisis," with many of the animals requiring urgent care.

The charity believes the cost-of-living crisis is preventing some owners from neutering their cats, leading to more unplanned litters.

Joe Cummins, assistant manager at Blue Cross in Thirsk, said: "I have never seen kittens coming in for help on a scale like this.

"Almost every week we are admitting kittens through other organisations such as councils and local vets.

"Some are incredibly young and need round-the-clock care to give them the best chance of survival.

"This is in addition to the many unplanned and unwanted litters being brought in as well as pregnant cats."

Blue Cross is currently seeking homes for Purdy and Smokey, two 19-week-old kittens found as strays and transferred to the centre by a vet.

The charity is appealing for support from the public to help continue its work, whether through donations, fostering, volunteering, or campaigning.

More information about volunteering opportunities, supporting Blue Cross campaigns, or rehoming a pet from the charity can be found at www.bluecross.org.uk.
