**Iga Swiatek secured her first WTA title of the season with a straight-set victory over Elena Rybakina in the final of the Canadian Open on Thursday.**

The six-time Grand Slam winner was cheered on by the crowd in Toronto as she beat world number two Rybakina 6-2 6-3.

Australian Open champion Rybakina struggled in the opening set and made 12 unforced errors, while Swiatek, 25, broke the Kazakhstan player's serve twice - and saved two break points of her own.

In the second set, Rybakina, 27, was unable to break world number eight Swiatek, and the Polish player clinched her 26th career title in one hour and 15 minutes.

"It means so, so much to me because the past months were not easy," said Swiatek, who will return to the top five in the rankings following her first Canadian final victory.

It has been a tough campaign for Swiatek, who will be looking to claim her first Grand Slam title since her dominant win in the Wimbledon final last year when the US Open starts later this month.

She advanced to the quarter-finals of the Australian Open in Melbourne in January but was beaten by eventual champion Rybakina. In the French Open and this year's Wimbledon, she only reached the fourth and third rounds respectively.

She appointed Spaniard Francisco Roig, who previously worked with Rafael Nadal and Emma Raducanu, to her coaching team in April.

Swiatek says it can be challenging to contend with external criticism while searching for her best form.

"I'm really happy that I kept focusing on growing, changing my game a little bit, improving despite all the opinions that many people have about me every day on the internet," said Swiatek.

"It's not easy to deal with all of that."

Rybakina led 2-1 in the second set before Swiatek won five of the final six games to end the match as champion.

Runner-up Rybakina had one ace and four double faults, hitting just 13 winners and making 23 unforced errors in total.

"I tried my best but it wasn't enough," said Rybakina, whose previous three matches went to three sets.

Meanwhile, Great Britain's Katie Boulter earned her first career win at the Cincinnati Open by defeating American Katie Volynets 7-5 0-6 6-4 in the first round.

After sealing her first victory since June, 30-year-old Boulter will face Wimbledon champion and sixth seed Linda Noskova in the second round.

## Shelton claims back-to-back titles in Montreal

![Ben Shelton smiles as he holds the Canadian Open trophy](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/0d2c/live/4815bfb0-97ac-11f1-b9b1-c18c7ed25243.jpg)

Ben Shelton has won seven singles titles on the ATP Tour

In the men's final, fifth seed Ben Shelton secured back-to-back Canadian Open titles with a 6-3 7-6 (7-4) victory over fellow American Brandon Nakashima.

The all-American final was the first at ATP Masters 1,000 level since Andy Roddick beat Mardy Fish in Cincinnati in 2003.

Shelton is the first Canadian Open winner since Novak Djokovic in 2016 to win the tournament without dropping a set, and also the first player since Rafael Nadal in 2019 to successfully defend the title.

The 23-year-old arrived in Montreal following a disappointing run of results, suffering a first-round exit at Wimbledon, a second-round defeat at Roland Garros and managing just one win across his first four Masters 1,000 events of the season.

"To play the way that I've played this week, to go through this draw without dropping a set, I think it speaks a lot to the work that I've been putting in, but it speaks a lot to my mental toughness," Shelton said.
