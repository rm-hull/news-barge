Following significant expenditures surrounding World Cup events and Amazon Prime Day promotions, U.S. consumers abruptly pulled back on spending during July, recording the sharpest reduction in the last year.

Figures published by the Commerce Department on Friday show retail sales dropped 0.6 percent last month, representing the steepest monthly decline since May 2025.

This followed an adjusted 0.2 percent rise in June, defying forecasters who had anticipated modest growth.

This unexpected downturn arrived after a remarkably pronounced surge in consumer purchases throughout April and May, supported by households spending their federal tax refunds.

The July contraction has sparked fresh anxieties among analysts regarding the endurance of household demand, which has driven economic expansion despite persistent inflation and escalating fuel costs. Still, writing off what has been a primary engine of recent American growth remains premature.

Nevertheless, the soft retail figures follow unexpectedly disappointing employment data from the previous week, collectively hinting that broader economic momentum might be cooling after robust household and commercial outlay across the year's opening six months.

In a related development, household sentiment deteriorated further this month, largely influenced by persistent living costs, as revealed in the University of Michigan consumer confidence survey published Friday.

Although most market analysts continue to anticipate respectable economic expansion throughout the third quarter, a substantial number have trimmed their projections following the retail performance report.

Unadjusted July revenues were heavily affected by reduced activity at service stations. Gasoline prices stayed suppressed until late in the month, when evidence of a diplomatic stalemate between Washington and Tehran in the Strait of Hormuz intensified. Consequently, gas station receipts slipped by 0.9 percent over the month.

Pump prices have tracked upward since late July, rising overnight to $4.08 per gallon from $3.85 four weeks prior, according to AAA data. Drivers are now paying 92 cents more per gallon than during the equivalent period last year.

Fuel costs generally moderate in August as summer driving season diminishes, yet AAA noted this week that encountering such elevated price levels this late in the year is without historical precedent.

Stripping out transactions at filling stations and automotive dealerships, overall retail trade dipped 0.2 percent during July.

Dealerships selling motor vehicles and component parts experienced a 1.8 percent decrease, reversing June's 1.9 percent advance that had been energized by manufacturer discount programs.

Expenditures on consumer electronics and home appliances dropped 0.5 percent. E-commerce sales retreated 2.2 percent compared with June, when activity was boosted by Amazon’s four-day Prime Day promotion scheduled earlier in the calendar than usual.

This reduction in internet purchasing exerted notable downward pressure on the core retail control group—a metric omitting food outlets, motor vehicles, construction materials, and gas stations to help gauge broader gross domestic product. That core indicator decreased 0.4 percent last month.

"American consumers are showing signs of fatigue, " Heather Long, chief economist at Navy Federal Credit Union, wrote Friday. "July retail sales were disappointing on all levels."

However, the broader slowdown in purchasing appeared to be contained within specific commercial categories.

Positive areas in the release included apparel and accessory retailers, home furnishings merchants, alongside vendors selling building products and garden equipment, all of which posted spending increases over the month.

While these figures capture only goods transactions and exclude broader service sectors such as lodging and travel, the single service element tracked—dining establishments—recorded a solid 0.5 percent gain.

"Though the latest numbers warrant a downgrade to the spending forecast, it’d be premature to write off the consumer," Bernard Yaros, lead U.S. economist at Oxford Economics said.

Fresh metrics published this week indicated that overall price pressures eased marginally in July due to minor declines in food and fuel costs, though inflation remains well above pre-Iran war levels.

The Labor Department reported Wednesday that consumer prices rose 3.4 percent year-over-year in July, easing from 3.5 percent in June. Annual inflation stood at 2.4 percent prior to conflict with Iran. Month-over-month, prices edged up by 0.1 percent.

That slight reduction represented the second consecutive monthly moderation following May, when surging gasoline prices drove inflation to a three-year peak of 4.2 percent.
