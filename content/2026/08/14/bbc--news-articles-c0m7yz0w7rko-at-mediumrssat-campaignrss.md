![The headline on the front page of the Times reads: "Houses burn and train derails on hottest day."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/ac09/live/e6494000-9764-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.png)

A striking photo of homes ablaze under the headline "Houses burn and train derails on hottest day" dominates the front of the Times. Perhaps unusually, it is a headline that encompasses three separate stories from Thursday - the fires that raged across the West Midlands, the rail accident near Lewes that left two people seriously injured, and the ongoing heatwave that saw the UK record its hottest day of 2026.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mail reads: "INFERNO."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/7303/live/84c7c8e0-9766-11f1-870d-5d08c49babb2.png)

By contrast, the Daily Mail opts for a much simpler single-word headline "Inferno" across a picture of burning houses in the West Midlands. It opens its coverage of the hottest day of the year saying: "Wildfires caused apocalyptic scenes last night as they engulfed homes."

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror reads: "Britain on fire."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1600/cpsprodpb/a4d4/live/28ef8ed0-9767-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

A similar photo of the houses on fire is also splashed across the front of today's Daily Mirror beside the headline "Britain on fire". It also runs an inset on the train derailment near Lewes as part of its coverage of what it calls "Heatwave devastation."

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Star reads: "Horror on the 2.24pm."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/4b7a/live/b5fb5890-9767-11f1-870d-5d08c49babb2.png)

The Daily Star knows when it is a day for serious news - and chooses the train crash to focus upon, with the stark headline "Horror on the 2.24pm" . A large photo shows emergency crews near the overturned train lying in shrubs next to the railway. It also runs a small image of the burning homes with the headline "'Apocalyptic' wildfire".

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph reads: "China 'funding Palestine marches'."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/ad76/live/b3e12a20-9772-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

The Daily Telegraph front page also carries a large photo of the derailed train with people standing atop it, but the larger headline of its front page is on claims about "China 'funding Palestine marches'." The paper says a "US committee claims Beijing is supporting anti-Israel protests on Britain's streets" after examining whether China has been backing the activities of Code Pink, "a far-left feminist protest group established in America".

![The headline on the front page of the Metro reads: "Record 2.5m trips to A&E."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1536/cpsprodpb/8147/live/282ffc70-9774-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

Metro is alone in choosing the high temperature as its main focus for the front page. It reports that the heatwave has driven a surge in attendance at A&E departments, which last month had "their busiest time ever" due to a "record 2.5 million patients" needing care. It reports that nurses collapsed in the "stifling heat" and "3,000 patients a day" were treated in corridors.

![The headline on the front page of the Guardian reads: "Universities fear financial turmoil amid slump in overseas students."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/865c/live/abd063f0-9768-11f1-870d-5d08c49babb2.jpg)

The Guardian shows Team GB athlete Morgan Lake seeking shade from the heat under an umbrella during the European Athletics Championship in Birmingham above an article headlined on temperatures hitting 38.1C "as fifth heatwave peaks". Its main front-page offering is coverage warning of financial turmoil in Britain's higher education system, after new figures suggested a collapse in international student numbers would deprive the sector of vital income."

![The headline on the front page of the i Paper reads: "24 million people face paying higher water bills... to firms losing 1.2 billion litres a day from leaks."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/831/cpsprodpb/a407/live/a575e600-976e-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

The i Paper highlights water regulator Ofwat's decision to allow five suppliers "to increase bills by an additional £3.4bn between them in the coming years". The paper's headline contrasts the 24 million customers now facing bigger bills with the companies who continue to lose 1.2bn litres in leaks every day.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Express reads: "Don't hand out 'rewards for their failure'."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/cafc/live/3cf53ec0-9771-11f1-870d-5d08c49babb2.png)

The "unexpected hike" in water bills households face is framed around "utility firms accused of being rewarded for failure and extorting customers" on the front of the Daily Express. And just like other papers who have combine Thursday's three big stories, it pairs the headline "Did record 38.1C hear cause train to derail" and a picture of the burning homes under the sub headline "Britain in meltdown."

![The headline on the front page of the Financial Times reads: "Anthropic investors bet on record $2tn IPO valuation."](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/790/cpsprodpb/78d0/live/962a3ab0-976f-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

The Financial Times is the only front page that does not carry any prominent coverage of the fallout of the hottest day. The hopes of investors in AI firm Anthropic for a "record $2tn IPO valuation" provide the lead story and it also runs a piece on boss of jewellery maker Swarovski headlined "Women buying own lab-grown diamonds is 'very endearing'".
