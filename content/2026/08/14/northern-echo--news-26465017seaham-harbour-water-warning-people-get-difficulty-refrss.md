Police have issued a fresh warning about the dangers of swimming in open water after responding to several incidents involving people getting into difficulty at Seaham Harbour.

Durham Police said officers had dealt with several incidents at the harbour over the past few days, as warm weather continues to make entering the water an attractive prospect.

The force warned that cold water shock, strong currents and hazards hidden beneath the surface can quickly turn a swim into a serious emergency.

*(Image: GOOGLE)*

Police said the risks can be particularly severe when alcohol has been consumed.

In a statement, Durham Police said: “Over the last few days we have responded to several instances of people getting into difficulty in the water at Seaham Harbour.

“We know that the warm weather can make taking a dip in the water look tempting, but please remember that it can be dangerous.

“Cold water shock, strong currents and unseen hazards can quickly turn a relaxing swim into a critical emergency, particularly after drinking alcohol.

“Stay safe, look out for each other, and supervise children closely.

“In an emergency, call 999.”


The latest warning follows similar advice issued by Durham County Council earlier this week as high temperatures prompted concerns about people attempting to cool off in rivers, lakes and reservoirs.

The council warned that open water can remain extremely dangerous even during hot weather, with cold water shock, hidden currents and steep banks among the potential hazards.

Residents were advised to avoid entering open water and instead use designated swimming pools or supervised bathing areas where possible.

The council also urged people never to swim alone, to supervise children closely around rivers and other bodies of water, and to stay away from dangerous edges.

Anyone who unexpectedly finds themselves in difficulty in the water is advised to try to remain calm and float on their back with their arms and legs extended while shouting for help.

The council has also warned against jumping or diving into cold water, as the sudden change in temperature can cause cold water shock and affect a person’s ability to breathe or swim normally.

The renewed safety message comes as people across County Durham continue to make the most of the warm weather.
