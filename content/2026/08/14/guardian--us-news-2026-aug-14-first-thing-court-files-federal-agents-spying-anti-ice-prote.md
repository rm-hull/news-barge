Good morning. The extent to which federal law enforcement targeted political opponents and peaceful organizers has been laid bare in newly disclosed court documents.

Internal investigative files reveal that the Department of Homeland Security launched a sweeping surveillance operation against anti-ICE protesters, major labor unions and left-leaning advocacy groups – sending undercover agents to infiltrate community meetings and issuing administrative subpoenas to seize financial records. The documents show that DHS investigators obtained financial records of unions and non-profits, as part of an inquiry into “domestic terrorist financing”.

- 
  **What did undercover government agents do?** They infiltrated community meetings, joined private activist Signal chats and surreptitiously recorded audio at local events – including a “resistance skills training” at a public library and a sparring session at a church gym, where an agent accidentally dropped his recording device. Agents posed as activists and actively engaged participants by offering to help with “direct-action” protests.
- 
  **How have activists responded?** Emilia González Avalos, the executive director of Unidos MN, a Minneapolis social justice non-profit group, said: “I believe that this is a clear attempt to intimidate us, to punish and politically repress disagreement and difference. And that is not the America that we have built through hundreds of years. And it’s definitely not the America that we are organizing for.”

## **Crimea bomb kills ‘Ukrainian sub commander who defected’**

![A man walks past a billboard displaying a portrait of Russia’s president, Vladimir Putin, in Sevastopol.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/60688a54bbd26e1a312dbf4312b3d3b0403d3283/612_7_5684_4547/master/5684.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

A Russian military officer labelled as a Ukrainian defector was killed in a bomb attack on Thursday in Russian-annexed Crimea. Robert Shageyev was described on Russian Telegram channels as a former Ukrainian naval officer who switched allegiance to Russia during Moscow’s annexation of Crimea in 2014 when he was commanding Ukraine’s only submarine, the Zaporizhzhia.

- 
  **Where did the attack take place?** The attack took place in Sevastopol, the biggest city in Russian-annexed Crimea. The regional governor, Mikhail Razvozhayev, said a woman had been arrested on suspicion of assassinating Shageyev on behalf of Ukrainian special services.
- 
  **What has Poland said about Russian activity there?** A Russian plan to kill a Ukrainian American citizen in Warsaw was thwarted by Poland before it could be carried out, the Polish prime minister has said. Donald Tusk said it was the first time that “someone on the order of Russia” had sought to attack a US citizen on the territory of another Nato country.

## **Massachusetts teen accused of killing mother and brother used ChatGPT**

![Arjun Aravind listens as his lawyer Debra DeWitt speaks.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/231fee0c3166dbe799914b7780b2a99fc6f1f55d/0_0_1796_1437/master/1796.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

A Massachusetts teenager accused of killing his mother and younger brother is being held without bail as authorities investigate a double-murder case that prosecutors say is connected to his use of ChatGPT. Arjun Aravind, 17, appeared for his arraignment in Concord district court on Thursday morning, where a not-guilty plea to murder and several additional charges was entered on his behalf.

- 
  **What did the county district attorney say?** Marian Ryan said in a press conference: “The investigation has so far shown that Arjun had recently been demonstrating some concerning behavior, including using the internet and ChatGPT to make searches for theoretical ideas or fantasy stories regarding the killing of his family.”
