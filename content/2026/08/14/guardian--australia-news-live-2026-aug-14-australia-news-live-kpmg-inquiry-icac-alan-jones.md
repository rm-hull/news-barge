Victoria’s recently deposed Labor premier, Jacinta Allan, has announced she will leave politics at the next election.

In a statement today – posted to her social media pages along with a picture of her at 25 years old – Allan announced she would not recontest the seat of Bendigo in November.

Allan said:

In my first speech to Parliament, I said I wanted to be a representative who cared about people and listened to them … I finished that speech with a promise:

‘I intend to make a difference.’

Today, almost 27 years later, I have announced that I will not recontest the seat of Bendigo East at November’s election.

But I look around the place I have called home my entire life and know that, together, we did make a difference.

Allan listed what she saw as her achievements during her time in politics, and said:

Politics has changed a lot since this photo was taken.

So have I …

And when I leave Parliament, I’ll leave the same way I arrived.

A daughter of Bendigo. A daughter of workers. A proud Labor woman.

Still believing in equality. Still believing in public education and public health. Still believing in the dignity of work.

Still believing government can - and must - make people’s lives better.

And still believing politics, at its best, is very simple:

Care about people. Listen to people. Speak up for people. Make a difference.

Airservices CEO on Sydney airport near-miss: ‘we don’t think the threshold for reporting was actually met’

Airservices Australia’s chief executive Rob Sharp has been speaking to media just now about the near-collision between two planes at Sydney airport last Sunday.

He said:

VA555 was approaching to take-off; a qualified, experienced controller advised clearance, but immediately cancelled that clearance.

The controllers were under what’s called an endorsement process, so they weren’t trainees, they were experienced and regular endorsement process.

So there was a qualified controller who was also supervising.

The aircraft remained at the beginning of the threshold for the runway and the commentary that’s been around on reporting in this instance – I just want to flag we do take our reporting requirements very seriously. We work closely with our regulators and very openly. In this particular case, however, we don’t think the threshold for reporting was actually met …

But we will work closely with the ATSB because there are some data points that still need to be resolved and clarified and we look forward to any learnings that emerge from that particular incident.

Optus to keep KPMG auditor despite ‘flagrant breach of duty’

Optus will keep KPMG as its auditor even though the firm broke its promises and leaked its documents to its Telstra team, a parliamentary inquiry has heard.

KPMG staff working on Optus’ audit shared confidential documents with colleagues who were pursuing, but eventually did not win, the contract for competitor Telstra.

Stephen Rue, chief executive of Optus, has told the inquiry KPMG promised its data and files would be accessible only by KPMG’s Optus team and that its Telstra team would not include any staff from its Sydney-based Optus team, working separately from Melbourne. He said:

My understanding is those assurances were not followed.

On top of the leaks, KPMG planned to have some of its Sydney-based partners work with Telstra if they had won the bid, Rue said. He said KPMG only told him about the confirmed leaks in “vague” terms in a phone call on 29 May.

Optus’ chair, John Arthur, said the leak was a “flagrant breach of duty” and an “egregious breach of professional responsibilities”. But he said the company decided to stick with KPMG as auditor as its financial year had already begun, on 1 April.

Arthur said Optus had changed the processes and personnel working on its accounts and boosted its focus on culture. He said:

We have what I would now describe as a much more rigorous control environment around our audit, which we will be watching like a hawk.

KPMG’s own documents suggest yet another breach of client trust, inquiry hears

A parliamentary inquiry has heard another KPMG client, Dexus, is worried the firm used its confidential information to win another contract with Dexus.

The real estate and infrastructure company had KPMG running its internal audit in 2023 and 2024, but the firm applied for and won Dexus’ external audit in 2024. A whistleblower alleged KPMG used confidential internal work to achieve that.

KPMG’s lawyers’ investigation in December 2025 found that KPMG staff believed they had told Dexus they were using internal knowledge to pursue the external contract.

Warwick Negus, chair of Dexus, spoke to the inquiry today. He said while KPMG’s own investigation suggested otherwise, KPMG’s leadership had told him that confidential information was not used in the tender process. He said:

It would have been completely inappropriate for the information to go in that direction. …I think our actions made it relatively obvious that this was not on.

He said Eileen Hoggett, the KPMG bidding partner, wanted to join Dexus’ internal audit committee meetings but Dexus rejected that as an “obvious conflict”.

Negus said KPMG’s lawyers did not interview Dexus about the issue. He told the committee:

The initial reaction is why didn’t you come and talk to us? .. Why wouldn’t you ask for our perspective on these things?

Johnny Young, the singer-songwriter and producer who created and hosted the popular variety show Young Talent Time, has died aged 79.

Young’s family announced his death in a statement on Friday, saying that Young died “following a brief battle with pancreatic cancer”.

The statement read:

Johnny was the creator, host and producer of the much loved television show Young Talent Time. He wrote and produced many iconic hit songs including The Real Thing and Smily.

Johnny leaves behind his wife Marisha, sister Cornelia, daughters Anna and Fleur, son Craig (dec.), seven grandchildren, and two great-grandchildren.

The family respectfully asks for privacy during this difficult time. Details of a memorial service will be announced soon.

Young Talent Time, which originally aired from 1971 until 1988, launched the careers of several Australian singers including Tina Arena, Debra Byrne and Dannii Minogue.

Melinda Cilento, the chief executive at the Committee for Economic Development of Australia, has been appointed as a member of the Reserve Bank’s interest rate-setting board.

Jim Chalmers made the announcement this morning, with Cilento taking the spot left vacant by Ian Harper – a longstanding board member and one of the country’s most distinguished economists.

The treasurer in a statement this morning said Cilento “is a highly respected economist with broad and deep expertise and experience”:

This appointment will help ensure the board has the right mix of skills and perspectives.

Cilento has led CEDA, a public policy think tank, since 2017.

She was a commissioner at the Productivity Commission, deputy chair at the Business Council of Australia and is a former Treasury and International Monetary Fund official. She has also held positions in the private sector, including as a non-executive board member at Woodside Petroleum.

Cilento joins a nine-member board, which includes the RBA’s chair and deputy chair, the Treasury secretary and six independents.
