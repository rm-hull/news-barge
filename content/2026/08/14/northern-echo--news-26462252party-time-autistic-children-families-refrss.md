A fun-packed summer party for autistic young people and their families has been organised by a North East charity described by parents as a “lifeline”.

The colourful event – featuring a disco, children’s characters, face-painting, popcorn, a candy floss machine, and a bouncy castle – was held by the North East Autism Society at New Warlands Farm, near Burnhope, in County Durham.

Young people from NEAS children's homes and short breaks service attended the party, which the charity hopes will become an annual event.

  A young guest at the party meets Woody 

*(Image: North East Autism Society)*

Donna Williams, Registered Manager for Children's Short Breaks, said: “The six-week school holiday is a long time for all children, and it can be especially challenging for our young people and their families, so this is a way of bringing everyone together and having a good time.

“It’s been a huge success, with everyone enjoying themselves in a safe, relaxed environment, so I think it’s definitely something we would want to do again. The staff have done an amazing job in pulling it all together, so we can’t thank them enough for making it happen.”

Short Breaks Assistant Manager, Theresa Arrowsmith, described the event as “a huge team effort”.

Children’s characters, supplied by Candy Kids Entertainment, included Toy Story’s Buzz Lightyear and Woody, plus Rumi, from KPop Demon Hunters, while Bobby Embo provided the disco.

  The party in full swing at New Warlands Farm 

*(Image: North East Autism Society)*

Donna’s son, Reece, played his part by using Makaton – a unique language programme combining signs, symbols, and speech – to help those with communication difficulties. Makaton International Awareness Day takes place on August 28, so it was a timely demonstration of the programme.

Andy Hadland, whose 17-year-old autistic son, Ethan, was among the party guests, praised NEAS for staging the event.

Thanks to the charity, Ethan, a student at The Oaks School, at Spennymoor, has a short break, including an overnight stay, every fortnight, providing vital respite for his family.

“NEAS is more than a charity – they become an extension of your family and friends,” said Andy, who has another son, Niall, aged 13.

“Today’s party is another example of the fantastic care and support they offer. I honestly don’t know how we’d manage without them.

“It means we can do things as a family, knowing that Ethan is happy and in safe hands. NEAS is a lifeline and I can’t thank them enough.”
