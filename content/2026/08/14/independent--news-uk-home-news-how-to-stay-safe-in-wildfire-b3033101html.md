Huge blazes have ripped across the West Midlands causing “apocalyptic” scenes as fire tore through homes and grassland.

A child and three firefighters were some of 19 taken to hospital and residents were told to evacuate their homes in Stourbridge.

It comes as dry weather and back-to-back heatwaves have created the perfect conditions for wildfires, with several starting this summer in the New Forest, Cairngorms National Park in Scotland and another just three miles from the Sizewell B nuclear power station in Suffolk.

![The burnt out remains of houses following a huge blaze in Stourbridge, in the West Midlands](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/14/12/01KZZY3YGQFK9JDRDM53028Z9R.jpg)

The National Fire Chiefs Council said England and Wales had been struck by 1,017 wildfires this year – the same as last year’s record-breaking total – and at least 458 in July alone.

But what should you do if you discover a wildfire, or worse find yourself in one?

## What should you do if you discover a wildfire?

If you see smoke or flames, and believe a wildfire is active, the London Fire Brigade urges you to stay calm and make sure you are away from any vegetation and smoke before calling 999.

If you are in a car, make sure all the windows and vents are closed and if you are inside your house make sure all doors and windows are closed but unlocked.

When calling 999 make sure to give a precise location and information about the size of the fire. The what3words app is recommended to help fire crews find the exact location.

The London Fire Brigade adds to not try and tackle the fire yourself and instead urges people to stay away from the blaze as it can smoulder or spread underground.

The Red Cross recommends checking updates from your local Fire and Rescue Service, local authority and the Met Office and prepare to leave the area if it is advised.

## How to prepare for wildfire in the UK

During the summer months the risk of wildfire increases in the UK making it important to familiarise yourself with advice to keep safe from the dangers.

The Red Cross recommends staying alert during heatwaves and making sure not to drop cigarettes on the ground, or use disposable barbecues and to make sure you dispose of any litter or glass bottles. That’s because sunlight can become focused as it shines through the glass, starting fires.

It’s also recommended to monitor wildfire reports and make sure you have an escape plan ready to use if you are required to evacuate.
