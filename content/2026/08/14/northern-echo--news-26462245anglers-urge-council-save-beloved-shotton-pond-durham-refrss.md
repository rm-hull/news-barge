Anglers have urged a council to protect and restore a beloved community fishing site.

Shotton and District Angling Club Lake, also known as Shotton Pond, is described by campaigners as an important asset for the community.

Nathan Langley, who is behind the petition, has called on Durham County Council to invest in the pond’s upkeep or consider leasing it to a fishing club or community organisation.

He said the pond provided “opportunities for recreation, outdoor activity, and social engagement” and offered a “safe and positive environment” for people of all ages.

Mr Langley said it was particularly valuable for young people, helping to encourage healthy hobbies and constructive activities.

But in a warning as part of the petition, he said that without investment and appropriate management, the pond risked continuing to decline.

“Without appropriate investment and management, there is a risk that this valuable resource will continue to decline,” he said.

The petition asks the council either to allocate funding to restore and maintain the site as a community fishing venue, or grant a lease to an organisation willing to take responsibility for its management.

Mr Langley said either option would help “secure the future of Shotton Pond” and preserve its social, recreational and environmental benefits.

He added: We respectfully ask that the council considers allocating funding and resources to restore and maintain Shotton Pond as a community fishing venue. If direct investment is not possible, we would ask the council to consider granting a lease to a local fishing club or community organisation willing to take responsibility for managing, maintaining, and developing the pond for the benefit of the public.”

The petition closed earlier this month.

Responding to the campaign, Durham County Council said the land was currently vacant and was being considered for lease.

Jack Marshall, the council’s asset strategy and property manager, said: “The land at Shotton Pond is currently vacant and we are looking to lease it to an external party.

“We have received several expressions of interest and are assessing their viability to ensure the area is cared for effectively.”
