Defence secretary Pete Hegseth has warned that the US military has enough assets to maintain a naval blockade of Iran "indefinitely", as talks with Tehran hit an impasse over the Strait of Hormuz.

"The United States Navy can maintain a blockade like that because we'll rotate ships in and out, as we have, and we'll continue to," Hegseth said, adding that he is advising president Donald Trump to keep applying economic pressure on Iran.

His statement comes as Iran said it could deliberately prolong the war with the US until Trump leaves office, in order to send a message that attacking the country has lasting consequences.

Mohammad Reza Naghdi, senior adviser to the commander of Iran’s Revolutionary Guards, said dragging out the war would show any future US administration that “there is a cost” to hostile endeavours against Iran."We have to attain deterrence so that the enemy never dares to attack us, so we can live with security," he told PBS NewsHour.

Trump earlier said that “praise be to Allah”, Washington has “total control over the Strait of Hormuz", adding: "I THINK WE WILL KEEP IT!”

## Yemeni soldier killed in clashes with Houthis

At least one government soldier was killed and another critically wounded in clashes with the Houthis in the mountainous province of Taiz, Yemeni military officials said.

The fighting came as part of a spate of recent Houthi attacks on government-held areas across the country. Dozens of government troops have been killed since last week, the Yemeni military said.

The Houthis also said they launched another drone attack on an Aramco oil refinery in Saudi Arabia. Saudi officials did not immediately comment on the reported attack in the city of Jazan.

Last weekend, the Houthis fired drones at the refinery, where the Saudi energy ministry reported a fire but no casualties.

## Hormuz shipping traffic below monthly average

Shipping traffic at the Strait of Hormuz fell below the monthly average towards the end of the week due to volatile sentiment between the United States and Iran, with both countries making competing claims over its control.

The Strait of Hormuz is "under Iran's control and management", the recently appointed head of Iran's Basij paramilitary unit said. Meanwhile, the US said it could maintain a naval blockade of Iran indefinitely and would ratchet up economic pressure on Tehran as ceasefire talks have floundered.

Commodity vessel transits at Hormuz picked up slightly yesterday with nine ships crossing, but remained lower than the month's daily average of 12, based on Kpler data in August so far.

## Jared Kushner to head to Middle East for Gaza talks

President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner will travel to the Middle East next week to try to advance plans for an end to the conflict in Gaza after a deal recently announced by the Trump administration ran into Israeli opposition.

An official with the Board of Peace, the body Trump established to oversee the fragile ceasefire in the Israel-Hamas war, said Kushner, board high representative Nickolay Mladenov, and executive board member Tony Blair would arrive in Israel on Sunday.

The group will then travel to Cairo, where meetings of a Palestinian technocratic group set to assume control of Gaza under a US-brokered ceasefire deal have been taking place for the past several months.

## Iran and Oman work to clean oil spills from attacked ships

Iran said it was working to clean up an oil spill off Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. Images distributed by Iran's semiofficial news agency ISNA showed oil fouling the island's beaches.

While Iran hasn't said what caused the spill, the firm TankerTrackers.com says it appears to have started with an Iranian attack on a bulk carrier off Oman.

Oman is responding to another spill in the Arabian Sea, coming from a tanker grounded off its southern coast. The tanker Caroline Bezengi, a sanctioned vessel believed to be part of Russia's "shadow fleet", has been grounded since reporting an explosion in June.

A British maritime risk and response company, which has been hired to salvage the tanker, said oil from the ship has reached shores hundreds of km north of the vessel.

## Vance says Iran war will end with US in stronger position

Vice president JD Vance appeared to be lowering expectations for the Strait of Hormuz to return to the way it was before the war.

He predicted in an interview on Fox News that the war will end with the US in a stronger position, with Iran not having a nuclear weapon, and with the Strait of Hormuz "returned to a place where oil and gas prices are stable for the American people".

"That's goal number one: keep oil and gas cheap for Americans all over our country," he said.

The second goal, according to Vance, is to ensure that Iran will never get a nuclear weapon.

## Oil steadies after US threatens indefinite blockade

Oil prices inched up this morning after the US threatened an indefinite naval blockade of Iran, reviving concerns about supply of crude after the previous session's fall on a weaker outlook for demand and a large build in US stocks.

Brent futures rose 1 cent, or 0.1 per cent, to $87.08 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate crude futures rose 6 cents to $81.31 a barrel.

Yesterday, the US warned that it could maintain a naval blockade of Iran indefinitely and ramp up economic pressure on Tehran as ceasefire talks have stalled.
