LEEDS Bradford Airport is taking the next step in a major transformation.

The airport, a key economic hub for Yorkshire and a major regional employer, has announced new proposals as part of its long-term upgrade programme—aimed at improving sustainability, passenger experience, and environmental impact.

Vincent Hodder, chief executive of Leeds Bradford Airport, said: "Leeds Bradford Airport is transforming into a modern gateway for Yorkshire and a catalyst for regional growth.

"Through significant private investment, industry-leading sustainability initiatives and a commitment to supporting local communities, we are building an airport that is fit for the future.

"Modernising operating conditions that were introduced more than three decades ago is an important part of that journey.

"It enables us to encourage quieter, cleaner aircraft while maintaining robust environmental controls and providing meaningful support for our neighbours.

"This is about creating a stronger, more sustainable and better-connected Yorkshire for the decades ahead.

"This is not the 'thousands more night flights' some would claim.

"The QCS LBA is applying for is set to match the existing noise footprint.

"The same noise limits, allocated differently."

Central to the proposals is a change in how night-time flights are regulated.

The airport has submitted plans to Leeds City Council to replace its current movement-based night flight restrictions with a modern noise-based system.

This approach is already in use at most UK regional airports.

The new system would encourage airlines to use quieter, newer, and more fuel-efficient aircraft by capping noise rather than the number of flights.

This would allow for some flexibility in operations while keeping noise levels within set limits.

The airport also plans to update its defined night-time hours to 11.30pm to 5.59am, bringing it in line with other UK airports.

As part of the changes, Leeds Bradford Airport has proposed a Night Noise Insulation Scheme.

The scheme would offer grants to nearby residents for measures such as improved glazing, ventilation, and—for the first time at a UK airport—funding for bedroom air conditioning.

This is designed to provide practical support to those most affected by aircraft noise during night-time hours.

The proposals form part of the airport’s wider LBA:REGEN investment programme, which includes a £200 million terminal extension that opened in 2025.

Refurbishment work is expected to continue through to 2027.

Environmental responsibility is a core focus of the airport’s plans.

Leeds Bradford Airport has committed to achieving net zero carbon emissions from its own operations by 2030—10 years ahead of the national Jet Zero target.

Progress to date includes removing gas heating from terminal operations and electrifying the airport’s airside light vehicle fleet.

The airport has also achieved Level 4 Airport Carbon Accreditation, recognising its efforts to align with the Paris Agreement and work collaboratively across the aviation sector to reduce emissions.

Business leaders from across the region have welcomed the airport’s plans.

Steve Heapy, CEO of Jet2, said: "This modernisation is welcome news, both for the regional economy and for local holidaymakers.

"We have been proudly operating from Leeds Bradford Airport since 2003 and our longstanding commitment has never been stronger.

"We look forward to continuing to work in partnership with the airport on the next exciting phase of development."

Olga Watterich, deputy regional director of CBI Yorkshire and the Humber, said: "Businesses across Yorkshire and the Humber depend on reliable international connections to reach customers, suppliers and investors around the world.

"The continued transformation of Leeds Bradford Airport strengthens the region's competitiveness and supports economic growth, trade and inward investment across Yorkshire.

"A thriving regional airport is essential infrastructure for a modern economy."

James Mason, chief executive of West & North Yorkshire Chamber of Commerce, said: "Leeds Bradford Airport is not only a major employer in its own right, but also supports a broader ecosystem of jobs, apprenticeships and skills development across the region.

"Its continued growth and investment create valuable opportunities for local people to build careers in aviation, engineering, hospitality and customer service.

"This exciting next phase of modernisation sends a clear signal of confidence in the region’s potential and will further enhance its appeal as a place to do business, invest and visit."
