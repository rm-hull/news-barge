HORSE-LOVERS turned out in force yesterday to be given a fascinating insight into life behind the scenes at a leading North East racing yard.

Successful trainer Michael Dods stages the annual open day as part of National Racehorse Week and the event gets more and more popular.

Visitors of all ages flocked to the Denton Hall stables, near Darlington, to enjoy getting up close to the horses.

  Future jockey? Ralph, 4, tries out the equicizer horse simulator 

*(Image: Peter Barron)*

Star attraction was unbeaten two-year-old Arapaho Gold, owned by Darlington businessman Doug Graham. Winner of the prestigious Al Basti Equiworld Dubai Gimcrack Stakes at the famous York Ebor Festival last week, Arapaho Gold is now one of the highest rated young horses in Europe.

Spectators were able to see horses going up the gallops and see a demonstration of the starting stalls. Other attractions included seeing farrier Danny Souter at work, a physio demonstration by Lee Clark, and horses in the equine spa and treadmill.

  Farrier Danny Souter gives a demonstration 

*(Image: Peter Barron)*

There was also the chance for visitors to test their jockey skills by having a go on the ‘equicizer’ horse simulator.

And Michael Dods, Assistant Trainer, Chloe Dods, and Arapaho Gold’s owner, Doug Graham, took part in a question and answer session.

Michael Dods said: “We always do our best to support National Racehorse Week because it’s a chance to show the public how much is involved in running a racing yard, and how much care the horses receive.

“We’ve held the open day for a few days now and it seems to be getting busier every year. We pride ourselves on being a friendly, approachable yard and it’s always nice to see so many people showing an interest. Hopefully, they found their visit interesting.”

  Watching the horses walk back from the gallops 

*(Image: Peter Barron)*

Anna Clayton, of Great British Horseracing, described the Denton Hall open day as a “great example” of trainers supporting National Racehorse Week”.

“It’s all about attracting families who might not necessarily be interested in racing, and giving them a fascinating insight into the sport,” said Anna.

“Lots of yards are opening to the public across the country and it’s lovely to see so many happy faces as young and old meet the stars of the sport.”

  Zac, 10, and Mae, 12, get up close with Northern Express 

*(Image: Peter Barron)*

Des Scott, of Darlington, has been a fan of the sport since childhood but this was his first visit to a racing stable.

“I’ve thoroughly enjoyed it,” he said. “Michael and his team have been wonderful in giving up their time and it’s been so interesting to see how much goes on behind the scenes.”
