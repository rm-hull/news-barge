A huge clean-up has transformed a back lane in Chester-le-Street after faeces, and discarded needles and overgrown plants filled the area.

Durham Police officers, neighbourhood wardens and residents joined forces to clear the lane behind Cooperative Street after anti-social behaviour concerns.

There were reports of drug use, discarded needles and people using the area for urination and defecation.

PCSO Adrian Richards coordinated a number of ‘Days of Action’ alongside Durham County Council’s Neighbourhood Wardens and members of the community.

The work included cutting back overgrown vegetation, safely removing discarded sharps and drug-related paraphernalia, and clearing fly-tipped waste.

  Residents during a clean-up operation in a back lane off Cooperative Street, Chester-le-Street. 

*(Image: Chester-Le-Police on Facebook)*

In a statement, officers said the area is not registered with the Land Registry and does not fall under the ownership or maintenance responsibility of Durham County Council.

As a result, ongoing upkeep of the lane is left to nearby residents.

Authorities encourage the community to help keep it clean and free from waste.

  A picture of the clean-up operation. 

*(Image: Chester-Le-Police on Facebook)*

Police said they will continue investigating reports of fly-tipping and anti-social behaviour and take enforcement action when those responsible can be identified.

Chester-le-Street North Councillor Tracie Smith took to social media to praise those involved in the effort.

She said: “Well done to the residents for having pride in their local community.

"Also many thanks to Durham County Council Neighbourhood Wardens and Chester-le-Street Police for organising this clean-up.”

The transformation forms part of wider community work under Operation Drive, which has also seen officers, PCSOs and the force’s problem-solving team carrying out patrols and engagement activities on Chester-le-Street.

Police said these patrols have included speaking to residents and businesses, providing crime prevention advice and giving people the opportunity to raise concerns directly with officers.
