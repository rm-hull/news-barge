Five young men who died after their vehicle hit a police car on a dual carriageway may have had links to “serious and organised criminal activity”, police said.

Cleveland Police said 12 other people have been arrested over alleged criminality leading up to the collision on Teesside in which seven people, including two police officers, were killed.

These included four people arrested on suspicion of participating in an organised crime group and four on suspicion of possessing a firearm.

Four of the suspects are also accused of possessing class A and B drugs with intent to supply.

Four of the five young men in the car had previous criminal convictions, and were believed to be associated with another car which was seen ramming properties earlier that morning.

PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38, were killed when a Volkswagen Passat – which had been pursued by police – was driven the wrong way along a dual carriageway near Middlesbrough and hit their marked armed response vehicle.

The five people travelling in the Volkswagen – Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Michael Robert Cahill and Jacob Matusiak – were pronounced dead at the scene at South Bank, early on Saturday.

  The five people who died in the A66 crash 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

Police said nine men and three women, aged between 19 and 61, have been arrested after an investigation over the weekend into alleged criminal activity before the crash.

The force said on the morning of the collision, it received several reports of two vehicles being driven dangerously.

The first vehicle, believed to be a Volvo XC60, was witnessed ramming properties in the Middlesbrough area.

The second vehicle, a Volkswagen Passat, is believed to have been associated with the activities of the Volvo.

Police said both vehicles were operating with cloned number plates but were picked up by automated number plate recognition technology.

  Scene of the crash 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The Volvo XC60 involved in the initial reports has been traced and recovered in Middlesbrough.

Police said there was no evidence that filming of the activities on Friday night and Saturday morning was “for the purposes of TikTok”.

Assistant Chief Constable Wayne Fox, responsible for the strategic leadership of the investigation, said: “This has been a fast-moving and complex investigation, with significant links to serious and organised criminal activity.”

He went on to say: “We have a duty to respond to, and thoroughly investigate, the serious criminal activity that resulted in this fatal incident, and to bring all those responsible to justice.”

Flowers left beside the A66 close to the crash site paid tribute to the officers who died.

A paramedic wrote a card with a bouquet which said: “Thank you for your service. RIP.”

Another said: “Behind every uniform is a person with a life, family, friends and people whose lives they have touched along the way.”

One card simply said: “For our 2 brave police officers, may you both rest in peace.”

  Floral tributes at the scene 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

More than £500,000 has been raised for the families of the two police officers who were killed when a Volkswagen Passat, that had been pursued by police, hit their marked armed response vehicle seconds after the chase was terminated.

Organisers said funds raised would be split equally between the two officers’ families and the federation would continue to provide support.

A second crowdfunder for the families had raised more than £36,900.

Cleveland Police launched the chase after receiving intelligence relating to “numerous incidents” linked to a Passat, which activated an ANPR camera and is believed to have been using cloned plates, investigating watchdog the Independent Office for Police Conduct said.

PC Blades and PC Clough were not involved in the original pursuit, but had been called to the area to assist with the search for the Passat.

Family members described former Civil Nuclear Constabulary officer PC Clough as “our hero” who was “always on public duty”.

  Scene of the crash 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The father-of-two’s family called him “our superhero” and said: “He gave his life in the line of duty, protecting and serving others with courage, dedication and selflessness.”

In a statement issued by Cleveland Police, PC Blades’ wife called him “my beloved husband, my best friend, and the most devoted father to our two young boys”.

She said he was “an adored son and brother, a perfect son-in-law, and the most fun uncle”.

“He was loved beyond words by all his family and friends, and his loss has left a space that can never be filled,” she said.

“Our children will grow up knowing what an incredible father he was and will always be, and I will carry his love, his memory and everything we shared with me always.”

She added: “Forever loved. Forever missed. Forever our superhero. Our Matt and Dad.”

He had recently joined Seaton Carew FC as a coach and was looking forward to teaching his son in its under-sevens Seahawks squad, the club said.

  Scene of the crash 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The officer also coached his eldest son and other youngsters in the under-10s Reds team at FC Hartlepool, which called him “a role model, a mentor, a friend, and someone the children looked up to and admired”.

It added: “He was able to share his love of football with his son while inspiring and supporting every child on the team.”

Earlier on Sunday, PC Clough’s family said: “He was our hero with a fantastic sense of humour. Jumped into action whenever needed, and willing to help anyone for any reason.

“Proudly fought for his country to make the world a better place. Always on public duty throughout his short life.”

It continued: “We as a family and Tom’s partner would like to express our sincere gratitude for the overwhelming support and condolences from friends, colleagues and the wider community.”

Tributes for the five young men were also shared on social media, with many of their friends saying they “can’t believe it” and were “heartbroken” and “lost for words”.

Balloon releases have been announced in their memory on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday at different locations, including in South Bank near where the collision occurred.

One message, dedicated to all five, said: “We all knew use and knew use had hearts of gold and most loyalist lads.”

The Passat they were travelling in was spotted and an armed response vehicle pursued for just under seven minutes before the car drove on to the A66 at South Bank against the flow of traffic.

Police aborted the chase and continued to travel on the correct side of the carriageway.

A silent prayer vigil will be held at St John the Evangelist Church in South Bank on Monday.
