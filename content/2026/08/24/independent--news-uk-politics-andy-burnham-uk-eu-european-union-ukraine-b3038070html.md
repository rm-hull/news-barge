Andy Burnham has suggested that Britain plans to take a "bolder" approach towards forging closer relations with the European Union.

Speaking to the President of the European Council Antonio Costa during an overnight train journey to Kyiv, Ukraine, on Sunday, the Prime Minister emphasised that "greater co-operation" was required across all sectors.

The comments come ahead of an upcoming UK-EU summit.

Initially scheduled for July, the meeting has been postponed to a yet-to-be-confirmed date later this year.

During his visit to Ukraine on Monday, Mr Burnham agreed to supply the nation with blueprints to produce long-range missiles.

A Downing Street spokesperson said: “The Prime Minister met the President of the European Council Antonio Costa on the train to Kyiv last night, as the two leaders travelled into the city to mark Independence Day.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham (left) meets with president of the European Council Antonio Costa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/24/09/01M0S9YWPQGRJB5WK89N2ZV0DY.jpg)

“It was a vital moment to show unity and solidarity with Ukraine, the leaders agreed.

“Turning to the UK’s relationship with the European Union, the two leaders underlined the importance of being ambitious to drive a step change in relations.

“The UK had to be bolder in terms of getting closer to the bloc, the Prime Minister said.”

“That included building on the close defence and security ties already forged between the UK and European allies to drive greater cooperation across all areas of the relationship,” he added.

“The UK-EU summit set to be held later this year, would offer a platform to continue that work, the leaders agreed.

“Both looked forward to seeing one and another at the Coalition of the Willing later today.”

## How rejoining EU single market would impact the UK economy

Economically, forging closer ties with Europe could provide a £92bn GDP boost to the UK, according to new economic research.

Commissioned by Best for Britain, which campaigns for full UK-EU reintegration, the research undertaken by Frontier Economics suggests at least 90 per cent of Brexit's economic hit could be recouped by the UK rejoining the EU.

## Burnham’s ‘ambitions’ for UK to get closer to EU

During the same journey to Kyiv, he spoke to the Prime Minister of Luxembourg, Luc Frieden.

According to a Downing Street spokesperson: “The Prime Minister set out his ambitions for the UK to get closer to the European Union, and both agreed on the important cultural and educational ties between the UK and Europe to deliver opportunity for the next generation of Brits and Europeans.”

Mr Burnham looks set to continue a policy started off by his predecessor Sir Keir Starmer of closer relations with the EU.

It echoes comments he wrote in the *Times* newspaper before he became Prime Minister, where he said: “I want to consolidate the progress made on the existing UK-EU negotiations and make further progress quickly, including by strengthening our co-operation on illegal migration, economic security and the broader resilience of our societies to external threats — from terrorism to AI-driven disinformation.”

During his campaign to win the heavily-Brexit voting Makerfield constituency in the by-election, Mr Burnham had said he was not in favour of the UK rejoining the EU, warning it could be a “permanent rut” of Brexit arguments.

He had previously said he wanted to see the UK back in the EU within his lifetime.

However during the contest, he said: “My view is that Brexit has been damaging, but I also believe the last thing we should do right now is re-run those arguments. Britain will be stuck in a permanent rut if we’re just constantly arguing and people are pulling away from each other.

“It is time, surely, isn’t it, to bring people back together, to focus on what we’ve got in common, to get the growth coming to all places, so it’s felt there.

“That is what we need in this moment and it’s really important that whatever comes out of this by-election there is a more unifying feeling about the change that we need to work towards.”

Last week, a a former Tory minister turned Labour adviser claimed taking Britain back into the EU single market would win Burnham the next general election.

Nick Boles, who served as a minister in David Cameron’s government before quitting the Conservatives in 2019 over the party’s Brexit stance, added that it would help Burnham achieve a legacy to rival Tony Blair’s.

Mr Boles has urged the new prime minister to seize the opportunity created by “pretty scary” world events since 2016 to strike a deal with European Union leaders that would protect the continent and offer the UK a much-needed economic boost.
