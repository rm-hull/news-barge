# Leeds Bradford Airport proposes changes to night-time operations and a Night Noise Insulation Scheme

![Leeds Bradford Airport (LBA) has announced the next step in its long-term transformation programme, reinforcing its position as a key economic driver for Yorkshire, a major regional employer, and one of the UK's most forward-looking regional airports.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI1ZDkxYmI3LTcyYjYtNGFhYS1iODVmLWM5MDM2NGQxMjk3YTo0OGEwNjcxZi1lMDEwLTRhNjMtOTljMy0yZDczMjU4OTNlZDE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=70&enable=upscale)



## Leeds Bradford Airport (LBA) has announced the next step in its long-term transformation programme including proposed changes to night-time operations.

The latest proposals submitted to Leeds City Council would “update planning conditions governing night-time operations, replacing a decades-old movement-based system with a modern noise-based framework”, the airport said in a statement on Monday.

LBA is proposing to update planning conditions that control night flights at the airport, varying conditions in three areas including converting Air Traffic Movement (ATM) restrictions to Quota Count System (QCS), introducing a modern cap system which incentivises quieter, more environmentally friendly planes at LBA and condensing night-time hours to 23:30 - 05:59.

The airport wants to introduce a Night Noise Insulation Scheme (NNIS).

The changes would retain existing limits on overall night-time noise while creating greater incentives for airlines to operate newer, quieter and more fuel-efficient aircraft, helping to deliver environmental improvements alongside economic growth for the region according to the airport.

As part of a £200m private investment programme, LBA:REGEN, a major terminal extension opened in 2025, with further refurbishment works continuing through to 2027.

The airport has also achieved Level 4 Airport Carbon Accreditation, recognising its work to align carbon reduction activities with the goals of the Paris Agreement and engage partners across the aviation ecosystem in reducing emissions.

The airport said the proposed move to a noise-based system would further support these ambitions by encouraging airlines to operate newer generation aircraft with improved fuel efficiency and lower noise impacts.

Alongside the proposed modernisation of night-time operating conditions, LBA has announced plans for a new Night Noise Insulation Scheme, designed to provide practical support for homes and community facilities most affected by aircraft noise.

The proposed scheme would offer grants towards insulation measures, including glazing, ventilation improvements and, for the first time at a UK airport, funding for bedroom air conditioning in eligible properties.

The airport believes the combination of maintaining a fixed overall noise limit while introducing targeted community support represents a balanced approach that benefits both local residents and the wider regional economy.

LBA's proposals form part of a broader strategy to ensure the airport remains competitive, sustainable and capable of supporting Yorkshire's future ambitions.

By updating operating conditions that date back more than 30 years, the airport aims to align with modern industry practice while maintaining robust controls on noise and environmental performance.

Vincent Hodder, CEO, Leeds Bradford Airport, said: “Leeds Bradford Airport is transforming into a modern gateway for Yorkshire and a catalyst for regional growth. Through significant private investment, industry-leading sustainability initiatives and a commitment to supporting local communities, we are building an airport that is fit for the future.

“Today we support thousands of jobs and generate hundreds of millions of pounds for the regional economy. As we deliver our Vision 2030 strategy and LBA:REGEN, that contribution will grow significantly, helping connect Yorkshire's businesses, people and communities to opportunities across the UK and around the world.

“Modernising operating conditions that were introduced more than three decades ago is an important part of that journey. It enables us to encourage quieter, cleaner aircraft while maintaining robust environmental controls and providing meaningful support for our neighbours.

“This is about creating a stronger, more sustainable and better-connected Yorkshire for the decades ahead, this is not the ‘thousands more night flights’ some would claim. The QCS LBA is applying for is set to match the existing noise footprint. The same noise limits, allocated differently.”

Steve Heapy, CEO, Jet2, said: “This modernisation is welcome news, both for the regional economy and for local holidaymakers. We have been proudly operating from Leeds Bradford Airport since 2003 and our longstanding commitment has never been stronger. We look forward to continuing to work in partnership with the airport on the next exciting phase of development.”

Olga Watterich, Deputy Regional Director, CBI Yorkshire and the Humber, said: “Businesses across Yorkshire and the Humber depend on reliable international connections to reach customers, suppliers and investors around the world. The continued transformation of Leeds Bradford Airport strengthens the region's competitiveness and supports economic growth, trade and inward investment across Yorkshire. A thriving regional airport is essential infrastructure for a modern economy. Leeds Bradford Airport's investment programme demonstrates confidence in Yorkshire's future and will help strengthen the region's appeal as a destination for business, tourism and investment."

James Mason, Chief Executive, West & North Yorkshire Chamber of Commerce, said: "Leeds Bradford Airport is not only a major employer in its own right, but also supports a broader ecosystem of jobs, apprenticeships and skills development across the region. Its continued growth and investment create valuable opportunities for local people to build careers in aviation, engineering, hospitality and customer service. This exciting next phase of modernisation sends a clear signal of confidence in the region’s potential and will further enhance its appeal as a place to do business, invest and visit.”
