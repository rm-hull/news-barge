Criminal damage and antisocial behaviour have led to a zero-tolerance crackdown at a North Yorkshire skatepark.

The problems at Norton and Malton Skatepark have created an environment that is "unpleasant and, at times, dangerous."

The issues include intimidation, bullying, vandalism, and littering, with broken glass and discarded fishing tackle regularly found at the site.

  


Ryan Swain, who runs Ryedale Skate School, said: "This is not about blaming young people, users or the skatepark.

"It is about the actions of a small number of individuals whose behaviour is affecting an entire community.

"And to those responsible: we know who you are.

"You are clearly visible on CCTV."

Concern has grown over reports of criminal damage and persistent antisocial behaviour, which have made the site unsafe for children, families, and other users.

Ryan, who campaigned to save the skatepark, added: "The way the park is being left is also completely unacceptable.

"Litter, smashed and broken glass and fishing tackle and equipment are being left around the skatepark.

"Fishing line and especially barbed hooks have absolutely no place in or around a facility used by children, skateboarders, scooters, BMX riders, families and even dogs.

"They are dangerous and could cause a serious injury."

The skatepark serves a wide range of users from across the area and is described as a vital community asset.

Ryan added: "There are far more people who use this skatepark, respect it, want it and need it than there are people trying to ruin it."

Norton-on-Derwent Town Council and North Yorkshire Police are now taking a tougher stance.

A zero-tolerance policy will be enforced against criminal damage, threatening behaviour, and persistent antisocial behaviour.

The skatepark is equipped with high-quality CCTV, and relevant footage can be provided to the appropriate authorities.

Efforts to engage positively with those causing problems have so far been unsuccessful.

Ryan said: "I’ve tried countless times with Ryedale Skate School over the years to engage positively with some of those causing issues.

"I’ve spoken about skatepark etiquette, encouraged and included people and even provided free skateboarding tuition to try to give them something to do and be involved with and they shun it.

"But there comes a point where opportunities to engage have to be met with personal responsibility and most importantly accountability."

The facility is seen as an important space for young people, especially those who do not connect with traditional sports or activities.

Ryan said: "It provides exercise, friendships, confidence, resilience, independence, community and somewhere to belong.

"For some young people, particularly those who don’t connect with traditional sports or activities, somewhere like this can mean an enormous amount.

"Don’t allow the actions of a few to destroy something that benefits so many."

Maintenance and repairs at the skatepark are the responsibility of the council, though community volunteers also contribute.

The public is urged to report criminal damage, intimidation, or antisocial behaviour to the appropriate authorities rather than intervening directly.

Skatepark-related issues and CCTV enquiries can be reported to Norton-on-Derwent Town Council by calling 01653 695348 or emailing [clerk@norton-on-derwent-tc.gov.uk.](mailto:clerk@norton-on-derwent-tc.gov.uk.)
