A YORK-based stone carver has taken inspiration from one of the city's most iconic buildings in his newest wildlife based creation.

Anthony Bartyla, of Raven and Hare Sculpture in York, who specialises in hand-carved relief sculpture, has begun adding stained glass to his pieces.

His newest work is inspired by York Minster, as well as the local wildlife - combining stained glass with traditional stone sculpture.

Working primarily with Yorkshire sandstone and limestone, Anthony uses traditional stone carving techniques.

He has now started incorporating bespoke glass elements into his work, exploring the relationship between light, landscape, and natural materials.

  Anthony Bartyla's work 

*(Image: Provided)*

Anthony has worked as a stonemason for the past 10 years and has developed his sculptural practice through self-directed study and experience, including around six months of full-time carving.

As reported by The Press in 2024, his dream is to sell enough of his work to fund his admission to a course at the Florence School of Art where he’ll spend years learning to sculpt in the style of the Florentine masters.

  Anthony Bartyla's work 

*(Image: Provided)*

Speaking to The Press, Anthony said: “I’ve always been arty, but around the age of 30, I just discovered I can do this, and these shows give me a great chance to showcase my work.”

He also created a forest scene, featuring an image of a fox and pheasant at sunset, which was on show at Harrogate Flower Show in April 2024.
