# Could a 700-year-old skeleton found at Stirling Castle be the first victim of a trebuchet?

![A trebuchet](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/19/7/03/Trebuchet.jpeg)

- Archaeologists have identified a skeleton found at Stirling Castle as likely belonging to the world's earliest-known trebuchet victim.
- Skeleton 150 belonged to a man in his 20s who died from a massive blunt-force impact during a siege approximately 700 years ago.
- Scans revealed over 160 fractures sustained around the time of death, including 61 to the skull and 57 to the ribs.
- The injuries were likely caused by a projectile from a massive trebuchet, such as Edward I's famous War Wolf, during the Scottish Wars of Independence.
- Researchers noted that the findings present the first known evidence of trebuchet trauma in the archaeological record.
