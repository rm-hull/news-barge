One Nation files three years of overdue financial returns

Sarah Martin

Pauline Hanson’s One Nation party has complied with a request from the Queensland regulator to file three years of overdue financial returns.

The party was sent a demand from the Office of Fair Trading (OFT) last month to submit the missing returns by 18 August or face compliance action.

An OFT spokesperson told the Guardian on Wednesday that the returns were filed after business hours on Tuesday, ahead of the midnight deadline. They said:

One Nation Queensland Division Inc has now lodged audited financial statements for the years 2022/2023, 2023/2024 and 2024/2025, as required by the Associations Incorporation Act 1981.

The Office of Fair Trading has notified One Nation Queensland Division Inc that any future failures to lodge annual returns within prescribed time limits may result in enforcement action.

Pauline Hanson speaks to One Nation senators Sean Bell (right) and Tyron Whitten in March. Photograph: Lukas Coch/AAP

Under the act, members of the management committee of an incorporated association must ensure financial statements are lodged for the association’s last reportable financial year.

Any further breaches could expose Hanson and the New South Wales One Nation senator, Sean Bell, to financial penalties for failing to comply with state legal requirements and could see the party struck off the state register of incorporated associations.

Guardian Australia has previously revealed that the party has repeatedly failed to meet the legal requirements for running an incorporated association in Queensland, including failing to hold annual general meetings on time and filing annual and financial reports late.

Returns filed until 2022, obtained by the Guardian and analysed by a leading accounting expert, revealed more than $1m in missing or worthless assets, which the party has refused to explain.

We don’t want a ‘bidding war’ between big tobacco and organised crime, says Labor minister

The aged care minister, Sam Rae, says reducing the tobacco excise would essentially lead to a bidding war between big tobacco and organised crime.

The government has shut down pressure from groups including One Nation to cut the tobacco excise to combat a soaring black market, and has said the focus should remain on law and order.

On the Today show earlier this morning, Rae said:

We don’t want to get into a bidding war on tobacco prices between big tobacco and organised crime. Now, state governments are working through the law and order issues that are very real here, and of course, the federal government’s supporting that through our Illicit tobacco taskforce. But if we make tobacco dirt cheap, it’s going to be harder for people to quit, and it’s going to be easier for young people to start.

The health minister, Mark Butler, also dismissed the call from One Nation to slash the excise by 75%, calling it a “cheap headline” on ABC News Breakfast earlier. He said:

Governments aren’t usually in the habit of engaging in price competition with organised crimes. That’s a hard line for us to cross. Equally, no one’s really said to me or to the government where the price point is that actually starts to move them out of the market … I think this requires more deep thought than frankly cheap headlines of the type we’ve seen this week. We’re not particularly interested in thought bubbles in an area that is this important.

‘Extraordinary negligence’: Welfare groups slam controversial decision on mutual obligations

Australia’s peak welfare groups have slammed the government’s decision to turn back on the targeted compliance framework [TCF], which runs the controversial mutual obligation scheme.

The TCF was paused after an investigation revealed it was illegally cancelling jobseekers’ Centrelink payments.

The Antipoverty Centre, ACOSS, Economic Justice Australia and National Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Legal Services are calling on the government to remove the framework immediately, including payment suspensions and financial penalties.

They are also calling on the government to provide full back-pay and compensation to every person affected by unlawful decisions.

The Antipoverty Centre co-coordinator Kristin O’Connell said:

The decision to prioritise turning cancellations back on is extraordinary negligence on behalf of Amanda Rishworth, who is responsible for both the wellbeing of welfare recipients and the lawful administration of welfare compliance rules. She is ignoring every alarm bell that is ringing about the widespread misuse of payment suspension penalties by privatised employment services, which are inflicted millions of times a year.

The bottom line is, we have zero confidence in DEWR’s capability and competence to know whether they are operating this system lawfully, and it will never be possible to fully protect the safety and wellbeing of people in poverty while compulsory activities remain.

Government claiming a victory for doing ‘the bare minimum’ on gambling, Pocock says

Independent senator David Pocock says Australians will be “hugely disappointed” by the gambling reforms passed in the lower house yesterday.

Pocock has been a vocal critic of the legislation which he’s said doesn’t nearly go far enough to combat the harms of online gambling advertising, and it’s fair to say he’s pretty furious about the result.

Pocock says gambling companies are currently regulated out of the Northern Territory, by a body which has no full-time staff. To make matters worse, he says the regulator has not contacted the wagering companies after shocking allegations of gamblers being offered drugs and sex workers were aired at a Senate inquiry.

He told ABC News Breakfast a little earlier that the legislation has “failed on so many levels”.

It’s tragic to [see] vested interests and their capture of the major parties meaning we don’t get evidence-based policy in this country and policy that puts Aussies ahead of the gambling companies … The late Peta Murphy said partial bans on advertising do not work and that’s why they went with a full ad ban. She said inducements should be banned immediately. This legislation essentially legitimises inducements and says let’s just get rid of the worst types of inducements.

The system is so broken and it’s no wonder we’re the biggest losers in the world per capita, and now the government is trying to claim this huge victory for just doing the bare minimum that really doesn’t deal with the issues that we’re facing.

Mulino ‘confident’ opposition will support news bargaining incentive bill after lifting levy for tech giants

Jumping back to Daniel Mulino on RN Breakfast, the financial services minister says he’s “confident” he can get the opposition on side to vote for the bill.

Mulino says the government will today introduce an amendment to the bill lifting the charge rate from 2.5% to 2.75%. What this means is that tech platforms including Meta, Google and TikTok will now have to make deals with at least eight news media organisations to pay them for their journalism or pay the government 2.75% of their digital advertising revenue which will then be given to news orgs.

‘No plans to lift the super guarantee’, says Mulino

Daniel Mulino, the financial services minister, says the government has no plans to lift the super guarantee from 12% to 15%. Currently federal public servants – including politicians – receive 15.4% super on top of their wages.

Labor has for a while now had a commitment in its national platform to “set out a pathway to increasing it to 15%”, but they’ve so far made no plans to do so. Wayne Swan was the last treasurer to raise the super guarantee from 9% to 12% over a few years.

Mulino tells ABC RN Breakfast:

There’s no plans to lift the super guarantee. Getting it to 12% was the work of Labor governments over a very long period of time. And I might say, in the face of opposition, it was fought tooth and nail every step of the way by the opposition.

Mulino is addressing the National Press Club today to announce measures to protect superannuation consumers. Why? He says it came from the collapse of superannuation providers Shield and First Guardian:

The scale of that collapse was shocking. Almost 12,000 Australian individual members of super funds and their families lost large parts, or in some cases, all of their super savings. But what was also increasingly clear was the predatory and inappropriate behaviour which led to those collapses. Lead generators reaching out to these people in unsolicited ways, undertaking highly manipulative interactions to convince them that their super products were not performing well or were inappropriate, and then manipulating them into inappropriate products for them.
