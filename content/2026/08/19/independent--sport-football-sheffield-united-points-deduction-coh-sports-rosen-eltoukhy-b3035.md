A business belonging to Sheffield United’s chairmen has been wound up by the High Court.

Legal action against COH Sports was brought by United World, the Championship side’s former owners.

American co-chairmen Steven Rosen and Helmy Eltoukhy lead COH Sports, which completed its acquisition of the club in December 2024.

United World, owned by Prince Abdullah bin Mosa’ad, submitted the petition last month over claims it is owed more than £35 million from the sale.

The matter was concluded in roughly 10 seconds at London's specialist Insolvency and Companies Court (ICC) on Wednesday.

![Prince Abdullah bin Mosa’ad submitted the petition over claims his company is owed more than £35 million from the sale of the club](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/19/12/33/_____.jpeg)

Angus Groom, representing United World, sought the "usual compulsory order", which Judge Paul Greenwood granted.

Sheffield United could face a 12-point deduction if the English Football League rules that ownership regulations were breached.

The club previously stated in June that Delaware-based 1919 Partners represents its parent business and "sits at the centre of the ownership structure".

Sheffield United were playing Premier League football just two years ago, and almost secured promotion back to the top-flight last year, only to lose to Sunderland in the Championship play-off final.

Last season was a disappointing one for the Blades as they finished down in 13th place after failing to challenge for a play-off spot.

They started this season with a 3-0 Carabao Cup win over Mansfield Town before being held to a goalless draw by Birmingham City at Bramall Lane on Saturday.

They are due to return to action this weekend when they face Swansea City away from home.

Chris Wilder’s side will be hoping to battle for promotion this season, but a point deduction would be a major setback for the club’s ambitions of achieving this aim.
