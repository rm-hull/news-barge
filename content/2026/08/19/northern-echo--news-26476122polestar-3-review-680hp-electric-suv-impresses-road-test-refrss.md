The Polestar 3 feels blisteringly quick – so much so that my mother-in-law let out a little gasp when the road conditions allowed me to give the accelerator a squeeze.

Startling mild-mannered ladies in their mid-70s may not be the aim of the game, but the fact the Performance version can hit 60mph in under four seconds would make most people sit up and take note.

Aside from the straight-line speed, it’s the handling that left a more lasting impression on me. On an undulating road, it tackles flowing corners with a level of composure and confidence I’ve rarely experienced in an SUV.

  The classy Polestar 3 Performance catches the sunlight


That’s high praise for a vehicle of this size. The Polestar 3 is a substantial, five-seat electric SUV, weighing between 2,525kg and 2,605kg depending on specification, yet I didn't think it ever felt cumbersome from behind the wheel. It is fast, certainly, but more importantly it has the poise and balance to make that pace usable on a demanding road.

In its most potent Performance Pack guise, the dual-motor all-wheel-drive Polestar 3 develops 680hp and can complete the 0-60mph sprint in a mere 3.8 seconds.

Those are figures to trouble serious performance cars, let alone a family-sized SUV. The delivery is as immediate as you would expect from an electric powertrain: a brief squeeze of the accelerator is all it takes to summon a wave of torque that sends the car forward with purpose. I’d advise treating it with the utmost respect, as anyone shelling out the best part of £100,000 surely would.

  The interior of the Polestar 3 Performance


On twisting B-roads, there’s a sense of being able to get into a flow through the corners. Indeed, there’s a pleasing sense that the chassis is working with the powertrain rather than merely trying to contain it.

Large SUVs can sometimes feel uneasy on undulating surfaces, with their mass shifting around as the suspension tries to keep body movements under control. The Polestar does not entirely disguise its weight, but it manages it with remarkable sophistication.

The steering is a major part of that impression. It’s direct and accurate, allowing you to place the front of the car confidently on the approach to a bend, with excellent ‘turn in’.

The near-even 49:51 front-to-rear weight distribution also helps. Rather than feeling nose-heavy, the Polestar 3 has a balanced, neutral character through a sequence of corners.

Much of that confidence appears to stem from the suspension technology.

Polestar says its active air suspension uses sensor inputs up to 500 times a second, adapting its response to the road and the way the car is being driven. At higher speeds, it can lower the ride height to reduce aerodynamic drag, but its greater contribution is in the way it maintains control over broken surfaces and sudden changes in elevation.

  The nose of the Polestar 3 Performance


The all-wheel-drive system and torque vectoring provide further reassurance.

Looks-wise, the Polestar 3 makes a bold first impression. It is a sizeable 4.9m-long SUV, but its low roofline, crisp bodywork and broad, planted stance prevent it from looking like a conventional luxury 4x4. In Performance Pack guise, 22-inch wheels and Swedish-gold brake callipers sharpen the visual impact, giving the car a suitably purposeful look.

The interior follows Polestar’s clean design approach. A 14.5-inch portrait touchscreen sits at the centre of the dashboard, operating the Google-based infotainment system with Google Maps and the Play Store built in. It is joined by a 9-inch driver display, with a head-up display available through the Pilot Pack. Four USB-C ports, rear climate controls, digital key technology and over-the-air software updates make the cabin feel well equipped for modern family use.

The optional 1,610W, 25-speaker Bowers & Wilkins system with Dolby Atmos surround sound is a suitably high-end accompaniment to the Polestar 3’s refined cabin.

There’s practicality too, with a 484-litre boot behind the rear seats, rising to 597 litres when loaded to the roof, plus a 32-litre front storage compartment. Its 107kWh usable battery delivers a claimed range of up to 368 miles, while 350kW rapid charging can take it from 10 to 80 per cent in 22–32 minutes.
