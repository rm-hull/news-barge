## What is ISP throttling?

Throttling means deliberately slowing down some or all of a customer's internet traffic. It's often done by Internet Service Providers (ISPs) to manage capacity, and in places such as holiday resorts, transport hubs, on trains and in public hotspots where Wi-Fi is offered but heavy data use is discouraged. NordVPN bypasses throttling by encrypting your traffic inside a secure tunnel, hiding your activity so ISPs can't inspect your data or slow down specific services.

## How do ISPs throttle video streaming?

- Indiscriminate throttling during peak periods, which means everyone's service is slowed or limited to manage excess demand
- Applying data caps, which limit your speed after using a certain amount of data each month
- App or content-based throttling, which slows down specific kinds of data but not your entire connection

The third kind of throttling is commonly used to limit or stop video streaming, and NordVPN can prevent that from happening to your connection. It prevents ISPs from identifying what kind of data or content you’re streaming by encrypting your internet traffic, hiding your IP address and obscuring your true location.

## What are the signs of ISP bandwidth throttling?

- Repeated buffering, where the stream pauses because data isn't arriving quickly enough to show anything;
- Poor image quality, such as noticeable blockiness, graininess or visibly low resolution;
- Streaming that just stops or goes out of sync.

Bandwidth throttling looks a lot like network congestion. You can tell the difference between congestion and throttling very easily: enable NordVPN. If your bandwidth is being throttled for video specifically, moving onto your VPN and streaming the same content will deliver a noticeable improvement.

Another way to test is to compare consecutive speed test results on Fast.com and Speedtest.net, because the former is operated by Netflix. A 15Mbps Fast.com result next to a 100+Mbps Speedtest.net result is a clear sign of targeted throttling.

## Is bandwidth throttling the same as geoblocking?

No, although they can both be equally frustrating. Bandwidth throttling restricts connection speeds but geoblocking is a complete block based on your location, so for example a UK streamer may block customers connecting from Europe or the US. A VPN such as NordVPN routes your connection through its own global server network, enabling you to choose the location that you appear to be connecting from. NordVPN also has SmartPlay DNS, which automatically bypasses geo-blocks without the manual server-switching and speed issues that plague some other providers.
