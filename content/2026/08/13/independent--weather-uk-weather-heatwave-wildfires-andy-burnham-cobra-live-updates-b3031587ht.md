Britons have been urged to keep out of the sun during peak hours as a blistering heatwave brings temperature highs of 37C to the country on Thursday.

The Met Office has issued an amber extreme heat warning for much of England, covering parts of the south and east, as well as swathes of the Midlands.

The warning highlights likely health impacts from the heat, as well as potential water safety incidents, travel disruption and power cuts in some places.

Dr Michael Gregory, regional medical director for NHS England in thtse North West, urged the public to take precautions and avoid becoming seriously unwell from overheating, dehydration, heat exhaustion or heatstroke.

“Staying out of the sun during the hottest part of the day, from 11am to 3pm, can lower the risk of becoming unwell, he said.

“If you are planning physical activity, such as exercising or walking the dog, try to do this during cooler parts of the day, such as the morning or evening.”

Some rail operators are also urging train passengers to only travel if necessary on Thursday as services will be disrupted because of hot weather.

## Train passengers urged to 'only travel if necessary' as temperatures soar

Train passengers are being urged to only travel if necessary by some operators on Thursday as services will be disrupted because of hot weather.

Chiltern Railways and East Midlands Railway issued alerts to “only travel if essential”.

Other operators will either run a reduced timetable or are warning about the potential for short-notice cancellations.

Northern said it will run fewer trains than normal on some routes in the North West and Yorkshire on Thursday because of the extreme heat.

It told passengers that high temperatures “increase the risk of issues on the railway and with the reliability of our trains”, which means it must make adjustments to its usual timetable “to keep trains running”.

Heat-related issues that can affect the railway include buckled rails, sagging overhead electric wires and lineside fires.Steel rails can exceed 50C when the air temperature is 30C.

Some ticket restrictions have been eased, and passengers are being asked to check for travel updates before setting off, as well as carry water with them to stay hydrated.

Chiltern Railways said temperatures are expected to reach highs of 38C on parts of its network on Thursday.

It will operate a reduce timetable between 11am and 8pm.

## Catch up: Burnham calls on every retailer to withdraw disposable BBQs after emergency Cobra meeting

Andy Burnham has urged retailers to stop selling disposable barbecues in a bid to prevent wildfires from breaking out as a blistering fifth heatwave continues.

The prime minister on Wednesday urged “all retailers big and small” to pull the barbecues from their shelves as he warned extreme heat conditions “could be with us for some weeks yet”.

His comments came after chairing a meeting of the government’s emergency Cobra committee in response to the prolonged spell of exceptionally hot and dry weather.

England and Wales have already seen as many wildfires this year as in the whole of 2025, and the Met Office says the UK is on course for its hottest summer ever, with drought conditions affecting 45 million people.

Some 1,017 wildfires have been recorded in England and Wales this year, according to data shared by the National Fire Chiefs Council (NFCC), the same as last year’s record-breaking total.

## Firefighters need ‘several more days’ to put out New Forest blaze as water shortages hamper relief

Firefighters tackling a major blaze in the New Forest expect to stay “for several more days” as they contend with water supply issues and volatile wind conditions.

Crews from Hampshire and drawn in from other areas spent a third day in the forest on Tuesday trying to contain the spread of a fire covering more than 1.6 square kilometres of land.

As dry conditions and a blistering heatwave endured, deputy chief fire officer Shantha Dickinson, of Hampshire & Isle of Wight Fire and Rescue Service, said that the teams expected to remain on scene through the week.

## Swimming pools and car washes could be banned as drought declared across most of England

Swimming pools and car washes are among the UK businesses that could be hit by a water ban as providers look to conserve resources amid drought conditions and an upcoming fifth heatwave.

Southern Water has applied to the government for permission to issue a “non-essential use ban” in Hampshire and the Isle of Wight. If granted and put into action, the policy would prevent businesses from activities like washing cars with hosepipes and filling private swimming pools.
