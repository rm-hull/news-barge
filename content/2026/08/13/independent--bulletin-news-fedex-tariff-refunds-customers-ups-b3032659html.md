### Shoppers should check bank accounts as UPS and FedEx start issuing tariff refunds

# FedEx begins issuing $800 million in tariff refunds to customers

- Americans who paid customs duties on overseas purchases last year may receive direct refunds as major logistics carriers pass along government-issued tariff reimbursements following a Supreme Court ruling against taxes levied by President Donald Trump.
- Customs and Border Protection is distributing around $100 billion in reimbursements to commercial entities after the Supreme Court struck down import duties collected under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.
- Shipping giants such as FedEx, UPS, and DHL are processing and returning funds directly to affected customers' bank accounts or credit cards, with FedEx confirming it has begun distributing $800 million in refunds.
- While major retailers like Amazon, Best Buy, and Costco are receiving duty refunds, direct customer payouts from stores are unlikely, with companies planning instead to direct refunded capital toward lowering overall product prices.
- Consumers have filed more than 80 class-action lawsuits against major retailers to recover money from tariff-driven price increases, though legal experts warn that proving specific price hikes were caused strictly by tariffs presents significant legal hurdles.
