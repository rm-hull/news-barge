An amber weather warning for “extreme heat” is in force in parts of England as the UK faces the prospect of the summer’s hottest day so far.

Britons have been urged to keep out of the sun during peak hours as a blistering heatwave brings temperature highs of 37C to the country on Thursday.

The Met Office has issued an amber extreme heat warning for much of England, covering parts of the south and east, as well as swathes of the Midlands.

The warning highlights likely health impacts from the heat, as well as potential water safety incidents, travel disruption and power cuts in some places.

Dr Michael Gregory, regional medical director for NHS England in the North West, urged the public to take precautions and avoid becoming seriously unwell from overheating, dehydration, heat exhaustion or heatstroke.

“Staying out of the sun during the hottest part of the day, from 11am to 3pm, can lower the risk of becoming unwell, he said.

“If you are planning physical activity, such as exercising or walking the dog, try to do this during cooler parts of the day, such as the morning or evening.”

Some rail operators are also urging train passengers to only travel if necessary on Thursday as services will be disrupted because of hot weather.

## Remains of lost village uncovered as drought lowers reservoir levels in record-breaking heatwave

The remains of a lost village demolished and flooded over 50 years ago have been resurfaced as England’s back-to-back heatwaves and dry weather continue to affect water levels.

Nether Hambleton, in the East Midlands, was submerged to create Rutland Water reservoir in the 1970s. It became Europe’s largest man-made lake at the time, with over 3,000 acres of land flooded.

Exceptionally low water levels in the reservoir have now revealed stonework and foundations of the lost buildings, around half a century after they were demolished.

The village was part of the wider parish of Hambleton, which now exists only as Upper Hambleton in the heart of the peninsula at the centre of the reservoir.

## Forecaster warns temperatures could reach 38C

A Met Office forecaster has warned that temperatures in parts of England today could reach as high as 38C and impact infrastructure including railway lines and roads.

Met Office forecaster Alex Burkill said: “Temperatures (in areas covered by the amber warning) could reach highs of around 36, 37, possibly 38C.

“Now when you get these sorts of high temperatures, well that’s when we could see impacts to our infrastructure, our railway lines, our roads, they are not designed for this sort of heat.

“And more people are likely to be impacted by how hot it is with regards to their health.”

He said there was a slim possibility of Thursday becoming the hottest August day on record, but that this was “actually pretty unlikely”.

“It could be the hottest day of the year so far if we get above 38C, and if we get above 38.5C, well it could be the hottest August day on record, but that’s actually pretty unlikely.”

## Mapped: Where drought has been declared in the UK as another heatwave looms

The hot and dry weather is set to peak on Thursday with nearly three-quarters of England and all of Wales now suffering from drought conditions.

Temperatures are expected to peak around 37C as a fifth heatwave of the year puts the UK on track for its hottest ever summer.

Some 71.3 per cent of England has been plunged into drought after a record dry July and a very dry start to August, putting immense pressure on water resources for homes and the environment.

Officials from the National Drought Group said on Monday that there are now 45 million people living in drought conditions, with 27 million already under water-use restrictions.

## Extreme heat alerts come into force across parts of England

A rare amber extreme heat alert is now in place across parts of England.

The warning, issued by the Met Office, says the heat is “likely” to have adverse health impacts for the most vulnerable, and that the wider population is likely to experience some adverse health effects including sunburn or heat exhaustion (dehydration, nausea, fatigue) and other heat-related illnesses.

Forecasters also warn of potential disruption to power lines, travel infrastructure, and an increased risk of water safety incidents.

The warning is in place until 23:59 on Thursday evening.

## Minister insists government is doing more than stopping disposable barbecue sales to tackle wildfires

The government is doing more to stop wildfires than just requesting shops to stop selling disposable barbecues, Baroness Jacqui Smith has said.

Speaking to LBC, the skills minister insisted “quite a lot more” came out of an emergency Cobra committee meeting chaired by prime minister Andy Burnham on Wednesday.

She said: “(There is) additional funding for firefighters with equipment and training to help with some of the wildfires that we’ve seen. We’ve already invested in new reservoirs, for example, to help to protect our water supply.”

Lady Smith added that the government was investing in nine new reservoirs and is working with farmers to help with irrigation issues.

She added: “I think it is fair enough for the prime minister to highlight that all of us have got a responsibility in these really sort of quite difficult, hot times to look after our neighbours, to make sure that you know, we’re keeping our homes cool, and there’s advice about how to do that, and frankly, not having fires or using disposable barbecues where that might be setting fire to things.”

## Heatwaves causing nurses to have seizures as hospital temperatures top 30C

Nurses are collapsing on shift while treating patients in conditions exceeding 30C, the Royal College of Nursing (RCN) has warned.

With the UK on track for its warmest summer on record, nursing staff have suffered seizures and described "dripping with sweat from head to toe" while working.

Concerns have also been raised over patient welfare, with some hospitals described as "unbearably hot" and "not fit for humans".

RCN chief executive and general secretary Professor Nicola Ranger said: “When nursing staff are collapsing, feeling dizzy, sick or even being admitted to the very hospitals they work in because their workplaces cannot handle the heat, it shows just how badly they are being failed.”
