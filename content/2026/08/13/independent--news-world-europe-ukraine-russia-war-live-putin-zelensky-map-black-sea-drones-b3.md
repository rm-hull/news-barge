Ukrainian forces have recaptured 745 sq km of territory in the southeastern Oleksandrivka sector since the start of the year, Volodymyr Zelensky said yesterday.

The operation, which Ukraine says began in January, has brought 26 settlements back under Ukrainian control across the Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk and Zaporizhzhia regions, the president said. He said the offensive was carried out “with precision – exactly as planned”.

Zelensky also said on Telegram that his forces struck Russia’s Novorossiysk naval base overnight with drones, missiles and unmanned boats.

Veniamin Kondratyev, the governor of the Krasnodar region where the port city is located, said a “massive” wave of Ukrainian attacks had targeted the area and killed at least two people, including an eight-year-old child.

Earlier, two of Russia’s biggest grain terminals at the southern Black Sea port city suspended operations as a result of the overnight Ukrainian drone strikes, industry sources told Reuters.

It comes despite Ukraine agreeing to pause drone strikes on oil tankers using the Novorossiysk port after a request from US vice president JD Vance, according to a report in the *Financial Times* citing Ukrainian officials.

## Ukraine destroys missile system protecting Putin’s $1bn Black Sea palace

Ukraine is destroying air defence systems near Russian president Vladimir Putin’s $1bn palace on the Black Sea coast, according to reports.

Two attacks over the last week have targeted military assets in Gelendzhik, around 12 miles from the Russian leader’s holiday home.

On 7 August, Ukrainian drone attacks struck Volna Kupol Garant, Russian electronic warfare equipment designed to jam Starlink communications over an area of 20 sq m, *The Moscow Times* reports.

On Sunday, a further strike was launched on an S-400 anti-aircraft missile system and launch pad just 500m away from the previous attack.

Major Robert Brovdi, commander of Ukraine’s unmanned systems forces said the S-400 had fired six missiles on Sunday night before it was left “ablaze and sputtering for three hours”.

## Moldova to give Ukraine a 50% discount on rail cargo shipments

Moldova has agreed to give Ukraine a 50 per cent discount on rail transit charges for Ukrainian cargo until the end of 2026, helping exporters use alternative routes through Moldova to Moldovan and Romanian ports.

The move comes as Russian attacks have disrupted Ukraine’s Black Sea ports and shipping.

Russia also struck Ukraine’s Izmail port area in Odesa region, damaging infrastructure and causing fires.

Izmail is a key Danube port for Ukrainian grain and other exports.

Meanwhile, Russia said it was seeking alternative routes for its own exports after Ukrainian attacks disrupted operations at the Black Sea port of Novorossiysk.

## Ukraine needs 5% of US Patriot interceptors to survive winter, Zelensky says

Ukraine needs five per cent of US Patriot interceptor stocks to get through the winter and 10 per cent to destroy all Russian ballistic missiles, Volodymyr Zelensky told CNN.

He said Ukraine had “two and a half times less interceptors than we had in 2025”, while Russia was firing twice as many ballistic missiles each month.

Zelensky said he was asking the US to sell Ukraine 5 per cent of its interceptor stocks.

Zelensky said Ukraine was currently receiving only “1 per cent” of what the US makes.

“From 1 per cent to 5 per cent... I have some months and millions [of] phone calls and anything else. I do some things which I can’t even share. It doesn’t mean that it’s out of the law. But I can’t speak about it.”

## Kyiv made significant battlefield gains ‘exactly as planned’, Zelensky says

Ukrainian forces have recaptured 745 sq km of territory in the southeastern Oleksandrivka sector since the start of the year, Volodymyr Zelensky said on Wednesday.

The operation, which Ukraine says began in January, has brought 26 settlements back under Ukrainian control across the Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk and Zaporizhzhia regions.

Zelensky said the offensive was carried out “with precision – exactly as planned”.

The gains broadly align with assessments by Ukrainian open-source monitoring group DeepState, which has confirmed the recapture of 19 settlements and Ukrainian advances in six other areas.

The reported territorial gains refer specifically to the Oleksandrivka sector, rather than Ukraine’s overall battlefield position.

Russia continues to make advances elsewhere along the front, particularly in Donetsk, although analysts say its overall rate of territorial gains has slowed this year.

## Relations with Armenia ‘even more constructive’, Zelensky says

After speaking with Armenian prime minister Nikol Pashinyan on Wednesday, Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said the relationship between the two countries was becoming “more constructive”.

He wrote on Facebook: “Relations between Ukraine and Armenia are now becoming even more constructive, and it is important that we maintain a continuous dialogue.”

The leaders discussed Zelensky’s recent visit to Armenia and identified priority areas for further cooperation, according to *The Kyiv Independent*.

Zelensky also thanked Pashinyan for congratulating him on the 975th anniversary of the founding of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra – one of Ukraine’s most prominent Orthodox Christian sites.

“It is an important date for Christians throughout our region,” Zelensky said.

Armenia has increasingly distanced itself from traditional ally Russia under Pashinyan’s government, seeking closer ties with the West.

Relations between Moscow and Yerevan have deteriorated since Russia failed to prevent Azerbaijan’s September 2023 offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh.

## Ukraine’s Izmail port infrastructure on fire after Russian attack, local authorities say

Russia launched an overnight attack on Ukraine’s Danube port city of Izmail in the southern Odesa region, sparking fires and damaging port infrastructure, local authorities said on Thursday.

The strike hit the port area, according to officials posting on Telegram.

Located near Ukraine’s border with Romania, Izmail is home to the country’s largest Danube River port and serves as a key export hub for grain and other commodities.

## Russian city's water supply suspended after Ukrainian drone strike

Andrei Kravchenko, mayor of the Russian city of Novorossiysk, has said that the Black Sea port's water supply had been suspended after damage caused by Ukrainian drones overnight.

An emergency situation had been declared, Kravchenko said according to Reuters, adding that officials were working to assess the damage.

He said in the same statement, which was posted on the Max messenger, that several educational facilities had been damaged in the strikes.

## Ukraine bombs Russia's Black Sea naval base with drones and missiles, Zelensky says

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky has said on Telegram that his forces struck Russia's Novorossiysk naval base overnight with drones, missiles and unmanned boats.

Earlier, two of Russia's biggest grain terminals at the southern Black Sea port city suspended operations as a result of the overnight Ukrainian drone strikes, industry sources told Reuters.

Russia is the world's largest wheat exporter and most of those exports are shipped via its Black Sea ports like Novorossiysk. Russia's main grain lobby group warned last month that Ukrainian drone attacks could shut down grain exports via the Black Sea in the near future, pushing up prices and causing hunger in Africa and the Middle East.

Veniamin Kondratyev, the governor of the Krasnodar region where the port city is located, said a "massive" wave of Ukrainian attacks had targeted the area and killed at least two people, including an eight-year-old child.

## Ukrainian strikes on Putin’s refineries force Russia to turn to India for gasoline

Russian officials have resorted to importing gasoline from the likes of India amid a rise in Ukrainian drone strikes on its major refineries, a new report has revealed.

Kpler, a data and market intelligence provider, said new data showed Russia received its first shipment on 5 August and more was likely on the way.

India’s oil processor Nayara Energy Ltd, which is backed by Russia’s top oil producer Rosneft PJSC, has been identified as the source of the fuel.

The transfers were made to Russia-linked tankers via shipments off Egypt, Bloomberg reported.

“The emergence of Indian barrels is particularly notable,” said Sumit Ritolia, lead analyst at Kpler.

The cargoes, which complement continued imports from Belarus and other neighbouring markets, highlight “the severity of the current domestic gasoline imbalance, and the extent to which lower refinery runs are reshaping Russia’s traditional product trade flows,” he said.
