# Avocado inspections resume after security scare sparked shortage fears in US

![The United States suspended avocado export inspections in the state following a security alert, a move that could delay shipments from Mexico's top producing region](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/13/20/54/2026-08-07T002359Z_209331237_RC26TMA13ZFO_RTRMADP_3_MEXICO-USA-AVOCADO.jpg)

- The United States government says avocado inspection operations have resumed in full across Michoacan, Mexico, after security concerns temporarily halted exports.
- The suspension was triggered by safety threats after the detention of alleged cartel leader Alfonso Fernandez Magallon and unconfirmed reports of threats against a U.S. inspector.
- In response to the security concerns, Mexico deployed over 1,500 soldiers and National Guard troops to the region to safeguard inspectors in Michoacan, the nation's top avocado-producing state.
- While partial inspections had resumed earlier, the full restart resolves immediate supply disruption concerns for the American market, following similar safety halts that occurred in 2022 and 2024.
- U.S. Ambassador Ron Johnson praised Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum and her Security Cabinet for implementing necessary measures to ensure a safe resumption of operations.
