# Google searches for ‘sore eyes’ spike as stargazers ignore eclipse advice

![People watch a partial solar eclipse through protective glasses from the Dune of Pilat in La Teste-de-Buch near Arcachon, France, August 12, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/13/06/2026-08-12T224457Z_194424191_RC26XMADOWS9_RTRMADP_3_SOLAR-ECLIPSE-FRANCE.JPG)

- European stargazers witnessed a rare double celestial event as the Perseid meteor shower peaked hours after a major partial solar eclipse.
- The Moon obscured over 90 per cent of the Sun, marking the greatest solar coverage above British skies since the 1999 total eclipse.
- The Royal Greenwich Observatory sold out 15,000 eclipse glasses, while John Lewis reported a 40 per cent rise in colander sales used for safe shadow projection.
- Google searches for sore eyes spiked by 650 per cent following the eclipse, highlighting eye safety concerns after the event.
- Following the 1999 total solar eclipse, the Royal College of Ophthalmologists reported around 70 cases of people experiencing vision problems after watching the eclipse. Around 40% had looked at it for less than a minute.
