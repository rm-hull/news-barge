*Blue Bloods* alum Jennifer Esposito has opened up about her difficult health journey and the severe depression and panic attacks that led to an “excruciating” stay in a psych ward. 

Appearing on a recent episode of the *Heal Squad* podcast, the 53-year-old — best known for playing Detective Jackie Curatola on the hit CBS show — detailed the myriad of physical and mental health symptoms she experienced as a result of undiagnosed celiac disease. 

Celiac disease is an autoimmune condition in which the consumption of gluten, found in rye, wheat and barley, causes the immune system to overreact and mistakenly attack the small intestine.

Esposito recalled that it was during her two-season stint on the sitcom *Samantha Who?* when “things started to collapse.”

“I had hair falling out, nails falling off,” she said. “A tooth fell out of my mouth, like literally in the middle of a scene. I was so depressed [about] being so sick all the time, and fighting panic disorder.”

![Jennifer Esposito was admitted to a psych ward because of severe depression and panic attacks](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/13/14/44/Jennifer-Esposito.png)

![Esposito eventually discovered her severe panic disorder was a symptom of undiagnosed celiac disease](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/13/12/50/Jennifer-Esposito.jpeg)

Eventually, she went to a female doctor who listened to her entire health history and took blood tests before discovering Esposito had one of the worst cases of celiac disease she had ever seen.

“She’s like, ‘I’m not sure how you’re alive; My liver was failing,” the actor recounted. She explained that her body was taking nutrients from her nervous system, which is why “my panic disorder was out of control.”

“When I stopped the gluten and went through this detox, they had me on four Klonopin a day” as a way to treat her panic attacks, she said.

“I was literally sleeping in my kitchen,” Esposito added, “because it was the only place that didn’t have a window because the stimulus from the light would make me tremble and shake.”

Months after her diagnosis, she connected with a medical expert who wanted to study her “because I was so ill.”

Esposito remembered on one particular visit, pleading with the doctor to keep her there “because I’m jumping out of my skin. I’m going to jump out the window.”

The doctor interpreted her remark as suicidal, “and within half an hour, I was wheeled upstairs to the psych ward,” she said. “I will say it was seven of the most excruciating hours of my entire life because I was yet again pleading for my sanity.

“You can’t put someone who’s having severe panic disorder in a locked space,” she noted, adding that the doctors continued to misunderstand her symptoms.

“And it was because I said I was going to jump out the window, they thought I was going to harm myself,” Esposito continued.

“I remember [the doctor] looked at my dad and he was like, ‘Is she always this hostile?’” she said. “And I just thought, if I ever get out of here … I have to do something.”

Her experience inspired her to share her difficult health journey in her 2014 memoir, *Jennifer’s Way*, and to connect with other celiacs through community groups.
