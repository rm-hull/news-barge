A Colorado mom had a police officer handcuff her son in a mock arrest when he refused to take a photo on the first day of school.

After 5th-grader Colton refused to take the picture on Monday morning, Keenesburg mom Amanda Stolz says that she called in a local officer named Jack.

On Facebook, she revealed that Colton “may or may not have ended up in handcuffs before school.”

“Officer Jack had to step in and inform him that refusing your mom a first-day-of-school picture is, in fact, against the law,” she joked.

Stolz shared images of the ‘arrest’ on social media, including the moment that Jack cuffed a laughing Colton. She also shared a photograph of the cop putting Colton into the cop car.

![An officer at the Keenesburg Police Department pretended to arrest the boy and even posed with him for some pictures](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/12/15/35/Screenshot-2026-08-12-at-16-23-17.png)

In one image, Jack even posed with the car while Colton grinned from the backseat.

Stolz told CBS Colorado that it was “awesome that Officer Jack took it serious.”

“We were like, 'Well, I guess we have you back-to-school pictures now, don't we?’” she said.

In her Facebook post, Stolz added that she was thankful for the work that Keenesburg Police officers do in the local community.

![A Colorado mom says she had her son, Colton, cuffed by police on Monday after he refused to take a first-day-of-school picture](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/12/15/00/Screenshot-2026-08-12-at-16-17-13.png)

“Moments like this are something these kids will remember, and I love seeing our officers taking the time to joke around, connect with them, and make their morning a little more fun,” she added.

*“* I love having fun with kids and showing them another side of policing,” Officer Jack, who is also known as Officer Ross, told*The Independent.*

Keenesburg Police Department Chief Jensen that he is “exceedingly proud of our officers for knowing when to be firm, when to be fair, and when to be fun.”

“Congratulations to Colton on surviving his first day of fifth grade, and to Mom for securing the evidence,” he said. “Here's to more smiles, more memories, and more moments that remind us why we love this little town.”

But while the police were happy to participate in this case, not every social media joke goes down so well with authorities.

Last year, cops in Michigan warned families about the ‘A.I. Homeless Man prank’, which saw fake images of vagrants men generated inside homes to scare family or roommates.

West Bloomfield Police Department hit out at the trend in a social media post, warning that while it may seem like a joke it could can cause panic, lead to dangerous or violent reactions and can waste emergency resources.

“Please talk to your kids about the real consequences of these trends,” the department said. “What seems funny online could have serious emotional and or legal consequences."
