A York head teacher has praised the school’s “wonderful” students on A-Level results day.

Fulford School head Russell Harris said it was an "amazing day" but an emotional one as he waved goodbye to pupils after they picked up their exam grades on Thursday morning (August 13).

Three Fulford students got into Oxbridge universities, eight will study medicine and two pupils will go on to study veterinary medicine.

Six students also got into “highly sought after” apprenticeships, Mr Harris said.

Overall, more than two-thirds of all A-Level grades at Fulford School were awarded at A*-B and over a third achieved A*/A grades.

Twenty per cent of the cohort at Fulford achieved A* grades in maths and the sciences.

More than three-quarters of vocational students also achieved a distinction* or distinction.

Mr Harris said: “The outcomes are, again, strong across the board. And we just want to celebrate how wonderful our students are, and the fact they’ve got into some amazing universities and next step apprenticeships.”

  Fulford School pupils collect their A Level results on Thursday (August 13)

  Ella Taylor and Evie Hudson collect their A Level results at Fulford School on Thursday (August 13)

The head teacher said the school was “really, really pleased with those outcomes. I think that’s what today is about – the celebration of those fantastic outcomes and the next steps.”

He said the results day "makes me feel extremely privileged to be a head teacher of any school, but even more so to be the head teacher of Fulford School with such amazing students. They are just wonderful people.”

Mr Harris did, however, insist that "results don't define you as a person", saying there are a "wide range" of options available for anyone who did not get the results they were hoping for.

“We’re here to support any young person that hasn’t quite met the entry requirements or achieved what they wanted to do," he said.

“But we do believe that the opportunities that they’ve had, the experiences, the extracurricular, the pastoral support, the personal development and the careers work that they’ve done here, will enable them to be successful in whatever they choose to do.

“We wish them all the best.”

## Student gets glowing exam results - and is off to Cambridge

Fulford School pupil Beth Ravilious was celebrating on Thursday after being accepted into the University of Cambridge to study natural sciences.

She achieved two A*s and two As after excelling in her A-Levels.

The 18-year-old decided to study natural sciences as she is interested in maths and science, and said the course would allow her to “combine them both”.

Beth’s mother, Kate Ravilious, said she was “delighted” by her daughter's results, adding: “An anxious wait is over.”

Fulford students Evie Jackson and Emily Van Tol were also celebrating after they both achieved two A*s and an A, meaning they are both off to study medicine at the University of Edinburgh.

"I think I'm still in shock," said Evie, 18, "I second guessed everything ... I'm on top of the moon. I think it's the improvement from feeling like you're going to be sick to elation.”

Meanwhile, Luke McCloskey set his ambitions sky high after collecting his A-Level results from Fulford School and securing a place at Newcastle University to study politics and philosophy.

"I'm probably going to graduate and become the prime minister," he said.

The 18-year-old said he was attracted to politics after being "disillusioned" with politicians and thinking: "If you can't beat them, join them."
