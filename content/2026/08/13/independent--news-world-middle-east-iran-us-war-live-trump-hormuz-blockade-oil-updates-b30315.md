Donald Trump asserted on Wednesday that the United States could “keep” the Strait of Hormuz in his latest exasperated rant on social media.

The US president insisted that Washington has “total control” of the waterway after Tehran said the US must offer compensationbefore it considers reopening the strait.

Tehran has effectively closed the strait under threat of military action since the US and Israel attacked in February. Iran and Oman have recently been discussing a mechanism to reopen it under their management in a move that would hand even more leverage to Iran, with US talks to end the war still on ice.

In his post on Wednesday afternoon, Trump said: “The U.S.A. has total control over the Strait of Hormuz, I THINK WE WILL KEEP IT!”

“Our Naval Blockade is being called, by everyone, ‘A WALL OF STEEL,’ and there is nothing Iran can do about it,” he added, before signing off: “Iran is all talk and no action, the Bully of the Middle East No Longer. Praise be to Allah! President DONALD J. TRUMP”

## Recap: Oil stockpiles ‘rapidly depleting’ despite Trump claiming ‘total control’ of Strait of Hormuz

A global energy watchdog has warned in a new report that oil stockpiles are “rapidly depleting”, as it urged for a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

“Although the market is projected to return to surplus towards the end of this year, risks remain substantial and the urgency of reopening the Strait has increased, as previously available inventory buffers are rapidly depleting,” the International Energy Agency wrote in a new report.

The agency said that supply is expected to fall by 4.3 mb/d (million barrels per day) in 2026 due to losses in the Middle East and Russia. It comes despite Donald Trump’s unfounded claim that US forces “totally control the Strait of Hormuz”.

“Nobody else, only us [controls it],” he said. “At some point, maybe they'll do something and then they get blown away.”

## US war impact: Inflation slowed last month as gas prices fell but costs remain elevated

Inflation in the United States slowed last month alongside a cooling in underlying price pressures, indicating that elevated oil and gas costs driven by the Iran war have had a limited effect on wider economic prices so far.

According to Labor Department data released Wednesday, consumer prices grew 3.4% year-over-year in July, representing a minor decrease from June's 3.5% rate. Inflation remains above its pre-war level of 2.4% recorded in February. Month-over-month, prices edged up by only 0.1% between June and July.

## Gulf oil spill fast becoming worst in years, agencies warn

A vast oil spill in the Gulf is quickly becoming one of the world’s worst in years after being allowed to spread almost unchecked for weeks, according to international agencies, satellite imagery and environmental groups.

The slick off the coast of Oman from a grounded tanker carrying Russian crude had reportedly reached the mainland by Wednesday, the UN’s shipping agency said.

The slick now covers an area of more than 2,000 square km, said John Amos, an oil spill specialist who reviewed satellite imagery obtained by Reuters.

The Caroline Bezengi, carrying an estimated 800,000 barrels of Russian oil and under international sanctions, ran aground on June 30 at an Omani island that is part of a marine nature reserve.

The ship first reported difficulties off Yemen on June 8. Maritime sources said a blast appeared to have occurred aboard the Caroline Bezengi.

No party has claimed an attack, but the vessel was navigating two separate wars on its journey from Russia to India.

## Desperate US sailors trying to jump overboard on extended Middle East deployment, families say

Desperate sailors aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln have tried to jump overboard during an extended deployment in the Middle East, their families say.

The aircraft carrier, crewed by around 5,000 sailors and Marines, has been deployed since November 21 but more lately has been supporting U.S. military operations in the war with Iran. The deployment had been expected to end in May but has been extended with no current return date, families told *The Military Times*.

Annabelle Loma, the wife of a sailor serving on the Abraham Lincoln, told the publication her husband is now on medical hold after trying to jump from the carrier.

## Just 29% of expected midterm voters say they approve of Trump’s handling of Iran

A cable TV polling expert claims that numbers from two surveys conducted two years apart show just how deeply President Donald Trump has disappointed America's voters when it comes to Iran.

CNN chief data analyst Harry Enten said Tuesday that a July 2024 poll of people who were extremely motivated to vote in that year’s presidential election found that 53 percent were confident Trump would be able to handle the situation in Iran, according to a video clip posted online.

But a July poll of people extremely motivated to vote in November's midterm elections shows that just 29 percent now approve of how Trump is actually handling Iran, Enten said.
