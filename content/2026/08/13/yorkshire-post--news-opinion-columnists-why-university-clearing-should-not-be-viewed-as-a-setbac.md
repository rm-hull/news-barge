# Why university Clearing should not be viewed as a setback - Gillian Murray

## Every summer, thousands of young people across the country receive their exam results and begin making decisions that could shape the course of their lives. For many, that journey leads through Clearing.

Clearing has often been portrayed as a last resort or a ‘Plan B’. In reality, it has become something very different. Today's applicants are more informed, more confident and more willing to explore their options. Increasingly, Clearing is not about recovering from disappointment. It is about finding the right opportunity.

As someone who has recently joined the University of Bradford as Vice-Chancellor, I have been struck by the ambition, resilience and determination of the students I have already met. Their stories are all different, but they share a common theme: talent.

The challenge for society is not that talent is in short supply. It is that opportunity is not always distributed equally.

That is why I believe higher education remains one of the most powerful ways of expanding opportunity.

Of course, prospective students rightly want to know where their studies will lead. In a world shaped by economic uncertainty and rapid technological change, questions about employability and future career prospects matter enormously.

Universities have a responsibility to respond to those concerns. Students should expect courses that are relevant, rigorous and connected to the real world. They should expect opportunities to gain practical experience, develop professional networks and acquire the skills employers need.

At Bradford, that means working closely with businesses, public services and industry partners to ensure students graduate with both academic knowledge and the confidence to apply it. But the value of university extends beyond employment alone.

Our Chancellor, Anita Rani, has spoken about university as a privilege because of the way it broadens horizons and helps people discover their potential. That observation resonates with many graduates. Years after completing their studies, they often reflect not only on what they learned, but on how the experience changed them.

University can build confidence. It can foster independence. It can introduce people to new ideas, new cultures and new possibilities. For many students, it is where lifelong friendships are formed and ambitions begin to take shape.

Those experiences matter just as much as the qualification itself.

At the University of Bradford, we are particularly proud of our longstanding commitment to social mobility and inclusion. Our history is rooted in the belief that education should open doors and create opportunities for people from all backgrounds.

That mission remains as important today as it was when our institution was founded.

The UK's future prosperity will depend upon how effectively we develop talent, support innovation and create opportunities for the next generation. Universities have a vital role to play in that mission, working alongside employers, schools, colleges, local authorities and community organisations.

But it starts with individuals. It starts with helping a young person see possibilities where they might previously have seen barriers. Which brings us back to Clearing.

For some students, Clearing will provide the chance to change direction. For others, it will open up options they had not previously considered. Many will discover a course, a university or a career path that turns out to be a better fit than the one they originally planned.

That should not be viewed as a setback.

If anything, it reflects qualities that will serve people well throughout their lives: adaptability, resilience and a willingness to embrace new opportunities.

Professor Gillian Murray is the Vice-Chancellor of the University of Bradford.
