Purchasing a vehicle often feels like an overwhelming task.

Regardless of whether you are searching for a brand new vehicle or a second-hand alternative, identifying the ideal match presents a distinct challenge.

Given the significant financial investment involved, ensuring that you select a model suited to your needs is essential.

Approaching the market with a clear, well-defined strategy makes navigating the buying process considerably smoother.

Here we have prepared a comprehensive plan below to assist you with your search.

## Pick a budget – and stick to it

Making yourself a firm budget is crucial when it comes to buying a car. By doing so, you’re avoiding some disappointment further down the line, while ensuring that you’re not going to be surprised by any extra costs.

Don’t just think about the outright cost of the vehicle – or the monthly payments, if you’re going down the finance route – but consider servicing, insurance and tax, too.

## Find a bodystyle which works for you

These days, there’s a bodystyle to suit all types of drivers. In recent times, the SUV has grown in popularity and today, it’s a style which many people like.

However, with so many niche-filling cars out there, you’re sure to find one which will work.

Consider what you need the most. Would a raised ride height make it easier to get in and out, or would an estate with a longer, lower boot be better for you? Don’t be afraid to visit a dealer and try cars out for practicality, too.

## Think about boot space

Boot space is another aspect to consider. In the UK, you’ll see that boot space is measured in litres, but sometimes it’s much easier to actually see a boot in-person. It’ll give you a better idea of what you can fit in there.

If you’ve got kids, then you may want to check out a car’s ISOFIX points for car seats. How easy are they to access? Are they kept hidden by any annoying covers?

## Combustion, hybrid or electric?

There used to be a time when you had two primary engine choices – petrol or diesel.

Today, however, there are far more to consider and which one you choose will have a big impact on your day-to-day driving experience.

Petrol and diesel are still there, of course, but modern hybrids offer a far more efficient option.

Plug-in hybrids allow you to charge the car up to make things even cheaper, but you’ll have to find somewhere that you can reliably top them up.

An electric car can make a lot of sense cost-wise but, again, you’ll need to think about where you charge it up and how expensive this could be for you.

## Check history

When looking at a used car, history is so important. Luckily, there are plenty of ways that you can find out more about a second-hand car.

The government’s free online MoT checker is a great place to start, and this will highlight previous MOT test results and any advisories that were issued.

![An electric car can make a lot of sense cost-wise but, again, you’ll need to think about where you charge it up and how expensive this could be for you](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/08/13/09/01KZQPYA50FEADHJZHBBYC7GT7.jpg)

It could be a good idea to run an online HPI check, too. These paid-for checks can show if a car has any outstanding finance on it, or if it has previously been crashed and written off.

Remember, if you buy a used car and it still has finance outstanding on it, then you’re liable for the amount, making these background checks an invaluable service.

## Consider a warranty

If you’re buying a used car, then a warranty could be a good option. New cars come with a standard manufacturer’s warranty, but second-hand examples often don’t.

However, with many manufacturers offering longer standard warranties on their cars, there’s a chance that you could find a second-hand model with some of this still intact, which could help to make motoring cheaper in the long run.

If there’s no manufacturer warranty remaining, then an aftermarket warranty could be very useful. There are plenty of providers, too, so take a look and compare them online.

## Remember to tax and insure

Before driving away, make sure that you have everything in place. If you’re buying a used car, then you’ll need to tax it – it’s no longer carried over on the vehicle itself – and make sure that you have insurance cover in place.
