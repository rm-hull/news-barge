A child and three firefighters have been taken to hospital as huge fires tore through homes in the West Midlands, with a local councillor describing the scene as “apocalyptic”.

On the hottest day of the year, with temperatures soaring to 38.1C in Kew Gardens, firefighters were tackling huge fires in the Stourbridge and Stratford-upon-Avon areas.

At Stourbridge golf course, an “extensive grass fire” has spread across roads in places with people urged to stay indoors.

West Midlands Fire Service said that multiple houses were on fire and people had been evacuated from their homes. The organisation said a child and three firefighters were taken to hospital.

Councillor Andrew Tromans, who represents Wollaston and Stourbridge Town on Dudley Borough Council, described the scene of the fire in Stourbridge as “apocalyptic”.

Meanwhile, in Stratford-upon-Avon a major incident has been declared at the scene of a wildfire. Warwickshire Fire and Rescue Service were called at 2.30pm to reports of a large plume of smoke on Tothall Lane, Salford Priors.

The Met Office has also issued an amber extreme heat warning for much of England, covering parts of the south and east, as well as swathes of the Midlands.

## 'It's shocking' - Resident in Stourbridge reacts to fire

In Stourbridge a man, who declined to be named, said: “At about two o’clock I was working from home.

“I saw smoke come down and it was already at the corner of Racecourse Lane and Norton Road. It’s shocking.”

Several homes nearby had been damaged, including two which could still be seen smouldering near the brow of a nearby hill.

## What we know so far about the fires across the West Midlands

## Stourbridge

West Midlands Fire Service said six houses were affected by the large blaze in Stourbridge, which is estimated to cover 40 acres of land, including a wood.

Wollaston and Stourbridge town councillor Andrew Southall said care home residents have been evacuated alongside others as a result of the fire near Stourbridge golf course.

About 60 firefighters attended the scene after being alerted to the blaze at about 1.30pm.

## Stratford-upon-Avon

In nearby Warwickshire, the county’s fire and rescue service said it is responding to a “large fire” in Salford Priors, Stratford-Upon-Avon, which has been declared a major incident.

Police, fire crews and ambulances are at the scene of the wildfire.

Warwickshire Fire and Rescue Service was called at 2.30pm on Thursday to reports of smoke on Tothall Lane in Salford Priors.

## Stoke-on-Trent

In Stoke-on-Trent, north of Birmingham, fire has hit “a small number of properties and gardens”.

Crews from Staffordshire Fire and Rescue are on the scene of the blaze in the Goldenhill area.

## Pershore

Residents have been evacuated after homes were damaged as a blaze hit Pershore, where around 200 properties are without power and a person has suffered smoke inhalation.

Hereford and Worcester Fire and Rescue Service said emergency services were called out at around 4.30pm on Thursday to deal with “a large” fire on Lancelott Court, off Three Springs Road.

## Scene of fire in Stourbridge 'apocalyptic' - local councillor says

Councillor Andrew Tromans, who represents Wollaston and Stourbridge Town on Dudley Borough Council, described the scene of the fire in Stourbridge as “apocalyptic”.

In a statement on Facebook, he said: “The apocalyptic scenes in Norton and across Stourbridge are sadly just a taste of what is to come if we don’t act.”

He added: “It is my view, and it has been for a while, that Dudley councillors must have an emergency meeting about we protect our borough from the impacts of climate change.”

## 200 homes without power in Pershore, Worcestershire

Residents have been evacuated after homes were damaged by fire in Pershore, Worcestershire, where around 200 properties are without power and a person has suffered smoke inhalation.

Hereford & Worcester Fire and Rescue Service said emergency services were called out at around 4.30pm on Thursday to deal with “a large” fire on Lancelott Court, off Three Springs Road.

A spokesman said: “One person has been treated for smoke inhalation. Thankfully no other injuries have been reported at this time.

“A number of homes have been damaged and 30 properties in the area have been evacuated.”

Residents in the wider area have been told to keep windows and doors closed as a precaution and surrounding roads have been temporarily shut.

The spokesman said “approximately 200 properties are currently without power”, adding: “We recognise this will be an upsetting time for the local community and would like to thank you for your patience and cooperation while we respond to the incident.”

## Residents use watering cans and buckets of water to dampen down smouldering fields

Several homes in Stourbridge were severely damaged by fire, with a smouldering section of adjoining fields being damped down on Thursday night by worried homeowners carrying water in buckets and watering cans.

Fire crews could also be seen hosing down hedgerows and bushes near damaged homes close to the junction with Fairways Avenue.

One local resident, who did not wish to be named, said: “The fire came within feet of the other side of the road.

“It has been terrible for those who have had their property destroyed but it was terrifying for everyone.

“It was all the way across the fields in no time at all. It was hard to believe it was happening.”

## Fire breaks out in Stoke-on-Trent

Fire has hit “a small number of properties and gardens” in Stoke-on-Trent.

Crews from Staffordshire Fire and Rescue are on the scene of the blaze in the Goldenhill area of the city.

In a statement, Staffordshire Fire and Rescue said: “A small number of properties and gardens in the Sandy Road area have been affected by fire.

“Sandyford Park remains cordoned off by Stoke-on-Trent Police.

“Goldenhill Community Centre, on Drummond Street, is open to provide support to local residents.

“Drivers are urged to follow Staffordshire Police officers’ directions and the diversion routes in place.”

## Fire in Stourbridge has 'several points of origin'

West Midlands Fire Service say the wildfire in Stourbridge has “several points of origin” as they warned of road closures and fallen trees.

“This is a wildfire with several points of origin, now involving multiple houses on fire, covering the area from Moreton Road to Iverley Lane, along the total length of Racecourse Lane from Norton Road to County Lane, and from Racecourse Lane to Worcester Lane,” the service said in a statement.

“This includes Stourbridge Golf Club and surrounding roads.

“Roads are closed in the area as they are affected by smoke, fire and in some cases, fallen trees. If you must drive, avoid this area to let us manage this incident.

“Our crews are working tirelessly to control the blaze, prevent it from spreading further and reigniting in these dry, hot conditions.

“Three members of fire service personnel have been taken to hospital, 6 members of the public have been treated for smoke inhalation as well as one child who has been taken to hospital.

“We are working alongside our emergency service partners, as well Severn Trent Water and West Midlands Railway.”

## Everything you can’t do in a heatwave as drought hits most of the UK

More than half of England and Wales has been plunged into drought as the UK braces for its fifth heatwave of the year.

This week could see highs of 38C in parts of southern England, with temperatures hitting the mid 30s for large areas of the country, as hot and dry weather persists.

Millions of people have restrictions on their water use prohibiting hosepipes, sprinklers, or pressure washers for non-essential domestic tasks to conserve public water.

Some 19 counties across England recorded effectively no rainfall – 1mm or less – during July, Met Office figures show, causing river levels to fall and leaving ground dry and at risk of wildfires.

## Union boss 'very concerned' over wildfires

“Firefighters are bravely tackling every blaze trying to keep the public and all our communities safe.

“Our members are also assisting other emergency services dealing with the derailed train in Lewes.
