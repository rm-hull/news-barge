Temperatures are expected to hit 37C in parts of the England on Thursday as an amber warning for “extreme heat” has been issued by the Met Office.

Hot weather will peak across Oxfordshire and the Chilterns on Thursday afternoon and could reach 37C, before cloudy weather brings some relief on Friday, when temperatures are forecast to drop to 33C in London and 25C in Manchester.

By the weekend most of the country can expect slightly cooler temperatures of 27C in London and 22C in Manchester.

The Met Office’s latest forecast for Oxford read: “Temperatures rising quickly under strong sunshine. Feeling hot or locally very hot. As a result, an amber extreme heat warning has been issued, and this covers part of London. Maximum temperature 37C.”

![Highs of 34C are expected in London on Thursday, according to the Met Office](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/13/10/15/Screenshot-2026-08-13-at-10-09-58.png)

It comes as almost three-quarters of England is now suffering from drought conditions, affecting 45 million people, with the country now on course to record its hottest ever summer.

The prime minister has now urged retailers to stop selling disposable barbecues while the dry conditions endure in a desperate bid to curb the risk of wildfires breaking out.

His comments came after chairing a meeting of the Government’s emergency Cobra committee in response to the prolonged spell of exceptionally hot and dry weather.

England and Wales have already seen as many wildfires this year as in the whole of 2025.

![Temperatures are expected to drop to the mid 20C on Saturday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/13/10/58/Screenshot-2026-08-13-at-11-29-41.png)

The Met Office’s deputy chief forecaster Mike Silverstone said the heatwave was expected to be fairly widespread across England and Wales.

“A fairly widespread heatwave is likely across large parts of England and Wales, with hot conditions developing in places,” he said.

“Humidity levels are also expected to increase through the week, which may make conditions feel more uncomfortable, particularly overnight.”

The UK has experienced a consistent North-South split in the weather across this summer, with Scotland and Northern Ireland experiencing heavy spells of rain last weekend.

![Met Office issues an amber warning for 'extreme heat'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/13/10/52/Screenshot-2026-08-13-at-11-37-54.png)

Rare amber “extreme heat” warnings have been issued by the Met Office for London and parts of the South East, East Midlands, East of England, West Midlands and South Yorkshire from 9am to midnight on Thursday.

All of England is under an amber heat health alert lasting until 9pm on Friday.

The UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) warned of risks to the lives of the elderly and vulnerable people and significant effects on health and social care services.

![All of England is under a heat health alert](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/11/15/18/Screenshot-2026-08-11-164359.png)

Train passengers are also being urged to only travel if necessary by some operators on Thursday as services will be disrupted because of hot weather.

Chiltern Railways and East Midlands Railway issued alerts to “only travel if essential”.

Other operators will either run a reduced timetable or are warning about the potential for short-notice cancellations.

Southern Water has meanwhile applied for a rare drought order which would ban businesses from non-essential water use, including cleaning cars and filling non-domestic swimming pools, saying an “exceptional” lack of rain has threatened supplies in Hampshire and the Isle of Wight.

It warned that 165,000 people in the Southampton West supply area could face restricted supplies.

## Met Office five-day weather forecast

## Today:

A dry, sunny and very hot day across many parts of England and Wales. Humidity levels will also be high for some. Cloudier across Scotland and Northern Ireland with showers, possibly heavy and thundery during the afternoon and evening.

## Tonight:

Cloudy with showers continues across Northern Ireland and Scotland, locally heavy with isolated thunderstorms, spreading southeast. Elsewhere, warm overnight with clear spells. Low cloud and coastal fog may develop southwest.

## Friday:

Staying hot across much of England and Wales before a weak cold front brings cooler, cloudier weather in from the west. Isolated afternoon thunderstorms are possible in the southeast.

## Outlook for Saturday to Monday:

Saturday brings sunny spells and scattered showers, heaviest in the southeast. Sunday is mostly dry with sunshine, though rain reaching the northwest later. Monday sees increasing rain, with breezy conditions.
