The recreation of one of Britain's oldest surviving stage sets is set to return to the spotlight at a North Yorkshire theatre. as part of the revival of a John Godber production.

The Woodland Scene, dating back to the early 19th century, will feature in a new staging of The Highwayman at the Georgian Theatre Royal in Richmond on October 30 and 31.

Written by acclaimed playwright John Godber, the play premiered at the same theatre in 2024.

The production was inspired by historical research that revealed the existence of the Woodland Scene, believed to be the oldest surviving stage set in Britain.

Painted between 1818 and 1836, the original canvas scenery includes a backdrop and eight flats, traditionally used to depict rural settings on stage.

A recreation of the Woodland Scene was produced specifically for The Highwayman, allowing the production to showcase a piece of theatrical history in the very place where it originated.

Set in Yorkshire in 1769, the play explores themes of money, social climbing, gambling and gin, painting a picture of a world where new wealth brings new temptations.

Blending romantic adventure with a lively history lesson, it raises questions about what people are willing to risk when the odds are against them.

The Highwayman will be performed at the Georgian Theatre Royal on Friday, October 30 at 7.30pm, and on Saturday, October 31 at 2pm and 7.30pm.

Tickets are available through the box office on 01748 825252 or online at www.georgiantheatreroyal.co.uk .
