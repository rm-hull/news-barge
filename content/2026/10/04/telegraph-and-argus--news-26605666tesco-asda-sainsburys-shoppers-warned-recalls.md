Shoppers face fresh recall warnings after products including mugs, bags and baby sleeping items were pulled from sale.

Retailers including Tesco, Sainsbury's, Argos and Asda have recalled products this week alongside an urgent alert from safety regulators.

This comes after earlier product warnings were issued across UK supermarkets for items ranging from skincare to garden tools.

## **Tesco recall**

Tesco has urged customers to stop using its F&F Home Glass Latte Mug because of a manufacturing fault.

![Tesco](https://www.southwalesargus.co.uk/resources/images/20609110.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Archive)*

The defect means the glass is prone to breaking during normal use, posing a risk of injury to users.

All batches of the mug purchased between September 2025 and August 2026 are affected by the notice.

Customers can return the mug to any Tesco store for a full refund without needing a receipt.

A statement from Tesco said: "As a precautionary measure, Tesco is recalling all batches of its F&F Home Glass Latte Mug purchased between September 2025 to August 2026 inclusive.

"This is due to a manufacturing issue that may cause the glass mug to break during normal use, posing a risk of injury.

"If you have purchased the product affected, please do not use it and instead return it to any Tesco store for a full refund."

Tesco added that no other products are affected and directed shoppers in Ireland to customer services on 1800 248 123.

## **Sainsbury's and Argos recall**

Sainsbury's and Argos have recalled three tote bags from the Tu clothing range over chemical safety risks.

![](https://www.southwalesargus.co.uk/resources/images/21513925.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Getty Images)*

Testing found the items contained excessive concentrations of phthalates, which are compounds used to soften plastics that can cause reproductive harm.

The recalled items are the Tu Pu Tan Curved Top Shopper with item number 147160412, the Tu Black Croc Winged Tote Bag with item number 147166081, and the Tu Brown Belt Handled Core Shopper with item number 147789704.

Sold at selected Sainsbury's supermarkets as well as through the Tu and Argos websites, the bags failed retailer safety standards.

A spokesperson for Sainsbury's said: "Sainsbury’s are recalling the above products due to excessive concentrations of phthalates, which pose a chemical safety risk.

"Prolonged exposure to phthalates may be harmful to human health.

"As a result, the products don't meet Sainsbury’s safety requirements.

"We're asking customers who have purchased any of these items to stop using them and return them to their local Sainsbury’s store for a full refund."

Shoppers can contact Sainsbury's on 0800 028 8303 or Argos customer services on 0800 011 3462.

## **Asda recall**

Asda has issued a voluntary recall for a ladies bag after chemical testing found excessive phthalate levels.

![Four people have been arrested after Asda staff were assaulted in Great Yarmouth](https://www.southwalesargus.co.uk/resources/images/20777992.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Google Street View)*

The affected item is the Front Pocket XBody Bag carrying product code 1100265415001.

Regulatory limits apply to phthalates such as Di(2-ethylhexyl) phthalate because prolonged exposure can pose health risks and damage reproductive systems.

No safety incidents have been reported, but the supermarket stated the recall was initiated as a precautionary step.

An Asda statement said: "While no safety incidents have been reported, we are taking this action to ensure compliance with applicable regulations and to maintain our commitment to consumer safety.

"If you have purchased this bag, please bring it back to your nearest store where you will be given a full refund.

"You do not need your receipt. We are very sorry for any inconvenience caused."

Customers with questions can contact Asda Customer Relations on 0330 053 0111.

## **Urgent alert on baby sleeping bags**

Parents and carers have been told to stop using baby sleeping bags that feature hoods or lack armholes.

The Office for Product Safety and Standards issued the product safety alert on Thursday, October 1, 2026.

Without armholes, infants can slip completely inside the bag where fabric covers their face, while hoods can slide over airways and cause overheating.

Officials confirmed these design flaws create severe hazards of suffocation, fatal injury and Sudden Infant Death Syndrome.

Nearly 50 product recalls and safety notices have been issued across the UK for baby sleeping bags since 2023.

Baby sleeping bags complying with voluntary British standard BS EN 16781:2018 remain safe to use and are exempt from the notice.

Outside pramsuits, footmuffs and specific swaddling products are also excluded from the alert.

Consumers who bought an unsafe sleeping bag marketed for unsupervised cot sleep can seek redress from the seller under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024.

## **What is a product recall?**

When a problem is discovered with an item, products can either be withdrawn from shelves or recalled from buyers.

A withdrawal removes stock from retail display, while a recall asks customers who already purchased the item to return it.

The Food Standards Agency monitors food safety alerts, while the Office for Product Safety and Standards oversees consumer product safety notices across the UK.

Retailers display point-of-sale notices in stores and publish recall guidance online explaining why products have been removed.

Anyone who has purchased a recalled product should stop using it immediately and follow the retailer's instructions to claim a full refund.
