The early years of a world-famous artist will take centre stage in a newly reopened gallery exhibition.

The David Hockney Gallery at Cartwright Hall Art Gallery in Bradford will reopen on October 21, telling the story of the Bradford-born artist’s beginnings and early artistic development.

## 'Before the World Knew Hockney'

Titled "Before the World Knew Hockney," the exhibition will run until October 2029 and will feature works from the Bradford District Museums & Galleries collection, offering a fresh interpretation of Hockney’s formative years.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21038452.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Hockney *(Image: Victoria Jones/PA)*

Bradford District Museums & Galleries described the exhibition saying "Our unique collection at Bradford District Museums & Galleries offers a fascinating insight into David Hockney’s early years, focusing on the period when he was learning to draw, developing his artistic voice and creating some of his earliest work.

"Together, these works reveal the beginnings of an artist who would go on to become recognised around the world.

## Celebrating Hockney's Bradford connections

"The refreshed gallery will bring new displays and interpretation, celebrating Bradford’s unique place in Hockney’s story and offering visitors the chance to discover the collection that shows where it all began."

A tribute to one of Britain’s greatest cultural figures, the gallery will also celebrate a living creative legacy, continuing to inspire new generations through Hockney’s work and story.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20954613.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cartwright Hall Art Gallery *(Image: Supplied)*

The exhibition aims to highlight Hockney’s deep connection to Bradford and the early influences that shaped his career.

## Renewed public interest

The reopening comes amid renewed public interest in the artist and his work.

A Guardian exclusive revealed a rare painting from Hockney’s Dog Series, titled Dog Painting 17 and featuring his dachshund Stanley, is expected to fetch up to £2.5 million at auction next month.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21038820.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Hockney *(Image: Agency)*

Just a handful of works from the series remain in private hands.

There has also been recent interest in Hockney’s childhood art after an Antiques Roadshow expert suggested a portrait he painted at the age of 12, while a pupil at Bradford Grammar School, could be among his earliest known works.

The portrait has remained with the same family ever since it was created.

The reopening of the David Hockney Gallery will give visitors a chance to explore the early influences and works that set Hockney on the path to becoming one of the most recognisable names in contemporary art.
