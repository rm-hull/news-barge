Iran has warned that the Strait of Hormuz will remain shut until the conditions it laid out in an interim agreement in June are met, according to Nournews.

Traffic has effectively come to a standstill with attacks in the vicinity of the waterway reported over the weekend.

“The Americans, who say something different in the media, have recently put forward proposals through a mediator. But they must understand that the period of dragging out the process and dictating one-sided demands is over,” Iran’s parliamentary speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said on Sunday.

“The position of the Islamic Republic of Iran is completely clear and firm, and the Strait of Hormuz will not be opened until our seven conditions, based on the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, are met.” The conditions include a lifting of economic sanctions and a complete stop to all hostilities.

Foreign minister Abbas Araghchi said separately on Sunday ‌that ⁠a plan put forward by Tehran could lead to the route reopening within seven days if accepted.

Meanwhile, Yemen's Houthis have claimed responsibility for carrying out a ballistic missile attack on the Aramco oil refinery in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia.

The Houthis said the attack was carried out in ​retaliation for Saudi attacks on Yemen's ​Sanaa and other provinces.

## Houthis claim missile and drone attack on Aramco facility in Riyadh

Yemen's Houthi rebels have claimed responsibility for a coordinated missile and drone strike targeting a Saudi Aramco facility in the capital city of Riyadh.

Witnesses reported seeing a large plume of smoke and fire rising near an Aramco installation in Riyadh earlier in the day.

The Houthi military has stated the assault was launched in direct retaliation for recent Saudi coalition airstrikes against Sanaa and other Yemeni provinces.

The reported strike marks a sharp escalation in cross-border targeting following claims last week of Houthi strikes against Aramco assets in Yanbu.

## Yemeni forces claim 97 'precision' attacks against Houthis

Yemeni government-aligned forces claim they conducted 97 “precision targeting operations” against the Houthis using aircraft, drones, artillery, missiles and snipers.

The attacks led to “neutralising (260) elements of the militia”, Yemeni military spokesperson Brigadier General Majid Abdullah Al-Nuzaili said in a post on X.

Earlier in the day, the Houthi military said it launched coordinated attacks targeting a Saudi Aramco facility in Riyadh in retaliation for recent Saudi coalition airstrikes against Sanaa and other Yemeni provinces.

## Iraq says it transported 2 million barrels of crude through Strait of Hormuz

Iraq's Oil Tankers Company transported 2 million barrels of Iraqi crude aboard a very large crude carrier (VLCC) through the Strait of Hormuz, its director general said in a statement on Saturday, describing it as the company's first such operation in decades.

## Russia 'will never sacrifice' relationship with Iran, Medvedev says

The deputy chairman of Russia’s Security Council, Dmitry Medvedev, said earlier today that Moscow would “never sacrifice” its relationship with Iran.

“We will never sacrifice our relationship with Iran for the relationship with any other country”, he said, Iran’s Fars news agency reported.

“We and Iran are strategic partners and will remain so forever,” Medvedev said.

## Australia's treasurer says US-Iran war 'has been a disaster'

## Iran says Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until conditions are met, Nournews reports

The Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until conditions set out in a June interim agreement with the US are met, Nournews reported Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bager Ghalibaf as saying on Sunday, a few days after Tehran received Washington's reply to its diplomatic proposal.

“The Americans, who say something different in the media, have recently put forward proposals through a mediator. But they must understand that the period of dragging out the process and dictating one-sided demands is over,” he was quoted as saying.

“The position of the Islamic Republic of Iran is completely clear and firm, and the Strait of Hormuz will not be opened until our seven conditions, based on the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, are met.”

## Iranian diplomats 'kicked out' of US after UN General Assembly – report

Two Iranian diplomats who were part of the delegation to the UN General Assembly in New York were “kicked out” of the US yesterday, Axios reported, citing a US official.

They were found still in New York “long after the UN General Assembly had passed,” despite the US State Department demanding that the entire Iranian delegation leave immediately after the session.

One of the Iranian diplomats left on Friday night and the second left on Saturday, according to the US official.
