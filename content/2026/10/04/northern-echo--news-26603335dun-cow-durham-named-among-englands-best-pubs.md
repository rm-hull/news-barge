A historic city-centre pub has been named one of England’s best places for a pint.

The Dun Cow, on Old Elvet in Durham, was included in a national list of the top 30 pubs compiled by travel website Big 7 Travel.

The list ranked venues on heritage, atmosphere, beer quality and overall drinking experience.

Described as a must-visit for those seeking a traditional pub experience, The Dun Cow was praised for its Tudor-style exterior, open fire, and separate drinking areas.

Big 7 Travel also commended its selection of local beers, including the County Durham-brewed Castle Eden Ale.

The pub, just a short walk from Durham’s busiest streets, was further lauded for maintaining its historic charm.

According to the Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA), the Grade II-listed building contains elements dating back to the 15th century.

Its interior is considered of special historic interest, with traditional wood panelling, a carved fireplace, and a characterful front snug.

Some of the fittings date back to the late 19th century, adding to the pub’s unique character.

CAMRA also notes the pub’s connection to local legend.

The Dun Cow is said to be named after the story of Durham’s founding, in which monks carrying the body of St Cuthbert were guided to Dun Holm by a milkmaid searching for her lost dun-coloured cow.

The national ranking included pubs from across England, from historic countryside inns to long-established city locals.
