A community café that began in a church hall 15 years ago has grown into a charity helping more than 8,000 people every year.

Footprints in the Community now operates with 20 staff, 200 volunteers, a High Street café, and an annual turnover nearing £1 million.

Ruth Fox, founder of the charity, said: "If you told me that then I would never have believed you.

"In fact, I'd probably have run a mile.

"But that's exactly what has happened."

The charity offers a wide range of services aimed at reducing poverty and isolation, including a woodworking group known as The Shed.

David Smithson, 56, joined The Shed 10 years ago while caring full-time for his wife, who has complex health needs.

Mr Smithson said: "It took me six months before I had the courage to walk through the door, but the minute I walked in I was made to feel like I’d been there for ages.

"We all come here for different reasons, but we are like one big family.

"For me, The Shed was an escape from all the responsibilities at home."

He credits the group with helping him cope during difficult times.

Mr Smithson said: "Without The Shed and Footprints in the Community I don’t think I could have got through it."

Ms Fox said stories like Mr Smithson’s are common.

She said: "There are hundreds more stories like his, each representing a life changed, a challenge overcome, or a brighter future made possible.

"I am incredibly proud of our staff and volunteers, who give their time, energy and compassion every single day to support others."

The charity recently received more than £300,000 from The National Lottery Community Fund to help expand its services and reach even more people across Redcar and Cleveland.
