Halloween pumpkin displays could land homeowners with fines of up to £5,000.

Property owners in England and Wales are being urged to inspect doorsteps and pathways before welcoming trick-or-treaters on October 31.

Under the Occupier's Liability Act 1957 and the Occupier's Liability Act 1984, householders must take reasonable care for the safety of visitors to their premises.

## **Why a doorstep pumpkin could cost you £5,000**

Housebuilder Barratt Homes issued a national warning to families decorating their homes ahead of Halloween festivities.

The developer said: "You do not have a duty to prevent all accidents, but you are obliged to deliver reasonable care for the safety of visitors to your property."

Failing to clear hazards could leave householders facing civil liabilities, invalid insurance claims, or court fines reaching £5,000 if an accident occurs.

## **Simple steps to keep trick-or-treaters safe**

Safety guidance advises keeping all lit carved pumpkins, candles and lanterns away from entrances and main walking routes.

Many young trick-or-treaters wear masks or bulky costumes that restrict their vision and make stumbling into obstacles far more likely.

Homeowners are encouraged to swap traditional candles for battery-powered LED lights to eliminate the danger of costumes catching fire.

Anyone installing electrical lights must check cables for frayed wiring and ensure extension cords do not create tripping hazards along the path.

## **Protecting pets and visitors**

Families should also keep household pets secured safely indoors throughout the evening.

Barratt Homes warned that an excited dog could easily knock down a visiting child or dash outside into the street.

Taking simple precautions ensures your Halloween display remains fun without risking costly legal trouble.

## **How to avoid a fine this Halloween**

These are the tips to ensure a stress-free spooky season:

* Lanterns, candles, and lights can obviously all be fire hazards. Make sure you place these out of the direct path to your door and directly around your entrance. Remember that many kids will be wearing masks during Hallowe'en so might have impaired vision.
* Lights and candles lit indoors should be kept clear of curtains, decorations, and other flammable objects. If you can, use LED lights instead of candles.
* If you are using a lot of electrical lights for your outdoor Hallowe'en display, make sure you check the lights and cords for frayed or bare wires. Do not overload your extension leads and ensure there are no wires that could become a trip hazard to little monsters coming up your path.
* Keep your pets in a safe area. The last thing you want is your cat to escape out the door or your excited dog to knock down a child. Make sure you keep your pets secure in your house or garden during Hallowe'en night.
