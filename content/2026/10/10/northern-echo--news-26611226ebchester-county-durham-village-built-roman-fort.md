Ebchester is a quiet County Durham village with far more history beneath its streets than first appears.

Ebchester today is a peaceful place of stone houses, woodland walks and riverside paths.

Much of the modern village sits over the remains of a Roman fort.

Known as Vindomora, the fort guarded an important River Derwent crossing and formed part of Rome's military network in northern England.

Most of the fort is now buried beneath roads, gardens and houses.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21543705.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ebchester *(Image: Peter Cannon/CAMERA CLUB)*

Roman stone may have been reused in later buildings, while parts of Ebchester still follow the shape of the settlement that grew here.

At its centre stands St Ebba's Church. The church is traditionally linked to Æbbe, a 7th-century abbess said to have founded a religious community here before establishing Coldingham Priory.

Its location adds another layer to the story. The church stands within the former fort, putting centuries of religious and military history side by side.

Beyond the historic centre, the River Derwent remains one of Ebchester's defining features.

Footpaths follow the river through woodland and open countryside, far from the industrial towns and villages elsewhere in County Durham.

The surrounding woods are popular with walkers, cyclists and wildlife watchers.

Kingfishers and other birds can be spotted along the river, while the wooded valley keeps the village secluded.

Another historic route runs close by.

The Derwent Walk follows the former Derwent Valley Railway and now forms a popular trail through the countryside.

The old railway line gives walkers and cyclists an easy route through the valley while preserving its transport history.

That combination of Roman remains, railway heritage and riverside scenery makes Ebchester unusual.

Derwent Reservoir lies further along the valley, while Derwentcote Steel Furnace and Beamish Museum reveal different chapters of the North East's industrial past.

Its location means visitors can easily pass through Ebchester on the way to somewhere better known.

A Roman fort lies beneath its streets, the River Derwent runs beside them, and walkers and cyclists now use the old railway line through the countryside.
