# Brits with high blood pressure could get £194.60 weekly DWP PIP payment

## Brits with high blood pressure (hypertension) may be able to claim Personal Independence Payment (PIP) from the DWP, worth up to £194.60 per week

Britons living with high blood pressure could be entitled to claim Personal Independence Payment ( PIP ), according to recent official data from the Department for Work and Pensions ( DWP ). High blood pressure, medically termed hypertension, is particularly prevalent amongst older people and "can lead to serious problems like heart attacks or strokes", according to the NHS website.

"There are usually no symptoms, so you may not realise you have it," it states. Nevertheless, it can trigger symptoms including headaches, blurred vision, chest pain, and significantly affect your capacity to care for yourself, potentially making you eligible for PIP.

PIP is a benefit designed to assist with additional living expenses if you're dealing with both a long-term physical or mental health condition or disability, and struggle with certain daily activities or mobility due to your condition, the GOV.UK website states.

Applications can be made for two components: daily living and mobility. The first assesses your ability to manage various household duties such as washing and cooking, while the second evaluates your mobility and transport needs. It's accessible to individuals aged 16 and above who haven't yet reached State Pension age, reports the Express.

If your claim is approved, payments continue beyond State Pension age, though new applications generally cannot be submitted after reaching that milestone. For the daily living component, the lower rate stands at £76.70 weekly , while the enhanced rate is £114.60 weekly .

For the mobility component, the lower rate stands at £30.30 per week, while the enhanced rate is £80. This means you could receive £194.60 per week in total should you qualify for the higher rate across both components in England and Wales.

That amounts to £10,119.20 per year if you were awarded the enhanced rate for both at 2026/27 rates. It is worth noting, however, that overall rates are reviewed and frequently increased annually, so the figure could be higher over 12 months if you were granted both enhanced rates now.

Northern Ireland also operates PIP, and although it is a devolved matter, the rules largely mirror those elsewhere in the UK.

In Scotland, PIP has been replaced by the Adult Disability Payment (ADP), though the eligibility criteria remain broadly similar.

PIP can be awarded on the basis of a wide variety of health conditions, hypertension among them.

The most recent quarterly figures to July 2026 recorded 1,684 cases in which hypertension was cited as the primary disabling condition for claimants receiving PIP entitlement covering both the daily living component and the mobility component at the enhanced rate.

It is crucial to bear in mind, however, that a diagnosis alone does not automatically guarantee support. A successful claim will hinge on demonstrating that the condition's impact on your daily life and mobility meets the eligibility threshold.

This evaluation is carried out by the DWP, guided by independent medical expertise. You should therefore not be deterred from submitting a claim simply because you haven't previously heard of someone receiving support from the department.
