Republicans join Democrats to condemn Trump's oil deal with Putin and plan to livestream execution of Fort Hood gunman

A number of prominent Republicans joined Democrats on Friday to condemn Donald Trump for lifting sanctions on Russian diesel and the plan to livestream the execution of the former army major who carried out the Fort Hood attack in 2009.

“Trump started a stupid & illegal war in Iran that has driven up diesel prices. Now he wants to bankroll Putin’s illegal war against Ukraine by buying diesel from him,” senator Chris Van Hollen, a Maryland Democrat, wrote in response to the news of Trump’s deal with Putin to lift sanctions and import Russian diesel. “One strategic disaster after the next.”

“70% of Americans support Ukraine,” congressman Don Bacon, a Nebraska Republican, chimed in. “Propping up Putin’s war economy is morally wrong as he uses that money to bomb cities. The President’s policies towards Russia and Ukraine are failures. We need moral clarity not moral ambiguity.”

“This announcement is a disgrace,” senator Elizabeth Warren, a Massachusetts Democrat, wrote. “You know how Trump could bring down gas and diesel prices? By ending his illegal war with Iran. Instead, he’s going to help fund Putin’s war machine.”

Warren also joined her Democratic colleagues, Jeanne Shaheen, of New Hampshire, andChuck Schumer, of New York, in a statement denouncing Trump’s deal with Putin.

“President Trump’s announcement that the United States will allow global purchases of Russian diesel for six months, is a total betrayal of Ukraine, our European allies and American national security,” the Democratic senators wrote. “After launching a war with Iran that has spiked gas and diesel prices for every American, the President is resorting to desperate ‘deals’ with a murderous dictator to attempt to clean up his own mess.

“Through the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, we gave the president significant authorities and leverage against China and Russia to bring Putin’s war to an end with a negotiated settlement,” congressman Michael McCaul, a Texas Republican posted on social media. “Unfortunately, while I understand the desire to bring down diesel prices, I am concerned the lifting of sanctions on Russian oil will only fund the Kremlin’s war machine—emboldening more violence and destruction, as we have seen in recent days.”

“Putin throws lifeline to try to save Trump in the midterms,” senator Sheldon Whitehouse, a Rhode Island Democrat, wrote. “In return, American dollars go to Russia and get put toward killing innocent Ukrainians. Trump/Russia right out in the open.”

Congressman Don Beyer, a Virginia Democrat, agreed, writing: “Infuriating—but this is what we warned anyone who would listen: Trump isn’t the slightest bit trustworthy when it comes to Russia. Giving him expanded tariff powers in hopes he would use them to help Ukraine was a mistake. Now he’s \*lifting\* sanctions on Russian fossil fuels.”

“The Fort Hood shooting was a horrific act of violence that took 13 innocent lives and devastated military families,” senator Mark Warner, a Virginia Democrat, wrote. But there is a fundamental difference between carrying out a lawful sentence and turning an execution into a public spectacle.”

“Public executions are the hallmark of authoritarian regimes like Iran and North Korea, not the United States of America,” he added. “There is a reason that almost no countries in the world engage in this barbaric practice. Beyond the profound moral concerns, a public execution could hand terrorist organizations a propaganda victory, fuel radicalization, and put American service members, diplomats, and citizens overseas at greater risk.”

“Secretary Hegseth should seriously reconsider this decision,” congressman Tom Barrett, a Michigan Republican fighting to hold his seat in a tight race with progressive Democrat Will Lawrence, posted. “Nidal Hasan is an evil person ... That does not change the fact that our American values should strive to elevate human dignity. Making a public spectacle out of an already tragic situation is unnecessary and does not serve a public interest.”

“There is no need to livestream any execution, including this one,” senator Lisa Murkowski, an Alaska Republican posted. “I would expect this from terrorists and dictators, but not here in America. It would give this individual, who deserves no further notoriety, the martyrdom he seeks. How we carry out justice matters just as much as the justice itself, so I urge the Pentagon to reverse this gruesome decision.

“I support bringing this terrorist to justice,” congressman Michael Baumgartner, a Washington Republican, wrote of the execution of the Fort Hood attacker. “I do not support the public livestream of the execution. This should be reconsidered.”

“Who still carries out public executions: The Iranian regime The North Korean regime The Taliban in Afghanistan Al-Shabaab in Somalia,” Nikki Haley, who unsuccessfully challenged Trump for the 2024 Republican nomination before supporting him, posted on social media. “That is not the company we want to keep or the country we want to be.”

James Talarico returns to campaign trail after illness

Texas Democrat James Talarico campaigned in person on Friday at the Austin City Limits musical festival, after missing more than a week due to illness.

Talarico’s campaign shared video of the candidate shaking hands and posing for photos with supporters at the festival, undercutting Republican conspiracy theories that the former state lawmaker, who is leading in multiple polls, was incapacitated.

At Republican rally in New York, Trump attacks Letitia James in crude terms, calling her 'a dog'

Ostensibly speaking in support of Republican candidates in New York state on Friday night, Donald Trump instead veered into airing his own grievances, viciously attacking Letitia James, the state’s attorney general who prosecuted him for business fraud.

Trump claimed that James “is being looked at for prosecutorial misconduct with respect to how she handled the case of your favorite president – me.”

He then invented a story about James, who successfully prosecuted him for fraud, falsely claiming that when prosecutors told her they could find no evidence of wrongdoing by Trump, she said “we don’t care – we’re going to get him anyway.”

Moments later he urged New York voters to reject both the state’s governor, Kathy Hochul, and James, in crude terms.

“New Yorkers should tell both Hochul and that disgusting, Letitia dog – she’s a dog - ‘You’re fired. Get the hell out of here. You’re fired.’ They’re corrupt, incompetent. The city and state are dirty, filthy places,” Trump said.

Donald Trump is now speaking at a rally in Syracuse, New York in support of Bruce Blakeman, the Republican nominee for governor who faces the Democratic incumbent Kathy Hochul in the November election.

He began by boasting about his gambit of renaming Lake Ontario “Lake America”, and then boasted about “the beautiful White House that I reskinned and made beautiful again.”

Trump suggests final decision has not been made on whether execution of Fort Hood gunman will be streamed live

On his way to a rally for Republicans in upstate New York on Friday, Donald Trump was asked about the controversy over his defense secretary, Pete Hegseth, saying that the execution of the gunman who killed 13 people at Fort Hood in Texas in 2009 would be livestreamed.

Hours after his vice-president, JD Vance, said that he did not know if the killing of Nidal Hasan, the former army psychiatrist, would be streamed live, Trump was asked by a reporter: “Do you want the execution of the Fort Hood shooter to be livestreamed? And are you worried that the video would be used as propaganda?”

“Well, they’re making a determination about that,” Trump said. “But he will be, uh, killed. He will be, through firing squad. He killed 13, 14 people,” the president said.

“He’s totally nuts, so I really don’t care how he goes, as long as he goes,” Trump continued. “And I think that the death penalty is a very worthy way of going for him.”

The president then ignored a follow-up question about whether he would watch the execution.

Several Republican lawmakers joined Democrats and human rights advocates in condemning Hegseth’s idea that the execution should be streamed live.

Minutes later, Trump leaned in to listen to a follow-up question from a Brazilian correspondent, Raquel Krähenbühl, who asked, in slightly imperfect English: “Did you personally authorize or approve the execution, the livestream execution of the Fort Hood-”

“Did I approve?” Trump cut in to ask.

“Did you approve?” the reporter repeated.

“Absolutely,” Trump said, although it was not entirely clear if he either heard or was responding to the part of the question about the execution being streamed live.

As Trump walked away, Krähenbühl sought to clarify, shouting “the live-streaming?” but the president did not reply audibly.

The correspondent for Brazil’s TV Globo later shared video of her exchange with Trump on social media, in which he did appear to nod in response to her follow-up question about the live stream.

“Zelenskyy said you are weak because of it,” the reporter told Trump. “He just criticized you.”

“Who said that?” Trump leaned in and asked.

“Zelenskyy, the Ukrainian president, just said you are weak because of the deal you made with Putin,” the journalist replied.

Trump then stepped away and called on another journalist instead of responding.

Zelenskyy denounces Trump's decision to lift diesel sanctions on Russia as 'weak'

Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, denounced Donald Trump’s decision to suspend sanctions on Russia, to let the US buy diesel fuel from the nation that kills Ukrainian civilians daily, in an interview with Axios on Friday.

Zelenskyy, who had traveled to Washington to plead with US lawmakers to pass tougher sanctions on Russia last month, reportedly told Axios on Friday that Trump’s decision, was “not fair and not honest” and “looks like a happy birthday present for Putin”.

“It looks absolutely terrible,” he added.

Ukraine’s president also criticized Trump’s decision in a statement on social media, in which he wrote:

Peace never comes through weakness. Today, representatives of the U.S. Congress were in Kyiv – a bipartisan delegation – and I am grateful to them for visiting Ukraine at such a difficult time. They asked: what is needed to end the war? The answer must be strong. There must be sanctions under the Lindsey Graham bill, there must be support for Ukraine in protecting lives, and there must be pressure on Russia for its war. And today, there was a conversation between the leaders of America and Russia. A conversation that leads to something different. …

Any easing of sanctions against Russia without a clear and lasting de-escalation agreement with Russia is an obvious weakness. It plays into Russia’s hands – allowing it to kill more, wage war for longer, have even less respect for America, and inflict even greater losses and damage on the world. …

Gifts to Putin will not bring peace or any benefit to the civilized world. Russia will “repay” the diesel with further terror and perfidy. Allowing Russia to sell petroleum products is an investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged. What is needed is real de-escalation with Russia on a reciprocal basis. We count on America’s fair support for our defense of life, for our defense of people in Ukraine – and on the United States having a correspondingly strong conversation with Russia. A strong one, not a weak one.

Donald Trump’s surprise announcement on Friday, that the US will buy diesel from Russia, and is lifting sanctions for six months to enable Russia to export diesel freely, came as two Republican senators, Kevin Cramer of North Dakota and Roger Wicker of Mississippi, were in Kyiv meeting with Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, to discuss implementing the tough new Russia sanctions law signed by Trump just three weeks ago.

Trump says he struck a deal with Putin for Russia to sell diesel to US, weeks after signing Russia sanctions law

Just weeks after signing a new law that gave him the authority to impose tariffs on countries that buy fuel from Russia despite US sanctions, Donald Trump announced on Friday that the US will now be buying fuel from Russia.

Writing on social media, Trump said that he stuck a deal with Russia’s president, Vladimir Putin, “wherein it was agreed that Russia will immediately supply over 300,000 Tons of Diesel Fuel to the American and Global Marketplace,” to be followed by another 4.5m tons of diesel fuel exports.

A short time later, the US treasury’s office of foreign assets control announced that, at Trump’s direction, it had lifted sanctions on the sale of Russian diesel to the global market, “including importation into the United States for six months.

Trump claimed that the deal, which suspends an effort to punish Russia for its full-scale invasion of Ukraine, would soon drive down the cost of fuel in the US, which has been a concern to voters casting ballots in the midterm elections.

The Lindsey Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act, signed into law on 18 September, authorized the president to impose tariffs on countries “facilitating Russian oil sanctions evasion”, but also gave him broad authority to waive any sanctions or tariffs. Those provisions had led some Democrats to oppose the legislation, arguing that it delegated too much authority to Trump.
