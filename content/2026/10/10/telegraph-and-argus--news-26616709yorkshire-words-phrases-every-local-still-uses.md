Yorkshire folk have long been known for their distinctive way of speaking. From the mills of Bradford and Halifax to the dales and market towns of the county, many traditional words and phrases have survived for generations.

While accents vary across God's Own County, these expressions remain a treasured part of Yorkshire identity, adding warmth, humour and character to everyday conversations.

Here are 20 classic Yorkshire sayings and what they mean.

### 1. Dinnertime and Teatime

**Translation:** Lunch and Dinner  
**Meaning:** In Yorkshire, you have your dinner at mid-day. Teatime is your evening meal.

### 2. Nah Then

**Translation:** Now Then  
**Meaning:** A common greeting from Yorkshire folk.

### 3. Ey Up

**Translation:** Hey Up  
**Meaning:** A greeting, often when meeting someone unexpectedly. Often used by Southerners as an example of how people from Yorkshire speak - 'Ey up lad!'

### 4. Be Reyt

**Translation:** Be okay  
**Meaning:** Everything will work out okay in the end.

### 5. Ow Much?

**Translation:** How much?  
**Meaning:** An expression of amazement at the price of something (usually when visiting a supermarket for the first time in awhile, or filling the car with petrol).

### 6. Owt

**Translation:** Anything  
**Meaning:** Refers to anything at all.

### 7. Nowt

**Translation:** Nothing  
**Meaning:** Nothing - such as 'It cost me nowt'.

### 8. Faffin'

**Translation:** Messing about  
**Meaning:** Wasting time on a task. usually an admonishment - 'Stop faffin' about'.

### 9. Scran

**Translation:** Food  
**Meaning:** Any food or meal.

### 10. Ta

**Translation:** Thanks  
**Meaning:** Expression of gratitude.

### 11. Summat

**Translation:** Something  
**Meaning:** An unspecified item.

### 12. Chuffed

**Translation:** Delighted  
**Meaning:** Very pleased or proud.

### 13. By 'Eck

**Translation:** Heck  
**Meaning:** Expression of surprise or amazement. Another favourite Yorkshire expression for southerners.

### 14. Ginnel

**Translation:** Alleyway or passage  
**Meaning:** A narrow path between buildings. Also known as a 'snicket' in some parts of Yorkshire.

### 15. Ee By Gum

**Translation:** Oh my goodness  
**Meaning:** Expression of surprise.

### 16. Gander

**Translation:** Look  
**Meaning:** To have a look at something.

### 17. Sound

**Translation:** Good, fine  
**Meaning:** Something that is perfectly acceptable.

### 18. Ta'ra

**Translation:** Goodbye  
**Meaning:** To say a friendly farewell.

### 19. Bap

**Translation:** Bread roll  
**Meaning:** A soft bread roll. Also known as sarnie or butty.

### 20. Mardy

**Translation:** Moody or sulky  
**Meaning:** To be grumpy or in a foul temper.

### 21. Gormless

**Translation:** Thick.  
**Meaning:** Looking confused or vacant.

### **22. Daft apethTranslation**: Stupid. See also, gormless. Normally a term of endearment rather than an insult.

### 23. Mithering

**Translation:** Nagging.  
**Meaning:** Also known as 'wittering'. In a state of some agitation.

## More Than Just Words

Yorkshire dialect is far more than a collection of amusing sayings. Many of its words have roots stretching back hundreds of years to Old English and Old Norse, reflecting the county's rich history and Viking heritage. Terms such as "bairn", "owt" and "nowt" have survived long after disappearing from much of the country.

Although modern life has changed the way people speak, these expressions remain woven into everyday conversations across Yorkshire. Whether you're greeting a friend with "Nah then", complaining "Ow much?" at rising prices, or reminding someone to "Put wood in t'hole", the dialect continues to be a source of pride.

As any Yorkshireman or Yorkshirewoman will tell you, it's nowt to be embarrassed about.

In fact, it's summat worth celebrating.
