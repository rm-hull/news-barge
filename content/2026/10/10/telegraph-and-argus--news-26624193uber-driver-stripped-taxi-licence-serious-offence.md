An Uber driver is one of 25 cabbies to be stripped of their licence in the last three years for an “incredibly serious” offence.

A Freedom of Information request to Bradford Council has revealed how many drivers have had their taxi licenses revoked after being convicted of driving while using a mobile phone.

Since 2024, 25 drivers have lost their licence for this reason.

## Uber driver on phone while driving

One driver who had his licence revoked for using a phone appeared at Bradford Magistrates Court this week to appeal the decision.

Zulfiqar Ali received six penalty points on his licence when a police patrol saw his taxi weaving in its lane on Rooley Avenue in February.

When they pulled up next to him, they saw he was holding his mobile phone against his steering wheel, and appeared to be using it.

He was given six penalty points, leading to his licence being revoked.

## 'Incredibly serious' offence

During the appeal, which was ultimately dismissed, magistrates heard about Bradford Council’s policy on taxi drivers who are caught using mobile phones.

The head of Taxi Licensing told the court that mobile phone offences are considered “incredibly serious” by the council.

The current policy is that any driver with a taxi licence issued by Bradford Council who is convicted of a mobile phone offence will have their licence revoked, and banned from re-applying for a licence for several years.

The Telegraph & Argus asked Bradford Council for a breakdown of how many drivers had been stripped of taxi licenses for mobile phone offences since 2019.

## 25 cabbies have licence stripped

The response said its current system meant the council was unable to provide data going back that far – but gave figures from 2024.

In 2024, eight private hire drivers had their licence revoked.

In 2025 another eight private hire drivers and one Hackney Carriage driver were stripped of their licence.

And so far this year, a further eight private hire drivers have lost their licence.

## Offence 3 months after he gained taxi licence

Mr Ali, 48, of Hopefield Way, off Rooley Lane, had his licence revoked in March, after being spotted by police on his mobile phone in February.

He appealed the decision, meaning he could keep his licence until the appeal was completed.

The appeal was heard at Bradford Magistrates Court on Wednesday.

Nicola Hoskins, representing Bradford Council, said Mr Ali had been granted his taxi licence by Bradford Council in November 2025.

The offence happened just three months later.

## Dropped phone once officer next to him

Detailing what led to the driver being pulled over, the court was told that an officer on mobile patrol had seen Mr Ali’s car “weaving in its lane.”

The officer pulled next to the vehicle while it was at the traffic lights near Asda, and looking into the Uber.

Miss Hoskins said: “The officer could clearly see the driver holding his mobile phone in the middle of the steering wheel.

“The screen was fully illuminated with an app on display.

"When he realised a police officer had pulled next to him, he dropped the mobile onto the passenger seat.”

He was given six points, and reported the offence to Bradford Council himself.

A decision was taken to revoke his licence on March 12.

Steven Knighton, regulatory and compliance manager at Bradford Council, told magistrates that the policy of revoking the licence of drivers found to have committed mobile phone offences was introduced in 2018.

The policy is intended to “ensure the safety of the travelling public.”

Mr Knighton said Mr Ali was interviewed, when he told council officers that the phone had fallen off a phone caddy on his windscreen.

He picked it up and was in the process of putting it back when the officer spotted him.

Miss Hoskins said: “Is a mobile phone matter considered minor or major?”

Mr Knighton replied: “Mobile phone offences are considered incredibly serious. The council sees it as an offence of choice, and it is very serious.

“The policy precludes someone from holding a licence for five years if they have committed this type of offence.”

## Training should have been 'fresh in his mind'

Pointing out that Mr Ali had only been granted his licence three months prior to the incident, he said: “He would have only recently completed training on the impact driving offences can have.

"It should have been fresh in his mind.”

Mr Iqbal, representing Mr Ali, suggested the council had been too rigid in applying the policy.

He said their had been other instances where the authority had deviated from its policy and allowed drivers to keep their licence.

Mr Ali, speaking through an Urdu interpreter, told the court he had been a taxi driver for 14 years – having held a licence in Birmingham before moving to Bradford.

There had been no incidents of concern before the mobile phone incident.

He denied that he was using his phone, repeating the claim that his phone had fallen from its caddy and he was putting it back on in place.

After an hour’s deliberation, magistrates dismissed the appeal, saying there was “no compelling reason” to allow him to keep his licence.

He will have to pay the council £640 in costs.
