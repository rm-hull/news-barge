**York branded 'muck hole' over litter and weeds**

I HAD the misfortune to have to go into town last week.

I 'debussed' at the concrete wasteland in front of the train station (not impressed) and walked through to Micklegate via Rougier Street.

The state of Rougier Street was unbelievable.

Litter and fag ends everywhere. It had not been swept for months.

Seeing this mess made me take greater notice of the other streets I had the misfortune to need to traverse. Muck and filth were everywhere. The street cleaning gangs that used to clean the streets have obviously gone. Weeds grow prolifically everywhere, and they help to harbour the litter.

Seeing this mess made me realise why I go into town as infrequently as possible preferring to obtain my needs in surrounding towns (or Amazon).

People come to see York. I always tell them it is a 'muck hole', don't bother. They will be very disappointed.

Just wondered if York council is ever going to clean the area for which it is responsible – anytime soon?

*D A Brown,*

*Brockfield Road,*

*Huntington,*

*York*

**Thanks for supporting our Palestinian fundraiser**

I AM writing to thank everyone supporting our recent Social Fundraiser for Medical Aid for Palestinians (MAP), which has already raised over £1,800.

The Methodist Church in Haxby made us very welcome and a team of home cooks created a wonderful spread.

We are very grateful to our local artists who donated their works to be auctioned.

Our guest speaker Zahra Yassine spoke with passion about the life saving work of MAP including projects with young people in Ramallah in occupied Palestine, and in Gaza delivering food and immunisation programmes for children at risk.

Donations to the appeal are still very welcome: www.justgiving.com/page/sue-cooke-1

*Sue Cooke,*

*Smithie Close,*

**A welcome home**

ALTHOUGH living in York, much of my work was in the midlands, southern England and Wales.

Andrew Morrison’s article reminded me how I would say out loud “goodbye beautiful river Ouse” as I sped over the A64 bridge, “see you again very soon”.

On my return I would say “Hello river Ouse, I’m glad to see you again”

Also, I always hope for clear weather just after turning from the A1 onto the A64, looking out for a glimpse of York Minster on the horizon. Home.

*Christopher Rainger,*

*Grange Street, York*
