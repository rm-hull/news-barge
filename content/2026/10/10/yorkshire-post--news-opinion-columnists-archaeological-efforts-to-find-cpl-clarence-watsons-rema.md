# Archaeological efforts to find Cpl Clarence Watson’s remains show the importance of remembrance

## The mission to find the last missing serviceman, 82 years after a US aircraft crashed into a hill above Holmfirth during the Second World War, is a reminder of the importance of remembrance.

The crash killed all but one of the 10 men on board. The body of Cpl Clarence Watson has never been found. The archaeological dig to find evidence of Cpl Watson’s remains, with his family’s blessing, is to be welcomed.

Tragedies such as this show that the sacrifices of war were not just restricted to the frontline. There are reminders all across the region of brave soldiers who gave up their lives in order to protect the liberties that so many take for granted these days.

As Tim Fletcher, the lead archaeologist on the project, says, “They were just regular guys. They came over here all the way from the States, doing their bit. To me, it’s so important not to forget the sacrifices that they made.”

Cpl Watson’s niece, Vicky Weatherby, was able to lay a memorial plaque bearing his name after visiting the site. It is good to see that a war grave will still be honoured on the site. A visible reminder of the tragedy can help future generations learn about the sacrifices that were made during the war.

While America understandably wants to repatriate Cpl Watson’s remains, if possible, this corner of Yorkshire will continue to remember the servicemen who lost their lives on that fateful day on October 9, 1944.

There are still many stories that need to be pieced together of soldiers who fell during the Second World War. And it is incumbent on future generations to continue the effort to do so.
