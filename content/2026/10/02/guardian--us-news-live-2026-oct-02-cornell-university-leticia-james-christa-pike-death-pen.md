Top Democrats slam 'Trump's failing economic agenda' following jobs report that experts call 'mediocre'

Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Senate banking committee, has blasted Donald Trump’s “failing economic agenda” following the latest jobs report – which many experts have described as “mediocre” and “weak”.

“Since January 2025, Trump’s economy has only added 33,000 jobs a month on average, far below the 120,000-a-month average in 2024,” Warren said. “Since the start of Trump’s war, real wages have fallen 0.7% as his failed agenda keeps pushing prices up.”

Ahead of the November midterms, Democrats are hammering home that many of the Trump administration’s policies have resulted in a cost of living crisis, and urging voters to eschew Republicans vying for seats in Congress at the ballot box.

Meanwhile, Chuck Schumer, the Senate minority leader, noted that “American families are being squeezed on all sides by Trump’s disastrous economy”, in response to Friday’s figures.

The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee (DCCC) – which serves as the official campaign arm for House Democrats – moved eight districts on their map of targets this midterm cycle to “in play”.

Their list now includes, Virginia’s sixth, Washington state’s fifth, North Carolina’s 13th, Kansas’ second, Texas’ ninth, Oklahoma’s fifth, New Jersey’s second and Michigan’s first. This means the DCCC has identified 66 critical districts as competitive, in order for Democrats to reclaim control of the lower chamber.

While some forecasters expect the party to win the House, many of the competitions are neck-and-neck. The last time Democrats saw a so-called “blue wave” was in 2018 – when they flipped 41 seats.

Top Republican Super Pac pauses ad spend in North Carolina, focuses on Kansas – reports

The top Republican aligned Super Pac, the Senate Leadership Fund (SLF), will pause it’s multimillion dollar ad spend in North Carolina in support of Michael Whatley, the GOP nominee for the open US Senate seat, according to multiple reports.

Democrats are hoping to flip the Tar Heel state in the upcoming midterm elections, where they need to net four seats in order to reclaim control of the Senate. Roy Cooper, North Carolina’s former governor, is the Democratic nominee and is leading Whatley in every poll, in some cases by double digits.

The SLF initially announced a $71m investment in the North Carolina race back in April – noting that Whatley – the former chair of the Republican National Committee with backing from Donald Trump – was a “battle-tested leader”. Semafor first reported the news that SLF shifting its spending priority, and is now reserving a seven-figure sum for the Senate race in Kansas – which has become increasingly competitive as Trump’s approval rating hits a record low nationwide.

While a Democrat hasn’t won a Senate seat in the Sunflower state in almost a century, the race between Republican incumbent Roger Marshall and his Democratic challenger, pastor Adam Hamilton, has become more of a concern for the GOP. The Cook Political Report, a prominent election forecaster, downgraded the competition from “leans Republican” to “likely Republican” at the end of September.

Kennedy Center Honors to be held off site for the first time

This year’s Kennedy Center Honors will be held at Washington’s Capital One Arena, not the arts institution that’s been at the center of a legal battle over adding Donald Trump’s name to the building, the Associated Press reports.

The 49th annual awards are set to take place at Washington’s downtown arena, a Kennedy Center spokesperson said. Few other details were immediately available, and an explanation for the change wasn’t given. The event will take place on 7 December.

The center’s Trump-picked board voted last month to close the performing arts venue after a federal judge blocked the institution from returning Trump’s name to the facade.

Trump has threatened in social media posts that safety repairs the center needs would only happen if the board was allowed to move forward with plans to add his name to the building.

A federal judge has twice said that can’t happen without the approval of Congress, which has allocated $257m for repairs.

The president hosted the 2025 awards , whose honorees included Sylvester Stallone and George Strait. He said at the time that he would rename the facility the “Trump Kennedy Center”, and not long after workers began adding his name to the building.

The board of trustees voted unanimously for the change, which has been challenged in court since.

Congress named the performing arts center as a living memorial to John F Kennedy in 1964, the year after he was assassinated.

The law explicitly prohibits the board of trustees from making the center into a memorial to anyone else, and from putting another person’s name on the building’s exterior.

Hegseth announces 'one-day stand-down' for service members to vote in midterm elections

Pete Hegseth, the US defense secretary, announced today that there will be a “one-day stand-down” this month, for service members to vote in the upcoming midterm elections. He added that many members of the military “face unnecessary, difficult ways of obtaining, completing, and returning absentee ballots while serving far from home” and said the directive would take place this year and every election year to come.

“We’re not telling you who to vote for, but we will give you every opportunity to do so,” Hegseth said in a straight-to-camera video. “Take that day to fill out your absentee ballot. Help your family members do the same, or go to the polling place yourself and exercise your right to vote.”

Trump claims Europe has agreed to release diesel oil reserves as he weighs export ban

On Truth Social, Donald Trump has claimed that Europe has “just agreed to release a massive amount of their heavily stocked Diesel Oil”. This comes after the administrationurged European allies to release their diesel reserves as fuel prices continue to rise due to the ongoing Iran war and the president continues to weigh an export ban. The president added that “the process will begin immediately”.

In the US the average price for a gallon of diesel sits at $6.3, up from $3.7 a year ago – a 70% hike.

Trump said on Wednesday that he was still “thinking about” a ban on diesel exports to curb the soaring cost of energy domestically. However, he noted that an embargo “might raise the price of other things.” Europe has warned of a further economic tailspin in the event of a ban.

A reminder that major sectors of US agriculture, manufacturing and commercial fishing depend heavily on diesel.

Europe has also becoming increasingly reliant on US diesel exports after Ukrainian strikes destroyed Russian oil refineries and Moscow implemented its own export ban earlier this year to preserve its supply.
