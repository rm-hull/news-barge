A Bishop Auckland attraction has been shortlisted for two top regional tourism awards for its visitor experience and role in regenerating the town.

Auckland Palace and Gardens is a finalist in the Large Visitor Attraction of the Year and Regenerative Tourism categories at the 2026 North East England Tourism Awards.

Vic Cunningham, head of visitor experience at The Auckland Project, said: "We’re incredibly proud to see Auckland Palace and Gardens shortlisted in two categories at the North East England Tourism Awards, recognising the difference we make for visitors and for Bishop Auckland.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20160268.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Auckland Project Walled Garden *(Image: Supplied)*

"Being recognised for both our visitor experience and regenerative tourism is particularly special.

"Everything we do is about giving people a memorable reason to visit Bishop Auckland while ensuring tourism brings lasting benefits to the town and its community.

"This recognition also reflects the passion and hard work of our staff and volunteers, who make visitors feel welcome and help bring the remarkable stories of Auckland Palace and Bishop Auckland to life every day."

The awards recognise excellence, innovation and best practice across tourism in County Durham, Northumberland and Tyne and Wear.

The shortlisting recognises Auckland Palace and Gardens’ visitor experience and The Auckland Project’s wider work to revitalise Bishop Auckland through culture, heritage and tourism.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21187664.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An aerial view of Auckland Palace and Gardens *(Image: Supplied)*

Auckland Palace was the Prince Bishops of Durham’s main country residence for more than 800 years and now includes the 150-acre Deer Park, 10 acres of gardens, St Peter’s Chapel and restored Georgian Gothic state rooms.

The attraction also houses Francisco de Zurbarán’s paintings of Jacob and His Twelve Sons.

Designed by Pip Morrison, the gardens include the 17th-century Walled Garden, Faith Garden, Paradise Garden and Wilderness Garden.

Produce from the Walled Garden is served at The Auckland Project’s venues, including Bishop’s Kitchen at Auckland Palace, El Castillo Spanish tapas restaurant and the Park Head Hotel.

Surplus produce is donated to local charities and food banks.

The Auckland Project aims to make Bishop Auckland a major cultural destination by creating jobs and opportunities in education, skills and sustainable economic growth.

Its wider attractions include the Faith Museum, Spanish Gallery, Mining Art Gallery, Auckland Tower, Weardale Railway and Binchester Roman Fort.

The awards are run with VisitEngland, and eligible Gold winners progress to the national VisitEngland Awards for Excellence.
