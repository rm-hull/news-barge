The Port of Middlesbrough is backing the next generation of rugby players through a new sponsorship of Lions Park.

This sponsorship supports the Mini and Juniors Academy at Middlesbrough Rugby Union Football Club (MRUFC) and reflects both organisations' commitment to youth development and community investment across Teesside.

The partnership supports MRUFC’s long-standing efforts to provide rugby opportunities to people of all ages and abilities.

Gary Dawson, chairman of AV Dawson, which owns and operates the Port of Middlesbrough, said: "At the Port of Middlesbrough, we believe that creating a lasting legacy means investing in people and communities.

"Lions Park is much more than a sports facility; it is a place where young people can learn, grow, and develop valuable life skills.

"We are proud to support MRUFC and help ensure both sporting and career opportunities remain available for current and future generations across Teesside."

The club’s Mini and Juniors section welcomes hundreds of children each year, helping them build confidence, teamwork, and resilience both on and off the pitch.

Phil Tilson, chair of MRUFC, said: "The support of the Port of Middlesbrough is a significant boost for our club and the wider community we serve.

"Their commitment to young people and grassroots sport aligns closely with our values and ambitions.

"This partnership will help us continue providing high-quality facilities, coaching, and opportunities for children and families across Teesside while strengthening the future of our club.

"We are very grateful to the Port of Middlesbrough, and we would encourage other businesses and Teesside leaders to partner with us."

The sponsorship comes at a pivotal time for MRUFC, which recently expanded its facilities through the acquisition of the former Tollesby site, now renamed Lions Park.

The site was developed with the help of £140,000 in community funding and now serves as the dedicated home of the Mini and Juniors Academy.

The club is also seeing growth across its programmes, including the return of its Women’s team after a 20-year absence, increased senior participation, and expanded touch rugby sessions.

Gemma Richardson, volunteers coordinator and head of the Lions Park Task Force, said: "Lions Park is more than just a place to play rugby.

"It's a special place filled with memories, friendships, laughter, and community spirit.

"It's where our rugby family comes together, supports one another, and creates something we can all be proud to call our home."
