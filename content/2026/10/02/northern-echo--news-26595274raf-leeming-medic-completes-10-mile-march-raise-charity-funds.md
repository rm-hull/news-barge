An RAF medic based in North Yorkshire has completed a gruelling endurance challenge to raise money for the RAF’s longest-standing charity.

Lewis Casey-Brown, 19, took on the PARAS’10 challenge on September 26 to raise money for the RAF Benevolent Fund.

The challenge is a 10-mile march carrying a 35lb bergen at Catterick Garrison, North Yorkshire.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21521866.jpg?type=mds-article-620)North Yorkshire RAF medic Lewis Casey-Brown completing the PARAS’10 at Catterick Garrison, while raising money for the RAF Benevolent Fund *(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Casey-Brown said: "I decided to raise money for the Fund because it’s a great cause that supports serving personnel, veterans and their families in times of need.

"Although my family and I haven’t received personal support, I am proud to raise money for a great cause."

The RAF Benevolent Fund supports serving and former RAF personnel and their families with financial, emotional and practical help, welfare breaks, mental wellbeing resources and youth programmes.

Bradford-born Mr Casey-Brown, an AS1 RAF medic at RAF Leeming for two years, trained by steadily increasing the weight he carried on practice runs.
