Christa Pike is on a ventilator in the hospital after her failed execution in Tennessee, her attorneys said in a court filing Friday.

Hospital staff were working to save her life and clear two doses of pentobarbital, the drug the state uses to execute prisoners, from her body in the hours after the botched execution, according to her lawyers.

“She is critically ill, unconscious, intubated and receiving breathing assistance through a ventilator,” her attorneys wrote in a statement. “She is being treated for the serious effects of pentobarbital administered during the execution attempt, including significant injuries to both arms.”

In the court filing, her attorneys requested an order requiring the Tennessee Department of Correction to preserve all evidence related to the failed execution attempt. State officials have not offered any explanation about what went wrong during the execution attempt.

![Death row inmate Christa Pike survived two lethal injections on Wednesday night in a failed execution](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/01/16/43/Christa-Pike.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Pike’s attorneys say they warned the state and the courts months earlier about “serious complications” that could occur during her execution.

## Attorneys point to possible cause of failed execution

The stunning failure in Tennessee on Wednesday night has left both sides on the death penalty debate demanding answers. The state's governor has ordered an independent review and halted future executions.

In the court filing, Pike’s attorneys said they believe problems with IV lines caused the lethal pentobarbital to flow into her body rather than her bloodstream.

“Apparently at no point did any member of the execution team realize that the IV lines were not correctly placed or that the veins had blown and that the pentobarbital was, in whole or in part, entering Ms. Pike’s body,” they wrote.

Instead of “remedying this failure,” they prepared and administered a backup dose of pentobarbital, her lawyers said.

![Emergency vehicles at Riverbend Maximum Security Institution after Pike’s execution was halted Wednesday night](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/01/16/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The execution team worked for an hour to administer the lethal drugs, according to Pike's defense lawyer Randy Spivey who said he counted at least seven needles in her left arm.

Pike was still snoring loudly when the execution chamber’s microphone was cut off more than an hour into the attempts.

“We are working closely with those closest to Christa to respect her wishes, safeguard her privacy and protect her rights as a patient during this extraordinarily difficult time,” Luke Ihnen, her attorney with Federal Defender Services of Eastern Tennessee, said in a statement on Friday. “We ask that the public and media respect that privacy as well.”

Tennessee Gov. Bill Lee has ordered a review of the state’s execution procedures and indefinitely postponed Pike’s execution and another slated for this year.

Other Republican political leaders in Tennessee said the state should examine whether it should stop single-drug lethal injections and consider other methods to carry out death sentences.

## ‘Satanic’ murder

Pike, now 50, was sentenced to death for a murder she committed at age 18. Pike and her boyfriend were both convicted of stabbing and beating 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer, their classmate at a job training facility in Knoxville.

The case drew a lot of attention because a pentagram was carved on Slemmer’s body and other elements of the crime fueled fears of Satan worship during the “satanic panic” of the 1980s and 1990s.

Tadaryl Shipp, Pike’s boyfriend, admitted that he was the one who carved the pentagram, a star-shaped symbol associated with Satanism, into Slemmer’s body. Shipp was 17 at the time of the crime and received a sentence of life in prison with the possibility of parole. Pike was the only one sentenced to death.

Her planned execution had already sparked outrage before the failed attempt as critics pointed out that she was a victim of sexual abuse dating back to when she was a child.

Pike was scheduled to be put to death at 10 a.m. on Sept. 30 but the execution was halted last-minute by the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Sixth Circuit to give judges time to review whether her allegations of sexual abuse and rape as a child were fully considered before she was sentenced to death.

The Supreme Court overturned the appeals court’s stay, clearing the way for the execution later Wednesday night.
