Andy Burnham should publish all details of his meetings with Manchester City’s owners, the Abu Dhabi United Group (ADUG), and declare all hospitality he has accepted at the Etihad Stadium, the Liberal Democrats have said.

The prime minister was roundly criticised this week for saying he would be “really concerned” if City’s Abu Dhabi owners sold the club after the club was found to have breached the Premier League’s financial rules by an independent commission, crediting them with being “a huge partner in the building of modern Manchester”.

Downing Street was forced to issue two clarifications of his position on Thursday, with a spokesperson for the PM insisting the guilty verdict against Manchester City is “serious”.

Mr Burnham’s comments drew criticism on social media and shadow sports minister Louie French called for the government to “keep politics out of football”, with the case set to proceed to an appeal board and a sanctions hearing.

Now, the Liberal Democrats have drafted a strongly-worded letter to the prime minister, criticising his defence of City as “extraordinary”.

![Andy Burnham speaks to Manchester City Chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak prior to the Vincent Kompany Testimonial at the Etihad Stadium in 2019](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/02/15/15/PA-86048293.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the letter, seen by the *Guardian*, the party’s spokesperson for culture, media and sport, Anna Sabine, wrote: “As you will know, an independent commission has found Manchester City guilty of 114 charges of breaching financial rules over a timeframe of nearly a decade, including the use of ‘sham contracts’ and schemes to disguise more than £830m of funding.

“Despite this, your response was to thank the club’s owners for their work and describe the findings as ‘contested’ and that it was ‘wrong to jump to a conclusion’. It is extraordinary that a prime minister would use his position to call into question the findings of the independent investigation, and risk undermining trust in the Premier League’s processes.

“This has been a difficult year for football’s reputation. Fifa’s conduct around the World Cup has already raised serious questions about integrity in the game we love. At a moment like this, fans expect their prime minister to stand up for the rules in place, not to appear to side with a club found guilty of breaking them.”

Ms Sabine told the *Guardian* that the prime minister’s ties to ADUG should be looked at, saying she is planning to write to Downing Street and the Greater Manchester Combined Authority (GMCA) to demand details of their meetings.

“Football fans up and down the country deserve to know that the rules apply to everyone, however rich and powerful they are,” she said. “When the prime minister leaps to the defence of a club’s owners within hours of a verdict, people are entitled to ask what their relationship is.

“Andy Burnham must come clean and publish details of every meeting and conversation he has had with City Football Group, Abu Dhabi United Group or UAE government representatives, going back to his time as mayor – and that includes any hospitality freebies received.”

A Downing Street spokesman said on Thursday: “As the prime minister made clear, the initial judgement is serious and there can’t be any suggestion that anyone is above the rules.

![The then Manchester mayor joins Prince Harry at Manchester City's training ground](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/02/15/06/PA-85362677.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"He also stressed that it is an ongoing independent process. It is essential that it is allowed to run its course and the outcome respected.

"Wherever wrongdoing is established, those responsible should face the appropriate consequences.”

Former City and FA chairman David Bernstein said he was “concerned” about Mr Burnham’s comments and the issue was “something that politicians should be seen to be staying well away from”.

He told BBC Newsnight: “It is absolutely vital that this independent regulator can perform independently and without political interference and I think there are issues coming out of all this in terms of the board of directors, possibly the owners as well, where the independent regulator will need to get involved.”

City confirmed on Friday that they have lodged an appeal against the guilty verdict, having claimed the commission’s decision contained “clear material errors, of law, principle and fact, and is unsafe”.

A No 10 spokesperson said: “As the prime minister made clear, the initial judgement is serious and there can’t be any suggestion that anyone is above the rules. He also stressed that it is an ongoing independent process. It is essential that it is allowed to run its course and the outcome respected.

“Wherever wrongdoing is established, those responsible should face the appropriate consequences.”
