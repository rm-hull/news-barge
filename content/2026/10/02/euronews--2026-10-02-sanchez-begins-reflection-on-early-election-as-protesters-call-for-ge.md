## Spanish Prime Minister will decide this weekend whether to call early elections after his two housing decrees were rejected, as housing activists prepare fresh protests and consider a general strike as the next step.

Pedro Sánchez enters a period of reflection. The Prime Minister will use this weekend to decide whether to bring forward the general election after Congress on Friday threw out two housing decrees put forward by his Socialist government.

The parliamentary defeat leaves the government facing a new scenario and Sánchez having to decide on the future of the legislature. According to government sources cited by several national media outlets, the prime minister will, over the coming days, consider whether to call elections before the end of the year.

Hours before the votes, the government had played down that possibility and insisted that an early election was not on the table because "hoy no toca" (today is not the day). After the result unfolded, the executive said it would analyse the situation without ruling out any option.

The two decrees were rejected after more than four hours of debate. The first fell by 178 votes to 172, with the Popular Party, Vox, Junts, and UPN all voting against. The second was defeated by 184 votes to 166, after the PNV and Canary Islands Coalition joined the 'no' camp.

## Sánchez's political future hanging

While Sánchez's political future hangs in the balance, the reaction outside Congress has been immediate. Protesters who had been camped out for days in Puerta del Sol marched to Congress on Friday to follow a vote they regarded as crucial to their demands.

Once the rejection of the first decree was confirmed, some of the protesters headed back to Sol chanting "general strike". A general assembly was scheduled for the afternoon to decide the next steps in the movement and the future of the camp itself.

The Confederation of Tenants' Unions has also called demonstrations this Saturday in more than 50 Spanish cities and is already signalling an escalation in protest: "the next step is a general strike", the organisation said.

The spokesperson for the Tenants' Union, Alicia del Río, had already on Friday called for a general strike over housing and warned that the mobilisation would not end with the vote in Congress.

## A weekend with two unknowns

The fall of the decrees opens a weekend marked by two key questions. The first will be answered in the political arena: whether Sánchez chooses to continue the legislature or bring forward the general election.

The second lies on the streets. Protests will continue this Saturday in dozens of cities, and housing campaign groups will have to decide how far to step up their mobilisation and how to put into practice their call for a general strike.

Two very different responses to the same vote, which has put on hold both the government's next move and that of a housing protest which, for now, shows no sign of ending.
