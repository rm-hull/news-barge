## Zelensky says Putin has told his generals 'there are no rules now' in Ukraine war

Volodymyr Zelensky says Russian president Vladimir Putin has instructed his generals to abandon the rules of war in pursuit of more territory in Ukraine, according to the Financial Times.

The Ukrainian president made the remarks during a visit to a bomb shelter inside a school that had been hit by a Russian drone on Thursday.

Zelensky said Putin gave the order last month, saying: "He said there are no rules now." Russia has since stepped up its air strikes on civilian targets, particularly in Kyiv, hitting data centres and energy infrastructure as well as schools and hospitals.

## Ukraine 'has more to lose' in row with South Korea

Cho Han-bum, a senior fellow at the Korea Institute for National Unification in Seoul, said any escalation by South Korea in its row with Ukraine would most likely involve support it already provides the war-hit country, including humanitarian, financial and reconstruction assistance.

"Ukraine has more ⁠to lose than South Korea because it is receiving support from Seoul," ⁠Cho said.

South Korea has provided humanitarian and financial assistance to Ukraine since Russia's 2022 invasion and in July pledged an additional $100 million innon-lethal support on the sidelines of the Nato summit.

"However, neither side benefits from a prolonged confrontation," ​Cho said, noting that South Korea relies on Ukraine for information related to North Korea's military activities.

## Ukraine wants to heal 'misunderstanding' with South Korea

Ukraine is working to heal a spat with South Korea over Kyiv's publicly announced transfer of two North Korean servicemen taken prisoner in the war against Russia.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky revealed while he was addressing the UN General Assembly last week that the prisoners had been sent to South Korea, angering Seoul which expected the transfer to remain secret.

Ukrainian Foreign minister Andrii Sybiha told journalists late on Thursday: "I would not like to call this an incident. Let's say, it's a diplomatic misunderstanding," according to a report run by the news website nv.ua and other media.

"I really hope that this situation ​will ⁠be resolved in the very ‌near future and we are working on it. For us, South Korea is an important country. And I really ‌hope for momentum, continuation, and concrete results that ‌we are truly achieving with them."

Ukraine captured the two North Koreans in January 2025, the first to be captured alive since Pyongyang entered the war in 2024 assisting Russia.

North Korea ‌has signed a strategic partnership ⁠agreement with Russia, including a mutual defence pact, and ⁠dispatched an estimated 14,000 troops to help Moscow evict Ukrainian troops from its Kursk ‌region.

Zelensky this week said more than 8,000 North Korean troops remained in Russia and 10,000 were being trained for a future deployment.
