Plans to move forward the stalled York Outer Ring Road upgrade have been unveiled.

City of York Council’s proposed improvements along the A1237 will now be done in four initial phases:

* **Phase 1A** – A19 to Wigginton Road Roundabout, including Clifton Moor Roundabout
* **Phase 1B** – Wigginton Road Roundabout to Haxby Road Roundabout
* **Phase 1C** – Haxby Road Roundabout to Strensall Road Roundabout
* **Phase 1D** – Strensall Road Roundabout to Little Hopgrove, including Monks Cross Roundabout.

The A19 and Wigginton Road phase includes dualling the road a larger roundabout at Clifton Moor and improvements for pedestrians and cyclists including new paths, crossings and underpasses.

Cllr Claire Douglas, the council’s Labour leader, said the proposals set out a practical and deliverable way forward for the project which has been talked about for a long time.

But Cllr Chris Steward, the council’s Conservative group leader, said the latest version of the scheme had been radically scaled-back in the years since its approval.

It comes after the council backed moves to deliver the scheme in four phases in July last year after estimated costs jumped by almost £100 million.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/york-councillor-labour-leader-claire-douglas.webp)

The project aimed to dual the ring road from the A19 to the Little Hopgrove roundabouts. Estimates in 2024 originally put the cost at £66.4m.

The forecast price tag stood at £164m as of October last year, in part due to inflation since the plans were approved.

Funding is set to come from the council, the West Yorkshire Combined Authority and the Department of Transport.

The Government confirmed last year that £25 million it previously pledged was still earmarked for the scheme.

## Work to start early next year

A permanent acoustics barrier to reduce the noise from traffic in Rawcliffe is set to be installed early next year ahead of work on the roads.

Efforts are set to continue to get more funding from the Government and York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority to complete the full dualling scheme.

It includes increasing capacity at key roundabouts, new bridges over the York and Scarborough railway line and the River Foss and three further underpasses.

An update to the council’s executive last month showed £306,000 had been spent on the project in 2025/6 on re-procuring contracts with designers and land agents on a phasing strategy.

It added the roughly £2.1 million budget for the scheme until March 2027 was funding work on pre-commencement planning conditions, talks with landowners and the preparation of a full business case.

Cllr Douglas said the unveiling of the latest plan was an important milestone for the project.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/york-conservative-leader-chris-steward.webp)

She said: “Making these improvements to the ring road are essential in making the lives and journeys of residents and visitors easier, while supporting York’s businesses by reducing the time lost to congestion.

“This plan focuses first on enabling upcoming developments that will bring the new homes, including affordable homes, we all want to see built.

“It will also reduce traffic congestion in the city centre and enable us to lock in significant improvements to bus and active travel options.”

## ‘Radically scaled back’

Cllr Steward said the little had happened with the scheme since its approval other than funds from it being diverted to plug budget gaps elsewhere.

The council approved moving £5 million from it to the Station Gateway scheme in July 2025, with the losses expected to be recouped through financial contributions from developers.

Cllr Steward said: “The proof will be in the pudding.

“The improvements being talked about now are a radically scaled-back version of the dualling that was approved.

“It seems increasingly clear that all the administration is seeking to do is get one spade somewhere in the ground in the vague vicinity of the ring road ahead of May’s local election and then it can look proudly at what it claims was about to happen.”
