### TechRadar Verdict

Star Wars: Galactic Racer is the Star Wars racing game I've always wanted and has sparked a competitiveness I never knew I had. Fuse Games has designed an exhilarating, highly engaging roguelike racer with amazing buildcraft customization that offers endless possibilities on the track.

#### Reasons to buy

* +

  Exhilarating, heart-pounding action
* +

  A diverse range of racetracks authentic to the Star Wars universe
* +

  An engrossing roguelike structure with tons of replayability
* +

  A simple yet effective campaign that works best in the roguelike framework
* +

  Excellent buildcraft customization with seemingly endless options

Why you can trust TechRadar

Fuse Games' *Star Wars: Galactic Racer* isn't what I anticipated. A fun Star Wars racing game that lets you take part in a series of high-octane relays across the galaxy? Sounds great; you had me at "Star Wars." But a heart-pounding roguelike sweat-fest that had me squeezing my DualSense wireless controller in a death grip and yelling at my monitor as Kestar Bool once again beat me in the final race by the skin of my teeth? Not what I was expecting!

**Platform reviewed:** PS5**Available on:** PS5, Xbox Series X, Xbox Series S, PC**Release date:** October 6, 2026

*Galactic Racer* takes place after the fall of the Empire and during the New Republic Era, where the lawless and ex-pilots of the Outer Rim take part in the unsanctioned, high-stakes Galactic League. You play as Shade, a faceless human cargo pilot recruited to the league and who has a major grudge against the oppressive Bool family, particularly Kestar Bool, the guy who also happens to be the reigning champion of the Galactic League. Shade, for reasons you'll discover as you progress the campaign, has a long history with Kestar and is dead set on beating him, and you feel that rivalry grow after every attempt at completing the tour.

This particular Star Wars tale is contained without the bigger stakes we often see in the movies or TV shows, and while it's not a sweeping cinematic experience like *Star Wars Zero Company* or*Star Wars Jedi: Survivor*, with not much exploration in the way of character depth for Shade as the playable character, it's simple yet effective;* Galactic Racer* is first and foremost a racing game with an emphasis on replayability, buildcraft customization, and a tailored progression system.

## A race across the stars

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/xFFCR7EdDPK4j95eT2cWxd-1200-80.jpg)

The freedom to choose how to proceed through the campaign is a core feature I greatly enjoyed, and players must first complete the qualifiers, then the main Galactic Tour. There's no randomized track selection; instead, a clever system of branching paths lets you choose a route and compete in races that fit your preferences, minimizing the risk of losing your League Tokens, which let you race in the first place.

There are standard races that present a variety of gripping courses, and you can come in first or 12th place; Field Tests, which I did find a bit repetitive and not as challenging after completing them numerous times; Mystery Encounters; and Eliminator races, an event where the driver in last place is disqualified until only the top three pilots remain, plus you'll lose a League Token, which makes it the riskiest race in the bunch.

Roguelikes can be hit or miss for me, but *Galactic Racer*'s run-based structure offers an exhilarating and chaotic gameplay loop that excels at providing a highly engaging, heart-pounding experience despite the rinse-and-repeat system.

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/iSopiMB54s7okVsDBVivhd-1200-80.jpg)

Even after beating my fellow racers and making it all the way to the finals, only to fail miserably, the notion of having to replay the entire process after losing should've been off-putting. Instead, I found myself not only eager to improve, but excited to get back on the track with the knowledge and skills I'd gained from previous runs, a feeling that not many roguelikes can achieve.

The game has a learning and difficulty curve due to its roguelike framework, and it may not be for everyone, but it's purposefully designed to make you become the best racer in the galaxy, since you likely won't be a winner straight away.

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/vzbLAZqwsGmnFhDzqGBrA9-1200-80.jpg)

**Star Wars: Galactic Racer's roguelike structure is the game's best feature, offering an incredible amount of replayability across a host of brilliantly constructed courses across the galaxy.**

More often than not, you'll smash headfirst into an obstacle or fly off a cliff because you didn't drift in time, so it's incredibly satisfying how the game makes you feel like you're gradually improving after every run.

At the beginning of each tour, you choose a loadout, including your vehicle, Favours that provide a competitive edge, and a Racer Style, an additional preset that dictates your speeder's behaviour and active perks and feels more personalized to your playstyle.

*Galactic Racer* offers three vehicle types — landspeeders, speeder bikes, and skim speeders, as well as podracers during select missions — but in the end, I personally favored the speeder bike; its compact agility, vectored thrust, and kinetic burst felt incredible as I sped across dunes and whizzed around tight corners on slippery glaciers.

## If at first you don't succeed...

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/5sGUP3UpqKHriEJvFmUPUd-1200-80.jpg)

When I started, I absolutely sucked. I frequently collided headfirst into walls and mismanaged my abilities, but the roguelike structure works so well that my muscle memory began kicking in the more I played; by the end of the game, I was hardly crashing and knew the tracks like the back of my hand.

It's no smooth ride; every choice shapes your prospects, and the challenge of not only trying to win while overcoming planetary effects like lava or acid, and outplaying your fellow pilots, takes the stakes even higher — it's a testament to how well the roguelike framework works and how brilliantly designed the courses are.

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/36ShCbqrVmsf2gNSHvVpdD-1200-80.jpg)

The game puts a huge emphasis on customization — the build possibilities are endless — and the system stands out against the competition as you gradually update your vehicles by applying upgrades, installing parts (and making sure they're not damaged), and unlocking new power-up abilities; there's an abundance of them, and they make a world of difference in close-call encounters and improve your odds of a podium position.

Not to beat the dead tauntaun, but seriously… this is podracing! Playing on the base PS5, *Galactic Racer* is a visual and audio feast, offering a gorgeously smooth experience at 60fps, while sweeping you up in the frenetic action.

The speeds you can reach are ridiculous when you have the right upgrades, and there's nothing more gratifying than going to the maximum with your Ramjet and executing perfect landings and sharp corners without crashing your speeder into a million pieces.

## The best of the best

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/dWQERditAB6m3Rktd6kywd-1200-80.jpg)

Racing is at the soul of the campaign, and it's a damn good time, but the game also allows you to interact with other pilots; they aren't just random racers, but fully written NPCs with unique personalities and playstyles, and I loved how Fuse represents these characters on and off the track.

You'll build rivalries, learn their backstories, and discover random encounters with multiple dialogue options, which also come with rewards and consequences, making the lawless world you're competing in all the more real.

With Kestar Bool, for instance, I chose to insult him after he tried to swindle me out of some credits, and since he basically runs the show, my speeder was sabotaged; it exploded at the start of the next race, and I lost two speeder upgrades in the process, putting me at a huge disadvantage for the rest of the tour. This is such a unique feature and a significant factor in the game's progression system that drives home the competitiveness among your opponents.

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ZvHKYUTzeDvJXith2UstSd-1200-80.jpg)

Jakku and Crait are probably my favorite out of the six available planets, which are all stunningly authentic to the Star Wars universe and a joy to experience from a brand-new perspective as a fan; the scale of their set pieces, whether it be the underbelly of a Star Destroyer skeleton or kicking up red salt in crystalline caverns, creates a sense of awe alongside the game's competitive atmosphere as you maneuver through various routes.

Fuse has designed a riveting racing game with creatively constructed tracks that compel you to keep playing even after a major fumble. At first, it's all about mastering the track — which corner to take to put you ahead, when to speed up or slow down — and the game does a great job of making each one distinctly different. Soon enough, however, these races get pretty aggressive, to the point where they'll turn into outright battles as your opponents try to ram you off the course (screw you, Griff Halloran).

![Star Wars: Galactic Racer](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/PtUYcMfWXFhnFA5qGD62md-1200-80.jpg)

Alongside the campaign, players can unlock Sebulba's storyline, a side activity where you play podraces from his past; it's a neat addition to the main story that offers up some amazing tracks while also providing additional backstory on the guy who had it out for little Anakin Skywalker. Surprisingly, Sebulba became a standout in the storyline, and I appreciate that Fuse didn't simply throw him in for the sake of nostalgia.

> Playing on the base PS5, Galactic Racer is a visual and audio feast, offering a gorgeously smooth experience at 60fps, while sweeping you up in the frenetic action.

The game also offers other side activities like Scenarios that let you play as each racer, an Arcade feature, both worthwhile additions for after you complete the campaign, and a proper multiplayer mode. During the review period, I had access to several multiplayer sessions, a standard online PvP experience featuring quick race modes and Tours with 12 players and crossplay enabled.

From what I was able to play, I enjoyed the multiplayer as much as the campaign, and it has a lot of potential if Fuse plans on updating the game post-launch; it feels just as entertaining and competitive as the story, with extra unlockables and missions that incentivize you to keep playing after the campaign. While I didn't get to play too much due to time constraints, I already know I'll be sinking more hours into it, and I'm particularly excited about trying out the Podracing Tour mode at launch — it was locked during the review period — because I couldn't get enough of it in the campaign.

## Should you play Star Wars: Galactic Racer?

## Play it if...

**You're a Star Wars fan**  
If you enjoy Star Wars beyond the movies and TV shows, *Star Wars: Galactic Racer* will take you on an exhilarating journey across the Outer Rim.

**You like roguelikes**  
If you welcome the challenge of roguelikes and don't mind the rinse-and-repeat process, *Star Wars: Galactic Racer* is a must-play.

**If you enjoy racing games and want fast-paced action***Star Wars: Galactic Racer* is one of the best racing games I've played in a long time and offers a high-octane experience like no other.

## Don't play it if...

**You don't get on with roguelikes**  
If you're a racing game enthusiast, you'll enjoy this game, but the roguelike structure may put you off if you don't like the idea of repeating races until you beat the game.

**You're a Star Wars fan just looking to experience the story**  
You may be put off by the game's roguelike system if you continuously lose, which in turn can get in the way of the main story if that's all you want to experience.

## Accessibility Features

*Star Wars: Galactic Racer* offers a ton of accessibility features in the form of your typical settings across visuals, audio, and subtitles. There are also some great controller remapping options, which can be adjusted to your preferences (these make a huge difference on the track), toggleable controls, and more.

I spent almost 30 hours playing *Star Wars: Galactic Racer* on the base PlayStation 5 with my Gigabyte M32U gaming monitor and my Sony WH-CH520 wireless headphones for audio.
