# Is Nigel Farage too negative to win the next election? - Chris Burn

## "I haven't come here to be the prophet of doom," Nigel Farage insisted to a crowd of Reform supporters in Doncaster yesterday as he claimed he was merely being "utterly realistic about the way things are" and told them their city is "pretty broken".

But after interviewing Mr Farage and then listening to his subsequent speech at Doncaster Racecourse, I left the venue wondering whether the dystopian picture he painted of a country assailed by multiple crises and in an effective state of national emergency will simply prove too bleak for many voters when it comes to the crunch.

While his mid-afternoon speech was lapped up by supporters in a crowd which appeared to be largely made up of a combination of local councillors and pensioners, the hundreds of empty seats at the back of the venue provided something of a different perspective on the party's current popularity.

![Empty seats at the back of the Reform party event in Doncaster](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/business/0&crop=&width=640&quality=65.jpg)


Mr Farage, an accomplished public speaker and media performer following decades of perfecting both arts, set out a series of undoubted problems facing the country - sky-high energy bills for households and businesses, a failure by successive administrations to get a grip of illegal immigration and the spiralling cost of Government borrowing, which has reached its highest level since 1998.

Each of those points on their own are pretty undeniable and do need addressing. But Mr Farage combined them to paint a picture of a country so on its knees, that in his telling any incoming Reform government would have just '100 days to save Britain'.

Leaving aside the fact that the next election may be years away and is certainly more than 100 days away (which rather undermines that self-imposed deadline), I came away from wondering whether his portrait of an utterly broken Britain in a state of terminal decline really accords with the day-to-day reality of most people's lives.

According to the most recent Office for National Statistics study into personal wellbeing - admittedly from 2023 - most people score their life satisfaction, happiness and the feeling that things done in life are worthwhile as well above a seven out of ten.

Perhaps I live in a bubble but when I think of the community I live in and the lives of my friends and family, I'd say those results are a fair reflection for many; yes there are problems and challenges for everyone but actually this is a country where you can live a positive, purposeful and happy life.

Of course, any opposition leader will paint the present in a negative light. But even Donald Trump's dark pronouncements about the state of America under the Democrats while twice challenging for the presidency were accompanied by bombastic promises of how things could improve in the future - whether you believed them or not.

In Doncaster yesterday, to me it felt like that was a lot of doom and gloom but a lack of a positive vision for the future.

Even if you agree with Mr Farage's concerns about the direction of the UK, I think there are legitimate questions to be asked of whether he is currently too negative to win the next election.
