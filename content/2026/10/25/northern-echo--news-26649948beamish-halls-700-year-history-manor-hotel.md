From its origins as a medieval manor gifted to a Norman family after the conquest of England to its modern life as one of County Durham's best-known hotels, Beamish Hall has a story stretching back more than 700 years.

The four-star country house hotel sits in grounds near Beamish and attracts visitors from across the region.

Behind its elegant façade is a history dating back to the 13th century.

The hall began as a fortified farmhouse and manor after the land was gifted to Isabella de la Leigh and Guiscard de Charron.

Over the centuries, it passed through the hands of powerful North East families including the Percys, Wrays, Davisons, Edens and Shaftos.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21613998.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: BEAMISH HALL)*

Generations of owners shaped the grand house seen today, leaving architectural features from the Elizabethan, Georgian, Victorian and Edwardian eras.

Beamish Hall's role changed dramatically after the Second World War.

After centuries as a private family home, it served as the headquarters of the Durham Coal Board and later became associated with the fledgling Beamish Museum before eventually being transformed into a hotel.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21613990.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: BEAMISH HALL)*

The hall's current chapter began when the Craggs family bought the estate in the early 2000s.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21613995.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: BEAMISH HALL)*

They have since transformed the property from a venue with two bedrooms and one function suite into a hotel with 43 bedrooms and the Charron Apartment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21613989.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: BEAMISH HALL)*

Its wedding and events facilities have expanded, making Beamish Hall a popular venue for celebrations across the region.

The Stables Bar and Restaurant occupies a converted 18th-century stable block at the rear of the hall.

Stone floors, exposed beams, antique furnishings and log fires preserve its historic character, while seasonal menus feature locally sourced ingredients.

The Coach House Café serves scones, cakes, pastries, sandwiches and pies prepared by the hotel's pastry team.

Beamish Hall's location makes it a popular base for tourists.

The hotel is a mile from Beamish Museum and within easy reach of Durham and Newcastle, combining rural peace with access to major North East attractions.
