Overnight Ukrainian drone attacks killed three people in southwestern Russia’s Belgorod region and damaged an industrial site in the Perm region of the Ural Mountains, officials said on Sunday.

In the city of Belgorod near the Russian border with Ukraine, an attack killed two people and injured 13, said Belgorod regional governor Alexander Shuvayev.

“The attack has damaged 30 blocks of flats, 35 cars, four houses, three administrative buildings, eight commercial premises and three social facilities,” he said.

Another person was killed and one was injured in a village near the town of Shebekino, authorities said.

A drone attack on Russia's Perm region damaged an industrial facility, injuring five of its workers, regional governor Dmitry Makhonin said on Sunday.

Emergency services were at the scene and that the injured people received the necessary medical help, he said on the state-backed MAX messaging app without providing further detail on the nature of the damage.

Meanwhile, Russia’s Defense Ministry said that its drones had struck a cargo vessel bound for the Black Sea port of Chornomorsk during an overnight attack. The ministry said the vessel was being used to supply the Ukrainian army.

## Bulgaria votes for president in wake of deadly Black Sea drone strikes

Bulgarians ⁠vote in presidential elections on Sunday as Russia's war in Ukraine spreads to the waters off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast.

Polls place acting President Iliana Iotova, 61, and ex-caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov, 50, as leaders in a race of 27 candidates.

While the presidency is largely ⁠ceremonial, Sunday's vote has taken on added ​significance ⁠after a series of drone attacks on merchant ships off Bulgaria's Black Sea coast that likely killed some two-dozen international seafarers.

The strikes, which sparked international condemnation and concerns about the growing reach of the war in Ukraine, prompted the government to seek allied assistance to bolster maritime security.

Bulgaria has stopped short of directly accusing Russia of carrying out the attacks, but prime minister Rumen Radev said their outcome "benefits the Russian Federation".

Both leading presidential candidates condemned the strikes, but they differ over the broader war.

Gyurov, who served as caretaker prime minister this year, is supported by pro-Western, reformist parties and has long expressed support for Ukraine.

He signed a security cooperation agreement with Kyiv ⁠during his time as premier.

"I want Bulgaria to have a voice of its own in Europe. A reliable NATO ally that defends its interests through preparedness, strong diplomacy and a clear position," he said at a rally on Thursday.

Iotova, formerly vice president, became acting president in January after Radev resigned to enter the parliamentary race, which he won on what many political analysts considered a Kremlin-friendly ticket.

Radev ‌supports Iotova and they both call for a diplomatic end to ​the war.

A victory for Iotova would make her Bulgaria's first elected female president. "My opponents argue that there must ⁠be a war of attrition." she told a rally on Thursday.

"The ⁠truth is, we are all the ones who are most exhausted."

## Ukraine launches massive drone attack on Russia’s Belgorod - report

A Ukrainian drone attack on Russia’s Belgorod region overnight on Sunday reportedly killed one civilian and injured 10 others, local authorities claimed.

The drones reportedly targeted the MegaGRINN shopping complex, the city’s largest retail and entertainment centre, which was closed and empty at the time, *The Kyiv Independent* reported.

Officials confirmed a "massive UAV attack" but did not identify specific targets.

Reports of explosions, fires and air defence operations circulated on social media, but *The Kyiv Independent* said it could not independently verify them.

The attack came a day after Ukraine said its Starshield system had been restored to full speed. The military satellite network, which had operated at sharply reduced speeds since 14 October, is used by Ukrainian forces to coordinate drone strikes and other military operations.
