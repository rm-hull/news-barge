## Sanctioned Russian energy giant secretly funded US gas project - report

Russia’s second-largest LNG producer Novatek reportedly secretly funded US company Polar LNG’s multibillion-dollar Alaska gas project, potentially violating US sanctions, *The New York Times* has reported.

Polar LNG’s former CEO John Riddle resigned in September after discovering the undisclosed Russian links. He said he alerted Trump administration officials and urged an investigation.

"It's not only unlawful, but it's not in the US interest to have Russian technology controlling the commercialisation of Arctic gas in the North Slope," Riddle told the NYT.

Novatek was also negotiating to supply technology to Polar LNG. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner discussed the proposed deal with Russian president Vladimir Putin in September, although it remains unclear whether they knew about the secret financing.

Novatek confirmed it was seeking "minority shareholder" status in the project.

The reported deal is among several energy agreements president Donald Trump is pursuing with Moscow amid efforts to end the Ukraine war, intensified Russian attacks and reduced US support for Kyiv.

## Recap: Elon Musk accuses Ukraine of violating Starlink usage terms with drone strikes

Ukraine said on Saturday that the Starlink satellite system and its military version Starshield were now fully functional after over a week of restrictions that disrupted military operations and intensified tensions with the US. But hours later, SpaceX CEO Elon Musk, whose company owns Starlink, accused the Ukrainian military of violating the company's terms. Neither Musk nor the US government, which operates Starshield using Starlink technology, had taken responsibility for imposing the curbs.

"Ukraine military violated Starlink terms of service and used it for offensive drone strikes to kill people. That loophole was closed last week," Musk said on X. Ukrainian presidential adviser Serhiy Beskrestnov said on the Telegram app that the country is "physically unable" to use Starlink for ⁠long- and medium-range attack drone operations.

![US President Donald Trump meets with Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskiy during the 81st United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) in New York City](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/24/23/2026-10-24T100014Z_973605904_RC2HONATRV49_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-ZELENSKIY-RELATIONSHIP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine's general staff and defence ministry did not immediately comment. Starshield's ​problems ⁠occurred after US President Donald Trump announced ‌a deal to buy Russian diesel while pressing Kyiv to stop striking Russian oil refineries.

Ukrainian officials said the restrictions, which capped Starshield operations at speeds of 160 kph (100 mph), hampered the military's ability to intercept Russian attack drones and forced the cancellation of some operations. "The main thing for today is that Starlink and Starshield have resumed operations at their pre-restriction speeds. The speeds are now at the level we need," President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said on Telegram before Musk's statement.

![Tesla CEO Elon Musk](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/24/23/2026-10-24T004742Z_1492344547_RC26ZNAFZCYR_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-USA-STARLINK.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

According to Zelenskiy, the speeds `started returning to normal overnight. Ukraine's defence ministry said on Saturday the connection remained stable at over 400 kph. Ukraine does not rely on Starlink for long-range drone operations on Russian territory, including attacks on refineries. It operates at speeds no higher than 70-90 kph, Beskrestnov said.

But it remains vital for frontline communications, and the deployment of fast Starshield-enabled medium-range attack drones has helped Ukraine gain the upper hand in several areas of the battlefield this year.

## Ukraine says Starlink speed limits lifted after a slowdown that hobbled its drones

Ukraine’s military has regained full use of Starlink satellite links for its drones and other fast-moving equipment, the defense minister said Saturday, ending speed restrictions that Ukrainian officials said had disrupted front-line operations for more than a week.

The restoration comes as U.S. envoys launched a new push to end the war. Russia agreed Wednesday to resume three-way negotiations with Washington and Kyiv next month. ## The Kremlin says a first tanker of Russian diesel sets sail for the US despite Ukraine's protests

## Ukraine’s survival cannot depend on Elon Musk and Donald Trump

Donald Trump’s account of events remains vague – but he did say that Elon Musk, whose SpaceX company owns the 10,000-plus satellites and their systems, had “put it back”. “It”, presumably, being the hitherto secure and reliable connections to both Starlink and its military variant, Starshield, both of which are heavily relied upon by the Ukrainian military.
