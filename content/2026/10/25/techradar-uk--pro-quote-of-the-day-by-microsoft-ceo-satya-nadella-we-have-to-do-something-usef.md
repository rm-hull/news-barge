As the AI buildout continues and models improve from generation to generation, fears persist over how useful this technology will actually be in practice and whether AI will reach a ceiling before it makes a meaningful impact.

Although roles and responsibilities, and jobs, are being replaced in the name of AI, does that mean it's changing things for the better?

## Energy scarcity

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella was speaking at the World Economic Forum in Davos in 2026 about the impact of AI on the global economy and the business world.

In his remarks, Nadella acknowledged the tensions at the heart of the AI expansionism that has captured not only big tech but the vast majority of the corporate world.

For AI to continue growing at the pace it has been – not just in terms of the compute required to train the models but also for inference as usage grows – we'll need plenty of data centers to churn through tokens. And for these tokens to be created, the AI pioneers will need plenty of energy.

## Encountering bottlenecks

Burning as much electricity as AI companies plan on doing so for AI has caused alarm among the public – particularly as much of this has demanded infrastructure built close to populated areas in towns and cities.

Nadella acknowledged there has been a productivity gap between the hype around AI and the meaningful impact on people's lives – warning that without something to point to, unrest will bubble up and boil over into something more obstructive.
