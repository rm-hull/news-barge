## Sycamore Gap Tree of Hope planted in Barmby Moor sensory garden

A Tree of Hope grown from a cutting of the well-known  Sycamore Gap tree was planted at Lavender Fields Care Village in Barmby Moor *(Image: Darryl Brooks)*

A Tree of Hope grown from a cutting of the well-known Sycamore Gap tree now stands at the heart of a new sensory garden.

It was planted during a special World Alzheimer’s Day event on September 21 at Lavender Fields Care Village in Barmby Moor, with the garden designed specifically for people living with dementia.

The garden and tree were created through a partnership between the care village and North Yorkshire charity Dementia Forward.

Chris Mitchell, chairman of Lavender Fields Care Village, said: "It has been an honour to be deeply involved in this prestigious event with Dementia Forward, and we were very happy to create the sensory garden for our joint causes.

"The fact that we are hearing that the original tree is showing new signs of life is indeed an affirmation of our theme of Hope.

"We look forward to watching the sapling, and indeed the entire sensory garden, mature and provide immense pleasure for all those living with dementia."

The event brought together residents, families, community organisations, and civic representatives, including HM Lord-Lieutenant of East Yorkshire and James Dick OBE.

Jo Phillips, who lives with young-onset dementia, spoke during the ceremony about the importance of not being defined by a diagnosis and the enduring symbolism of hope.
