A Darlington salon owner who turned her business into a specialist wellbeing space for people with special educational needs is “absolutely over the moon” after being shortlisted for an award.

Emma Kane, 39, who runs Two Hoots Wellbeing on North Road, is a finalist in the Northern Echo's Best Beauty Salon Awards.

Emma launched the business in 2018, working alone as a children's hairdresser.

The salon has grown year on year and is now known for tailored treatments and support for people with special educational needs (SEN).

Half the salon's workforce have special educational needs and are apprentices gaining workplace experience.

Emma said: “Half of our workforce have special educational needs and they're employed under an apprenticeship scheme to try and get them into work.”

The salon's approach has already won national recognition.

Last year, Emma was crowned North East Beauty Therapist of the Year at the British Hair and Beauty Awards.

She said the business creates a personalised treatment plan for every client.

“We retail and make all of our packages and treatments how you'd like,” she said.

“Within the hour you get with us, we'll discuss what you're struggling with and what your problems are, and then we build a treatment plan around that.

“Every time you visit us it'll be something different depending on how you're feeling.

"It could be that we do 20 minutes on a facial, then hot stones on the back and a deep tissue massage on the legs.

"It's all personalised to everybody.”

Emma said professional experience and personal circumstances shaped the salon's move into a specialist SEN-friendly space.

“We do that because people with special educational needs have very big sensory responses, so touch, surroundings and even smell are really important for people with those needs to be able to relax,” she said.

“There are two reasons we focus on this.

"One is that I've had two strokes, one just after Covid and another four years ago, and a lot of it was down to stress and anxiety.

“There was nowhere at that point where I could go and say, 'Right, I'm struggling.'

“I had no movement on my left side, I'd just had a child and it was really difficult for me.”

Meanwhile, Emma's eldest daughter Chloe was diagnosed with autism and struggled to find a suitable path after turning 16.

“She knew she couldn't continue in an educational setting because she'd had 12 years of hell, but you have to stay in education at that age,” Emma said.

“So we took Chloe on as an apprentice.

“She's become a completely different person.

"She used to not be able to speak in public, order a meal in a restaurant, go on public transport and some days she couldn't even leave the house.

“Now you wouldn't even know she has autism. She speaks to people, advises them on treatments and shares her experience.

"She's a completely different child.

“Because that's been such a massive success, we've put everything together and that's what we wanted to focus on.”

The salon now has four staff - Emma, two SEN apprentices and a full-time member of staff who supports them.

Emma, who moved from Skipton to Darlington in 2003, says raising two autistic children helped shape the business.

Reflecting on the nomination, she said: “It's amazing. I'm absolutely over the moon.

“I couldn't be more proud of the girls.
