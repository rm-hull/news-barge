A second dispersal order has been imposed after ongoing antisocial behaviour, criminal damage and graffiti in a town’s parks were branded “completely unacceptable”.

The order covers Rossmere Park in Hartlepool and came into force at 3pm on Saturday, October 3.

It follows reports of large groups of young people causing noise, disorder and crime, leaving others feeling intimidated in the town’s green spaces.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21534229.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The second dispersal order *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

The move comes on the same day police introduced a separate 48-hour dispersal order around Bishop Cuthbert Park following reports of youth antisocial behaviour.

Police were called to Bishop Cuthbert Park on Friday night after a large group apparently gathered for a pre-planned fight.

Fireworks were later launched at residential properties and towards a care home.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21533768.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The first dispersal order *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

The second order covers Owton Manor Lane, Catcote Road, Brierton Lane and Stockton Road back to Owton Manor Lane.

Hartlepool Neighbourhoods Inspector Adrian Dack said: “Unfortunately we are seeing ongoing issues of general nuisance, crime and costly damage to public property and community facilities in our parks, which is affecting others’ peaceful enjoyment of the town’s green spaces.”

“We’re working with our local authority partners and the community to address the issues through engagement and enforcement.”

“Officers engage with youngsters wherever possible and explain the consequences of becoming involved in this type of completely unacceptable behaviour; there are also targeted interventions with identified young people, while high-visibility patrols aim to deter trouble and reassure the public.”

“Under a dispersal order, my officers will not hesitate to direct anyone involved in, or suspected of becoming involved in, ASB or crime to leave immediately. Anyone who returns within 48 hours could be arrested.”

“Ultimately, these powers protect people who want—and are entitled—to use the parks.”

Police also appealed to parents and carers to know where their children are going in the evening and what they are doing.
