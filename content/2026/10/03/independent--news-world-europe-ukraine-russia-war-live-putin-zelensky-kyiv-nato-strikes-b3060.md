## Zelensky says Putin has told his generals 'there are no rules now' in Ukraine war

Volodymyr Zelensky says Russian president Vladimir Putin has instructed his generals to abandon the rules of war in pursuit of more territory in Ukraine, according to the Financial Times.

The Ukrainian president made the remarks during a visit to a bomb shelter inside a school that had been hit by a Russian drone on Thursday.

Zelensky said Putin gave the order last month, saying: "He said there are no rules now." Russia has since stepped up its air strikes on civilian targets, particularly in Kyiv, hitting data centres and energy infrastructure as well as schools and hospitals.

## Russian strikes on Kyiv bridge causes traffic chaos

Russia struck a bridge over the Dnipro River that linked two sides ​of Kyiv, forcing full or partial traffic suspension across it.

A drone struck the Souther Bridge around 5.40pm local time yesterday, marking the second attack on the structure in two days.

Nearly 90 per cent of transit traffic between the two banks of the river passes through the Southern Bridge.

The latest attack also coincided with Kyiv's evening rush hour.

The Russian aerial attacks on the bridge are part of an intensifying campaign on infrastructure across Kyiv, disrupting daily life in the Ukrainian capital.

They have led to long traffic jams across the city throughout the day.

## Hungary welcomes Ukraine's bill strengthening Hungarian minority rights

Hungary has welcomed Ukraine’s decision to submit a draft law to parliament seeking to strengthen the education rights of its ethnic Hungarian minority.

"The (Ukrainian) bill strengthens the ​legal ⁠guarantees of education in the Hungarian language and the network of educational institutions," Hungarian prime minister Peter Magyar said in a Facebook post.

The move could help Kyiv get backing for its EU membership from Hungary, which had said that progress on boosting the rights of ethnic Hungarians in Ukraine is a prerequisite for Budapest's backing of Ukraine's EU accession.

## Zelensky urges countries to help stop Russian drone production

The Ukrainian president urged countries to strengthen sanctions that can help prevent the production of drones by Russia.

“No matter how hard Moscow tries to fully localise the production of its drones and missiles, its ability to escalate remains critically dependent on foreign components,” Zelensky said in a post on X.

“These are supply chains running from Europe, the US, China, Japan, and many other countries. Most of these companies are well-known manufacturers, but their components still make their way to Russia,” he said, adding that “sanctions must work and must be strengthened”.

“Every Russian drone can actually be stopped right at the production line. What is needed is the political will of these countries,” the Ukrainian president said.

## Latvia goes to poll amid Ukraine war and drone incursions

Latvians vote today in parliamentary elections in the country amid potential Russian aggression across Europe, including the Baltics, and a growing number of airspace incursions related to Moscow's war in Ukraine.

Russia has denied accusations that it is planning to attack Nato, but security concerns have weighed heavily on the Latvian election campaign.

The election is likely to keep power in the hands of pro-Ukrainian Prime Minister Andris Kulbergs, according to opinion polls.

"We have to give Ukraine everything necessary right now (so) that they can have the upper hand and win", Kulbergs told Reuters last month.
