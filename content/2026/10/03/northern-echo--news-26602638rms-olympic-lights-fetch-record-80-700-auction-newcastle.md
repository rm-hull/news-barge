Bidders paid a record £80,700 for two lots of light fittings salvaged from the RMS Olympic, the Titanic’s sister ship, during a strong week of auctions in the North East.

The fittings, removed from the RMS Olympic, were sold at Anderson & Garland’s recent auctions.

They were salvaged when the ship was dismantled for scrap in the late 1930s.

![Lots at auction](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21532046.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Part of the Olympic lights lot *(Image: Anderson & Garland)*

A pair of ormolu and cut-glass ceiling lights sold for £37,700, while a brass three-branch fitting fetched £43,000 to set what is believed to be a saleroom record for RMS Olympic lights.

Fred Wyrley-Birch, managing director at Anderson & Garland, said: "These were among our most successful Country House & Fine Interiors and Fine Art auctions in a few years, and it was wonderful to see such strong demand from both the trade and private buyers.

"Achieving a saleroom record for lights from RMS Olympic was the highlight, but the strength of the week ran right through the sales, with traditional pictures and mid-century design both performing far beyond expectations.

![Lots at auction](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21532047.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Olympic in New York *(Image: Anderson & Garland)*

"It is a real vote of confidence in the market, and we are delighted for our vendors."

Both RMS Olympic lots beat the auction house’s previous records.

The brass fitting beat the £23,400 paid for two three-branch lights in 2023, while the ormolu and cut-glass pair almost doubled the £19,500 fetched for a similar pair in 2024.

Other highlights included a wood and gesso figure of John the Baptist, attributed to Spanish sculptor Juan Martínez Montañés (1568–1649), which sold for £4,550.

![Lots at auction](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21532044.jpg?type=mds-article-620)First Class staircase on the Olympic *(Image: Anderson & Garland)*

Mid-century furniture also outperformed estimates in the Modern Art & Design sale.

A pair of FD130 teak-framed armchairs by Peter Hvidt and Orla Mølgaard-Nielsen for France & Son sold for £1,950, beating their £600–£1,000 estimate.

Several traditional works also beat expectations in the Fine Art auction.

![Lots at auction](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21532043.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The First Class lounge on the Olympic *(Image: Anderson & Garland)*

Edward Hersey’s oil painting South Country Mill sold for £5,460 against an estimate of £1,000–£1,500.

Frederick Richard Lee’s A Distant View of Dunbar Castle also sold for £5,460, against an estimate of £2,000–£3,000.

William Albert Clark’s painting of Ursula Raglan, a cow, sold for £1,690 against an estimate of £200–£400.

The telephone bidder said Ursula was "particularly well endowed."
