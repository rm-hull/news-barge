A Bradford salon where clients simply visit for the company has been shortlisted among the top 10 in its city.

Beauty & Aesthetics by Emma JB on High Street in Queensbury has been nominated in the Telegraph & Argus' best beauty salon competition.

Founded around five years ago by Emma John Baptiste, the salon is now managed by Emma Arnett, who joined the salon in June last year and has been in the role for six months.

Ms Arnett said: "Oh wow, look at us go.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21527164.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the salon *(Image: Beauty and Aesthetics By Emma JB)*

## A ‘safe space’ for clients

"It makes me really proud to be able to turn around and say, these are my girls, these are the people that I work with."

She described the salon as a "safe space" and a "social salon" that offers more than just beauty treatments, with clients often staying for a chat or a coffee.

Ms Arnett said: "We're providing services for people that are in need of them.

"We provide coffees for people that want to chat, we provide advice.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21527166.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the salon *(Image: Beauty and Aesthetics By Emma JB)*

"Because as much as being a beauty therapist, the therapist part of it is actually a lot bigger than what you think."

While the salon offers a range of beauty services, nails and waxing remain its most sought-after treatments.

She said the salon’s welcoming environment is a key part of its appeal.

Ms Arnett said: "We're constantly trying to strive to be an open, welcoming salon.

"What makes us primarily different from other places is we class ourselves as a social salon.

"It's not you come in, you get your nails done, you leave.

"It's you come in, we talk about you, we talk about what you need, what you're looking for."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21527167.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Staff at the salon *(Image: Beauty and Aesthetics By Emma JB)*

Some clients visit simply for the company, she said.

Ms Arnett said: "We've got clients that sometimes are like, 'I've had a really bad day, can I just nip up for a coffee?'

"They don't want a service.

"They just want to come in and see the friendly faces."

## Community support drives success

She credited the salon’s success to word-of-mouth recommendations and social media support from the community.

Ms Arnett said: "I think a lot of our community and the people that know us are finally like, 'These girls need recognition for everything that they do.'"

The salon hopes to expand its community work, having recently opened a sister aesthetics clinic.

Ms Arnett said: "We're constantly doing community days.

"I think my goal would be to get us even further inserted into the community."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21527165.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the salon *(Image: Beauty and Aesthetics By Emma JB)*

## What winning would mean to the team

If the salon wins the competition, she said it would be "like getting the ultimate five-star review."

She said: "It'd mean the absolute world because it shows that all the stuff we are doing for the community is appreciated and not gone unnoticed.

"I'd say thank you to everyone for voting for us."
