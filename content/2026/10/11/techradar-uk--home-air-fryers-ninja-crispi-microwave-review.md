### Ninja Crispi Microwave: 30-second review

The Ninja Crispi Microwave combines the power of an air fryer with the speed of a microwave. It sounds like an almost impossible feat, yet Ninja has seemingly done the impossible with one of the most versatile kitchen appliances around.

Things get off to a good start out of the box. One of the common frustrations with microwaves are their controls, and how it feels like you need an engineering degree to understand how it all works. Not with the Crispi Microwave. A brief flick through the instructions, and you’ll be off, with easy to use buttons and modes to help you cook the food you want.

The included glassware dish with removable crisper tray, and the rails its slides onto in the microwave, are great additions for air frying and FusionCrisp cooking. And when microwaving you don’t need to worry about any rotating turnable. You get the whole surface of the microwave cavity to place your food, allowing you to cook more at once.

![Ninja Crispi Microwave with glass dish](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/vGfD43GhkczEKmoDUVFxvf-1200-80.jpg)

Then there’s the actual cooking. Whether I was air frying, grilling or baking, or using one of the many microwave presets, the Crispi Microwave delivered great food every time. And to really unlock the power of this appliance, FusionCrisp uses the best of both cooking technologies, for wonderful moist in the middle, crispy on the outside cooks.

If you’ve got the space on your counter, and are looking to consolidate the number of appliances in your kitchen, the Ninja Crispi Microwave is an excellent all-rounder.

### Ninja Crispi Microwave: design

* **Premium, modern design, with cookbook in the box**
* **Slightly bigger than a standard microwave, but needs more clearance**
* **Internal light is dim, front is a dust and fingerprint magnet**

The Ninja Crispi Microwave is a smart looking appliance, with a modern aesthetic, clean lines, and chunky dials and buttons.

Considering the additional air fryer tech squeezed inside the Ninja Crispi Microwave, it’s not hugely oversized. At 44.6 (D) x 51.6 (W) x 35.8cm (H) it’s bigger than my current unit, but it’s not aggressively massive.

While that’ll be good news for those hoping it will fit where their current microwave resides, you need to take airflow into consideration. As this product also has an air fryer built into it, you need to allow for a minimum of 12 inches (30.5 cm) of clearance above the unit for adequate airflow and safe operation.

Unlike a traditional microwave, there’s no rotating platform inside. Instead there’s a glass service that you place dishes on, and you need to be careful when cooking is finished as this surface can be very hot.

What that does mean is you get a bigger cooking surface area compared to a turntable (up to 20% more, according to Ninja), with the ability to place dishes across the entire base of the microwave.

The quick start guide and recipe book included in the box will get you up and running quickly though, and these offers are better than some manufacturers - I’m looking at you, Philips.

The smart, modern design looks good on the counter top, but the glossy door and instrument panel are an absolute dust and fingerprint magnet. You’ll need to keep a microfibre cloth handy if you want to maintain a clean look.

![Person pressing Ninja Crispi Microwave open button](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/bniP9h9MsznKjt2vnCSAUf-1200-80.jpg)

Press the large door release button, and it swings open nice and wide, going beyond 90 degrees to give you plenty of space to move things in and out. It’s especially welcome when sliding the hot glassware tray out.

That glass tray is included in the box (along with a removable, metal crisper tray), and at a capacity of 5.5 quarts / 5.2 liters Ninja says it can hold an 8lb chicken, or four slices of pizza

And the runners that hold the glassware near the top of the cavity when in air fry or FusionCrisp mode have a nice party trick. Slide the glassware most of the way out, and it’ll drop into a small groove on the runners.

This angles the tray slightly towards you, allowing you to mix, stir or turn your food without the glassware sliding all over the place or needing to remove it completely. Your oven racks might well have a similar feature, and I was pleased to see the inclusion here.

![Ninja Crispi Microwave door](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/NRLFrjopLLbNMcaRc7AmXf-1200-80.jpg)

Plus, Ninja has included a childlock feature on the door release button, preventing curious little hands from reaching up and simply popping the door open when cooking. To enable child lock, grab a Philips-head screwdriver and turn the switch on the side of the door release button. You will then have to press this side switch and the main door release together to open.

Another thing I like are the buttons, which have great tactility and feel premium under finger. They’re clearly labelled and are relatively intuitive - though you will need to refer to the manual initially while the process of getting the right settings becomes engrained.

As an example of how good the controls are, I was able to change the clock time on the microwave with ease, something that can’t be said for many microwaves I’ve used in the past.

Something that hasn't shone so bright is the light inside the microwave. It’s a little dim, even when you have the door open. When it’s closed, it’s not easy to see your food due to the heavy tint on the door as well as the under powered light.

The Ninja Crispi Microwave offers an array of cooking modes. There’s the traditional microwave option, allowing you to quickly reheat food, cook microwave meals, and pop some popcorn. Press the microwave button, set the time and hit go.

But there’s more here than first meets the eye. Hit the microwave button twice and you’ll get an illuminated selection of additional sub modes; defrost, beverage, popcorn, potato, soup, soften butter, frozen vegetable, and porridge. This makes for quicker and easier cooking for some of the core things you use a microwave for.

Then there’s the air fryer mode, which itself also has a few sub-options to choose from. If you’re familiar with Ninja’s air fryers these will be familiar; air fry, max crisp, air roast, air bake, air grill.

Then there’s a third mode, FusionCrisp. Ninja claims FusionCrisp takes the best part of microwaving and air frying, and combines them, giving you food that retains moisture, cooks quickly, but doesn’t go soggy.

* **Brilliantly versatile as an air fryer and a microwave**
* **FusionCrisp mode combines the best of both**
* **Good enough to replace multiple appliances in your kitchen**

The performance of the Ninja Crispi Microwave is impressive. Whether you’re air frying, microwaving, reheating, baking, grilling or softening butter (yes, it really has its own dedicated setting), this appliance delivers.

I used a combination of FusionCrisp and Air Fry to make a family-sized portion of mac and cheese. I added macaroni, water, milk and oil to the glassware and cooked it on the FusionCrisp mode for 25 minutes. ### Buy it if

**You want to consolidate kitchen appliances**

With a versatile air fryer mode, traditional microwave, and Ninja’s unique FusionCrisp setting, there’s an awful lot of ways to cook, bake, grill, air fryer, reheat and more.

**You’re cooking for a family**

The sizable cooking capacity inside the Ninja Crispi Microwave means it’s possible to cook an entire chicken, or multiple side dishes at once. If you have a family, the size here will be a big plus.

**You want ease of use**

With multiple useful preset modes, controls that don’t require an engineering degree to work out, no annoying rotating platform, and dishwasher safe accessories, the Ninja Crispi Microwave makes cooking almost effortless.

### Don’t buy it if

**You’re happy with your current air fryer**

If you already own an air fryer, and are happy with its performance, but need a microwave, there’s no need to upgrade Ninja Crispi Microwave. Save yourself some money and go for a microwave that’s a third of the price.

**You have limited counterspace**

The Ninja Crispi Microwave is on the larger end of the microwave scale, and coupled with the fact it needs decent air flow space around it, small kitchens and worktops with low hanging wall cabinets might not be suitable.

**You want to steam food**

The one function the Crispi Microwave doesn’t have is steam. For that, you’ll want to look at the Samsung Combi Smart Microwave or the Philips 5000 Series Megabasket with Steam air fryer. Both have their limitations compared to the Ninja, but if steaming is your thing they’ll be the better bet for you.
