Revisiting one of Omega Force’s most beloved works via *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* has been interesting. I got into the series quite late, so I’ve never had the same reverence for the earlier games that many other fans do. I’m happy to say that has changed with this remastered version.

**Platform reviewed:** PC  
**Available on:** PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Nintendo Switch 2, PC  
**Release date:** October 1, 2026

This is a very different game compared to modern entries like *Dynasty Warriors Origins* or*Hyrule Warriors: Age of Imprisonment**.* Objectives are straightforward, battles (for the most part) tend not to last as long, and your KO count per battle is much lower than the thousands you rack up in newer installments.

Satisfaction lies elsewhere in *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered.* It trades bombastic theatrics and ‘rule of cool’ battlefield domination for a more grounded approach. Instead of tearing across battlegrounds like a Category 5 hurricane, you’ll need to pick your fights more carefully and move with your army. Even basic peons are far more aggressive in this game, so ditching your allies isn’t exactly the wisest decision.

There are some cool quality-of-life additions, such as being able to counter enemy Strong and Special attacks with your own. A handy checkpoint feature lets you rewind the battle to key moments if you die, and there’s an honest-to-goodness dodge button that helps even the odds and can even be followed up with a counter if timed correctly. Alternatively, you can set the entire game to a ‘Classic’ mode, ditching all of this in favor of the original gameplay experience.

It’s an incredibly thoughtful remaster in that regard, offering options for newcomers, purists, and everyone in between. The visual facelift is nice, but not overbearing. Plus, additional content from the Xtreme Legends expansion is included here, too, making *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* an extremely beefy package for its relatively budget price tag.

## Same as it ever was?

![Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered screenshot showing officer Meng Huo riding a war elephant into battle](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/KU5QPDmrKz7sr2Q4TRSbGd-1200-80.jpg)

*Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered,* as ever, presents a loose interpretation of Luo Guanzhong’s*Romance of the Three Kingdoms.* Which in itself is a romanticization of this turbulent wartorn era of Ancient China.

As an earlier entry, *Dynasty Warriors 3* pretty much gives you the highlights rather than delving deep into character drama or motivations. It’s quite refreshingly simple, though individual playable officers do get their own cutscenes, endings, and the like.

You’ll spend the majority of your time in Musou Mode — effectively the game’s campaign. You will then choose an officer from one of four factions: Wei, Wu, Shu, or Other. The latter of which featuring officers with no set affiliation like Lu Bu, Dong Zhuo, and Diaochan.

![Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered screenshot showing officer Lu Bu charge head-on into an army of peons](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/V72MV6KhbqKxSAYLu3j6Kj-1200-80.jpg)

Each officer has their own bespoke Musou Mode route, though some are longer than others. Even so, your goal on each map typically boils down to defeating a key enemy officer, while ticking off smaller objectives to boost your side’s morale or rob the enemy of powerful weaponry.

That might sound repetitive, and it can be if played over longer sessions. However, I think *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* is tailored to shorter bursts of play, and the bite-sized Musou Mode campaigns for each officer support that. Playing through a campaign will bolster that officer’s stats, as well as earn them new weapons and equippable items — the latter of which can be shared between all officers.

Part of the beauty of *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* is that it feels like a snowball of unlockables. You’ll routinely be bagging new playable officers, more powerful weapons, and potent rare items should you meet their conditions during a stage.

Some stages can also grant you an officer’s fourth or fifth weapon. These are extremely strong, but are typically gated behind higher difficulties and some rather stringent objectives so be sure to bring upgraded officers and plenty of patience for these.

## Old and new

![Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered screenshot showing officer Zhao Yun counter a group of enemies with spear thrusts](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/RdUcDDWhyKJGxWTVVqvF84-1200-80.jpg)

One of my favorite things about *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* is that it has options for basically everyone. You can play the entire game in either modern or classic modes. Classic is just that:*Dynasty Warriors 3* as it played on the PlayStation 2, for the most part. No fancy extras. Does it feel a bit dated? Yes absolutely, but I’m happy it’s here as an option for purists.

The more modern take applies a bunch of quality-of-life changes and gameplay additions. As mentioned at the top, these include Strong and Special attack counters, a dodge, and a quick mount option for your horse. It helps the game feel a lot more fluid, in my opinion.

Was I still getting juggled to death by lowly peons for daring to take on an officer on my lonesome? Yep. More than a few times. But ultimately I did appreciate the more humbling experience here when I’m so used to treating enemy armies like mowing the lawn in more modern Musou games.

![Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered weapon screen. A level 3 Dragon Spear is transferring skills to a more powerful level 4 Fierce Dragon Spear](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/gqgWspVCqxPnegThZARs9C-1200-80.jpg)

**Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered is a reminder of how cool unlockable content used to be in this era of gaming. You’ll have to put the work in to unlock most characters as well as their signature weapons. It’s the kind of stuff that’ll keep you busy for potentially tens of hours without feeling overly tedious.**

What’s nice is that if you’re somewhere in the middle — say, wanting a hybrid modern/classic experience — *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* supports that. Quality-of-life additions can be toggled individually in the options menu, allowing for a widely customizable experience.

Additions aside, Omega Force has elected to keep the original game’s amazing soundtrack and cheeseball dialogue fully intact. I love this because there are some truly hilarious line deliveries across cutscenes, and the overt mispronunciation of officer names just adds to the charm of it all. Battles have been spiced up with combat lines from later entries like *Dynasty Warriors 8,* though, which is a nice touch.

## Should I play Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered?

## Play it if…

**You’re a newer Musou fan**  
If you’ve only recently gotten into Omega Force’s *Warriors* franchise, I implore you to give this one a go. It’s a great change of pace from more modern entries and very much bears a ‘substance over style’ approach.

**You love unlockables**  
There are boatloads of officers and weapons to unlock, and most play rather differently from one another.

## Don't play it if...

**You’re not keen on repetition**  
There isn’t really any grinding to be done here, but replaying Musou Mode with many different officers can grow tiresome. Oh, and as a bonus pro tip: you only have to do a particularly aggravating objective during the Nanman campaign mission once, but my word is it a slog that first time.

## Accessibility features

There aren't any dedicated accessibility toggles in *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* such as subtitles or colorblind modes. However, individual game settings can be toggled based on preferences. These include target-facing assists, dodging, power guards, and strong attack counters.

## How I reviewed Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered

![Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered screenshot showing officer Lu Xun swipe at an enemy group with dual swords, applying a harmful status effect to them](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/cSNSXiAk4b6aJxe5vjRTpM-1200-80.jpg)

I played *Dynasty Warriors 3: Complete Edition Remastered* for 15 hours on PC via Steam for this review. Being a remaster of an older title, my Nvidia GeForce RTX 5060 was more than enough to run the game on maximum settings with zero hitches or issues.

My gamepad of choice was the Valve Steam Controller (2026), and I often hooked up my RIG 900 Max HS gaming headset to enjoy the game's banging soundtrack and corny dialogue sequences.

The majority of my playtime was spent in the main Musou Mode, unlocking characters, weapons, and optional rare items. Though I did delve into the Free and Challenge modes for a change of pace, too.
