For centuries, one hilltop landmark has stood watch over one of North Yorkshire's most dramatic landscapes.

High above the Esk Valley between Danby and Lealholm, Danby Beacon is a familiar sight for walkers and cyclists in the North York Moors National Park.

The 5.5-metre steel beacon was unveiled in 2008 as a modern tribute to the site’s long and varied history.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/3343752.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Beacon lighting *(Image: Provided)*

Danby Beacon's story stretches back thousands of years, with the landmark standing on a Bronze Age burial mound.

Archaeological remains suggest that people have used Beacon Hill for thousands of years.

Its elevated position later made it an ideal site for warning beacon - part of a national network of signal fires used to alert communities to danger.

The Danby Beacon Trust says Britain has used beacon systems since Roman times, with fires lit on high ground to send alerts during national threats.

During the Napoleonic era, soldiers and civilian watchers guarded the beacon, with "watchers at the beacon" parish records noting “watchers at the beacon”.

In 1937, Danby Beacon became home to one of Britain’s early Chain Home radar stations, which warned of approaching enemy aircraft.

The station played a crucial role during the Second World War, helping to protect industrial areas across northern England and the North Midlands.

On February 3, 1940, the radar station helped guide Flight Lieutenant Peter Townsend to intercept a German Heinkel bomber over the North Sea, resulting in the first enemy aircraft shot down over England during the war.

Flight Lieutenant Townsend later wrote: "I shall never forget that day; Danby Beacon deserves to be remembered by succeeding generations who owe their freedom from Nazi tyranny to the young men and women, crews of RAF radar stations like Danby."

The radar station remained operational throughout the war and into the early years of the Cold War before closing in the 1950s.

After the original wooden beacon rotted away, residents formed the Danby Beacon Trust in 2004 to build a replacement honouring the site’s history and serving the community.

Unveiled in 2008, the current beacon is constructed from weathering Corten steel, chosen for its rust-coloured finish that blends with the surrounding moorland.

The flame-shaped basket at the top of the beacon is made from blued stainless steel, while bronze details honour the burial mound below.

More than 200 people watched the beacon’s first lighting on a windy day in October 2008, marking Trafalgar Day and the anniversary of Admiral Nelson’s victory.

![The new beacon under construction](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/656672.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new beacon under construction in 2008 *(Image: Provided)*

The trust still lights Danby Beacon for national commemorations, royal celebrations and private events to this day.

At around 301 metres above sea level, it offers sweeping views of the moors, Cleveland Hills and the Yorkshire coast.

The beacon itself may be new, but the hill has overlooked North Yorkshire for thousands of years - making it one of the region’s most enduring landmarks.
