At first glance, Cockfield is a quiet County Durham village surrounded by rolling countryside.

Stone houses line its streets, a parish church stands at its heart, and the Durham Dales stretch beyond it.

But one of County Durham’s most unusual historic landscapes lies on its doorstep.

Cockfield Fell is far more than a stretch of common land. It is also one of the largest scheduled ancient monuments in Britain, with traces of centuries of industry still visible across the landscape.

Long before walkers and wildlife arrived, mining shaped the fell.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21543590.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cockfield Fell *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Bell pits, spoil heaps and other remains survive across the open ground, providing clues to generations of people who worked the land for coal.

Today, those industrial piles sit among grassland and open fields.

This peaceful landscape was once at the working heart of a community built on farming and mining.

The village itself retains reminders of that long history.

St Mary’s Church stands near its centre and dates back to medieval times, linking the village to its distant past.

Traditional stone buildings and cottages surround it, many dating from the 18th and 19th centuries.

Together, they preserve the look of a village shaped by farming and industry.

But the fell is what sets Cockfield apart.

Paths cross the common, letting walkers explore the countryside and its industrial remains together.

From parts of the fell there are broad views across the surrounding County Durham landscape.

The open ground has also become important for wildlife. Birds, wildflowers and small mammals thrive in its varied habitats, which change character with the seasons.

Cockfield is also within easy reach of some of County Durham’s best-known attractions.

Raby Castle lies only a short distance away, with its historic buildings, gardens and deer park.

Nearby Barnard Castle and the Bowes Museum offer market-town history, architecture and art.

Hamsterley Forest offers miles of walking and cycling routes, while Locomotion in Shildon charts the region’s role in Britain’s railway history.

With so many attractions nearby, visitors can easily overlook Cockfield.
