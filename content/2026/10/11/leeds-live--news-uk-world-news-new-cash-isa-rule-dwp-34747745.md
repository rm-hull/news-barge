# New Cash ISA rule for DWP state pensioners confirmed under Andy Burnham

## Cash ISA limits will be cut to just £12,000, down from their current £20,000, as of April 2027

A modification to Cash ISA regulations initially conceived by former Chancellor Rachel Reeves will come into effect under Andy Burnham and his newly appointed Chancellor, John Healey, delivering a welcome advantage for all state pensioners.

Changes to Cash ISAs, amongst the most substantial decisions made during Reeves' time at the Treasury, are set to go ahead as scheduled from April onwards, HM Treasury has confirmed. For all state pensioners and individuals over 65 who haven't yet reached state pension age - currently being phased up from 66 to 67 - an exemption will apply, granting them a more generous Cash ISA allowance than other savers.

Cash ISA allowances are being reduced to merely £12,000, down from the present £20,000 threshold, from April 2027. However, the updated regulations won't affect state pensioners or those aged over 65, with special provisions maintaining the existing £20,000 Cash ISA annual contribution ceiling, reports the Express.

The state pension age stands at 66, increasing to 67, meaning all state pensioners will qualify for the new Cash ISA safeguards, alongside those aged 65 and above who haven't yet reached state pension age.

During her Autumn Budget, Reeves ultimately confirmed a long-anticipated and widely feared reduction to Cash ISAs, despite opposition from leading financial advocates such as Martin Lewis. Under the revised regulations, savers will continue to be permitted to deposit £20,000 annually into tax-free ISAs as they currently can, however Cash ISAs will be capped at merely £12,000, rather than the complete £20k.

Those wishing to utilise the full £20k allowance will need to place the remaining £8,000 into a Stocks and Shares ISA alternatively. However, state pensioners will be permitted to retain the full cash allowance, Ms Reeves confirmed in her address.

Cash ISAs, which enable savers to set money aside and shield it from taxation each financial year, have come under examination. At one stage, speculation indicated the cap could be established as low as £10,000. Digital platforms will be established, intended "to help people invest" in the UK, Rachel Reeves announced as she outlined reforms to the ISA framework.

The Chancellor informed MPs: "From April 2027, I will reform our Isa system, keeping the full £20,000 allowance while designating £8,000 of it exclusively for investment, with over-65s retaining the full cash allowance. And thanks to our changes to financial advice and guidance, banks will be able to guide savers to better choices for their hard-earned money.

"Over 50% of the Isa market - including Hargreaves Lansdown, HSBC, Lloyds, Vanguard and Barclays - have signed up to launch new online hubs to help people invest here in Britain."

Savers who would typically surpass that threshold will require alternatives before the modification takes effect, such as utilising Stocks and Shares ISAs, though the alteration will not impact existing deposits. Andy Burnham's Chancellor John Healey has yet to disclose the complete details of his spending plans - or their costings - but the Budget is just a fortnight away, scheduled for 28 October.

The Chancellor faces increasing pressure from gilt and bond markets as the ramifications of US involvement in the Middle East begin to affect Britain's borrowing costs, eating into crucial financial flexibility. Nevertheless, the Chancellor has no intention of abandoning Ms Reeves' ISA plans, which remain on track to come into force from 6 April 2027 , according to the Treasury.
