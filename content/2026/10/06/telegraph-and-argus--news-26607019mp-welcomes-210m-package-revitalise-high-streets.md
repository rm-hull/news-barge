**This week's MP column comes from Bradford South MP Judith Cummins**

Bradford is a city built on industry, business and entrepreneurship.

From its proud industrial heritage as a powerhouse of manufacturing and innovation to the diverse economy it supports today, the city has a long history of creating jobs, developing skills and driving growth.

That legacy continues in the businesses that call Bradford home today.

We have major employers with local, national and international reach, from metalworking firms such as Barrett Steel, which play an important role in supporting the UK's industrial supply chains, to global chemical companies developing products that help feed the world and make water safer.

These businesses provide highly skilled, well-paid jobs, invest in our communities and strengthen both our local economy and our country's national resilience.

Bradford also has a multitude of smaller businesses that sit at the heart of our communities: the cafés, restaurants, pubs, shops and community spaces that bring our high streets to life.

They create places where people meet friends and family, support local jobs, keep money circulating in the local economy and give our towns and villages their distinct character.

If we want more business, more industry, more jobs, thriving high streets and opportunities for future generations, we must continue investing in skills, infrastructure and local communities.

Earlier this month, I was delighted to formally open Bradford College's new Junction Mills building, which will provide training in modern automotive and digital engineering.

This investment will help equip young people with the skills needed in rapidly growing industries and ensure Bradford remains at the forefront of emerging technologies, including the electric and hybrid vehicle sector.

It is also why I welcome this week's announcement of a £210 million Government package to help revitalise high streets by tackling derelict properties and bringing vacant buildings back into productive use.

![Judith Cummins, MP for Bradford South](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21146574.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Judith Cummins, MP for Bradford South *(Image: Newsquest)*

In many places, empty and neglected buildings have become symbols of decline.

Supporting their regeneration can help attract new businesses, create jobs and restore pride in our high streets.

This builds on earlier measures to support retail, hospitality and leisure businesses, including business rates relief and planning reforms designed to give local communities a stronger voice in shaping their high streets.

I will be working to ensure Bradford secures its fair share of this investment.

Building on more than £20 million that I have already helped secure for neighbourhoods and communities across Bradford South through the Pride in Place and Impact Funds to support local projects that improve the places where people live, work and spend their time.

I know this support is needed because whenever I speak to local business owners or residents on the doorstep, concerns about the future of our high streets are frequently raised.

People do not want to see shuttered shopfronts, declining footfall or well-loved businesses forced to close their doors.

That is why practical local campaigns matter too.

I was proud to cut the ribbon following the successful campaign to reopen Tong Street Post Office after almost a year closed.

High street services are often lifelines, particularly for older residents and those who rely on being able to access essential services close to home.

I am pleased that local people can once again use those services in the heart of their community.

Supporting local businesses also means making high streets accessible.

Take Wibsey, for example.

It is a vibrant village centre with excellent independent businesses and huge potential, yet the council-owned car park on Tennyson Road remains underused because short-stay parking charges encourage shoppers to look elsewhere.

People want convenience.

They do not want to spend time searching for parking when making a quick visit to a local shop.

If accessing the high street becomes difficult or expensive, many will simply choose other destinations.

That is why I want Bradford Council's leadership to do what it promised on parking: free daytime parking and free evening parking at the car park on Tennyson Road, in Wibsey.

While free parking is not a solution to every challenge facing our high streets, it is a practical step that can increase footfall, support independent retailers and encourage people to shop locally.

It also takes the strain off congested local streets for people.

When people feel positive about their local high street, and when visiting it is straightforward and convenient, they are more likely to spend locally, support local businesses and contribute to the success of their community.

Ultimately, that is what this is all about.

Bradford deserves thriving businesses, strong high streets, good jobs, quality training opportunities, effective transport links and vibrant communities.

Achieving that requires investment at every level, from major employers and skills provision to our high streets, local business and community services.

As the Member of Parliament for Bradford South, my priority remains clear: securing that investment, standing up for people and local businesses, supporting our communities and fighting for the opportunities that my constituents deserve.
