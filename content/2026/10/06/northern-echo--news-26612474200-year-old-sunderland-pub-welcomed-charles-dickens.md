A pub that once hosted Charles Dickens has reopened after a major refurbishment.

The Bridge Hotel Vaults, located on High Street West in Sunderland, is steeped in more than 200 years of history.

It is also thought to have welcomed a string of notable guests, including the famed Victorian novelist.

Dickens is said to have stayed at the pub in 1852 during a visit to Sunderland, where he performed at the nearby Lyceum Theatre.

Another high-profile guest believed to have spent the night is Ulysses S. Grant, the American Civil War general who later became the 18th president of the United States.

Grant is believed to have stayed at the hotel during his 1877 tour of the North East, although the claim is presented as a belief rather than a confirmed historical fact.

The building dates back to the 1790s and originally served as the Sunderland home of the Lambton family.

Designated as a Grade II listed building in 1975, the pub closed in 1998 and spent years in alternative use before a £250,000 refurbishment brought the ground floor back into use as a pub in 2024.

Long before that, though, it was converted into a coaching inn and renamed the Bridge Hotel following the opening of the first Wearmouth Bridge in 1796.

The bridge was a major engineering achievement that provided a direct crossing over the River Wear.

Today, the Bridge Hotel Vaults proudly celebrates its heritage with wood panelling, historic tiled exteriors, and displays of Vaux memorabilia.
