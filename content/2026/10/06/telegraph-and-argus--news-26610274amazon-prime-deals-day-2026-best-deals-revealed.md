## Prime Day scams to watch out for

There is another reason to be cautious during the sale period: scammers know millions of shoppers will be expecting Amazon emails, order confirmations and delivery updates.

Consumer scam protection experts at TrendLife say 89.7% of the Amazon-related scam activity it detected in the UK around the previous Prime Day appeared shortly before the event began.

The organisation also warns that generative AI is making it easier for fraudsters to create convincing-looking websites and promotional material.

Some of the scams to watch for include:

* Fake delivery messages: Fraudsters may claim a parcel has been missed, needs a signature or requires an address to be updated.
* Urgent payment warnings: Messages may claim that a Prime payment has failed or that an account is about to be cancelled.
* Requests to reply to a text: Be particularly suspicious of messages asking you to reply with something such as "Y" before a link is activated.
* Fake Amazon websites: Fraudsters can reproduce familiar branding in an attempt to persuade shoppers to hand over login or payment details.

## How to stay safe

* The simplest rule is also one of the most useful: don't use a link in an unexpected email or text to access your Amazon account.
* Instead, open the Amazon app or type the website address into your browser yourself.
* The same applies to delivery messages. If you're expecting a parcel, check its progress through your Amazon account or the official website or app of the delivery company.
* And never hand over bank or card details simply because a message tells you that your account is at risk.

## The Prime Big Deal Days checklist

Before you press "buy", ask yourself:

### Do I actually need it?

A discount isn't a saving if you wouldn't have bought the item otherwise.

### Is it genuinely cheaper?

Check the price against other retailers and, where possible, its previous selling price.

### Am I buying from the right place?

For Amazon-related emails, payment warnings or delivery messages, go directly to the official Amazon website or app rather than clicking a link.

### Is the pressure to buy coming from me — or the website?

Countdowns and "limited stock" warnings are designed to encourage quick decisions. Take a moment before committing.

### Have I checked the small print?

Codes, membership requirements and promotional dates can affect the final price.

Prime Big Deal Days may offer plenty of opportunities to pick up things you genuinely need for less. But the best approach is to treat it as a shopping event, not a race: compare prices, ignore the hype and keep an eye out for scams.
