# DWP to pay out £192 weekly to basic state pension claimants

## The triple lock guarantee ensures that the State Pension rates increase based on the highest out of three key measures

State pensioners are poised to pocket an additional £7.20 each week from next April, thanks to the triple lock guarantee.

The triple lock ensures Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) State Pension rates rise in line with whichever is highest among three crucial measures: inflation, average wage growth, or 2.5 per cent. Predictions indicate next year's State Pension rates will be determined by average wage growth standing at 3.9 per cent, which exceeds both the 2.5 per cent minimum threshold and present inflation levels.

A 3.9 per cent uplift would push the full basic State Pension to £192.10 weekly, representing an annual increase of £374. Those claiming the full new State Pension could benefit from an extra £9.40 per week, taking their weekly payment to £250.70. Pensioners who depend entirely on their State Pension might find themselves liable for tax for the first time owing to the frozen personal tax allowance, although the government intends to avoid creating administrative hassle for those on minimal incomes, reports Birmingham Live.

Helen Morrissey, Head of Retirement Analysis at Hargreaves Lansdown, commented: "According to the ONS, average wage growth stood at 3.9 per cent. We may have just over a month to wait until the relevant inflation figure is published, but it currently stands at 2.9 per cent, so unless there's a real surge it seems likely that the average wage figure will be used.

"Such an increase would put someone on the full new state pension on course to receive £250.70 a week from next April – up from the current £241.30 per week. Someone on a full basic state pension would receive £192.10 a week - up from £184.90."

"This will be a welcome boost to pensioner incomes but even a full state pension is only ever going to cover the basics. If you want to live well in retirement, then you will need to take your pension planning into your own hands."

Labour Party cabinet member Torsten Bell said: "In line with the commitment made at Budget 2025, pensioners who only just exceed the personal allowance will not have the administrative burden of paying small amounts of tax in this Parliament. The Chancellor will set out further details on how that commitment will be delivered at the Budget."
