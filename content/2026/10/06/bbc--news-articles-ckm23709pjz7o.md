Lubna Speitan gave evidence on behalf of a British national who was accused of plotting to carry out a suicide attack at Heathrow Airport

**The Green Party member behind a motion which declared Zionism to be racism was named in court documents as a "close friend" of a man who was jailed after being accused of plotting a suicide bomb attack at Heathrow Airport.**

Lubna Speitan, a British-Palestinian artist, proposed the Green conference motion defining Zionism as an ideology supporting "an ethnonationalist Jewish state".

Court documents from 2015 show she offered to testify as a defence witness in the trial of Minh Quang Pham, who was jailed for 40 years in the US after pleading guilty to supporting al-Qaeda. He denied planning a suicide attack.

The BBC has contacted Speitan for comment. The Green Party said she holds no official positions and the party "takes all allegations of this nature seriously".

The motion proposed by Speitan, which was passed on Sunday to become party policy, called for a "single democratic Palestinian state in all of historic Palestine", which includes modern-day Israel.

In response, Israel banned Green leader Zack Polanski from entering the country. while Jewish groups described it as an "attack" on their community and some Labour and Lib Dem MPs reported the party to Great Britain's equality watchdog.

Speitan argued its backers wanted to voice opposition to "racist systems of oppression".

On Tuesday, it emerged she wrote a letter in 2016 to the New York judge in the Pham case describing him as "one of the kindest, honest, most sincere" people she had known.

The existence of the letter was first reported by the Jewish Chronicle, external and it has been seen by BBC News.

Pham, a Vietnamese-born British national who had lived in London, was arrested at Heathrow Airport in July 2011 after spending seven months in Yemen. He was found in possession of a live round of ammunition.

In 2015 he was extradited to the US and in 2016 sentenced to 40 years in prison after he admitted three counts based on his support for the group al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula (AQAP), an offshoot of al-Qaeda.

![A black and white mugshot of Minh Quang Pham.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/224/cpsprodpb/58d8/live/c2036500-c1c0-11f1-babe-4199b0e7ccea.jpg)

Minh Quang Pham was arrested at Heathrow in 2011 and later convicted in the US

US prosecutors said Pham was directed by al-Qaeda leader Anwar al-Awlaki to detonate explosives in Heathrow's arrivals area after spending several months in Yemen being trained by him.

In US court documents from 2015, Speitan was listed as a "close friend" of Pham who would provide "critical testimony regarding Pham's mental state".

The documents said that would be "crucial in establishing a mental health defense", adding that "bears on the issue of guilt".

Speitan had a medical condition that prevented her from giving evidence in person, the documents add.

In a later letter to the US District Judge, Speitan said Pham was one of the "few people I regard with such high respect and esteem".

She said if the judge "truly knew the Minh [Pham] that and others grew to love and respect", they may have "probably had the whole case dismissed".

She acknowledged he "may have had and developed some behavioural issues" but added: "He turned out to be one of the kindest, honest, most sincere, trustworthy and respectful people I have ever known."

There is no suggestion in the documents that Speitan knew of Pham's criminal activities at the time they knew each other.

Speitan has not responded to the BBC's request for comment. The Telegraph reported that when it asked her if she remained friends with Pham, she replied: "Yes, it's still the case."

She was reported as saying she believed Pham had been "targeted" by "the system".

A Green Party spokesperson said: "Where members have expressed views that do not fit in with the party's values, we have internal processes to investigate."

It came as Green leader Polanski grappled with division among the party's members, MPs and deputy leaders over the motion.

Some Green councillors have resigned since it was passed. Green deputy leader in Wales, Phil Davies, resigned and cancelled his membership on Tuesday, describing the motion as "the final nail in the coffin".

Polanski released a lengthy statement on Monday night to say he would not accept anyone being excluded from the party "because of the religious or communal body they are part of".

The party leader, who is Jewish, added that "antisemitism and criticism of the Israeli state are, and must always be, completely distinct".
