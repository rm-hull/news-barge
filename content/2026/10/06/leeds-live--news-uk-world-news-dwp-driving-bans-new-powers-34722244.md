# DWP driving bans as new powers to disqualify benefits claimants rolled out

## The DWP will gain new powers to issue driving bans to benefit claimants with debts over £1,000 from October 2026

The Department for Work and Pensions is preparing to introduce driving bans for benefit claimants from October 2026, following the introduction of sweeping new powers.

These measures form part of the Public Authorities (Fraud, Error and Recovery) Act 2025, designed to recover welfare debt and tackle fraudulent claims. In the most serious cases, courts can be called upon to revoke driving licences from persistent offenders where the outstanding debt stands at a minimum of £1,000.

Claimants can avoid losing their licence by getting in touch with the DWP within four months, with staff on hand to provide guidance and arrange manageable repayment plans. Crucially, nobody will face disqualification if they rely on their licence for essential purposes, such as employment or caring responsibilities, reports Birmingham Live.

Any driving ban that is imposed will initially remain suspended, provided repayment agreements are honoured. Labour's Work and Pensions Minister for Transformation, Andrew Western, said: "Hardworking taxpayers deserve a system that pursues those who deliberately dodge their debts, and that is exactly what these new powers deliver.

"To anyone with an outstanding debt - our door is open and DWP will always work with you to find an affordable way to repay. But for those who can pay and won't - we're going further than ever before to claw back cash and crack down on fraud."

Cabinet Office Minister Satvir Kaur said: "Fraud against the public sector and unrecovered debt deny our vital frontline services of the funding they deserve. Under these new powers in the PAFER Act, this Government will deliver on its promise to protect hardworking taxpayers and clamp down on those who try to cheat the system."

The DWP clarified: "Courts can only impose a driving ban where the debt is at least £1,000, and no one can be disqualified if they have an essential need for their licence, for example work that relies on driving, such as a courier or caring responsibilities."

They added: "Any ban is initially suspended as long as repayment terms are kept to."

The department further explained: "Other powers under the PAFER Act, which will be operational in future, include the Eligibility Verification Measure, which will allow DWP to require limited data held by banks and financial institutions to help identify incorrect benefit payments, ensuring claimants are paid accurately and allowing errors to be found and resolved sooner."
