# Previous fatal tiger attacks at UK zoos and wildlife parks following Yorkshire tragedy

## The death of a man following a tiger attack at Yorkshire Wildlife Park has brought renewed attention to previous fatal incidents involving the animals at zoos across Britain.

Several zookeepers have lost their lives in tiger attacks at UK wildlife parks over the past five decades, with some incidents prompting investigations into safety procedures and the handling of dangerous animals.

The latest tragedy happened on Saturday (Oct 10), when a man died after entering a tiger enclosure at Yorkshire Wildlife Park near Doncaster.

South Yorkshire Police said emergency services were called at around 1.35pm, but despite the efforts of medical personnel, the man was pronounced dead at the scene.

The tiger involved, a tiger named Altai, was unharmed, with the park confirming that the animal would not be put down.

Yorkshire Wildlife Park remained closed on Monday (Oct 12) while investigations into the incident continued.

Here are five documented previous fatal tiger attacks at zoos and wildlife parks across the UK.

## **Brian Stocks – Howletts Wild Animal Park, Kent (1980)**

Brian Stocks, 29, was killed by a Siberian tigress named Zeya at Howletts Wild Animal Park near Canterbury in August 1980.

The keeper was inside the animal's enclosure when the fatal attack occurred.

His death was the first of two fatal incidents involving the same tiger at the wildlife park within a matter of weeks.

## **Bob Wilson – Howletts Wild Animal Park, Kent (1980)**

Just a month later, on September 22, 1980, keeper Bob Wilson, 28, was killed by the same tiger.

He had been carrying out routine cleaning duties alongside two other keepers when the animal attacked.

Following the second death, Zeya was shot by the park's owner, John Aspinall.

The two fatalities prompted questions about safety procedures and the risks associated with allowing keepers into enclosures containing dangerous animals.

## **Trevor Smith – Howletts Wild Animal Park, Kent (1994)**

More than a decade later, another keeper was killed at Howletts Wild Animal Park.

Trevor Smith, 32, died on November 13, 1994, after being attacked by a Siberian tiger.

Mr Smith was cleaning a tiger enclosure when the animal attacked him, biting him on the neck.

An inquest in February 1995 recorded a verdict of accidental death.

Following the incident, Canterbury City Council took action to prevent keepers from entering tiger enclosures, although the prohibition was subsequently overturned on appeal.

## **Sarah McClay – South Lakes Wild Animal Park, Cumbria (2013)**

Sarah McClay, 24, died after being attacked by a Sumatran tiger at South Lakes Wild Animal Park in Dalton-in-Furness on May 24, 2013.

She had been carrying out cleaning and feeding duties when the tiger gained access to a corridor used by keepers through a door with a defective bolt.

Ms McClay was airlifted to hospital but died from her injuries.

In June 2016, the zoo's operating company was fined £255,000 after admitting health and safety breaches connected to her death.

The tiger was not put down following the attack but was later euthanised in 2016. Ms McClay's family had previously asked for the tiger to be spared.

## **Rosa King – Hamerton Zoo Park, Cambridgeshire (2017)**

Rosa King, 33, was killed by a Malayan tiger at Hamerton Zoo Park near Huntingdon on May 29, 2017.

The experienced keeper had been cleaning the enclosure's viewing windows before the park opened when the attack happened.

Her body was discovered by a visitor, who raised the alarm.

An inquest in July 2019 concluded that her death was accidental after hearing that a metal sliding barrier designed to separate the tiger from keepers had been left open.

A police investigation found no mechanical faults with the enclosure's gates or sliding barriers.

The tiger was not put down following the attack, a decision supported by Ms King's family.

All five historical fatalities involved people working with the animals, while the latest tragedy at Yorkshire Wildlife Park involved a member of the public.
