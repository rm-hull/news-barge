Drying laundry indoors during cold weather could leave households facing £3,000 repair bills, home insurance experts have warned.

Specialists are cautioning Britons that hanging wet clothes inside poses serious health risks and can create an ideal breeding ground for toxic mould.

As temperatures drop, drying washing outside becomes challenging, while reluctance to run tumble dryers amid high energy costs is driving many families to opt for indoor air drying.

Placing damp garments on radiators or heated drying racks to speed up the process is considered even riskier.

## **How to Dry Clothes Indoors During the Winter**

Consumer experts at Quotezone.co.uk say doing so triggers condensation, causes indoor air pollution, and drastically increases the likelihood of dampness developing.

Greg Wilson, founder and chief executive of Quotezone.co.uk, said: “Many people are completely unaware of the consequences of air drying clothes inside their homes.”

He said: “Using air dryers, radiators and heated drying racks indoors may seem like a good idea during the colder months, but it can pose a serious health risk.”

“Wet clothes release moisture into the home as they start to dry which can develop into mould and damp, causing mild allergies, aggravating asthma or worse.”

Mould exposure can trigger allergic reactions including sneezing, a runny nose, skin rashes and asthma attacks.

In more severe cases, prolonged exposure can lead to weight loss and even hospitalisation.

Aside from the medical ramifications, tackling mould and dampness can quickly turn into a costly nightmare for homeowners.

Once established, airborne spores migrate through building materials to contaminate floors, ceilings and walls across the property.

Households struggling with an infestation could end up spending more than £3,000 to successfully treat and eradicate the issue.

Crucially, families could also find their insurance cover invalidated if condensation damage is deemed preventable.

## **How to Get Rid of Mould in Your Home**

Mr Wilson warned: “If people need to make a claim on their home insurance due to mould or damp then the insurer is unlikely to cover them if the property is not well maintained or is damaged due to a lack of care.”

He noted: “Mould and damp are not just expensive issues to fix, they are also very dangerous and, in many cases, possible to avoid.”

“Drying clothes in the winter can feel like an impossible task, especially with the weather in the UK,” he said.

“Ideally, homeowners should download a weather app and keep an eye out for dry days, even if it’s cloudy, to help at least start drying the clothes outside.”

“If they do have to bring the clothes inside they could start them in the tumble dryer then finish them off on a drying rack - making sure the room is well ventilated, a dehumidifier can help to reduce moisture levels and promote airflow.”
