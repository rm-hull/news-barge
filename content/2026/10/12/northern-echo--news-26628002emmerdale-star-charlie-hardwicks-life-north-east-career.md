From coalfield dramas to soap stardom, Charlie Hardwick has built a career spanning more than four decades with the North East at its heart.

Born in Wallsend in 1960, Hardwick made her name in the North East through Byker Grove in the 1990s and the 1996 drama Our Friends in the North, alongside former Doctor Who star Christopher Eccleston.

Hardwick later starred in the critically acclaimed film The Scar, which portrayed women living with the aftermath of East Durham’s pit closures.

Charlie played May Murton, earning the Best Actress award at the 1998 Monte Carlo International Television Festival.

She then extended her North East film streak in 2000, acting in Newcastle-oriented comedy film Purely Belter and Billy Elliot.

Hardwicks most well-known role came in 2004, when she began playing Val Pollard in popular soap opera Emmerdale, a character she would portray until Val's death in 2015.

Hardwick's performance as Val Pollard earned her several nominations and one win at the British Soap Awards, along with widespread praise from fans.

Even after her departure, Hardwick returned to the show as Val for occasional ghost appearances.

She also played Sue Carp in Channel 4’s 2019 drama Ackley Bridge and appeared on BBC2's Robson Green’s Weekend Escape in 2023, which showcased a Tees Valley wellness choir alongside outdoor activities on Durham's east coast.

The stage remains important to Hardwick. In 2023, she played Fairy Bon Bon in Sunderland Empire’s Beauty and the Beast.

She told the Shields Gazette that working with her pantomime castmates was an "absolute delight," adding: "I do mainly television and film now, but my first love and I suspect my last love will be being on the stage."

The soap star's North East contributions go far beyond the screen and stage. Hardwick is a patron of the Bubble Foundation, which supports children with life-threatening immune conditions in the Great North Children's Hospital in Newcastle.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/18022177.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hardwick alongside her fellow Bubble Foundation patron Alexander Millar and founder Gill Wheeldon. *(Image: BUBBLE FOUNDATION)*

In 2016, Hardwick opened a £6m facility at North Tyneside General Hospital supporting older people with dementia and other mental health problems. She told the Chronicle:

"I'm also very proud to be one of the Alzheimer's Society's celebrity supporters, especially as this gives me the opportunity to play a part in highlighting the excellent work it does."

That year, Hardwick read writings by then-Poet Laureate Carol Ann Duffy at Durham Cathedral’s Durham Hymns event, marking the centenary of the Battle of the Somme.

Hardwick said her grandfather went to fight in the First World War aged 16 and became a police officer in Bedlington, Northumberland, after returning home.

While many still know her as Val Pollard, Charlie Hardwick’s story stretches far beyond soap stardom.

Through decades of acting, community work and advocacy, she has proudly represented the North East, leaving a legacy that continues to resonate both on and off the screen.
