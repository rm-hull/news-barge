One Teesside town has ranked among the worst places in England and Wales for burglary, new figures have revealed.

Middlesbrough recorded 6.06 burglaries per 1,000 daytime residents over the last year - making it the country’s third worst-hit area.

Only Kensington and Chelsea, at 6.68, and Leeds, at 6.4, recorded higher rates.

The figures come against a backdrop of recent burglary cases across Teesside, including one offender who targeted a business, a home and a garden shed during a two-week crime spree.

Wider Home Office figures also show that Cleveland Police recorded the highest overall crime rate among forces in England and Wales.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21358197.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Following the A66 tragedy in August, the Home Office revealed that wider figures show Cleveland Police has recording the highest overall crime rate in England and Wales. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Liz Hunter, home insurance expert at Money Expert, said: "October marks National Home Security Month, making it the perfect time for homeowners to review how secure their properties are and make simple changes to help protect their homes."

In light of the figures, Ms Hunter has shared key steps homeowners can take to reduce the risk of burglary, particularly when leaving their property unattended for extended periods.

She said: "Lock every door and window. It sounds obvious, but it's one of the easiest things to overlook when you're rushing to leave.

"Before you head off, do one final walk around your home and check that all doors, windows, garages, sheds and side gates are securely locked.

"If you have uPVC doors, remember to lift the handle and fully lock them with the key."

Making your home look occupied can also help deter burglars.

"An empty house can attract unwanted attention," Ms Hunter said.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/13918029.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Middlesbrough records third highest burglary rates in the UK. *(Image: Internet)*

She added: "Use timer plugs or smart lighting to switch lamps on during the evening, giving the impression that someone is home.

"A pile of parcels, newspapers or overflowing post is also a clear sign that the house is vacant.

"If you're away for more than a few days, pause regular deliveries and ask a trusted neighbour to move bins, collect post or park on your driveway occasionally."

She also advises homeowners to test security devices before travelling and hide valuables from view.

Ms Hunter said: "If you have a burglar alarm, video doorbell or security cameras, make sure they are working properly before you leave.

"Check batteries are charged, notifications are enabled and, if applicable, that you can access live footage remotely.

"Keep laptops, jewellery, car keys and other valuable items out of sight.

"It's also worth avoiding leaving packaging for expensive purchases where it can be seen from outside, especially if you've recently bought new technology."

Finally, Ms Hunter urges homeowners to check their insurance before travelling so they know what cover applies if their home is left empty.

She said: "Some insurers have conditions if a home is left empty for an extended period, so knowing the details before you travel can help avoid surprises later."

Nationally, police forces in England and Wales recorded an eight per cent decrease between 2025 and 2026.

For people in Middlesbrough, though, the latest figures highlight the importance of keeping homes secure.
