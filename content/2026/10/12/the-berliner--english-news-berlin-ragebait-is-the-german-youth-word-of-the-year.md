The ‘Jugendwort des Jahres’, or German Youth Word of the Year, is an annual campaign that documents the popularity of new slang expressions. On Saturday at the Frankfurt Book Fair, publisher PONS Langenscheidt announced ‘Ragebait’ as the 2026 winner. The word refers to content that is deliberately attempting to frustrate someone or cause outrage, typically online.

‘Ragebaiting’ gained popularity online on platforms such as Instagram, TikTok and X. A post or a comment that fuels outrage is more likely to perform well on social media channels as it generates emotional reactions and higher share counts.

The shortlist this year included ‘peak’ and ‘crashout’. Peak refers to the pinnacle of something; “That was peak”. Crashout refers to an emotional breakdown or losing your temper, often said with a touch of humour or hyperbole and originally comes from the English term “to crash out”.

All three terms are borrowed from English, demonstrating once again the language’s significant influence on German youth culture. Around 1 million young people, aged 11 to 20, have voted since August 28 for their favourite term. ‘Ragebait’ obtained 36.5% of the votes, followed by 33.8% for ‘Peak’ and 29.7% for ‘Crashout’.

“The close result shows that all three terms have a firm place in the vocabulary of generations Z and Alpha,” said Artur Raiser, marketing manager at PONS Langenscheidt, in a press statement.
