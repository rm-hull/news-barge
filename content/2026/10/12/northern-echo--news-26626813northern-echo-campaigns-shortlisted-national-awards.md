Two campaigns by The Northern Echo are in the running for a national journalism award celebrating the power of local newspapers to drive change.

Maya's Law and the TEWV NHS Foundation Trust Scandal, both driven by the Darlington newsroom, have been shortlisted for the News Media Association’s Making a Difference awards.

And it's a public vote - so we are calling on our readers to support us. You can vote here

These honours form part of the NMA’s annual Journalism Matters campaign, which highlights the influence of journalism in shaping society.

Theo Bamber, chief executive of the News Media Association, said: "Having sources of news and information has never been more important than it is today, yet government has too often failed to deliver the right interventions to create an ecosystem in which journalism can flourish.

"That has to change.

"Journalism Matters is a celebration of journalism’s vital role as part of our national infrastructure, and we call on politicians and policymakers to treat it accordingly.

"Journalism is not a luxury; it is a vital component of our democratic society."

Voting for the Making a Difference awards is now open to the public and will run for two weeks, closing at 5.30pm on October 23.

Winners will be revealed during a parliamentary reception on November 2, marking the start of this year’s Journalism Matters campaign.

Supporters of The Northern Echo’s campaigns, as well as readers across the country, can cast their votes online to help decide this year’s Making a Difference winners.

The full list of shortlisted campaigns is available on the News Media Association’s website, where voting is now live.

In addition to The Northern Echo’s entries, 19 other regional campaigns have been shortlisted, covering themes from healthcare injustices and legal reforms to community action and environmental concerns.

* The citation for Maya's Law campaign reads:

"When families raised concerns about avoidable deaths and serious incidents in the Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust, the initial response from authorities was slow, fragmented and often defensive.

"The Northern Echo’s investigations changed that dynamic. By systematically documenting individual cases, scrutinising internal reviews and highlighting patterns of failure across multiple deaths and wards, the paper built a compelling case that this was not a series of one‑offs but a structural crisis in mental health care.

"Crucially, the Echo did not simply publish families’ allegations; it followed each one through coroners’ inquests, regulatory findings and board papers, confronting the Trust’s leadership and national NHS bodies with the uncomfortable evidence.

"The coverage kept the story alive when institutional fatigue might otherwise have set in, ensuring that each new revelation added pressure rather than dissipating into the news cycle.

"It adds: " The decisive impact came when the Health Secretary announced a full public inquiry into the Trust – a rare, high‑level intervention in a single trust’s affairs.

"Families and their legal teams explicitly linked that decision to sustained media scrutiny, including the Echo’s coverage, which made it politically untenable to continue with piecemeal reviews.

"The fallout will hopefully continue the inquiry’s work: with executives compelled to give evidence under oath; systemic failings examined in public; and future mental health policy in the region will be shaped in the shadow of testimony that might never have been heard without this investigative pressure."

Other shortlisted regional campaigns include Child Killers Excluded From Early Prison Release Scheme (The Chronicle, Newcastle), Holly's Law (Lincolnshire Free Press), and Fair Deal For Hospices (Express and Star).

At the national level, the shortlist features Campaign For Democracy (The Telegraph), Killer Kitchens (The i Paper), and Secret Afghan Airlift (The Daily Mail).

This year’s Journalism Matters campaign will underline journalism’s role as an "economic enabler, a defence against corruption, and a pillar of national resilience", as highlighted in a recent UNESCO report.
