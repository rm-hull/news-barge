Nineteen workers at a Walmart Distribution Center in Lebanon, Tennessee, were reportedly transported to area hospitals following a suspected foodborne illness outbreak late last week.

Health and emergency authorities are investigating what caused the sudden outbreak of illnesses at the facility, located roughly 30 miles east of Nashville.

Emergency crews were first dispatched to the distribution center late Thursday night after receiving a report of “a female experiencing symptoms of a possible food-related illness,” as reported by ABC affiliate WKRN, citing the Wilson County Emergency Management Agency.

“Upon arrival, additional units were requested to assist,” the agency said in a statement shared Sunday. “Approximately 25 to 30 patients were triaged on scene, with 19 patients transported.”

Assistive support was provided by Mount Juliet EMS and Rutherford County EMS to manage the patients on site. Early accounts had indicated that up to 120 people felt sick and 30 were taken to hospitals, though official agency tallies later clarified the lower transport figures. Affected employees reported sudden nausea and vomiting stretching into Friday morning.

![A suspected foodborne illness outbreak at a Walmart distribution center in Lebanon, Tennessee, sent 19 employees to local hospitals late last week](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/12/19/39/GettyImages-2154744934.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The health and safety of our associates is a top priority,” Walmart said in a statement to WKRN on Sunday. “We’re working with local emergency management and health officials as they investigate.”

Public health officials have not yet confirmed the source responsible for the outbreak. It remains unclear whether those affected consumed food from an on-site cafeteria, a catered event or personal meals brought into the plant. Neither the Wilson County Health Department nor the Tennessee Department of Health had issued formal public statements as of Sunday evening.

The *Food Poison Journal*, published by the food safety law firm Marler Clark, reported that the sudden overnight onset of heavy vomiting is characteristic of toxins produced by bacteria such as* Staphylococcus aureus*or* Bacillus cereus*, or pathogens like norovirus.

The firm noted that confirming a source would require stool testing, dietary history interviews and laboratory testing of food samples, and advised any affected workers to seek medical care and notify local health officials.
