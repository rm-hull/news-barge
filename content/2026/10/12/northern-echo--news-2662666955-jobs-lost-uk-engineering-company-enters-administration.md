More than 50 jobs have been lost after an automotive engineering company collapsed into administration.

Pneuride specialised in advanced electronic control systems and air suspension components for the automotive industry, and employed 55 staff.

The company, established in July 2007, has faced significant financial challenges in recent months, enduring a sustained period of soft trading and mounting pressure on cash flow and profit margins.

## Pneuride closes after entering administration with 55 jobs lost

As a result of these financial challenges, Pneuride ceased trading in early September after nearly 20 years in business.

Richard Harrison and Chris Pole of Interpath were then appointed joint administrators on September 30.

Mr Harrison, managing director at Interpath and joint administrator, said: "The automotive sector continues to undergo significant transformation and adapting to such change – amidst significant competition – adds further pressure on businesses in the supply chain that are already struggling to maintain margins and profitability.

"Pneuride was no exception and couldn’t continue in its current form.

"As we see through an orderly wind down of operations, we’re focused on supporting employees through this difficult period."

Most staff have been made redundant, though a small number have been retained to help with winding down the business.

The directors had explored a solvent solution, including marketing the business for sale, but with no viable offers or investment, administration was deemed the only option.

Director at Interpath, James Portwood, said: "Anyone with an interest in acquiring the business and assets should contact the Interpath team as soon as possible."
