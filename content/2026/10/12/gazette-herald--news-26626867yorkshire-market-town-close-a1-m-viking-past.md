Just a couple of miles from one of northern England’s busiest roads lies a historic market town where life moves more slowly.

Bedale lies near the A1(M) at Leeming Bar, but its cobbled marketplace, independent shops and old buildings feel far removed from the traffic.

Known as the “Gateway to Wensleydale”, the North Yorkshire town has long been linked to farming and the countryside.

Tourism now plays a growing role as visitors discover Bedale’s 1,000-year history and the Yorkshire Dales on its doorstep.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21543382.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bedale Market cross *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Bedale is mentioned in the Domesday Book, although its roots stretch back even further.

The Bedale Hoard underlined the town’s importance during the Anglo-Saxon and Viking periods.

The silver and gold objects offered a glimpse of life here more than 1,000 years ago and pointed to wealthy Viking activity around the town.

At the town’s heart, St Gregory’s Church has an imposing tower that once served more than a religious purpose.

The fortified tower offered refuge during raids from Scotland, making the church both a place of worship and defence.

Inside, a rare grave marker offers another link to the area’s Viking past.

Visitors can also see St George slaying the dragon, unusually shown fighting left-handed.

A short walk away, the Grade I-listed Market Cross recalls Bedale’s past as an important trading centre.

Georgian façades and older buildings give the marketplace much of its character.

Bedale Hall and its grounds add another historic landmark to the town centre.

Not all of Bedale’s attractions date back hundreds of years.

Bedale also has a long railway history.

Bedale station is part of the Wensleydale Railway, which resumed passenger services in 2003.

Trains run through the countryside to Leyburn, giving visitors another way to see North Yorkshire.

Although the station is near Aiskew, the railway remains closely linked to Bedale and is now part of its tourism appeal.

![Bedale town centre](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21543384.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bedale town centre *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Walkers can follow paths alongside Bedale Beck.

Bedale Beck runs through the countryside towards the River Swale, putting open fields and rural walks within easy reach of the marketplace.

Bedale has retained something many small towns have lost.

Independent shops, cafés and pubs still cluster around the centre instead of national chains dominating the high street.

The result is a lively marketplace where visitors can still explore at a relaxed pace.

Several popular attractions lie just beyond Bedale too.

Nearby, Thorp Perrow Arboretum and Big Sheep Little Cow Farm draw families and other visitors.

Alongside the railway, historic buildings and countryside, they make Bedale more than a place to pass on the way to the Yorkshire Dales.

To motorists on the A1(M), Bedale can seem little more than a name on a road sign.

Leave the motorway behind and a very different place awaits just minutes away.

With its Viking links, fortified church, heritage railway and traditional marketplace, the “Gateway to Wensleydale” is worth stopping for.
