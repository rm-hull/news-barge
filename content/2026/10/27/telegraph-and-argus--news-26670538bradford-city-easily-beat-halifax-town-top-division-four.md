BRADFORD City were into their fifth successive season in Division Four by October 1976, with Bobby Kennedy and his players keen to end that misery.

The Bantams had finished down in 17th a few months earlier, but a brilliant run to the FA Cup quarter-final, where they were narrowly knocked out by eventual winners Southampton, gave them a platform to build on.

And they were riding high on this day 50 years ago, knowing a draw in their West Yorkshire derby with visiting Halifax Town that night would put them top of the table.

Our Bantams correspondent at the time, David Markham, warned that Halifax were coming to Valley Parade with a decent away record.

But as it turned out, neither he, nor the City supporters, had any reason for concern.

The free-scoring hosts were far too good for their visitors, as they romped to a 3-0 victory.

The Bantams were not especially tight at the back that season, but that was suddenly less important than it had been for the previous 90 years, as goal average had been replaced by goal difference for the first time.

After scraping past Workington two days on from the evisceration of Halifax, City hit a little bump in the road, winning just twice more in the league before the turn of the year.

But two runs of four victories in a row, one in late February and early March, with the other in April, kept them firmly in promotion contention.

And the job was done for City on May 7, as a 1-1 draw at home to Bournemouth confirmed they were heading back to the third tier.
