An Edinburgh bakery has been named the winner of this year's Scotland's Best Croissant competition.

Organised by The Edinburgh Butter Company, the annual event took place at The Balmoral Hotel in Edinburgh on Monday, October 26, drawing 17 entrants from across the country.

This included The Palmerston and Peregrine in Edinburgh; Birch Bakery, with sites in both Inverness and Skye; Eusebi Bakery and Outlier in Glasgow; and The Fat Batard in the Scottish Borders.

Each bakery presented six plain croissants to a panel of expert judges, including Cassie Bekas, last year’s winner and owner of Wheatberry Bakery; Darcie Maher, owner of Lannan Bakery; and Zoe Ridgway, head pastry chef at The Balmoral.

Entries were judged on appearance, including uniformity, colour and shape, as well as lamination skills, texture and flavour.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637201.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The first place trophy created by O&Co Blacksmiths *(Image: Edinburgh Butter Company)*

Taking first place was Patina, a sustainable bakery located at Airborne Place in Edinburgh, which received a specially commissioned trophy by O&Co Blacksmiths.

Clothilde Vadin, assistant head baker at Patina, said: “We’re really happy with Patina’s progression in the competition, having placed 3rd and 2nd in previous years.

"That’s all due to our head baker Linda; she really brings the team together and brings that level of excellence every single day.”

Linda Vittoni, head baker at Patina, added: “I want to say a huge thank you to all of my team, you are amazing.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637204.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scotland's Best Croissant\_winners Patina with judges. L-R (Zoe Ridgway, Chloe Black, Linda Vittoni, Clothilde Vadin, Cassie Bekas) *(Image: Edinburgh Butter Company)*

Past winners Maple Leaf Bakery took second place, whilst Kaf placed third.

New for this year’s competition, there was also a separate judging category for the best bake made entirely from the off-cuts of the lamination process. All entrants were invited to submit a second item, with no restrictions on flavour or decoration, offering an opportunity to showcase creativity beyond the traditional croissant.

Placing first for their pecan, date and sea salt twist, Ayrshire-based Buns & Crumbs received a gift box from Table Chocolate. Sloe Lane took second place, whilst Patina placed third.

Winner of the off-cuts competition Erika Sionis, founder of Buns & Crumbs, said: “We were eager to enter the competition today as a chance to meet more people.

"We’re so glad to be here and to take home a prize, it’s been a great chance to network, too.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637202.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Each bakery presented six plain croissants to judges *(Image: Edinburgh Butter Company)*

Chloe Black, director of The Edinburgh Butter Company, added: “Every year this competition gets bigger and better, and the level of skill and passion from these bakers never ceases to amaze us.

"It’s so great to see Patina win. They placed 3rd in 2024, 2nd in 2025, and then this year came out on top. It's very well deserved.

“Having the additional off-cuts competition this year was another way for these bakers to showcase their creativity.

"Well done to everyone who took part.

"The pastries really were exceptional.”
