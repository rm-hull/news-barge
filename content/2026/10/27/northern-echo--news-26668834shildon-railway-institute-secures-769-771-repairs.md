A historic railway institute at the heart of County Durham life for almost 200 years is set for vital repairs after securing nearly £770,000 in funding.

Shildon Railway Institute has been awarded £769,771 for urgent work to its Grade II-listed home, with £617,905 coming from Historic England's Heritage at Risk Capital Fund and £151,866 from Durham County Council's Towns and Villages Investment Programme.

Dave Reynolds, chair of the institute, said: "When our founders established the Institute in 1833, they hoped it would bring benefit to 'generations as yet unborn'.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21629516.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Major investment will help protect Shildon Railway Institute for generations to come *(Image: Supplied)*

"Nearly 200 years later, we remain committed to that promise. This extraordinary support from Historic England and Durham County Council is about much more than repairing a historic building.

"It is a tremendous vote of confidence in our community, our heritage and our determination to ensure the Institute continues to enrich lives for generations to come."

The first phase of a wider restoration project will tackle critical repairs above the roofline, preventing further damage and securing the building’s future.

The funding follows more than seven years of planning by the institute’s management committee, members, volunteers, stakeholders and advisers.

Iona Howell of Mosedale Gillatt Architects, lead architect on the project, said: "Having worked closely with Shildon Railway Institute over the past year on the future of the building, we’re delighted to hear that they have been successful in gaining funding for these much needed roof works.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21629528.jpg?type=mds-article-620)he Grade II-listed Shildon Railway Institute is set for vital restoration work *(Image: Supplied)*

"By addressing these urgent repairs, we are not only preserving a valued historic building but also protecting a place that remains at the heart of the community.

"We look forward to working with them to deliver this project over the next 12 months."

Founded in 1833, the institute still serves Shildon’s people nearly 200 years later.

It remains a centre for social, cultural and community activities, and the repairs will help keep it open to future generations.

The funding is part of a wider plan to secure the building’s future, with more restoration planned once it is stabilised.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21629527.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Grade II-listed Shildon Railway Institute is set for vital restoration work *(Image: Supplied)*

Louise Brennan, director of regions at Historic England, said: "Historic buildings are an important part of what gives places across England their character and identity, and they can be powerful assets for the communities around them.

"By investing in buildings at risk, we have an opportunity not only to protect important heritage, but to unlock their potential, breathe new life into historic places and showcase what makes communities distinctive.

"This funding will support urgent conservation needs while helping projects move towards sustainable futures, whether through continued use, new uses or bringing valued buildings back into active use. We are pleased to support projects across England that demonstrate how investment in heritage can help create stronger, more distinctive and more vibrant places for the future."

Councillor Karen Allison, Durham County Council’s cabinet member for leisure, tourism, regeneration and high street, said: "We are delighted to be working with Shildon Railway Institute, alongside Historic England, to secure the future of this beautiful, and historic building.

"This demonstrates why the Towns and Villages Programme is so important, and the benefits and regeneration it can bring to communities in the county.

"We are determined to continue protecting the heritage and history of our towns and villages however we can."
