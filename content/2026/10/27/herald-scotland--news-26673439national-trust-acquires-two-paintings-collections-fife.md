The National Trust for Scotland has acquired two paintings for its collections in Fife at Hill of Tarvit Mansion and Garden and Kellie Castle as they now undergo vital conservation to protect them for the future.

‘A Hopeful Forecast,’ a painting by Scottish artist Robert Gemmell Hutchison, depicts women’s golf in the early 20th century and will go on display at Hill of Tarvit, home of Kingarrock Hickory Golf. It was purchased at auction with support from Art Fund.

‘The Birthday Party’ is a painting by John Henry Lorimer, whose family lived at Kellie Castle, and his piece depicts a child’s birthday party set in the Earl’s Room there. It was acquired at auction through the Trust’s Restricted Collections Fund.

Hutchison’s early 1920s oil on canvas painting features a young female golfer checking a barometer in the hope of good weather before her round. The Trust has brought the work into its collection to enhance the story of the Sharp family, who lived in Hill of Tarvit for 40 years between 1908 and 1948 and who created their own personal golf course in the 1920s.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21635871.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Robert Gemmell Hutchison RSA RBA ROI RSW (British, 1855-1936) A hopeful forecast *(Image: Bonhams)*

Lorimer’s painting shows the Earl’s Room as a stage for a party with lanterns strung up in front of the window. It captures the celebrated light and architecture of the rooms and presents a fascinating look at Lorimer family life. Kellie Castle features extensively in Lorimer’s art and often appears as a setting for scenes real or imagined.

Both paintings now require essential conservation to preserve them for future generations to enjoy. This will include cleaning to remove engrained dirt and discoloured varnish layers; consolidation to re-adhere lifting or flaking paint layers; and stabilisation to address any issues with the frame or repair any historic damage to the canvas.

Sileas Wood, the National Trust for Scotland’s Curator for Edinburgh & East, said: “The Trust is here to care for and share what is special about Scotland’s heritage for everyone. These paintings help us to tell an important part of the stories at Hill of Tarvit and Kellie Castle respectively, giving us a deeper understanding of what life may have been like in these places in times gone by.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21635887.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hill of Tarvit Mansion & Garden *(Image: National Trust for Scotland)*

“The Hutchison painting for Hill of Tarvit provides an opportunity to speak about women’s golf and leisure pastimes in the first half of the 20th century. Hill of Tarvit has a rich history of sport and leisure connected to the Sharp family, and through the invited guests and local players who enjoyed the golf course. This painting shows a 1920s golfer holding a set of Hickory clubs, exactly like those still in use at Kingarrock. This artwork is a great way to explore the stories of sport, leisure, and life at Tarvit.

“The painting by Lorimer for Kellie Castle is a wonderful addition to our collection at this special place, telling the remarkable story of Kellie and the family that called it home. Lorimer was only 18 when his parents took on a lease at Kellie in 1878 and it became an important home to him and his family, including his brother the architect Robert Lorimer. Already well represented at Kellie, Lorimer’s works reflect both the significant impact of the family on the site, and the lasting impact Kellie had on the artist. This painting shows a playful side to Kellie as the setting of a children’s birthday party, although it’s an imagined scene it reflects the Lorimer family’s long links to Kellie as a family home.

“We hope that the paintings will be enjoyed by visitors to these iconic Fife locations. It is with enormous thanks to Art Fund that we have been able to acquire ‘A Hopeful Forecast’ and we are grateful for this support. We’re also incredibly thankful to our members, donors, and visitors who support our vital conservation efforts as we protect these precious paintings for generations to come.”

Jenny Waldman, Director, Art Fund, said: “Robert Gemmell Hutchison’s A Hopeful Forecast offers a fascinating glimpse into the history of women’s golf in 20th century Scotland. We’re delighted to have helped bring the painting into the public collection at Hill of Tarvit, thanks to our National Art Pass members and supporters, whose generosity helps museums acquire and share remarkable art for everyone to enjoy.”

To support the Trust’s conservation of the artworks, visit [https://www.justgiving.com/campaign/fife-painting-conservation.](https://www.justgiving.com/campaign/fife-painting-conservation.)

Following conservation, the paintings will be on display from spring 2027.
