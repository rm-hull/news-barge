Carers could see a major change to Carers’ Allowance as the Government moves to end a controversial earnings rule that can strip people of their entire benefit for earning just a few pence too much.

Chancellor John Healey is expected to replace the current cliff edge with a taper, meaning payments would fall by £1 for every extra £1 earned above the earnings limit, rather than support being withdrawn suddenly.

The change is part of a package of cost-of-living measures announced by Prime Minister Andy Burnham on the eve of Wednesday’s Budget.

The Government has promised a series of “everyday fixes” aimed at easing pressure on household finances, including changes to mileage allowances, cheaper food imports and expanded parental pay.

## Carers’ Allowance rule set for major change

The current Carers’ Allowance earnings limit has been criticised because carers can lose their entire benefit if their earnings exceed the threshold, even by a small amount.

Campaigners have described the sudden loss of support as a scandal, with the rule leaving some unpaid carers facing a sharp financial hit after taking on extra work.

Under the expected change, the benefit would instead be reduced gradually by £1 for every additional pound earned, avoiding the immediate loss of the entire payment when the limit is breached.

The announcement will be closely watched by unpaid carers who rely on the benefit while balancing caring responsibilities with paid work.

## More cost-of-living changes expected at Budget

The Government has also outlined a series of other measures intended to help households and businesses.

These include:

* **Higher mileage allowance:** Making the 55p-a-mile tax-free mileage allowance permanent.
* **Cheaper food:** Cutting tariffs on popular products including chocolate, beans and gherkins.
* **Lower costs for farmers:** Tariff changes are also intended to reduce the price of fertiliser.
* **Heating costs:** The measures include reducing tariffs on kerosene used by some households for heating.
* **Parental pay:** Extending support to more than 150,000 parents each year, including self-employed fathers who previously missed out.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said: “We are taking on the big challenges facing the country but that doesn’t mean losing sight of the everyday problems that cost people money or simply aren’t fair.

“If you’re a nurse driving for work, a parent spending those precious first weeks with your new baby, or an unpaid carer picking up an extra shift, these changes will make life a little easier and put more money back in your pocket.”

## Minimum wage and fuel duty changes reported

Further Budget announcements have been reported by national newspapers.

The Times reported that the national living wage is expected to rise by nearly 4%, from £12.71 to £13.20 an hour.

The newspaper also reported that the Chancellor is expected to end the practice of increasing the minimum wage for workers aged 18 to 20 by more than the rate for older workers. Instead, their minimum wage would rise at roughly the same rate as the national living wage.

The Sun reported that Mr Healey will freeze fuel duty at 52p per litre, despite plans to end the freeze for the first time in 16 years. The newspaper said concerns about the impact of the Iran war on motorists were behind the reported decision.

These measures have been reported ahead of the Budget and should be distinguished from policies formally confirmed by the Government.

## £432million boost to tackle homelessness

The Budget is also expected to include £432million to help ensure homeless people are brought off the streets by Christmas.

The funding was disclosed on Monday, with Mr Burnham saying he wanted to make “every single person” sleeping on the streets an offer of help by the end of the year.

The Prime Minister, who is in his 100th day in office, has already announced measures including a cut to VAT on energy bills this winter and lower business rates for pubs and clubs.

He said the Government would “go further to give families and businesses the breathing space they need” in Wednesday’s Budget.
