A murderer serving a life sentence made a bid for freedom at a North Yorkshire hospital, York Crown Court heard.

Sean Matson gave his prison escort no reason for concern as they waited for more than three hours for him to be seen by medical staff at Harrogate District Hospital, said Sarah Margree, prosecuting.

But then he said “See you”, slipped the handcuffs that were attaching him to a prison officer and ran off.

The two prison staff with him chased after him down a hospital corridor and caught up with him at a staircase.

He later told police officers he was trying to get out of the building, but after that had nowhere to go.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21631417.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Murderer Sean Matson as he was when he was arrested 23 years ago *(Image: Police)*

Matson was 20 when he was jailed in 2003 for killing 17-year-old Chris Cave.

He is now 43, has spent more than 20 years behind bars and has had at least one application for parole rejected.

Since the escape he has been moved to HMP Long Lartin, a high security prison in Worcestershire.

At the time of the escape, he was serving at a jail near Harrogate.

He appeared before York Crown Court via a video link from his current prison.

When asked his plea to the charge of escape from lawful custody, he said, “Guilty, guilty, guilty, guilty.”

He did not have a barrister representing him and said he didn’t need one.

He did not give any mitigation and was given a six-month prison sentence, to be served at the same time as his life sentence.

Ms Margree said Sean Matson self-harmed in prison by injuring his ears and prison staff decided he needed to go to hospital for treatment.

They escorted him to the hospital in single cuffs with him attached to one of the escort at all times.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21631520.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Harrogate District Hospital *(Image: Google Street View)*

The staff member had thin wrists and was using an “insert” to make the handcuffs secure.

Matson had to wait for more than three hours to be seen by medical staff and twice he was given water.

Then he said he wanted some fresh air and he and the escort moved towards the entrance to the room where they were.

“He spontaneously, it would seem, said ‘See you’, slipped off the cuff and ran off down the corridor,” said Ms Margree.

After he was caught, he was double cuffed and extra security measures were taken until he was back in prison.

In 2003, Teesside Crown Court heard that Matson, then of Redcar, killed Mr Cave with a knife after drinking six litres of cider and vodka. He had also taken diazepam.

The then Recorder of Middlesbrough, Judge Peter Fox, said that Matson had violently and repeatedly stabbed the teenager to death "without the slightest provocation or any possible acceptable excuse".

He told the defendant: "You should know that although it is said with force that you might not have committed this dreadful crime if you had not been affected by drink and drugs, that provides you with no excuse whatever. Neither does it reduce your responsibility for the killing."

Following her son’s death, Mr Cave’s mother Theresa created the Chris Cave Foundation as part of her campaign against violence with knives and guns.
