Sandie Peggie's legal team is seeking copies of the documents NHS Fife has released to The Herald about its controversial employment tribunal statement, saying they are "highly relevant" to her ongoing legal claim.

Internal emails were released on Monday showing that the then chief executive, Carol Potter, added her own wording to statement and weighed in on individual sentences before it was published.

They also showed that a lawyer advising the board raised doubts about its opening section.

They also reveal that David Miller, NHS Fife’s director of people and culture, was asked to check the draft for inaccuracies and said he was ‘slightly uncomfortable’ about mentioning the disciplinary process in a media statement, though he added it was ‘factually correct’.

Ms Peggie’s legal team has lodged a separate claim against Mr Miller over an alleged failure since June 2025 to give her assurances on access to female changing rooms.”

Margaret Gribbon, Ms Peggie's solicitor, said: "Fife Health Board's (FHB) media statement issued on 18th July 2025 is the subject of live legal proceedings before the employment tribunal.

"These proceedings, raised in November 2025, assert that in authorising the media statement both FHB and their former CEO, Carol Potter, unlawfully victimised Sandie Peggie.

"The legal team is taking steps to obtain copies of the large volume of responses FHB has provided to journalists' FOI requests as they appear highly relevant to the ongoing legal claim.

"We are grateful to those members of the media who persisted in pursuing these information requests."

The 1,700-word statement was issued on 18 July last year, while Ms Peggie's employment tribunal was under way in Dundee. It sought to address what the board described as "misinformation" surrounding the case.

Soon after it went online it was criticised by the Scottish Information Commissioner. Last year, The Herald revealed that the fallout from the statement led to John Swinney personally requesting a briefing from NHS Fife.

It appeared to link threats of violence against staff to Sex Matters, the campaign group backing her. A section on the group was later removed, and a clarification said NHS Fife was not suggesting anyone involved with Sex Matters had contributed to the behaviour described.

Maya Forstater, chief executive of the charity, said: "NHS Fife's behaviour towards Sandie Peggie and to Sex Matters last year was extraordinary, starting with blustering accusations linking us to threats of violence which were gradually and clumsily withdrawn, without any apology or explanation.

"This case has been about NHS Fife's misunderstanding of the Equality Act, its seeming ignorance of the Workplace Regulations, and its treatment of Sandie, but also about the need for transparency and open justice.

"As soon as NHS Fife took the decision to allow a male doctor to pose as a woman and to force other staff to pretend he was a woman it was plunged into a web of deception and opacity.

"NHS Fife's statement suggests that NHS Fife took an activist approach rather than focusing on legal compliance. Lashing out at Sex Matters was a particular low point of the proceedings, which then led to a further 15-month battle to try to keep the decision-making process around that secret."

The emails were handed over after the Scottish Information Commissioner, David Hamilton, ruled in a case brought by The Courier that NHS Fife had wrongly withheld the records and had not even searched for them.

The Herald made its own request and the heavily redacted documents were released this week.

The emails show the statement went through around eight drafts.

They show how it evolved from a defensive explainer into something more combative. In an early draft cover note on 2 July, a communications officer said two points were worth keeping in because they were "newsworthy" and had not "been discussed publicly as of yet". One was the threats made against staff. The other was the claimant's refusal to enter mediation.

The statement said the threat had "required the involvement of Police Scotland".

An email sent to Police Scotland's press desk at 1.54pm that day gave advance notice that its officers might be asked to verify this. It said the staff member involved had been in touch with an officer, and that: "I understand [redacted] wasn't given a crime reference number."

Later that afternoon, the board drafted a line for journalists asking about the police response. It said that "no formal crime was recorded", but that Police Scotland "reviewed the material shared and took precautionary steps, including placing an alert on the individual's home address as a safeguarding measure".

When The Herald asked the police for a comment at the time, a spokesperson said: "We have received information and it is being assessed."
