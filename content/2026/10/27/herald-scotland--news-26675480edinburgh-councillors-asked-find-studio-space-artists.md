**Could Edinburgh’s displaced artists and makers find permanent homes in council-run facilities?**

SNP councillor Finlay McFarlane wants to find out.

A motion lodged by the city centre representative asks council officers to work closely with tenants at St Margaret’s House in Meadowbank, who have been told they must vacate the building by January 2027.

If approved, officials would conduct a review of the council’s estate and identify sites which could be used as “sustainable and equitable studio space for small and medium creative organisations”.

Council leader Jane Meagher would also write to Caledonian Trust, the owners of St Margaret’s House, asking them for an extension to allow artists and makers to secure new space.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21516645.jpg?type=mds-article-620)St Margaret's House in Meadowbank. *(Image: City of Edinburgh Council planning portal)*

“I am calling on the council to recognise the collective importance of artists, makers and creative businesses and to do what we can to safeguard and promote opportunity and equity of access to affordable studio spaces,” Councillor McFarlane told The Herald.

“Our global reputation as a city of culture is created on the backs of many small creative enterprises who for the last few years have seen a sustained decline in available and affordable space to work in the name of private commercial development.

“Were this a trend in any other economic sector, alarm bells would be ringing, strategies drafted and interventions made. My council motion is hopefully the first step in creating a parity of esteem for the creative workforce in the city.

“It is crucial that while we are limited in the concerns of private sites and developers the council starts thinking strategically about the sector, availability of opportunity and equitable access to the spaces that allow these businesses to flourish and continue supporting our global reputation, our shared artisanal heritage and skills, and circular economy”, Councillor McFarlane added.

Last month, Scot-Art, which lets St Margaret’s House from Caledonian Trust, gave notice to more than 200 tenants, sparking concerns from artists and community groups.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21568852.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Part of Summerhall could be turned into a hotel or flats. *(Image: Cat Thomson Photography)*

At the time, the owners told The Herald that they were in discussions with a 'preferred party' about purchasing the site.

“Scot-ART has held the lease on the building for many years, on very generous terms, and is now serving notice to its sub-tenants," a spokesperson said.

"Vacant possession is targeted for January 2027, with plenty of time for people to plan ahead.”

Meanwhile, property firm AMA is keen to redevelop part of popular arts venue Summerhall for commercial use.

More than 100 artists and other small businesses risk being turfed out during the construction process, and many fear they will not be able to return.

The property firm wants to turn several buildings in the historic complex, which has served as an arts and culture space since 2011, into flats and a hotel.

“At this stage we're not prescribing what goes where and what uses,” Dr Ali Afshar, managing director of AMA, told The Herald earlier this month.

“What we are saying is in order to pay for the art facility, you need to generate money from somewhere else.

“And then hopefully, if you put the right uses into what you produce, that will help to make this a place where people visit, stay, and look at art.”

A similar motion to Councillor McFarlane's, urging support for St Margaret's House tenants, has been put forward by Leith Walk councillor Jack Caldwell.

“The charities and small businesses at St Margaret's House play a massive role from carer support to independent arts," Councillor Caldwell told The Herald earlier this month.

“As such, the council can absolutely play an active part such as signposting other studio space, or speaking directly with providers such as Scot-Art about future opportunities both on the site and across the city to meet the high demand of affordable studio and office space.”
