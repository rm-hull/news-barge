BRADFORD (Park Avenue) have not been a Football League club for nearly 60 years.

But now you can play as them, or City strictly speaking, in a brand-new computer game that takes you back to 1959.

‘The Gaffer’ comes out on Steam this Friday (October 30) and has been produced by Pitchwork Studios Ltd.

It is a football management game set in English football from 1959 to 1989, drawn in pixel art like the home computer games of the 1980s.

Every career starts in the Fourth Division in August 1959, Avenue spending a second successive season there in real life after being relegated in 1958.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632243.jpg?type=mds-article-620)'Bradford' celebrate after being crowned as Division Four champions. *(Image: Pitchwork Studios)*

Readers can take charge of Avenue and manage them through the next 30 seasons, bringing players through from academy prospects to greats of the game.

Every town in ‘The Gaffer’ has its own researched local history, and the local newspaper is part of the story too.

On your first day as manager, its reporter is already waiting on the doorstep.

With the T&A involved in game, we felt it was important to bring you some clarifications.

The players and teams are not licensed, only real towns and cities, so technically Bradford is used as a placeholder team name, meaning you could be Avenue or City, depending on what takes your fancy.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632245.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Each city has just the one club, hence the Bradford name being used as opposed to City or Avenue. *(Image: Pitchwork Studios)*

Given Bradford is the name historically used to refer to Avenue though, some Bantams fans might baulk at that idea.

Those particularly knowledgeable about football in Bradford will also note that City were in the Third Division in August 1959.

But you start in Division Four regardless of which of the 92 teams you select, even if that is Wolverhampton, reigning champions of England at the time.

The game involves people from the city too, from businessmen, to fans down the pub, and local councillors.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632244.jpg?type=mds-article-620)You can make improvements to your club by unlocking certain achievements. *(Image: Pitchwork Studios)*

And in a bid to add even more authenticity, the rules of the game adapt as they did in real life, so substitutes are introduced in 1965, three points for a win in 1981, while there are even 12 clubs below the Football League fighting to be elected.

The board may lose patience with you over your three decades, but even if you are let go, you can take charge of another club.

A more detailed description of the game can be found on the Steam website.
