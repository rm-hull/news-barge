*(Image: NYC)*

People across North Yorkshire are being urged to help shape the county’s future in a consultation on its 20-year planning strategy.

North Yorkshire Council launched a six-week consultation on Monday, October 26, as it develops a countywide plan under the Government’s updated planning system.

The plan will guide housing, jobs, infrastructure and environmental protection until 2045, replacing plans drawn up by the former district and borough councils.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21631732.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The consultation invites views on what the local plan should contain and how North Yorkshire Council should engage with residents, businesses and organisations throughout its development *(Image: NYC)*

Cllr Mark Crane, deputy leader of North Yorkshire Council and executive member for open to business, said: "The local plan will play a crucial role in setting out where we feel planned, coordinated and sustainable development should take place across North Yorkshire for years to come.

"It will influence where new homes, jobs and services are located, how we protect our environment, support our economy and create thriving, sustainable communities.

"We are at an early stage in preparing the plan, and this consultation provides an important opportunity for people to tell us what issues they think it should address and how they would like to be involved as it progresses."

The council wants residents, businesses, community groups and other organisations to help shape the plan.

It is the first of three formal consultations, with two more to follow as the plan develops.

The planning system introduced by the Government in March puts more emphasis on online engagement, accessibility and standardised data.

A 2025 “first conversation” consultation gathered views on the plan’s key issues and options.

The council must restart the process under the revised national system, but will still use feedback from the earlier consultation.

A spokesperson for North Yorkshire Council said: "We want to hear from as many people as possible because their views will help shape the plan and ensure it reflects the needs and aspirations of communities across North Yorkshire."

The consultation will shape the plan’s content and how the council engages with the public.

It will also consider how to balance growth with protecting landscapes, heritage and the environment.

After the consultation closes on Monday, December 7, the council will publish a summary of the feedback.

The feedback will shape the next stage of the plan.

The council expects to pass the first formal “gateway” in April 2027 and adopt the completed plan by the end of 2029.

Residents and organisations can find information and submit their views at www.northyorks.gov.uk/localplan.

The council says wide participation is vital to ensure the final plan meets communities’ needs across the county.
