**Emergency services and local authorities responded to more than 1,500 calls for help as storms Austen and Boelo brought flooding, fallen trees and dangerous structures to communities across North Yorkshire over the weekend.**

Scarborough was among the first areas affected, experiencing torrential rain on Friday evening that overwhelmed drainage systems and flooded residential streets.

At its peak, rainfall was equivalent to three inches per hour, with properties on the Esplanade, Falsgrave Road, St Johns Road, Westwood Road and Westbourne Grove inundated. Several cars were stranded in flood water.

Whitby and Harrogate were also among the areas most affected, while debris was reported on roads across the county.

North Yorkshire Council’s highways teams dealt with almost 200 calls between Friday and Monday morning, including requests for sandbags and reports of fallen trees, some of which were blocking roads.

The multi-agency response was coordinated through the North Yorkshire Local Resilience Forum, led by North Yorkshire Council on behalf of its partners. Emergency services, utility providers and local authorities worked together to respond to incidents, maintain essential services and check on vulnerable residents.

### Fire crews attend flooding and dangerous structures

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service attended 32 weather-related incidents, including 12 flooded premises, 14 dangerous structures and two incidents involving people trapped in vehicles in flood water.

> **Garry Mackay, Deputy Chief Fire Officer at North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, said:**
>
> This weekend was a real example of partners working together, both to prepare for the storms and to respond when communities needed us. Our crews had a busy weekend, attending more than 30 weather-related incidents, and I’d like to thank everyone involved in the response.
>
> Although the storms have passed, further rain is forecast this week, so please continue to take care. If you come across flood water, don’t attempt to drive through it. Turn around and find a safer route.

### Police handle more than 2,000 calls

North Yorkshire Police’s Force Control Room handled 758 emergency 999 calls and 1,314 non-emergency 101 calls across Saturday and Sunday. A total of 1,322 incidents were recorded.

> **Superintendent Andy Nunns of North Yorkshire Police, who chaired the multi-agency response, said:**
>
> I would like to thank all our partners who worked tirelessly throughout the weekend in preparation for, and response to, Storm Boelo. Their hard work, commitment and collaboration helped keep our communities safe during a period of disruption.
>
> Thankfully, the weekend passed without any major incidents, and the coordinated efforts of our partners ensured that essential services were able to continue operating as normally as possible.

### Welfare checks prioritised during power cuts

The Local Resilience Forum used postcode data from Northern Powergrid to identify areas experiencing power outages. This information was cross-referenced with the council’s register of people receiving social care support or being assessed for a care package, helping partners prioritise welfare checks.

> **Matt Robinson, Chief Resilience Officer for the North Yorkshire Local Resilience Forum, said:**
>
> We are fortunate to have a strong community spirit in York and North Yorkshire, and we are very grateful to all those who looked out for anyone close to them who may have needed an extra bit of help over the weekend.
>
> We also know that the farming community helped clear roads of debris assisting highways teams to keep routes open or reopen them as quickly as possible and we want to say thank you to them.

Mr Robinson said the information-sharing between agencies had helped them identify residents who might need assistance during the storms.

Further rain was forecast for the week following the storms, with residents urged to take care around flood water and avoid driving through flooded roads.
