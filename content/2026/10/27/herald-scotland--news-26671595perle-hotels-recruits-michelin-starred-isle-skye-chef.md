An independent Scottish hotel operator has made a bold move to revolutionise its restaurant offer by recruiting an acclaimed chef as its first culinary director and brand ambassador.

Perle Hotels has made a “significant investment” to bring Michael Smith on board just days before he and his wife Laurence close the doors at their celebrated Loch Bay restaurant on the Isle of Skye.

Mr Smith, who has been chef-proprietor of the Michelin-starred Loch Bay since 2016, will join Perle on November 10 after concluding the last service at the restaurant in the fishing village of Stein this Saturday (October 31).

Speaking to The Herald, Mr Smith said the response to the decision to close Loch Bay has been “very warm and humbling”, with locals and tourists eager to visit the restaurant, renowned for its locally landed seafood, one final time.

However, he said the opportunity offered by Perle owner Beth Rehman was too good to turn down.

“When I had conversations with Beth and she presented me with the idea, it seemed like a very exciting opportunity at this stage of my career to try something relatively new,” Mr Smith, who worked previously at the famous Three Chimneys restaurant on Skye, said.

“The [hotel] sector is new to me to some degree. If I’m able to influence young people in the industry, and if I can use my experience in some small way to help the next generation build long-lasting careers within hospitality in Scotland and beyond, then that’s something that appeals to me very much. It’s something Beth and I share a common view on.”

Mr Smith added: “Within the food, drink and hospitality industries, I don’t think there has ever been such interest from young people. If you look online, you can see so many are passionate about food, ingredients, recipes, global cuisine and all aspects of food and drink.

“It’s about making sure there is a network that can support, train, nurture and retain these people, allowing them to build professional, long-lasting careers. We can enhance and improve what already exists.”

Ms Rehman, whose firm currently owns five hotels across Skye, Oban and Glencoe, told The Herald that she is “genuinely overjoyed to finally be talking about” Mr Smith’s appointment. The pair previously worked together when Mr Smith developed the menu at Dulse & Brose, the restaurant at Perle’s Bosville hotel at Portree, shortly after he left the Three Chimneys.

Ms Rehman said: “We always wanted that restaurant [Dulse & Brose] to have its own identity, separate from the hotel itself. The location in Portree is very good for passers-by and visitors dropping in, and it’s been our most successful restaurant concept to date.

“We stayed in touch over the years. Over time, particularly during the last 18 months, we started talking more about the future. Initially, it was simply a friendship conversation. I was interested in what his plans were beyond running the restaurant. Gradually the discussion evolved into conversations about where Perle Hotels was heading and what we were trying to achieve.

“It grew naturally from there. We recognised there was an opportunity to further develop our food and beverage offering, and Michael seemed like an excellent fit for the business.”

Ms Rehman was fulsome in her praise of Mr Smith’s achievements at the “tremendously successful” Loch Bay, which gained a Michelin star within 18 months of opening and has since retained the accolade.

“The concept was fantastic, receiving seafood directly from the boats across the water and bringing it straight into the kitchen,” Ms Rehman said. “It was highly respected throughout the industry. I think this was more about embracing change. Our needs as a business and Michael’s desire for a broader role happened to align at the same time, which worked well for both parties.”

Asked what Mr Smith will initially be asked to do when he joins Perle, Ms Rehman said that she would like him to “reassess the food and beverage side of the business”.

Ms Rehman, whose firm is developing a hotel across three townhouses on Edinburgh's Castle Street, said: “Historically, we've been accommodation-led. Our primary focus has always been getting the guest experience right, particularly in terms of our rooms and hotels.

“Food and beverage has always been important, but perhaps it hasn’t always been treated as a distinct discipline in its own right. It has often been heavily dependent on our resident guests.

“What we don’t want to become is a hotel group known simply for having hotel restaurants. By that I mean restaurants with broad menus designed to appeal to everyone but focused primarily on serving hotel guests.

“I think each restaurant should tell its own story. It should have its own identity separate from the hotel. That’s something we’re actively working on, and Michael’s arrival comes at exactly the right time.

“Earlier this year we appointed Mark Milne as executive chef on Skye. He has already helped move our restaurants forward significantly, and I think Michael working alongside Mark and our wider chef teams will make a real difference.”

Asked what his immediate priorities will be when he joins Perle, Mr Smith said: “I would like to get to know the group and the individual hotels. I’ll be working closely at the start with Beth, Claire and the rest of the team, and getting to know the staff in each business will be my priority.

“As early as possible, wherever I can use my experience to help and guide people, that’s what I’ll be doing.”

Mr Smith went on: “Making immediate or rapid changes is not the priority. It’s about getting a feel for the group and understanding each hotel, because they are all individual businesses in their own right. You’ve got Glencoe, and then the future Edinburgh property. Each is unique.

“Once I get to know them properly, we can start looking at how things might evolve and where we can enhance what already exists.”
