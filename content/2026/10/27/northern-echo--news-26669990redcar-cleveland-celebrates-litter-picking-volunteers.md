Volunteer litter pickers who help keep a North East community clean have been celebrated during a week of events.

Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council organised events last week to thank volunteers who keep neighbourhoods, beaches and green spaces free of litter.

Councillor Ceri Cawley, Mayor of Redcar and Cleveland, said: "Our litter picking volunteers are an inspiration.

"They give up countless hours to improve their local areas, protect the environment and bring communities together.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21632120.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mayor Councillor Ceri Cawley with litter picking volunteers from across the borough holding the Thank You banner donated by Graphics Solutions *(Image: Supplied)*

"Their dedication and enthusiasm make a real difference across the borough, and it has been a pleasure to meet many of them and thank them for everything they do."

The mayor met groups on October 20 and 22 to thank them, while volunteers received a ‘Thank You’ banner donated by Graphics Solutions.

South Gare Litter Pickers now has more than 40 regular volunteers, five years after Matt Stilwell founded the group.

Mr Stilwell said: "I have even been contacted by people from America saying that we've inspired them to start their own groups.

"It's nice to hear about people seeing what we do and getting inspired by it."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21632121.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mayor Councillor Ceri Cawley with litter picking volunteers from across the borough holding the Thank You banner donated by Graphics Solutions *(Image: Supplied)*

Other recognised groups included Marske Litter Action, Grangetown Revival and Keeping It Clean at Saltburn (KICAS).

In Carlin How, a small group of residents has spent 15 years keeping the village tidy.

Michael Greensmith said: "This is our village.

"We live here and we want to keep it tidy.

"Many people don't realise what they've got."

The council launched a litter-picking map on October 21 to help residents find volunteer groups and join local clean-ups.

Councillor Neil Bendelow, cabinet member for neighbourhoods, said: "Our litter picking volunteers make an enormous contribution to communities across Redcar and Cleveland.

"Whether they are cleaning beaches, parks, streets or green spaces, they are helping to keep our borough looking its best and creating places that residents can be proud of."

Residents can view the map at the council website.
