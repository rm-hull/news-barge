A York head teacher has said schools must “put screens aside” for the next generation to “thrive in the age of AI”.

York Steiner School head teacher Tracey Lucas said delaying the introduction of screen-based technology has been “a core tenet” of the Fulford Cross school’s philosophy.

The private school, which had 188 pupils on roll when last visited by Ofsted inspectors, follows the Steiner Waldorf curriculum, a “holistic, age-appropriate” type of education that aims to integrate intellectual, artistic and practical learning.

Writing in The Press, Ms Lucas said: “As schools nationwide introduce smart phone bans and grapple with the impact of screen addiction, it seems that the mainstream conversation is finally catching up.

“As adults, having experienced the lure of screens and smart devices ourselves, we may instinctually understand why limiting screen use in schools is important.

“And, with the advent of AI, we can all agree that now more than ever, we must protect, nurture and celebrate our children’s capacity for independent thought.

“Yet for our school, delaying the use of screen technologies is not a defensive retreat from the modern world. For us, it is part of a step-by-step introduction to humanity's capacity to innovate and invent.

“We teach children how throughout history, natural materials have been resourced and transformed into tools by humans, to meet human needs.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21628585.jpg?type=mds-article-620)York Steiner School head teacher Tracey Lucas *(Image: Supplied)*

She said the school does not introduce any IT into the classroom until children are above the age of 12, and the technology only becomes a daily fixture when pupils are 14. Ms Lucas said there is an exception for SEND pupils who require earlier access to digital technologies to support their learning.

The head teacher said IT is introduced to be used as a tool, “with the same conscious focus on safe and appropriate use as we have for all other tools”.

“We use IT as a tool enabling deeper research, or for a specific purpose such as using design software, video editing or computer programming. This teaches students to implement IT purposefully, rather than use it as a default.

“We nurture our students' ability to question the ethical application of technology, including AI, and its benefits and risks.”

She said IT is introduced after York Steiner School pupils learn about the origins of physical materials and tools, “and how we can use tools to achieve more than we could with our bare hands”.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21628590.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kindergarten child uses a saw at York Steiner School *(Image: Supplied)*

She also said media education at the school begins with analysing the printed word and learning how to scrutinise information sources.

Ms Lucas stressed that the school is not “anti-technology”, writing: “What we are is pro human endeavour. We believe that learning is present in striving to problem solve, as much as in the solution we reach.

“We applaud the move by more educational establishments to take a human endeavour approach to general learning, whilst also embracing the benefits that technology, IT and AI can bring to the human capacity to progress.”
