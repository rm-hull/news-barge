As households continue to face pressure on their finances, paying for a TV licence can be another cost to find in an already stretched budget.

The issue has come into focus after the BBC announced plans to move BBC Three and BBC Scotland online-only and close Radio 4 Extra as part of a £500 million savings drive.

For people struggling with household bills, however, the more immediate concern may be what happens if they cannot afford their TV licence, which currently costs £180 a year.

Arielle Flynn, Head of Advice at UK Debt Expert, has explained what households need to know, including whether they actually need a licence, what financial help may be available and what could happen if they watch TV without one.

She said: “When money is tight, households can find themselves having to make difficult decisions about which bills they can afford to pay.

“While a TV licence might seem like a relatively small expense compared with other household costs, £180 a year can make a real difference to someone already struggling to make ends meet as other bills see increases too.

“The important thing is to understand your options before falling behind in monthly bills.

"There may be ways to spread the cost, and some households may be eligible for a free or reduced-cost licence.

“It’s also worth checking whether you actually need a licence, as the rules depend on what you watch and how you watch it.”

## Do you actually need a TV licence?

A TV licence is generally required if you watch or record live television on any channel or service, or watch programmes on BBC iPlayer.

However, if you only watch on-demand content on services such as Netflix and do not watch live TV or use BBC iPlayer, you generally do not need a TV licence.

## Can you get a free or cheaper TV licence?

Some households may be entitled to a free or reduced-cost TV licence.

People aged 75 or over can qualify for a free licence if they, or their partner living at the same address, receive Pension Credit.

Certain people living in qualifying residential care accommodation may also be eligible for a concession.

It is therefore worth checking the eligibility criteria rather than assuming you have to pay the full amount.

## Can you spread the cost of a TV licence?

Paying £180 in one go can be difficult for households managing a tight monthly budget.

TV Licensing spreads the cost with Direct Debit, allowing you to pay monthly or quarterly.

Monthly costs from £15; however, for the first six months payments are higher at £30 a month.

Quarterly is from £46.25 and is four payments over the year, with each payment including a £1.25 charge.

## What happens if you don't pay for a TV licence?

If you need a TV licence but watch live television or use BBC iPlayer without one, you could face prosecution and a fine of up to £1,000.

However, not having a licence when you do not need one is not an offence, making it important to check which rules apply to your household.

Flynn added: “If you’re struggling to keep up with household bills, it’s important to look at your finances as a whole rather than focusing on one payment in isolation.

“Understanding what you owe, which costs are essential and what support is available can help you make more informed decisions.
