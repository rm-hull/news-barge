An East Coast hotel noted for its Sunday roast afternoon tea has gone on the market for £1.5million.

The Park Manor Hotel, Scarborough, has been put up for sale by the family who have owned it for more than three decades.

According to the hotel listing on Colliers International, the 43-bed property is an "attractive" purpose-built hotel dating back to the 1930s.

The main hotel building fronts onto Northstead Manor Drive and is a whitewashed three-storey building with a red-tiled roof.




To the rear, there are various extensions, which are largely single-storey with complimentary rendered elevations with flat roof coverings.

The main hotel has been connected to an adjoining hotel dwelling via a conservatory-style infill extension.

The building is set back and raised from the roadside by a sloping lawned area, which provides views over parkland.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21630349.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Park Manor Hotel, Scarborough, from above. *(Image: Colliers International)*

Immediately to the front of the building is an external seating area, with seating in the summer provided outside the conservatory area.

The main entrance leads into the large reception area, with a desk to the left and a lift and stairwell to the rear providing access to the upper floors.

To the left, the reception gives way into the lounge area, which predominantly falls within the conservatory-style extension.

It has casual seating for 70 covers and a bar servery.

Adjacent to the lounge is a substantial games room with pool tables, a ping pong table, and table football.

To the right of the reception is the dining room, which is a formal space seating 70 covers.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21630355.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A hotel dining area *(Image: Colliers International)*

Adjacent to this is the Manor room, which serves as a private dining/function space with a capacity for 36 people.

To the rear of the ground floor trading area are the leisure facilities, which include a heated 30ft swimming pool, jacuzzi, steam room, and associated changing rooms.

The leisure facilities are popular with both residents and a local membership of around 100.

The property is served by a main car park and has room for around 15 vehicles.

In addition, there is a further staff parking area to the left with informal capacity for three to five cars.

The business is multi-faceted, with a variety of income sources, with the majority from accommodation sales, supported by food and beverage income, event business and the leisure facilities.

The hotel is popular with small weddings, functions and funeral teas and runs around 90 events per year. The latest year end draft accounts (March 2026) show a net Turnover of £1.005,778, the sales particulars added.
