**Tuesday's top story: Police shoot car after suspects drive into group of people in Lower Saxony**

In Neustadt am Rübenberge, Lower Saxony, near Hanover, a car deliberately drove into a group of people.

Four to five people were injured, some of them seriously, a police spokeswoman said. The injured were taken to the hospital, but their lives are not in danger.

Police officers who were nearby for a traffic stop opened fire on the car to try and stop it.

The suspects fled on foot, abandoning the car. An initial search for them in the area was unsuccessful, the spokeswoman said.

A dpa reporter described several cars with damage – flat tires, a shattered rear window, and bullet holes.

The circumstances surrounding the incident late Monday evening were unclear, but investigators believe that the people involved knew each other.

The suspected perpetrators likely targeted this group of people deliberately, not at random, the spokeswoman said.

The investigation is ongoing, and the collection of evidence is continuing.

**German leadership considers candidates to the presidency**

On January 30th, the Federal Assembly will elect a new German President. However, who will be nominated to succeed Frank-Walter Steinmeier is still undecided.

It remains unclear whom the parties will nominate.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz (CDU) had announced at the beginning of the year that, following the state elections in September, he would put forward a joint proposal for Steinmeier's successor together with the party leaders of the CSU and SPD. But that decision is still pending.

Support is growing, however, for CSU politician Ilse Aigner, currently president of Bavaria's state parliament.

Saxony’s Minister-President Michael Kretschmer praised Aigner as an outstanding, cross-party figure capable of uniting Germany. Bremen Mayor Andreas Bovenschulte said her expertise, manner and empathy made her suitable, adding that the country was ready for its first female president.

CSU leader Markus Söder is also strongly backing Aigner, but Chancellor Friedrich Merz avoided committing himself at the CSU conference over the weekend.

Other possible CDU candidates include Education Minister Karin Prien, Bundestag President Julia Klöckner, Constitutional Court President Stephan Harbarth and former Interior Minister Thomas de Maizière.

Any member of the Federal Assembly can nominate candidates, but typically the parties represented in the Federal Assembly agree on candidates beforehand. A party that does not have a member of parliament or a delegate in the Federal Assembly has no opportunity to nominate anyone.

**Merz hails student-led movement's victory in Serbia vote**

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Monday said Berlin will "actively support Serbia's European path" after a student-led movement defeated longtime leader Aleksandar Vucic's right-wing populist party in Sunday's parliamentary election.

Merz congratulated the movement in a statement on X and said that "with its demand for the rule of law, fighting corruption and fundamental freedoms it has reached the people".

**German business morale rises sixth time in a row**

Business sentiment in Germany has risen for the sixth month in a row, a closely watched survey said Monday, adding to signs of tepid recovery in Europe's top economy.

The Ifo Institute's closely watched business climate index rose to 90.2 points in October, beating expectations in an analyst poll by financial platform FactSet.

The indicator is now at its highest level since May 2023.

"The economic recovery is continuing," Ifo head Clemens Fuest said in a statement.

For the first time in more than three years, German manufacturers were positive on both their current business situation and on their outlook.

Germany's economy has struggled since a burst of pent-up pandemic demand at the end of 2022, registering only low growth as fierce Chinese competition has eaten into export markets for products from cars to chemicals.

A run of positive data has nevertheless raised hopes that the worst may be over and the government earlier this month lifted its full-year projection to 1.3 percent, up from its April forecast of just 0.5 percent.

"The business climate continues to creep upwards," LBBW bank economist Jens-Oliver Niklasch said. "It is particularly encouraging that optimism is on the rise."

**Funeral costs rise faster than inflation in Germany**

Funeral costs and cemetery fees rose faster than general inflation in Germany last year, according to new figures from the Federal Statistics Office.

Prices increased by 3.2 percent compared with 2024, while consumer prices overall rose by 2.2 percent. Coffins, urns, headstones and other funeral goods became 1.9 percent more expensive.

Cities and municipalities outside Germany’s three city-states collected around €952 million in cemetery and funeral-related administrative and usage fees in 2024. That was 2.4 percent more than a year earlier and just over one-third above the figure recorded in 2014.
