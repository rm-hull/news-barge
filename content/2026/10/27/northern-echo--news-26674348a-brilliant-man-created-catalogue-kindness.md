Hundreds of mourners turned out in Darlington today to say farewell to a charity campaigner awarded the MBE for services to his community.

Darlington Crematorium was overflowing for the funeral of Alan Charlton, who was killed alongside his partner, Joy McCulloch, in a crash.

Celebrant Laura Parks told the congregation: “Alan had an untold impact on a great many people out of the goodness of his heart.”

She went on to describe Alan as a “brilliant man” who created a “catalogue of kindness”.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21541034.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Charlton MBE with his partner Joy McCulloch MBE

“Seldom do people give so much time to good causes,” she added.

“Everyone remembers Alan as a truly lovely man, who was endlessly positive and enthusiastic, and known for his loyalty, organisational skills and stoic nature.

“Alan was one of those fantastic, life-affirming people, who set a staggering example to us all.”

Alan, 90, and Joy, 74, died after a two-vehicle crash on the A171, near Scaling Dam, while on their way to a weekend break at Whitby on October 3. The couple were killed in the crash.

The tragedy led to a flood of tributes, recognising their contributions to community life, which had led to them both being honoured by their country.

Alan was awarded the MBE in 2006 for serving in a wide range of voluntary roles, including being the long-standing chair of The Friends of Darlington Memorial Hospital. Volunteers from the charity were among the pallbearers who carried his coffin.

Joy was awarded the MBE in 1999 for services to health care, having served as a cardiac nurse at Darlington Memorial Hospital.

The congregation heard that Alan was born into a mining family in Lanchester, County Durham, on March 10, 1936.

His organisational skills quickly came to the fore when he chose to do his national service in logistics in the RAF.

In his professional life, Alan rose to become managing director of Farmway, a Darlington-based farmers’ co-operative until his retirement, when he dedicated much of his time to charitable activities.

He was a devoted Rotarian, serving as president of the Darlington branch in 2006-7, and was also a passionate Freemason. He was named Darlington Citizen of the Year in 2004 – two years before he was presented with the MBE by Queen Elizabeth.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21637111.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan was a proud Rotarian and Freemason

Among his local initiatives were the launch of the Young Citizen of the Year award, and he was a key supporter of events such as the Darlington 10k and Proms in the Park.

His love of classical music was reflected in his family’s choices for the funeral service, which began with Nimrod, by Edward Elgar. Elgar’s Chanson de Matin was played during a reflection of Alan’s life, and the service ended with The Banks of Green Willow, by George Butterworth.

Alan’s wife, Audrey, died eight years ago and the couple had three children, Nick, Adele, and Andrew, who sadly passed away in 2023. He was also a grandfather to Matthew Tom, Harriet, Verity, Barney, and William, and he met his first great-grandchild, Alfie, just a week before the crash.

In recognition of his grandfather's lifetime of achievements, Matthew read a fitting poem called Success, by Ralph Waldo Emerson.

Celebrant Ms Parks said Alan had started a joyful new chapter in his life when he met Joy through their work at Darlington Memorial Hospital. They became a devoted couple who were “entirely content together”.
