Jet2 has pledged not to introduce fuel surcharges on bookings until at least summer 2028.

The airline has given customers peace of mind that the price they pay at booking will not change later, even as global oil prices rise due to ongoing conflict in the Middle East.

Some airlines have already introduced fuel surcharges to offset these costs.

In April, it was reported that Virgin Atlantic added fees, including £50 in economy, £180 in premium economy, and £360 in business class.

The surcharges, which can be applied after a booking is confirmed, have drawn criticism from politicians and consumer advocates, who say the charges are unfair and sometimes excessive.

Jet2 says it was the first airline to commit to removing the clause in its contract allowing for such a charge, even though it had never applied one.

It's claimed its promise will apply to all Jet2 flights and holidays booked through any channel, including online, their mobile app and even independent travel agents.

David Hills, chief customer officer at Jet2, said: "Customers work and save hard for their holidays so they deserve certainty from the moment they book.

"As they plan their getaways this winter and beyond, we are reaffirming our No Fuel Surcharges guarantee.

"We are making it clear that the price customers book with Jet2 is the price they will pay, with no fuel surcharge added later.

"That reassurance applies to all customers booking with Jet2, whatever time of year they choose to travel, making now the perfect time to book a well-deserved holiday."

## Jet2 reveals new feature coming to its aircraft in 2027

Jet2 revealed earlier this year that it will upgrade its passenger experience with new, lightweight seats in 2027.

The seats will be installed on its Airbus A321neo aircraft, of which the company has been phasing in more since its arrival a few years ago.

![](https://www.theboltonnews.co.uk/resources/images/21389697.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jet2 revealed earlier this year that it will upgrade its passenger experience with new, lightweight seats in 2027. *(Image: PA)*

The aircraft has two engines and one aisle, with rows of seats on either side. It can carry up to 244 people, depending on its layout.

New engines and curved wingtips, known as “sharklets”, help reduce fuel use and carbon dioxide emissions compared with older aircraft.

The Airbus A321neo is also recognised as the most fuel-efficient aircraft in its class.

It reduces fuel consumption and CO2 emissions per seat by over 20% and provides a 50% lower noise footprint compared to the current fleet average.

The airline reached 30 operational A321neo aircraft by mid-2026, while documents show 124 more are scheduled for delivery by 2035.
