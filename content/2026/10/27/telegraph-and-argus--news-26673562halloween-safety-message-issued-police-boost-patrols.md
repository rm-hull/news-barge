EXTRA police patrols will be on the streets to keep communities safe during Halloween and Bonfire Night.

West Yorkshire Police are increasing their presence across the region and urging the public to celebrate responsibly, reminding residents that the season isn’t enjoyable for everyone.

Officers are encouraging trick-or-treaters and partygoers to remain mindful of vulnerable residents who may not wish to participate.

A 'Sorry, no Trick or Treat' poster is available to download and display in windows to help people clearly communicate if they do not want visitors.

Chief Superintendent Sareth Humpage of West Yorkshire Police said: "We will have an increased policing presence on our streets over the Halloween and Bonfire period to help reassure communities.

"While we want those enjoying Halloween parties and out trick or treating to have a good time, we urge people to do so responsibly.

"Halloween isn’t an enjoyable time for everyone as we recognise that things that might seem like harmless fun to some can cause a lot of anxiety, stress and worry for some people."

She also encouraged parents to supervise children and ensure they only visit homes of friends and neighbours.

The period is traditionally one of the force’s busiest, with high call volumes.

Chf Supt Humpage said: "We urge residents to please only ring the 999 number appropriately for an emergency or to report ongoing criminal activity, which requires an immediate police presence.

"Calls on 999 for matters which do not require immediate police attendance, can potentially prevent us attending genuine emergencies where our officers are truly needed.

"We continue to operate both 101 and our online Livechat systems where non ongoing emergency incidents can be reported."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21635973.jpg?type=mds-article-620)West Yorkshire Police are increasing their presence across the region and urging the public to celebrate responsibly, reminding residents that the season isn’t enjoyable for everyone *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

West Yorkshire Police are also working with partners in fire and trading standards to clamp down on illegal fireworks sales.

Dale Gardiner, district commander at West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, said: "Candles can help create a Halloween atmosphere, but it's important to use them safely.

"We've already seen an increase in candle-related incidents this year, and we want to prevent more fires from happening as the Halloween season approaches.

"Where possible, choose battery-operated LED candles as a safer alternative.

"If you do use real candles, keep them well away from curtains, Halloween decorations, and any other flammable materials, and never leave them unattended."
