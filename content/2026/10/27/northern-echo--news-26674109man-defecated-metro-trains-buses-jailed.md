A “disgusting and disruptive” man who repeatedly defecated on train passenger seating areas has been jailed.

Richard Hall caused "significant disruption” across the region’s public transport network on trains, buses, and most frequently the Metro in Newcastle and Gateshead.

Most shockingly, some of the 41-year-old’s repeated behaviour since July 2025 included urinating and defecating inside vehicles, including in passenger seating areas.

This would cause disruptions to services, force vehicles to be taken out of operation for cleaning, and generally inconvenience passengers.

Hall has been a regular topic of discussion between Northumbria Police and partners as a result, and through intelligence sharing, Hall has been slapped with a Criminal Behaviour Order.

The CBO, which came into place on September 23, means he is now banned from using the Metro and approaching members of the public to buy alcohol.

The order has also provided police with extra powers to intervene, and failure to comply with the restrictions has resulted in the arrest of Hall, of no fixed abode, on two occasions.

Hall appeared at South Tyneside Magistrates’ Court on October 10 charged with two breaches of a CBO, 16 counts of shoplifting, two counts of failure to comply with a Community Protection Notice, one count of common assault and one section five public order offence.

He was jailed for 40 weeks.

Chief Inspector Phil Baker, of Northumbria Police, said: "This result is a fantastic example of what can be achieved when police and partners work together towards a common goal.

“Hall’s behaviour was having a significant impact on our communities and transport network.

"Through effective information sharing and collaboration, we were able to secure measures that helped bring the matter before the courts and ultimately achieve a positive outcome for everyone who uses our public transport system.

“We understand the negative impact anti-social behaviour can have on our community.

“That’s why it is important we continue to work with partners to bring this behaviour to a stop and ensure our Force area remains and safe place to live, work and visit.”

Northumbria Police and Crime Commissioner Susan Dungworth said: “I am pleased to see that an individual who has been committing disgusting and disruptive behaviour on our public transport has been brought to justice.

“He’s caused really unpleasant experiences for fellow passengers and at the same time has created more work for staff across our buses and trains.

“His behaviours really have been totally unacceptable - nobody should have to tolerate or clean up after things like this, and thanks to the efforts of the Safer Transport Northumbria Partnership – no one will have to tolerate these behaviours anymore.
