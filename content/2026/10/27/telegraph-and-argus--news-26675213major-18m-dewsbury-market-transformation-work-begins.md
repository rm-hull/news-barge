WORK has begun on the £18 million transformation of Dewsbury’s historic market, a key part of Kirklees Council’s long-term Blueprint regeneration programme.

The redevelopment will preserve the market’s iconic glazed roof and metal structure while creating a more flexible layout designed to meet future needs. Construction is expected to be completed in early 2028, with the revamped market opening soon afterwards.

The project also includes a new town park and outdoor events space featuring a children’s play area, water fountains, planting and public art, aimed at encouraging more people into the town centre.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638339.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A CGI of what Dewsbury market could look like in the future.

To allow trading to continue during the works, a temporary market has opened across Market Place, Longcauseway and Foundry Street, with stallholders operating from shipping containers. Some traders previously voiced concerns about the temporary arrangements and the future of the market.

Kirklees Council's Cabinet Member for Regeneration, Cllr John Hardie, described the start of construction as an “important moment” for Dewsbury. He said the investment would protect the town’s heritage while creating a space fit for future generations.

The market redevelopment follows other recent projects, including the restoration of Dewsbury Arcade and improvements around the Town Hall and Memorial Gardens, which the council says are helping to revitalise the town centre.
