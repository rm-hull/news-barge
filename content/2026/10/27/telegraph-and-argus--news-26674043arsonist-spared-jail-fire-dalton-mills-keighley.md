![The fire that ripped through Dalton Mills, Keighley, in 2022](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/18951248.jpg?type=mds-article-962)

Keighley MP Robbie Moore has slammed a suspended sentence handed to one of two teenage arsonists who burned down a historic mill as “absolutely pathetic” and “woeful”.

Mr Moore made his comment after the T&A reported that McKenzie Mottram, one of a pair of youths who were convicted of setting fire to Dalton Mills in 2022 and causing damage estimated at £15 million, had avoided custody.

Mottram, formerly of Mexborough Road, Bradford, but now living in the Sheffield area, was described in court as “a reformed character”.

He walked out of Bradford Crown Court on Monday having received a sentence of 18 months’ imprisonment suspended for two years.

The father-of-one, now 20, was 15 when the mill burned down.

He and his friend Keaton Greenman, who was also 15 at the time, were convicted of arson in August 2024 after a trial.

Greenman was said to be the principal offender and had “led on” Mottram, but Mottram had encouraged Greenman by his presence at the mill and his videoing of the fire.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632564.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Greenman (left) and Mottram *(Image: T&A)*

Greenman, now 19 and who also faces being sentenced for rape, will return to court at a later date.

Asked for his reaction to Mottram’s sentence, Mr Moore replied: “Absolutely pathetic.

“£15 million pounds worth of damage, lost revenue of £130,000 to impacted business, and lives put at risk.

“This woeful sentence will be another kick in the teeth for the Keighley residents and local businesses who have constantly been affected by the ongoing arson attacks at Dalton Mills.

“It is nothing but a slap on the wrist and does nothing to form a deterrent.”

![Robbie Moore Mp Speaking In Parliament](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20822685.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Keighley MP Robbie Moore has made his views clear on the sentence

Mottram’s sentencing on Monday followed a protracted delay caused by Greenman’s separate trial for serious sexual offences, including rape.

Greenman, formerly of Shelby Grange, Shipley, was convicted of three counts of rape in August this year. Mottram could not be sentenced until that trial concluded.

However, Greenman was not sentenced with Mottram on Monday as no pre-sentence report was available for him.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/13537236.jpg?type=mds-article-620)

He will next come before the courts sometime in 2027 and has been warned to expect a substantial custodial sentence.

Mr Moore added: “This suspended prison sentence [for Mottram] does not reflect the seriousness of the crime, the risk put on our local community, nor the huge damage it caused to our key heritage asset in the town.

“I continue to demand Bradford Council uses its statutory powers to secure the site, to which they have failed to do so to date.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/13537951.jpg?type=mds-article-620)

“It is vital that they act now before further damage is caused to Dalton Mills and neighbouring property, or someone is seriously injured or killed.

“I fear that this feeble sentence has just given the green light for further arson, as anyone found guilty will now know they can just get away with it.”

Bradford Council was approached for comment.

At the time of the mill fire both Mottram and Greenman were students at Keighley College’s pupil referral unit.

On the morning of the fire, they left college and went to the mill, entering through an unsecured window.

Videos downloaded by police from the duo’s mobile phones showed them exploring Grade II\*-listed Genappe Mill and finding a small fire, which Greenman fed with pieces of a polystyrene gargoyle that may have been left behind by a film crew.

The court heard that Greenman could be seen kicking polystyrene towards the fire to feed it once it had been lit.

The resulting devastating blaze, which quickly spread, engulfed the building causing the entire second floor to collapse.

When the fire was put out, the mill had been gutted.

Two tenants on site were said to have suffered losses totalling £130,000. The cost of repairs to the mill building has been estimated at £15 million.
