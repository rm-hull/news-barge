**Glasgow City Council has tendered a contract seeking private-sector partners to provide up to 1,000 furnished temporary homes for people experiencing homelessness, with contracts worth almost £178 million.**

The local authority has launched a procurement exercise for private rented sector accommodation and associated property management services to help fulfil its statutory homelessness duties, in a move that could end its reliance on hotels.

The Public Contract Notice (PCN) was released last week by the council.

The council will award up to four contracts, with each successful provider expected to deliver 250 properties in the first year and maintain that supply throughout the contract term.

The accommodation will be used to house single people, couples and families with children who have presented as homeless, with providers required to supply fully furnished homes that meet all legal standards.

A proportion of properties must also be suitable for residents with mobility, sensory or other accessibility needs.

The contracts have an estimated total value of £177.7m and will run for an initial four years, with the option to extend for a further three years.

Under the proposed arrangements, Glasgow City Council will act as the leaseholder, while successful bidders will provide the properties and deliver a full housing management service.

Price will account for 70% of the evaluation score, with the remaining 30% based on quality measures including property supply, implementation plans, partnership working and Fair Work commitments.

The move comes as councils across Scotland face continuing pressure on homelessness services and temporary accommodation provision.

The council appears to be expanding its stock of self-contained temporary accommodation, which is generally considered more suitable for homeless households than hotel or bed-and-breakfast placements, particularly for families.

Our sister title, the Glasgow Times, has campaigned for years for the local authority to move away from its "homeless hotels shame".

It reported in May that in the last year alone, April 1, 2025 to March 31, 2026, Glasgow City Council paid out £67,815,500 to hotels.

Tenders must be submitted by noon on November 20.

The Herald revealed last week that National Records of Scotland (NRS) is proposing to release its standalone **homeless deaths statistics** only once every two years as part of a wider shake-up of official data publications.

The change would come more than two years after the **Scottish Government** declared a**national housing emergency**, with the latest figures showing 231 people died while homeless in 2024.

That figure represented an almost 48% increase since tracking began in 2017.

Glasgow’s homelessness deaths rose to 36 in 2024, with almost 300 total recorded in the city over five years.
