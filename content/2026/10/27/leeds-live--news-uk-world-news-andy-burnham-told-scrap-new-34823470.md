# Andy Burnham told to scrap new £210 charge for certain drivers doing 7,000 miles

## Motorists are calling on Andy Burnham to scrap the EV pay-per-mile tax ahead of his first Budget on Wednesday

Andy Burnham has been urged to abolish the EV pay-per-mile levy, maintain the fuel duty reduction and cut VAT on public charging points. Motorists are calling on Labour Party Chancellor John Healey - and his Prime Minister, Mr Burnham - to take action in the first Budget on Wednesday.

A survey of over 1,000 drivers by Carwow revealed that the top priority for British motorists - mentioned by more than one in four (28%) - is for the Chancellor to postpone or abandon the pay-per-mile charge on electric vehicles (EVs) scheduled to begin in April 2028.

The 3p eVED rate means drivers covering typical mileage - 7,000 miles annually - would face a £210 bill. A continuation of the fuel duty freeze follows closely behind (26%). The 5p reduction was implemented as a temporary 12-month measure in March 2022, but successive administrations repeatedly extended it, with the Government confirming in May 2026 that it would continue until the close of 2026, reports Birmingham Live.

For nearly one in five respondents (17%), the primary Budget priority is making the Electric Car Grant more generous. While only 9% ranked reducing VAT on public charging as their top concern, when asked directly about the disparity between public and home charging rates, 62% believed they should be equalised, bringing VAT on public charging down from 20% to 5%. With minimal hints about what the Budget might contain for drivers, anticipations remain modest.

Nearly two-thirds (61%) of respondents believe the Budget will render car ownership less affordable. The Budget's ambiguity is also influencing vehicle purchasing and selling decisions. Among those who had intended to change their vehicle, 63% are waiting until after the Budget announcement: 22% are postponing purchases, 3% delaying sales and 38% putting off both.

Iain Reid, Global Content Director at Carwow said: "Drivers are clearly worried about the cost of motoring, and the Budget is adding to that uncertainty. From fuel duty to the proposed pay per mile tax on EVs, motorists want reassurance that the cost of getting around won't keep rising.

"With nearly two-thirds of prospective car buyers pausing their decisions, it highlights the significant impact that uncertainty has on consumer confidence.

"Whatever the Chancellor announces, clarity and consistency will be crucial to helping drivers make informed decisions about their next car - particularly those considering switching to an electric vehicle. If the government wants more drivers to make the switch, it needs to give them confidence that the financial benefits of going electric won't be undermined by changing policies and additional costs."
