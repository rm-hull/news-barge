Funding towards Redcar and Cleveland’s Council highways maintenance budget is to almost double over the next five years after being boosted in line with a similar uplift across all Tees Valley councils.

The local authority will receive about £33m over the next five years, compared with about £17m over the previous five.

The cash boost has come courtesy of an increased five year settlement from the Government.

The Tees Valley Combined Authority, as the regional transport authority, receives the funding and then distributes it to its partner authorities.

Councillor Carl Quartermain, the cabinet member for highways and transport on Redcar and Cleveland Council, said previous highway maintenance allocations for the borough had “not been good enough” and he had argued against a “business as usual” approach.

Cllr Quartermain also complained that the council had on some occasions been forced to “prudentially borrow” money from the bank simply to maintain roads and infrastructure.

Announcing the increase at a council meeting, he said: “This council’s highways and transport department is now in a much stronger position than where we started three years ago and I look forward to continuing working with the department to help scope out proposals to bring forward later this year.”

Last year a report on behalf of Cllr Quartermain for council members said the external funding it was allocated was insufficient to maintain all the highways and coastal assets it was responsible for and warned some future work may have to be reduced.

It also described “unforeseen failures” which had impacted the council’s budget because of the repair works required – these included a landslip in Easington and storm damage to a slipway on Redcar seafront.

The report said funding given to the council had had no “inflationary indexation” and was impacted by rising operational and material costs.

It argued there was a need for further funding to ensure a range of structures maintained across the borough were fit for purpose, safe and compliant.

Labour’s Cllr Quartermain has had to fend off attacks over the state of the borough’s roads with potholes, a particular criticism.

Earlier this year he said the local authority was on course to repair 11,000 potholes in 2025/26 “despite challenges” and rejected a claim from independent councillor Steve Kay roads in his East Cleveland area were being neglected.
