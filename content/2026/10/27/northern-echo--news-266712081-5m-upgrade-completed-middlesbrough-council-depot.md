A £1.5 million upgrade to a key Middlesbrough council depot will help keep vehicles clearing roads and moving traffic fit for service.

Middlesbrough Council’s Cargo Fleet depot, known as Resolution House, now has a 17-metre inspection pit, hydraulic play-detection plates, a 32-tonne flush-mounted HGV ramp and a load-simulated HGV brake tester.

Councillor Peter Gavigan, executive member for environment and sustainability, said: "It won’t look pristine for long, but it’s a hugely improved inspection resource for our hard-working fitters.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21633737.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Left, Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke and Councillor Peter Gavigan, executive member for environment & sustainability, visit the new fleet workshop at Middlesbrough Council’s Cargo Fleet depot *(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

"They’ll be working with the same equipment or better than that used by DVSA, meaning the public can have full confidence in the inspection, maintenance and repair of the Council’s fleet."

Other improvements include exhaust ventilation for longer vehicle run-times during testing and diagnostics, plus a heating system for colder months.

The upgraded facilities are now in use and will help keep the council’s fleet running through winter.

Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke said: "We expect our hard-working vehicles to be available around the clock, and ready to face the very worst the weather can throw at them.

"They keep our roads clear and traffic flowing freely, but that requires them to be well maintained and repaired so this new workshop space is a welcome addition at this important site."
