The number of Yorkshire businesses in 'critical' financial trouble has dropped by 16.2 per cent compared to last year.

This is according to the latest Red Flag Alert research from BTG, a financial and real estate advisory group.

The data shows that, as of late September, 3,007 businesses in Yorkshire were in critical financial distress.

Julian Pitts, BTG’s Leeds-based national managing partner for restructuring, said: "The latest figures offer some encouragement, with a significant fall in the number of Yorkshire businesses experiencing critical financial distress and a much slower rate of growth in early-stage distress than we saw in recent years.

"However, the figures should be treated with some caution.

"More than 40,000 businesses in the region are still experiencing significant financial distress, and the picture varies considerably between sectors.

"Businesses continue to face pressure from employment and operating costs, subdued demand and uncertainty ahead of this month’s Budget.

"For some, these pressures will continue to constrain investment and growth, even if they have not reached a critical point.

"Businesses that are concerned about their financial position should seek advice early.

"Identifying problems before they become critical can give them more options to protect the business and put it on a firmer footing."

The fall in Yorkshire’s figures aligns with a UK-wide decline of 20.7 per cent, with 44,033 businesses across the country in critical distress.

However, the number of Yorkshire firms experiencing 'significant' or early-stage distress rose by 1.7 per cent to 49,495.

This increase, though modest, shows a marked slowdown from previous years.

In Q3 2025, Yorkshire saw an 11.1 per cent rise, while in Q3 2024 the figure was 33.5 per cent.

Twelve of 22 sectors in Yorkshire saw a year-on-year decline in significant distress, including utilities (down 30 per cent), manufacturing (down 17.8 per cent), wholesale (down 17.1 per cent), and hotels and accommodation (down 16.2 per cent).

Critical distress levels fell in 20 sectors, with the biggest drops in food and beverages (down 42.1 per cent), telecommunications and IT (down 40.5 per cent), media (down 37 per cent), and financial services (down 34.7 per cent).
