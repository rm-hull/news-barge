A dog groomer turned author has shared how a simple conversation led her to write a book during a long winter evening.

Rebecca Barnes, a Skipton Gargrave author, said the idea first came up during a chat with one of her customers, who suggested she should write a book.

Her book, It's Gone to the Dogs – Hapless Hounds and Hopeless Humans, was the result of her newfound hobby.

Ms Barnes said: "I didn't think much of it at the time.

"Months later, on a cold winter evening and largely out of boredom, I opened my laptop and started typing.

"To my surprise, the words came tumbling out.

"I wrote, rewrote, then rewrote again.

"Before long, I had a manuscript.

"Eventually, I had a book."

She said writing kept her so engaged that winter seemed to pass "almost unnoticed".

Ms Barnes said she suffers from Seasonal Affective Disorder (SAD) and typically struggles during the darker months.

She finds herself spending too much time indoors and losing motivation for outdoor activities, which normally lift her mood during the spring and summer.

She said: "I suffer enormously from the winter blues, otherwise known as Seasonal Affective Disorder.

"As the nights draw in and the weather worsens, I often find myself spending far too much time indoors, scrolling through endless television series or finding increasingly inventive ways to clean the house.

"For me, it was writing a book.

"Winter will always be winter, but perhaps the answer isn't simply trying to endure it."
