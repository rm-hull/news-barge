A woman is marking 13 years since a life-changing kidney transplant by encouraging others to sign up to be organ donors.

Rehana Kosar, a supermarket employee from Bradford Moor, reached the milestone on October 19.

The 54-year-old celebrated with hospital staff and colleagues at St Luke's Hospital in Bradford, where she was regularly receives treatment.

A special cake was baked to honour the momentous day.

![large group of people](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21606114.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mrs Kosar with her colleagues and hospital staff *(Image: T&A)*

## 'There are no barriers – only opportunity'

She is now using her platform to encourage others, especially those from South Asian backgrounds, to consider organ donation.

NHS figures show people of Asian heritage accounted for four per cent of deceased organ donors in 2022-23 but 19 per cent of patients on the transplant waiting list.

People are more likely to be matched with donors from similar ethnic backgrounds because blood and tissue types are often shared, meaning a shortage of Asian donors can lead to longer waits for transplants.

She said: "I want people to know that within our South Asian and Islamic culture, organ donation is permissible.

"There are no barriers – only the opportunity to give someone the most precious gift of all."

![Two women standing in a street](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21606112.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lots of colleagues came in support of Mrs Kosar *(Image: T&A)*

## Where did it all begin for Mrs Kosar?

Mrs Kosar told the T&A: "My journey began back in 2013, when I spent nine months on dialysis.

"It was a difficult time, and I faced many challenges.

"Then came the call that changed everything: a kidney was available.

"My transplant came from a deceased donor, and I will always be deeply grateful to her."

Mrs Kosar continues to attend the hospital every three months for check-ups and has to take immunosuppressant medication for the rest of her life to prevent organ rejection.

![close-up of a celebration cake with icing decoration](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21606236.jpg?type=mds-article-620)What a spectacular cake! *(Image: Rehana Kosar)*

## 'It has given me my life back'

She added: "There is not one day that goes by when I do not feel thankful for my transplant – it has given me my life back."

The supermarket employee also wants to praise the support she has received throughout her journey from both her employer and her union.

She said: "I feel very fortunate to have remained in employment throughout my journey.

"There are days when I struggle, but the support plan put in place for me at Tesco has been absolutely fantastic, and I cannot thank my manager enough.

"As a proud Usdaw union representative, I have also received wonderful support from my union, which has made such a difference to me over the years."

Her advocacy extends to the wider community.

She recently attended the South Asian Renal Patient Education Seminar, where she spoke with others living with kidney disease.

She said she spoke with patients who are just starting on dialysis and reassured them that things do get better and there is hope, and that one day they too may receive that life-changing call.
