* **Strava has added new route planning features to its app**
* **The changes cover route discovery, mapping, offline tools, and more**
* **Strava has also recently upgraded its strength training features**

You might think of Strava as built for runners and cyclists, but it can track many more activities. With its latest updates, it’s added a few more features for walking, hiking, and strength training that fitness fans might enjoy.

According to a new press release, Strava has improved its route discovery and mapping tools, with the changes being “aimed at making it easier for users who love outdoor activities to find, plan, and follow the best routes.”

To that end, the search function has been made more intuitive and should understand prompts made “in your own words.” More routes are available, offline maps are simpler to use, and route cards have been updated with a new design.

Strava also says paying subscribers will soon be able to hear turn-by-turn audio directions without using their hands. Community tagging of points of interest will be available in the future, and users will also be able to find new routes on the web, not just in the app. These features aren't in the app just yet, but Strava says they are “coming soon.”

## More features for strength training

![A person using the strength training feature in the Strava app.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/uvzQ5HrH3eGSWH2EiDJ6BF-1200-80.jpg)

The routing update follows hot on the heels of another change to Strava in October, which brought strength workouts designed by fitness coaches to the app. In that instance, subscribers can now see animated guidance for strength exercises, with live set and rep tracking as the workout progresses. Free users, meanwhile, will get a “sample workout” for free, and “a number of additional workout series” and new ways to track strength workouts are due before the end of 2026.

Together, the updates to running and strength training help to make Strava a broader fitness app, one that is moving away from its origins in tracking running and cycling. With the addition of coach-created workouts, it’s taken a step toward a more hands-on, guided approach than we’ve seen before.

Strava is already one of the best fitness apps around, and these updates might help make it more attractive to anyone who is currently on the fence about joining. With more features on the way, it could be a good time to get started.
