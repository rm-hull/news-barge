The Scottish Government has announced plans to provide free backpacks to P1 pupils starting school next year. A two-year contract was confirmed with APS manufacturing group in Edinburgh, revealing the scheme will cost almost £3 million. This comes to around £30 per school bag.

The Welcome to School bags will be given to 47,000 children across Scotland and include essentials such as pencils and a pencil case, a water bottle and a notebook. The bags will be manufactured in China, and the contents of the bag will be manufactured across the UK and Europe. The Scottish Government stated that the school bags will be “assembled” in Scotland to “ensure maximum value for money”.

The plan shares similar traits from former SNP leader, Nicola Sturgeon, who introduced Scotland’s Baby Box scheme in 2017, also supported by APS.

Education Secretary, Màiri McAllan said: “Building on the success of our Baby Box offer, we want to ensure children start school on an equal footing and with a feeling that they belong.”

The bags are customisable, and children will have the choice of five different colours: Blue, green, pink, red or yellow. “As well as being available in five colours, children will have the option of adding a drawstring to personalise their welcome to school bag, to help them start school excited and with all the essentials for their first steps into the classroom,” McAllan added.

The bags will also include literacy and numeracy resources, which it is hoped will encourage further education outside of school. The government has also included information for parents in the bags, informing them on things like social security entitlements and online safety advice.

Children will be given their Welcome to School bags during their transition visits leading up to the start of the school year following the summer holidays. The bags will be provided to all P1 students, regardless of their household income. Private schools are not included.
