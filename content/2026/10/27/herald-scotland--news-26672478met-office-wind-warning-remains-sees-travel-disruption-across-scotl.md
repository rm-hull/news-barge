A weather warning for wind remains in place across Scotland after storms over the weekend left two men dead.

The Met Office said a yellow weather warning for strong winds and potentially damaging gusts is in place until 9pm on Tuesday for most of Scotland and the far north-east of Northern Ireland.

The continued extreme weather could hamper recovery efforts in some areas after Storm Boelo battered the north of the UK over the weekend, bringing gusts of up to 84mph and a third of a month's rainfall to parts of Scotland.

Road and rail travel have been disrupted this morning, **with a fallen tree blocking the A9**.** The Tay Bridge is also subject to restrictions** due to high winds.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21634556.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A person struggles to hold onto their umbrella during strong wind *(Image: PA)*

The strongest winds will continue to affect Scotland, particularly in the far north and north-west, where gusts in excess of 70mph are possible.

The Hebrides are most likely to experience the strongest winds, which could persist for several hours.

Elsewhere, there will be sunny spells and showers, with some heavy rain expected in western areas.

Conditions will begin to settle from Wednesday as high pressure increasingly influences the UK, the Met Office said.

Flood warnings have been issued for dozens of locations across Scotland as severe weather continues to raise the risk of coastal and river flooding.

The Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA) has 42 flood warnings in place, meaning immediate action is required to protect people and property.

The warnings cover a wide area of the country, including communities in Aberdeenshire, Argyll and Bute, Ayrshire, Perthshire, the Highlands and several island groups.

Among the areas affected are Stonehaven, Helensburgh, Oban, Fort William and parts of the Ayrshire coast, including Ayr, Troon, Saltcoats and Ardrossan. Flood warnings have also been issued for stretches of the River Tay, with locations around Crieff, Ballinluig and Logierait among those facing an increased risk of flooding.

Island communities are also affected, with warnings covering parts of Orkney, the Western Isles and Barra, including Stornoway, Castlebay and Stromness. Several causeways and low-lying coastal routes are among the locations at risk from flooding and wave overtopping.

Meanwhile, ferry passengers are facing widespread disruption after several sailings were cancelled due to adverse weather conditions.

CalMac's ferry network has been severely disrupted by the bad weather, with 12 routes cancelled as of 9am on Tuesday. In total, 27 of the operator's 30 routes were listed as disrupted on Tuesday morning.

Services to and from Arran have been affected, with both the Ardrossan-Brodick and Troon-Brodick routes cancelled.

The crossings provide key links between the mainland and the island, and the disruption is expected to affect both residents and visitors.

Elsewhere, the passenger ferry service between Gourock and Kilcreggan has also been cancelled. The route is a vital commuter link across the Clyde and is used daily by passengers travelling between Inverclyde and the Rosneath Peninsula.

Greg Dewhurst, senior operational meteorologist at the Met Office, said: "Most will have a day of sunny spells and blustery showers on Tuesday, with the heaviest and most frequent showers in the north.

"Thereafter, for the week ahead, we will see a change in the weather. High pressure will build from the south over the coming days, generally bringing more settled conditions but potentially some overnight fog at times.

"This high pressure looks set to remain over the UK for the weekend too, meaning most places will be dry, although there may be some patchy rain in the north at times.

"Daytime temperatures will be near or a little above average, reaching around 12C to 16C, although some chillier nights are possible by the end of the week as high pressure builds in."

Two men were killed by falling trees on Sunday: a man in his 20s in Finchley, north London, and Richard Baines, 60, in Dumfries and Galloway.

Mr Baines, a curator at Logan Botanic Garden, a regional garden of the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh, died while walking near his home during the storm.

David Knott, director of horticulture at the Royal Botanic Garden Edinburgh, said Mr Baines was an "enthusiastic plantsman" who would be "deeply missed by many".

Scotland's First Minister John Swinney expressed his condolences to the families of the two men on Monday.

Two women were also taken to hospital after being struck by a falling tree in Aberfeldy, Perth and Kinross.

Power cuts affected 838,700 properties during Storm Boelo and its predecessor, Storm Austen.

SP Energy Networks, which operates across central and southern Scotland, said power should be restored to all customers by Tuesday morning.
