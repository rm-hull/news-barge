As Halloween weekend draws closer, many Halloween-themed events around County Durham are selling out quickly.

The Northern Echo has a round-up of a selection of spooky-themed events happening this week for those looking for last-minute plans.

### Walking with the Ghosts of Durham

If you’re interested in local history, folk tales, and legends Andrew Ross organises private walks around the city while telling all the ghost stories of Durham.

He calls himself The Ghost Whisperer, and has 30 years of experience in paranormal investigations.

Though Ross only started his ghost walks in 2022, he boasts 120 5-star reviewers on Tripadvisor, where he was also awarded the Travellers Choice award in 2026.

Reviewers describe Ross as “very knowledgeable” and an “excellent and informative guide”.

The walks take place in the evenings, and he charges £5 a head for a three hour tour. Spaces are fully booked for this week, but there are five more dates available in November.

Contact Andrew Ross at [durhamghostwhisperer@yahoo.com](mailto:durhamghostwhisperer@yahoo.com) or call this number: +44 7939 419474.

### Samhain Witches Fayre

The annual Samhain Witches Fayre at Barnard Castle is free entry for all this Saturday from 10am to 3pm.

It is a market held in The Witham’s Main Hall, packed with artists and jewellers selling handcrafted pagan creations, making perfect yule-inspired gifts this spooky season.

There will also be two highly experienced spiritual readers for anyone interested in getting a glimpse into their future.

Attendees are encouraged to wear witchy costumes and grab refreshments at the onsite café to make a full day of their visit.

### Pyromusical

![The annual Pyromusical returns this Halloween, promising thousands of fireworks choreographed to music and a few seasonal surprises](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20022624.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A Halloween-themed pyromusical fireworks show will return to Bishop Auckland on Saturday *(Image: Kynren)*

Bishop Auckland’s Pyromusical does not fall on Halloween every year, making this year’s event not-to-miss.

At 7pm this Saturday, 25 minutes of fireworks will commence, choreographed to an “epic” soundtrack.

A shuttle bus service from Bishop Auckland Market Place to the venue is provided from 4.30pm onwards, allowing time for food and drinks before the show.

The show is designed to be safe and comfortable for all ages. Ticket prices land at £18 for adults, a discounted £15 for under-18s, and they remain free for children aged three or under.

## Family-Friendly Events

### Halloween Trails

![Raby Castle](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/17328915.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Raby Castle

For more family-friendly halloween fun, **Raby Castle** is offering an all-inclusive discounted day for families as part of its Halloween Family Explorer Ticket.

The ticket costs £60 for two parents and two children, and includes a “Haunted Forest Trick or Trail” tour, alongside the usual Castle Tour.

A separate ticket offer includes a Haunted Ghost Walk Tour of the castle at dusk. For more offers and information, visit [https://www.raby.co.uk/halloween/.](https://www.raby.co.uk/halloween/.)

Another Halloween Trail option is offered by **Thorp Perrow** in North Yorkshire, where family tickets cost £48.90.

The Arboretum is open 10am-4pm and runs until the 1st of November - entry times are flexible. A particular highlight of the event is the bird of prey flying displays.

The Crook Community Centre is hosting a **“Phantom Panto”**, organised by The Empire Electric Palace Theatre.

With the aim of supporting live entertainment events at affordable prices, this charity has worked to deliver a performance including magical illusions and pop songs for just £5 a seat. Visit www.empirecrook.co.uk to book your place for 4pm this Saturday.

### Events for Children

Perfect Pippi ceramics is running a **90-minute Season Ceramics workshop** this Saturday at 11am and 2pm. Ideal for children and teens, attendees will be able to design and paint a unique handmade piece to their own tastes.

Tickets are £10 per child with accompanying adults able to attend for free, and all booking information can be found via The Light Durham’s website.

Situated in its beautiful grounds, Ramside Hall has organised a **Children’s Halloween Disco Party on Saturday** from 2-4pm.

Costumes are highly encouraged as there’ll be a fancy dress competition included in the children’s ticket, alongside party food and games. Tickets are £17.95 for children and £5 for adults, and are available to book now on Ramside Hall’s website.

### The South Causey Inn

The South Causey Inn, just north of Durham, is running a range of events this week, catered to both families and adults.

The Scarecrow Festival and the Halloween Shopping Market are both free entry and require no tickets. Visit the [https://southcausey.co.uk/halloween/](https://southcausey.co.uk/halloween/) page for the full list.

### Halloween Night Movie Screenings

For those looking to only be spooked from the screen this Halloween, many cinemas are doing special screenings of some fan favourite Halloween and horror films.

**The Gala Theatre in Durham** is holding a special screening on the night of October 31. The film selected for this year’s Halloween audience choice is The Silence of the Lambs (1991), starring Jodie Foster and Anthony Hopkins.

The screening begins at 8pm on Saturday. Tickets cost £5, subject to an additional £2 online booking fee.

**Darlington Vue Cinema**’s Halloween legacy screening this year is Hocus Pocus (1993), which begins at 2.45pm and still has many tickets available.

For those who did not catch Obsession in cinemas earlier this year, it is also making a return for an evening screening at 9pm.

New releases include the 2026 film Resident Evil, from the same filmmaker who made Weapons in 2025. For those with children, Shaun the Sheep: The Beast of Mossy Bottom will be a daytime screening at 12:15pm. All tickets start at £4.99 for online booking.

Hocus Pocus is also being screened at the **Grand Cinema in Sunderland** on Thursday the 29th October.

The Grand Cinema is a 1950’s building design, brought to life in the middle of the **Beamish Museum** 1950’s Town, and promises a nostalgic return to the “golden age of cinema”.
