Two teenagers at a church in the east end of Glasgow have become the youngest people in recent Church of Scotland history to be appointed to key leadership roles.

Erica Matteo and Luke Anderson, both 19, were ordained as elders at a special service in Sandyhills Parish Church, Baillieston, and are now part of the Kirk Session, which is responsible for the spiritual life and local leadership of the congregation.

A third of the congregation at Sandyhills are under the age of 30 and people travel from as far away as Ayrshire to attend its Sunday night group for young adults.

The parish church is growing and has seen more than 50 new members join in the last few years.

And the two teenagers are in good company when it comes to youthful appointments, as Sandyhills minister Rev Norman Afrin was one of the Church of Scotland’s youngest new ministers when he began serving there in 2018 at the age of 26.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21636263.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Teenage Church of Scotland elders Erica Matteo and Luke Anderson, with Rev Norman Afrin, minister of Sandyhills Parish Church in Glasgow *(Image: Church of Scotland)*

He created an internship programme for the two teenagers which saw them attend meetings of the Kirk Session over the last 18 months.

Erica, who lives near the church and works in a smoothie bar in the Buchanan Galleries shopping centre in Glasgow, said: “Norman had a chat with me one Sunday and asked if I would like to be an intern elder, and I was just thinking, ‘Why me?’.

“When I told one of my friends, she did say isn’t that for older people, but all my friends and family have been really supportive.

“I was quite nervous before going into my first Kirk Session meeting but when I walked in I knew everyone there and I calmed down. I’ve really enjoyed every meeting, finding out how the church is run.

“The biggest thing for me has been experiencing the love of my church family, that they wanted younger people to step in and see what being an elder is like. It's all to do with the love the church family have for us.”

Luke, a third year student in sports business management at the University of Stirling, added: “I quite like being in leadership and seeing how stuff works, and I thought I would maybe have some good ideas to contribute. As soon as I heard Erica was the other intern elder, I knew we would work well as a team and bounce ideas off each other.

“The whole experience has been amazing. We've both had communion duties and it’s been an absolute honour to serve communion, to serve God in that way.

“There’s been some interesting times at the Kirk Session meetings but it’s all done through love and brought back to prayer.”

Luke, from Kilsyth, North Lanarkshire, has preached at Sandyhills and nearby churches and is considering becoming a minister.

He added: “I've felt ministry coming into my life. I can't really see myself doing much else other than standing in the pulpit on a Sunday morning. I've got a real heart for church and real heart for the Church of Scotland.”

Rev Afrin said: “It had been mentioned a number of times at Kirk Session meetings that we needed younger elders and Luke and Erica's names both came up as two individuals we see a heart for Jesus in, a passion for the Bible in and a desire for the Kingdom of God in.

“They have brought a depth, and spiritual insight that was very welcomed. They carried a maturity and wisdom and it has been such a joy to watch them grow into this office - they never felt like interns.

“Their contributions were always well received and they were met with warmth, love and open arms. For me as the minister it is just further evidence of how God is at work in Sandyhills Church. How He is raising up a people for such a time as this.”

Rt Rev Gordon Kennedy, Moderator of the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland, added: “What wonderful and inspiring news to hear about young people engaging with the Church in such a committed way.

“Erica and Luke, I am really encouraged by your responding to the call of King Jesus to you to walk in his ways of service as elders in the Church.

“This is a high and holy calling, and it will be empowering for you to hold onto your sense of call and the promise of King Jesus to pour out his Holy Spirit upon you both.

“My prayers, and I’m sure the prayers of people across the Church of Scotland, go with you both, Erica and Luke, as you walk into this way of service and faith.”

The ordination service for Erica and Luke took place on Sunday, October 25.
