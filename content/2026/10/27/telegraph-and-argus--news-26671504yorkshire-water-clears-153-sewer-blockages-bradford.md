A major sewer cleaning programme has removed blockages and tonnes of silt from the wastewater network.

Yorkshire Water’s desilting project began in April and has already cleared 32,356 metres of sewers in Bradford alone, removing 153 blockages and 7.6 tonnes of silt.

The work is part of a wider effort to reduce the risk of flooding and improve the reliability of the wastewater system across Yorkshire.

Dominic Laherty, operational programme and performance manager at Yorkshire Water, said: "The programme and CCTV inspection plan is well underway and to date over 1,810 blockages have been cleared.

"As well as demonstrating strong operational performance, it’s preventing the likelihood of repeat internal and external flooding."

The company is investing £1.2 million per year for the next five years to support the programme, which covers nearly 460km of high-risk sewer network.

Across the region, 405km of sewers have been inspected using underground camera technology, with 239 tonnes of silt removed and more than 1,200 blockages cleared so far.

Mr Laherty said: "This comprehensive desilt and sewer maintenance programme is improving network resilience.

"Blockages and silt restrict the flow of wastewater through the network and can lead to sewage escaping from manholes during periods of heavy flows."
