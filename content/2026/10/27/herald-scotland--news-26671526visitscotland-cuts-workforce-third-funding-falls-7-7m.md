Scotland's tourism agency has slashed its workforce by a third amid a slump in funding — and warned further cuts will threaten future plans to boost visitor numbers and grow the economy, The Herald can reveal.

VisitScotland’s headcount has fallen from 493 to 323 full-time equivalent posts since 2022/23, a reduction of around 35%, while the agency's income has dropped from £53.6m to £45.9m over the same period.

And the agency has now warned ministers that any further cut to its core budget would make it difficult to deliver its priorities for next year.

Tourism is estimated to generate £11.4bn in annual visitor spending and supports 239,000 jobs across Scotland, with VisitScotland arguing that continued investment is needed to capitalise on growing international demand.

In a briefing seen by ministers about the forthcoming Scottish Government 2027/28 budget, the national tourism agency warned: "Any reduction in our core budget settlement would make it challenging to deliver on our priorities for the next financial year."

It added: “Rising costs, inflationary pressures and constrained public sector budgets continue to place significant pressure on our resources and our ability to invest in core activity."

VisitScotland’s total income has fallen by 14% (£7.7m) without taking inflation into account, from £53.6m in 2022/23 to £45.9m this year.

VisitScotland’s core Scottish Government revenue budget has fallen by £2.55m, from £41.41m in 2022/23 to £38.86m in 2026/27.

Including funding ring-fenced for specific projects, its revenue funding has fallen from £49.94m to £44.16m over the same period, a reduction of £5.78million. Its total income has dropped by £7.69million, from £53.56million to £45.88million, after other income also fell.

The agency said the fall in other income was partly due to the closure of all 25 of its remaining visitor information centres, known as iCentres, which provided tourists with face-to-face advice on attractions, accommodation and places to visit, as well as selling guidebooks, maps and other items.

Meanwhile, the agency has undertaken a major restructuring programme in parallel, with its headcount dropping from 492.7 full-time equivalent posts in 2022/23 to 322.5 in the current financial year.

VisitScotland said the changes were intended to make the organisation more focused on activities that deliver economic growth and make better use of public money.

Its briefing stated: “Our strategic change programme has made us a more agile organisation and released budget to deliver more activity that drives the visitor economy.”

The agency said it had reviewed its workforce and its ability to use data and digital technology as part of the changes, which ran from 2023 to 2025.

The cuts have taken place against a backdrop of rising prices and pressure on public spending, with VisitScotland saying it has had to maintain its main functions while operating within a broadly flat cash settlement in recent years.

A VisitScotland spokesman told the Herald: “Tourism and events is one of the most inclusive, scalable and resilient sectors of the Scottish economy. Strong interest in Scotland as a destination, alongside growth in global travel, presents a clear opportunity to grow Scotland’s visitor economy in a way that benefits people and places across the country.

“We recently completed a three-year strategic change programme which reviewed all the work we do and the roles and skills we have. This has made us a more agile and focused organisation, enabling us to direct our resources and expertise towards the areas where we can make the greatest impact.

"This includes reaching the right audiences with the right messages, providing the right support to businesses and destinations, and prioritising the markets that can drive sustainable growth now and in the future.

“However, like many public bodies and the wider tourism and events industry, we continue to operate in a challenging economic landscape. Against this backdrop, we remain focused on making the best use of the resources available to us and prioritising activity that will deliver for Scotland.”

The agency has also made clear that the overall funding figure includes money ring-fenced for particular Scottish Government projects, including rural tourism infrastructure, major events and route development. Such funding is intended for specific purposes rather than being freely available to pay for all its core activities.

VisitScotland argues that the country has an opportunity to attract more international visitors, but says it needs sufficient funding to compete with other destinations investing in overseas marketing.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/16874377.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tobermory, Isle of Mull *(Image: NQ)*

The agency points to Ireland, which has allocated around £61m to overseas tourism marketing in 2026, compared with VisitScotland’s £38.86m core budget. But while Ireland’s fund is specifically for attracting visitors from abroad, VisitScotland’s core funding also pays for support to tourism businesses, developing destinations across Scotland, helping attract major events and improving visitor experiences, as well as promoting the country at home and overseas.

VisitScotland said additional investment would allow it to strengthen activity in established markets and develop opportunities in countries including China and India.

It all comes as tourism’s contribution to Scotland’s economy grew faster than the economy as a whole. Since 2022, tourism gross domestic product increased by 10.4%, compared with 4.2% growth across Scotland’s overall economy.

But the latest visitor figures suggest that the recovery is uneven, with international demand strengthening while domestic travel remains under pressure.

VisitScotland said international visits rose by 8% in the first half of 2026 compared with the same period in 2025, while spending by international visitors increased by 12% and passenger traffic grew by 5%.

Domestic visits, however, fell by 3%, while spending rose by just 1% in cash terms, representing a fall after inflation.

The agency said household financial pressures were continuing to affect holiday choices, with visitors looking for ways to reduce the amount they spend on trips.

They warned: “Domestic visitors still want to travel but say they will find ways to cut spend on their trips.”

VisitScotland said the industry continued to face significant operational pressures, including rising costs, workforce shortages and concerns over profitability.

![Glasgow's Buchanan Street](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/16179875.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Glasgow's Buchanan Street *(Image: George Clerk)*

“Many businesses have expressed significant concerns around business rates, taxation and energy costs going forward,” the agency stated.

These pressures were restricting investment and business growth, while extreme weather, transport disruption and changing travel patterns were adding to the challenges facing the sector.

It has identified support for businesses, the development of regional destinations, international marketing and major events as priorities for the next financial year and beyond.

VisitScotland said the case for continued investment was strengthened by growing global demand for travel and Scotland’s appeal to overseas visitors.

They said: “Global travel demand is growing and with continued focus and investment, Scotland has the appeal, assets and connectivity to capture a greater share.”

The agency said it wanted Scotland to compete for major international events through to 2030, while developing regional tourism and encouraging visitors to travel outside the busiest periods.

It also said additional funding would allow it to reach more potential visitors in priority markets and help Scottish businesses turn interest in the country into bookings and spending.

They concluded that tourism had the potential to deliver further economic growth, jobs and benefits for communities, but says continued support is needed to make the most of those opportunities.

A Scottish Government spokesman said: "VisitScotland plays a leading role in showcasing Scotland’s vibrant tourism sector. Like all public bodies, the organisation continues to ensure it delivers the best value possible for the taxpayer as it works to grow the value of the visitor economy to Scotland.

“The 2027-28 draft Scottish Budget will be presented to parliament later this year.”
