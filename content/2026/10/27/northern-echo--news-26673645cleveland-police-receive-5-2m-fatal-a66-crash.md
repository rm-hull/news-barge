Cleveland Police will receive a total of £5.2m from the government to cover the additional costs the force has incurred following a fatal crash on the A66.

£2m was announced by Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood in September, as up to 600 officers were drafted in from other police areas following the tragic collision in South Bank which left seven people dead, including two on-duty police officers.

It emerged last week that the cash has not yet been handed over from the government, to help cover the mutual aid bill. That money is due to be transferred to the force in November.

Now, policing minister Sarah Jones has confirmed that an additional £3.2m will be provided, “to meet the additional costs that have been incurred”.

On Monday, October 26, Ms Jones told the House of Commons that the government is also “considering” Cleveland Police’s application for exceptional funding.

The newly announced £3.2m will be handed over in December, to take the total government support since the August crash to £5.2m.

Ms Jones had been challenged in the House of Commons by Stockton West Tory MP Matt Vickers, who serves as shadow policing minister.

Mr Vickers said: “Cleveland has the highest recorded crime rate in the country, yet the latest Home Office data reveals that it has 17 fewer police officers than it had a year ago.”

Mr Vickers later welcomed the financial support but warned it “will not add a single permanent officer to Cleveland’s streets” and criticised the current police funding formula.

Also speaking in the Commons, Andy McDonald, Labour MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East, described the financial support as “most welcome”, but decried the police funding formula once again as “not fit for purpose”.

He added: “Although I welcome her indication about exceptional funding, does she agree that the situation will not be resolved until there is a complete reconfiguration of policing in this country? The people of Cleveland cannot wait for police restructuring.”

Ms Jones responded, saying: “We are looking at the funding for next year as part of an exceptional request made by Cleveland.

"We have sent the request to the Chartered Institute of Public Finance and Accountancy, which will go through the figures to ensure that we have a robust response for the next year.

"We will then prioritise the funding formula, which my hon. Friend is right to say is no longer fit for purpose.”

The additional cash was also welcomed by Labour MP Luke Myer, who represents Middlesbrough South and East Cleveland.

He told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): “I’m pleased we’ve now secured the full £5.2m for our force to cover the extra support in recent weeks. That’s money which can now be focused on keeping our communities safe.

"But I won’t stop here. I have raised police and fire funding constantly in Parliament in recent weeks and I’ll keep fighting for the funding we need to protect local people and tackle crime.”

Speaking to the LDRS, Labour’s Police and Crime Commissioner for Cleveland, Matt Storey, said he was “pleased” the government had announced further financial support, adding: “I would urge them to release the full £5.2m as a matter of urgency to help cover the costs of mutual aid.

"Now we need the government to deliver the extra £26m, year-on-year, that Cleveland needs to make up for the years of chronic underfunding we have faced since 2010. I look forward to further announcements to that effect.”
