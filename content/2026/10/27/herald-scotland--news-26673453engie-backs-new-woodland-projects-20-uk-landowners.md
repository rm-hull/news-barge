More than 20 woodland owners are joining forces to create new woodland across the UK.

The partnership, supported by energy company ENGIE in collaboration with CarbonStore and The Shared Wood Company, aims to create diverse woodland, support rural communities and capture carbon through large-scale tree planting in Scotland, Wales and England.

The effort is funded by ENGIE’s purchase of Pending Issuance Units (PIUs), which represent the future carbon removal value of the new woodlands under the UK’s Woodland Carbon Code.

Miya Paolucci, chief executive of ENGIE UK, said: "As a long-term investor in the UK’s energy transition, we want to deliver benefits that are felt in communities across the country.

"This programme supports the creation of high-quality new woodland, working with local landowners to bring forward projects that can enhance the local environment and create a lasting legacy for the areas in which they are based.

"Working with CarbonStore and The Shared Wood Company has enabled us to support Woodland Carbon Code projects at meaningful scale, while directing funding into communities and landscapes across the UK."

The carbon units give landowners the financial backing to establish and manage new woodland, with each PIU reflecting the carbon dioxide expected to be captured by the trees as they mature.

CarbonStore, the woodland carbon division of Tilhill Forestry, has worked to identify suitable sites for planting, while The Shared Wood Company has helped coordinate the portfolio, bringing together more than 20 landowners and corresponding to more than 200,000 PIUs.

The project portfolio includes sites such as the award-winning Ardachuple Forest in Argyll, Scotland. and Brynllewelyn in South Wales, where 44 hectares of diverse broadleaved trees have been planted on an organic hill farm.

All projects include a high proportion of native trees and are being developed under the Woodland Carbon Code, the UK’s voluntary standard for woodland creation projects.

David McCulloch, head of CarbonStore, said: "This is a very encouraging endorsement of the Woodland Carbon Code, of CarbonStore and of UK forestry from ENGIE as a major international corporation.

"For woodland creation to thrive at scale, landowners and investors need a model that works economically, environmentally and socially.

"This investment supports the creation of diverse woodland with a high proportion of native trees that would not have been financially viable without carbon funding.

"It is a powerful demonstration of what can be delivered for UK landowners, the forestry industry, the corporate sector and, above all, the environment."

Clément Chenost, co-founder of The Shared Wood Company, said the model puts landowners at the centre of each project.

He said: "This shows that woodland creation can be financed at scale while keeping landowners at the heart of every project.

"Our role has been to assess the projects identified by CarbonStore, carry out the necessary due diligence and bring together more than 20 woodland owners in one robust portfolio for ENGIE.

"This builds on the model we have developed in France, where we have supported more than 600 small landowners to create and restore over 4,500 hectares of forest under the Label Bas-Carbone, the French government’s carbon standard.

"We are proud to bring this approach to the UK."

Tim Joynson, a farmer at Brynllewelyn in South Wales, said the partnership has made woodland creation possible for smaller landowners.

He said: "We are an organic hill farm, so ENGIE were a perfect fit due to their environmental ethos.

"We’re not big investors, so working within this partnership made the scheme financially viable and allowed us to plant 44 hectares of trees."
