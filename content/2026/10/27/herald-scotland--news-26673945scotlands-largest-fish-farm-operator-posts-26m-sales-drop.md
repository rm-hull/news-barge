Scotland’s largest fish farm operator has reported a drop in sales of almost £26 million.

The company, which operates in 50 Scottish locations, increased the number of employees by 62.

Mowi Scotland Limited reported a decrease in turnover of almost 4% to £633m, while profit after tax dropped from £37m to £28m, accounts for the year ended December 31, 2025 show.

It said that there is potential for demand in the development of new markets such as the “growing middle class in Asia”.

The company said harvest volumes rose 14%, but this was outweighed by a 16% fall in achieved sales prices, which Mowi attributed to increased market supply.

The company engages in only one class of business, the sale of Scottish farmed salmon. All turnover arose in the UK, and was sold to other countries.

UK sales were up to £324m from £320m in the prior period. North American sales rose to £49m in 1995 from £31m the year before, while Rest of World sales increased to £16m from £11m.

The average number of employees rose from 773 to 835.

The directors said in the strategic report: “Production volume over the last five years has fluctuated between 48 and 72 thousand tons and is dependent mainly on the production cycle of the biomass in the sea and biological performance.

“With annual total Scottish salmon production of between 151 and 205 thousand tons, Mowi Scotland Limited is the biggest market player in Scotland with a market share of around 30%.”

The firm sites are made up of smolt hatcheries, freshwater farms, cleaner fish hatcheries, seawater farms, primary processing units and a feed production factory.

The directors said: “On the production side, the challenges faced by the company relate to fish health and growth primarily affected by sea lice, Cardio Myopathy (CMS), Gill Disease, treatment losses and to a lesser extent algal bloom.

“These biological challenges result in higher mortality, lower biomass growth and downgraded product all of which lead to increases in the production cost per kilo and decreased sales prices.”

The directors also said: “The markets are reached through a combination of spot sales and annual contracts with customers. Sales prices are highly correlated with the sales prices of Norwegian salmon.

“Like many commodities, salmon prices undergo significant fluctuations on the spot market reflecting the short term supply and demand of the salmon market in Europe.

“Over the last five years we have experienced very high salmon prices, resulting from increasing world demand for this protein source at the same time as a limited supply.

“Demand is growing due to the increased consumer awareness and the development of new markets, for example, the growing middle class in Asia."

### **Satisfactory**

The directors said: "Supply of salmon is limited and has been stable overall over the last couple of years, a consequence of industry regulation and environmental constraints.

“The financial performance of the company has been satisfactory over the last five years, with on average, double digit operating profitability.

“In the view of the directors, the positive market trends should continue in the future with increasing consumer demand for seafood as a source of protein and the growing consumer awareness of the advantages to the environment and the sustainability of salmon production in comparison to other animal protein production.”
