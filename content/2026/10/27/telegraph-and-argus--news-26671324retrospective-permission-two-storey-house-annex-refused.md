Retrospective planning permission for a two-storey residential annex that includes a cinema room has been refused by councillors.

At the most recent meeting of the Bradford Planning Panel, members were shown photos of the unauthorised building on land behind 29 Beech Crescent in the Undercliffe area, including one that showed a crack in the structure.

Planning officers told members that building control has never signed off on the annex, something one councillor said he was “extremely concerned” about.

Work on the annex was completed last year, and in March a retrospective application for the building was submitted to Bradford Council by Haseeb Liaqat.

It described the annex as “a simple design that is in keeping with typical domestic ancillary structures.

“The proposal represents a well-designed, sustainable, and appropriately scaled proposal that integrates comfortably into its surroundings.”

The application went before the planning panel on Wednesday, and a report from planning officers recommended members refuse the scheme.

The report said: “The dwelling has been considerably extended, although the balcony area and pergola structure formed at the rear do not appear to have benefitted from planning permission. Historic images of the site indicate that the site has previously been cleared of all vegetation, regraded and subsequently hard surfaced throughout.

“The annexe building is more akin to a detached dwelling. Indeed, the internal floor space of approx. 120sqm is equivalent to a good sized dwelling.

![The unauthorised annex](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632839.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The unauthorised annex *(Image: T&A)*

“Other unauthorised works, not subject to this application, have been carried out to facilitate the building. Granting consent for this development would, de facto, grant further unauthorised works.”

It recommended the retrospective application be refused, saying the building was “an over dominating and visually jarring structure.”

At the meeting, planning officer Andrew Moxon added: “This building doesn’t benefit from building control sign off. Various works have been carried out without planning permission, but we are only discussing the two storey annex today.

“Hard standing has been created and there have been significant changes to the ground level.

![The drawing of the annex shown to the panel](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632925.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The drawing of the annex shown to the panel *(Image: Cleek Design)*

“The building is question is already showing signs of deterioration – there appears to be a significant crack and signs the building is showing weaknesses.”

Asked about the building control issues, Mr Moxon said: “No building control officers have visited the site so it has not been possible to undertake a survey.”

He showed members a photo of the building that shows the crack.

The building looms over neighbouring homes, and Councillor Paul Sullivan (Cons, Bingley West) said: “If this wall goes, will there be a mini Aberfan? Will there be people under it?”

Asked if the plans would have been approved if work had not already taken place, Mr Moxon said: “It would have been recommended for refusal for the same reasons.”

Councillor Hassan Khan (Lab, Bowling and Barkerend) said: “They’ve spent money on this and I think it looks nice. It will affect the view of one or two houses, but I think it looks OK.”

Mr Moxon said: “This is very overbearing for neighbours. Members may take a different view from that, but I’d say that there are strong planning reasons why this should be refused.”

Agent for the application Michael Ainsworth said the building complied with planning guidance, and described it as “relatively modest.”

He told members much of the work could be done under permitted development, without the need for planning permission.

He said: “I’m not aware that any neighbours have made a complaint. There have been zero objections from the public.”

Councillor Rizwana Jamil (Lab, Bowling and Barkerend) spoke in favour of the applicant, telling the panel there had been numerous complaints about fly tipping on the land before the applicant bought it.

She said: “This annex doesn’t overlook other properties, and it is not out of character for the area. The development is an enhancement to the area, rather than being a detriment.”

Cllr Jamil told members that the applicant had a large family, and they needed the extra space the annex provided.

Councillor Ian Reed (Reform, Wyke) said: “The annex building is more akin to a detached dwelling. We don’t know what work has been done to support this building. I’m extremely concerned about the safety of this.”

Councillor Khalid Mahmood (Green, Heaton and Frizinghall) said: “The question seems to be whether this is safe or not safe. Are there any recommendations we can make to make this safe?”

Mr Moxon said: “I’m not a structural engineer, but the advice we’ve had is that this building doesn’t benefit from building control.”

Seven members of the committee voted to refuse the plans, four voted against refusal and one member abstained.
