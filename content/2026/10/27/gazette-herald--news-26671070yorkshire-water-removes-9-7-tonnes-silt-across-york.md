More than 38,000 metres of sewers have been cleared in a major maintenance programme to reduce flooding in York.

The work was carried out by Yorkshire Water as part of a wider £1.2 million annual investment across the region to reduce flooding by targeting high-risk sewer networks, particularly during the drier summer months.

The programme, which began in April 2026, has so far cleared 214 blockages and removed 9.7 tonnes of silt from York’s sewer system.

Dominic Laherty, operational programme and performance manager at Yorkshire Water, said: "The programme and CCTV inspection plan is well underway and to date over 1810 blockages have been cleared.

"As well as demonstrating strong, operational performance, it’s preventing the likelihood of repeat internal and external flooding."

Across the whole region, 239 tonnes of silt have been cleared — equivalent to the weight of 20 double-decker buses.

Mr Laherty added: "This comprehensive desilt and sewer maintenance programme is improving network resilience.

"Blockages and silt restrict the flow of wastewater through the network and can lead to sewage escaping from manholes during periods of heavy flows in the network."

Yorkshire Water’s efforts form part of a larger programme to improve the wastewater network through early intervention and pre-emptive maintenance.
