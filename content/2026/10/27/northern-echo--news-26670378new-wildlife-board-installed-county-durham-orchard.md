A County Durham conservation group is helping visitors discover more about the wildlife on their doorstep after securing funding for a new information board at a popular community orchard.

The West Rainton & Leamside Green Group received a £1,400 Banks Community Fund grant to install the board at its orchard on the site of former Middle Rainton.

The board highlights the plants and animals found around the orchard, a popular spot for walkers and dog owners.

Annie Hastie, a member of the West Rainton Green Group, said: "The group members do a lot of work to keep our community in the best possible shape and have helped to make this area into a really attractive green space.

"The footpath leads on to Rainton Meadows nature reserve and offers some fantastic views up to the Angel of the North and sometimes as far as the Cheviot Hills.

"It’s a very popular route with both residents and visitors, especially when the crops on the fruit trees we planted are ready to be picked, and we wanted to give them a better understanding of all the different species that call this place home."

The group established the orchard three years ago and also runs a volunteer clean-up team that maintains green spaces across the area.

The orchard has apple, cherry, nut, pear and plum trees, whose fruit the public can pick.

The new board, developed with Durham County Council’s support, sits alongside a Durham Freemen-funded display about Middle Rainton’s industrial and social history.

Middle Rainton was largely demolished by the late 1960s after being designated a Category D village following the colliery’s closure in 1951.

Durham County Council owns the special countryside site and manages it with the West Rainton & Leamside Green Group, part of the wider village partnership.

Ms Hastie said: "Our members pooled their knowledge to prepare the information on the new board and we hope it will encourage everyone to stop and take a proper look at what’s around them.

"A small community group like ours simply couldn’t have afforded to make this project happen without the generous backing we’ve had from the Banks Group and the Durham Freemen, and we hope that these boards add to the enjoyment that everyone gets from the time they spend in this beautiful green space."

Banks Homes is building 150 homes at Cathedral Meadows and has backed local projects including a steel band practice room at West Rainton Primary School and the village’s biggest annual event, The Big Lunch.

Kate Culverhouse, community relations manager at the Banks Group, said: "The Green Group’s members rightly take great pride in their community and in the impressive impact that they have on its environmental well-being.

"These new boards help to tell the fascinating story of this community’s past and present, and we’re very pleased to have helped the Green Group bring their plans to reality."

Point North, formerly the County Durham Community Foundation, independently manages the Banks Group’s community funds.
