A beekeeping society is fighting to protect the right to what it describes as a common phrase.

The Yorkshire Beekeepers’ Association (YBKA) has launched a campaign and petition to oppose an application to trademark the phrase "The Yorkshire Beekeeper."

The application, filed in December 2025, covers six classes of goods and services including honey, beeswax, lip balm, candles and retail services.

If successful, the trademark will give exclusive rights to Matthew Bravery of The Yorkshire Beekeeper, an independent business based in North Yorkshire.

Richard Senior, vice chair of the YBKA, said: "‘Yorkshire’ is a place and ‘beekeeper’ is what we do.

"Anyone who keeps bees in this county should be free to say so on a jar of honey, a stall sign, or a website.

"We don’t object to anyone protecting their own business name or logo.

"What we can’t accept is one person owning a phrase that describes thousands of us."

The YBKA learned of the trademark application from a member who received an email, citing trade mark law, demanding they stop using the words "Yorkshire Beekeeper" on their labels, marketing and online.

The email gave the beekeeper 30 days to remove the phrase from digital materials and 60 days to update printed stock, with written confirmation required within seven days.

The member had used the phrase in small print beneath their business name to describe themselves.

After seeking advice, they challenged the demand and raised the issue with the Intellectual Property Office (IPO), as did others.

On February 26, the YBKA filed a formal opposition to the trademark application, arguing the phrase lacks distinctive character, simply describes the goods and their origin, and is customary in the trade.

The applicant has denied all three claims.

The YBKA’s evidence is due to be submitted to the tribunal by November 30.

Mr Senior said the ideal outcome would be for the application to be refused or withdrawn, allowing Yorkshire beekeepers to continue using the phrase.

He said: "We’ve been around since 1882, and “Yorkshire beekeeper” is what our members are.

"It’s on honey labels, at shows and markets, in talks to schools.

"Most of our members are hobbyists or small sellers.

"They don’t have the money or time to fight a legal demand, and they might simply give in.

"We think that’s wrong, and as a charity set up to support beekeepers, it’s our job to stand up for them."

Mr Bravery declined to comment.
