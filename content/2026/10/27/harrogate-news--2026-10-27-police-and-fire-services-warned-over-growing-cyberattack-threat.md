**Police forces and fire and rescue services across England are not fully prepared for the growing threat of cyberattacks, according to a national report published by His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Constabulary and Fire & Rescue Services (HMICFRS).**

The report warns that organisations must urgently strengthen their cybersecurity arrangements, with inspectors highlighting weaknesses in outdated computer systems, backup and recovery arrangements, supplier management and staff practices.

HMICFRS said cyber risks in the UK and globally are increasing in scale, complexity and impact. Although inspectors found committed staff working to keep systems secure and some gradual improvements across both sectors, they concluded that many organisations still had significant work to do.

Inspectors identified legacy technology as a major concern, with all the organisations examined relying on older systems to some extent. Leaders identified these systems as their highest strategic cybersecurity risk.

The report also raised concerns about risks associated with third-party IT suppliers, particularly where they store or manage sensitive police data. It found that some forces and fire services were not doing enough to prevent human error from increasing the risk of cyberattacks.

### Cybersecurity is a leadership issue

HMICFRS said cybersecurity must be treated as a core organisational priority rather than solely a technical issue.

> **His Majesty’s Inspector of Constabulary Michelle Skeer OBE QPM said:**
>
> The cyber threat to police forces and fire and rescue services is significant and expanding rapidly. A major cyberattack is not a hypothetical scenario and successful attacks have been disruptive, put the public at risk and were expensive to fix.

She said many forces and services needed to increase their efforts to respond to the growing threat, with leaders required to prioritise investment in preventing, detecting and responding to attacks, as well as recovering from them.

The inspectorate also found that preparedness and resilience arrangements were often underdeveloped, including unsatisfactory arrangements for backing up and restoring IT systems following an attack.

Although organisations were improving their ability to monitor systems and detect cyberattacks, inspectors found that monitoring coverage and capability were insufficient in many cases.

The report identified leadership as an underlying problem, with several senior police and fire leaders saying they did not consider cybersecurity part of their daily operational decision-making. This meant investment was being directed towards other priorities.

### 13 recommendations to improve security

HMICFRS has made 13 recommendations aimed at strengthening cybersecurity across policing and fire and rescue services.

These include ensuring all forces and services meet baseline cybersecurity standards, introducing robust external validation of cybersecurity assessments and improving the procurement and management of third-party IT suppliers.

The recommendations also call for better co-operation between government and policing cybersecurity leads, greater use of the Police Digital Service’s National Management Centre and the creation of a similar national capability for fire and rescue services.

Other recommendations focus on improving preparedness for cyberattacks and providing better learning and development for senior leaders and cybersecurity staff.

The inspectorate said the government’s programme of police reform and local government reorganisation could provide opportunities to improve and co-ordinate cybersecurity arrangements across both sectors.

### North Yorkshire response

North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service said it regularly reviews its cybersecurity arrangements as part of its wider risk management, business continuity and digital transformation programmes.

Chief Fire Officer Jonathan Dyson said the service maintained arrangements designed to protect its systems and information, support continuity of services and enable recovery if disruption occurred.

> **Jonathan Dyson, Chief Fire Officer at North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, said:**
>
> Cybersecurity is no longer simply an ICT issue. It is a resilience issue that has the potential to affect public services and organisational continuity.

Mr Dyson said the service was continuing to strengthen its digital capability, improve resilience and preparedness, and ensure cybersecurity was considered as part of major technology investments and digital change programmes.

He said the service would continue to work with government, the National Cyber Security Centre, the National Fire Chiefs Council, North Yorkshire Police and other partners to strengthen resilience and learn from emerging threats.

**The Harrogate Informer contacted North Yorkshire Police, the Deputy Mayor of the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority, and North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service for comment. North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service provided a response, while replies from North Yorkshire Police and the Deputy Mayor had not been received at the time of publication.**
