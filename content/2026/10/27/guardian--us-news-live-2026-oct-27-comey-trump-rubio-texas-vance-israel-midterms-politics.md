Trump and Rubio descend on Texas to campaign for Paxton, and to tout Venezuelan oil

President Trump is set to headline back-to-back campaign events in Texas on Tuesday, as Republicans look to bolster Senate candidate Ken Paxton in the face of a strong challenge from Democrat James Talarico.

Trump will rally in Corpus Christi this afternoon, then head to Lubbock for an evening event. He’ll be joined on the campaign trail by Secretary of State Marco Rubio – a highly unusual move for the nation’s top diplomat.

Together, the pair are set to flaunt an oil tanked filler with Venezuelan crude bound for Texas refineries, according to officials familiar with the plans.

Trump has frequently touted the oil deal his administration struck with the new, US-backed Venezuelan leadership after deposing its former leader, Nicolás Maduro, earlier this year, as a windfall for the nation. Rubio, a chief architect of Trump’s foreign policy, especially in Latin America, has been deeply involved in those negotiations.

The stunt also comes at a moment when surging oil prices, an aftershock of the war in Iran, have kneecapped the Republican party going into the midterms, countering Trump’s 2024 election promises to bring down prices.

Those grievances have put states once considered reliably Republican into play for the midterms, and Texas may be chief among them. The state hasn’t sent a Democrat to the Senate in decades, yet recent polling has found a closely contested race between Paxton (a divisive candidate dogged by scandal) and Talarico.

Speaking to reporters on the tarmac before departing for a campaign event in Michigan, JD Vance responded to a question about President Trump’s remarks branding Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed as a “jihadist.”

Vance, who has sparred with the progressive El-Sayed repeatedly on the campaign trail, took a somewhat softer tone. But he didn’t walk away from Trump’s remarks.

Vance said that El-Sayed had identified himself with a segment of Democrats “who seem to align with people who justify violence against the United States of America.”

“I don’t know what’s in the guy’s heart,” Vance said. “I just know he has a lot of weird friends.”

Shadow from Trump's Washington arch would land on Arlington Cemetery, Washington Post finds

Since President Trump first proposed the installation of a monumental new arch in Washington, critics have argued it would detract from another important site nearby: Arlington National Cemetery, the nation’s largest military cemetery, which sits just across a nearby bridge.

A U.S. Army honor guard member stands vigil at dawn at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier in Arlington National Cemetery. Photograph: Mark Schiefelbein/AP

But a new analysis by The Washington Post has found that the arch would, quite literally, cast a shadow over the hallowed burial ground.

On 284 days out of the year, the arch would block sunlight from portions of the cemetery, its shadow passing over the graves of more than 75,000 people, as well as several notable memorial sites. Those include the eternal flame honoring former president John F. Kennedy and the Military Women’s Memorial.

The shadow would also pass over the grave sites of several Supreme Court Justices, including Thurgood Marshall and Ruth Bader Ginsburg, the Post found.

Strong early turnout in Israeli election, with Netanyahu's political future in the balance

Israeli voters are going to the polls today to decide the future of prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who president Trump has strongly backed and who worked with him to launch the ongoing war with Iran.

Early turnout in the election, branded by some as the most consequential in Israeli history, was the highest it’s been in decades, according to data released by Israel’s Central Elections Committee.

Netanyahu, Israel’s longest-serving prime minister, faces his first true election test since the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas attacks on Israel and the beginning of the war in Gaza. That conflict touched off a wider regional conflict that increasingly drew in the US, culminating in the current US-Israeli war on Iran, which Trump and Netanyahu jointly launched earlier this year.

That war and its economic aftershocks (the soaring price of fuel, in particular) have loomed large over the US midterm election. Today’s election in Israel will help determine the future course of that war, and what Israeli leadership will be working with the US to see it through, as well as implement Trump’s peace plan for Gaza.

While Donald Trump is heading to Texas, JD Vance, his vice president and a top MAGA campaign surrogate, will be in Howell, Michigan, campaigning for Republicans in the state.

The stop in Michigan is part of a campaign blitz that just yesterday took Vance to Montana and Kansas. The latter of those two states has become the site of an unexpectedly competitive Senate race, with Democrat Adam Hamilton mounting a strong challenge against the Republican incumbent Roger Marshall.

Vance has already made multiple midterm stops in Michigan, where the Senate race between Abdul El-Sayed, a Democrat, and Mike Rogers, the Republican, has seen some of the fiercest campaigning of this election season.

Vance and El-Sayed have repeatedly sparred over campaign remarks. The vice president has called El-Sayed “very evil,” and El-Sayed, a Muslim who has faced frequent GOP attacks on his faith, has accused Vance of Islamophobia. We’ll see if that back-and-forth continues today.

Three tankers carrying Russian diesel are en route to the United States, with a fourth expected to depart Wednesday, Moscow has said, part of a deal that triggered a fierce Ukrainian and European backlash.

US president Donald Trump this month struck an agreement with Russia’s president Vladimir Putin to ease sanctions in exchange for Moscow releasing diesel onto global markets.

The measure was seen as an attempt to lower fuel prices for American consumers ahead of the US midterm legislative elections on 3 November and drew sharp criticism from Ukraine and its allies.

“At present, three tankers” with Russian fuel are on their way to the United States, Russian deputy prime minister Alexander Novak told reporters on Tuesday.

A fourth was expected to set off on Wednesday, he added.

Social media feeds are awash with an unprecedented flood of AI-driven disinformation ahead of November’s US midterm elections, according to experts, who warn many posts are likely “slipping through the cracks”.

As tens of millions of Americans prepare to cast their vote, social media companies have scaled back their efforts to protect voters from lies meant to manipulate elections.

While the backlash against Facebook after the Cambridge Analytica scandal prompted social media giants to bolster their trust and safety operations, a counter-backlash from the right over fears of speech restrictions – which intensified after Elon Musk’s takeover of X, formerly Twitter, and the election of Donald Trump in 2024 – has led to significant layoffs from relevant teams.

Artificial intelligence has made it easier than ever to launch a disinformation campaign. And platforms are increasingly relying on AI, rather than human intervention, to respond.

“You can run an information operation now with quality that was impossible in 2016 for super, super cheap,” said Frances Haugen, a former Facebook data engineer turned whistleblower and advocate for transparency and accountability in Big Tech. Operatives playing dirty tricks can get around content moderation rules “because you can just keep tweaking the post a little bit each time you create them, and some of them are going to get through”, she said.

President Donald Trump and secretary of state Marco Rubio on Tuesday plan to show off a newly arrived oil tanker filled with Venezuelan crude to be processed in Texas refineries, according to a congressional aide and two US officials.

The visit to Port Arthur, Texas, comes on the same day that Trump is campaigning in the state for Republican candidates in the midterms, AP reported.

The president has rallies scheduled in Corpus Christi and Lubbock. Trump is trying to shore up support for US Senate candidate Ken Paxton, who is locked in a tight race with Democratic candidate James Talarico.

It’s not clear whether Rubio, who also serves as Trump’s national security adviser, would take part in any campaign events. But it’s the first time that he’s hitting the road with Trump as the president barnstorms the country.

The two US officials and the congressional aide spoke on the condition of anonymity to discuss Rubio’s plans before they were publicly announced.

It’s highly unusual for the nation’s top diplomat to participate in political travel. Rubio’s trip would be the latest example of how Trump’s second presidency has blurred the lines between governing and campaigning. Attorney-general Todd Blanche has already been appearing at rallies alongside Republican candidates.

Rubio previously ran for president in 2016 and is considered a top prospect in 2028. He has not said whether he will run, and his political activities have been limited since becoming secretary of state.
