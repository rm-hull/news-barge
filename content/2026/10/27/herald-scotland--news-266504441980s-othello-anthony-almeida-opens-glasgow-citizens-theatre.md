Marking a year since its reopening, Glasgow’s much-loved (and much-needed) Citizens Theatre announced a programme of new main stage productions and co-productions back in April. These included Chris Hannan’s box fresh adaptation of George Orwell’s 1984, and a 30th anniversary production of Ayub Khan’s ground-breaking play East Is East. Both are now in the rear-view mirror having come and gone, though 1984 is currently playing in Edinburgh (see listings).

But the upcoming heavy hitter in the programme is Othello, William Shakespeare’s peerless study of – well, pretty much whatever you want to make of it, such is the latitude available for directors in the storied playwright’s work. Love, trust, honour, duty, racism, paranoia and betrayal are all in there to varying degrees and all still relevant. Not bad for a play written in 1603.

The man who has re-shaped Othello for 2026 is fast-rising director Anthony Almeida. He has won major awards for both theatre and opera direction, and cut his teeth at London’s Royal Opera House and the Almeida Theatre (no connection). He has previously directed touring productions of Cat On A Hot Tin Roof and The Beekeeper Of Aleppo, the second an adaptation of Christy Lefteri’s 2019 novel which toured the UK and Ireland to acclaim earlier this year. Almeida was also associate director for a large scale production of Hamlet which ran at New York’s huge Park Avenue Armory venue in 2022.

Set across what was then contemporary Venice and Cyprus, Shakespeare’s play turns on the fall of Venetian general Othello – described simply as “the Moor” in the Dramatis Personae – as he is manipulated into suspecting his wife Desdemona of infidelity by Iago, supposedly a loyal friend. “O, beware, my lord, of jealousy; It is the green-eyed monster which doth mock the meat it feeds on,” says Iago at one point, in what is one of the play’s most quotable lines.

Unsurprisingly, Othello is a notable role for any Black actor, and among those who have stepped into his shoes over the last decade or so are Hollywood A-lister Denzel Washington, as well as British actors Chiwetel Ejiofor, Adrian Lester, Hugh Quarshie, David Harewood and Sir Lenny Henry. Here Dwane Walcott plays the doubt-racked Othello and Phoebe Smyth, in her stage debut, takes the role of Desdemona. Maanuv Thiara is the dastardly Iago and also in the cast are Rebecca Banatvala, Chris Garner, Will Stanton, Matthieu Sys and Grace Hodgett Young.

Almeida’s new take on Othello sees the action moved to the 1980s, against a background of the Falklands War and the serious civil unrest which erupted in inner-city areas of London, Birmingham, Liverpool and Leeds as a result of – well, take your pick again from heavy-handed policing, perceived social and racial injustice, poor housing and even worse employment prospects.

“The Britain of the early 1980s is a country in transition,” explains Almeida. “Institutions still carry enormous authority, but the world around them is changing. It’s a society where professionalism, appearance and belonging matter deeply, making it a compelling home for Shakespeare’s story. The play’s conflict between Venice and Turkey over Cyprus also finds an unsettling echo in Britain’s own military and political climate.”

Shakespeare, he adds “made theatre about the world around him. We wanted to do the same.”

For his part, Citizens Theatre’s artistic director Dominic Hill calls the play “a fantastic, gripping examination of relationships, a society, racism, and [of ways of] navigating all these different issues.”

The Citizens’ Othello is a co-production with Northern Stage and HOME Manchester production, in association with Northampton’s Royal & Derngate art and theatre complex.
