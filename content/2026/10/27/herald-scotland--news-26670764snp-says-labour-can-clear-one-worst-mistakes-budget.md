SNP economy spokesperson Kirsty Blackman MP says the new Chancellor can clear up the “disaster” from one of Labour’s “worst mistakes” and “give a huge boost to workers”.

Ms Blackman’s comments come ahead of Chancellor John Healey’s debut Budget tomorrow.

The SNP MP for Aberdeen North described the rise in employers’ national insurance contributions not only as one of the UK Government’s “worst mistakes” but also as a “tax on jobs”.

Former chancellor Rachel Reeves announced a £25 billion-a-year rise in employers’ national insurance in her debut Budget in October 2024.

This increase took effect in April 2025 and comprised an increase in the employers’ national insurance rate from 13.8% to 15% and a reduction in the threshold above which employer contributions are paid from £9,100 to £5,000 per employee.

The SNP said that House of Commons Library research which it commissioned shows "the number of unemployed people in the UK has increased from 1.48 million when the Labour government came to power in July 2024 to 1.76 million in April – an increase of 284,000 unemployed people”.

It added: “The analysis suggests the Labour government's employers' national insurance tax hike carries significant blame for the rise in UK unemployment by making it more expensive for employers to grow their companies, create jobs, hire people and pay good wages.”

Ms Blackman said: “The national insurance hike was one of the worst mistakes of the Labour UK government - it was a political trick that hammered both workers and businesses across Scotland.

“John Healey has the chance to clear up that disaster and back Scottish workers at the Budget - he can do that by scrapping Labour’s tax on jobs.”

She added: “Hundreds of thousands of people are now unemployed, our local authorities have been hammered and our businesses have been put to the wall while energy bills go through the roof, compounding the damage in a perfect storm.”

And Ms Blackman declared: “If the UK Labour government fail to act then it will confirm what most Scots are already thinking - Scotland needs a fresh start with independence so we can build a stronger economy and a better future.”

Separately, the Scottish Tourism Alliance is calling for the employer national insurance threshold to be moved to £10,000 per employee following the impact of the increase in NI contributions "on wage costs across tourism and hospitality”.

The tourism industry body says the change would help support job opportunities for young people, part-time and seasonal workers who make up an important part of the sector’s workforce.

The Scottish Tourism Alliance is also calling for a reduction in value-added tax (VAT) on hospitality from 20% to 10%.

Marc Crothall, chief executive of the Scottish Tourism Alliance, said: “Our tourism and hospitality sector is a vital part of Scotland’s economy, and there is a significant opportunity for growth, particularly from our international markets.

“We already know that people want to come to Scotland. The opportunity now is to capitalise on that demand, but to do that we have to stay competitive on price and continue to invest in the quality of experience people expect when they get here.”

He added: “That is getting much harder for businesses. The cost of doing business continues to bite hard, with increased employment and operating costs and high levels of taxation all putting pressure on businesses and their ability to invest.

“We have been very clear in our ask to the UK Government to lower VAT for our sector to 10% and look again at employer national insurance. Both would make a real difference to businesses, allowing them to employ people, invest in their product and continue to grow.”
