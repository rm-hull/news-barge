A SHAKE up of the planning system that will be introduced later this week has been described as a “planning power grab” by Bradford’s Reform group.

As previously reported, the changes being introduced on October 31 will dramatically reduce the number of planning applications that will be decided by councillors sitting on planning committees.

Only the most significant applications – for major housing schemes and applications for large scale business developments, would be decided by elected councillors.

The vast majority of applications would now be decided by planning officers behind closed doors.

The Government has said the changes will cut bureaucracy and speed up development.

But earlier this month, members of one of Bradford Council’s planning committees said they felt “really uncomfortable” with the changes.

Now Bradford’s Reform Councillors have written to the Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government to oppose the plans.

They have also raised concerns about recent changes that allow developers to build on Green Belt by having it de-classified as “grey belt.”

In their letter to the Secretary of State, Deputy Leader of Bradford Council, Councillor Judson, and Reform councillors on the District Planning Panel shared examples of four local ‘greybelt’ sites which have since been granted development permission, against significant public opposition.

These sites are Fleet Lane in Queensbury, Harden Lane in Wilsden, Otley Road in Menston, and Highgate Grove in Clayton Heights.

Referring to the National Scheme of Delegation, the party says the changes will “dangerously undermine democratic accountability in the planning process.”

The Scheme will also grant “call-in” powers to the Secretary of State to overturn local refusals of applications totalling more than 150 dwellings.

Wibsey and Odsal representative, Councillor Jason Townend, said: “Reform UK recognises that local people know their communities best, much more than national Government ever will.

“Residents rightly expect their councillors to closely scrutinise local applications, and to be their voice against inappropriate overdevelopment. In grabbing power from council planning committees, Angela Rayner MP has undermined our important input and shown that this Labour Government is not serious about local devolution.”

Councillor Judson, who is also the Bradford Council Executive Member responsible for Planning, said: “Precious green spaces across Bradford District deserve strong protection.

In that spirit, we have reduced greenbelt allocations by half since we inherited the Local Plan, which was left in a poor state by the previous Labour Administration. However, our good work is undermined when the UK Government allows for ‘grey belt’ applications which threaten local greenbelt land.”

In their letter, Councillors Judson (Keighley West), Townend (Wibsey and Odsal), Baker (Keighley East), Evans (Queensbury) and Broadbent (Windhill & Wrose) urge the Secretary of State to ‘withdraw NSoD implementation, rescind ‘greybelt’ land categorisation, and progress genuine placemaking alongside local communities.’

The LDRS has contacted the Department for Housing, Communities and Local Government for a comment.
