## 160 youngsters to take part in filmmaking initiative in York

160 youngsters will be taking part in the Aesthetica filmmaking summer school >(Image: Aesthetica)

More than 160 young people have created eight original films in a programme designed to nurture the next generation of creative talent.

The Filmmaking in Schools and Young Filmmakers Summer School programmes, led by Aesthetica, gave participants aged 11 to 16 the opportunity to write, direct, act in, and produce eight original films across genres including documentary, thriller, comedy, science fiction, and drama.

Cherie Federico, director of Aesthetica, said: "At Aesthetica, we believe every young person deserves the opportunity to be creative, to have their voice heard and to discover what they can achieve.

"Filmmaking is a brilliant way to make that happen because it brings together storytelling, technology, teamwork and imagination.

"Young people are not simply learning about film; they are making it themselves, making decisions, solving problems and seeing their ideas become something real.

"That experience can be transformative for their confidence and their aspirations."

Now in its third year, the scheme forms part of York’s wider ambition as a UNESCO City of Media Arts, helping young people gain practical skills and explore future opportunities in the film and media industries.

Participants took part in every stage of production, from script development to operating cameras, directing scenes, and performing on screen.

A special screening will take place at the Yorkshire Museum on November 8, followed by a reception at Thor’s Tipi.

Ms Federico said: "York has an extraordinary cultural identity, and being a UNESCO City of Media Arts should mean something to the young people who live here.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21633641.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cherie Federico of Aesthetica *(Image: Aesthetica)*

"They should be able to see that creativity belongs to them, that their ideas matter and that there are exciting opportunities to explore right here in their own city.

"When a young person picks up a camera, writes a script or directs their first scene, they begin to see themselves differently.

"They start to understand that they can be a creator, not just a consumer of culture."

The programme is supported by partners including LNER, City of York Council, PQA, and Viridian.

Ms Federico said: "This work is only possible because organisations believe in what young people can achieve and are willing to invest in their futures.

"We are incredibly grateful to our partners, including LNER, City of York Council, PQA and Viridian, for supporting an approach that puts young people at its heart."

More than 500 young people have taken part in the scheme since it launched in 2024, and the work will continue in 2027 through the Cultural Passports initiative, which aims to embed creative education as part of a longer-term journey.

For the 160 young filmmakers involved this summer, the screening will be a chance to see their work on the big screen and celebrate months of imagination, collaboration, and practical learning.
