Police will carry out eyesight tests over the next two weeks in a road safety operation.

West Yorkshire Police will run stop sites across the county over the next two weeks, checking eyesight and vehicle roadworthiness while advising people how to protect vulnerable road users.

A spokesperson for West Yorkshire Police said: "Our Roads Policing and Neighbourhood Policing Teams will be out across West Yorkshire over the next two weeks carrying out stop sites to help keep our roads safe."

The spokesperson said: "Checks will include eyesight tests, vehicle roadworthiness and advice on protecting vulnerable road users."

The spokesperson said: "With winter approaching, let’s all look out for each other."

The checks will take place at sites across the county.

UK car drivers must be able to read a vehicle number plate from 20 metres away, roughly five car lengths, to meet the legal eyesight standard.

Drivers who fail to meet the eyesight requirement can face a fine of up to £1,000, three penalty points and immediate licence revocation under Cassie's Law.
