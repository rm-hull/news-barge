# Banks flagging benefits claimants to DWP after checking two details

## The DWP has made the first use of its new powers to force banks to run savings checks on claimants' accounts

Banks have begun reporting Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) claimants to the welfare office as part of a major new initiative from the Labour Party government.

The DWP has exercised its powers for the first time, requiring banks to conduct account checks on claimants as part of what it describes as a "crackdown" that will be "saving taxpayers £1.2 billion by 2030/31."

The Eligibility Verification Measure (EVM) – projected to deliver savings of £1.2 billion by 2030/31 – will enlist banks to help spot claimants who may have undisclosed savings or have spent longer abroad than benefit regulations permit.

The department will utilise its authority to issue Eligibility Verification Notices (EVNs), compelling banks to flag accounts that meet specific criteria based on benefit regulations, reports Birmingham Live.

The DWP announced in a press release: "They will also help identify incorrect payments sooner, ensuring people receive the support they are entitled to while reducing the risk of overpayments building up and claimants falling into avoidable debt.

"The move marks a significant step forward in the Government's drive to create a fairer welfare system by cracking down on fraud, reducing costly error and ensuring support is targeted at those who genuinely need it."

Labour's Minister for Innovation, Fraud and Debt, Lilian Greenwood, declared: "Benefit fraudsters should be looking over their shoulder. These new powers will help us crack down on those who deliberately abuse the system, while ensuring people receive the right support at the right time."

She emphasised: "We are not interested in where people do their weekly shop or how much they're spending on new clothes. Banks will simply be asked to identify if somebody may no longer meet the rules for the benefits they receive, allowing us to investigate further where necessary."

Adding: "With £1.2 billion in savings expected by 2030/31, this is a major step forward in tackling fraud and error, protecting public money and ensuring support goes to those who genuinely need it."

The Department for Work and Pensions has offered reassurance to those claiming benefits, clarifying that these measures don't grant the DWP access to individuals' bank accounts, transactions or spending patterns.

Rather, financial institutions are merely required to cross-reference relevant accounts held by benefit recipients against specific eligibility criteria, highlighting instances where additional investigation might be warranted. It's important to note that being flagged doesn't suggest fraud or misconduct, and no determination regarding someone's benefit entitlement can be reached based solely on EVM data.
