# People of Knaresborough 'refuse to stand by helplessly' during the war in Ukraine

## “Seventeen humanitarian trips have been made to the town of Volodymyr, each one carrying essential items that most of us take for granted”

The people of Knaresborough are to show once again that they “refuse to stand by helplessly” during the war in Ukraine.

![Taking place at Frazer Theatre at 7.30pm on November 7,  An Evening for Ukraine will see an exciting line-up of  live music in support of Ukraine.](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/27/14/25/UkraineMusicianKnaFrazerTheatre.jpeg?trim=0,159,0,159&crop=&width=990&quality=65)


Taking place at Frazer Theatre at 7.30pm on Saturday, November 7, An Evening for Ukraine will see an exciting line-up of live music in support of Ukraine, including talented musicians from that country.

The event is being organised by Knaresborough lorry driver Bon Frendt who has led multiple long-distance humanitarian missions to Ukraine to deliver medical supplies and humanitarian aid.

A key figure in Knaresborough Aid For Ukraine, Bob has made 17 trips to Volodymyr in Ukraine, taking essential everyday items such as walkers, mattresses, laptops, toys and sweets, as well as two fire engines.

The music evening at Frazer Theatre will feature an exciting and varied programme which include:

Oleg Malan is a Ukrainian tenor with a powerful voice, a deep musical passion, and a bright future ahead.

Mariia Petrovska is a charismatic bandura player and singer, and one of the rising stars of Ukrainian music.

Chechelele, pronounced cheh-cheh-leh-leh, is a word from the South African Ndebele language meaning ‘Happy’. Chechelele is a York Based SATB A Cappella World Music Choir.

Knaresborough Aid For Ukraine said: “Ever since Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, the suffering of innocent people has only deepened.

“Over the past four years, the people of Knaresborough have refused to stand by helplessly.

“Seventeen humanitarian trips have been made to the town of Volodymyr, each one carrying essential items that most of us take for granted.”

Funds raised by An Evening for Ukraine will go towards a target of £20,000 to bring a junior football team from Ukraine to Yorkshire in 2027.

For these young players — many of whom have grown up surrounded by conflict — this visit will offer safety, friendship, and a chance to experience life away from the pressures of war.

The visit will be far more than football. Alongside a series of friendly matches, the team will enjoy trips to Knaresborough, York, Scarborough, and the Leeds United training ground.
