Scottish Water has rubbished claims that it has caused structural damage to a historic Edinburgh bridge.

Online claims suggested that utility work at Waterloo Place caused a structural crack in Regent Bridge, a Category A-listed Greek Revival road bridge that links the east end of Princes Street toward Calton Hill and the A1.

A photo circulating online showed a crack near the bridge/spandrel area, leading to speculation on social media about structural damage, vehicle impacts, or utility work.

Social media users were quick to highlight that the photo appeared to be AI-generated.

In a statement issued on social media on Tuesday, October 27, Scottish Water said: "We’re aware of misinformation circulating online suggesting that our work at Waterloo Place has caused a crack in Regent Bridge.

"We have investigated these claims and can reassure people that they are simply not true - we have not caused damage to the bridge."

Work to upgrade and repair the sewer and water network that lies beneath Waterloo Place began in September and is expected to last 11 weeks.

It involves the closure of the westbound carriageway on Waterloo Place to allow Scottish Water to carry out the work safely.

A diversion route for westbound traffic is in place via London Road - Leith Street.

Mackenzie Construction is carrying out the work on behalf of Scottish Water.
