As York pushes ahead with plans to clamp down on aggressive begging, pushy street sellers and rowdy behaviour across the city centre, a Lib Dem councillor says it’s disappointing to see it can’t be extended to Acomb quickly.

A new three-year Public Space Protection Order would see anyone intimidating shoppers or blocking cash machines potentially fined up to £1,000.

**Tanya Lyon, head of community safety at City of York Council**, told the People Scrutiny Committee last night how the order would work.

“The new behaviours we’ve identified as needing to be addressed are intimidation and coercion, persisting in requests, pestering or pursuing people, invasion of personal space, and disorderly group behaviour.

“The deputy mayor has given her support on the proviso that enforcement is evidence-based and proportionate, and that implementation should be accompanied with a strong emphasis on support for those with vulnerability.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/york-cllr-andrew-waller-meeting.jpg)

**Andrew Waller is a Lib Dem councillor** covering the Acomb area and wants to see this protection order extended there too.

“I am disappointed. There’s been a spate of incidents over many months now, and residents have been approaching me saying that we need the same protection.

“There is a process that has to be gone through, and I have asked that the Council Executive start that process immediately, and so we’ll find out at the next meeting whether they will take that up.

“I’ve been in constant contact with the police. A Public Space Protection Order would give them more powers to act when incidents are kicking off.”

North Yorkshire Police says it is doing something about the issues already, as Superintendent Ed Haywood-Noble told councillors last night.

“To give some reassurance that in Acomb we do have a problem-solving plan running at the moment, Operation Haven, which involves high visibility police patrols targeted at specific times, dates, locations, use of dispersal powers, and also engagement with businesses as well.

“So although we don’t have Front Street included in this PSPO, if we were to go down that route, there’d be a process to follow. But there are steps that have been taken in the interim to bridge that gap with the problem-solving plan.”

Back in June, Police took action following drink-fuelled trouble and imposed a 48-hour dispersal order for Front Street.
