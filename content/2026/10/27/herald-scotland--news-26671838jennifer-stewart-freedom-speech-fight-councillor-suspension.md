An Scots councillor suspended for four months has claimed she was denied a fair hearing after a standards panel refused to hear witnesses she wanted to call in her defence of her freedom of speech rights.

Independent Aberdeen city councillor Jennifer Stewart has spoken out as she became the latest to warn that disciplinary action against elected representatives risks creating a “climate of fear” that could restrict their ability to challenge those in power.

Mrs Stewart claims she was interrupted at least 14 times during the hearing which led to her suspension, and says the panel refused to hear evidence she believes could have supported her case.

The councillor who is fighting the Standards Commission for Scotland's ban on appeal said: “The decision to find against me was inevitable given I was not allowed to put my case.”

In December 2022, Mrs Stewart, who represents the city’s Hazlehead, Queen's Cross and Countesswells ward, accused the local authority's Lord Provost, David Cameron of sexism after being told she was “going on” during a speech.

The Aberdeen councillor says she wanted the city’s Lord Provost and chief executive to appear as witnesses, arguing they could have provided important context about what happened during council meetings.

She also says evidence she submitted was not accepted.

Mrs Stewart further claims that two former members of the Lord Provost’s staff who supported her were no longer available to give evidence.

She believes the restrictions prevented her from properly explaining her actions and challenging the allegations against her.

“I was denied that request and the panel never heard from the accuser which could have put a differing meaning onto what I said and how the accuser the Lord Provost thought of what I said,” she said.

The **ethics watchdog has been facing calls for its abolition over what some see as the heavy-handed actions of the commission in policing councillors under a Code of Conduct** drawn up by ministers, after The Herald revealed investigations into councillors have more than doubled in a decade amid claims of a “climate of fear”.

There **were 244 distinct councillor cases investigated in 2024/25, compared with 111 ten years earlier, sparking concerns over the curbing of the human rights o** f elected representatives in Scotland who are fighting for their local communities.

Ms Stewart questioned the way her hearing was conducted, claiming she was repeatedly interrupted while trying to present her defence.

The councillor said: “The effect on my health and my family has been horrendous.”

Her comments further raised questions about the balance between enforcing standards of conduct among elected representatives and protecting their ability to challenge senior figures in public life.

Mrs Stewart was suspended after a hearing of the Standards Commission for Scotland in Aberdeen on August 5, 2025. The panel found she had breached four provisions of the councillors’ code of conduct over her behaviour towards the Lord Provost at council meetings and comments published in a newspaper article.

The four-month suspension took effect on August 18, 2025, preventing her from attending council and committee meetings and other bodies on which she represented the authority.

The complaints concerned her conduct at meetings in December 2022 and March, April, September and October 2023, as well as comments published in an article on October 14, 2023.

The panel found that Mrs Stewart had made repeated personal accusations against the Lord Provost which they believed amounted to disrespectful conduct and harassment. It also found breaches of rules covering discrimination and compliance with the chair’s rulings during meetings.

The Commission’s decision said the panel had considered her right to freedom of expression, including the enhanced protection given to political speech. But it concluded that restrictions were justified to protect the rights and reputation of others and to allow council business to be conducted effectively.

Mrs Stewart disputes the fairness of the process and argues that the witnesses and documents she wanted to present could have helped explain the background to her remarks.

She said: “It is a fundamental right in the UK that a person has the right to face the accuser. I asked for the Lord Provost and the chief executive to be part of my witnesses so that they could explain what actually went on within the chamber.”

The Commission’s written decision says the panel had told Mrs Stewart in advance that it would not accept evidence about the Lord Provost’s alleged conduct because its task was to decide whether she had breached the councillors’ code, rather than investigate the separate allegations she had made against him.

It considered the proposed witnesses and documents not directly relevant to the complaints before it.

The panel said it did not consider a formal censure or a shorter suspension of one to three months sufficient, given the seriousness and extent of the breaches it had found. It concluded that four months was proportionate and the minimum necessary.

Mrs Stewart, however, believes the punishment was "way over the top.”

She has also raised concerns about the wider consequences of disciplinary action against councillors who challenge those in authority.

“Finally there is a feeling about climate of fear that it is said now exists that restricts councillors from doing what they are elected to do,” she said.

Her complaint about the Lord Provost was investigated separately by the Ethical Standards Commissioner, but the panel noted in its decision that the investigation had not resulted in a finding that he breached the code.

Mrs Stewart has also questioned whether the disciplinary proceedings were politically motivated, claiming the SNP had “always had it in for me” and describing the case as “payback time”.

Those claims have not been established by the Commission’s decision.

She argues that her record of public service should have been given greater weight when the sanction was decided.  
  
The commission said: "Councillor Mrs Stewart has lodged an appeal against the Standards Commission’s decision to find her in beach of the Code and to suspend her. As the appeal has not yet been heard and is presently sisted, the Standards Commission does not consider it appropriate to make any comments at this stage."
