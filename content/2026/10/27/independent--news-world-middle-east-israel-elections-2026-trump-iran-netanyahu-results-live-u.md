Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu is tipped for a stunning defeat in today’s legislative elections despite receiving a glowing endorsement from Donald Trump.

Sixty per cent of betters on prediction market Polymarket backed centrist challenger Gadi Eisenkot to become the next premier, ahead of 27 per cent for Netanyahu.

Israeli opinion polls suggest a tight contest, with neither Netanyahu’s ruling Likud bloc nor an opposition alliance around Eisenkot likely to secure the 61 seats needed for a majority.

Polls opened at 7am local time on Tuesday and will run until 10pm in the country’s first opportunity to deliver a verdict on the response to the greatest domestic security failure in generations - the October 7 attacks - and the subsequent conflicts with Hamas and Iran.

Before polls opened, US president Trump reiterated his “complete and total” support for Netanyahu, writing that he had been “strong, brave and brilliant in the face of tremendous opposition”.

He alluded to the unresolved US-Israeli conflict with Iran, writing: “Unless they change their ways, rapidly, we will soon finish the job.”

“If Israel has the wrong leader, which would be anyone other than Bibi [Netanyahu], America will have to do this work ourselves, and I am not inclined to do so.”

## In the region: Officials meet in Tehran to de-escalate Middle East conflict as Israelis head to the polls

Top officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan met Iranian officials in Tehran on Monday in a bid to de-escalate the Middle East conflict, as Yemen's Houthis launched new attacks on Saudi Arabia and Egypt approved defence support for the Gulf kingdom.

The talks were the first high-level contact between Iran and the three nations — which signed the Mecca mutual defence pact in August — since a war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran also spread to Saudi Arabia and Yemen.

Saudi Arabia said the visit was intended to open negotiating channels to "prevent the return of military escalation".

It was represented by Defence Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, younger brother of Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman; Pakistan by army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir; and Turkey by intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin, Saudi and Iranian officials said.

## Furhter afield: Houthis claim strike on Saudi refinery with ballistic missile as war expands

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Tuesday they had hit Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, the ⁠biggest in the kingdom, with a ballistic missile.

The Houthis also said they had attacked Saudi military aircraft and repelled an advance by Saudi-backed forces near the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, an important Red Sea shipping route.

Intensifying hostilities between Yemen and Saudi Arabia have further disrupted oil supplies and heightened the risk of a wider regional conflict, eight months since the Iran war began.

The Houthis' military spokesperson, Yahya Saree, said the strike on the Ras Tanura refinery had caused a fire at the complex, which also includes a shipping terminal and is located on the Gulf, west of the Strait of Hormuz.

## Record broken as voter turnout swells to 44.9 per cent

Voter turnout was at 44.9 per cent in Israel by 2pm, becoming the highest ever recorded turnout at this hour.

By 2pm in the 2022 elections, voter turnout sat at 38.9 per cent.

The previous record was set in 1988, with a turnout of 42.2 per cent.

## UN inquiry says Israel kept committing genocide in Gaza after truce

Israel continued to commit genocide in Gaza after last October's US-brokered ceasefire in the enclave, a UN commission said in a report published on Monday that Israel dismissed as part of a "misinformation campaign".

The report by the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem, and Israel examined violations against Palestinian children since the start of the war between Israel and Hamas on October 7, 2023 up to September this year.

It said Israeli authorities had kept committing "genocidal acts with the genocidal intent to destroy, in whole or in part, the Palestinians in Gaza", and that the situation had also continued to deteriorate in the occupied West Bank.

Another report by the commission in September last year found that Israel had committed genocide ​in Gaza and that top Israeli officials including Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu incited these acts — accusations that Israel called scandalous.

## UK watchdog says Reform's Tice breached parliament rules over Israel trip declaration

Reform UK deputy leader Richard Tice breached parliament's code of conduct by inadvertently failing to declare a trip to Israel during a debate on pro-Israel influence, Britain's parliamentary standards watchdog said on Tuesday.

The Parliamentary Commissioner for Standards said Tice should have declared a visit to Israel in 2025 that was paid for by Reform Friends of Israel when he participated in a debate on UK democracy and Israeli influence.

The commissioner, Daniel Greenberg, concluded that Tice had breached rule six of parliament's code of conduct, which requires lawmakers to declare relevant interests when speaking in parliamentary proceedings.

![Richard Tice, MP for Boston and Skegness](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/27/13/2298453777...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Greenberg said the Israel trip had met the test for declaration because it could "reasonably be thought by others to influence" a member's actions or words.

Tice accepted the finding, acknowledged the breach and apologised, the commissioner said, adding that the inquiry was now over.

## Preview: Gadi Eisenkot launches a challenge to Netanyahu's reign... but is yet to carve out a clear path to power

Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot has presented himself as one of the possible outlets for change.

Eisenkot, who lost a son in Gaza, has projected an image as a political outsider and security hawk whose humble background and family sacrifices stand in vivid contrast to Netanyahu's decades in high office and ‌lingering corruption cases.

Both Eisenkot and Netanyahu, along with the leaders of nearly all other Jewish-led parties, oppose the establishment of a future independent Palestinian state alongside Israel.

![Former military chief Gadi Eisenkot](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/27/07/2026-10-27T052202Z_58968677_RC2HBOACF7UX_RTRMADP_3_ISRAEL-ELECTION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

They both say that Israel must retain ultimate security control over all territory between the Jordan River and Mediterranean Sea. And they agree that Israeli troops must not withdraw from Gaza until Hamas is disarmed.

Eisenkot has framed the continued rule of Hamas in Gaza as a failure of state, vowing that within 18 months of taking office he would ensure the administration had been replaced, either through a US-brokered plan or through military force.

But to unseat Netanyahu, he is likely to rely on the support of the Democrats, including the rump of the once dominant centre-left Labour Party that has has withered as the country has shifted to the right.

## Preview: Survivalist Netanyahu faces another test after 19 years in office

Benjamin Netanyahu heads into this election with 19 accumulated years in office since he was first chosen as prime minister in 1996.

The office has no term limits and a prime minister can serve an unlimited number of four-year terms so long as they maintain the confidence of a majority in the *Knesset*, or parliament.

Since his last election in 2022, ‘Bibi’ has led Israel through its response to the October 7 Hamas attacks, the subsequent war in Gaza and hostage crisis, a ground invasion of Lebanon, strikes on Syria, attacks from and against Iran, and, most recently, into the ongoing conflict with Tehran alongside the United States.

![Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/26/14/2026-10-21T154804Z_1906111492_RC2Q7OAF71W7_RTRMADP_3_ISRAEL-MILITARY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Domestically, he is still embroiled in a long-running corruption case, after being indicted in 2019 on charges of bribery, fraud and breach of trust in three cases, accused of receiving expensive gifts in exchange for political favours. The 77-year-old has called the case a witch hunt and proceedings stalled during the war in Gaza.

Netanyahu is also under an arrest warrant issued by the International Criminal Court (ICC) for suspected crimes against humanity and war crimes, and faces criticism from abroad for presiding over a rapid expansion of settlements in the West Bank.

Many Israelis now say it is time for a change in the seat of government. A poll of 4,000 Israeli voters this month predicted that Netanyahu’s Likud party would secure only 19 seats, down from 32 currently, while the opposition, led by Yashar’s Gadi Eisenkot, could secure the most with 20.

## Explained: How will the Israeli election work and when will result be announced?

Israelis will head to the polls on today to cast their vote to elect the Knesset, after three years of war waged in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran, which have plunged the Middle East into turmoil.

Much of the election is centred around Benjamin Netanyahu, who is seeking another term after a combined 19 years in power, but neither he nor the opposition has a clear path to a governing majority.

Due to the fragmented political system, the country may have to wait weeks or even months before a new government is confirmed.
