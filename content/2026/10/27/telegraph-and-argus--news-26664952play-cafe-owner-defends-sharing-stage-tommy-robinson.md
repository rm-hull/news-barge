A play cafe owner whose community interest company has received £20,000 of council-backed funding has said he should not be judged on the basis of a past appearance at a public rally where far-right activist Tommy Robinson was present.

Joseph Buthee, who runs Bows and Beams in Saltaire with Naomi Buthee, confirmed he spoke and led a prayer at a Unite the Kingdom event in London in 2024. However, he rejected suggestions that this meant he was affiliated with Tommy Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon.

The funding was awarded through the Shipley Towns Fund, which supports projects aimed at boosting business growth, diversifying the high street and encouraging new ventures in the area.

## 'I attended a public rally in 2024'

Concerns were raised with the Telegraph & Argus by a local source who questioned whether someone with Mr Buthee's public associations should be benefiting from council-backed funding.

Responding to those concerns, Mr Buthee said: "I attended a public rally in 2024 and was invited to speak and pray. My reason for doing so was pastoral and from a burden I had for the large number of angry and disenfranchised people and wanted to point them toward Jesus rather than toward political solutions.

"At that time I worked as a missionary in London. My Lord also went to the tax collectors and went to many of the lost. No one is outside of his love.

"In the videos I state clearly that nationalism will not work and that any lasting identity has to be found in Christ. I have no affiliation with Tommy Robinson or his organisations."

Mr Buthee described the café in Saltaire is a family business, stating that it "is not a political or campaigning project."

He added: "I would ask that my family and I are not characterised on the basis of a past public appearance."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21550333.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Naomi and Joseph Buthee opened Bows and Beams in Saltaire *(Image: UGC)*

In addition to his work at the play café, the source claimed Mr Buthee works as a private tutor and in local churches.

The source told the T&A: “There has been much chatter amongst local community groups and concern is certainly growing.”

Though Mr Buthee was appointed Family and Children's Worker and Family and Youth Co-ordinator at St Peter's Church, Shipley, in March 2025, St Peter's Church has since confirmed he is no longer involved there.

Mr Buthee himself added: "I helped with Sunday school at a local church until May 2026. That role has now ended. I have not undertaken any tutoring or teaching work outside that church context since 2024."

## Appearance at Tommy Robinson rally

Video footage from the 2024 event shows Mr Yaxley-Lennon criticising the opening ceremony of the Paris Olympics, describing it as an "absolute disgrace" and a "total attack on Christianity", before introducing "Joseph".

Mr Buthee is then handed the microphone and says: “Praise God, Hallelujah. We've got four lions behind us on Nelson's Column. I want to tell you that the fifth lion is standing here today - Tommy Robinson. I want to honour this man for the courage, the bravery and the long-suffering he's gone through for truth.”

In another video, Mr Buthee says: "We need to make sure that it’s Christ taking this country back otherwise we leave a vacuum, that’s what’s happened, wokeism has come in, wokeism is Christianity without Christ.”
