An “unused” office building next to a Wearside dairy distribution depot could be converted into a home under new plans.

An application to Sunderland City Council’s planning department is seeking permission to convert part of the Hanover Dairies site in Matamba Terrace into a residential use.

The plans, from applicant HD Homes, note the part of the building proposed for development is an “unused ancillary office” and has been “strictly limited to office use only and not used as industrial”.

It was noted that the remaining storage and distribution area of the building will “continue to operate as existing” and that “all office/admin work is carried out at head office at another site”.

Plans for the office building include a change of use to a two-bedroom home with a kitchen, living area and cycle storage.

An accompanying parking assessment states the development would “not be expected to result in an adverse impact on on-street parking availability or the operation of the local highway network”.

Meanwhile, a noise assessment submitted on behalf of applicants acknowledges that mitigation is needed as a dairy distribution depot operates within the same building envelope, to the east of the proposed home.

It was noted that the party wall between the proposed dwelling and adjoining industrial operation is “constructed in solid brickwork of approximately 560 mm overall thickness, running to full height from ground floor level to the roof ridge”.

Measures to reduce noise disruption for people living in the two-bedroom house include infilling a doorway opening in the party wall at ground floor level and high-performance double glazing with “acoustic trickle ventilators”.

The noise assessment adds: ” Subject to implementation of the above measures, it is the opinion of L A Environmental Ltd that the proposed two-bedroom dwelling at Hanover Dairies, Matamba Terrace, Sunderland, will achieve the ‘good’ design criteria for internal noise levels in living rooms and bedrooms, and that the site is suitable for residential use.”
