JD Wetherspoon has banned phone calls on speaker and music on phones in pubs across the UK as a customer was reportedly "turned away over a poppy".

The national chain has outlawed punters from playing music or taking calls on speaker after complaints that the noise was driving people “nuts”.

The ban has been introduced at its 792 pubs, with bosses saying sounds from smartphones and tablet computers had become an “increasing problem in recent years”.

“Following complaints from customers, who are being driven nuts by other people’s videos and amplified phone conversations, Wetherspoon has asked customers to switch their phones to silent or to use earphones," a spokesman for the chain said.

Wetherspoon chairman and founder Sir Tim Martin added: “In a world dominated by other people’s music and amplified sound, Wetherspoon pubs are an oasis of tranquillity and contemplation.

“In order to avoid customers being driven chicken jalfrezi by a cacophony of sound, we are kindly asking phone users to pipe down.”

This comes amid Wetherspoons issuing a statement after a customer wearing a poppy was allegedly turned away.

The pub chain spoke out after reports that a customer wearing a poppy was turned away from a venue.

The incident happened at an anti-immigration demonstration outside The Star pub in Gosport, Hampshire, on Sunday, October 25.

Pub bosses have now explained extra security was in place during an outside demonstration, leading staff to refuse entry to several visitors, including poppy wearers.

A spokesman for Wetherspoons said: "In fact, Wetherspoons has not banned veterans wearing poppies.

"Wetherspoon is a long- standing supporter of the Royal British Legion Poppy Appeal and sells poppies in its pubs."

A video posted on social media shows one security staff member answering questions about people entering wearing a poppy.

In the video, he said: "You put it in your pocket and take it off, mate; yes, you can, but don't put it on inside... you'll be asked to leave."
