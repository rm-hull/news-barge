The suicide prevention and mental health support charity the Samaritans is to close and merger branches in a major overhaul to the charity, The Herald can reveal.

Volunteers were told of the changes by chief executive Julie Bentley on Monday with the reforms sending shock waves through the organisation.

Four Scottish branches, in Caithness, Orkney, Shetland and Stornoway, will be among seven across the UK to close with ones in Grimsby, Scunthorpe and Worthing also shutting.

Inverness and Elgin will merge into a new location to form a new Highlands branch, under the overhaul with a separate merger also taking place in the north of England, where Halifax and Huddersfield will close and merge into Bradford to form a new West Yorkshire hub.

The charity currently has 19 branches in Scotland which it says "provides a vital lifeline for people and communities across the country".

One senior volunteer told The Herald: "This is a complete betrayal of the volunteers and goes against everything the central charity told us last year when they said they suggested that nothing would happen until 2028.

"Closing these branches is just the start. It will break essential links with communities all over the Highlands and Islands and make it much harder for people to volunteer. What is happening is a national disgrace.”

Last year The Herald revealed that two volunteers were dismissed by the Samaritans for speaking out against the reforms when they were proposed.

They said the closures and mergers would lead to less support for people suffering a mental health crisis as people in branches that were closing would leave the charity.

The dismissals of the senior volunteers last year led to the charity being accused of "silencing dissent".

![The closures and mergers of Samaritans branches comes amid a growing mental health crisis in Scotland.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20449168.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The closures and mergers of Samaritans branches comes amid a growing mental health crisis in Scotland. *(Image: Newsquest)*

The changes set out in an email by Ms Bentley this Monday, which has been leaked to The Herald, are part of the charity's aim to cut the number of branches across the UK by 50% within the next ten years, and are being made within a wider set of reforms to the organisation's operation, and which were agreed by the charity's Board.

She told volunteers the closure and mergers had been agreed by the charity's Board and listed the changes as follows:

"• Seven branches will close: Caithness, Grimsby, Orkney, Scunthorpe, Shetland, Stornoway and Worthing.

• Three branches will be supported with investment to support long-term growth: Bradford, Brighton and Reigate.

• Five branches will be part of two separate mergers: Halifax and Huddersfield will close and merge into Bradford to form a new West Yorkshire hub; Inverness and Elgin will merge into a new location to form a new Highlands branch."

Ms Bentley said the changes were being made to ensure "Samaritans can be there for more people in emotional crisis, more quickly, and for many years to come" and were being brought about for a number of reasons including "changing patterns in volunteering, greater governance and compliance requirements, and growing pressure" on resources.

"Many of you will remember that we first began discussions around our future in spring 2025. Following the Board decisions last autumn we have implemented a number of the agreed changes this year including stepping back from several UK services and working to explore the development of others, such as expanding online chat and piloting home listening," she said.

"In my last Future Proofing email update to you, I shared a reminder of the key challenges we’re facing including changing patterns in volunteering, greater governance and compliance requirements, and growing pressure on our limited resources."

She added: "As the Board shared previously, it is our view that over the coming 10 years Samaritans’ branch network will reduce by at least 50% as we move to fewer but larger and more accessible branches, in strategic locations across the UK and Channel Islands. Larger branches can provide greater service resilience, and more consistent service delivery, helping us answer more calls.

"We committed to doing this in a gradual and careful way and previously identified two UK Pathfinder regions (Yorkshire and Humberside and South East) and one Pathfinder nation (Scotland) to help us shape a future model. These three areas are where we intend to begin testing the scale up of some branches, alongside the merger and closure of some existing locations."

She continued: "The Pathfinders will also be leading the development of new approaches to outreach, and better service delivery collaboration via shared regional and national rotas. If you volunteer outside of these Pathfinder areas, please be assured there are no immediate changes or decisions pending for your branch."

Ms Bentley said the reforms "have not been easy conversations for anyone involved", noting the situation was "particularly difficult for the volunteers who are directly impacted by the changes".

She continued: "I want to be clear that the decisions to close and merge these branches is in no way a reflection on the commitment or contribution of the volunteer teams at these locations, past or present, or on the tireless work of local branch leadership. These changes form an important part of our test-and-learn approach. Learning from their implementation will help inform future decisions as Pathfinder work continues."

The closures and mergers of Samaritans branches comes amid a growing mental health crisis in Scotland.

Last year the professional body representing Scotland’s 1,500 psychiatrists said the nation is facing a mental health emergency with cases more than doubling while the psychiatric workforce has reduced by almost a quarter.

The Royal College of Psychiatrists in Scotland (RCPsych) published a manifesto ahead of the 2026 Scottish parliamentary elections calling for the government and all Holyrood candidates to support a statutory commitment that at least 10% of NHS spending should go on mental health services.

Neil Mathers, Executive Director for Samaritans Scotland, said: “Samaritans is making changes to how we deliver our listening service across the UK, moving towards fewer, larger branches so we can strengthen volunteer capacity and provide more reliable support for anyone who needs us.

"This will help ensure callers can reach a volunteer more quickly, particularly during times when callers wait too long for an answer."

He added: Volunteers are at the heart of everything we do, and we are hugely grateful for the dedication and compassion shown by those across Scotland. We recognise that it is difficult for volunteers to hear their branch will close or change. We are working closely with everyone affected to offer support and explore new ways they can continue contributing to Samaritans’ work.

"Although the way we organise our listening service is changing, our commitment to communities remains the same. We will continue to be visible and active across Scotland, working with volunteers and partners to maintain local presence, outreach, and representation.”

Mr Mathers added: "Our helpline operates nationally, connecting callers to the next available volunteer wherever they are based. Anyone who needs support can continue to contact Samaritans free, day or night, on 116 123."
