A leading North Yorkshire steel fabrication company has spoken out following the Government's latest review, which confirms a significant shortage of skilled welders.

The Migration Advisory Committee’s July 2026 report recommends adding the trade to the Temporary Shortage List, with 18 months of access.

Chris Houston, managing director of Tadcaster-based Tadweld, said: "The welding trade has been the backbone of British industry for generations, and we now have Government confirmation that there is a genuine shortage of skilled people in the profession.

"This is no longer simply an industry warning.




"The evidence is there.

"We have a shortage of welders at a time when the UK is simultaneously trying to expand manufacturing, strengthen defence, deliver new infrastructure and accelerate the transition to clean energy."

Welding is now listed as a priority occupation within advanced manufacturing, according to Skills England.

Its 2026 assessment found that 71 percent of advanced manufacturing’s priority roles are already experiencing critical or elevated demand.

Skills England and the Department for Business and Trade estimate that between 2025 and 2035, the sector will need 47,000 additional workers, with a further 101,000 required to replace those leaving the workforce.

This brings the total estimated need to 148,000 workers.

Mr Houston said: "Welding is one of those skills that can easily be overlooked until you need it.

"But whether we're talking about structural steel, energy infrastructure, defence, marine engineering, construction or advanced manufacturing, somebody has to have the skills to actually fabricate and join those components.

"You can have the investment, the designs and the technology, but without skilled people on the shop floor, those projects cannot become reality."

He said the recruitment and training of new welders is now an urgent national issue.

Mr Houston said: "We need to stop thinking of welding as an old-fashioned trade.

"It is a highly skilled engineering profession, and it is going to be essential to some of the UK's biggest economic and infrastructure projects over the next decade.

"The question isn't whether we will need welders.

"The Government's own evidence makes clear that we already do.

"The question is whether we can train enough people quickly enough."

Tadweld is supporting initiatives to develop the next generation of skilled workers through apprenticeships and training schemes.

In March 2026, Skills England approved a new publicly funded Level 2 Welding (mechanised) apprenticeship unit, requiring a minimum of 90 hours of training.

The unit aims to help employers upskill existing welders in mechanised processes such as submerged arc, orbital TIG, and plasma arc welding.
