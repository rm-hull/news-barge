## 'We're one of the few venues left' - Crescent owner's plea over developer plans

After more than a year, The Crescent owners Ed and Bob Leyland are still awaiting the decision of the planning application for the former malt house next door, which could, they say, place the venue under threat *(Image: Google Maps)*

The owner of a popular York music venue says he has spent more than a year monitoring a controversial planning application which could threaten both his business and the city’s wider music scene.

Ed Leyland has owned The Crescent with his brother Bob since 2015.

Together, the brothers have transformed the venue from a working men's club, whose roots stretch back to 1904, into one of York's leading independent live music venues.




But this, Ed says, could all come to an end should the former malt house next door be converted into 20 hotel rooms, residencies and commercial space.

Ed fears the music venue would spark noise complaints from the hotel and residential properties - and ultimately jeopardise The Crescent's future.

The developers, Crescent Developments (York) Ltd, insist the scheme would have “no adverse impact” on The Crescent - a claim contested by 872 objectors to the planning application.

![Plans for the former malt house, next door to The Crescent, include a proposal to convert the building and the adjacent yard to 20 hotel rooms, nine residences and commercial space](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21633582.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Plans for the former malt house, next door to The Crescent, include a proposal to convert the building and the adjacent yard to 20 hotel rooms, nine residences and commercial space *(Image: Crescent Development (York) Ltd)*

Ed said: "It's been more than a year now and we're still waiting for a decision.”

The application has been granted two extensions by City of York Council - the latest while planners awaited a noise assessment of the site.

Ed added: "We've had to carry that uncertainty for a year while trying to run the business as normal."

Speaking about the proposal, Crescent Developments Ltd said: “The redevelopment proposals have been fundamentally shaped by the need to respond successfully to the sensitivity of the site surroundings, in terms of both established uses and existing character.

“We understand that construction close to homes and businesses is a concern.

“If permission is granted, contractor access, deliveries and working hours would be managed through a Construction Management Plan, developed in liaison with neighbouring residents and businesses, to minimise disruption during construction.”

## 'It's such an easy conflict to avoid if common sense is applied from the start'

Ed argued the development would bring little benefit to residents while increasing pressure on York's already “fragile” live music sector.

Pointing to similar disputes elsewhere in the city, he said complaints often stem not from music itself but from people queuing, arriving at and leaving venues.

He said: "Once people are out on the street, it's not the music venue creating the noise anymore.

"The moment you introduce something that affects the venue, you create conflict between two businesses where none existed before.

"The hotel could receive poor reviews because of noise, which would then put pressure on The Crescent.

"It's such an easy conflict to avoid if common sense is applied from the start."

![“The accommodation next to the venue is commercial space with hotel rooms on the upper floors with 24-hour on-site management; it is not Airbnb accommodation](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21633588.jpg?type=mds-article-620) “The accommodation next to the venue is commercial space with hotel rooms on the upper floors with 24-hour on-site management; it is not Airbnb accommodation", the developer confirmed *(Image: Crescent Developments (York) Ltd)*

Crescent Developments (York) Ltd said that specialist acoustic advice concluded that noise from the venue would not “adversely affect the development”.

A spokesperson added: “The accommodation next to the venue is commercial space with hotel rooms on the upper floors with 24-hour on-site management; it is not Airbnb accommodation.

“The residential apartments are positioned on the opposite side of the development, away from the venue.

“This application follows planning consent for an office scheme granted in May 2023.

“The current proposals retain the same overall height and massing parameters, with the design further developed through engagement with council officers to accommodate the revised mix of uses.

“The overall relationship with neighbouring properties is consistent with that already found acceptable under the approved scheme.”

## 'We're one of the few independent venues of this size left'

Ed warned York's music scene already lacks enough venues to support artists as they grow.

"We don't have a very healthy music ecosystem," he said.

"Local bands progress to venues like ours and then there's no obvious next step in York, so they end up moving on to Leeds or Hull.

"We're one of the few independent venues of this size left."

The plans have also drawn objections from York Civic Trust, the Council for British Archaeology, Historic Buildings and Places, The Victorian Society and York CAMRA.

A statement from the Music Venue Trust – a charity which represents more than 800 grassroots music venues across the UK – said that there was an “increased risk” that the development would lead to noise complaints.

A representative said: “The proximity of a new residential unit to this venue would place the future of the grassroots music venue under sustained risk of permanent closure.

“Across 2024 and 2025, the Music Venue Trust Emergency Response Service saw a significant increase in emergency cases.

“Threats to venues from noise complaints were a key trend, while noise abatement orders and other neighbour disputes were identified as a contributing factor to permanent closure.”

## 'I'm not defeatist'

Despite the delays, Ed said he remains optimistic that “common sense will prevail”.

He said: “I’m not defeatist.

"If this application stands up to rigorous examination by a committee, I think they'll look at it and say it probably isn't going to work. I find it hard to believe people would think it's a logical thing to do.”

He added: “York needs more music venues. It needs more studio space for artists.

"There are buildings that could have been used as creative spaces for years while people decided what to do with them, but instead they've been left empty.

"The malt house is one of the few remaining examples of that kind of historic industrial building in the city centre, and that's important."

The developer confirmed that it had continued to work with council officers to ensure all the requirements for the application were, including clarification on noise and design issues.

A spokesperson for the developer said: “We hope the application can proceed to planning committee shortly."
