The chancellor has unveiled cuts to the cost of supermarket staples including baked beans, gherkins and chocolate ahead of his major Budget announcement this week.

John Healey has announced the removal of import tariffs on over 100 products, as Andy Burnham pledges to deliver “breathing space” for families and businesses in the first Budget under his leadership.

The move is one of a series of so-called “everyday fixes” announced before the Budget on Wednesday, and comes alongside changes to parental pay and carers allowance.

Products expected to see their tariffs cut also include jams, marmalades, garlic and toffees, alongside kerosene and fertilisers.

Meanwhile the chancellor announced he will make the 55p per mile tax-free mileage rates permanent, and that parental pay will be extended to more than 150,000 additional parents per year, including self-employed dads and partners.

The prime minister said the changes will “make life a little easier” for Britons, and said: “At Wednesday’s Budget, we’ll go further to give families and businesses the breathing space they need today, while we build a stronger economy, transform our country and bring down cost for people long-term.”

## Chancellor set to add extra 2p to price of a pint

The price of a pint will rise by 2p when the chancellor hikes alcohol taxes on Wednesday.

John Healey is poised to announce an increase in alcohol duty at the Budget, which will be line with Retail Prices Index (RPI) inflation, which is set at 3.9 per cent.

It would add 2p to the duty on a pint, around 10p to 12p on a bottle of wine, and 35p to a bottle of 37.5 per cent gin.

Several industry groups had urged the chancellor to drop plans to increase alcohol taxes.

Miles Beale, chief executive of the Wine and Spirit Trade Association, said the government should be “supporting not squeezing” the British drinks sector.

“The Chancellor cannot control global fuel prices, but he can put the brakes on relentless and punishing alcohol tax increases, which have consistently failed to raise revenue. He needs to change the record – because it’s broken,” he said.

“Labour should not be continuing a failing tax regime that was designed and started by a Conservative government. Wine and spirit producers and retailers across the country are struggling to absorb the hefty duty increases and consumers can’t afford them. Another tax rise will simply mean that more businesses will fold.

“The WSTA continues to call for Government to recognise the immense pressure consumers, businesses, pubs and restaurants are under and put a stop to the tax raids.”

## Chancellor: 'Everyday fixes are small part of giving families breathing space'

John Healey said: “Today’s everyday fixes are just a small part of what we are doing to give families a bit of breathing space.

“Conflict in the Middle East and Europe is hitting families here at home through higher everyday costs.

“We can’t control global events, but we can act where we can make a difference.”

## Burnham wants to give every rough sleeper an offer of help by year’s end

Andy Burnham said he wanted to give “every single person” sleeping on the streets an offer of help by the end of this year.

It comes as the Budget is set to deliver a £432-million boost to help ensure homeless people are off the streets by Christmas.

Speaking at a rough sleeping summit in Downing Street, Mr Burnham said: “Just think how it will feel if we get to the end of this year… and we know at a national level, that we did give every single person an offer of help this end of year.”

He went on: “I am asking you all from the front here today. Please, we must turn this into something tangible for everybody.

“It doesn’t mean that everybody will be able to take up the offer and everybody will be able to have their life turned around.

“But the fact that we give them that offer will put enough hope in their heart that something will be coming and something will change as we go into the new year.

“That’s our collective mission, everybody.”

## Ministers unveil five ‘everyday fixes’ to help with living costs ahead of Budget

![Andy Burnham (right) meeting with John Healey at No11 Downing Street](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/26/20/01M5WS77R711JMDJ2R89GA6F44.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ministers have unveiled five “everyday fixes” to help people with the cost-of-living crisis ahead of Wednesday’s Budget.

A higher tax-free mileage allowance will be made permanent, and tariffs on a range of foods will be slashed to bring down their prices, the chancellor and prime minister have announced as part of a series of steps to help with heightened living costs.

Andy Burnham also said the government will do more to give people and businesses “breathing space” at Wednesday’s fiscal event, beyond the measures he and John Healey have already announced.

Under the raft of measures revealed on Tuesday, and which are expected to be included in Wednesday’s Budget, the chancellor will lock in the 55p mileage rate for those who have to drive for work, for the first 10,000 miles.

Rates above that will be raised from 25p to 35p, with the government hoping the overall package will help a total of three million employees and self-employed people.

The government is also removing tariffs on more than 100 products, including favourite foods such as baked beans, chocolate and gherkins.

This will save households some £21 on food shopping over the course of the year, according to the Treasury.

Tariff cuts on fertilisers are meanwhile aimed at farmers, while a tariff cut on kerosene will help homes which use the fuel as a heating oil.

Ministers also plan to extend parental pay to more than 150,000 parents each year, including self-employed fathers who had previously missed out.

A cliff edge on carer’s allowance will be ended, with the government instead tapering away £1 of the benefit for every £1 earned above the weekly limit.

## Stocks rally before Budget and interest rate calls

Stocks in London started the week on the front foot, ending Monday in the green, as investors looked ahead to the autumn Budget and a busy week of central bank interest rate calls.

In London, the FTSE 100 index ended up 76.97 points, 0.7 per cent, at 10,767.33. The FTSE 250 ended 263.86 points higher, 1.1 per cent, at 24,501.25, and the AIM all-share advanced 6.51 points, 0.9 per cent, at 753.18.

In London, investors are looking ahead to Wednesday’s autumn Budget at which chancellor John Healey is expected to announce an investment boost, and modest tax rises, as he seeks to placate the City, and keep backbench MPs on side.

“Wednesday’s Budget will be the main event for UK investors, with the reaction in bond and currency markets likely to matter more than the individual measures announced,” said Tom Stevenson, investment director at Fidelity International.

“With long bond yields in the UK approaching 6%, the most important job for the chancellor, John Healey, will be to reassure investors about the outlook for borrowing, growth and the public finances,” he added.

*The Financial Times* reported that Mr Healey is preparing to announce £20.4 billion in additional funding for the country’s state-backed business lender and infrastructure bank.

The funding, to be provided over roughly five years, is expected to create and support 100,000 jobs in sectors including artificial intelligence, defence and green energy by 2035, according to the Treasury.
