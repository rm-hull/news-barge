Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has led a cross-party group of 130 MPs and peers calling on the Chancellor to reform student loans ahead of the Autumn Budget on 28 October.

In a letter dated 26 October to Chancellor John Healey, parliamentarians from Labour, the Conservatives, the Liberal Democrats, the Greens, Plaid Cymru and other political groupings have urged the Government to address concerns over student loan interest, repayment thresholds and changes to loan terms.

The letter argues that the current system is placing an unfair burden on graduates, particularly younger adults facing rising living costs, difficulties entering the housing market and a challenging graduate jobs market.

The signatories have called for three immediate measures:

* Moving student loan interest from the Retail Prices Index (RPI) to the Consumer Prices Index (CPI), through a cross-party commission on student loan reform.
* Reversing the planned freeze on the Plan 2 repayment threshold before it takes effect in April 2027.
* Guaranteeing that student loan terms will not be changed retrospectively in future.

The Plan 2 repayment threshold was frozen for a further three years from April 2027 as part of the 2025 Budget. The letter argues that this should be reversed before the change begins affecting graduates’ pay packets.

It also refers to a Treasury Committee report, *Student loans: Broken and unfair?*, which called on the Government to reverse the freeze in the Autumn Budget, describing it as a moral obligation.

Gordon says parliamentary questions he tabled revealed that a record two million graduates can no longer keep up with the interest accruing on their loans, meaning their outstanding balances continue to rise despite repayments.

The letter argues that graduates were originally offered loans on terms that have subsequently changed, undermining confidence in the system.

> **Tom Gordon MP said:**
>
> As I set out when introducing my Student Finance (Review) Bill in September, no party comes to this with clean hands. All parties of government in recent decades have played a role in how we got here. That is exactly why we have come together across party lines to write to you. No party can claim the moral high ground, so every party can be part of the solution.

Beyond the immediate Budget measures, the group is calling for a full cross-party review of student finance.

The proposed review would examine repayment rates, the relationship between repayment thresholds and average earnings, and the original terms on which loans were offered. It would also consider how reforms could benefit existing borrowers as well as future students.

Gordon introduced his Student Finance (Review) Bill in September, calling for a review of student finance in England.

The letter concludes by asking the Chancellor to meet with the group to discuss a long-term solution.
