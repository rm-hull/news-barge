Alan Shearer has praised a Northumberland dad’s incredible determination to walk for charity, eight years after a life-changing injury left him paralysed.

Keith Dungait, 50, from Morpeth, sustained a spinal cord injury in 2018 when he dived into a swimming pool while on holiday in Majorca and hit his head on the bottom.

Doctors feared he would never walk again.

But, after years of physiotherapy and rehabilitation, he has gradually regained the ability to take supported steps.

When Mr Dungait heard about the Alan Shearer Foundation’s 'Move a Mile' fundraising challenge, he set a goal of walking 2,775 steps - equivalent to one mile based on his stride length.

Starting with just 50 steps a day using a Zimmer frame, he steadily worked towards his target, eventually completing the full mile and raising £2,775 for the Foundation’s Million Pound Mission appeal.

Alan Shearer, the former Newcastle United and England captain, was so moved by Mr Dungait’s story that he visited his home in Morpeth on Friday, October 23, to thank him in person.

Mr Shearer said: "When I first heard about Keith and what he’d been doing for the Foundation, I was absolutely blown away.

"To go through what he has and then take on a challenge like this to help other people is just incredible.

"Move a Mile was always about showing that anyone can get involved, no matter their ability or circumstances, and I don’t think there’s a better example of that than Keith.

"I wanted to come and meet him personally, thank him for everything he’s done and let him know just how much his support means to us.

"Every pound raised brings us closer to building a second hydrotherapy pool at the Centre and helping even more families across the North East.

"People like Keith are making a real difference, and I’m incredibly grateful."

During the visit, Mr Shearer joined Mr Dungait for a few steps before being presented with a cheque for the funds raised.

The money will go towards the foundation’s goal of raising £1 million to build a second hydrotherapy pool at the Alan Shearer Centre in Newcastle.

The centre supports more than 10,000 members and their families across the North East, offering therapies and services for people with complex disabilities.

Mr Dungait said: "After my accident, I was told I might never walk again, so to be able to say I’ve now walked a mile is something I’m really proud of.

"It’s taken a lot of hard work to get here, and some days are much harder than others, but I’ve always been determined to keep going and see what I can achieve.

"When I saw the Move a Mile challenge, I thought it was something I could get involved in, even if it meant doing it a little differently."

He added: "I knew it would take me a while, but I wanted to give it a go and hopefully raise some money for a brilliant cause along the way.

"I never expected anything like this when I started, and I’m just pleased that my efforts can help other people and families who need that support."
