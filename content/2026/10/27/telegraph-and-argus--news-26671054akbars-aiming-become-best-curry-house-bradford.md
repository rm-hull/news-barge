A curry house that has been a fixture of Bradford's dining scene for more than 30 years is aiming to win a top award.

Akbar's, on Leeds Road, wants to add another accolade to its history after reaching the final of the Telegraph & Argus Best Curry House 2026 competition.

The restaurant is one of 10 shortlisted from reader nominations, and a spokesperson said the team was "absolutely delighted" to make the final.

"It is a real honour to be recognised, particularly in a city with such a proud food culture and so many fantastic curry houses," the spokesperson said.

## From 28 seats to 10 restaurants

Akbar's opened on Leeds Road in 1995 with 28 seats and has since grown into a 10-restaurant group without losing its focus on authentic Pakistani food.

"We pride ourselves on offering authentic Pakistani cuisine inspired by the different regions of Pakistan," they said.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/18731284.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: T&A)*

Akbar's was founded by the late Shabir Hussain, whose travels across Pakistan helped shape many of the recipes and cooking styles still used today.

The spokesperson said authenticity and quality still guide the restaurant.

"Our customers are the reason Akbar's has been here for over 30 years, and we are incredibly grateful for their continued loyalty and support," the spokesperson said.

"We have had the privilege of welcoming different generations of the same families through our doors, which is something very special."

## Proud of Bradford roots

The spokesperson said it was a source of pride to be part of a city widely recognised as the UK's curry capital.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/18731282.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: T&A)*

"That recognition celebrates not just the restaurants, but the people, families, skills and traditions that have helped establish Bradford as a destination for exceptional curry," the spokesperson said.

Like many hospitality businesses, Akbar's has faced mounting pressure from rising energy, food and staffing costs.

The spokesperson said the business had tried to shield customers from the full impact.

"We do not believe that continually increasing menu prices should be the first response to rising costs," the spokesperson said.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/18731279.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: T&A)*

"People work hard for their money, and we want families and friends to be able to enjoy a meal together at Akbar's without feeling that dining out has become unaffordable."

## 'Incredibly proud' of recognition

Community involvement also remains important, with the restaurant regularly supporting students celebrating GCSE and A-level results.

The spokesperson said becoming a finalist had been a boost for staff, who were "incredibly proud" of the recognition.

"Behind every successful restaurant is a dedicated group of people who work hard every day to deliver great food and excellent service," the spokesperson said.

"Winning would be a fantastic achievement and a wonderful way to recognise their commitment and hard work."
