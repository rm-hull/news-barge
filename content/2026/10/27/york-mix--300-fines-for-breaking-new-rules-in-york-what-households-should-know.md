New rules controlling what wood burning stoves people can have and what they can burn in York are set to come into force in days.

Open fires must use smokeless fuel and households will have to have Government-approved stoves across York under the Smoke Control Area (SCA) which comes into effect on Sunday, November 1.

The new rules will also ban prolonged smoke from chimneys attached to buildings for more than 15 minutes after lighting a fire and repeat rule breakers could face £300 fines.

Cllr Jenny Kent, City of York Council’s Labour environment spokesperson, said the changes would help improve air quality and support better health for everyone in the city.

Peter Roderick, the council’s public health lead, said solid fuel burning emitted particles that harm everyone especially children and older people.

The replacement of various existing control orders which already cover about four fifths of the council’s administrative area with one blanket SCA was backed in March.

Stoves and solid fuel burning will not be banned outright but the rules set out which appliances households can own and what they can burn.

Requirements under the new regime also include properly drying and seasoning wood before burning it.

People should also look for the Ready to Burn logo which indicates that wood fuel approved for use.

Council officials are set to work with households to understand the changes but repeat offenders could face a fine of up to £300.

Outdoor barbeques, chimineas, fire pits and bonfires are not subject to the new rules and are covered by separate laws.

More than a third of York households which burn solid fuels are outside existing controlled areas which do not cover some places set for future house-building, according to council officials.

Solid fuel burning is a major contributor to PM2.5 fine particulate matter, a pollutant responsible for one in every 23 deaths in York in 2023 according to public health estimates.

Recent research has highlighted the harm that can be done to the health of children in homes with wood-burning stoves, councillors heard.

The elderly, pregnant women and people with some pre-existing health conditions are also vulnerable to pollutants produced by burning solid fuels.

Cllr Kent said the changes were about ensuring everyone could benefit from cleaner air.

She said: “This is not about saying people can’t use stoves or open fires.

“It is about helping people use the right fuel in the right way.”

Mr Roderick said expanding the SCA was a proportionate, evidence-based step to reduce preventable deaths.

The official said: “Solid fuel burning accounts for nearly a third of PM2.5 emissions in York.

“There are no safe levels of these particles, they harm everyone.”
