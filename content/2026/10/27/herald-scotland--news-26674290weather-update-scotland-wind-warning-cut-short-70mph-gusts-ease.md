A Met Office yellow warning for high winds covering the whole of Scotland and the far north-east of Northern Ireland has been cut short.

Forecasters are now predicting the bad weather to end at 6pm - three hours earlier than expected.

The reprieve from the elements comes on a day when of schools were shut and people faced travel disruption as as gusts of up to 70mph hit the country.

All schools in the Western Isles closed on Tuesday, while in the Highlands 30 primary schools and six secondary schools shut due to weather-related issues, alongwith eight in Aberdeenshire.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637152.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Wind surfers brave the conditions on North Beach during storm Chloe on October 27, 2026 in Troon, Scotland *(Image: Jeff J Mitchell/Getty Images)*

The Met Office warned that gusts of 50-60 mph were possible, while some exposed or coastal areas in the north-west and far north of Scotland could see gusts of 70 mph or more.

In North Ayrshire, the search for a person thought to have been swept into the sea at Largs by large waves was suspended after nobody was found.

HM Coastguard said a coastguard helicopter and aeroplane went to the scene, along with RNLI lifeboats from Largs and Troon and coastguard rescue teams, when the alarm was raised at about 12.20am on Tuesday, as did police and the fire service.

The coastguard said that following an “extensive search”, nobody was found and searches were suspended at about 9am, pending any new information.

Police Scotland said: “A search-and-rescue operation was launched but the person remains missing.”

The Met Office said that a gust of 76mph was recorded at Stornoway Airport on Tuesday and one of 71mph on Tiree.

The latest weather warning comes as the clean-up continues following Storm Boelo which hit the UK over the weekend and left two men dead.

On the railways, speed restrictions are in place on some routes in the north and west of Scotland on Tuesday.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637151.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A fallen tree between Drumchapel and Drumry on the Singer line. *(Image: Network Rail Scotland /Press Association)*

Network Rail Scotland said restrictions are in place on the west Highland line (including to Oban and Mallaig), far north line, Highland main line (Blair Atholl – Dalwhinnie), Inverness – Kyle of Lochalsh, and Aberdeen – Inverness routes.

In the west, they are in force on the Girvan – Stranraer, Kilwinning – Largs and Dalreoch – Helensburgh Central routes.

ScotRail said train services between Edinburgh and Dundee will be delayed or revised due to high winds on the Tay Bridge.

In Ayrshire services between Kilwinning and Largs/Ardrossan were suspended late morning due to high waves near the railway line between Stevenston and Saltcoats.

Some ferry services are also disrupted due to the weather while on the roads the northbound onslip onto the A9 at Stockbridge, Dunblane, was shut due to a fallen tree, according to Traffic Scotland.
