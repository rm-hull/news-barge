Residents have been urged to prepare for severe weather after emergency crews tackled a string of storm-related incidents.

West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service (WYFRS) received a high volume of calls over the weekend due to Storm Boelo.

## Planks from scaffolding blown over

At Byram Court on Shipley Fields Road, strong winds blew wooden planks from scaffolding outside a block of flats.

A resident said one plank narrowly missed a car, while firefighters removed about 20 planks and taped off the area.

A spokesperson for the fire service said: "We attended an incident at Byram Court on Sunday following a call received at 12:07pm.

"The incident was attended by Odsal, who secured the scaffolding planks before leaving the scene at 1:48pm."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21628926.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A wooden plank which was blown off scaffolding in Shipley *(Image: UGC)*

## How do I report fallen trees?

Crews also tackled several weather-related emergencies over the weekend, including fallen trees and debris.

Dave Walton, deputy chief fire officer at WYFRS, said: "Over the weekend, West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service (WYFRS) received numerous calls relating to fallen trees and storm-related debris.

"While we understand the concern that fallen trees, branches and debris can cause, the Fire and Rescue Service will only attend these incidents where there is a threat to life, a risk of serious injury or a significant and immediate risk to public safety."

He urged residents to report non-emergency hazards to their local council, not the fire service.

Mr Walton said: "Fallen trees and storm debris that are obstructing roads, footpaths or public spaces, but do not present an immediate danger to life, should be reported to the relevant local authority through the appropriate reporting channels."

![The splintered trunk shows the force of the winds brought by Storm Boelo.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21630266.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The splintered trunk shows the force of the winds brought by Storm Boelo. *(Image: T&A)*

## Call 999 if there is a risk to life

With more storms expected during the autumn and winter months, Mr Walton urged residents to take simple steps to protect their homes and reduce pressure on emergency services.

He said: "The advice from WYFRS will remain consistent, and this calmer period provides an ideal opportunity for residents to prepare for future adverse weather.

"Loose outdoor items such as trampolines, garden furniture, bins and temporary structures should be secured where possible.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21630949.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Highway maintenance workers carry out repairs *(Image: T&A)*

"We also encourage people to keep up to date with weather warnings, plan journeys carefully and allow extra time for travel where conditions are challenging."

He stressed that using the right reporting channels helps keep fire crews available for emergencies.

Mr Walton said: "By taking simple preventative measures and using the correct reporting routes for fallen trees and storm debris, people can help ensure fire crews remain available to respond to emergencies where their specialist skills are most needed.

"If there is an immediate risk to life or a genuine emergency, always call 999."
