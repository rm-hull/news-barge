YORK’S council is facing a **£32.3 million** budget black hole, prompting plans to approach the Government for talks about its financial options.

**Cllr Katie Lomas, City of York Council’s Labour finance spokesperson**, said officials would explore all available options but there would be no easy solutions given the scale of the challenges.

**Labour Council Leader Cllr Claire Douglas** said it came as demand for services and adult social care particularly had risen rapidly while national funding had reduced.

**Liberal Democrat opposition leader Cllr Nigel Ayre said** the council needed to be open about what support it is seeking and whether it would involve council tax rises or borrowing.

**Conservative group leader Cllr Chris Steward** said they were concerned that the council had gotten into such a bad financial position and warned against kicking the can down the road.

It comes as the council’s executive is set to receive an update on the authority’s finances on Tuesday, 3 November as work gets underway on its budget for 2027/8.

The report stated the council was expected to see some of the most significant financial challenges it has ever experienced.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/cllr-claire-douglas-city-of-york-council.webp)

Officials added the measures required to deal with the £32.3 million black hole forecast between 2027 and 2030 would be extremely challenging for residents.

Factors including inflation, borrowing and rising costs for adult social care and other services have all been blamed for pushing the budget gap above the £20 million previously forecast.

Work remains ongoing to reform adult social care in York and to make council services more modern and efficient to help save money.

Around £40 million has already been saved from council budgets in the last six years, with the report stating finding more savings was becoming increasingly difficult.

The report stated £18.1 million in savings were set to be needed in the next financial year.

It added options facing the council included major restructuring, cutting or stopping some services or seeking help from other organisations to provide them, and reducing borrowing for projects.

Funding allocated to major infrastructure schemes and capital projects cannot generally be used for day-to-day spending.

The forecast £32.3 million gap also follows changes in the way councils are funding nationally which has cost York millions.

Ministers have said Fair Funding reforms were needed to ensure public services are properly funded and cash reaches more deprived areas where it is needed most.

But officials in York have warned the changes would leave the council the lowest-funded authority per head in the country.

Estimates of the losses to York have ranged from £20 million to as high as £38.4 million by 2029.

The report also stated it was receiving £7.8m less in funding due to York’s council tax rates being below national averages.

The Band D charge for the current financial year in York, £1,817 excluding other precepts, is 8.1 per cent less than the level assumed by the Government for allocating funding.

## ‘Scale of challenge significant’

Cllr Douglas said they remained committed to protecting services but the financial outlook facing local government was very different to that seen a few years ago.

She said: “We have a strong track record of managing our finances responsibly and have delivered millions of pounds in savings while continuing to provide vital services for people across the city.

“However, the scale of the challenge we now face is significantly greater than in previous years.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/cllr-nigel-ayre-lib-dems-york-summer.webp)

Cllr Ayre said Labour locally and nationally were to blame for the council’s financial situation.

He said: “The report skirts around the issue but the reality is it seeks permission to go cap in hand to Government to ask for either a payday loan or council tax rises.

“If the council intend to ask for exceptional financial support they should be open with the public and explain what they are asking for and when.”

Cllr Steward said the situation was a low point for York.

He said: “Whilst the council says this will give it time to make changes it has had enough time to do that and we are unconvinced of their ability to deliver improvements.

“This looks like making the situation even worse by kicking the can down the road.”
