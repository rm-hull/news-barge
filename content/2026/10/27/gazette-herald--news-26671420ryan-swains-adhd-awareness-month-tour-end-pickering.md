A national ADHD awareness tour will conclude with a free family event in Pickering.

The event will be led by Malton-based advocate and speaker Ryan Swain, who is inviting families from across North Yorkshire to attend the session at Ryedale Special Families on Outgang Lane, Pickering, at 5pm on Wednesday, October 28.

The event marks the finale of Ryan ’s voluntary ADHD Awareness Month campaign and aims to bring together parents, carers, children, young people and professionals for an evening focused on ADHD, neurodiversity, education and inclusion.

Ryan said: "Over the last few years, I’ve dedicated myself to visiting schools, meeting families and speaking to thousands of young people, all voluntarily, because I genuinely believe education and early understanding can change lives.

"This isn’t just about children with ADHD or autism.

"It’s about every child, every classroom and every school.

"We cannot expect inclusion without education, and we cannot expect understanding if we never teach it.

"To finish ADHD Awareness Month here in North Yorkshire, alongside families and an organisation doing incredible work within our community, means a great deal to me.

"I want families to know they are welcome, whether they have a diagnosis, are awaiting an assessment, are supporting someone or simply want to learn more."

Throughout October, Ryan has travelled the UK, visiting 27 schools and educational settings to deliver talks and raise awareness about ADHD and other neurodevelopmental conditions.

By mid-October, his campaign had already reached more than 3,000 students and 250 school staff.

Special invitations to the Pickering event have been extended to MP Kevin Hollinrake and the Lord-Lieutenant of North Yorkshire in recognition of the campaign’s potential impact on education and the wider community.

The event will cover topics including emotional regulation, behaviour, mental health, common misconceptions, and the importance of early intervention.

It will also provide practical advice and support for families.

Ryan , who lives with ADHD and Tourette’s syndrome, has become a leading voice for neurodiversity awareness in education, using his experiences to challenge misconceptions and promote greater understanding.

His advocacy has reached Parliament through contributions to the All-Party Parliamentary Group on ADHD.

He has also launched a UK Parliament petition calling for mandatory, age-appropriate ADHD and neurodiversity education in schools, arguing that all pupils should understand neurological differences and that teachers and families need better information and support.

The event is free and open to parents, carers, children, young people and families from across the region.

Ryan hopes the session will inspire more people to support his petition and help drive meaningful change in ADHD education nationwide.
