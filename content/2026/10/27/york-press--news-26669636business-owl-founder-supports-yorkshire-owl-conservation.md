A business owner is marking a decade in business by helping to protect one of Yorkshire’s most well-known birds.

Jen Workman, founder of virtual assistance company Business Owl, is supporting a barn owl conservation project as part of her company’s anniversary celebrations.

She has partnered with her local woodland, Make it Wild, to sponsor a Barn Owl Box, which will help provide safe breeding sites for the birds in the Yorkshire countryside.

Ms Workman said: "I see this as a practical way to create a memorable connection between the company’s identity and its environmental credentials, including its status as Carbon Literate."

The boxes provide owls with an alternative nesting place, to compensate for the loss of traditional nesting sites in old barns, buildings and mature trees through development, renovation and changes in land use.

Helen Neave, founding director of Make it Wild, said: "We are grateful to Jen for choosing to celebrate her ten-year business milestone by sponsoring a Barn Owl Box with Make it Wild.

"These wonderful birds can struggle to find suitable habitat in which to breed and raise their young.

"We are confident this box will be occupied soon and will help this iconic species to thrive in this part of Yorkshire."

Ms Workman launched Business Owl in October 2016, drawing on her legal executive qualifications and her experience in high-level administration.

She identified a gap in the market for flexible business support that didn’t require the long-term commitment of hiring an employee.

Over the past decade, she has worked with more than 70 businesses, many of them in the local area.

Ms Workman said: "Working hard is essential in business, but working smarter will help you build resilience, which is necessary in the current economic climate."
