### Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC: one-minute review

The Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC is simply a fantastic version of an already world-beating wireless gaming headset. While it shares an awful lot with the ‘original’ Audeze Maxwell 2 that launched earlier this year, the addition of genuinely excellent active noise cancellation (ANC) means that it offers an enhanced experience compared to its brethren.

The design and build remain very similar to that older model, with the big, weighty earcups sitting comfortably over your ears while the leather headstrap keeps things secure. Controls are also nearly identical, bar the addition of the ANC button on the front of the left cup. It’s a sturdy and robust build which gives great confidence, but if you’re in any way wary of weighty sets, then the Maxwell 2 ANC might be a bit much for you — it’s a chonker at 19.75oz / 560g.

However, the heft is unequivocally worth it in my opinion: when it comes to performance, the Maxwell 2 is absolutely fantastic. The non-ANC Maxwell 2 is my pick for the best-sounding gaming headset money can buy right now, and the ANC variant sounds just as brilliant in all scenarios across different platforms.

The ANC itself is a welcome addition and a genuinely impressive one, blocking out all exterior noise in my testing, as well as offering a transparency mode. The quality of the ANC makes it perfect for busy environments, or just achieving maximal immersion in your games.

This is up there with the absolute best in the premium ANC business — like the SteelSeries Arctis Nova Pro Omni and Nova Elite I tested in recent months — and I can’t recommend it more if it fits in your budget.

### Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC: Price and availability

* **List price: $429 / £429 / AU$699**
* **Chunky increase in price over the non-ANC model**
* **Priced alongside competitors from the likes of SteelSeries, but above other wireless contenders**

The Maxwell 2 ANC is more expensive than its ‘regular’ counterpart: it’s $100 / £90 / AU$130 more. With only a few features that are different from its non-ANC brother, its value proposition will be called into question.

For that price, it’s above the likes of the SteelSeries Arctis Nova Pro Omni (a direct competitor to the Maxwell 2), which is a headset with excellent ANC and one I really would recommend as well.

### Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC: Design & features

* **Robust build with a great feature set that builds on predecessor**
* **A little heavier than the non-ANC headset**
* **Weaker battery life**

The Maxwell 2 ANC gives great confidence with its build. It feels robust, solid, and able to withstand a knock or a drop (something I can attest to), and a lot of that comes from its weightiness. Some folks might immediately associate ‘heavy gaming headset’ with ‘bad,’ but I really think that is a grave misconception and something drilled into us by the marketing of other brands.

In reality, the design and build are so nicely done that it offsets the weight, and the comfort too — largely down to its new memory foam cushions and ventilated leather head strap — means that I’ve never really noticed wearing the Maxwell 2 ANC for even the longest sessions.

In terms of on-board controls, the left cup houses the volume dial, the game-chat mix dial, an audio jack, a USB-C port, a mic port, and the new ANC button. The right cup has the mic mute switch and the power button, which also doubles up as the Bluetooth pairing button. Simple. The dual microphone is very welcome, and means you can deploy the Maxwell 2 ANC for entertainment and music, and still rely on something for comms, without plugging in the detachable boom mic.

![A picture of the Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC gaming headset sitting on a shiny surface with pale tiles in the background](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/5dxPkuNcb8VcT4g97fKBuV-1200-80.jpg)

Small improvements are also present and welcome. One of which is that the wireless dongle is much shorter than the lollipop stick found with the non-ANC model, and makes for a much tidier look on the front of a PS5 or PC. The head tracking features provided by Embody Immerse Gaming Hive and Dolby Atmos Rendered compatibility are fun and a novelty, though they still feel a little niche for me.

The most notable thing in the features of the Maxwell 2 ANC, however, sadly, is that the battery life has taken a dive compared to the regular Maxwell 2. While the non-ANC version could get you 80-odd hours of use, the ANC version is limited to about half of that with ANC off, and 35 hours with ANC on.

It’s perfectly serviceable and still provides you with comfortably more than a full day, but it is a shame the difference is so massive.

### Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC: Performance

* **Incredible audio quality across all games**
* **Excellent ANC performance that doesn’t kill bass**
* **Comfortable to wear, plus a really solid mic**

The Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC is simply fantastic when put to the test. The gaming audio it provides is every bit as good as the audio I love from the regular Maxwell 2.

No matter what I’ve been playing, the Maxwell 2 ANC provides thumping combat sounds, beautifully crisp environmental audio, and rich, rounded soundtracks. It’s a joy to use as a media and entertainment pair of headphones too, with music singing clearly into my ears. Planar magnetic driver audio really is something else.

Backing this up is the microphone combo, which ensures I’m clear and crisp with my teammates even in the most frantic of online multiplayer battles.

Key to the audio sounding so good all the time is that the main USP of the headset, the ANC, *doesn’t* kill bass like almost every other ANC I’ve tried in my years of testing.

This means that you really are getting uncompromising audio, *and* some very effective ANC too; it’s excellent for immersion, and even in a busy household with a few people, I’ve been unbothered by external noise. This is undoubtedly aided by Audeze’s SLAM (Symmetric Linear Acoustic Modulator) tech, which is proven to assist in bass performance.

![A close up picture of the Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC gaming headset&#039;s SLAM vent](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/kSLrpGvqzJanycvL9L5DQ8-1200-80.jpg)

The weight of the headset doesn’t impact the performance of the Maxwell 2 ANC from a comfort perspective either. It might be heavier than other headsets, sure, but it’s never been uncomfortable on my head, even in the longest of sessions. I've almost come to prefer it over lightweight models you totally forget you’re wearing.

Elsewhere, I will say that by default the Maxwell 2 ANC can be quite quiet, which was a mild annoyance, though easily solved on PC or PS5 by manually adjusting the volume. The companion Audeze app on desktop and mobile has also been handy, facilitating firmware updates and allowing for EQ tinkering.

All in all, the Maxwell 2 ANC has all that is great about the non-ANC variant and adds excellent ANC. If you have to have the latter and are eyeing up the best version of the best-sounding headset, then this is the one to get.

### Should I buy the Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC?

## Buy it if...

**You want the best version of the best-sounding gaming headset going**  
If you’re on the hunt for the best-sounding gaming headset money can buy right now, and want to make sure you get the most up-to-date variant, then the Audeze Maxwell 2 ANC is the one for you.

**You need excellent ANC in a gaming headset**  
If ANC is at the top of your desired feature list, then what’s on offer here in the Maxwell 2 ANC - combined with its sheer audio quality and prowess - is certainly something to take notice of.

**You’re an audiophile gamer chasing the best in audio from specialist drivers**  
If you’re someone who really, really cares about your gaming audio and wants something extra special that’s geared toward audio lovers, then the Maxwell 2 ANC’s planar magnetic drivers, in conjunction with its ANC and other features, are perfect for you.

**You’re on one, maybe two, platforms only**  
While it can’t connect to lots of devices simultaneously like some of the competition, if you’re looking for one of the best gaming headsets money can buy for a small number of platforms, then this version of the Audeze Maxwell 2 needs to be considered.

## Don't buy it if...

**You’re wary of weighty headsets**  
Personally, I think this is a grossly overstated ‘con’ of gaming headsets, but the fact remains: if you’ve grown used to lightweight headsets, then the Maxwell 2 ANC won’t be for you.

**You already have a Maxwell 2**  
As great as the ANC is on this new version of the Maxwell 2, if it’s a feature you can do without, then I’d recommend the normal version of the Maxwell 2 to save some cash but still get that supreme audio experience.
