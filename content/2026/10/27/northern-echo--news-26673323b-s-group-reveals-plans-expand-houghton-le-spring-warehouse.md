A business providing pharmaceutical products to hospitals, surgeries and pharmacies across the region has submitted plans to boost storage and streamline its operations.

The application to Sunderland City Council’s planning department is seeking permission for a single-storey warehouse extension to unit one in Dubmire Industrial Estate, Houghton-le-Spring.

The plans aim to provide extra internal storage, sortation and loading space for business B&S Group, which is involved in the distribution of pharmaceutical products.

An accompanying planning statement says the plans would support the firm’s “continued operation and efficiency” through the installation of automated picking equipment and better storage and sortation space.

Those behind the scheme added that plans would allow operations to be more scheduled and efficient and would “support business efficiency and service provision to hospitals, surgeries and pharmacies, particularly within the northern regions”.

The planning statement goes on: “The extension would be used as an extension to the existing storage and sortation area.

“It would provide additional room to hold stock on racking, improve the layout of the operation and allow the business to introduce an automated picking system similar to its London operation.

“The purpose of the proposal is to support the continued operation of the existing business, improve efficiency, increase internal storage capacity, provide improved weather protection and streamline loading and distribution arrangements.”

Planning documents confirmed that the proposed development would not change or increase the hours of operation at the site and added there would not be any processes generating odour, fumes, dust, vibration or significant noise.

Applicants said the proposed extension would reduce external disruption by bringing more activity indoors, with deliveries loaded directly into the warehouse rather than through the open yard area.

The planning statement adds: “The development would support the continued operation of the existing pharmaceutical storage and distribution business at Unit 1 Mulberry Way.

“It would provide additional internal storage and sortation space, allow improved picking and packing efficiency and help streamline loading and dispatch operations.

“The site is located within an established employment area and the proposed use would remain consistent with the existing warehouse/distribution function of the premises.”
