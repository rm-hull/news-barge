A new national company dedicated to traditional music is needed to address a major “anomaly” in the arts in Scotland, Màiri McAllan has declared.

The Scottish Government’s culture secretary has insisted it must have “parity” with other art forms, including theatre, opera and classical music.

Ms McAllan has defended plans to create the new company at a time when the country’s existing national companies are campaigning for a rethink of their financial support after more than a decade of “standstill” support.

However, the culture secretary insisted she is “open-minded” about the form the new company will take, after she was warned about its potential impact on the country’s traditional arts “ecosystem”.

Ms McAllan was speaking at Holyrood’s culture committee, where she refused to say who had been consulted on the proposed national company before it was included in the SNP’s Holyrood election manifesto.

She suggested that the new company, which the SNP has pledged will have an initial annual budget of £5m, will ensure traditional music is given recognition “on a similar footing” to the five existing companies – Scottish Opera, Scottish Ballet, the National Theatre of Scotland, the Scottish Chamber Orchestra and the Royal Scottish National Orchestra.

The Herald last week told how more than 150 singers, musicians and grassroots organisations had backed a letter to the culture secretary raising concerns that the new national company may “unintentionally privilege” certain artists and material.

The government has been urged to “allow further time” for further discussions about the “scope, structure and timeline” of the new company, which has been championed by John Swinney and was launched by the First Minister at Bute House in August.

Initial concerns were raised with Ms McAllan at a follow-up “roundtable” discussion with music industry leaders.

The letter, instigated by the Traditional Arts and Culture Scotland (TRACS) network, was sent to Ms McAllan after the closure of an official government consultation on the new company which has been running in recent weeks.

The letter welcomed a commitment from the government that it had “not yet determined” what the new national company would look like.

However, it added: “Many prominent artists and organisers will rightly have an important role to play. However, no single person or group can represent the full diversity of Scotland's traditional arts community.”

The five national companies issued a joint statement condemning their treatment by the government earlier this year when it emerged that their calls for a new long-term financial settlement had been snubbed. They claim they have effectively been left to grapple with “standstill” funding settlements for 15 years.

Evidence lodged recently with the culture committee by the National Theatre of Scotland on behalf of the five companies said they were seeking assurances that the new traditional music company “is not established at the expense of those already delivering for Scotland”.

Speaking at the culture committee, Ms McAllan said: “I consider it to be a considerable anomaly that we have national performing companies for theatre, orchestra, opera and more, but do not have one that reflects the traditional music and song of our nation.

“That is the simple justification and the reasoning behind it. It is to offer parity to something that is defining of music and performance in Scotland.

“I would not like us to go on any longer without traditional music having parity.

“That is not to say that this fits tidily into the model that the other performing companies occupy.

“I was clear from the outset that I was very open-minded about what it could do.

“I could see that while we have had, at Celtic Connections and elsewhere, orchestras of traditional musicians coming together, that perhaps that is not necessarily what is best for that sector, which is more diverse and does not traditionally operate like that.

“But we need something, we need it to be right and we need it to be authentic.

“I have held a roundtable with a significant number of industry experts to take their views.

“Certain things came through as very important, including infrastructure for the sector and for artists, which they feel is lacking just now.

“They were clear to me that this is a diverse sector, which looks very different and goes beyond music, to things like traditional dance and crafts.

“All those views were collected at the roundtable we held. We have also run an open survey to gather as broad a spectrum of views as we can. We will collate all of that.

“But I think there is very strong justification for the development of this.

“I am open-minded about the form that it takes. I am very keen that it will be helpful and authentic to the sector.

“But I want to see traditional music sitting alongside opera, orchestra, theatre and others as part of Scotland’s offer.”

Asked by Labour MSP Jenny Young who was consulted about the new national company before it appeared in the SNP manifesto, Ms McAllan said: “I would say that, for the most part, people have been very supportive of this, but they are very keen that it takes the right form.

“I think that would probably sum up the views of most people and it would certainly sum up mine.

“There have been calls for the recognition of traditional music on a similar footing to the other performing companies for some time.

“As a government representative, I am not really here to talk about the manifesto or party engagement on the development of a manifesto.

“My job as a government minister is to implement the manifesto. I am doing that and developing this policy in close consultation with the sector.”
