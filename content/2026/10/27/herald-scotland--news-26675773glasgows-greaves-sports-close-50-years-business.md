**A Glasgow sporting store has announced its impending closure after being bought out by Mike Ashley's Frasers Group.**

Greaves Sports, located on Gordon Street, has launched a closing-down sale, marking the end of a long and storied presence in the city centre.

The shop, one of Scotland's oldest independent sports retailers, has served customers since the 1930s and has become a familiar name for generations of Glaswegians.

A notice in the shop window confirms the closure, but Greaves has yet to make a public statement on its website or social media channels.

The business began as the Clydesdale Rubber Company, selling Wellington boots and sheepskin coats, before evolving into a multi-sport retailer under the direction of third-generation family members Jim and Bill Greaves in the 1950s.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637642.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Glasgow Times)*

In 1959, the brothers expanded the business by acquiring city rival Lumley’s, doubling their retail footprint in Glasgow.

The company shifted its focus to sports retail and rebranded as Greaves Sports in 1967, becoming a well-known name in the industry.

Over the decades, Greaves built a reputation for serving athletes at all levels, from amateurs to professionals and has welcomed a number of high-profile customers over the years.

Celebrities such as Bing Crosby and Frank Sinatra visited in the 1950s to buy golf clubs, while more recently, Samuel L Jackson and Eminem stopped by while touring in the UK.

![Tennis star Andy Murray spotted at city centre shop during visit to Glasgow](https://www.glasgowtimes.co.uk/resources/images/15239910.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Andy Murray visiting Greaves *(Image: Greaves Sports)*

We previously reported that the business was acquired by Mike Ashley’s Frasers Group in July, 2026.

The group also owns House of Fraser, Sports Direct, Flannels, GAME, Evans Cycles, and USC as well as the House of Fraser building on Buchanan Street, Braehead shopping centre in Renfrew, and the Overgate centre in Dundee.

A supplier notice from Greaves confirmed the acquisition and stated that the company had changed its name to Clydesdale Sports Retail Limited, a nod to its origins.

![Greaves Sports](https://www.glasgowtimes.co.uk/resources/images/21637641.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The sign now on the front window of Greaves *(Image: Glasgow Times)*

The closure marks the end of an era for Glasgow’s retail and sporting communities, with punters now largely reliant on nationwide chain stores which lack the soul of the homegrown outlet.

Greaves and Frasers have been approached for comment.
