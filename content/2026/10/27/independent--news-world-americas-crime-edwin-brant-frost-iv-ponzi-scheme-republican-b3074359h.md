A prominent figure in Georgia’s Republican circles has been handed a 15-year federal prison sentence for orchestrating a Ponzi scheme that stripped over 300 victims of more than $76 million, according to federal prosecutors.

Edwin Brant Frost IV, 69, entered a guilty plea to wire fraud in May following an inquiry sparked by the downfall of First Liberty Building and Loan in June 2025.

Alongside his prison term, Frost, who offered a public apology for his actions, was instructed to pay back more than $76 million in restitution.

Addressing the court during his sentencing, Frost acknowledged that it was "difficult to be here and to listen" to victim impact statements, The Atlanta Journal-Constitution reported.

"I have messed up a lot of good people's lives," he said.

Chief U.S. District Judge Leigh Martin May explained that the duration of the sentence directly corresponded to the magnitude of the devastation caused, the newspaper noted.

Investments in First Liberty were frequently promoted on conservative media broadcasts hosted by figures such as Erick Erickson, Hugh Hewitt, and Charlie Kirk.

The operation offered potential returns reaching 16% through high-interest corporate loans.

![Republican Ponzi Scheme Georgia](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/27/14/Republican_Ponzi_Scheme_Georgia_53_4.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Frost, an influential evangelical figure, marketed the opportunity as providing "Wall Street returns for Main Street investors."

However, a lawsuit from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission detailed how he siphoned $17 million for personal use, family members, and associated enterprises, while extending millions in defaulted loans.

Headquartered in the Atlanta suburb of Newnan, the firm's undoing sent shockwaves through the political and religious spheres that supplied its capital.

The fallout threatens to alter Georgia’s Republican landscape by severing financial support for far-right candidates backed by Frost and his relatives. Authorities noted that $570,000 in diverted client funds was funneled into political donations.

U.S. Attorney Theodore S. Hertzberg characterized Frost as a "false prophet and ravenous wolf in sheep's clothing," accusing him of leveraging religious faith, political ties, and pledges of prosperity to exploit victims for self-enrichment.

"This was a stunning, sinful, and sacrilegious abuse of trust that wrecked hundreds of investors, including retirees and seniors who lost their life savings," Hertzberg stated in a press release.
