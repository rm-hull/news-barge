A Bradford school has commented on figures that show just 3 per cent of pupils can swim 215 metres.

This is according to new figures revealing low swimming proficiency among Year 6 pupils in parts of the district.

The data, obtained by the BBC through a Freedom of Information request to the Department for Education, highlights the proportion of the pupils at individual state schools able to swim 25 metres, use a range of strokes, and perform self-rescue techniques.

At Brackenhill Primary School on Dracup Road in Great Horton, just three percent of pupils achieved each of the aforementioned measures.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21543473.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A swimming pool *(Image: PA)*

## Comment from the school

A spokesperson for the school, which has 453 pupils on its roll, said: "Swimming is an important part of our curriculum, and we remain committed to helping all pupils develop confidence and competence in the water.

"While published figures can vary between cohorts, we continue to work with pupils and partners to provide high-quality swimming opportunities and support as many children as possible to achieve the expected standard."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/18722644.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Eccleshill Pool *(Image: Newsquest)*

## Low swimming rates across Bradford

Other schools reported similarly low results.

Meadow Bank Community School, on Fearnville Drive in Laisterdyke, recorded no pupils able to swim 25 metres, though 26 per cent could use a range of strokes.

Miriam Lord Primary School on Bavaria Place in Manningham reported 10 percent across all categories, while Peel Park Primary School and Nursery in Undercliffe recorded 11 percent able to swim 25 metres and use a range of strokes, but none meeting the self-rescue standard.

Green Lane Primary School in Manningham saw 15 percent of pupils able to swim 25 metres, eight percent able to use a range of strokes, and two percent meeting the self-rescue criteria.

The T&A also approached these schools for a comment.

## 'A serious swimming deficit'

Councillor Joe Wheatley (Independent, Bingley East) raised concerns about swimming provision in the district during a recent council meeting.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21631466.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Joe Wheatley *(Image: Bradford Council)*

He said: "58 per cent of Year 6 children in this district are able to swim 25 metres.

"We've got a serious swimming deficit across this district.

"It is demonstrative of a need to actually invest in swimming across the district."

“Right now we've just got an executive that don't seem to recognize that reality."

## What the council is doing

This summer Bradford Council’s Reform UK-led Executive voted to scrap plans for a new swimming pool and leisure centre at the Squire Lane site.

Council bosses said the £32.9m scheme would be too costly for the Bradford taxpayer, even though £20m of that cost would come from a Government grant.

Earlier this month, Bradford Council Leader Stephen Place (Reform UK) announced that the authority does not have the budget to re-open Bingley Pool.

The council paused plans to sell the building, but Cllr Place said it would be up to campaign group Friends of Bingley Pool to identify a way forward for the facility.
