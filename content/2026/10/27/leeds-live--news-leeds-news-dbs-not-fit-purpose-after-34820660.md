# DBS 'not fit for purpose' after Leeds paedophile allowed to work at nursery

## Kristian Parry also faked references and was jailed last week

A nurseries charity has said the disclosure and barring service is "not fit for purpose" after a Leeds paedophile was jailed after faking references to land a job in childcare.

Kristian Parry was jailed at Leeds Crown Court for two years last week for fraud by false representation after faking references to land a nursery job. West Yorkshire Police said Parry, 27, of Middleton, is already serving a sentence for possessing indecent images of children.

Parry was first arrested in May 2025 at a nursery where he worked, on suspicion of possessing indecent and prohibited images. Officers found thousands of images and videos he had downloaded onto his phone, including a screensaver of a baby in a nappy.

Two months after his arrest, he got a job at a different nursery.

Parry should have completed a DBS [disclosure and barring service] check before he was hired. A DBS helps employers make safer recruitment decisions by checking criminal records and barring lists.

A check should have flagged Parry's arrest, but it did not, as his new employer used the update service, which only checks for non-conviction information every nine months.

West Yorkshire Police has contacted the Home Office in relation to the service.

Following Parry's sentence, The National Day Nurseries Association, (NDNA), based in Huddersfield, said the DBS is "not fit for purpose."

Tim McLachlan, NDNA’s CEO, said: “This case has been particularly shocking for the parents, families and nurseries involved. This man was clearly a committed and devious individual who sought to work around the system which has been designed to protect children.

“But there was a glaring shortcoming in this system because the DBS Update Service didn’t pick up details of his arrest or bail conditions which prevented him from being allowed to work with children.

“NDNA has been raising concerns with the Government about the failings of the DBS Update Service because it clearly is not fit for purpose. Nurseries must be able to rely on a thorough check which is watertight, reliable and will prevent this type of situation from ever happening again. They cannot keep children safe if the information they receive from the DBS Update Service is inadequate. Until this loophole is fixed, we are advising nurseries to carry out full enhanced DBS checks before any new employee is taken on.

“We really hope the nurseries involved can now move on from this awful chapter and focus on what they do best – caring for and educating our youngest children.”
