A BUS driver has turned his 3D printing hobby into a growing business with a new stall at Keighley Market.

Chris Smith, owner of The 3D Fox, first explored 3D printing while making items for his daughter.

The hobby quickly evolved into a business as friends, family, and others began requesting his creations.

To help cover the cost of materials and equipment, Mr Smith started selling his 3D-printed products.

He has spent the past 18 months promoting his business through craft fairs and car boot sales across the region.

Mr Smith said: "One of the things I enjoy most about 3D printing is being able to bring people’s ideas to life.

"Whether it’s a personalised gift or a completely unique design, it’s rewarding to create something special for customers.

"I still work as a bus driver, so growing the business has meant fitting orders and printing around my day job.

"Seeing the business grow and hearing positive feedback from customers makes it all worthwhile."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21631981.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chris Smith first explored 3D printing while making items for his daughter *(Image: Supplied)*

He continues to work as a bus driver, managing orders, design, and printing in his spare time.

The 3D Fox offers novelty gifts, collectables, and personalised items, as well as bespoke 3D-printed pieces tailored to individual requests.

This custom design service has helped Mr Smith build a loyal customer base and a strong reputation among local shoppers.

With demand increasing, he has now opened a permanent stall at Keighley Market.

Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council's Executive Member for Economy and Regeneration, said: "We are delighted to welcome Chris and The 3D Fox to Keighley Market.

"Markets provide an important platform for local entrepreneurs and independent businesses to grow, innovate and reach new customers.

"Chris’s journey from hobbyist to business owner, while continuing to work as a bus driver, demonstrates the determination and entrepreneurial spirit that exists across the district.

"His stall adds something new and distinctive to the market offer, and we wish him every success in this exciting next chapter."

The stall gives customers the chance to browse ready-made products and discuss custom commissions in person.

Mr Smith said: "Opening at Keighley Market is an exciting next step, and I’m looking forward to meeting customers and helping bring their ideas to life."

Keighley Market, operated by Bradford Council’s Markets team, is home to a wide range of traders and provides affordable opportunities for businesses to establish themselves, grow and reach new customers across the Bradford district.
