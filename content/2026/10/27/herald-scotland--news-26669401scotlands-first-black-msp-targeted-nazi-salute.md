One of Scotland's first black MSPs has revealed how masked men made Nazi salutes and shouted “Heil Hitler” at her during a protest in Glasgow, warning that racist rhetoric is becoming increasingly normalised in Scottish politics.

Scottish Green MSP Iris Duane said she was personally targeted by extremists who recognised her during counter-protests at the Buchanan Steps over the summer.

In an interview with The Herald ahead of leading a parliamentary debate marking the 25th anniversary of Black History Month in Scotland, she said she continued to experience racism daily.

She accused Reform UK of pursuing “dog whistle politics” which she believes is emboldening racists, revealing she received online abuse from people claiming they were voting for the party to have her deported.

The MSP also distanced herself from a controversial Scottish Greens motion seeking greater recognition of polyamorous relationships, describing her party group's decision to sponsor it as a “mistake”.

Recalling the Glasgow incident, she said: “To see a group of masked men Sieg Heiling and actually Heil Hitler at me and knowing who I was and making targeted offences [was] difficult.

“My great-grandad and my granddad fought the Nazis. I'm incredibly proud.

“To know that there has been a normalisation, to an extent, of these extremist groups in my region, but across the country, is terrifying.”

Ms Duane stressed that most Scots were not racist but warned extremists were increasingly emboldened to intimidate minority communities.

“I have faced racism my entire life. I continue to face racism sometimes on a daily basis. I will not let that hold me back,” she said.

“I'm not there because of the colour of my skin, but I know my experience will differ because of the colour of my skin.”

The Glasgow MSP said abuse had increased since she became a public figure.

“During the election, I would regularly receive abuse on social media from people [using] anonymous accounts saying they were voting Reform to have me deported,” she said.

“Now, I don't even have a passport. I can't go on holiday. Where are you deporting me to?”

Ms Duane said the abuse showed how immigration rhetoric was increasingly directed at British people because of their skin colour.

“That's not based on worries about integration. That's not based on worries of cultural change. That's not based on worries of immigration.

“I am not an immigrant. All of my grandparents were born in this country.”

She said rhetoric supposedly about immigration had “ultimately boiled down to the normalisation of racism against all people of colour in the country”.

Accusing Reform of deliberately provoking reactions, she said: “There is a deliberate dog whistle politics that is now being wheeled out.

“I would say that Reform are focusing on that dog whistle politics in order to provoke those reactions.

“It's really disappointing to see in 2026 when we know the real harm that can cause.”

She cited reports of constituents being attacked for wearing headscarves, racial abuse on public transport and a fire at a social housing property amid rumours it could house an asylum seeker.

“We need to be clear: this doesn't come out of nowhere, and I'm not blaming that on the Reform Party. But playing into those narratives is not helping anyone,” she said.

However, she suggested some Reform MSPs were uncomfortable with the rhetoric of senior party figures.

“There are a number of people on those benches that I believe are probably quite uncomfortable with the way that some of their politics is conducted.”

Her comments follow a row over former home secretary Suella Braverman questioning whether being born in England necessarily makes someone English.

Ms Duane praised Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch for criticising the remarks.

“As much as myself and Kemi Badenoch disagree on most of our politics, I think she really did have an astute observation with that,” she said.

“Like Kemi's children, I am mixed race.

“I know exactly where I'm from, and I know where I'm going.

“If the Reform Party wants to waste time debating people of colour's right to be English, Scottish, Welsh, or Northern Irish, they can waste the time doing that.”

Meanwhile, Ms Duane criticised a motion submitted to the Scottish Greens' conference in Inverness seeking legal recognition for polyamorous relationships.

The proposals, formally backed by the party's Greens of Colour committee, sought changes covering inheritance, pensions, housing and healthcare to recognise relationships involving more than two people.

The motion was not debated after being given a low priority by delegates and the conference ending early amid Storm Boelo transport disruption.

Ms Duane, a member of Greens of Colour, said she opposed the proposals and confirmed the group had withdrawn its sponsorship.

“To be clear from the front, I don't support this motion,” she said, “The Greens of Colour sponsored the motion. They didn't put it forwards themselves. It was written by someone else.

“I'm actually not sure who put that forwards, but they've most recently removed their sponsorship for the motion.”

She added: “There are some very large implications of this motion that haven't been fully thought out, and I think we have much larger issues pressing on the country, the economy, on the housing regime, on our immigration system, on our local government financing.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21205792.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scottish Green MSP Iris Duane, Scotland's first black MSP, has revealed how masked men targeted her with Nazi salutes at a protest in Glasgow. *(Image: Steve Welsh/PA Wire)*

She described the sponsorship as a mistake but said she was not worried about Greens of Colour, which was still establishing its governance arrangements.

Ms Duane also expressed disappointment over controversial comments about rape by fellow Green MSP Q Manivannan, welcoming their subsequent apology.

“Those certainly aren't comments that I agree with,” she said.

“Violence against women and girls is no joke, and we should all be taking that seriously.”

Ms Duane said some people were unhappy to see her in Holyrood because of her race.

“I do understand that there are some individuals out there that are unhappy that I sit in the hall of power because of my race, and that's something that is quite disappointing,” she said.

She will lead a members' business debate on Tuesday celebrating Scotland's 25th Black History Month, calling for black history to be recognised as an integral part of Scottish history.

“I'm incredibly proud to lead this debate to actually highlight that so-called black history has, in many cases, been just regular Scottish history,” she said.

Reflecting on her childhood, she added: “I was a young child of colour that lived in a society that didn't want to accept me, and confused as to why that was, given that I've not lived anywhere else.

“So I will always fight for a world where those children know that they're growing up into a world that invites them with open arms, that they can be whatever they want to be, irrespective of their characteristics.”

Responding to the Green MSP's comments, Thomas Kerr, deputy leader of Reform UK Scotland, insisted there was nothing racist about debating immigration.

He said: “There's nothing ‘racist’ about debating immigration numbers and the pressures it places on housing and services – these are very real issues Scotland is struggling with, and voters want to see raised in Parliament.”

Mr Kerr also accused the Greens of ignoring working-class communities, claiming they had been “shafted by strangers who are regularly prioritised over them”.

He added: “Reform won't get caught in a tit-for-tat about abuse on social media, while the extremist Greens pile on hatred towards ordinary Scot's daily by ignoring their concerns and gaslighting them.”
