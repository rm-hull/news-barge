As thousands of North East households prepare to carve pumpkins ahead of Halloween, experts are warning that spooky displays could attract more than just trick-or-treaters.

Once carved, pumpkins quickly begin to break down, with exposed flesh, moisture and natural sugars capable of attracting insects such as ants, fruit flies and wasps, while rodents and wildlife may also be tempted by pumpkins left outside homes.

With autumn bringing increased pest activity as animals search for food and shelter, one property expert has shared three low-cost ways to help keep Halloween displays pest-free.

Property Buyers Today founder Saif Derzi said these low-cost, natural solutions come just in time for the nation's favourite spooky decoration to stay fresh:

"Pumpkin carving is one of the best parts about Halloween, but unfortunately, it can attract unwanted pests to your home."

### Tip 1- Vinegar solution

After removing the seeds and pulp, dry the pumpkin with kitchen roll, then apply equal parts white vinegar and water inside and out to help limit bacterial growth.

Mr Derzi said: "The vinegar solution will kill bacteria and mould spores that attract bugs, as well as repel rodents with the sharp, acidic scent."

White vinegar can cost as little as 35p, he said, making it a cheap way to tackle one of Halloween’s most irritating problems.

### Tip 2- Vaseline on carved edges

A light coat of Vaseline or petroleum jelly on the pumpkin's edges can help keep moisture in and create a barrier against insects.

Mr Derzi said, adding: "This doesn’t need to be a heavy coat. A light coat is enough to make it more difficult for insects to land and feed on the pumpkin."

Families using this method should choose battery-powered lights, as petroleum jelly is flammable and could pose a fire risk near candles or tea lights.

### Tip 3- Cloves inside the pumpkin

Whole cloves can be placed inside the pumpkin to give off a strong smell that may repel pests, especially rats and ants, Mr Derzi said.

The advice comes as millions of pumpkins are expected to be bought across the UK this Halloween. Carved pumpkins begin decomposing almost immediately, making this advice especially fitting to combat rodent activity during autumn.

Mr Derzi said: "Taking these simple measures can keep pests out of your ghoulish pumpkins this Halloween and even make them last longer."

An estimated 24 million pumpkins are sold each year nationally, with up to 18,000 tonnes potentially discarded afterwards.

Mr Derzi added that the tips use inexpensive household ingredients that many families already have at home, helping decorations stay looking their best throughout the Halloween period while reducing the risk of attracting unwanted visitors.
