## 24-hour supported living HMO planned for Scarborough guesthouse

53 North Marine Road, Scarborough. Google Maps

A FORMER Scarborough guesthouse could be converted into an HMO that provides supported accommodation with round-the-clock staffing.

The former guesthouse in Scarborough’s North Bay could be turned into a larger House in Multiple Occupation (HMO) providing shared accommodation for up to 10 residents, operated by YMCA North Tyneside.

The Thoresby, on North Marine Road, currently contains 11 bedrooms and has previously operated as tourist accommodation.

The scheme aims to provide tenancy-related supported accommodation, giving residents a safe, stable place to live while helping them move towards independent living.

Under the plans, residents would have their own bedrooms and bathrooms while sharing communal kitchen and living facilities.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21602402.jpg?type=mds-article-620)53 North Marine Road, Scarborough. Google Maps.

“The core aim of the service is to prepare residents for successful independent living.

“Support is outcome-focused and designed to build the confidence, skills, and resilience needed to sustain future accommodation,” plans state.

The accommodation would be staffed throughout the day by a manager and support workers, while overnight cover would be provided by a night concierge service.

“One member of staff remains awake and based within the building between 8pm and 8am to respond to emergencies, welfare concerns, and incidents requiring immediate intervention.

“Day staff are based on site and provide tenancy related support interventions tailored to the individual needs of residents.”

No external alterations to the property are proposed and according to the applicant, the scheme would make use of the existing layout of the building and would not require extensions, additional parking, or significant building works.

The supporting statement says the accommodation would provide a structured environment for residents while helping them develop the skills needed to progress towards independent tenancies.

“The service aims to empower residents to make informed choices, increase self-confidence, and develop the practical skills necessary to live independently within the community.”

The application comes amid continuing debate over the concentration of supported accommodation and HMOs in some parts of Scarborough, particularly in areas close to the town centre and seafront.

Last week, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime Jo Coles said she was concerned about the impact that decisions on housing vulnerable people were having in some coastal communities.

Speaking at a meeting of the North Yorkshire Police, Fire and Crime Panel, she said applications for HMOs appeared to be “predominantly affecting coastal communities” and stressed the importance of understanding the implications for policing when accommodation was being provided.

North Yorkshire Council has not yet set a date for deciding on the application, which is open to public comment.
