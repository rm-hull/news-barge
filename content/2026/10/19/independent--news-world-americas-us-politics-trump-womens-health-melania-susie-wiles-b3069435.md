President Donald Trump signed an executive order Monday to create a new women's health division at the National Institutes of Health and a dedicated White House task force to bolster federal research into women's health.

The initiative was pushed by his wife, first lady Melania Trump, and the panel was set to hold its first meeting after a Rose Garden ceremony attended by the Trumps.

White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, who recently completed breast cancer treatment, and Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. will lead the task force, which will coordinate with various federal agencies and programs, the Trump administration said. No specific funding goals were included in the order.

“Beginning today we will change the reality of women’s health,” Melania Trump said at the Monday event. “Previous generations fought for women’s rights. Our generation will fight to discover the cures that will ensure a woman can live her life in health and to the fullest.”

![First Lady Melania Trump, seen at a Rose Garden executive order signing ceremony on Monday, helped shape the order and the task force](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/19/16/47/GettyImages-2300985725-e7249142.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The president, who spoke after his wife, said the order will “promote new research and really go after the understudied diseases affecting women.”

Beyond research, the order is intended to promote diagnostic technology and improve treatment. It calls for targeting maternal health, mental health and endometriosis. Other health concerns and diseases are also mentioned, including Alzheimer's, osteoporosis, heart disease and stroke.

The first lady helped shape the order and the task force in consultation with physicians, White House officials, Kennedy and the Republican president. The initiative adds a new dimension to her policy portfolio as first lady and it followed a series of meetings she had with federal health officials.

The president called it a "historic action” and praised the first lady's work.

“She’s accomplished so much,” he said before pivoting into unscripted commentary on White House renovations, including the patio stone that now covers what was once the Rose Garden lawn.

Returning to women's health, the president expressed surprise that some of the order's requirements had not already been imposed.

“Who the hell wouldn't have a women's health division?” he said, after recalling that he'd asked Melania Trump, “Are you sure they don't have that?”

![White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, who recently completed breast cancer treatment, and Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. will lead the task force](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/19/16/32/GettyImages-2300983786-8f23152f.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In Trump's second term, the first lady has focused on improving foster care systems and expanding technology and artificial-intelligence access in education. During Trump's first presidency, her signature initiative, Be Best, focused on child well-being, safe social media use and reducing drug abuse.

The White House ceremony comes weeks before Nov. 3 elections and as the president's job approval ratings have declined, including among women.

About 4 in 10 independent women supported Trump during the 2024 election against Democrat Kamala Harris and in the pre-presidency period, according to AP-NORC polls.

That dropped to around a quarter in the opening months of this midterm election year, the period marked by Trump's hard-line immigration crackdown in U.S. cities and the opening weeks of the Iran war.
