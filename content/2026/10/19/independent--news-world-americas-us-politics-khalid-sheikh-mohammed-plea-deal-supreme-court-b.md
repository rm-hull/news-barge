The US Supreme Court has declined to hear a bid by Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, the accused mastermind of the September 11, 2001, attacks, to revive a 2024 plea agreement that would have spared him the death penalty in exchange for a guilty plea.

The deal was scrapped by former Defense Secretary Lloyd Austin.

Supreme Court justices on Monday turned away appeals by Mohammed and two co-defendants, who had also signed plea agreements, of a lower court's ruling that upheld Austin's decision to void the deals. The US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit rejected an argument that Austin, who served under former President Joe Biden, had acted too late.

![The justices turned away appeals by Mohammed and two co-defendants who had also signed plea agreements of a lower court's ruling that upheld Austin's decision to void the deals](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/07/11/18/21/SEI258685585.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Supreme Court's action marks the latest milestone in a military prosecution of the detainees held in Guantanamo Bay, Cuba, that has been beset by two decades of legal gridlock.

Mohammed remains the most well-known inmate at the Guantanamo detention facility, which was set up in 2002 by then-US President George W. Bush to house foreign ⁠terrorism suspects ​following the 9/11 attacks, which killed nearly 3,000 people. Mohammed is accused of orchestrating the plot ​to fly hijacked passenger jetliners into the World Trade Center in New York City and into the Pentagon in Arlington, Virginia.

In August, a US Air Force judge set the trial for Mohammed and his co-defendants for June 2028.

The plea agreements for Mohammed and co-defendants Mustafa al Hawsawi and Walid bin 'Atash had been offered and accepted by the US official overseeing the Pentagon's Guantanamo war court. The move prompted criticism by Republican lawmakers. Austin revoked the deals two days later.

A military judge ruled that Austin lacked authority to invalidate the plea deals. That decision was upheld in December by the US Court of Military Commission Review.

The judge then scheduled prompt plea hearings. The D.C. Circuit paused those proceedings.

In July 2025, a panel of that court, in a 2-1 vote, backed the decision to scrap the plea deals, saying that Austin "indisputably had legal authority to withdraw from the agreements."

Defense attorneys for Mohammed and al Hawsawi, including lawyers from the American Civil Liberties Union, urged the Supreme Court to hear the case.

They said Austin's action violated controlling military law and "prolonged the best chance for finality in the September 11 case in a quarter century."
