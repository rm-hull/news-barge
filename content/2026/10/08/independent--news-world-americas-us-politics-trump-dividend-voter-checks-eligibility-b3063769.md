When he is not issuing political threats over a potential Democratic victory or making urgent appeals for support on the trail, President Donald Trump has consistently dangled an enticing offer to voters ahead of the upcoming midterm elections: cash in their mailboxes.

Governments worldwide have long experimented with initiatives to counter declining voter turnout, ranging from designated voting holidays to organized transport to polling stations, according to International IDEA. Trump has taken a different route, promising a standard $5,000 payout if Republicans sweep both houses of Congress in November.

Yet, as the election nears, shifting details regarding who would actually qualify for the money have left voters increasingly skeptical.

A September survey conducted by *The Economist* and YouGov found that only 21 percent of registered voters believed Trump would “definitely or probably” issue the $5,000 dividend if Republicans retain control of Congress. More than half — 57 percent — said he probably or definitely would not deliver on the promise.

Even within his own party, less than half of Republicans — 45 percent — expect the president to follow through.

![Skepticism around the ‘Trump Dividend’ follows previous cash pledges that never materialized — both a proposed $5,000 ‘DOGE dividend’ and a tariff-funded check failed to reach taxpayers](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/08/22/2298610014...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Among likely voters, skepticism runs higher: 61 percent say he will not deliver, compared to 21 percent who believe he will. Public support for the concept itself remains split, with 45 percent opposing the payments entirely and 43 percent in favor.

Much of the public doubt stems from conflicting messages coming directly from the president, along with a history of unfulfilled payout proposals.

Trump first introduced the idea at a Republican midterm convention in Dallas on September 9, framing it as a reward for the country’s economic standing.

“If the Republicans win, you win with us, and you get $5,000,” Trump told the crowd. “It will be called the Trump Dividend.”

However, the defined pool of recipients has fluctuated between posts and appearances. On October 2, Trump posted on Truth Social: “The highly popular $5,000 Trump Dividend will be distributed to every U.S. Citizen if, and when, the Republicans win the Midterm Elections! Thank you for your attention to this matter.”

![More than half of registered voters — and 61 percent of likely voters — say they do not expect the payout to be delivered](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/08/21/27/GettyImages-2289075584.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

That same day, during a campaign event in Alabama, Trump stated the check would go to “all adult citizens.”

He modified the statement again in an October 3 Truth Social post, writing: “If Republicans win the House of Representatives and the Senate in the 2026 Midterm Elections, I’m going to give all Adult Citizens in the United States of America $5,000.”

The distinction between “every U.S. citizen” and “every adult citizen” represents a gap of roughly 73 million people.

Data from the Kids Count Data Center shows there were approximately 270 million adult U.S. citizens in 2025. Sending $5,000 to every adult would cost the federal government roughly $1.3 trillion.

Extending those checks to all 343 million U.S. citizens — a population figure tracked by the U.S. Census Bureau — would push the total cost past $1.7 trillion, adding a $400 billion discrepancy.

Trump has acknowledged the high cost, but insists the country can afford it.

“The Trump Dividend is approximately $1 trillion, but we have a record-setting $21 trillion that’s being invested in our country in just the last 15 months,” he said in October.

![The proposal first surfaced on September 9 during a speech by President Trump at a Republican midterm convention in Dallas, where he pitched the payment as a reward for the country’s economic standing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/08/21/36/GettyImages-2298461849.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When asked by *Fortune* to clarify the official eligibility criteria for the proposed dividend, White House spokesperson Davis Ingle did not provide specific parameters.

“President Trump has consistently proven his doubters wrong,” Ingle said in a statement, adding that “with the continued support of the American people, President Trump will keep delivering real results.”

Voters’ hesitation to take the president at his word is also tied to previous financial promises that failed to materialize.

In 2025, Trump backed a proposed $5,000 “DOGE dividend,” which was intended to distribute government cost savings generated by Elon Musk’s efficiency initiative directly to taxpayers. The plan stalled when DOGE’s initial $2 trillion savings projections failed to materialize.

Later that year, Trump claimed Americans — excluding high-income households — would receive a tariff-funded dividend of at least $2,000 per person. Those checks were never issued, though the administration ultimately disbursed $100 billion in tariff refunds through the end of July.

*The Independent* has contacted the White House for further comment.
