Book lovers will descend on Durham this weekend as the Durham Book Festival returns with three days of events celebrating literature, creativity and debate.

The festival will run from October 9 to 11, bringing together acclaimed writers, new voices and readers from across the region for what organisers describe as a packed programme of books and ideas.

This year's event has a particular focus on celebrating voices from the North East while also welcoming authors from further afield.

As part of 2026's National Year of Reading, festival organisers are launching a North East Big Read, encouraging people across County Durham to read and discuss The Hunting Party by bestselling author Lucy Foley.

Free copies of the mystery novel will be made available through libraries and community venues across the county, with residents invited to take part in local reading group sessions.

The festival will also showcase a series of new writing commissions from a number of leading and emerging authors, including Lucy Rose, Romalyn Ante, Naomi Booth, Sarah Hall and Jake Morris-Campbell.

The events will be held at Gala Durham, Clayport Library and other venues across Durham.

Most of the events can be watched from home through a live-stream for anyone unable to make it.
