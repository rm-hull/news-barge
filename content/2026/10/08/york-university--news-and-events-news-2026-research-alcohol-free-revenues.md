# I’ll drink to that - offering alcohol-free beer on tap boosts pub choices while keeping revenues intact, new study shows

Posted on Thursday 8 October 2026

![Picture of beer available on draught at a pub in York, including a NoLo option](https://www.york.ac.uk/media/news-and-events/pressreleases/2026/NoLo%20pic.jpg)

The study, published in *Drug and Alcohol Review*, evaluated how introducing alcohol-free (AF) beer through three sequential behavioural ‘nudges’ influenced consumer habits and revenues across three distinct UK pubs.

**Hospitality sector**

Researchers found that introducing alcohol-free beer - particularly on draught - successfully increased consumer uptake. Crucially for the hospitality sector this shift had no negative impact on drinks revenue.

Nudges are subtle, without restricting freedom of choice. Over the study period, researchers tested three cumulative interventions:

* Availability nudge: adding canned alcohol-free beer to fridge displays.
* Information nudge: installing promotional posters behind the bar and in customer toilets.
* Functionality nudge: offering alcohol-free beer on tap by replacing one regular draught option

While promotional posters showed limited impact, physical changes to availability led to increases in sales. Offering alcohol-free beer on draught proved particularly popular, with consumers frequently opting for a freshly poured pint over canned alternatives.

Rather than displacing traditional alcoholic beer, the introduction of AF options led to a drop in sales of wine and spirits. Environmental factors also played a role, with warmer weather driving higher demand for NoLo (no-and low-alcohol) beverages.

**Drinking behaviour**

Dr Nadine Waehning, one of the authors of the study from the School for Business and Society at the University of York, said it was important to create inclusive drinking environments to address the changes in drinking behaviour.

She said: “Our findings show that simple nudges - like serving alcohol-free beer on tap - give customers attractive, authentic alternatives without reducing total venue revenue.

Professor Victoria Wells added: “Modifying the drinking environment provides a scalable, commercially viable path to support lower-risk drinking within everyday social settings and this is great news for pubs that want to engage in Stoptober or Dry January, as well as responding to moderation trends.”

**Rowntree study**

The findings build on previous research looking at the role of pubs in York.

Professor Wells added: “It’s also very clear that more people are moderating, as our chapter on pubs in York in the new ‘Poverty and Progress? 125 years on from Rowntree’s study of life in York’ shows so this is a key trend that pubs can confidently engage with”.

The authors of the study say the findings offer valuable insight for pub managers, policymakers, and licensing authorities looking for proportionate, evidence-based approaches to promote moderation while maintaining a thriving hospitality sector.

## Further information

* In 2021, nearly 20% of adults in England were drinking at ‘in- creasing’ or ‘higher’ risk levels. The economic and social burden of alcohol harm in the UK was estimated at 1.3%–2.7% of gross domestic product (£21–£52 billion) in 2016.
* A UK Parliament report (2023) found an 89% rise in alcohol-related deaths between 2002 and 2022, spiking sharply in 2020 following the Covid-19 outbreak, linking alcohol to over 100 illnesses and 42% of violent crime and urging government action to reduce alcohol consumption.
* No-and low-alcohol (NoLo) products are increasingly included in harm reduction policy, including the UK 10-Year Health Plan and World Health Organization prevention strategies
