## Picky Cat? Try Shreds

If you have a cat, you already know they’re weird about wet food. You never know which textures and flavors they’re going to reject outright, or later reject after you’ve bought an entire flat of it at Costco. Friskies clearly knows its customers, as the company makes a downright encyclopedic variety of textures, from chunks and filets to paté and, as seen here, “shreds.” This is the only texture that WIRED pet tech reviewer Molly Higgins’ cats will tolerate; the can contents look like little meat noodles in a puddle of viscous “gravy.” I know this because my cats will only eat the paté texture, so I’ve spent quite a bit of time looking at untouched plates of Friskies Shreds. This is not the healthiest or cleanest wet food, according to recent news reports (spoiler: The vast majority of them aren’t), nor is it even that great a sale, but it is cheaper than buying it at most stores. It’s also more nutritionally complete than the houseplants, flies, and plastic bags that would make up my cats’ diet if they had their way.

## A Trip to the Mechanic? Not in This Economy

Do you have an older vehicle, or a vehicle of dubious reliability? It would probably make sense for you to have an OBD II reader. If your check engine light comes on, you’ll be able to interpret the code that caused it and determine whether it’s A) something you can ignore until you have the time or money to get it fixed, B) something you can fix yourself, or C) something severe enough to stop driving the car. Or, if there’s an entire library’s worth of codes, give up and sell the car to someone on the side of the road for $2,500, as I did once with an old Camaro I was tired of fixing. Anyway, I bought this OBDII reader a few months ago for my 2nd gen Tundra (just in case!) and have been quite impressed with the features and the quality for the price—which is even better right now at 25 percent off.
