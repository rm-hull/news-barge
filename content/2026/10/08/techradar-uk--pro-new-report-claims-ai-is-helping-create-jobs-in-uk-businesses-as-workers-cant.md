* **Four in five say AI has or will create jobs in the next two years**
* **Two in five are still worried about AI replacing them**
* **Managers are more likely to use AI agents**

A new HP survey of nearly 20,000 desk-based workers globally has revealed that nearly half (46%) of Brits believe AI has positively affected their lives, with one in two (52%) using employer-provided AI tools daily or weekly – up from 38% last year.

But rather than replacing workers, three in five UK business leaders say AI adoption has actually created new roles – or even entire teams – within their organizations.

Additionally, an extra one in five (19%) expects AI to create new roles over the next one to two years, totally countering previous narratives that AI would wipe out human workers.

## HP data shows that AI could actually be creating jobs

On the flip side, just 10% say AI is primarily replacing or reducing roles – and this isn't just a trend that's native to the UK. It aligns broadly with the global average, with 59% of business leaders and 62% of IT decision-makers (ITDMs) reporting job creation.

Agentic AI use is also on the up, with 45% of UK desk-based workers now opting to use increasingly automated systems that would previously have been seen as a threat to their livelihood. However, HP's data does align with other recent research to reveal an organizational disparity – agent usage falls when a worker becomes less senior. Just 38% of knowledge workers use agents, compared with 56% of business leaders and 71% of ITDMs.

"For businesses, the priority now is to help people seize those opportunities by investing in the skills, tools and development they need to adapt and grow," HP UK&I MD Ruth Patterson explained, noting the ongoing shift in job roles.

"Work is changing rapidly, but this year’s findings show that people are adapting with it."
