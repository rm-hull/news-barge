A protester wearing a soccer jersey of the Spanish national team was lead out of a campaign rally in San Antonio, Texas, on Wednesday. Photograph: Mandel Ngan/AFP/Getty Images

A demonstrator holds a Cuban flag as Donald Trump speaks. Photograph: Evan Vucci/Reuters

The interruptions seemed to momentarily derail the president as he repeated a lie about James Talarico, the Texas Democrat he claims is a vegan who only pretends to eat meat and secretly vomits every time he does. There is no evidence for this imaginary tale the president has repeated multiple times in recent weeks as Talarico has pulled ahead of the Trump-backed Republican candidate for the US Senate, Ken Paxton.

Trump began his riff with the rare admission that he has been mispronouncing the word vegan, as “vaygan”.

“He is a fake,” Trump said of the Democrat. “He’s a vegan. You know, my wife told me, ‘Sir, do me a favor. You have to do me just a little bit of a favor. It’s not vaygan; it’s vegan’”.

As the president told this story about being asked to pronounce the word correctly, he appeared to get confused about who, exactly, had corrected him. Trump does not usually refer to his wife calling him “Sir”. Moments later he said that the person who made this request of him was a man.

“I don’t know about that stuff. I’m not into it. And he said ‘It’s vegan! Please pronounce that word properly. I called it, vaygan, vaygan. I thought it- how the hell would I know? I like steak.”

Trump repeats false claims of booming US economy and invented investment figure, ignoring inflation and spiking fuel prices

In his speech to supporters in San Antonio, Texas, which is still in progress, Donald Trump has been repeating the same false claims about the US economy he has made again and again in recent weeks.

Ignoring soaring inflation and fuel prices exacerbated by the war he started with Iran and has been unable to stop, Trump repeated his entirely imaginary claim that he has already secured $21tn in foreign direct investment in the US since returning to office lat year.

In fact, as CNN reported last month, federal data shows that new foreign direct investment in the US was about $232bn in 2025, and Trump appears to be “counting trillions in vague investment pledges — pledges that were about ‘bilateral trade’ or ‘economic exchange’ rather than investment in the US — and vague statements that didn’t even rise to the level of pledges.”

Trump also repeated his false claim that tariff revenues have swelled federal coffers so much under his administration that he was able to pay a $1,776 bonus to military service members and would also cover the $5,000 checks to all US adults he is promising voters if they keep Republicans in the majority in the House and Senate.

The $1,776 payments to troops that Trump first announced in December, which he said would be paid for by tariff revenues, were not funded by the import taxes Trump imposed. Democrats sue Trump administration, asking judge to declare Trump ads paid for with taxpayer dollars illegal propaganda

The Democratic National Committee sued the Trump administration in federal court on Wednesday, asking a judge in Washington to stop the president from using taxpayer funds to air a series of ads promoting himself, arguing that the ads are illegal government-sponsored propaganda.

The ads promoting Donald Trump, including one recycled from his 2024 presidential campaign, have cost taxpayers more than $12m so far, according to the media tracking firm AdImpact.

The DNC complaint, filed in the US district court for the District of Columbia, alleges that the ads violate federal law, including a ban on congressionally appropriated money being used for “publicity or propaganda”.

The complaint gets right to the point in its opening lines:

The Trump administration is using taxpayer dollars to air political advertisements, a blatant violation of the statutory ban on the use of appropriations for ‘publicity or propaganda purposes’. President Trump personally directed this illegal activity. The White House office crafted the partisan campaign. The office of management and budget shifted $20m to pay for it. And US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) has carried it out. These unlawful actions are ultra vires and violate the Administrative Procedure Act (APA).

The suit also notes, as the Guardian reported last month, that the first ad in the series features an image of Fifa’s president, Gianni Infantino, awarding Trump the so-called ‘Fifa peace prize,’ to support its case that the ads all violate a prohibition on the use of taxpayer funds for “self-aggrandizement”.

According to the suit, the ads have been broadcast on ABC, NBC, MS Now, CBS, CNN, Fox News and Newsmax and appeared during a variety of programs, including popular NFL and college football games, and news and entertainment shows on which the president has been criticized, including Meet the Press, Jimmy Kimmel Live! and Saturday Night Live.
