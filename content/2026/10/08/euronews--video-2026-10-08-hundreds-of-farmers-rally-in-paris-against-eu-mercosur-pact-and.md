France saw **hundreds of farmers**gather outside the** National Assembly**in** Paris**on 8 January, to protest against the** EU-Mercosur trade deal** and demand greater protection for French agriculture.

Around **100 tractors** entered the capital before dawn despite a**police ban**. Some reached the Arc de Triomphe and the Eiffel Tower before farmers** gathered outside parliament**, carrying flags and banners warning of the growing pressure on their livelihoods.

The protest was organised by **Coordination Rurale**, whose members travelled from several French regions to demand stronger protection for domestic agriculture. They also criticised the government's response to an outbreak of** lumpy skin disease** affecting cattle.

The **EU-Mercosur agreement** would expand trade with**Brazil, Argentina, Paraguay** and**Uruguay**. French farmers fear** competition** from cheaper beef, poultry and sugar produced under different environmental and animal-welfare standards.

**Brussels** says European food-safety rules would still apply and that sensitive agricultural imports would be subject to**quotas**.

Tensions rose outside parliament when National Assembly President Yaël Braun-Pivet was **booed** by demonstrators. President**Emmanuel Macron**, meanwhile, said France would** oppose** the agreement ahead of a crucial European vote.

The protests extended across the country, bringing together around **2,200 farmers** and**625 tractors** in 39 departments, according to the Interior Ministry.

Coordination Rurale called for an end to the demonstrations later that day but insisted the **fight was far from over**.
