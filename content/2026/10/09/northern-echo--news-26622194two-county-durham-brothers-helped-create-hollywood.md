Long before Hollywood became the global home of cinema, two brothers from a County Durham pit village helped lay its foundations.

Today, millions of tourists visit Hollywood Boulevard every year, posing beside iconic landmarks and celebrating the stars of the silver screen.

Few realise that part of Hollywood's story began in West Stanley, where brothers David and William Horsley grew up before emigrating to the United States and helping create what would become the world's film capital.

For Stanley, it is an extraordinary claim to fame: local brothers founded the company behind the first permanent movie studio in Hollywood.

## From a County Durham pit village to America

David Horsley was born in Stanley, County Durham, on March 11, 1873.

He and his older brother William spent their childhood in the heart of the North Durham coalfield before their family emigrated to the United States in 1884.

The move came after a life-changing accident for David.

While living in West Stanley, he suffered severe injuries in an incident involving a coal train, ultimately losing part of an arm.

His family later sought a new future in America, settling in New Jersey.

Far removed from the collieries of County Durham, the brothers would go on to become pioneers of an entirely new industry.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/18976025.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

## The birth of an independent film company

In the early 1900s, motion pictures were still in their infancy.

Alongside William Horsley and filmmaker Charles Gorman, David established the Centaur Film Company in New Jersey.

Founded in 1907, it is widely regarded as America's first independent motion picture production company.

The company quickly expanded, producing a steady stream of silent films at a time when the industry was dominated by powerful competitors linked to inventor Thomas Edison.

The Horsleys became part of a new generation of independent filmmakers challenging the established order.

By 1910, their operation was producing several films every week.

## The brothers who arrived in Hollywood first

The decision that changed movie history came because of something simple: sunshine.

Early film cameras relied heavily on natural light and poor weather regularly disrupted production on the East Coast.

David Horsley realised California offered a solution and moved production west.

In 1911, the Horsleys opened the Nestor Film Company in a former tavern at the corner of Sunset Boulevard and Gower Street.

It became the first permanent motion picture studio in Hollywood.

At the time, Hollywood was little more than a rural community on the outskirts of Los Angeles.

There were no giant studio lots, no Walk of Fame and no celebrity culture.

Yet other filmmakers quickly recognised the advantages of Horsley's move and began following him to California.

The migration helped transform Hollywood into the centre of the global film industry.

## A key role in the creation of Universal

The Horsleys' influence did not end with Nestor.

In 1912, their company became part of the newly formed Universal Film Manufacturing Company, the business that evolved into Universal Pictures, one of Hollywood's most famous studios.

David Horsley accepted shares in the new company and became its treasurer.

Although both brothers would later move on from Universal, their place in film history had already been secured.

Without their willingness to move west and establish a studio in Hollywood, the industry's development may have looked very different.

## Stanley's forgotten Hollywood pioneers

Unlike many Hollywood legends, David and William Horsley are not household names.

Yet their achievements remain remarkable.

The brothers left a County Durham mining community and helped establish the first permanent movie studio in the place that would become synonymous with cinema around the world.

More than a century later, Hollywood still stands as the global capital of film.

And hidden within that story is a little-known North East connection: two brothers from Stanley who helped make it happen.
