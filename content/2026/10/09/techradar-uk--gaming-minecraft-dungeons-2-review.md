### TechRadar Verdict

Minecraft Dungeons 2 looks pretty and offers impressively seamless co-op play, but is otherwise a boring slog filled with shallow, repetitive combat and a linear open world that feels painfully underutilized.

In my books, the most satisfying part of any action role-playing game (ARPG) like *Diablo* or*Titan Quest* is the constant feeling of progression. Nothing quite compares to watching your little on-screen hero grow from a tiny ant that can barely bash their way through a pack of lowly wolves to a maxed-out bringer of destruction that mows through entire armies without breaking a sweat.

*Minecraft Dungeons 2*’s aggressive level scaling robs the player of that experience. In the more than 12 hours it took for me to beat the story campaign, I never felt more powerful. Sure, I unlocked more interesting abilities and got to try a variety of weapons, but the amount of time required to take down each newly spawned foe remained near-constant throughout.

A friend even joined my game towards the end of my playthrough, with their level 1 character effortlessly keeping pace with my level 40 one throughout the final encounters, really underscoring the system’s ridiculousness. I’m sure the intention here is to make it easy for friends to jump into the same lobbies and play together without having to worry about their respective levels, but it left me wondering what the point of all the grinding actually is.

## Blocky buddies

![A Minecraft Dungeons 2 screenshot taken on PC&amp;gt;](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/98J4BRp3NweabFgHaCZQHf-1200-80.jpg)

It’s a shame, because there is still some good in *Minecraft Dungeons 2*. This standalone spinoff recreates the blocky look of the popular* Minecraft* survival game expertly, with its own more stylized twists that make the world instantly recognizable, yet refreshingly new thanks to more expressive lighting and Pixar-like character animations that make everything pop even from the distant isometric perspective.

I may have lamented the fact that I couldn’t wow my friend with my character’s power, but the co-op experience is otherwise well done. Creating a group with up to three companions is impressively straightforward no matter where they play, thanks to seamless cross-platform connectivity. There’s even couch co-op, which is quite rare in 2026 and worth praising. The ability to play with others easily goes a long way in making *Minecraft Dungeons 2* genuinely worthwhile for younger players.

For everyone else, however, everything here is just too straightforward to appeal. The enemy variety is sorely lacking for starters. The expected *Minecraft* favorites like the Creepers, Zombies, Skeletons, and, of course, the iconic Chicken Jockey are all present and accounted for, along with the evil grey-skinned Illagers from the first*Minecraft Dungeons* game. Unfortunately, most enemies function indistinguishably. Those with projectile weapons spray arrows at you from afar, while the rest just rush towards you in a big group.

The strategy in every encounter is to simply mash the attack button and occasionally dodge, even as more powerful Illagers with magic attacks are introduced. The boss fights are equally bland, forcing you to whittle down gigantic health bars for minutes at a time while waves of low-level enemies spawn in. To make matters worse, many of them are repeated throughout the campaign with little variation.

![A Minecraft Dungeons 2 screenshot taken on PC&amp;gt;](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/FCw9ZH66knMHLc3AP8Vm9f-1200-80.jpg)

It’s frustrating because, on paper, the combat does have some depth: you have a melee and ranged attack, plus three equipped abilities granted by items that you’ve managed to find.

The weapon selection is decently varied, with scooping shovels and fast-attacking double daggers performing very differently from slow-swinging war hammers or claymores, and a steady trickle of new gear encourages you to experiment. They can be upgraded too, with unlockable enchantments that add little mechanics such as a lightning strike that triggers at the end of a chain attack.

Your abilities are powered by a blue soul-meter, which fills up a little with each defeated foe. I think the intention is to create some form of rhythm, with you alternating between melee, ranged, and your abilities, but the fundamental problem is that there’s just no real strategy to any of it.

Sure, you could probably spend ages creating a build with complementary gear – though there’s no real reason to bother when the aforementioned enemy scaling wipes out any real power gain.

## Lacking craft

![A Minecraft Dungeons 2 screenshot taken on PC&amp;gt;](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/iapbHbt7ZTZ7bkDtgcgDWe-1200-80.jpg)

Much was made of *Minecraft Dungeons 2*’s world in its marketing. It’s much more open than the previous game, though I wouldn’t exactly call it a true open-world.

The map is designed like a linear corridor that funnels you from one story mission to another with no real room for exploration. Yes, there are side quests and optional dungeons scattered around, but none of the ones that I sampled offered anything that felt meaningfully distinct from the main story.

![A Minecraft Dungeons 2 screenshot taken on PC&amp;gt;](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/gaRXAnABmH8xbBWEnPkyQe-1200-80.jpg)

**The world ofMinecraft Dungeons 2looks fantastic. The art direction is a more stylized take on the universe from the regularMinecraftgame, with bright, popping colors and expressive animations.**

Regarding the story, the most polite way to describe it would be ‘functional’. It’s presented entirely in second person through a narrator that is devoid of personality. There are no memorable non-player characters (NPCs), no charming companions, no real atmosphere at all — just a bland summary of events.

The game also serves as the *Minecraft* community’s introduction to The Sift — a new dimension that’s being added to the main game sometime next year. It shows up a few times in the campaign in both procedurally generated encounters and a hand-crafted map. It's got a very distinct aesthetic and its own unique enemies, though it's sadly mechanically indistinguishable from the normal world as you navigate through it spamming attacks.

Like many elements of *Minecraft Dungeons 2*, it feels like a missed opportunity. Everything here is perfectly functional, but it’s just bland to the point of tedium. If the pitch was a skimmed-down version of* Diablo* for kids, then developers Mojang and Double Eleven have delivered a serviceable product.

Creativity is a core part of the *Minecraft* brand, and here I wish the team had been more adventurous.

## Should I play Minecraft Dungeons 2?

## Play it if…

**You want an easy co-op experience**  
The seamless co-op play is one of the best parts of *Minecraft Dungeons 2,* as you can easily team up with friends no matter what platform they’re playing on.

## Don't play it if...

**You’re an action RPG die-hard**  
*Minecraft Dungeons 2* simply does not have the depth you find in other action RPG games. Avoid it if you’re familiar with the genre already and are looking for your next*Diablo*-like fix.

**You want a game to occupy you for a long time**  
The main story here is short, and while there is some side content and even a post-game on offer, you’re unlikely to want to stick around for long given how repetitive the action is.

## Accessibility features

*Minecraft Dungeons 2* has some accessibility options. The game features a guidance trail that is enabled by default. Subtitles are used throughout, with customizable sizing, color, and background opacity.

There are two font options: the default stylized font and a simplified, easier-to-read version. There are also multiple color blind modes, a high-contrast mode, and the ability to set your own HUD colors.

## How I reviewed Minecraft Dungeons 2

I played more than 13 hours of *Minecraft Dungeons 2* on my RTX 4090-powered PC using the Turtle Beach Pacific Skyline Wireless Controller as my main peripheral, along with a 1080P Lenovo monitor and Logitech computer speakers.

I completed all missions of the single-player campaign and experienced a decent amount of side content both solo and with a friend.
