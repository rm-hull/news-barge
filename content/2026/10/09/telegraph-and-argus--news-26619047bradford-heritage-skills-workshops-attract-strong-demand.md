Demand for traditional building skills is soaring in Bradford, with dozens attending a specialist course and the next session already fully booked.

Thirty people recently took part in free, hands-on lime mortar training through the Bradford Heritage Action Zone programme, which protects and revitalises Bradford city centre’s historic heart.

Held in late September at Darley Street Market’s Courtyard, the sessions were run by Womersley’s Ltd and focused on conserving traditional buildings.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21555990.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Attendees practise mixing and applying lime mortar as part of the Bradford Heritage Action Zone programme *(Image: Supplied)*

Ian Womersley, of Womersley’s Ltd, said: "It was a great pleasure to work with homeowners in Bradford's Heritage Action Zone, discussing the care and restoration of older buildings and demonstrating best practice for high-quality repointing using lime mortars.

"The hands-on training was really well received and gave attendees the confidence and knowledge to use lime mortars themselves.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21555998.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Attendees practise mixing and applying lime mortar as part of the Bradford Heritage Action Zone programme *(Image: Supplied)*

"We're looking forward to delivering another session and continuing to work with Bradford Council to promote the use of traditional building materials."

The workshops gave participants practical experience repointing with hot, non-hydraulic and natural hydraulic lime mortar.

Participants learned to identify, mix and apply lime mortar, remove damaged mortar and prepare joints for repointing.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21555988.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The popular heritage skills sessions were so well received that the next workshop is already fully booked *(Image: Supplied)*

The sessions also showed why lime mortar matters when repairing and preserving historic buildings.

Cllr Andrew Judson, Bradford Council’s executive member for economy, regeneration and planning, said: "Bradford has a fantastic historic built environment and looking after it depends on having people with the right specialist skills and knowledge.

"Through the Bradford Heritage Action Zone programme, these workshops gave people the chance to get hands-on experience and learn traditional techniques directly from skilled professionals."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21555992.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Attendees practise mixing and applying lime mortar as part of the Bradford Heritage Action Zone programme *(Image: Supplied)*

Attendees gave overwhelmingly positive feedback, praising the practical training and the instructor’s expertise.

One participant said: "The session was really useful. I particularly liked all the hands-on work."

Another described the workshop as "very useful," adding: "The trainer was very knowledgeable, engaging and answered all questions."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21555996.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lime Mortar sessions at Darley Street Market *(Image: Supplied)*

A third said: "Absolutely brilliant. Very structured, informative, fun, interactive and understandable without being too long. It was a great way to introduce us to this skill and I would highly recommend Ian as a tutor."

Buoyed by demand, the Bradford HAZ team is exploring more heritage skills workshops for the new year.
