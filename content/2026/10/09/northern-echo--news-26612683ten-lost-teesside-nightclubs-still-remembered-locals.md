Long before social media, a night out on Teesside meant queuing in the cold, meeting friends under neon lights and dancing until the early hours.

Giant smoke-blowing faces, foam parties, celebrity appearances and packed dancefloors helped define nights out for thousands of Teessiders.

Most have disappeared, but memories of packed dancefloors and late nights live on.

Here are ten Teesside nightclubs still remembered for packed dancefloors and unforgettable nights out.

## 1. The Mall, Stockton

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/3502754.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Mall later became Zanzibar, followed by Glam on Stockton High Street. *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Few Teesside nightclubs are remembered more fondly than The Mall.

The former Odeon cinema on Stockton High Street became a nightclub in 1987.

The 1,600-capacity venue quickly became a nightlife hotspot, famed for its spectacular light shows.

It became Visage in 1995, Zanzibar in 2000 and finally Glam before closing in 2013.

For many clubbers, The Mall defined the late 1980s and early 1990s, hosting acts including Take That and PJ & Duncan.

## 2. Arena, Middlesbrough

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21546211.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Arena, Middlesbrough *(Image: Flickr/ CHRIS)*

If The Mall was the mainstream giant, Arena was the serious clubber’s choice.

Arena took over the former Pavilion Theatre on Newport Road and became one of the North East’s biggest dance venues.

House music fans travelled from across the region to attend nights there, with major bands including Oasis performing there before going onto become household names.

Former DJs described it as an iconic, no-frills warehouse venue with dark rooms, strobe lighting and packed dancefloors.

Arena closed in 2005 but, more than 20 years later, remains one of Middlesbrough’s most legendary nightclubs.

## 3. Lava Ignite, Middlesbrough

Some of the slightly younger Teessiders will remember Lava Ignite as the place to be during the 2000s.

The Wilson Street venue opened in 2005 after a £500,000 refurbishment of the former Time and Envy nightclub, adding multiple rooms, VIP areas, new lighting and video screens.

It became a Middlesbrough nightlife staple for a decade before closing around 2015.

## 4. Millennium, Teesside Park

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/18494924.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The former Millennium nightclub at Teesside Park *(Image: UGC)*

Millennium was unlike anything else on Teesside.

Between 1996 and 2002, Millennium was famous for its extravagant interior.

Giant smoke-blowing 'God' faces loomed over the dancefloor, alongside colourful lighting and foam parties that made Millennium one of the North East’s most unusual nights out.

Though only open for six years, Millennium left a huge impression and continues to generate nostalgic memories of the early 2000s.

## 5. Cassidy's, Middlesbrough

Cassidy’s opened in 1992 and quickly became one of Middlesbrough’s leading LGBTQ+ venues.

Renamed Desirez in 2006, it became known for its welcoming atmosphere and loyal crowd before closing in 2011.

## 6. Silks, Redcar

Silks was a fixture of Redcar nightlife for many years and attracted generations of drinkers and clubbers.

It later operated mainly as a pub and bar before closing in 2016, but locals still remember its packed nightclub weekends.

For many Redcar residents, no night out was complete without a stop at Silks.

## 7. Madison, Middlesbrough

Mention The Madison to Middlesbrough clubbers of a certain age and you will often get a smile.

The club became renowned for its lively atmosphere, themed nights and huge popularity among students and young professionals.

The Madison closed in 2000 but still represents a golden era of Middlesbrough nightlife.

## 8. The Cornerhouse, Middlesbrough

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21546212.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Cornerhouse, Middlesbrough *(Image: Flickr/ CHRIS)*

The Cornerhouse opened beneath the railway arches in 1994, offering a different kind of night out.

Part bar and part nightclub, it became known for combining music with cutting-edge artwork and creative installations.

The venue attracted major acts and gained a devoted following among those looking for an alternative night out in Middlesbrough.

The Cornerhouse closed in 2010, and the building was demolished in 2023 during the redevelopment of Middlesbrough railway station.

## 9. Tall Trees, Yarm

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21392527.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Yarm's Tall Trees *(Image: ARCHIVE)*

No list of Teesside nightclubs would be complete without Tall Trees.

Tall Trees was a nightlife institution, attracting big-name DJs and hosting major dance events that put the area’s club scene on the regional and national map.

For many people who grew up near Yarm during the 1990s and early 2000s, visiting Tall Trees was a rite of passage.

The venue closed in 2010, was later gutted by a fire and has since been demolished.

## 10. Klub Kaos, Middlesbrough

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21546296.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Klub Kaos was a staple nightclub on Zetland Street in Middlesbrough in the early 2000s. *(Image: Google Maps)*

Klub Kaos was synonymous with student nights, cheap drinks and packed dancefloors.

While larger venues focused on dance music, Klub Kaos drew students from across Teesside with chart hits and themed nights.

Queues stretched outside on busy weekends as Klub Kaos became one of the liveliest spots in town.

The venue closed in the late 2000s, but its name remains one of the most recognisable among Teesside’s lost clubs.
