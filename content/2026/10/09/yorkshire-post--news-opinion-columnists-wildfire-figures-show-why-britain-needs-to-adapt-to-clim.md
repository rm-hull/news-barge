# Wildfire figures show why Britain needs to adapt to climate change and not just fight it

## The fact that England has seen the second highest year on record for the number of wildfires this year is an illustration of why the dangers of climate change cannot be ignored. It is no longer an issue that impacts far flung places in other parts of the world but one that the whole of Europe including Britain needs to confront.

Between January and August this year, there were 1,354 wildfires. It follows the record of 1,592 for the whole of 2022. Both years have been characterised by unprecedented temperatures.

While the 2026 data does not yet cover the whole year, it is unlikely the number will surpass 2022, given the cooler and wetter conditions expected in October to December.

All the evidence points to more extreme weather confronting all corners of the planet.

Britain needs a two-pronged approach to the climate crisis. Firstly, the Government needs to look at mitigating risks that come with extreme weather events. For example having a clear strategy to prevent wildfires starting and in the event that they do, relevant authorities are equipped to respond quickly. Secondly, the need for Britain to meet its obligations on carbon emissions cannot be overlooked. It isn’t just about the country’s carbon footprint but rather about setting an example to the rest of the world so that they too follow in Britain’s footsteps.

Record levels of wildfires are also a reminder of the importance of tapping into the expertise that Britain has in its agricultural communities. There are few people who understand how to manage land better than farmers.

The response during the Langdale Moor wildfire last year was illustrative of just how valuable the expertise is of farmers, many who helped firefighters build firebreaks and fetched water to help douse out flames.
