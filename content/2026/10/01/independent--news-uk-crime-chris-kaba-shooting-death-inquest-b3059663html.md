The Metropolitan Police are “resisting” an inquest into the fatal shooting of Chris Kaba by an officer, lawyers for his family have alleged.

Kaba, 24, was a member of one of London’s most violent street gangs and had a long history of violence, with a string of convictions for knife crime dating back more than 10 years.

He was shot in the head in Streatham, south-east London, on 5 September 2022, after trying to ram his way past police cars following a chase.

He was taken to hospital but died of his injuries the following day.

It later emerged that Kaba had shot a man in the legs in a nightclub in the week before he died.

Metropolitan Police marksman Sgt Martyn Blake was charged with Kaba’s murder, but was found not guilty after an Old Bailey trial in October 2024.

![Chris Kaba died after being shot in the head by a Metropolitan Police marksman](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/05/12/18/6802862-library-copy.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Blake insisted that he feared for his life and his fellow officer’s safety. He apologised for the “distress” caused to Kaba’s family but insisted the shooting was “lawful, proportionate and necessary”.

Blake, also known by the cypher NX121, was also due to face disciplinary proceedings. However, those did not go ahead after the government changed regulations around police use of force.

An inquest into Kaba’s death was opened and immediately adjourned in October 2022, amid an investigation by the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC).

A pre-inquest review began on Thursday at Southwark Coroner’s Court to hear legal submissions before senior coroner Dr Julian Morris rules on whether to resume a full inquest into Mr Kaba’s death.

Matthew Ryder KC, representing the Kaba family, said there was “sufficient reason” to resume the inquest, adding it would allow the family to be “involved in broader issues of fact-finding”.

In his legal submissions on Thursday, he said the family wanted to find out “why did this police officer shoot our son?”

![A screengrab issued by the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) of the incident where Chris Kaba was fatally shot by Martyn Blake](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/05/13/01KZ8ZA3GBT1ZHB8979EQ7YEX7.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Mr Ryder told the court: “They want there to be an investigation to provide at least an opportunity to find out if the officer acted unreasonably in perceiving their son as a threat for which lethal force was needed.”

In his legal submissions, Mr Ryder said the criminal trial was “not a substitute for an inquest” and an inquest would help “learn lessons from those tragic events”.

Mr Ryder said he could not think of “any case in modern history or an equivalent where a police officer has shot someone in public and there has not been an inquest”.

He said Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley was “positively resisting the resumption of an inquest” and the family found this “disappointing and misguided”.

Mr Ryder told the court: “The family find it very difficult to understand why the commissioner does not want to know through the process of an inquest.

“Why did this officer shoot Mr Kaba and, in particular, whether the officer had a reasonable belief about the threat posed by Mr Kaba when he did so.”

He added that his death was an “immense tragedy” for his family and resulted in public demonstrations of support, “particularly in the black community”.

The pre-inquest review hearing continues.
