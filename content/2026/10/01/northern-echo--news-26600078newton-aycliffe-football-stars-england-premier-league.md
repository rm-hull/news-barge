When football talent is discussed in County Durham, attention often turns to places such as Sunderland, Darlington and Bishop Auckland.

Yet one town with a remarkable knack for producing footballers is Newton Aycliffe.

The town, founded as one of Britain's post-war new towns, has quietly built a reputation for nurturing talented players who have gone on to perform at various levels of the professional game.

From Premier League stars to Football League regulars, Newton Aycliffe's footballing footprint is larger than many might expect.

### Jason Steele

Perhaps the most recognisable name currently linked to the town is Jason Steele.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21527440.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Newton Aycliffe-born goalkeeper Jason Steele during his time at Sunderland. Steele has gone on to enjoy a long professional career, later reaching the Premier League with Brighton & Hove Albion. *(Image: John Cripps)*

Born in Newton Aycliffe, the goalkeeper progressed through the academy system at Middlesbrough before enjoying a lengthy professional career.

He played for Blackburn Rovers and Sunderland before establishing himself in the Premier League with Brighton & Hove Albion.

### Lewis Wing

Another player to emerge from the town is Lewis Wing.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21527401.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lewis Wing, one of Newton Aycliffe's best-known football exports, warms up for Middlesbrough during his rise through the professional game. *(Image: Jon Hobley)*

The midfielder made his breakthrough at Middlesbrough and has built a reputation for his energy, creativity and eye for goal.

His career has included notable spells with Rotherham United, Wycombe Wanderers and Reading, with his rise from local football to the professional ranks serving as inspiration for young players across County Durham.

But Newton Aycliffe’s football story began long before Steele and Wing.

### Lewis Vaughan Lodge

Lewis Vaughan Lodge, born in 1872, represented England and enjoyed a distinguished sporting career that also included first-class cricket.

More than a century later, his achievements remain part of the town's rich sporting heritage.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21527410.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lewis Vaughan Lodge, the England international footballer and first-class cricketer born in Newton Aycliffe, pictured during his playing career in the late 19th century. *(Image: Northern Echo)*

The town has also produced numerous players who made their mark closer to home.

### Chris Mason

Chris Mason became a well-known figure in the North East non-league game through his long association with Spennymoor Town, while former Darlington forward Dale Anderson made appearances in the Football League.

Former goalkeeper Simon Smith, who later moved into coaching and worked with Newcastle United, also hails from Newton Aycliffe, further adding to the town's footballing credentials.

For a town of its size, the list is impressive.

It speaks not only to individual talent but also to the grassroots clubs, school teams and volunteers who have helped foster a love of football across generations.

Today, youngsters kicking a ball around parks and pitches in Newton Aycliffe can look to figures such as Steele and Wing as proof that a journey from County Durham to the professional game is possible.

While football's brightest lights may shine elsewhere, Newton Aycliffe continues to show that talent can emerge from any corner of the North East.

And for a town with such a strong footballing pedigree, there is every chance the next name to make headlines is already honing their skills on a local pitch.
