Darlington has moved a step closer to gaining a direct rail link with the South Coast after Grand Central secured four trains for its proposed Newcastle to Brighton service.

The new route would connect the North East directly with Brighton, stopping at Darlington, and would bypass central London.

If approved by the Office of Rail and Road, the service would run up to five times a day each way between Newcastle and Brighton.

It would stop at Newcastle, Durham, Darlington, Northallerton, York, Doncaster, Sheffield, Derby, Burton-on-Trent, Birmingham New Street, Warwick Parkway, Banbury, Oxford, Reading, Wokingham, Guildford, Redhill, London Gatwick, Haywards Heath and Brighton.

Grand Central, Arriva’s open-access operator, has agreed to lease four additional five-car Class 180 trains from Angel Trains.

The deal will expand Grand Central’s fleet from 10 to 14 trains and move the new route plans forward.

Grand Central submitted its application to the rail regulator, the Office for Rail and Road (ORR) in December 2025 and the proposed service remains subject to approval.

If approved, it would be the first direct train service between Newcastle and Brighton via Gatwick.

The service would offer more than 1,000 seats a day each way, giving passengers direct journeys between the North East, the South Coast, Gatwick and Reading.

The four Class 180 Adelante trains have been out of service for almost three years.

They will be refurbished at Arriva TrainCare’s Eastleigh depot before entering service, subject to route approval.

The work is expected to support skilled engineering jobs at Eastleigh and benefit the wider rail supply chain.

Chris Brandon, director at Grand Central, said: "Passengers in York, Darlington and Durham have historically had little choice for their journeys south. It's either change in London or add hours on to your journey travelling by road.

"People deserve the option to travel directly by rail, and Grand Central has a proud track record of offering underserved communities direct links to other towns and cities, and to areas of opportunity. Our agreement to lease four new trains means we’re one step closer to this route becoming a reality, and we look forward to the ORR's response."

The announcement follows Arriva’s £300 million investment, announced last year, in nine new Hitachi Rail tri-mode battery-hybrid trains for Grand Central’s existing routes.

Craig Nicolson, business development director at Arriva, said: “Securing these trains is a significant milestone in our plans to deliver a new direct service linking Newcastle and Brighton.

"Subject to ORR approval, this route would connect communities that currently lack a direct rail option, making journeys simpler, faster and more attractive for passengers travelling between the North East, the South Coast and London Gatwick.

"Grand Central has a strong track record of opening up new travel opportunities and improving connectivity where it is needed most. This proposal would build on that legacy while encouraging more people to choose rail as a sustainable alternative to travelling by road.

"The agreement also demonstrates our continued confidence in the growth potential of open access rail services and our commitment to investing in the UK's railway.”

If approved, the five daily services could begin running in December 2026.
