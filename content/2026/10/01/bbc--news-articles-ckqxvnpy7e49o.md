**The Celebrity Traitors is back for a second series, and viewers will see the likes of Michael Sheen, Jerry Hall, Richard E Grant and Amol Rajan at the round table along with Bella Ramsey, Leigh-Anne Pinnock, Maya Jama and King Kenny.**

There are 21 celebrities taking part in total - two more than last year - and there will be an extra episode after the huge popularity of last year's series.

The extended 75-minute debut episode starts at 20:00 BST on Thursday, with Claudia Winkleman again hosting the murderous game.

Viewers can expect some fresh ideas, and executive producer Sarah Fay said they "always want to push the envelope" with the show's elaborate tasks.

"We're always trying to evolve existing ideas to bring them into a new life or coming up with different cultural references," she told the show's press launch.

![The Celebrity Traitors stood in the pews of a church with Claudia Winkelman standing in the middle; they are all smartly dressed](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/7873/live/4a975360-bbfc-11f1-90b9-913e4354ed35.jpg)

The Celebrity Traitors are finally gathered for episode one of series two

The celebrities will be playing for a £100,000 cash prize for charity.

The final amount will depend on how well they perform in the tasks, regardless of any possible tensions over who is a faithful and who is a traitor.

In the show's trailer, external, they are seen arriving in a church, smiling as they greet each other, but looking nervous.

They later travel to the castle in a fleet of black cars, before Winkleman chooses her traitors while everyone wears their blindfolds.

They then take part in their first mission in a lake in wetsuits, ending with Winkleman saying ominously: "Fame won't save them."

Romesh Ranganathan, Myha'la and Richard E Grant are also taking their places at the round table

Speaking at the press launch, Winkleman said she is totally focused on the celebrities while she's in the castle, and is glued to the monitors showing footage of them throughout the day.

"All of them were tremendous, and when we do any series I have this room with the monitors. I'm like a weirdo just applying fake tan, watching," she said.

The presenter added that even when she's told to go home, she tells the producers "I am not leaving", joking that she got barely any sleep.

Ed Gamble, host of BBC Two spin-off show Uncloaked, added that he watched "hours of footage a day" as well.

"So when they're eating breakfast, I'm eating breakfast watching them eat breakfast. It's actually quite sad... I'm normally sat there in my pyjamas eating cereal, watching them eat cereal," he told BBC News.

Viewers can also look forward to more of Winkleman's eclectic wardrobe choices.

"I like the fact that it is 'human crow meets golf caddy meets the Artful Dodger'. We're pretty happy with it," she joked about this year's outfits.

![Claudia Winkleman in a white shirt, black sleeveless jumper and long black skirt; she's holding a white lace handkerchief](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/64bf/live/3a962b00-bc0e-11f1-a524-3125ac41b7f5.jpg)

Claudia Winkleman said her clothes are as distinctive as ever on the show

The celebrities have also been speaking about the show before its launch, with comedian and presenter Romesh Ranganathan saying that despite being best friends with fellow comic Rob Beckett, he would have no problem stabbing him in the back.

"I mean, he's just as easily going to be on my side as he is going to twist the knife in my back given the opportunity," he said.

The show's other comics include Joe Lycett, James Acaster and Joanne McNally.

"I think we're going to be the first ones under the magnifying glass being looked at," Acaster said over suspicions one will be a traitor.

Comedian Alan Carr won the first series of Celebrity Traitors, which was the most watched TV programme of 2025 in the UK, watched by 15 million people.

The Last of Us and Game of Thrones star Bella Ramsey already has experience of playing similar games at drama clubs and at home, which is why they joined the show.

"I've been playing a version of The Traitors since I was 10 years old," they said.

Mathematician Hannah Fry, who called herself "toxically competitive", added she has already looked for "statistical patterns" in the data for previous games; while Little Mix singer Leigh-Anne Pinnock said she took advice from singer Cat Burns, who reached the final as a traitor last year.

"She had good advice. She said it might get intense around that round table – but remember, it's just a game," Pinnock said.

## 'I wouldn't be a contestant'

The show is also something of a love letter to the Scottish Highlands, where it is filmed, with sweeping drone shots of the scenery and Ardross Castle in Inverness, where the celebrities meet, eat and of course sit at the round table.

"It's breathtaking," Winkleman told the press, adding that she and her family were returning to the Highlands for a holiday.

"My husband was like, 'You're already there quite a lot, what's the matter with you?'" she said.

"It's the most beautiful place I've ever been in the world, is the truth."

After she recently chose to end her recent BBC One chat show, Winkleman is back on familiar ground with Celebrity Traitors.

BBC News asked if she would ever want to try being a contestant on the show, perhaps in the US or Australian versions.

"No, I'm too thick," she said in typical self-deprecating style.

"I mustn't play it in public. I must not.

"I'm just being honest - it's a privilege to play that game and to be at the round table, but I think I would just be too awful at it."
