The Trump administration’s brand new America.gov website has multiple images taken outside the country, according to a report.

​Launched at the end of September, the website offers users access to government information via a chatbot powered by artificial intelligence. President Donald Trump has lauded the platform, even describing its launch as “one of the most revolutionary product launches of all time.”

​However, an analysis carried out by *The Daily Beast* found that at least six out of the 11 stock pictures used on the webpage were actually taken outside of the country.

​One of the images, an image showing a mother and daughter looking at a tablet, was taken as far afield as Beijing - the capital of China, the U.S.’s major rival in the A.I. race.

![America.gov was launched by the Donald Trump administration this week - but feature pictures not taken in the US](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/29/17/2026-09-29T152458Z_794373886_RC23TNAM7GLR_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​Another showed a young couple, one of whom can be seen beaming at the camera. That image was taken in the Spanish city of Vigo. A second picture was taken in Spain, this time in Madrid. The image shows a child looking out of a window at commercial jets.

Trump has long criticized the European country’s government, even once suggesting that the U.S. could cancel all trade with Spain.

​A fourth picture, showing a young person jumping into a lake, was taken at Salt Spring Island in British Columbia, Canada - another country whose government has an increasingly difficult relationship with the Trump administration.

![This image was taken in Beijing, China, according to an analysis by The Daily Beast, but still appears on America.gov](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/01/13/42/Screenshot-2026-10-01-at-14-53-27.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=21%2C0%2C22%2C0&auto=webp)

​A fifth picture was taken in Poland, with a sixth being captured in Estonia, *The Daily Beast* discovered.

​At Tuesday’s National Design Studio launch event for the new tool, Trump heaped praise onto the platform, which he claimed was “not simply a website.”

​“It’s a restoration of America’s founding promise,” he declared. “And it’s a reinvention of your government for the 21st Century and beyond.”

​Despite that, even the business through which the six pictures were sourced is based outside of America. The images are registered on a website named Stocksy, which is in British Columbia, Canada, *The Daily Beast* revealed.

![This image was taken in Madrid, Spain, despite Trump having long clashed with the Spanish government](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/01/13/54/Screenshot-2026-10-01-at-14-53-07.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=24%2C0%2C25%2C0&auto=webp)

​*The Independent* has contacted the National Design Studio over the use of the stock photos.

​During the launch event, Trump praised Joe Gebbia, the founder of Airbnb and the architect of America.gov. Gebbia leads the National Design Studio as the U.S.’s first chief design officer.

​Trump also revealed that 20-year-old Edward Coristine, better known as “Big Balls” while working for Elon Musk’s Department of Government Efficiency, was the lead engineer on the project.

​The website made headlines earlier in the week after it declared that former President Biden was the winner of the 2020 election, despite Trump’s claims to the contrary.

​The launch of America.gov on Tuesday also came just days after Trump hosted Dario Amodei, the CEO of Anthropic, for a private dinner at the White House on Sunday.

In recent weeks, Amodei has warned of the need for greater regulations to be imposed on the development of A.I.
