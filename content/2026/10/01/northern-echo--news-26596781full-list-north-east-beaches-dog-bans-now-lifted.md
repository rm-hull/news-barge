Dog walkers in County Durham can once again enjoy unrestricted access to one of the region's most popular beaches after seasonal bans came to an end.

Restrictions introduced on beaches across the North East each year to protect visitors during the busy summer season were lifted yesterday (September 30), meaning dogs are now allowed back on previously restricted stretches of coastline.

For Durham residents, the change means dogs can once again be walked on the restricted section of Seaton Carew Beach, between North Shelter and Seaton Carew Golf Club.

Many County Durham beaches, including Seaham Hall Beach, Blackhall Rocks and Horden, welcome dogs all year, but Seaton Carew bans them from May to September.

The annual bans aim to prevent clashes between beach users during the busiest months and reduce dog fouling.

Elsewhere in the North East, dogs are now allowed back on restricted sections of Whitley Bay, Tynemouth Longsands, Cullercoats Bay, Sandhaven Beach, Roker Beach and Seaburn Beach.

Most Northumberland beaches welcome dogs all year, and restrictions have ended at Newbiggin-by-the-Sea, Blyth Beach and Amble Little Shore Beach.

The change comes as quieter autumn conditions return, putting beach walks back on the agenda for dog owners across the region.
