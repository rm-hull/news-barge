# The costs associated with caring for adults in North Yorkshire - Carl Les

## One of the major areas of expenditure for North Yorkshire Council, the largest in cash terms, is the money we spend on caring for adults. Adults of all ages and abilities, but especially those who are elderly, frail, and with vulnerabilities both mental and physical.

Unlike highway and footpath maintenance and repairs, visible to all, or education services like schools and home to school travel, this spend is often unseen unless it is happening for you and your family members. It is highly personal in most cases, although much is to do with groups in communities, and is often bespoke, an individual contract between the Council or a third party carer, designed with the person concerned and delivered by care professionals who deserve all the respect we should show them. Sometimes overlooked, they are truly an integral and vital part of the national health of our country.

As it is such a big part of our budget it is right that we look to make savings and efficiencies, new ways of working even, as the costs within care have their own inflationary pressures, like payroll taxes, energy costs, food inflation, that often outstrip headline inflation figures and government support, and especially as demand for these services is ever increasing year on year. However any such review must be done sympathetically as these measures affect real people, and real peoples’ lives.

Last week along with councillor colleagues I was pleased to be shown examples of our working assumptions as we start to develop a model for the future. This was policy development being done the right way with elected Members on a scrutiny committee, but open to all, at the start of the development of ideas.

![A care home resident holds hands with her daughter. PIC: Andrew Matthews/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/news/opinion/columnists/smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65.jpg)


The purpose of the discussion was to see where we had come from to where we want to get to, check out a proposed procurement plan, confirm our financial modelling and its estimated impact, provide an overview of any redesign to services, and importantly identify assurance on risks and mitigations, and how the governance of such a change will be carried out.

The present arrangements, comprising of an Approved Provider List (APL) covers Care Homes, Home Support, Support in the Community, and Supported Living. It will end in November 2027 and although two 12 month extensions are permissible, it is considered better to reprocure most aspects now ready for 11/27 rather than extend and procure at a later time. A post-implementation review, usual in major policy introductions, identified benefits that should be retained, along with areas requiring improvement. The numbers are eye-watering, the seven year contract period covers expenditure of over £3.5bn, broken down into £2.2bn on care home provision, £700m of home-based support, £100m on support based in the community and £550m on supported living.

Workstreams are being established to look at the areas of service design, finance, new contracts, systems, quality, and workforce, and bringing all this into a single project plan with a phased approach to delivery. Service continuity is vital, as are excellent communications with providers, partners and the health service and especially our clients, the people with the lived experience.

The procurement plan needs to balance affordability, pace of change and deliverability, and will replace the APL with four separate open frameworks, each one tailored to the service market concerned. Service continuity is paramount so we will avoid introducing all four major frameworks at once. In fact the Supported Living APL will be subject to a 12 month APL Extension.

Service design is being done with extensive engagement with those people with the lived experience, carers, providers, our own teams and system partners, but will be done with reference to the independent Cost of Care review we have recently commissioned. Draft proposals will be tested with providers to evaluate operational feasibility, and formal consultations will guide the final designs and plans for implementation.

The Care Home framework deals with the largest financial call, and there will be 4 tiers of provision covered, Tier 1 is standard, Tier 2 enhanced, Tier 3 is specialist dementia and frailty, and Tier 4 is further specialist care for people with profound and multiple disabilities.

The £700m market for home care will be considered in two phases. The first being a four locality framework, with providers competing for the business on price and local capacity within an agreed floor and ceiling of costs. The second phase will be ten community alliances with a small number of providers to be determined for each identified community.

The £100m open framework for community based care will encourage growth and diversity in the market, both for services delivered on-site and out in the community, and also for employment support for those who want to consider such activities. There will be the ability to commission transport and block contracts within the framework.
