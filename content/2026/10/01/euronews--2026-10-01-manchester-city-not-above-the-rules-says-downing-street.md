## After PM Andy Burnham's - a former mayor of Greater Manchester - admission of his fears that the club's owners might leave because of the investigation into 'creative book-keeping', his spokesperson has issued a clarification.

Downing Street said Thursday that Manchester City were not "above the rules", following criticism of British Prime Minister Andy Burnham's comments about the case.

In a statement, a No 10 spokesperson said: "As the prime minister made clear, the initial judgement is serious and there can't be any suggestion that anyone is above the rules."

Burnham, the former mayor of Greater Manchester and an avid supporter of Everton, told the BBC on Wednesday he did not want to see the club's Emirati owners walk away as a result of the case. "I would be really concerned to lose them," he said, highlighting the investment the club's controlling Abu Dhabi United Group had made in Manchester.

The 10-time English champions, who deny any wrongdoing, face an uncertain future after being found guilty of more than 100 breaches.

The Abu Dhabi-backed super-club were found to have arranged "sham" deals to artificially inflate revenues and reduce costs by more than £900 million (€1.05 billion) between 2009 and 2018.

Downing Street said in its statement the "ongoing independent process" into the club must be "allowed to run its course and the outcome respected". "Wherever wrongdoing is established, those responsible should face the appropriate consequences," the spokesperson added.
