AN ANIMAL rescue centre has hit back at claims that two cats were rehomed without their owner's permission.

Rabia Mukhtar told the T&A that Allerton Cat Rescue rehomed her cats, Lily and Sooty, despite her request that they be kept until her ex-partner could adopt them.

However, the rescue centre has rebuked the claims, stating that Ms Mukhtar signed the cats over to them.

### 'We are not a cattery and do not board cats'

Allerton Cat Rescue said the cats were signed over to their care and that Ms Mukhtar was told they would be put up for adoption.

A spokesperson for the centre said: "The cats were brought to us and signed over.

"Rabia was asked if she was sure she wanted to give them up as, being spayed/neutered, vaccinated and microchipped, they were ready for adoption.

"She stated her ex-husband had nowhere to live so could not take them.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21630871.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The site of Allerton Cat Rescue on Allerton Road *(Image: Google Maps)*

"We are not a cattery and do not board cats.

"Rabia was made aware that the cats would be put up for adoption.

"She signed the cats over to the rescue and we rehomed them together."

The centre said it cannot share information about the cats' new owners due to data protection regulations.

### 'I explained I wanted my ex to be given the opportunity to adopt my cats'

Ms Mukhtar said she needed help with the cats following a relationship breakdown.

She said: "I have two cats that I bought in July 2021 and 2022, so I have owned them both for almost five years.

"I spoke to my ex to see if he wanted to take care of the cats, as I didn't feel I was able to take them on at that time.

"I enquired with Allerton Cat Rescue and they arranged for me to drop the cats off.

"Before I visited them, I explained that I wanted my ex to be given the opportunity to adopt my cats before they got rehomed."

### 'The following day, he was told they had been rehomed'

Ms Mukhtar said that she took both Lily and Sooty to the rescue centre on October 10.

She said that she was asked to sign a form, but maintains that it was not a surrender form.

The following day, Ms Mukhtar received an email from the rescue centre stating they could not contact her ex-partner directly.

She said: "I was concerned that the cats were going to get rehomed.

"My ex said he had got in touch with the centre and said he was interested in adopting my cats.

"The next day, he was told that they had already been rehomed."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21630838.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lily and Sooty *(Image: Rabia Mukhtar)*

Ms Mukhtar visited the rescue centre on Monday, October 12, to find out what had happened.

She said: "I was told that it's a rescue centre, and what did I expect to happen.

"I asked them what the process of rehoming was.

"When I enquired beforehand, they never said it would be an issue for my ex to take the cats."

### 'I didn't abandon them there - it was a very difficult decision'

Ms Mukhtar is now looking for answers as to the whereabouts of Lily and Sooty.

She said: "I just want an explanation of what has happened to my cats.

"The people at the centre are now ignoring me.

"I just need to know what's happened to them.

"It's really unfair."

### Petition set up to reunite cats with original owner

A petition has since been set up to return Lily and Sooty to Ms Mukhtar.

The description reads: "In a heart-wrenching twist of events, Sooty and Lily, two beloved family cats, have been caught in a limbo.

"Let your voice be heard so these cats can be safely returned to their loving home."
