Bradford's Dream Art Jiu Jitsu academy have enjoyed an extraordinary weekend of success.

On Sunday, Dream Art Bradford finished first in the NoGi team standings and third in the Gi standings at the Elite Junior European Gi and NoGi Championships in Wolverhampton, while athletes also struck gold at major competitions in Coventry and Leeds.

The achievement in Wolverhampton was particularly impressive given that much of the squad was made up of young and relatively inexperienced competitors.

Among the standout performers was Hayden Quirke, who secured gold in both Gi and NoGi.

Melia Annoville and Abdur-Raheem Khan also claimed double gold, while Ar-Rayyan Butt, Evelyn Shah, Szymon Skoberia, Akim Evsyukov, Amanda Caune and Mihai-Alexandru Rosioru all won their respective divisions.

For Amanda and Alex, the victories were particularly special, with both youngsters taking gold in their first ever competitions.

The previous day, another group of Dream Art Bradford athletes competed at the ADCC British Open Championships in Coventry.

Akim continued his outstanding form with another gold medal, while Melia added a silver medal to her recent successes.

Meesam Hussain, who was recently crowned an IBJJF Kids NoGi European champion in Lisbon, also won his division with an impressive foot lock following a saddle entry.

His younger brother, Abbas Hussain, produced one of the weekend's most memorable performances.

After suffering defeat in his weight division, Abbas recovered to claim bronze before entering the absolute category.

There, he faced the same opponent who had defeated him earlier in the competition, reversed the result and went on to claim absolute gold.

Meanwhile, at the Empire Grappling Northern Sub Only Championships in Leeds, Otis Young claimed gold in the Juvenile Male Intermediate Gi division.

Otis faced the same opponent three times, winning the opening match, losing the second and then showing impressive resilience to win the deciding contest and secure the championship.

Dream Art Bradford head coach Craig Tetley said the performances highlighted the development of the academy's young athletes.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638862.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Craig Tetley is proud of his athletes, for their performances but mainly for their attitudes. *(Image: UGC)*

He said: "To finish first in NoGi and third in Gi at the Junior Europeans with such a young team is absolutely fantastic.

"We're incredibly proud of what these kids are achieving, and the standard they're reaching is a reflection of the work they're putting in.

"But for me, some of the most important victories over the weekend weren't necessarily the ones that came away with medals."

Tetley highlighted the experience of young competitor Adamjeet Singh, who became overwhelmed by nerves upon arriving at the Wolverhampton venue.

He said: "Adamjeet was absolutely overwhelmed by fear and told us he didn't want to compete.

"So we had a conversation and changed what winning looked like for him.

"It wasn't about beating an opponent or winning a medal. His victory that day was finding the courage to put his Gi on, step through that fear and get onto the mat.

"Of all the performances I witnessed over the weekend, that was one of the moments that made me most proud."

Tetley also praised Hayden Quirke, who lost his first 10 competitions before developing into one of the academy's most experienced junior competitors.

He said: "Hayden's a fantastic example of what these experiences can give a young person.

"He lost his first 10 competitions, but he kept coming back.

"This weekend he won double gold, but what impresses me most is the composure he's developed.

"Winning, losing, pressure and disappointment have all become normal experiences for him. He's incredibly calm in situations that would overwhelm many people.

"And I look at him and think about what those qualities will mean throughout his life."
