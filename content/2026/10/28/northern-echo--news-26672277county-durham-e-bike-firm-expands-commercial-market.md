A County Durham e-bike specialist is moving into the commercial market after securing funding to help businesses switch to greener transport.

Velo Electric, based in Consett and led by Richard Boulger, is offering electric cargo bikes to businesses, councils and other organisations.

The company is based on the former Consett steelworks site, redeveloped through a wider regeneration project.

Mr Boulger said: "Velo Electric has been trading for three years, and we are the only specialist e-bike retailer in the North East of England.

"The funding has enabled us to expand the range of e-bikes and e-cargo test bikes that we have in the shop, so we can continue to expand the consumer side of the business at the same time as moving into business-to-business opportunities for commercial customers.

"We are trying to remove barriers that prevent people trying e-bikes and here on our premises, people have the opportunity to test them."

A regeneration fund loan from Tata Steel’s business support subsidiary, UKSE, has funded the expansion.

Velo Electric’s aims to promote active travel, improve public health and cut carbon match the goals of organisations including UKSE.

Mr Boulger said: "For the last 10 years, I have seen how e-bikes give people more opportunities and independence.

"We have customers who have had heart attacks or strokes, undergone hip or knee replacements, or simply aren’t as fit as they once were.

"I’ve seen how e-bikes have helped them regain their confidence, get back outdoors and enjoy cycling again."

Businesses across the region are also using cargo e-bikes for deliveries and transport around their sites.

UMi connected Velo Electric with funding at its FUEL Roadshow.

Mr Boulger said: "UMi set me up with an appointment with UKSE at the FUEL Roadshow and it went from there.

"The loan from UKSE has been so useful, as we continue to develop the business-to-business side of Velo Electric’s operations and further expand the consumer side.

"Both UMi and UKSE have been lovely to work with – fantastic."

Claire Hinde, UMi’s business funding executive, said the roadshow connects businesses with finance to help them grow.

She said: "We met Richard at our Newcastle FUEL Roadshow, which was the first one of a series we are running throughout the UK.

"The overarching goal of the roadshows is to make it easier for businesses to access finance by facilitating meetings with potential funders and investors or, if they are at an earlier stage in their business journey, help them access information to get them investment ready.

"We are delighted to work with organisations like UKSE and to see a business like Velo Electric grow as a result of gaining access to finance through one of our events is great."

Peter Taylor, UKSE’s business development manager, said supporting Velo Electric fits the organisation’s wider goals.

He said: "Through our work, we are not only committed to providing business support and finance.

"We are also dedicated to regenerating the communities we serve, contributing to improving the environment and promoting health and wellbeing.

"This makes being able to support a company like Velo Electric, which is actively helping businesses and communities to reduce their carbon footprint while promoting health and wellbeing, particularly rewarding.

"We hope to see Richard’s business continue to go from strength to strength."
