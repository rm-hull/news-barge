A local authority has offered affected residents its “sincere and unreserved apologies”, following an email blunder.

Stockton Council emailed “a number of people”, regarding changes to consultation events, but failed to utilise the Bcc function, meaning the email addresses of individuals on the council’s mailing list were visible to other recipients.

The initial email was sent to residents on Monday (October 26), at 12.35pm, with a follow-up from the local authority shortly after, in which the council said that the mistake was caused by “human error”.

The council told recipients that it had been an “isolated” incident and offered “sincere and unreserved apologies”.

The authority added that it recognised the importance of protecting personal information and that it understands the “concern that this incident may cause”.

Those who were sent the email were also told that the council is reviewing its procedures and implementing additional protocols to make sure that “it does not happen again”.

Recipients were asked to delete the email that had been sent at 12.35pm, and refrain from using the “Reply all” feature in relation to that original message.

One of the email recipients said that 250 peoples’ email addresses had been revealed as a result of the error, and said that they had asked the council to confirm whether the incident had been formally logged as a personal data breach, and referred to the data protection officer.

The recipient described the original email in question as a “Local Plan consultation update”.

The council’s follow-up email to affected residents, sent on Monday afternoon, added: “This data breach has been reported to our information governance team and is being managed in accordance with our data protection procedures.

"Should you have any questions or concerns regarding this matter, please do not hesitate to contact us.”

When contacted for comment, a spokesperson for the council said: “We are aware of an incident which has taken place and it has been referred to the council’s data protection officer for further investigation.”
