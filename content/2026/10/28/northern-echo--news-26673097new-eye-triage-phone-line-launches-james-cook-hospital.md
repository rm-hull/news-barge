Patients with urgent eye problems will be able to access specialist care more quickly through a new service being introduced in the North East.

The James Cook University Hospital is launching a dedicated triage telephone line for urgent, non-sight-threatening eye conditions.

The new service will begin on Tuesday, November 3.

Kathryn Young, clinical matron at James Cook, said: "Introducing a dedicated triage telephone line will help ensure patients receive the right care, in the right place, at the right time for their individual needs.

"The service will provide quicker access to specialist advice for patients with urgent eye conditions while helping our teams prioritise those with sight-threatening eye emergencies."

Patients are being advised to call 01642 854096 before visiting the hospital if they experience symptoms such as suspected eye infections, new onset of flashes or floaters, a dark curtain over vision, red or painful eyes, or flashing lights and floaters without blurred vision.

A triage nurse will assess each caller’s symptoms over the phone and direct them to the most appropriate care pathway.

The telephone line will be available Monday to Friday from 8am to 4pm, and on weekends and bank holidays from 8am to 12 noon.

Walk-in appointments at the emergency eye care department will still be available during these hours for patients with sight-threatening emergencies.

These include sudden loss of vision, chemical burns, newly developed double vision, severe pain with nausea or headaches, flashing lights and floaters accompanied by vision loss, and complications following intraocular eye surgery within the past month.

Patients with acute eye concerns such as mild irritation, dry or sticky eyes or gradual blurred vision should contact their GP or local optician instead.

Those experiencing emergency eye problems outside of the department’s opening hours should attend their nearest emergency department or urgent treatment centre.

For more information about opening times, location details, and treatment pathways, patients can visit the emergency eye care department page on the South Tees Hospitals website at southtees.nhs.uk/services/ophthalmology/eye-casualty-department/.
