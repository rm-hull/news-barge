The "father of PlayStation" has praised the North East's creative legacy after getting a taste of the region, including a seaside trip to Saltburn.

Japanese engineer Ken Kutaragi, the man behind PlayStation, visited Teesside as a keynote speaker at Game Republic New Horizons, held from October 14 to 16 at Middlesbrough's Riverside Stadium.

During his stay, Kutaragi revealed that he had explored the North East coast with a trip to Saltburn.

Reflecting on the seaside town, he told the BBC: "Wow, a very good, beach.

"I had a chance to eat very big, super fine, fish and chips."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20726502.jpg?type=mds-article-620)PlayStation creator Ken Kutaragi *(Image: Supplied)*

The North East's creative heritage particularly struck Mr Kutaragi, who drew parallels between its past and its emerging industries.

Discussing his first impressions, he said: "For the first time I'm here, but I had some images: many brick buildings.

"I love science fiction movies - Blade Runner was one of the best movies.

"Ridley Scott came from here, but just like Blade Runner, the new industry is coming from here."

Ridley Scott grew up in the North East before going on to direct blockbuster films including Alien, Gladiator and Blade Runner.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/17470272.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ridley Scott *(Image: Ian West/PA Wire)*

Born in Tokyo in 1950, Kutaragi joined Sony in 1975 and oversaw the creation of the original PlayStation, PlayStation 2, PlayStation Portable and PlayStation 3.

He later became chairman and group chief executive of Sony Computer Entertainment, helping take PlayStation and the games industry into the global mainstream.

His Middlesbrough appearance was billed as a rare UK visit and was one of the headline attractions at this year's Game Republic New Horizons.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21610073.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kutaragi speaking at the Game Republic conference *(Image: Tom Banks)*

Kutaragi also spoke about his love of science fiction and the ambitions that shaped his approach to the industry.

He said: "I love science fiction novels. I had a big dream that someday we, our human species, can achieve something.

"Gathering many creators, many designers, and even the future players, getting together in this industry – not the PlayStation industry, in this industry, that was our intention."

Game Republic New Horizons returned to Tees Valley for its second year in 2026, bringing together games companies and creative and digital leaders from across the UK and overseas.

More than 400 game developers, publishers, studio leaders, entrepreneurs and investors attended the sold-out conference, which attracted delegates from across the UK, Europe, Asia, Canada and the United States.

The programme included an indie games showcase, masterclasses, business workshops, technology sessions, an expo and an investor zone, alongside Kutaragi's keynote interview.
