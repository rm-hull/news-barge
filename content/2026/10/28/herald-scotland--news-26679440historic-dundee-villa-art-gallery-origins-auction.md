A historic villa with art gallery origins is heading to auction.

The Vine, a Category A-listed neo-Greek villa in Dundee's sought-after West End, is set to go under the hammer in November with a guide price of £475,000.

Built in 1836 for George Duncan MP, The Vine was originally commissioned as a private art gallery and remains an architectural landmark today.

The substantial single-storey villa is set on a raised plinth and features two principal façades, including a grand eastern entrance and an elegant south-facing frontage centred around a library window overlooking the gardens and River Tay.

In more recent years, the property has been used as meeting rooms and a business centre, but offers flexible accommodation with significant scope for refurbishment and alternative future uses, subject to the necessary consents.

Highlights include a series of large reception rooms, a one-bedroom flat, lower ground storage areas and a dramatic cupola, while the property will be offered with vacant possession.

Mhairi Archibald of Savills Auctions said: Opportunities to acquire a building as distinctive as The Vine are exceptionally rare. Its architectural significance, fascinating history and prominent West End location make it one of the most unusual offerings we've brought to auction in Scotland this year.

"Whether a buyer is looking to restore it as a unique private residence, continue a commercial use, or explore alternative opportunities subject to consent, the property offers tremendous potential and is likely to attract interest from a wide range of purchasers."

The property will be offered as Lot 104 in Savills' auction on November 3, 2026.
