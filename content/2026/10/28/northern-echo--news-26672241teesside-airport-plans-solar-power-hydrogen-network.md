Teesside International Airport is set to generate its own solar power as it works towards becoming operationally net zero by 2030.

The airport has teamed up with renewable energy specialists Geo Green Power to generate 1.4MW of solar power on site, with plans to expand as demand grows.

The project supports the airport’s aim to become operationally net zero by 2030 and lead on clean energy and low-carbon transport.

Phil Forster, managing director of Teesside International Airport, said: "We've said time and again that this is a growing airport building a future backing jobs, investment, and cleaner industries.

"This agreement helps us do just that – getting solar power on site and opening up a route to a hydrogen-powered future.

"This will mean cleaner ground operations, cleaner transport around the airport, and a site fit for the future.

"It's great to forge this partnership with Geo Green Power as we strive to lead the way in becoming the UK’s greenest airport.

"I’m excited to see us deepen our relationship and drive forward these plans."

The airport also plans to work with North East energy company GeoPura to build a hydrogen network across the site and nearby transport routes.

Solar power could be used to produce green hydrogen on site, cutting emissions from airport operations.

The hydrogen could replace diesel generators and power ground vehicles, taxis, last-mile transport and possibly airport buses.

Derek Bulmer, senior vice president and chief development officer at GeoPura, said: "Airports are complex energy environments, with significant power demand across terminals, ground operations, logistics, and the wider transport network that supports them.

"We look forward to collaborating on an integrated approach – starting with renewable solar generation today and building towards a wider hydrogen ecosystem that can support the airport as its energy requirements grow.

"The potential goes well beyond replacing one source of power.

"By looking at how renewable electricity, hydrogen production, and hydrogen-powered transport can work together, Teesside has an opportunity to demonstrate a model that could be replicated at airports and transport hubs elsewhere."

Reliable, clean energy will become even more important as the airport expands its business park and cargo facilities.

A new cargo facility is being built east of the main terminal, while defence firm Draken is investing £7 million in a hangar at the site.

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said: "This deal with Geo Green Power shows how we lead the way in clean energy – and how our airport has a key role to play.

"As the airport continues to expand, it's vital that growth and sustainability go hand in hand, and this agreement is another important step towards our ambition of securing our airport's future and ensuring it thrives."

"1.4MW of solar is a significant amount, roughly equivalent to putting solar on 350 to 400 houses and, coupled with green hydrogen, creates a substantial source of clean energy for the airport."
