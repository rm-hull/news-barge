A major music festival has announced it will return to the North East for the first time in more than a decade.

BBC Radio 6 Music has confirmed that its next 6 Music Festival will be based in Newcastle, marking its first visit to the city since 2015.

The 2027 festival will be the first time in five years that it has not taken place in Greater Manchester, and will feature live performances and DJ sets across a variety of independent venues across the city.

The 6 Music Festival will run from March 15 to 19 next year, with specific acts and venues to be announced. Samantha Moy, head of BBC Radio 6 Music, said: "For the next chapter of the 6 Music Festival, we will take our celebration of independent and grassroots venues to Newcastle.

"The city and the region have such a rich musical legacy and a thriving creative community, which will make for another wonderful setting."

The 6 Music Festival began in 2014, touring cities across the UK each year before settling in Greater Manchester in 2023.

Newcastle and Gateshead co-hosted the festival as Tyneside in 2015, with acts such as Interpol, Royal Blood, Glass Animals and Hot Chip performing across the weekend.

During a promising time for North East music, the 6 Music Festival is now set to return after over a decade away.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/18448220.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sunderland-born broadcaster Lauren Laverne said it was "fantastic" to see Newcastle host again. *(Image: Ian West/PA Wire)*

Sunderland-born broadcaster Lauren Laverne said it was "fantastic" to see Newcastle host again, adding that she couldn't wait to join audiences for a "brilliant celebration of live music."

Laverne said: "Fresh from the Mercury Prize taking place in Newcastle for the second year, I'm thrilled that the 6 Music Festival will be heading back to the city in March.

"Having grown up in the North East, I know just how much music means to people here and the important role it plays in local culture and communities."

The festival has previously been held in cities including Glasgow, Liverpool, Cardiff, and London, featuring performances from some of the world's biggest alternative artists such as Depeche Mode, Little Simz, Primal Scream, Wet Leg, and Arlo Parks.

Jim Mawdsley, principal advisor for events and culture at Newcastle City Council, described music as a "vital element of our cultural lifeblood," in the North East, adding: "Since it was last here, Newcastle has become a true music city with many of our artists being recognised, not least by awards like the Mercury Prize.

"It has shortlisted Sam Fender, Jade and Knats in the last two years, but there is a real collaborative spirit in the City and North East region that is definitely making us stronger, and we believe that our moment is now.

"The 6 Music Festival will shine a light on the amazing grassroots venues and promoters we have in the city that are a vital and essential part of both the eco and artist support systems."

The full line-up and list of venues will be announced in 2027.

The festival's 2015 Tyneside edition spanned six stages over three nights, with daytime events in Newcastle's Ouseburn and Stephenson Quarter.

Highlights from the festival will be broadcast on BBC Radio 6 Music and BBC Sounds, as well as on BBC Music’s YouTube channel.

The festival’s return to Newcastle follows a growing recognition of the city’s music and cultural scene, with events like the MOBO Awards and the Mercury Prize also recently hosted in the North East.

Fans across the UK will once again have the chance to experience the city’s energy and musical spirit through the festival’s national coverage and live events.
