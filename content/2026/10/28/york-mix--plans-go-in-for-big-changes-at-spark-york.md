Fresh plans have been submitted to overhaul York’s popular container destination Spark York.

Co-founders Tom McKenzie and Sam Leach have put forward a revised scheme for the Piccadilly venue to make it safer, more accessible and easier to navigate for visitors.

The new planning application replaces a previous one which was withdrawn, and contains revised proposals following talks with council planning officers.

Having secured permission in autumn 2025 to stay on Piccadilly until September 2030, the community interest company is now looking to invest in a major “Refresh Project”.

The most notable change is the complete removal of a previously planned entrance canopy, dropped to address heritage concerns regarding nearby listed buildings and the historic core conservation area.

A previously proposed lightwell near the south stair has also been scrapped.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/spark-york-revised-entrance-oct-2026-pd.webp)

Under the revised blueprint, Spark’s forecourt will be decluttered and resurfaced to create a cleaner, step-free entrance from Piccadilly.

The existing double entrance gate will make way for a simpler single gate to open up sightlines into the courtyard.

Inside, heavy fixed tables and benches inside the ground-floor entrance hall are set to be replaced with flexible, modular furniture.

This will allow the 89-square-metre space to host pop-up art exhibitions, workshops, maker classes, and community gatherings alongside daily street-food dining.

To maintain overall eating and drinking capacity while freeing up that ground floor space, first-floor decking will be extended in two compact areas totalling 38 square metres.

Both extensions will sit behind existing shipping containers, aiming to shield neighbours from noise and avoiding any new exposed seating near nearby homes.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/spark-york-ground-floor-evening-pd.webp)

Moving between floors is also getting a substantial upgrade. The passenger lift is being shifted to the rear of the site, opening up views straight across the venue from the front entrance.

And the scaffold stairs are being replaced with permanent, bespoke steel staircases complete with timber handrails, LED safety lighting, and midway landings to make climbing between floors easier for all visitors.

In their submission letter to City of York Council, the co-founders stated: “We believe these changes are necessary in making our venue more accessible, welcoming and fit for purpose until our lease end date of 2030.”

Since launching eight years ago, Spark has welcomed more than 400,000 visitors annually, provided space to over 100 community groups each year, and helped launch 26 independent businesses into permanent high street venues across York.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/spark-york-sept-2025-insta.webp)

The site at 17-21 Piccadilly has a long history in the city, having once served as the 1931 Airspeed aviation factory, a trolleybus depot, and later Reynard’s Garage.

After sitting derelict for nearly two decades from the mid-1990s, the buildings were demolished in 2015.

Spark opened its doors in May 2018 with 23 repurposed shipping containers, transforming a city centre brownfield into a lively community and street food hotspot.

Since its launch, Spark has welcomed more than 400,000 visitors annually, provided space to over 100 community groups each year, and helped launch 26 independent businesses into permanent high street venues across York.
