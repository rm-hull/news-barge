North East businesses remain under financial pressure despite a fall in company insolvencies across England and Wales, restructuring experts have warned.

The Insolvency Service recorded 1,882 company insolvencies in England and Wales in September 2026.

That was 3.6 per cent below August’s 1,953 and 4.6 per cent lower than September 2025’s 1,973.

Azets, a top-10 UK accountancy and business advisory firm with offices in Durham, Newcastle and Teesside, said many companies were still struggling despite the monthly fall.

Richard Oddy, restructuring and insolvency partner at Azets, said: "September is typically when the true picture of the summer trading period emerges, and where business owners and management teams decide whether they can keep going or need to close the doors.

"With the holidays ending and court-time capacity returning to its usual level, it’s also a month where HMRC and other creditors resume chasing debts.

"Despite the monthly fall in insolvencies, this September marked the end of a chapter rather than a change of season for many businesses as the economic alarm bells continue to sound.

"Costs are still rising, profits are still shrinking, and vacancies are at a 12-year low as many businesses simply can’t afford to hire.

"With Christmas on the horizon, a number of firms will be hoping for an end-of-year boost to their finances, but in the current climate it’s hard to see where that will come from – and it could be that the final three months of this year are the final three months of trading for some."

Mr Oddy said the fall in administrations was the main reason insolvencies dropped last month, after more than 250 connected real-estate companies entered administration between March and August.

But compulsory liquidations rose for a fourth consecutive month, reaching their second-highest level of the year.

He said: "Compulsory liquidations have increased for the fourth consecutive month to the second highest figure this year as creditors in both public and private sectors continue to chase down debts, and Creditors’ Voluntary Liquidations remain high as directors continue to run out of fight and funds after years of tough trading conditions."

Rising costs remain the “biggest issue”, he said.

"Increases in wages and materials have squeezed margins tighter – to a point of distress for many.

"These rises in outgoings, coupled with fuel prices remaining high as a result of the ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and rising energy bills, have made it tougher and tougher to do business this year and there appears to be no relief on the horizon."

Mr Oddy warned that any further financial strain could trigger more insolvencies.

He said: "Right now, firms are feeling the pressures of six years of rising outgoings and economic and geopolitical volatility, and any further increases in expenses could tip more into insolvency."

He urged business owners to act at the first sign of trouble.

"Anyone worried about their business or its finances should seek advice as soon as possible.

"The warning signs are common: delays in paying invoices or taxes, shrinking cashflow, or mounting stock levels are signs a firm is either financially distressed or about to become financially distressed, and its management team needs to seek advice before the problem becomes more serious.

"We know it’s a hard conversation to have, but starting it when the signs of financial distress first start showing themselves gives you more time, more options and a better chance of turning your situation around than if you’d waited till the issues became more severe and potentially less salvageable."
