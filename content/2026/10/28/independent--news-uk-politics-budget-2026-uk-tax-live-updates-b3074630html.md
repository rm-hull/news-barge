John Healey is setting out Labour’s much-anticipated tax and spending plan in his autumn Budget.

The chancellor, who has said he will focus on creating “breathing space for Britain”, opened his statement to the Commons by saying it was for “the working people of Britain, for British businesses, big and small”, built on “fiscal discipline”.

He said he was delivering his Budget “at a time when global uncertainties and pressures are swirling worldwide and hitting people here in Britain”.

Ahead of the announcement, it emerged the government will abolish standing charges on gas bills for millions of people, reportedly saving households up to £50 a year - a move welcomed by consumer expert Martin Lewis.

Mr Healey is also expected to announce a freeze in fuel duty and an increase in minimum wage by nearly 4 per cent.

The chancellor is sticking with the fiscal rules set by predecessor Rachel Reeves, requiring day-to-day spending to be financed by taxes rather than borrowing - which indicates there could be tax rises unveiled.

## Healey hits out at Liz Truss ‘blowing a black hole’ in the British economy

*Political reporter Athena Stavrou:*

The chancellor has criticised former prime minister Liz Truss over her disastrous mini budget and her new book.

John Healey said the IMF has said Britain now “stands out” on its action to “reduce the debt and deficit”.

He said the last time this happened was when Liz Truss crashed the economy."Her new book claims she could have taken the British people to Mars," he said.

"In reality, what she achieved in government – helped by the shadow chancellor – was blowing a black hole in the budget.“That is the difference between a Conservative government and a Labour government.”

## Healey says he won’t raise tax on working people

*Whitehall editor Kate Devlin reports:*

The chancellor says he will use some of the Treasury’s ‘headroom’ to absorb part of the impact of the Iran war.He told MPs that would protect the economy and protect family budgets.

He added: “My choices in this Budget mean I have kept the Labour manifesto promise not to raise tax on working people.”But this led to jerring from opposition MPs.One of the central pledges of the Labour’s 2024 manifesto was to not raise taxes on “working people”.

But ex-chancellor Rachel Reeves faced accusations she did just that last year by freezing personal tax thresholds until 2031.

## John Healey slashes headroom from £23bn to £15bn

*Political correspondent Millie Cooke reports:*

John Healey has reduced the fiscal headroom from £23bn to £15bn."The OBR confirms today that headroom against the stability rule is £15bn in 2029/30, before rising to £22bn in 2030/31", he said. "That’s what a buffer is for. Use it when you need it. Rebuild it when you can."

He added: "And so, despite the global challenges, this Budget puts us on track to eliminate the current budget deficit and get Britain’s finances into the black within 3 years."

## Analysis: Healey sticking to Rachel Reeves’ borrowing rules

*Political editor David Maddox:*

John Healey wants to keep the gilt markets calm over the level of borrowing especially after he confirmed that the interest payments on UK debt has risen £14bn largely as a result of the Middle East war.

It means that the new chancellor has confirmed that he will “stick to the fiscal rules” which were set by Rachel Reeves when she was chancellor in an attempt to ensure that the markets do not panic and prevent the UK from defaulting on its debt.

This is despite huge pressures to spend more especially on defence and welfare.Instead Mr Healey has confirmed that he will pay off debt over the next three years.

## £14bn more to be spent on debt interest, OBR predicts

*Political reporter Athena Stavrou reports:*

John Healey has said that Britain will spent £14 billion more on debt interest this year, according to the OBR’s predictions.

Highlighting the economic impact of conflict in the Middle East, he said government borrowing costs across the G7 “are reaching highs not seen for decades”.

“In today’s forecast, the OBR predict that we will spend £14bn more on debt interest this year,” he said.“Those are the storms we face. We can’t wish them away. We must choose the course we steer through them.”

## John Healey warns of 'economic storms' from Iran war

*Political correspondent Millie Cooke reports:*

As he opened his first Budget, John Healey has warned that Britain is facing "economic storms" as a result of conflict in the Middle East, the Ukraine war and global trade tensions.

The chancellor told the Commons: "Madam Deputy Speaker, analysts like to speak of economic headwinds. But what we face today are global economic storms… …with trade tensions acting as a drag on global growth, and Putin’s illegal invasion of Ukraine approaching the end of its fifth year.

"The OBR sets out that the war in the Middle East is a key driver affecting today's forecasts. And this government continues to work with our allies to end the conflict and restore shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

"Madam Deputy Speaker, we did not want this war… …and we rejected the calls from the Leader of the Opposition to jump two-footed into it. It’s a war that has driven up costs on British families and businesses: Inflation, which was due to fall earlier this year to the 2% target, has now risen, driven by higher oil and gas prices, Oil is up from around $70 a barrel to well over $100; and gas prices have nearly tripled since February."

## Healey jeered as he pays tribute to Rachel Reeves

*Political reporter Athena Stavrou reports:*

John Healey has paid tribute to his predecessor Rachel Reeves as he begins to deliver his first Budget.

He said: “At the outset, I want to pay tribute to my predecessor, my Right Honourable Friend the member for Leeds West and Pudsey.”

"The address was met by loud jeers from opposition benches, but he added: “Britain’s first ever female Chancellor, after 800 years.“She brought fiscal discipline to this country, And she took the decisions required to boost investment, cut NHS waiting lists and lift half a million children out of poverty."

## Healey declares a Budget for business and working people but warns of ‘global uncertainties’

*Whitehall editor Kate Devlin reports:*

John Healey did not shy away from the challenges affecting his Budget, two weeks after he warned of the “tough” circumstances behind it, including the war on Iran.

He began his statement by telling MPs it was a “Budget for the working people of Britain.

It’s a Budget for business, big and small. Delivered at a time when global uncertainties and pressures are swirling worldwide and hitting people here in Britain."

“A Budget built on fiscal discipline… …which matches immediate action today, with clear direction for the future”.
