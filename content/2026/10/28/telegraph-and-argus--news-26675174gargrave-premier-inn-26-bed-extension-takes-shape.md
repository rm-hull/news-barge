An extension to a Premier Inn hotel is taking shape - with 26 bedrooms to be added.

The detached, two-storey annexe to the Gargrave hotel - about five miles west of Skipton - was given planning permission by North Yorkshire Council in April last year.

Built in the car park of the hotel, next to the A65, the annexe when finished will provide an additional 26 bedrooms to the hotel's current 64. Landscaping will shield the building from the A65 and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal.

Premier Inn, part of Whitbread, said in its initial application to North Yorkshire Council that it had "identified a demand for additional bedrooms" at the Gargrave location.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638105.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Gargrave Premier Inn annexe is taking shape *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

It cited the draw of the 'historic town of Gargrave', popular hiking routes and close proximity to the Leeds and Liverpool Canal.

The 'substantial' car park, which serves both the hotel and Anchor Inn, recently taken over by Queensway Inns, will be rearranged with none of the 140 parking spaces lost.

The original hotel was given planning permission in 2007 and extended in 2009 and 2016, with the earlier extensions being to the hotel itself, and not an annexe as with the latest development.

Supporting documents with the application state: "Premier Inn has identified a considerable demand for additional budget hotel accommodation in this location.

"It is also acting to reconfigure the restaurant provision to ensure it best fits the needs of customers in select locations.

"This proposal for additional bedrooms and an adjusted restaurant provision would meet Premier Inn's operational requirements at this location and go some way in helping to address this bedroom demand.

"Thus, the proposal would facilitate optimal operation of the hotel bringing substantial economic and social benefits."

The council officer report, which recommended the approval of planning permission, said the overall design of the new annexe was very similar in scale, design and use of materials to the original hotel.

It said while the annexe will be more prominent than the main part of the hotel, which is largely obscured by the Anchor restaurant, it is at the edge of the village within a large site surrounded by other large buildings, including the Anchor Inn and Twin Locks garden centre.

It states: "The proposal is considered acceptable in principle as it would extend an existing business which in turn would benefit the rural economy. The rearranged host hotel building would allow for an increase in accessible rooms which would promote more people to the area."

The Craven Herald repeatedly asked Premier Inns when the new extension was due to open, but received no reply.
