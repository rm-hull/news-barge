The crash occurred between **Urda and Los Yébenes, in Toledo province**, when a train travelling from Madrid towards Seville** struck the rear of another that had stopped on the same track**.** Two carriages were knocked off the rails**, one of them torn apart, with twisted metal and debris scattered down a gravel embankment. Aerial footage shows the damaged trains surrounded by firefighters, police and rescue teams in a flat, rural landscape. Castilla-La Mancha government spokeswoman Esther Padilla** confirmed 10 deaths but warned that the toll was likely to rise**.

The collision involved a **RENFE train carrying 397 passengers** and an**IRYO service with 147 people** on board. The**cause remains unknown**. Emergency workers concentrated on reaching victims in the two worst-damaged carriages, while injured passengers were taken to nearby hospitals. A** military emergency unit was deployed**, and** rail services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia were suspended**.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez **cancelled his engagements to focus on the disaster**. It was** Spain's second major rail tragedy of 2026**, following a** January collision in southern Spain that killed 45 people**. That earlier accident had already raised concerns about safety on the country's extensive high-speed rail network.
