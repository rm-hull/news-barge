A federal judge has ruled that Republican Bill Essayli is not legally serving as the top US prosecutor in Los Angeles.

Essayli was disqualified from overseeing two criminal prosecutions. The order, however, will not immediately go into effect, and the Department of Justice (DoJ) has said it intends to appeal.

The ruling was part of a long-running legal battle challenging the Trump administration’s maneuvers to keep Essayli in office despite not being confirmed by the US Senate.

Essayli, a former Republican state lawmaker and ally of Donald Trump, became an interim US attorney in April 2025, but he was not formally nominated by the president, which allowed him to lead the office without going through the standard confirmation process.

After Essayli’s “interim” appointment ended last July, the Trump administration gave him an “acting” title, then kept him on as a “first assistant” after a judge ruled he was unlawfully serving as US attorney.

But in August, a federal appellate court ruled against a Nevada justice department leader in a similar case, and the judge overseeing Essayli’s case cited that higher court’s ruling on Tuesday, issuing another order saying he was still acting unlawfully.

Michael Seabright, the federal judge, said Essayli was illegally serving, even with a “special attorney” and “first assistant” designation by the Trump administration, and the judge said he could no longer participate in or supervise two ongoing prosecutions where the attorneys had challenged his position.

“We respectfully disagree with the court’s order,” Essayli said in a statement on Tuesday. “The President and the Attorney General have every right to select supervisors within the Justice Department.”

Faced with widespread voter discontent over the spike in oil prices since he attacked Iran, Donald Trump staged his rally in Corpus Christi, Texas, on Tuesday alongside a vast oil tanker, the Angerona, which the president said had just arrived from Venezuela bearing a personal gift for him: a million barrels of oil.

A senior state department official told the Associated Press that the US will “will issue visas to Flávio Bolsonaro and his family and allow them entry into the United States”.

In his remarks to supporters in Lubbock, Texas, Trump attacked James Talarico, the Democratic nominee for Senate, for having shown compassion for transgender children who demonstrated for their rights at the Texas state capitol in 2023.

Trump lied that “our gasoline prices are lower than when Barack Hussein Obama – our oil prices, gasoline, lower than they were with Obama”.

Flávio Bolsonaro, defeated right-wing Brazilian presidential candidate, expected to arrive in Miami

Flávio Bolsonaro, the right-wing Brazilian presidential candidate who lost the country’s election to left-wing incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, is expected to arrive in Miami on Wednesday after fleeing Brazil, Reuters reported.

According to the outlet, Bolsonaro and his family – after crossing by land into Argentina, and from there departing to Ecuador – took a flight bound for the US, where the Trump administration’s State Department plans to grant them visas.

Flávio Bolsonaro is the son of Jair Bolsonaro, the Trump-allied former leader of Brazil who was arrested and handed a 27-year sentence for attempting a coup to overturn the result of his own 2022 election defeat to Lula. After his election defeat by Lula over the weekend, and before fleeing to the US, he had been ordered to turn over his passports in connection with a criminal investigation.

Trump did not outright endorse the younger Bolsonaro this time around. But he has publicly sparred with Lula frequently in his second term, including imposing tariffs on Brazil in response to its criminal prosecution of Jair Bolsonaro. And Trump has boasted about his role as an apparent kingmaker in Latin America, where Brazil is one of the few holdouts still controlled by left-wing governments as right-wing leaders are ascendent through the region.

Throughout his reelection campaign, Lula accused the Trump administration of trying to interfere with the vote, and of having colonial ambitions in Brazil.

The US Federal Reserve is expected to leave interest rates unchanged on Wednesday during its last rate-setting meeting before the midterm elections, keeping rates at one of the highest pre-election levels seen in over 20 years.

Last month, Fed officials raised rates for the first time since 2023, citing rising inflation. Inflation has hovered for years above the Fed’s target rate of 2%, and for most of this year, the annualized inflation rate has remained above 3%.

The move infuriated Donald Trump, who said that “interest rates in the United States should be 1% or less”.

Though inflation rose higher in September, going from 3.4% in August to 3.7%, a pause would signal the central bank is taking a wait-and-see approach ahead of the midterms next week.

Interest rates determine the cost of interest for loans, including for credit cards, mortgage and some savings accounts. During times of high inflation, raising rates can slow price increases.

Multiple Fed leaders have suggested in recent weeks that the central bank has room for a pause, even if most Fed economists are predicting one more rate hike before the end of the year.

“The hikes do not need to come at consecutive meetings, but they should be in place in an acceptable period of time,” Fed governor Christopher Waller said earlier this month.
