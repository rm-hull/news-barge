A simple mistake when buying a Halloween costume could have devastating consequences, County Durham firefighters have warned.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service (CDDFRS) is urging parents to check their children's costumes ahead of Halloween, amid concerns that many costumes are less fire-resistant than everyday clothing.

Many fancy-dress costumes are highly flammable because they can be sold as toys, CDDFRS said.

The service stressed the importance of checking that costumes carry the CE or UKCA mark on their labels before letting children go trick-or-treating.

These marks indicate that the costumes have been tested and meet basic fire safety standards.

Kevin Bell, community safety manager at CDDFRS, said: "Don’t let your Halloween celebrations turn into a nightmare by failing to make one simple check when buying costumes for your children.

"Please take a second to check that any fancy dress outfit you buy has the UKCA or CE mark on the label which means that they have been fire safety tested.

"Remember, if your costume does catch fire, remember to stop, drop and roll."

He also advised parents to help prevent fires by not using real candles in pumpkins and decorations, and instead opting for battery-powered or LED candles.

Mr Bell added: "We know Halloween is supposed to be scary, but don’t let tick-or-treating end in tragedy by acting now to ensure your loved ones are safe from fire.

"Stay safe and have a Happy Halloween!"

The warning follows similar advice from Northumberland Fire and Rescue Service and Trading Standards, who reminded families across the region to take extra care with costumes, decorations and accessories.

Their advice included avoiding long, trailing fabrics or loose accessories and buying costumes from reputable retailers.

The Chartered Trading Standards Institute's (CTSI) 'Look after Mabel, Check the label' Halloween safety campaign encourages parents to verify the authenticity of CE markings, as they can be "faked by counterfeiters or those looking to cut corners."

CTSI tests of 11 children's costumes bought from third-party online sellers found that 10 failed to meet UK safety laws, with issues including missing warnings and potential strangulation risks.

The CTSI said that labelling should include a manufacturer’s name or registered trademark, and that safety instructions should be present.

They added that the middle line of the 'E' in the genuine CE mark should be shorter than the other parts.

All advice agrees that no garment is fireproof, and that parents should stay aware of fire safety standards to ensure trick-or-treaters can stay safe this weekend.
