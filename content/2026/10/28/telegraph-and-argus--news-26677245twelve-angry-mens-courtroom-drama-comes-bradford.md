A very different kind of engrossing courtroom drama is gripping Bradford city centre this week.

I think that Twelve Angry Men, which was staged on opening night at The Alhambra on Tuesday, is a play very much about human nature.

Set in 1950s New York, the drama exposes deep-rooted prejudice as 12 jurors from different backgrounds decide whether a young defendant stabbed his father to death.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641070.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A scene in Twelve Angry Men, which is being staged at The Alhambra

I have wanted to see this West End play for years because it was the first show I saw and reviewed in an American theatre nearly 30 years ago.

I credit Twelve Angry Men with sparking my interest in theatre.

The intriguing story was made into a 1957 three-time Academy Award nominated film, starring Henry Fonda.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641083.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Gray O'Brien, who plays Juror 8 in Twelve Angry Men at The Alhambra

Reginald Rose’s gripping courtroom thriller features strong performances in a production by Bill Kenwright Ltd at The Alhambra.

Gray O’Brien plays Juror 8, an articulate architect who doubts the young man’s guilt from the start.

With 11 of the 12 jurors convinced of the youngster’s guilt, the seemingly open-and-shut case becomes anything but simple.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641113.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kenneth Jay, left, who plays Juror 11, and Ben Nealon, who plays Juror 12 in Twelve Angry Men

O’Brien makes a forceful case for reasonable doubt, warning the other jurors that a guilty verdict will send the young man to the electric chair.

Bill Ward’s Juror 3 is determined to find the accused guilty, whatever the evidence.

Juror 3 is an amazing study in character development, and Ward brilliantly brings out the anger and deep-rooted personal issues from the character’s life.

Ricky Norwood is refreshing in the role of Juror 7, a light-hearted character who is keen to come a quick verdict so he can watch New York Yankees baseball game that night.

Tristan Gemmill is compelling as Juror 10, a flat-capped, opinionated garage owner who dominates several intense scenes.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641093.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tristan Gemmill played Juror 10 in Twelve Angry Men at The Alhambra

I also really liked Mark Heehan as Juror 4, a well-dressed, thoughtful stockbroker.

The actors’ performances on the night were met with appreciative applause at both the interval and again at the end.

I’d highly recommend The Alambra’s staging of Twelve Angry Men, which I think is a fantastic tribute to the man who managed the theatre where I first saw it in the 1990s.

Norman Filbert was president of the Chanticleer Theatre in Council Bluffs, Iowa.

He was an influential figure in the local theatre community, spending decades as a director, actor and administrator with the Chanticleer Players.

Twelve Angry Men runs at the Alhambra Theatre until Saturday, October 31.
