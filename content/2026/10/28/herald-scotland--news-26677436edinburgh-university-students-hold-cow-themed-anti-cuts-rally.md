**Staff and students are to stage a cow-themed rally on Friday in protest over programme closures at Edinburgh University.**

Members of the University and College Union (UCU) at the institution have been on strike for five weeks, following the news that 90 programmes are set to be ‘taught out’ from 2027.

Concerns have also been raised over a £140m savings goal, which union officials say could cost 1,800 people their jobs.

"Management wants to treat us like cash cows. Let’s give them what they want,” an Instagram post from Edinburgh’s Student Staff Solidarity Assembly states.

“Don your finest cow couture. Grab your cow bells. And let’s get loud.

“They don’t negotiate. They don’t listen. But we are here till the cows come home.

“Students and staff are the university, and we have three demands. Stop the cuts; disclose, divest, democratise and resign, resign resign.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21529900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UCU members and student supporters from universities across Scotland rallied outside the Scottish Parliament earlier this month. *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

A Instagram post from Edinburgh UCU adds: "Rally this Friday, 12.30 at Old College, to finish off five weeks of strike with a bang, for staff and students to come together and celebrate the strength and solidarity we have built, for people and politicians around Edinburgh to join the fight for higher education.

“Senior management has stonewalled workers and students - come to let them know: we aren’t going anywhere. This is our university.”

In mid-September, The Herald revealed that 90 programmes in the university’s College of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences would close to new enrolments from next year, including 48 in the School of Literatures, Languages and Cultures (LLC).

At the time, a spokesperson said that the university regularly reviews its academic portfolio to make sure it aligns with “student demand, a constantly evolving job market and other external needs”.

Last week, The Herald published an email sent to a student by university principal Sir Peter Mathieson, outlining his personal thoughts on the closures.

Sir Peter, who recently announced plans to leave the university in 2028, told the student: “I am puzzled to know what part of the concept of affordability you don't understand.

“Maybe you live in a world where the cost doesn't matter or where there is no need to be concerned about the future, about sustainability or resilience, or about whether there is enough income to justify and support expenditure.

“The University of Edinburgh does not live in such a world.

“[Provost] Kim Graham and I, along with huge numbers of subject experts and committed colleagues, are leading the tough decisions that responsible leaders need to take."
