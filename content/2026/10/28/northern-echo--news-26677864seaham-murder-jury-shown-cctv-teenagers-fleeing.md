Jurors have watched CCTV footage of the actions of two teenagers in the minutes leading up to the alleged murder of Daniel Ambler.

The 23-year-old suffered horrific injuries when Ashton Anderson drove his Nissan X-Trail into him during the feud.

Mr Ambler was pronounced dead after the crash at the Esso Asda Express service station in Seaham, County Durham, on January 15.

CCTV footage showed Barry Anderson, his sons Ashton and Logan, Aja Henry and Tyler Leadbetter hunting down and attacking Mr Ambler and his friend Reece Wilkin.

The Teesside Crown Court jury watched footage of Leadbetter and Henry joining the attack on Mr Wilkin outside The Mallard pub.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20437048.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Crime scene investigators at the scene

Prosecutor Matthew Donkin KC showed the jury footage of the pair jumping from the Nissan X-Trail after Ashton Anderson drove it into a wall while his father Barry fought Mr Wilkin.

The pair joined the Andersons in the attack before climbing back into the car as Ashton Anderson drove towards the Esso garage.

Jurors watched both teenagers jump from the car and flee seconds after Ashton Anderson drove at Mr Ambler, smashing him against shop-front shelving.

The jury heard the families’ bitter feud began when Barry Anderson was stabbed last August.

Mr Ambler’s brother David and his friend Jed Wilson fought Barry Anderson, 52, who was stabbed during the struggle.

Barry Anderson, 52, of Canon Cockin Street, Sunderland; Ashton Anderson, 20, of Ryton Crescent, Seaham; and Barry Logan Anderson, 19, of Nursery View, Shotton Bank, face charges of murder and violent disorder.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20439774.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Esso New Seaham Service Station, in Stockton Road, Seaham, the scene of Daniel Ambler's death

Ajay Henry, of Melrose Crescent, Seaham, and Tyler Leadbetter, of Elgin Avenue, Seaham, were both 17 when charged with murdering Daniel Ambler.

Ashton Anderson has pleaded guilty to violent disorder. The jury is expected to decide whether the other four defendants are guilty of the same charge.

The prosecution claims the attack was coordinated and that the four defendants aided or encouraged Ashton Anderson to use unlawful violence against Mr Ambler.

Witness Alexis Prest told the jury that nobody in the Nissan X-Trail encouraged Ashton Anderson to drive onto the forecourt or celebrated after Mr Ambler was struck.

The trial is expected to last up to five weeks at Teesside Crown Court.
