York Central MP Rachael Maskell says today's budget is "fresh hope for the future and real life changes that all add up to supporting working families."

The Labour MP welcomed a raft of measures from Chancellor John Healey, which he announced in his first budget from 12.30am this afternoon.

Rachael Maskell MP said: "Taxing banks' bumper profits to pay for energy bills relief for families is a common sense measure.

"I have long advocated for more progressive taxes on those who must pay what is due for our society to be more equal.

"Plans such as the household energy payment and removal of the gas standing charge, will put pounds back into people’s pockets to help with the cost-of-living today, whilst building the right foundations for tomorrow with a new First Time Buyer ISA and the removal of business rates from many of our local high street businesses."

The MP continued: "The multiple different forms of relief will provide families and communities a bridge to better financial security, while the Chancellor prepares for the Comprehensive Spending Review next year, like a rise of 3.9% in the National Minimum and National Living Wages and cutting the personal allowance for those on the basic state pension, taking many pensioners out of tax altogether."

*"Having campaigned for small business rates relief for many years, I really welcome the additional support for our small and independent businesses in York by removing business rates from around half of the cafes and shops in our city."*

Ms Maskell added: "This is a Budget with fresh hope for the future and real life changes that all add up to supporting working families.”

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, was similarly supportive of his Chancellor.

He said: "The Budget gives families and businesses in York and North Yorkshire breathing space today and hope for the future."

The Labour mayor continued: "Direct support to help people with energy bills and hospitality businesses, which are so important to our regional economy, will ease the pressure that many people and businesses are feeling right now.

"Investment in local apprenticeship services and greater local decision-making will mean that more of the money raised here is spent here.

"That means fewer decisions about key spending will be made in London, with more decisions made right here in York and North Yorkshire.”
