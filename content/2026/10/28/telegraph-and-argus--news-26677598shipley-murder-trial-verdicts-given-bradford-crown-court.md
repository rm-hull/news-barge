A man and a woman who beat their friend to death following an all-day drinking session will be sentenced on Thursday.

Zbigniew Chlebek, 67, and Beata Walczak, 53, were convicted of the murder of Jaroslav “Yanic” Daniszewski following a 12-day trial at Bradford Crown Court.

The battered body of Mr Daniszewski, 43, who was Chlebek’s friend and Walczak’s partner of 11 months, was found in Chlebek’s blood-spattered flat on Stead Street in Shipley on April 22 last year.

The jury heard how he had been beaten unconscious with an iron in “a violent and vicious attack” before being attacked again to “finish him off”.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641768.jpg?type=mds-article-620)WALCZAK *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Mr Daniszewski suffered 24 separate injuries to his head and face consistent with blunt force trauma.

In opening the trial, barrister Jason Pitter KC described how Mr Daniszewski may have been fatally injured on the evening of April 20 when he, Walczak, Chlebek, and another man, Bolestaw Roj, gathered at Chlebek’s flat to socialise and drink alcohol together as part of an Easter Sunday celebration within the Polish community.

Mr Roj, who has since died, made more than a dozen attempts to call an ambulance during the course of the evening but failed due to language barriers.

He then left the flat, leaving the other three together.

Mr Pitter said: “It is within that backdrop that Daniszewski was murdered.”

He added: “Whilst we cannot say precisely what happened or indeed why, we say that the proper conclusion is that these two defendants are responsible for the killing, fatally injuring him, or at the very least assisting or encouraging the other to do so.”

Mr Pitter suggested that “there were tensions brewing” amongst the group at the flat and that something triggered a situation that would leave Mr Daniszewski dead “following a violent and vicious attack from his close associates.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641778.jpg?type=mds-article-620)CHLEBEK *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

All the participants were said to be “heavily in drink” with Mr Daniszewski four times over the legal driving limit.

He would have been rendered unconscious – and therefore defenceless – through a combination of being struck to the head and the amount he had drunk.

From around 9.30pm Roj’s phone called the emergency services 13 times to ask for assistance, including an ambulance to Stead Street, but was unsuccessful.

In his final call a female voice, said to Walczak’s can be heard saying: “Take him as quickly as possible, because things are really bad.”

Roj left after that final call. Walczak, Chlebek, and Mr Daniszewski remained at the flat. No further 999 calls were made.

The police and ambulance service were not contacted again until April 22, more than a day later.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/19361107.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Newsquest)*

Both Walczak and Chlebek denied knowing what had happened to Mr Daniszewski and woke up to find him dead.

Police who arrived found blood spattered and smeared around the living room and in the bedroom area.

There was also a broken iron, with blood on it.

Mr Daniszewski’s blood was present on Chlebek and Walczak’s clothing which, said Mr Pitter, was consistent with one of them delivering blows whilst the other was close by “or even both of them delivering blows”.

The jury heard that Walczak walked through the dead man’s blood whilst wearing socks and, after attempts were made to clean his face, Mr Daniszewski’s body was carried to where he was later found.

Mr Pitter added: “He must have survived for at least 30 minutes or so … before there was further injury to his brain, which could have been what ultimately caused his heart to stop beating.

“In other words, it could have finished him off.”

During interviews with police Walczak repeatedly denied being responsible for the death of Mr Daniszewski or knowing who might have killed him.

She told police that she woke from a stupor on the morning of April 21, Easter Monday, to find Mr Daniszewski lying in a doorway between the living room and the bedroom.

He was lying on his back. His mouth was open. He was not breathing. There was blood around his head.

In one interview with police, Walczak said: “He was not breathing, and his cheeks were cold.

“There was [no pulse] whatsoever.”

In one of four interviews with police, Chlebek said he could not remember anything between being in the living room of his flat with Walczak and Mr Daniszewski and later waking up in his bed.

Asked about waking up and finding Mr Daniszewski’s body, which was lying in the doorway between the bedroom and the living room, Chlebek said: “Yanic was already cold.”

He said he then had a drink of cider and lit a cigarette.

Asked if Mr Daniszewski’s body was still on the floor, he said, “Yes.”

Chlebek denied that there had ever been violence between him and Mr Daniszewski, or with Walczak.

He said: “What can I say? We have lived in peace. We would have a laugh sometimes together.”

Asked directly in his police interview how Mr Daniszewski died, Chlebek replied: “I don’t know.”
