A car crash victim may never fully recover from the injuries she suffered when a driver cut across her path without warning, York magistrates heard.

Rybicki Cezary, 64, didn’t signal before turning right at 12.30pm as the woman was driving towards him in the opposite direction, said Antony Farrell, prosecuting.

“That took her by surprise,” said the prosecutor.

“She tried to take evasive measures but unfortunately both front ends of the cars made contact and she was spun around.”

The victim’s car ended up in a roadside ditch and she suffered a broken arm, broken coccyx at the bottom of her spine and other injuries.

Cezary stopped his car, got out and went to the victim’s car with his hands up saying “sorry, sun.”

The woman had been driving southwards along Fenton Lane between Church Fenton and Sherburn-in-Elmet with the sun in her eyes and Cezary had been driving northwards, said Mr Farrell. She had her sun visor down.

“It was a cold day, visibility was clear except for the glare from the sun,” said Mr Farrell.

The road surface was wet and bright with sun reflecting off it.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637674.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fenton Lane with the entrance to British Gypsum on the right that Rybicki Cezary tried to turn into. *(Image: Google Street View)*

In a personal statement made 21 months after the crash the woman said: “I continue to struggle financially, physically, emotionally and psychologically.”

She still suffers pain from her injuries and has difficulty getting to sleep or sitting comfortably.

She said: “Physiotherapists advised me that I may have to live with these effects for the rest of my life.”

Cezary, of Wistow Road, Selby, denied a charge of causing serious injury by careless driving but was convicted at trial.

He represented himself at court and when asked for mitigation said: “I have nothing to say.”

Magistrates gave him a 12-month community order with probation supervision concentrating on improving his thinking skills and victim awareness and must do 250 hours’ unpaid work.

He was banned from driving for 12 months and ordered to take an extended driving test before driving unsupervised again.

He must pay £650 prosecution costs and a £114 statutory surcharge.

Mr Farrell said the woman was driving southwards on Fenton Lane towards Sherburn-in-Elmet on January 10, 2025.

The woman drove the road every day and was driving at 45mph in the 60mph zone because of the road conditions.

British Gypsum was on her left.

Cezary was driving towards Church Fenton on the same road and tried to turn right into the British Gypsum site.
