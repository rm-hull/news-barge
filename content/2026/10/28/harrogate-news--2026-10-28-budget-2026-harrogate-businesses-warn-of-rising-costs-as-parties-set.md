Rising business costs, pressure on household finances, affordable housing and the future funding of local services are among the issues being raised by political representatives and business leaders ahead of the Chancellor’s Budget.

With independent businesses facing higher operating costs and households continuing to feel the effects of the cost of living, The Harrogate Informer asked representatives from across the political spectrum what they want the Budget to deliver for Harrogate, Knaresborough and the wider North Yorkshire area.

Responses from the Green Party and Reform UK highlight different approaches to taxation, public spending and economic growth. Meanwhile, hospitality leaders Simon Cotton of Fat Badger Group and Martin Greenhow of MOJO have both raised concerns about the combined effect of rising costs on businesses and their ability to invest.

## Hospitality businesses squeezed by rising costs

Simon Cotton, managing director of Fat Badger Group, said the hospitality sector continued to face a challenging combination of higher operating costs, pressure on margins and customers looking for value for money.

He identified staffing as a significant expense, particularly following increases in the National Living Wage and employer National Insurance contributions. Business rates, energy bills, food and drink costs and other supplier expenses were also putting pressure on margins.

> **Simon Cotton, managing director of Fat Badger Group, said:**
>
> Individually, each increase can be difficult enough to absorb; collectively, they make it harder for businesses to invest, recruit and grow.

Mr Cotton said Fat Badger Group wanted to open new sites, invest in its existing properties and create more jobs, but rising costs were making those ambitions increasingly difficult to achieve.

He also pointed to changes in consumer behaviour, with people becoming more conscious of the cost of living and making decisions about how they spend their money.

His main request to the Chancellor is a reduction in VAT for the hospitality sector, bringing the UK closer to the lower rates applied in many other European countries.

> **Mr Cotton said:**
>
> A reduction in VAT would give businesses more breathing space to reinvest in their sites, improve the customer experience, take on more staff and open new locations.

He said supporting hospitality was not simply about helping individual businesses, but also about protecting local jobs, suppliers, high streets and Harrogate’s appeal as a place to live and visit.

## MOJO boss calls for lower taxes and more disposable income

Martin Greenhow, managing director of MOJO, also highlighted the pressure on hospitality businesses, arguing that higher operating costs were coinciding with reduced disposable income among customers.

He pointed to employer National Insurance contributions, National Living Wage increases, capital gains taxation and energy costs, alongside the effect of frozen income tax thresholds on household finances.

Mr Greenhow said there was no single factor responsible for the difficulties facing the sector, but argued that government policy was contributing to both sides of the problem: increasing the costs businesses face while limiting the money customers have available to spend.

> **Martin Greenhow, managing director of MOJO, said:**
>
> We need a smaller state that spends less and requires less tax to support it meaning people would have the choice of how to spend their money, and businesses would have the resource to allocate to investment and the growth we hear so many platitudes about.

Mr Greenhow said a VAT reduction or lower National Insurance contributions could help, but expressed concern that any relief might be offset by additional costs elsewhere.

> **Mr Greenhow said:**
>
> We need affluence in our guests not effluent in government trying to pick winners and losers within the economy.

His preferred approach is a smaller state, lower taxation and less government intervention, which he believes would leave businesses better placed to invest and consumers with more money to spend.

He also referred to an October business confidence report from NIQ, saying he was concerned that an apparent improvement in hospitality confidence could quickly reverse depending on the Budget measures announced.

## Green Party calls for fairer local government funding

Arnold Warneken, chair of the Green Party, identified a fairer, long-term funding settlement for local government as his party’s single most important Budget priority for the area.

He argued that North Yorkshire Council needed sustainable funding that reflected the costs of delivering services across a large rural county, helping to protect social care, education, healthcare and support for vulnerable residents without continually increasing council tax or cutting services.

On the cost of living, Mr Warneken said people should be able to afford their homes, essential bills and opportunities for family life, leisure and rest. He called for fair wages alongside action on housing, energy and transport costs, including better support for rural public transport.

On housing, the Green Party wants genuinely affordable, energy-efficient homes, appropriate infrastructure and protection against excessive rent increases. Mr Warnekan also supported exploring government-backed low- or zero-deposit mortgages for first-time buyers with proven rental payment histories, subject to affordability safeguards.

He said development should meet community needs while protecting valuable green spaces.

For local businesses, the party wants fairer business rates, effective taxation of large multinational corporations and support for smaller firms facing higher energy and employment costs.

Mr Warneken also stressed the need for transparency and accountability in public spending, arguing that major infrastructure projects should properly consider their impact on local businesses.

> **Mr Warneken said:**
>
> A successful Budget would improve living standards, support responsible businesses, deliver affordable housing, invest in education and infrastructure, and protect our environment.

He said prosperity, fairness and environmental responsibility needed to work together, and that government should be judged not simply by what it spends, but by the lives it improves.

## Tom Gordon MP sets out Budget priorities

Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has called for action on household energy bills, support for local businesses and changes to student loans ahead of the Budget.

He said the cost of living was his absolute priority, pointing to households struggling with heating costs and families putting off meals out during the school half-term holiday.

The Liberal Democrats are calling for an essential energy guarantee, which would provide households with an essential amount of energy equivalent to half their typical consumption, giving people more breathing space with their bills.

Mr Gordon also called for a VAT cut and lower employer National Insurance contributions to support local businesses, particularly those in the hospitality sector.

On student loans, he said the Budget was an opportunity to help graduates who were struggling to keep up with interest accumulating on their loans.

> **Tom Gordon MP said:** There are 2 million graduates who can’t even keep apace of the interest on their loans, the system is a racket and not the one we were sold. He can start by unfreezing the loan thresholds and reviewing the interest rates.

He also highlighted the importance of decisions affecting household energy costs, saying the Chancellor’s announcements would have a direct impact on people’s ability to afford to heat their homes.

> **Tom Gordon MP said:** A successful budget will make residents and local business feel like they’re thriving, not just surviving.

His comments put the focus on the practical effects of the Budget for households and businesses in Harrogate and Knaresborough, particularly the cost of energy, the pressures facing hospitality businesses and the financial burden on graduates.

## Reform UK calls for tax cuts and questions transport spending

Reform UK’s Harrogate and Knaresborough branch has called for lower taxes on working households, reduced business costs and tighter control of public spending.

Branch chairman Jonathan Swales said the Chancellor should tackle high energy costs and reduce the tax burden on working people. Reform UK proposes abolishing income tax on overtime for eligible workers earning under £75,000, arguing that additional work should be rewarded.

The branch also criticised the impact of Net Zero policies on energy costs and called for their economic consequences to be reassessed.

On housing, Reform UK said Harrogate needed genuinely affordable homes for younger people and local workers, with roads, schools and healthcare planned alongside development. It argued that unnecessarily expensive infrastructure could increase development costs and potentially house prices.

For businesses, the branch wants the VAT registration threshold increased to £150,000, alongside lower business rates, reduced regulation and action on energy and employment costs.

Its chosen local Budget priority is targeted business rates relief for independent high-street businesses, which it said could help protect jobs, encourage investment and revitalise town centres.

## Questions over the cost of local schemes

Reform UK also questioned whether cheaper alternatives had been properly evaluated for several local transport projects.

The branch cited the £14.6 million Harrogate Station Gateway scheme, further 20mph proposals involving signage and traffic calming, and the £3.8 million Beckwithshaw roundabout scheme.

> **Jonathan Swales, chairman of Reform UK’s Harrogate and Knaresborough branch, said:**
>
> Every pound wasted on questionable schemes is a pound unavailable for roads, social care and essential services. Government must spend taxpayers’ money with the same care taxpayers exercise when spending their own.

Reform UK called for sustainable funding for essential local services but said this should be accompanied by greater accountability for how public money is spent.

Nationally, the branch argued that rising welfare costs and government debt interest were consuming resources needed for essential services. It said its alternative Budget contained 64 measures intended to reduce living costs, remove burdens on businesses, lower energy prices and stimulate economic growth.

## Different approaches, common concerns

The responses reveal differences in how the political parties believe the Chancellor should address the economic pressures facing the area.

The Green Party emphasises a fairer funding settlement for local government, better living standards, affordable housing and environmental responsibility. Reform UK prioritises lower taxation, reduced regulation and tighter control of government spending.

The two hospitality leaders approach the issue from the perspective of businesses facing higher costs and customers with less money to spend. Both highlight the pressure on margins and the difficulty of balancing affordability for customers with the need to maintain viable businesses.

Mr Cotton wants a reduction in hospitality VAT to encourage investment and expansion. Mr Greenhow favours a broader reduction in the size and cost of government, arguing that lower taxes would benefit both businesses and consumers.

The Budget will determine whether the Chancellor adopts measures that address any of these priorities. For Harrogate businesses, the immediate questions concern the cost of employing staff, energy and other operating expenses, while households and local authorities will be watching for decisions affecting disposable income, housing and public services.
