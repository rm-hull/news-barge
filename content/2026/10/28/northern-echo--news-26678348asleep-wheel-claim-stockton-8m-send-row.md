A ‘call in’ led by Conservatives on Stockton Council which aimed to re-examine a planned £8m investment in SEND services has been rejected.

The group said it did not oppose investment for children with special education needs and/or disabilities, but sought to challenge whether a decision “involving millions of pounds, a joint venture and wide-ranging delegated powers had been taken with the accuracy, evidence and openness residents should expect”.

At an executive scrutiny committee meeting, Labour – which has minority control of the council – and independent members voted to reject the call-in which, if successful, would have sent the matter back to the council’s cabinet for further consideration.

The £8m capital spending programme will go towards the creation of a new 50 place school for students with social, emotional and mental health needs, expansion of an early support nursery in Thornaby and creating additional places at Ash Trees Academy, a specialist primary school in Billingham.

It is hoped these projects will eventually lead to savings through reducing reliance on costly out of area SEND provision.

Tory councillor Steve Dodds said there was concern that some money had already been spent, while a decision record showed no alternative options had been put forward and considered by the cabinet.

He also questioned what he said was “flawed” paperwork which formed part of the process and had elements missing.

Cllr Dodds said: “This has all the hallmarks of an arrangement agreed in private and rubber-stamped in public.

“Labour are asleep at the wheel, blindly following what is put in front of them instead of reading the papers, challenging the details and protecting the public purse.

“If councillors are being asked to approve multi-million-pound decisions, the very least residents should expect is accurate documentation, a clear assessment of the alternatives and a transparent account of what has already been spent.

“That basic standard was not met.”

Stockton Council deputy leader, Councillor Paul Rowling said he and Councillor Clare Besford, the cabinet member for children and young people at the local authority, had attended the executive scrutiny committee meeting and “answered all questions asked by members of the committee”.

![Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21514357.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Paul Rowling from Stockton Council. Picture: LDR.

He said: “We demonstrated that we made a fully informed decision in accordance with the council’s constitution.

“This was reflected in the vote of the committee to reject the request to send it back to cabinet.”

Cllr Rowling acknowledged there had been a “mistake” in a cabinet report, but this had made “no difference”.

He said: “Cabinet was clear about what decisions we were making, and what the result of the decisions would be.

“The Conservatives who appear to have little knowledge of how council decisions are made, seem only interested in playing politics, in this case trying to disrupt much needed expansion in SEND capacity.

“Meanwhile, we have a laser focus on delivering for the residents and businesses of Stockton.”

A spokesman for the council said officers had existing budgets with delegated arrangements – separate from any wider capital investment – and would also assess proposals before they were presented to the cabinet.

The spokesman also clarified that, following the meeting, the executive scrutiny committee had agreed to include “updated information” in the meeting minutes.
