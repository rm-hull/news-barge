Yorkshire Water will begin ground investigations in Knaresborough on 2 November as part of plans to reduce discharges from storm overflows into the River Nidd.

Five boreholes will be drilled on The Parsonage to investigate ground conditions and monitor groundwater levels. Each borehole will be approximately 150mm in diameter and 5m deep.

The work will help Yorkshire Water determine the most effective and safe way to reduce discharges from the Waterside 49 combined sewer overflow (CSO) into the River Nidd.

The investigations are expected to take around one week. Yorkshire Water says the boreholes will then be backfilled and the area reinstated, with the information gathered used to develop a suitable solution.

## Seven projects planned for Knaresborough

Yorkshire Water is investigating and planning seven potential storm overflow discharge-reduction projects in Knaresborough, which it expects to complete by 2030.

The company says the ground investigations form part of its £1.5 billion programme to reduce discharges from combined storm overflows across Yorkshire between 2025 and 2030. Further ground investigations are planned in Knaresborough as work progresses on the other six projects, with residents to be informed in advance.

A combined sewer overflow carries both wastewater and rainwater. During heavy rainfall, an overflow can release diluted wastewater into a watercourse when the sewer network reaches capacity, helping prevent sewage backing up into homes, gardens, businesses and streets.

## Target of fewer than 10 discharges a year

Yorkshire Water has confirmed that the aim of the scheme is to reduce discharges to fewer than 10 per year, in line with government targets.

However, the projects remain at the planning and investigation stage, and the company has not yet confirmed a construction timetable.

> **A Yorkshire Water spokesperson said:** The aim of the scheme is to take it down to under 10 discharges per year, in line with government targets. As it’s still in planning and investigation, we don’t have a timeframe for construction yet, but as soon as we do, we’ll be letting everyone know.

## Yorkshire Water investment

Athif Khan, project manager at Yorkshire Water, said the company had recorded a 24.5% reduction in storm overflow discharges last year, which he attributed to investment in upgrading its network.

> **Athif Khan, project manager at Yorkshire Water, said:** There’s much more to do, and these boreholes are the next step in planning one of seven projects that we’re expecting to complete in Knaresborough.

He added that the company would seek to minimise disruption and thanked residents for their patience.

Yorkshire Water said ecology and arboricultural surveys had been carried out before the borehole work. The investigations are being undertaken by contract partners Ward and Burke.

The £1.5 billion storm overflow programme follows a further £180 million of investment in the preceding couple of years and forms part of Yorkshire Water’s wider £8.3 billion investment programme for 2025 to 2030.
