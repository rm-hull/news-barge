A film about a world champion bare-knuckle boxer’s battles inside and outside the ring will have its first screening outside London in the North East.

Angel of the North follows Jimmy “Gypsy Boy” McCrory, who became a global bare-knuckle boxing star while battling addiction.

The film will premiere in the region at Anarchy Brewery in Heaton on Sunday, December 13.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21635897.jpg?type=mds-article-620)John Duddy as James McCRory. Still from Angel of the North *(Image: Daniel Patrick Vaughan)*

Jimmy McCrory, from Seaton Burn, said: “Although I would still fight if the purse was big enough, it’s been amazing seeing my life on the big screen.”

He began boxing aged 10 and became a world champion as the sport grew an international following.

McCrory unofficially retired about a decade ago.

Directed by Cy Biggs and Sean Cronin, the film has already screened at London’s Raindance Film Festival, where it was praised for its “raw, human storytelling”.

The screening will take place in Anarchy Brewery’s Taproom, which supports new talent.

Director Cy Biggs said: “Angel of the North came about by accident when co-director Cy Biggs was surfing the internet one evening and discovered the world of bare-knuckle boxing.”

“Obviously it’s got a huge international following now, but when we started putting the film together it was less well known to the general public and Jimmy McCrory was, basically, one of the pioneers of the then early evolving sport.”

“He had a hard life growing up but he knew from a very early age that he could fight and he could win and every spare moment went into training to become the very best fighter he could be.”

“He’s also had to tackle possibly his most dangerous opponent yet: addiction.”

“But he’d done it and he’s still standing.”

“His is a tremendous tale – and actually a very moving one – and I hope North East audiences will enjoy learning about this warrior in their midst.”
