November in Germany can feel caught between seasons. Autumn colour is fading, darkness arrives alarmingly early and the country’s Christmas markets are not quite in full swing.

Look more closely, though, and hidden gems appear across the country. Science escapes the laboratory in Berlin, cinema from across northern Europe comes to Lübeck, horses fill the streets of a Bavarian spa town and fairground lights illuminate Hamburg.

**Berlin Science Week: November 1st – 10th**

If the prospect of spending November listening to a lecture sounds rather austere, Berlin Science Week takes a broader view of what science can look like.

This city-wide programme mixes talks and workshops with exhibitions, walks, performances and events in bars. Topics range from artificial intelligence and medicine to the hidden electromagnetic landscape of Berlin.

The 2026 theme, “In Touch”, examines how research affects everyday life and the people behind it. The main festival centre at the Haus der Kulturen der Welt runs from November 6th to 8th, with more than 100 events under one roof and a free three-day pass available.

Hundreds of events are free and the programme includes both German- and English-language options; individual listings state the language and whether registration is required.

For five days, Lübeck looks north across the Baltic as the Nordic Film Days bring new features, documentaries, shorts and series to the Hanseatic city.

Founded in 1956, the festival concentrates on work from northern and north-eastern Europe. The 2026 edition opens with *Ulya*, a drama about Latvian basketball player Uļjana Semjonova, and includes a retrospective marking the 125th anniversary of Thomas Mann’s Lübeck-based masterpiece* Buddenbrooks*.

The timetable includes both daytime late evening screenings. Many films are shown in their original languages with English subtitles, although viewers should check each listing rather than assume every screening is English-friendly.

Standard evening tickets cost €15, while screenings beginning before 6pm or from 9:30pm cost €11.

Reduced tickets are available, as are cheaper tickets for the children’s and youth programme. Tickets are sold online and, once the festival begins, at the CineStar Stadthalle box office.

**ART COLOGNE: November 5th – 8th**

ART COLOGNE is the sort of event where a visitor can turn one corner and find a monumental installation, then turn the next and encounter a work small enough to hang above a sofa.

Founded in 1967, the fair brings modern, post-war and contemporary art to Koelnmesse. Its various sections combine established galleries with younger spaces and emerging artists, while the programme adds talks, panels, special exhibitions and guided tours.

November 5th is given over to an invitation-only preview and a public vernissage from 4pm to 8pm. General visiting hours are from 11am to 7pm on November 6th and 7th, and from 11am to 6pm on November 8th.

The vernissage costs €60, while a standard day ticket from November 6th to 8th is €34, or €29 reduced. Public tours in English are offered at 1pm on all three general-admission days and cost €18 in addition to the entrance ticket.

**International Filmfestival Mannheim-Heidelberg: November 5th – 15th**

At the International Filmfestival Mannheim-Heidelberg, the appeal lies less in spotting familiar names than in encountering directors before everyone else knows who they are.

Germany’s second-oldest film festival celebrates its 75th edition in 2026. Its central “On the Rise” competition focuses on first and second feature films, while the wider programme includes a retrospective, family films, talks and screenings with visiting filmmakers.

The festival opens at Cineplex Mannheim with the German premiere of Lili Horvát’s English-language drama *My Notes on Mars*.

Screenings take place in both Mannheim and Heidelberg, so it's worth checking the city as well as the time when choosing a film. The festival’s official website carries the full timetable and ticket shop, as well as information on languages and subtitles.

**Tölzer Leonhardifahrt: November 6th**

Hooves, church bells and decorated wooden wagons replace the usual traffic in Bad Tölz when one of Bavaria’s best-known horse pilgrimages makes its way through town.

The Leonhardifahrt honours Saint Leonard, traditionally regarded as the patron saint of livestock. Up to 80 elaborately decorated four-horse wagons gather in the Bäderviertel before the procession crosses the Isar, passes through the Marktstraße and climbs to the Kalvarienberg for the blessing of horses and pilgrims.

![Women dressed in traditional costume sit in a horse drawn wagon on Leonhardi day in Bad Tölz](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/AFP__20251106__838V2K8__v2__HighRes.jpg)

The procession begins at 9am on Friday, November 6th, and ends with a final blessing at the Mühlfeldkirche at around 1pm. Watching from the route doesn’t require a ticket, although it’s generally a good idea to arrive early to get a good spot.

This is a religious and community tradition rather than a staged historical show, and was added to Bavaria’s and Germany’s registers of intangible cultural heritage in 2016.

Visitors should also note that dogs are prohibited along the procession route and on the Leonhardiwiese.

While Hamburg’s weather generally provides plenty of drama by November, the Winter DOM throws a healthy measure of rollercoasters and illuminated rides into the mix.

Held on the Heiligengeistfeld in St Pauli, the DOM is northern Germany’s largest funfair. The winter edition combines thrill rides with family attractions and fairground food.

The fair opens from 3pm to 11pm Monday to Thursday, from 3pm until midnight on Fridays and Saturdays, and from either 2pm or 3pm to 11pm on Sundays.

The atmosphere is generally family-friendly earlier in the day and then livelier after dark, when the fairground lights are at their best. Dogs other than guide and service dogs are not permitted.

**Berlin Freedom Week: November 7th – 14th**

Berlin Freedom Week uses the anniversary of the fall of the Wall to ask what freedom and democracy mean now.

The city-wide programme brings together eyewitness testimony, exhibitions, films, guided tours, discussions and events dealing with political repression, human rights and democratic resilience. The Berlin Freedom Conference on November 10th gathers speakers from politics, business, culture, the media and civil society.

Other events include the Women Freedom and Security Summit at the Bundestag on November 9th and “Stories of Freedom”, a programme of international documentaries followed by discussions at the Colosseum Berlin on November 11th.

Some activities are free, while others require registration or a ticket.

**Tollwood Winter Festival: November 24th – December 23rd**

Tollwood occupies the Oktoberfest grounds in Munich, but the focus here is less on beer and more on international food, contemporary circus, environmental debate and fire-lit performance.

The festival combines a Market of Ideas with organic food, fair-trade crafts, live music, art installations and a children’s programme.

![Tollwood winter festival 2021](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/267006178-scaled.jpg)

Its 2026 stage programme includes three international circus and dance productions, beginning with *Ten Thousand Hours* on November 25th.

Entry to the Theresienwiese festival grounds and market is free, as are more than 90 percent of the events. Tickets are needed for selected theatre, circus and other performances and can be bought through the festival’s authorised ticket channels.

The market opens at 2pm from Monday to Friday and at 11am on weekends. It closes at 10:30pm on Sundays and Mondays and at 11:30pm from Tuesday to Saturday.

The smell of *Christstollen* and incense drifts across Dresden’s Altmarkt as the Striezelmarkt opens its wooden stalls for the 592nd time.

First documented in 1434, the market brings together more than 200 traders, with a particular emphasis on Saxon and Ore Mountain traditions. Among its landmarks are a 14.61-metre tiered pyramid, a walk-through *Schwibbogen* candle arch and workshops demonstrating regional crafts.

The market opens from 4pm to 9pm on November 25th. From November 26th to December 23rd, it runs daily from 10am to 9pm, before closing at 2pm on Christmas Eve.

Those seeking a quieter look around may prefer a weekday morning. Evenings arguably provide more atmosphere, but also considerably larger crowds.
