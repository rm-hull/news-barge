DRIVERS are being urged to stay alert as the risk of animal-related road collisions rises during the darker months.

Ray Chapman Motors Bradford is raising awareness of the increased danger, especially on rural routes, after new analysis by Volvo using Department for Transport data revealed 52 casualties in the region due to animal-related collisions between 2021 and 2025.

Duncan Chapman, managing director at Ray Chapman Motors Bradford, said: "Animal-related collisions can happen instantly and often when drivers least expect them.

"As we head into the darker months, reduced visibility and increased wildlife activity can make driving conditions more challenging.

"Simple steps such as reducing speed, paying attention to wildlife warning signs and staying alert around bends can make a difference in helping drivers avoid potentially serious accidents."

Animal-related collisions were most common in October and November.

Yorkshire and the Humber accounted for 9.6 per cent of all such incidents nationwide.

Across the UK, there were 1,503 animal-related road collisions resulting in 1,939 casualties over the five-year period.

Mr Chapman said: "To help combat the problem, our vehicles feature tried and tested technology that can detect large animals on or near the road, alert drivers to a potential hazard and, if necessary, automatically intervene."
