More than eight in ten UK adults fear rising food prices as the Chancellor prepares to deliver today’s Budget.

Polling of more than 2,000 adults found that 83% were concerned about food prices, while 82% were worried about inflation.

The survey was carried out by Opinium for the British Retail Consortium (BRC).

The findings were published on October 28, ahead of the Budget.

They came after the BRC reported that shop price inflation had risen to 2.1% in October.

That was the highest rate recorded since February 2024. Food prices were 2.9% higher than they were a year earlier.

The BRC said retailers were trying to absorb costs and keep prices as low as possible. However, it said rising taxes had made this increasingly difficult.

Retail businesses are expected to face £41 billion in taxes during 2026/27. This is a 20% increase in two years.

Retail, alongside hospitality, has the highest effective tax rate among major economic sectors, according to the BRC. Nearly £3 in tax is paid for every £4 of profit, equal to 72%.

The figure for the financial sector is less than £2 for every £4 of profit, or 41%.

The BRC is calling on Chancellor Healey to reduce business costs and share the tax burden more evenly across the economy.

It warned that further taxes on retailers would be likely to increase prices for households.

Business rates are due to rise by 3.5% in April 2027, in line with the Consumer Price Index for September. Retail already pays one fifth of the UK’s business rates.

The increase would add about £250 million to retailers’ costs, the BRC said.

Helen Dickinson, Chief Executive of the British Retail Consortium, said: “The message from the public is clear: the cost of living remains a top concern.

Households, retailers and the Government share a common aim of easing pressure on family finances and fixing the youth employment crisis. The Budget must deliver on both.”

The retail industry also employs large numbers of young people. One in five people begin their careers in the sector, with shops and warehouses providing the biggest source of flexible and local jobs in Britain.

In September, the industry announced plans to create 100,000 jobs for people not in employment, education or training by the end of this Parliament. The plan includes thousands of work placements.

However, employment costs have increased by £6.5 billion in two years.

More than £2 billion of that increase was linked to changes to employer National Insurance contributions and the payment threshold.

The BRC said those costs had limited retailers’ ability to create jobs. It said 122,000 retail jobs had been lost in two years.

There are nearly one million young people not in employment, education or training, according to the figures provided by the organisation.

Helen Dickinson, Chief Executive of the British Retail Consortium, said: “To support this, the Chancellor must address the barrage of costs and taxes currently raining down on the industry.

"Chief among these are business rates, which are set to rise by another £250m for retailers next April, and employer NICs, which are up by over £2 billion in the last two years. Every pound spent on taxes and regulation is a pound not spent on keeping prices low for consumers.”

The BRC and its members want the Government to raise the employer National Insurance threshold from £5,000 to £6,000.

They are also calling for the Consumer Price Index-linked increase in business rates to be frozen.

A third request is for retail premises to be exempt from the high-value multiplier.

The organisation said reducing costs would give retailers more room to keep prices down and provide jobs.

Helen Dickinson, Chief Executive of the British Retail Consortium, said: “Action on business rates and NICs would go a long way to helping retailers deliver even better value for households and more jobs for young people.

A Budget for shops and shoppers is also a Budget for communities everywhere.”

The BRC said the Budget offered an opportunity to support household finances and employment.

It said action on taxes and business costs could help retailers deal with price pressures while creating opportunities for young people.
