**Businesses are being urged to prepare for new laws covering the sale of tobacco, vaping and nicotine products, which come into force on 29 October 2026.**

Trading Standards officers at City of York Council are working with Humber and North Yorkshire’s Centre for Excellence to help retailers understand the changes and their legal responsibilities.

Under the new rules, it will be illegal to sell vaping or nicotine products to anyone under 18. It will also be an offence for an adult to buy, or attempt to buy, these products on behalf of someone under 18.

The age restrictions cover nicotine and non-nicotine vapes, nicotine pouches and other nicotine products. Nicotine replacement therapies and other medicines or medical devices are excluded.

Retailers are being advised to review their age-verification procedures, ensure staff understand the new requirements and take steps to prevent underage sales.

**New restrictions on vending machines and promotions**

The legislation also bans vending machines selling tobacco products, herbal smoking products, cigarette papers, vaping products or nicotine products.

The ban applies to anyone responsible for premises where such a machine is installed, even if customers pay for the products elsewhere.

Businesses will also face restrictions on giving away the products as free samples or offering large promotional discounts.

Failure to comply could result in a fixed penalty notice of £200.

> **Cllr Jenny Kent, Executive Member for Environment and Climate Emergency at City of York Council, said:**
>
> These changes are an important step in helping to protect children and young people from the risks linked to vaping and nicotine products.

She urged retailers to check which products were covered by the legislation, refresh staff training and ensure effective age checks were in place.

**Support available for people who want to quit smoking**

The council is also encouraging residents to seek help to stop smoking through its Health Trainers service.

The free service offers up to 12 weeks of one-to-one support, access to the Smoke Free app and help through the Swap 2 Stop programme, which provides regulated vaping products to support people trying to quit smoking.

Residents can also access six months of free use of the Smoke Free app, which offers tools to manage cravings, track health improvements and monitor money saved.

> **Peter Roderick, Director of Public Health at City of York Council, said:**
>
> These new laws are an important step in helping prevent children and young people live a life free from addiction to nicotine products and support wider efforts to create a smoke-free generation.

He also encouraged adults who smoke to seek support, saying people were more likely to quit successfully with help than by trying alone.

More information about the council’s stop-smoking support is available at www.york.gov.uk/HealthTrainers.
