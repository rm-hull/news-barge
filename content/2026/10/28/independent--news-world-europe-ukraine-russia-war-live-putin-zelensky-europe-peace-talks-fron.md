Volodymyr Zelensky says Ukraine's battlefield position is the strongest it has been in some time, after his forces recaptured territory through a series of offensives.

Zelensky revealed details of the previously secret Operation Veles, where Ukrainian troops used underground gas pipelines to outflank Russian positions near Kupiansk in the northeast.

He also referred to an air assault operation in eastern Ukraine and Operation Vivaldi further north, aimed at preventing Russian advances in key areas.

His remarks came as Finland's president and the Norwegian prime minister held a rare direct phone call with Russian president Vladimir Putin.

Finnish president Alexander Stubb and Norway's Jonas Gahr Stoere spoke to Putin for around 90 minutes on Tuesday before holding a separate conversation with Zelensky.

While Kyiv says it was involved in planning for the phone call, it has drawn criticism from other European leaders, many of whom are opposed to direct engagement with the Russian leader.

The Kremlin showed no indication of any softening in its position during the call, insisting that any lasting peace must "unconditionally" take Russia's interests into account.

## Recap: Finnish and Norwegian leaders hold rare call with Putin to urge end to Ukraine war

The leaders of Finland and Norway have held rare direct calls with Russian president Vladimir Putin, urging him to end the war in Ukraine as diplomatic efforts gain momentum ahead of fresh peace negotiations.

Finnish president Alexander Stubb and Norwegian prime minister Jonas Gahr Stoere spoke to Putin for around 90 minutes on Tuesday before holding a separate conversation with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky.

The call marked a significant departure from the approach taken by most Western European leaders, who have largely avoided direct contact with the Kremlin since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

![Store and Stubb made contact with Putin on Tuesday, urging an end to the war in Ukraine](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/27/17/2058101575...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The discussion (with Putin) was frank and substantive. We are neighbouring countries to Russia. The outcome of this war will have a direct impact on our and the broader European security. Ending the war requires direct discussions,” Stubb said.

Stoere said he and Stubb had "stressed the importance of de-escalation." Both voiced strong support for Ukraine.

The two leaders said the initiative was linked to planned peace talks between Russian, Ukrainian and US officials in Abu Dhabi next month.

However, the Kremlin showed no indication of softening its demands, insisting that any lasting peace must fully take Russia's interests into account.

The diplomatic outreach also exposed divisions among European leaders, with Danish prime minister Mette Frederiksen warning against countries acting alone and Sweden's Ulf Kristersson expressing little hope of a breakthrough.

Ukraine welcomed the initiative, saying the talks had been coordinated with Kyiv.

## Zelensky touts Ukraine's strong battlefield position after recapturing territory

President Volodymyr Zelensky has said that Kyiv’s battlefield position is the strongest it has been in some time after his forces retook territory through a series of offensives.

It includes one operation previously unannounced in which Kyiv's troops used gas pipelines to outflank Russian positions.

Zelensky, speaking in his nightly video address, cited a report by senior commanders to say that, for the first time, the "Veles" operation used pipelines in Ukraine's northeast.

He also referred ⁠to an air assault operation in eastern Ukraine ​and ⁠the "Vivaldi" operation further to the north, designed to prevent Russian advances in key areas.

He said Operation Veles was conducted near Kupiansk, ⁠a city in northeastern Kharkiv region recaptured by Ukrainian forces in late 2022, but ​which has been under constant Russian pressure.

## In depth: Putin targets Ukrainian civilians in barbaric ‘safari’ attacks as Trump turns blind eye

Vladimir Putin is targeting Ukrainian civilians in a surge of barbaric attacks across the country despite Donald Trump claiming that Russia is acting “fine” in the war.

New figures released by the UN this week show the number of Ukrainian civilian casualties in September rose by 85 per cent compared with the year before.

First-person drones, long-range glide bombs, ballistic rockets and new jet-powered Shahed cruise missiles are being used in the attacks as winter closes in. No one is off limits. Children, babies, mothers in the park, elderly fruit vendors.

From Kherson to Kharkiv, Kyiv to Kramatorsk, “hunting drone safaris” picking off ordinary people are taking the war far beyond the front lines.

Of the 328 people killed and 2,105 injured last month, 53 per cent of casualties were caused by missiles and long-range drones while 23 per cent were caused by short-range drones.

## Kyiv plans to build water crossings as Russia escalates bridge strikes

Ukraine's Kyiv plans to allocate 1 billion hryvnias ($22 million) to build water crossings over the River Dnipro that divides the capital into two parts, the city mayor said on Tuesday, as Russia escalates its attacks on bridges.

Russia has significantly stepped up its strikes on logistics in and around Kyiv in recent months, and in October it struck bridges, disrupting transportation for hundreds of thousands of people.

Mayor Vitali Klitschko said on Telegram that the city's defence council discussed "the project to build combined water crossings over the River Dnipro and the access roads leading to them."
