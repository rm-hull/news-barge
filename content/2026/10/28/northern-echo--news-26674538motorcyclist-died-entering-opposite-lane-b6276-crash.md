A retired motorcyclist was flung 12ft into the air when his bike veered into oncoming traffic and hit a car head-on on a rural County Durham road, an inquest heard.

An inquest into his death at Crook Coroners Court last week (Wednesday, October 21) heard there was "nothing" the doctor driving the car could have done to avoid the cras, having only a "split second" to react after seeing Peter coming towards her.

Despite CPR being carried out, the 76-year-old retired civil servant, from Greater Manchester, was pronounced dead at the scene.

The inquest heard how Mr Cole had been riding with members of the South Lancs Advanced Motorists Club when the crash happened.

The Coroner heard the group, made up of around 25 motorcyclists, had set off from the Preston area that day, meanwhile Laura Booth, the doctor in the VW, had left home at around 12.36pm to travel to Northumberland for a family holiday.

Ms Booth said she was travelling at around 40mph and had slowed down as she approached the bend, having seen several motorcyclists travelling in the opposite direction.

She said: "As I got to the beginning of the bend, in a split second, I saw a motorcycle directly in front of me on my side of the road driving straight towards me, and directly at me.

"All I thought was 'I am going to die' and next thing I know the airbags had deployed."

Ms Booth said she got out of her car with the engine still running because she was "in shock" and saw a man lying on the road.

She said: "I looked at the male, and as a doctor, I thought by looking at him I knew he was in cardiac arrest."

The court heard Ms Booth's iPhone detected that she had been involved in a crash and automatically called the emergency services.

Ms Booth said: "On reflection, the whole thing happened so quickly. I had no time to react to the motorbike driving head-on into my car."

Peter Stevens, a member of the club who had been riding around 25 metres behind Mr Cole, said the weather conditions were "bright and clear" as they travelled along a road made up of various bends.

He described the group as an "advanced group" who "always travel safely" and said he believed he and Mr Cole had been matching each other's speeds of around 30mph.

Mr Stevens said the crash happened on a left-hand bend which was "quite sharp on appearance".

He said: "As soon as I saw Peter enter the bend, I saw him collide with a light blue car approaching on the opposite side of the road."

Mr Stevens said the car was on the correct side of the road and "never wavered".

He said he saw Mr Cole being thrown around 12ft into the air before landing on the road and not moving.

Mr Stevens said the driver of the car was "understandably distressed" by the incident.

He added: "My only explanation for this is that Peter entered the opposing lane.

"I cannot comment on Peter's riding experience. I had only met him once before two weeks prior."

Thomas Black and his wife Andrea, who were also members of the motorcycle group riding behind Mr Cole, said they saw a "cloud of dust" following the collision.

The couple said they could tell Mr Cole was not breathing and his lips had turned blue and began CPR until ambulance and air ambulance crews arrived.

PC Harrison, of Durham Police, was the first police officer at the scene.

The court heard Ms Booth told the officer she had been travelling at "no more than 40mph" and had remained on her side of the carriageway.

Drug and alcohol tests were carried out on Ms Booth, and both came back negative.

Dr Cooper gave Mr Cole's cause of death as head, chest and pelvic injuries, with the examination finding "heavy bleeding around the brain", multiple rib fractures and multiple pelvic fractures.

There was "no evidence of intoxication", Mr Cole had been wearing a helmet and had held his driving licence for more than 50 years.

His motorbike was examined following the collision and "no defects were found" that could have caused or contributed to the crash.

Examinations also showed Ms Booth was braking at the time of the impact.

Coroner Rebecca Sutton said the exact speed of Mr Cole and Ms Booth "cannot be determined", although Mr Stevens' account indicated the motorcycles were travelling at around 30mph.

The court heard it was unknown whether Mr Cole had a sun visor on his helmet, while he had been travelling in the direction of the sun when the collision happened.

The coroner said Mr Cole may have made an error while negotiating the bend or may have been affected by the position of the sun.

The court also heard there was an area of slightly uneven road where repairs had been carried out.

Ms Sutton said: "It's not possible for me to say even on a balance of probabilities whether or not that area of road repairs affected him."
