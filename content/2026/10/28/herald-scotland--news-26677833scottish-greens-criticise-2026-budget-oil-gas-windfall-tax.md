**The Scottish Greens have accused the UK Government of missing an opportunity to tackle soaring energy costs after the Chancellor didn't strengthen the windfall tax on oil and gas companies in the 2026 Budget.**

Co-leader Ross Greer said ministers had "let oil and gas giants off the hook" by failing to increase taxes on fossil fuel producers and use the proceeds to help cut household energy bills.

The criticism comes amid ongoing concerns about fuel poverty and follows the approval of the controversial Jackdaw oil and gas development in the North Sea. Greer argued that, as households face some of Europe's highest energy bills, the Budget should have done more to make major energy firms contribute towards easing pressure on families.

He said: "This budget was a chance to make oil and gas giants fund a cut in household energy bills by taxing their huge profits. Instead of taking that action, the Chancellor has let these companies off the hook.

"It is working families that are paying the price for soaring energy costs while fossil fuel companies benefit from the same instability that is driving those bills up."

The MSP said one in three households in Scotland lived in fuel poverty and claimed higher taxes on energy companies' profits could have funded more support for consumers.

He also called for increased investment in home energy efficiency, renewable energy and green jobs, arguing that reducing reliance on fossil fuels would help shield households from future price shocks.

Greer criticised both Westminster and Holyrood's approach to North Sea oil and gas, claiming the Scottish Government had shifted away from its previous climate ambitions.

Referring to former first minister Nicola Sturgeon's opposition to developments such as Rosebank and Jackdaw, he said the current SNP administration was prioritising the interests of oil and gas companies over consumers.

The Greens co-leader added: "As Jackdaw is given the go-ahead, and with the very real prospect of Rosebank being approved too, we must tax the super-rich super-polluters that have taken Scotland for a ride for far too long, leaving working families skint while the wealthiest in society add more zeroes to their bank accounts and trash our environment.

"Scotland's workers and communities deserve investment in their future, not UK and Scottish governments hell-bent on drilling for what little is left in the North Sea."

Greer added that further devolution of powers over energy and taxation would allow Scotland to take a different approach to tackling the cost of living and investing in the country's economic future.

During his Budget, Chancellor John Healey said he was “setting a new course on energy to get UK energy costs down in line with Europe”. He said the Government’s priority was “first and foremost cheaper energy”, with clean power key to achieving it, and that it remained “unshakably committed to net zero by 2050”.

He said this would require deep energy-system reform, starting in the Budget and continuing through the 10-year plan.

The Chancellor said that for many years under Conservative governments policy costs were “simply loaded onto household bills through levies”. He said: “Today, I am putting an end to that.”

He announced a new cost control framework to drive down all aspects of energy bills.

Mr Healey said he would scrap the upcoming gas shippers obligation and levy, which was to fund hydrogen projects. He said the Government would instead back those projects directly.

The Chancellor praised the Energy Secretary for approving the Jackdaw gas field, which he said boosts energy security, protects “hundreds of jobs” and adds an estimated £2.9 billion to the UK economy. He called it “a pragmatic, common sense decision”.

He said the Government had also launched a new energy wealth fund.

The SNP also criticised parts of the Budget, warning some Scottish households could pay more for gas under plans to abolish standing charges.

Westminster leader Dave Doogan argued that Scotland's colder climate meant many households could see bills increase if costs were instead recovered through higher unit rates.

Responding to the Budget, Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander said: “This is a budget that puts money back in the pockets of Scottish households, backs businesses, and puts Scotland firmly at the heart of the UK government’s plan for growth and reindustrialisation.

"There’s a boost for Scotland’s public services too, with an extra £600 million for the Scottish Government to spend.”
