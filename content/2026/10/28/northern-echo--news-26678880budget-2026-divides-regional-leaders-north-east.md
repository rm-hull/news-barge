The North East is set to host a major international investment summit and receive new powers to fund regeneration projects following a Budget which has divided political leaders across the region.

Chancellor John Healey's first Budget, delivered on Wednesday (October 28), included £100 energy payments for millions of households, business rates relief and significant changes to how money raised locally can be spent.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640775.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey *(Image: Jordan Pettitt/PA MEDIA)*

Gateshead will host the UK's International Investment Summit in spring 2027, while projects including Gateshead Quays and proposed film and television studios in Sunderland could benefit from new regional funding arrangements.

Elsewhere, work on the long-awaited A66 dualling scheme will begin next month, but the major upgrade linking North Yorkshire and Cumbria will be delivered over nine years, the Government revealed in background Budget documents.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21007541.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kim McGuinness, North East Mayor *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

However, while North East Mayor Kim McGuinness welcomed the announcements, Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen questioned whether families would actually be better off.

Ms McGuinness described the Budget as a significant step towards giving the North East greater control over its economic future.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21257614.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tees Valley Mayor, Ben Houchen *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

"We will also get new powers to keep tax raised from these major developments and others. It's about time we stopped sending all of our tax to Westminster and instead use it right here in the North East."

Under his devolution plans, mayors will receive a share of business rates revenue from next year and a share of income tax receipts from 2028.

Mark Ferguson, Labour MP for Gateshead Central and Whickham, called the investment summit a "huge vote of confidence" in the town.

He said: "Gateshead helped build Britain's industrial past, and by hosting the upcoming international investment summit, we now have the chance to show the world how we can help build Britain's future."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21352664.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The A66 near Scotch Corner *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

The Budget also included £7 million for a feasibility study into a potential Olympic and Paralympic Games bid in northern England.

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen delivered a contrasting verdict, arguing the Budget lacked substantial measures to improve living standards.

He said: "After all the build-up, I don't think there's much to this Budget.

"There are some welcome announcements, but ultimately I think most people will be left wondering whether they're actually going to be any better off."

Mr Houchen welcomed further devolution but warned important details remained unresolved.

He added: "Ultimately, people will judge this Budget on whether they've got more money in their pockets and whether businesses have the confidence to invest and grow.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19922299.jpg?type=mds-article-620)MP for Durham City, Mary Foy *(Image: PARLIAMENT)*

"Right now, I'm not convinced this Budget delivers that."

The Chancellor extended the temporary zero-VAT rate on household electricity until July 2027 and continued the 5p fuel duty cut until the same month.

Mary Foy, Labour MP for the City of Durham, said more than 41,000 households in her constituency would benefit from the energy payment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19655851.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Luke Akehurst, MP for North Durham *(Image: SUBMIT)*

She said: "This budget includes genuine steps to support people through difficult times, and I'm pleased that constituents and businesses in the City of Durham will benefit from these measures."

Sharon Hodgson, MP for Washington and Gateshead South, welcomed a £2.5 billion, ten-year partnership with Rolls-Royce to develop next-generation aerospace gas turbines, highlighting the company's factory in her constituency.

She said the investment would "secure skilled jobs and apprenticeships for local people".

Meanwhile, Kate Osborne, MP for Jarrow and Gateshead East, and North Durham MP, Luke Akehurst, welcomed the cost-of-living measures but expressed disappointment that the remaining £1.2 billion from the Mineworkers Pension Scheme Guarantors' Fund was not transferred immediately.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643232.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Prime Minister Andy Burnham *(Image: PA MEDIA)*

They pledged to continue fighting for former miners and their families.

Mr Healey also announced a 3.9 per cent increase in the minimum wage, an increase in the small business rates relief threshold from £12,000 to £18,000 for eligible retail, hospitality and leisure premises, and the continuation of the £20,000 cash ISA allowance.

First-time buyers will benefit from a new £4.7 billion government-backed equity loan scheme, while paid parental leave will be extended to self-employed fathers and partners.

The Government also announced additional funding to tackle rough sleeping, changes to Carer's Allowance and £10 billion in additional economic and military support for Ukraine.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham defended the announcements, describing the decision to help 20 million households with energy costs as "pretty bold".

But Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch dismissed the Budget as "not good enough, not strong enough, not bold enough".

Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey called for a "real growth plan with Europe at its heart", while Reform UK leader Nigel Farage argued the measures did not achieve "very much overall".

Mr Healey insisted his Budget would provide "breathing space" for households and businesses while laying the foundations for future economic growth.
