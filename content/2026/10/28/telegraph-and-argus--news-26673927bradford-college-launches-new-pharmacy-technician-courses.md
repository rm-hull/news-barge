NEW higher education programmes have been launched to support advanced skills and career progression for pharmacy technicians.

Bradford College has unveiled a Level 4 Higher National Certificate (HNC) and a Level 5 Foundation Degree (FdSc) in clinical practice for pharmacy technicians, validated by the University of Greater Manchester.

The programmes are designed to help pharmacy technicians develop advanced clinical skills to meet the evolving needs of modern healthcare.

Charlotte Wright, school of pharmacy professional development lead at Bradford College, said: "As a registered pharmacy technician myself, I'm incredibly excited to be part of such an important step forward in the development of our profession.

"Having witnessed firsthand how the role of pharmacy technicians has evolved over the years, I've seen the positive impact that expanding our knowledge, skills and responsibilities can have, not only on patient care but on wider healthcare services."

The college is among the first further education institutions in the UK to offer these programmes, building on nearly 70 years of experience in pharmacy technician education.

The courses are tailored for registered pharmacy technicians who wish to continue working while studying.

They combine live teaching, structured independent learning and workplace-based application, enabling learners to apply new skills in real time.

Course content covers a broad range of contemporary and emerging topics, including applied therapeutics, medicines optimisation, patient consultation, clinical examination, health promotion, quality improvement, pharmacogenomics and advanced pharmacokinetics.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21636527.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pharmacy Technician *(Image: Supplied)*

Programmes were described as "pioneering" during the validation process by members of the validating committee, reflecting the quality of the curriculum and the college’s commitment to industry-responsive education.

Ms Wright said: "We've worked hard to develop programmes that are relevant, accessible and genuinely responsive to what pharmacy technicians and their employers have told us they need.

"The programmes have been shaped by experienced pharmacy professionals, including registered Pharmacist Sarah Hughes, who played a key role in developing the curriculum and will continue to be involved in teaching and assessment."

The launch strengthens Bradford College’s commitment to supporting career development and workforce needs in the pharmacy sector through close collaboration with employers and healthcare providers.

Past graduates have gone on to work in community and registered pharmacies, GP practices, hospitals, care homes, prisons, mental health services, the armed forces and the pharmaceutical industry.

The new programmes build on the college’s existing Level 2 and Level 3 pharmacy qualifications, offering a pathway for pharmacy technicians to expand their clinical expertise and prepare for future roles in a rapidly changing healthcare environment.
