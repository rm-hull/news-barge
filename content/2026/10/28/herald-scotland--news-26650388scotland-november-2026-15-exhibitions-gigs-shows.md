**EXHIBITIONS**

## David Bowie: On Tour

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21121123.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Polaroid of David Bowie's costume fittings for the character of Jareth in the 1986 film, Labyrinth, part of V&A David Bowie: On Tour *(Image: David Parry/PA Media Assignments)*

Bringing together around 100 objects from the singer’s archive, this blockbuster exhibition offers fans a peek behind the creative curtain to show how Bowie conceptualised and realised his tours, artwork and, of course, his famous rock star personas. Included in the show are handwritten lyrics, performance notes, Polaroids for make-up and costume fittings, the costumes themselves and unrealised scripts. Ziggy played guitar, as the man himself once sang – and you can see one of them in the collection of instruments which are also included in the show.

## Roman Scotland: Life On The Edge Of Empire

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21627882.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The altar stones discovered in a 2nd century Roman fort at Inveresk *(Image: Duncan McGlynn)*

Two spectacular altar stones discovered in a 2nd century Roman fort at Inveresk form the centrepiece of this exhibition, which also includes loans from museums across Europe and previously unseen archaeological discoveries.

## Ade Adesina: Bard

A major solo show for the Nigeria-born, Aberdeen-based printmaker whose huge, intricate, magic-flavoured woodcuts are a wonder to behold – and you really do need to peer closely at them in order to pick out the rich detail. They have profound political and social underpinnings as well, dealing with everything from climate change to colonial histories.

**City Art Centre, Edinburgh (November 7-April 11, 2027)**

## France-Lise McGurn: The Charm Offensive

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21602728.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Installation shot of France-Lise McGurn's show at DCA *(Image: Ruth Clark)*

Largest UK show to date for the Glasgow-born artist who, though primarily a painter, is here also exhibiting drawings, studio ephemera and even furniture across both galleries of Dundee Contemporary Arts. It’s a homecoming of sorts as well: McGurn trained at the city’s Duncan of Jordanstone College of Art and Design before stints at the Royal College of Art in London and New York’s Hunter College of Art.

**CONCERTS**

## Verdi Requiem

Royal Scottish National Orchestra (RSNO) under the baton of Patrick Hahn is joined by the RSNO Chorus and the mighty Edinburgh Festival Chorus for a performance of the Italian composer’s famous Requiem, from 1874. Soloists are Jennifer Davis (soprano), Deniz Uzun (mezzo-soprano), Mario Lerchenberger (tenor) and Edwin Crossley-Mercer (bass).

**Usher Hall, Edinburgh (November 6), Glasgow Royal Concert Hall, Glasgow (November 7)**

## Seasonal Quartet

One of the highlights of Aberdeen’s innovative and envelope-pushing Sound Festival, this event features Netherlands-based contemporary music collective New European Ensemble performing a new piece inspired by Scottish author Ali Smith’s so-called Seasonal Quartet of novels – complete with readings by Smith herself.

**Cowdray Hall, Aberdeen (October 30)Read more**

## Sheku Kanneh-Mason Plays Haydn

Kanneh-Mason was the first Black musician to win the BBC Young Musician Of The Year award and in the decade since has proved himself one of British music’s rising stars. Here he makes his debut with the Scottish Chamber Orchestra (SCO) with a performance of Joseph Haydn’s much-loved First Cello Concert. Also on the programme is the world premiere of Jay Capperauld’s Sinfonietta: Pathways, an SCO commission, and Beethoven’s Symphony No.3. Conductor is Andrew Manze.

**Usher Hall, Edinburgh (November 12), City Halls, Glasgow (November 13)THEATRE**

## 1984

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21627875.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The latest 1984 adaptation is not to be missed *(Image: Mihaela Bodlovic)*

It’s hard to argue against the continued relevance of George Orwell’s famously dystopian critique of totalitarianism so it’s no surprise that theatre, one of the nimblest and most political of art forms, finds continued mileage in it. This new adaptation is by storied Glasgow playwright Chris Hannan and is directed by Dominic Hill, artistic director of the Citizens Theatre, where it premiered in late August. A co-production between the Citz, Dundee Rep and Edinburgh’s Royal Lyceum Theatre, it lands in the capital after stints in Dundee and Aberdeen.

## Confluence

Eden Court in Inverness celebrates its 50th birthday with an immersive event in which audiences move through the iconic building and encounter five intimate live performances along the way. Among them are PCK Dance’s Postscript, which features live music and an illuminated landscape, Lewis Hetherington’s single-hander The Entertainer, and Resonant Ground, which fuses Gaelic, Indian and Pakistani music.

**Eden Court, Inverness (October 30-November 8, times vary)**

## The Haunting Of Hill House

A little late for Hallowe’en, perhaps, but no matter: this adaptation of novelist Shirley Jackson’s Gothic chiller by BAFTA winning writer Stef Smith will send shivers in, up and down all the right places. Stephen King called the novel “near perfect”, Netflix turned it into a hit TV series, and in this touring stage adaptation directed by Martin Constantine there’s plenty in the way of special effects and what you might call noises off. Only Scottish date.

**CINEMA**

## Heysel 85

Admittedly reviews were mixed when it premiered at this year’s Berlin film festival, but Belgian-Romanian director Teodora Ana Mihai’s film about the disaster at the 1985 European Cup Final between Juventus and Liverpool, in which 39 mostly Italian supporters were killed, will still draw interest on this side of the North Sea.

**General release (November 20)**

## Primetime

Robert Pattinson plays American TV journalist Chris Hansen in this dramatisation by documentary maker Lance Oppenheim of the true life story behind a reality show aimed at catching child predators which ran on US TV in the 2000s. Indie music darling Phoebe Bridgers co-stars alongside (among others) Alex Winters of Bill And Ted’s Excellent Adventure fame, himself a victim of child sexual abuse.

**General release (October 30)**

## I Love Boosters

Nope’s Keke Palmer stars in this absurd comedy from Boots Riley, rapper-turned-filmmaker and the director of 2018 under-the-radar hit, Sorry To Bother You. Palmer is Corvette, leader of a trio of shoplifters in San Francisco who make ends meet by selling the clothes they ‘boost’. The all-supporting cast includes Demi Moore, LaKeith Stanfield, Don Cheadle, Viggo Mortensen alongside Taylour Paige and British actress Naomi Ackie, as Corvette’s partners-in-crime Mariah and Sade.

**General release (October 30)**

## Wild Horse Nine

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21602711.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sam Rockwell and John Malkovich in Wild Horse Nine *(Image: Searchlight Pictures)*

John Malkovich won the best actor award at September’s Venice film festival for his role in this black comedy from Anglo-Irish director Martin McDonagh, about a CIA agent sent to Easter Island just before the American-backed coup in Chile in 1973. Sam Rockwell, Steve Buscemi, Tom Waits and Parker Posey co-star, which is quite a line-up in anyone’s book.

**General release (November 6)DANCE & OPERA**

40th Anniversary Gala Scottish Dance Theatre celebrates four decades of ground-breaking performance with a bumper gala. Centre stage is 40 By 40, an improvised, one time only performance bringing together 40 former and current Scottish Dance Theatre dancers accompanied by live music from Glasgow-based musician Jer Reid. Also in the programme are O’ Chiadain an Lo by SDT’s Artistic Director Joan Clevillé, Sofia Nappi’s Moving Cloud, and the recently premiered Rotten Work by Emilie Leriche.

## Turandot

In the year Turandot turns 100, Scottish Opera mounts a co-production with Irish National Opera of Puccini’s famously unfinished 1926 work, directed here by Sir David McVicar. Stuart Stratford conducts The Orchestra of Scottish Opera for a cast including Trine Møller, Victor Starsky, Insung Sim, and Hye-Youn Lee. Everyone ready for Nessun dorma?

**GIGS**

## Yard Act

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21602723.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Leeds provocateurs Yard Act *(Image: Yard Act)*

James Smith’s spiky Leeds provocateurs bring their post-punk sound and sharp observational songwriting to Glasgow as part of a major UK headline tour. Debut album The Overload won them a Mercury Prize nod and third album, You’re Gonna Need A Little Music, hit number three in the Scottish and UK album charts when it was released in July. Plenty of fans on both sides of the border, in other words. With Fontaines DC’s Glasgow show sold out, this is your next best bet for an electrifying live performance.

## Beabadoobee

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21602730.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Beabadoobee. Pic Erika Kamano *(Image: Erika Kamano)*

Something of a veteran now, the British-Filipino singer-songwriter released her fourth album Pylon last month and brings her sassy indie-pop to Glasgow. It’s the first night of an eight date UK tour which winds up at London’s Alexandra Palace and follows an 18-date US tour and successful few years in which she had topped the UK album charts and supported Taylor Swift on her Eras tour.

**OVO Hydro, Glasgow (November 14)**

## Birdbox

Billed as something of an indie-folk supergroup, Birdbox consists of regular Karine Polwart collaborator Inge Thomson, bassist Charlotte Printer, Sarah Hayes of Admiral Fallow and Jenny Sturgeon, singer with alt-folk trio Salt House. Debut album Shamming The Drama is out now, and blends influences running from Giorgio Moroder and Björk to Kate Bush and the Cocteau Twins.

**The Tunnels, Aberdeen (October 29); Sneaky Pete’s, Edinburgh (October 30); CatStrand, New Galloway (October 31); The Art School, Glasgow (November 7)**

## Joan As Policewoman

Only Scottish date for US singer-songwriter Joan Wasser, aka Joan As Police Woman, whose every new album feels like a paradigm shift. She has collaborated with Tony Allen, Lou Reed, Anohni, Meshell Ndegeocello, John Cale, and David Byrne to name just a few, is a member of Iggy Pop’s touring band, and is as likely to throw out a cover of a song from Grease as she is one of her own leftfield soul excursions. “The coolest woman in pop” was one critic’s description, and it’s hard to argue.

**Oran Mor, Glasgow (November 9)**
