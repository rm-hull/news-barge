County Durham and Darlington’s Police and Crime Commissioner has welcomed new powers to tackle persistent anti-social behaviour and give victims a stronger voice.

The measures came into force on Monday, October 26, under the Crime and Policing Act 2026 and include Respect Orders for adults aged 18 and over in England and Wales.

The orders replace civil anti-social behaviour injunctions and let police, councils and social housing providers ban repeat offenders from specific areas or from drinking alcohol in public.

Breaching a Respect Order is now a criminal offence.

PCC Joy Allen said: "I strongly welcome the new measures which are urgently needed.

"They recognise the enormity of ASB on mental and physical wellbeing and provide reassurance to communities that police and partners are exhausting all efforts to protect them from this appalling behaviour."

The changes also strengthen Anti-Social Behaviour Case Reviews, formerly known as the Community Trigger.

Victims of persistent ASB can now request a review if their concerns have not been properly addressed, if agencies say the threshold has not been met or if they remain unhappy after a review.

PCCs must now monitor ASB case reviews and hold community safety partners to account for how they handle cases.

Victims must be consulted before action plans are finalised and invited to submit impact statements or attend initial review meetings.

They are also entitled to support under the Victims’ Code.

Ms Allen has made tackling ASB a top priority since her election in 2021.

She said: "ASB remains a top concern for our communities.

"For too long, victims have been forced to endure further anxiety and distress as complaints have passed between departments with no action and no redress.

"Now, agencies will be legally bound to handle complaints with greater care, compassion, and efficiency, ending the postcode lottery of support, while victims have a clear route to challenge inadequacy."

Ms Allen is believed to be the first PCC in the country to let ASB victims attend case reviews and give personal statements about the harm they have suffered—a practice now enshrined in law.

Ms Allen also appointed an Anti-Social Behaviour Champion to support victims and research their experiences of the justice system.

Her office also created a dedicated Review Coordinator role, funded from her budget, to give victims one point of contact throughout the review process.

The office has also appointed two volunteer ASB case review chairs and introduced a case management system to track cases more efficiently.

Ms Allen said: "While I am proud that Durham implemented many of these changes ahead of these new rules, it is not enough.

"We can always do more—with the public’s help.

"I would appeal to all residents and businesses to keep on reporting ASB, so we can remain one step ahead and deliver the meaningful solutions we need to help residents feel and be safer."

The Act also raises the maximum Fixed Penalty Notice for breaching a Community Protection Notice or Public Spaces Protection Order from £100 to £500.

Registered social housing providers can now issue closure notices and seek closure orders independently for properties they own or manage.
