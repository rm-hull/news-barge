A mother and daughter from Middlesbrough have recovered £56,000 in life savings after falling victim to a devastating scam.

Sandra Taylor-Warren, 46, and her mother Sue Taylor, 78, from Tollesby, endured nearly four years of financial hardship and distress after being scammed by three rogue traders.

Their ordeal began in summer 2022 when a leaking shower caused the ceiling in their breakfast room to collapse.

They called a handyman who had worked for them before, but decorating work soon spiralled into costly roof repairs.

The trader claimed the roof was leaking, then removed ceiling tiles, entered the loft and moved roof tiles, leaving the home worse off despite the pair paying him around £18,000.

The family then sought help from roofers who had put a leaflet through their front door.

The roofers appeared legitimate and professional, but the family said they caused more damage and charged them more than £30,000.

![Sue Taylor (left) and Sandra Taylor-Warren (right) were scammed out of £56,000 in total by three rogue traders.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21631876.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sue Taylor (left) and Sandra Taylor-Warren (right) were scammed out of £56,000 in total by three rogue traders. *(Image: Provided)*

Sandra said: “It was leaking into next door. The guttering wasn’t attached and the tiles were coming off. We were worried that the roof was going to come in.”

A third trader was brought in to fix the damage, but the problems worsened again, costing the family another £8,000.

The three traders were not connected, but the family fell victim to “bouncing the job” - minor repairs escalating into unnecessary work and mounting costs.

By the end of the ordeal, around £56,000 of Sue's savings had gone.

Sandra said: “It was mum’s money. I felt awful because it was me who’d gone forward with it all, thinking I was helping.”

After years of failed attempts to recover the money from the bank, Trading Standards referred the case to Safer Communities’ Financial Fraud Advocacy service.

Financial Fraud Advocate Vicky Beaumont helped the family pursue the refund.

Sandra said: “Vicky was amazing. She helped us get in touch with the bank and speak to the bank, and she knew what she was asking and what to do basically, and she guided me.

“I don’t think I could have done it without her.”

On July 14, 2026, Nationwide refunded the family’s full £56,000.

Sandra said the “horrendous” ordeal had taken a toll on her health, while mum Sue called the refund “a very great relief” after “a lot of sleepless nights”.

However, the ordeal has left the family afraid to trust legitimate roofers with the repairs still needed at their home.

Vicky said: “By the time people come to us, they may already have spent months or sometimes years trying to resolve what has happened.

“Fraud affects people emotionally as well as financially.”

Funded by Cleveland’s Office of the Police and Crime Commissioner and local Trading Standards teams, the Financial Fraud Advocacy service has helped victims recover more than £1.5 million.
