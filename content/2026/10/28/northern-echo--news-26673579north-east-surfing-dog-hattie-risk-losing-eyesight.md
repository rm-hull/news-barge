A fundraising appeal for a famous North East surfing dog at risk of losing her sight has raised more than £8,200 for life-changing eye surgery.

Twelve-year-old cocker spaniel Hattie has won hearts across the region with her surfing exploits on the Yorkshire coast.

Now, supporters have rallied around the much-loved pet to fund treatment for worsening cataracts after she was diagnosed with diabetes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490814.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie with Lisa, who set up the fundraiser that has already raised thousands *(Image: PROVIDED)*

A GoFundMe appeal set up by family friend Lisa Davis, from Middlesbrough, has reached £8,203, with further money raised through raffles and donations.

Hattie has already undergone specialist eye consultations and spent two weeks on eye drops to see whether they would help her condition.

The treatment involved four different eye drops, which had to be administered throughout the day.

Following her assessment, a consultant confirmed surgery could improve her eyesight.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490815.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie with her eye medication *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Surgery on both eyes costs £6,100, while the money raised covers the operation and two weeks of aftercare.

About £8,200 has been raised to cover this surgery and other treatments Hattie has previously had.

However, Lisa explained that appointments after the initial two weeks of aftercare would normally cost around £125 each - therefore more fundraising still needs to be done.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490809.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie, the 12-year-old famous surfing pooch *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Owner Andrew has secured a discount for the appointments, bringing the cost down to about £80 per appointment, and donations and raffle ticket sales have already raised the first £80.

Remaining raffle prizes include two nights at Lyndale Apartments in Windermere, a three-night stay at Sunflower Lodge at Primrose Valley, bottles of spirits and wine, Hattie cushions and several canvases featuring the famous dog.

Hattie became well known through regular visits to Bridlington, where she holidays twice a year with owner Andrew Bacon.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490816.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie and her owner Andrew *(Image: PROVIDED)*

She is best known for surfing on her board at Wilsthorpe Beach and has become a familiar face around the seaside town.

Andrew took early retirement to care for Hattie full-time after her diabetes diagnosis because he feared she could suffer a dangerous hypoglycaemic episode alone.

He also bought her a pram so she could enjoy daily walks and sunshine despite her deteriorating eyesight.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490808.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie, the 12-year-old famous surfing pooch *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Hattie was named Greater Manchester’s top hero dog of lockdown after helping Andrew at work and bringing smiles to people on social media.

Lisa previously described Hattie as an “amazing helper” during lockdown.

She said: “She was an amazing helper during lockdown, helping her daddy at work, so now it is our turn to pay back for all her hard work and dedication.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490810.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie at her dad's work *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Andrew and Lisa said they were “totally overwhelmed and humbled” by the support for Hattie.

In an earlier appeal update, they said: “The specialist treatment is very expensive and we have been totally overwhelmed and humbled by the kindness and generosity of everyone that has donated to the fund which will be an enormous financial help.”

Lisa hopes the treatment will help Hattie recognise her friends in Bridlington when she visits next summer.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21490811.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hattie the famous pooch that brought smiles to lots across lockdown from her social media page *(Image: PROVIDED)*
