The UK Government has committed £6 million to get plans for a new outdoor events arena beside the Hydro and Armadillo venues in Glasgow off the ground.

The funding, part of its Budget spending commitments, will help pay for the creation of a 10,000-capacity facility which will be used for open-air concerts, events and festivals.

The support, which was announced by Chancellor John Healey in his Budget speech, is expected to unlock the first phase of a long-term transformation of part of the Scottish Event Campus, which already welcomes more than 2.5 million visitors every year.

The funding was secured weeks after The Herald revealed ambitions to “reimagine” the entire site as a new riverside “destination” and “city quarter” through the creation of new indoor and outdoor arenas, a creative industries hub, waterfront shops, bars and restaurants, and a new viewing platform or podium above the Clyde.

Chief executive Dominic McKay said the long-term vision was aimed at ensuring Glasgow can keep pace with the likes of Melbourne, Lisbon, Bilbao, New York and London.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21437290.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Undeveloped land currently used for car parking will be turned into a new outdoor arena beside the Hydro and Armadillo venues at the Scottish Event Campus in Glasgow. *(Image: Holmes Miller Architects)*

A new masterplan is aimed at “unlocking” around 60 per cent of the 64 acre site, which is still undeveloped and is largely used for car parking.

This includes the site earmarked for the new outdoor arena, which is said to be around the same size as George Square.

The Scotland Office said the new open-air venue would allow the Scottish Event Campus to "go bigger and better" by chasing festivals, outdoor gigs and public events which are "currently out of reach".

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21437291.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A new outdoor arena and viewing platform will be created opposite the Hydro arena under expansion plans for the Scottish Event Campus in Glasgow. Image: Holmes Miller *(Image: Holmes Miller/Scottish Event Campus)*

It is hoped the long-term vision for the campus - which dates back more than 40 years - will boost the annual economic impact of the site from the current level of around £605m to more than £1 billion.

In an exclusive interview with The Herald last month, Mr McKay said the use of the space outside the Hydro and the Armadillo for the Commonwealth games had demonstrated the future potential of the site, which is normally only used for car parking, but was temporarily transformed into a fan zone for the sporting event.

Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander said: “Glasgow built the stage for COP26 and the Commonwealth Games, and this investment will help build the next one.

“By backing the SEC's ambitious expansion plans with a £6 million commitment, the UK Government is helping to boost Scotland's economy, create jobs, show the world Scotland means business, and ensure Glasgow remains a leading global events city.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21432696.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A fan zone was created outside the Armadillo and the Hydro when the Scottish Event Campus hosted the Commonwealth Games this summer. *(Image: Colin Mearns/frame/Herald Picture Agency)*

Mr McKay said: “This is extremely welcome news and a significant endorsement from the UK Government of the vital role the SEC plays in underpinning Glasgow's and Scotland's position as a global events and conference destination.

“The SEC generates more than £605m in economic value, welcomes 2.5 million visitors annually and supports 5,300 jobs.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21432674.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An overhead view of what the Scottish Event Campus in Glasgow currently looks like. *(Image: Scottish Event Campus)*

“This welcome support, along with our wider campus development plans, will allow us to not only significantly enhance our economic and social contribution, but to also secure the SEC's and Scotland’s position as a leading international venue for live entertainment, conferences and exhibitions for decades to come.

“The UK Government’s £6 million funding will help deliver the first phase of our plans to develop our campus for the next generation."

Glasgow City Council leader Ricky Bell said: “The Scottish Event Campus is already a real asset to Glasgow and hugely important to the city’s ability to successfully host major events – which we saw again this summer, when it played an absolutely key role in the delivery of a great Commonwealth Games.

“But we all know that we face increasing competition from other cities across the globe, that are now investing in their venues. To stay ahead, Glasgow needs to be on the front foot and build on what it already has.

“This investment has the potential to really kick-start the wider development of the campus – and I’m delighted to see the Chancellor back the hard work and that vision the team at the SEC has.”

Speaking to The Herald last month, Mr McKay admitted that the three existing buildings at the campus - the original SEC Centre, the Hydro and the Armadillo – were now “creaking at the seams”.

At the time, Mr McKay said he hoped that private investment would provide the bulk of the funding for the project, which is planned to be carried out in phases, is expected to take more than a decade to complete and is aimed at attracting at least another 500,000 visitors to the campus each year.

He said at the time: "We had 2.5 million people come through the campus last year, which was a record number for us. They contributed more than £600m to the Scottish economy.

“That is a significant return for Glasgow and Scotland. It is something we are proud of but we are really keen to build on it.

"We have been enormously successful over the last 40 years, but we need to think about what the future may hold.

“We have quite a lot of space around our three wonderful buildings. Around 60 per cent of our campus has not been developed. We are having a good think about how to reimagine our whole campus and what that space could look like in future.”

The new vision has been led by Mr McKay, who was appointed at the start of 2025, after previous proposals for an £80 million expansion of the original SEC Centre, which were heavily reliant on public funding, were shelved.

He has pointed to the success of the retail and leisure facilities around The O2 in London and suggested the SEC aimed to make much more of its riverside location.
