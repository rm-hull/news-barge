An MP has called for a ban on e-bike conversion kits following a rise in "catastrophic" injuries linked to illegal vehicles.

Anna Dixon, MP for Shipley, raised the issue in a parliamentary debate last Wednesday highlighting concerns from residents, police and healthcare professionals in her constituency.

The Government says such e-bikes "have no place on our roads" and add that police have been given stronger powers to seize vehicles without needing to issue a warning.

## Catastrophic injuries rise

Ms Dixon said she recently spoke with an A&E doctor who had seen a rise in catastrophic injuries, particularly to children and young people.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21632326.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shipley MP Anna Dixon *(Image: Jamie Parkinson)*

Ms Dixon said "get to the source and ban conversion kits to stop these deadly bikes."

The MP described illegal e-motorbikes as "a blight" on her constituency and said police officers had identified them as the vehicle of choice for organised criminals.

She called on ministers in the Department for Business and Trade to ban non-compliant conversion kits from sale as soon as possible.

## Calls to tighten the law

Ms Dixon supported the Government’s plans to give police stronger powers to seize and destroy illegal bikes, including those stored in garages and lock-ups.

She described these measures as a proportionate way to tackle the problem at its source.

A Government spokesperson said, "E-bikes that have been modified to be faster and more powerful than the rules allow are motorbikes, and unless they have been registered, taxed, and insured, they have no place on our roads.

“We’ve given police stronger powers to seize vehicles without needing to issue a warning, and we are cracking down on unsafe products sold online.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20811994.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An e scooter in Bradford city centre *(Image: T&A)*

Her comments follow a letter she sent to the Secretary of State for Business and Trade, urging the Government to use powers under the Product Regulation and Metrology Act 2025 to designate illegal e-bikes and conversion kits as "unsafe products."

This would give Trading Standards the authority to act at the point of sale.

Ms Dixon also called for binding rules requiring online marketplaces to verify sellers and proactively remove listings for non-compliant kits.

She urged the closure of the "off-road" loophole that allows products capable of exceeding legal speed limits to be sold for off-road use.
