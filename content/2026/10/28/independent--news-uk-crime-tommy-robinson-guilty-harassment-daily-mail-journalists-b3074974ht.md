Far-right activist Tommy Robinson has been warned he could face jail after being found guilty of harassing two journalists from the Daily Mail and causing them to fear violence following a trial at Southwark Crown Court.

Robinson, 43, who told his trial his full name is Stephen Lennon, was labelled a “bully” by prosecutors after freelance reporter Andrew Young and Daily Mail editor Jacob Dirnhuber felt forced to either leave their homes or employ extra security due to concerns over a “vigilante” attack.

Jurors took 11 hours and 25 minutes to deliberate before finding Robinson guilty by a majority of 10 jurors to two of both charges of harassment causing fear of violence.

A judge said he would be considering “all options” with the offence carrying a maximum sentence of up to 10 years in prison.

Robinson’s trial heard how he “retaliated” after the Daily Mail and its sister publication the MailOnline published a story about him “stoking riots from his sunbed” during unrest in the UK in August 2024 following the murders of three girls in Southport.

The anti-migrant activist made a series of posts on social media about the unfolding situation while on holiday in Cyprus, prompting the newspaper to send Mr Young and a photographer to the island.

![Robinson has been found guilty of harassing two Daily Mail journalists](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/28/13/2302379233...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Once the MailOnline had published its story about him, Robinson hit out at Mr Young and Mr Dirnhuber on his X account because of their involvement in the article.

Robinson went on to hire private investigators to find the home addresses of his victims, as well as the editor-in-chief of the Daily Mail Ted Verity.

The jury panel accepted the prosecution’s case that the decision to hire detectives was designed to “intimidate” his victims, with photographs of Mr Dirnhuber outside his home and cropped images of Mr Young’s family posted online by Robinson.

As well as social media posts and direct messages to Mr Dirnhuber, Robinson also became “increasingly menacing” on a telephone call with Mr Young on 5 August, 2024 – telling the freelance journalist: “I’m coming to get you.”

Jurors rejected claims from the activist that the Daily Mail had put his family in danger by publishing the story, with the defendant weeping in the witness box on Thursday as he spoke about the relationship he had with his children.

Prosecutor Ben Holt previously told the court Mr Young was given a close protection officer by the Daily Mail after photographs of his wife and children were posted on Robinson’s X account.

Mr Dirnhuber had to move house because of the harassment and Daily Mail correspondent George Odling also felt the need to leave his family home after becoming concerned with the publicity surrounding his colleagues.

Mr Verity also provided evidence to police of a voicemail he received from Robinson, who told him: “I’ve already located every f\*\*\*\*\*\* one of you.”

The activist posted on X before the trial began to thank Tesla owner Elon Musk for funding his legal fees.

![Robinson thanked Elon Musk for funding his legal fees](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/28/12/2203703206...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A spokesman for the Daily Mail said: “We welcome today’s verdict and are pleased that Stephen Yaxley-Lennon has been found guilty of harassment causing fear of violence to two of our journalists.

“Yaxley-Lennon’s behaviour was reprehensible and had grave consequences for our reporters and their families.

“Through his actions, Yaxley-Lennon sought to undermine our right to hold him to account. We stand by our journalists and their freedom to report legitimately in the public interest.”

Frank Ferguson, head of the Crown Prosecution Service’s special crime and counter-terrorism division, said Robinson had “weaponised social media” in his campaign of harassment.

He said: “The evidence in this case showed that he targeted the victims directly and indirectly through social media and by telephone. He also published images relating not only to them, but also their families.

“The jury heard from the victims and witnesses in this case, ultimately deciding that this behaviour crossed the line into criminality. Nobody should be made to fear violence because of the work they do.

“Journalists play an important role in a democratic society and should be able to carry out their professional duties without being subjected to harassment.”

Robinson was granted bail ahead of his sentencing on 16 December.
