A fresh take on faith, rebellion, and purpose arrives on stage in an award-winning comedy.

Poor Clare, written by American playwright Chiara Atik, will be performed at Theatre@41 in York from November 3 to 7, offering a modern retelling of the early life of St Clare of Assisi.

Set in 13th-century Italy, the play follows Clare, a young noblewoman whose encounter with Francis of Assisi leads her to question her privileged life and embrace a path of service and sacrifice.

Jim Paterson, director of the production, said: "I was hooked from the moment I first read Poor Clare.

"Chiara Atik has crafted a funny and unique play that makes Clare’s story come to life whilst also posing questions for us about our own attitudes and responsibilities when it comes to questions of poverty and inequality."

The play uses contemporary, witty dialogue to explore timeless themes and has already won critical acclaim, including the 2022 American Theatre Critic Association's New Play Award.

Isabel Azar, who plays Clare, said: "Clare is such a deeply relatable role.

"She’s a young woman figuring out her vocation, and she’s so raw and honest.

"I love her integrity, her fervent faith, and her intense hunger and thirst for righteousness."

The cast and creative team are part of Black Treacle Theatre, whose recent productions include Anne Boleyn and Educating Rita.

Poor Clare begins its York run on Tuesday, November 3, with evening performances at 7.30pm and a Saturday matinee at 2.30pm.

Tickets are available at tickets.41monkgate.co.uk.
