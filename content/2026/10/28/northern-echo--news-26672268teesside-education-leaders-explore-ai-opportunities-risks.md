Education leaders across Teesside have joined forces to explore how artificial intelligence can be used in schools and colleges while protecting young people from its potential risks.

The inaugural meeting of the Tees Digital Exchange, held at Middlesbrough’s Riverside Stadium, brought together headteachers, CEOs, researchers, and teaching staff from across the region to discuss the impact of AI on education and how it can be harnessed collaboratively for the benefit of students.

Dr Nichole Munro, CEO of Atomix Educational Trust, said: "This is the beginning of a conversation about what we might collectively build together in the Tees Valley."

She added AI was "here and part of our everyday life."

Organisations are already making decisions about its use.

She said: "AI presents some real opportunities and also some very serious questions."

The event was organised by Atomix Educational Trust in partnership with the Association of Education Advisers and the Institute of AI Education.

Dr Munro emphasised that this was not just another conference but the start of an ongoing, region-wide collaboration.

She said: "Can it improve teaching and learning? Can it reduce workload for our staff? How do we prepare young people for a labour market in which AI is going to be commonplace?

"How do we protect our young people, academic integrity, and our data?

"How can we use it to reduce inequality rather than deepen it?"

She was clear that these are not questions any single institution can answer alone.

She said: "None of those questions can be answered by one school, one college, one Trust or one business and we should not strive for answers alone."

Dr Munro described the Tees Valley as having "extraordinary assets" in the form of schools, colleges, Teesside University, a growing digital sector, and experienced educational leaders.

She said the Tees Digital Exchange aims to unite these resources to address the challenges and opportunities AI presents in education, with the goal of building a "regional ecosystem" and a "regional hub of digital excellence."

She said: "We’d like to see a teacher experimenting with AI in one school sharing what they’ve learned across the region, researchers working alongside teachers, and businesses helping educational institutions understand how the world of work is changing."

Attendees were invited to share their aspirations for how AI could be implemented in their institutions, with their feedback collected by Dr Beth Lane and Narinder Gill of the Institute of AI Education.

This information will be used in their research and fed back to delegates and the wider Tees Valley educational ecosystem.

During a panel discussion, Ms Gill explained that the idea for the Institute of AI Education emerged when ChatGPT was introduced in her trust.

She said: "We’ve got 350 staff in 18 schools across North Yorkshire, the smallest has 22 pupils and the largest has 450, and we needed something to connect our schools together on how to utilise this technology and understand it a bit more."

She said there was "a lot of noise around AI and people not knowing what to do with it – or buying tools and not really knowing what they were for."

She said: "The Institute is something we want to stand the test of time as something that will continue to serve our youngest people, because we saw an issue, we saw a concern, where people were talking about AI and we wanted to bring those voices together."

Les Walton CBE also spoke at the event, highlighting the importance of building equitable education systems that listen to and learn from those within them.

He said effective leadership involves connecting with stakeholders and taking their ideas forward in a meaningful way.

Al Kingsley MBE, another panel member, discussed the questions schools and trusts should be asking before adopting AI tools.

He said: "We assume conversations around AI will be somehow different to broader digital conversations and when it comes to introducing any digital technology, we should be asking, 'why are we doing it?'"

He emphasised the importance of clarity and focus.

He said: "Then once we know why we are doing it, how we will measure it?"

He added it’s much better "to do one or two things well when you look at a digital journey" than introduce lots of different tools and processes.

Dr Mansoor Soomro, author of The Generalist Advantage, also addressed delegates, arguing that generalists — individuals with broad skill sets and experience across multiple fields — are particularly well-placed to shape the future workforce in an AI-driven world.

Through interactive discussions, he demonstrated how diverse experience and adaptability will be critical in navigating the evolving demands of the workplace.

Dr Munro reiterated that the Tees Digital Exchange is not a finished model but a call to collective action.
