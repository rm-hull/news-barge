**A former presiding officer of the Scottish Parliament has been honoured for her “lasting contribution to Scottish public life”.**

Alison Johnstone, who has also served as a Lothian MSP and City of Edinburgh councillor, received an honorary doctorate from Edinburgh Napier University on Tuesday.

Born and raised in Edinburgh, Ms Johnstone began her political career as an assistant to Robin Harper MSP, the first Green parliamentarian elected in the UK.

She served as a councillor for Meadows/Morningside ward between 2007 and 2012, and was elected to the Scottish Parliament via the Lothian regional list in 2011, 2016, and 2021.

![Presiding Officer Alison Johnstone.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20758215.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Former presiding officer Alison Johnstone. *(Image: Duncan McGlynn)*

In May 2021, she was elected to the non-party political role of presiding officer, relinquishing her affiliation with the Scottish Greens to do so.

“I'm delighted to be awarded an honorary doctorate from Edinburgh Napier University,” Ms Johnstone said on Tuesday.

“It’s extremely special to be awarded alongside so many students celebrating the fulfilment of their hard work.

“The impact of Edinburgh Napier's teaching and research is recognised globally and I am thrilled to be awarded such an honour by an institution that I hold in the highest regard, and which has such a significant influence on the life of the region I have lived in and represented throughout my working life.”

Ms Johnstone was awarded the degree during an autumn graduation ceremony for Napier’s School of Arts and Creative Industries and School of Computing, Engineering & the Built Environment at the Usher Hall.

“I first became actively involved in politics when I joined neighbours campaigning to save school playing fields, just a few minutes from Napier’s Craiglockhart Campus.

“I have been privileged to serve as a City of Edinburgh councillor, as a member of the Scottish Parliament and as presiding officer,” she added.

“I have greatly appreciated the opportunity to work with others determined to ensure that all voices are heard and that barriers to equal representation and participation are removed. A thriving democracy demands this.

“This honour will drive me on and I will continue to do what I can to help ensure that those who may struggle most to be heard, have that opportunity.”

Presenting the award to Ms Johnstone, Edinburgh Napier strategic advisor Thomas Feige remarked:

“Throughout her career, Alison Johnstone has demonstrated a profound belief that democracy is strengthened when all voices can be heard.

“Her leadership has helped to foster respectful debate, support democratic accountability and promote a more inclusive political culture.

“In doing so, she has made a lasting contribution to Scottish public life and has become an inspiring role model for future generations, and particularly for women seeking leadership roles in public service.”

![Should Presiding Officer Alison Johnstone be asked to come up with a new procedure for referring ministers to an independent adviser?](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/17482339.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ms Johnstone served as presiding officer between 2021 and 2026. *(Image: PA)*

This year's graduation celebrations come as Edinburgh Napier is in the midst of a funding crunch.

University bosses are looking to sell the institution's Merchiston campus, which they say is “no longer fit for the future”.

Last summer, the university announced plans to cut up to 70 jobs amid a budget shortfall.

In May 2026, The Herald revealed that almost 200 “core staff” had left the institution since the proposals were made public in August 2025.

According to a Freedom of Information request, 191 employees left the university during the eight-month period.

Fifty-five people departed via “targeted voluntary redundancy”, accepting “enhanced terms” after their jobs had been identified as potentially at risk.

Speaking to The Herald ahead of her departure from Holyrood prior to the 2026 parliamentary elections, Ms Johnstone raised concerns about growing polarisation in Scottish politics.

“I think politics, and I suppose our national discourse generally, has been impacted by social media.

“People aren’t taking the opportunity to reflect and that means you’re not having those nuanced discussions,” she said.

“It can feel very, very polarised at times and I know from my conversations, with young men as well as young women, sometimes [politics] feels like a hostile space and one that they may not want to be involved in.”
