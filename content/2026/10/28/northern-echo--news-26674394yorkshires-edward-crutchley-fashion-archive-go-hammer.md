More than 100 lots of rare designer fashion assembled by Yorkshire-born designer Edward Crutchley are going under the hammer, including pieces from major luxury labels.

The Edward Crutchley Research Archive will be auctioned at Tennants Auctioneers on November 20, with pieces from Louis Vuitton and Christian Dior.

Edward Crutchley said: "Throughout my career I have collected not simply beautiful objects, but sources of inspiration.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21638090.jpg?type=mds-article-620) Neon green oblique Silk oversized shirt *(Image: Supplied)*

"Every textile, garment, book and artefact has been acquired because it taught me something about the history of dress, the ingenuity of its makers or the cultural stories woven into cloth.

"It is the working archive that informed my practice as a designer, and it is my hope that these objects will continue to inspire their next custodians, just as they have inspired me."

A standout lot is a pair of Air Dior x Air Jordan 1 High trainers designed by Kim Jones for Air Jordan’s 35th anniversary.

The trainers are expected to sell for between £5,000 and £8,000.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21638091.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Japanese robes *(Image: Supplied)*

Launched at Dior Men’s Pre-Fall 2020 show in Miami, the collaboration was limited to 8,500 pairs, many reserved for Dior VIP clients.

Another rare lot is a Louis Vuitton x Supreme Monogram Red Denim Christmas Stocking, designed by Kim Jones for their groundbreaking 2017 collaboration.

Only three of the stockings are known to exist; Kim Jones and Supreme’s former owner James Jebbia own the other two. The lot is estimated at £800 to £1,200.

The auction also features Dior Homme pieces from its collaboration with Lewis Hamilton, including a Neon Green Oblique Silk Oversized Shirt and Short Set from the Fall 2025 Capsule Collection, estimated at £600 to £800.

The collection blended luxury, sport and cultural identity with traditional craftsmanship, African influences and Afrofuturism.

The archive also includes textiles and garments from around the world, reflecting Crutchley’s fascination with global fashion traditions.

Highlights include a Hinggi Ikat Cloth from East Sumba, Indonesia, featuring Dutch colonial-inspired heraldic crests and estimated at £100 to £200, plus two Japanese Formal Silk Furisode from the mid-to-late 20th century, each estimated at £100 to £200.

Mr Crutchley said: "People often think fashion is about trends or taste.

"I have never really believed that.

"At its best, clothing is one of the most immediate records of human culture we possess.

"It tells us who people were, what they valued and how they wanted to be seen."

Crutchley grew up in the Yorkshire Dales and fell in love with textiles after a school art project.

He studied at Central Saint Martins, worked with Betty Jackson and joined Pringle of Scotland under Claire Waight Keller, where he developed his passion for luxury fabrics and craftsmanship.

He spent 11 years in Paris designing prints and textiles for Louis Vuitton under Marc Jacobs and Kim Jones while working with artisans worldwide.

Travels through Japan, China and Indonesia helped shape the archive now heading to auction.

While working in Paris, Crutchley launched his own label, which showed at London Fashion Week for more than 10 years.

Lady Gaga, Harry Styles and Nicole Kidman have worn his designs, which have also been exhibited at the V&A and at Kensington Palace.

In 2019, he became the first designer to win both the International Woolmark Prize and its Innovation Award.

Highlights are on display at Tennants’ galleries, while the full archive can be viewed at its Leyburn salerooms on November 18 and 19.
