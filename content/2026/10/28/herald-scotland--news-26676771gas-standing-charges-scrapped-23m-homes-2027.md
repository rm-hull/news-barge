Some Scottish households could end up paying more for gas under the Chancellor's plan to scrap standing charges, the SNP has warned.

The Treasury confirmed this morning that John Healey will announce in today's Budget that standing charges will be removed from gas bills for around 23 million homes.

A standing charge is a fixed daily fee added to a bill however much gas is used. It averages £108 a year for a typical household. The Chancellor will ask Ofgem to remove the charge in 2027, or as soon as possible after.

The Treasury says the fixed costs will instead be recovered through the unit rate, the price paid for each unit of gas used.

It says that could save 60% of account-holding households an average of £45 a year.

That means households that use the least gas gain the most, and those that use the most could pay more.

![Based on the energy use of a typical domestic household, from July the price cap will rise by £18 a month](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/14661266.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: PA)*

The Treasury says those who do not see an automatic saving will still have better control over their bills, and that it will set out "targeted mitigation for a small number of households" in due course.

However, SNP Westminster leader Dave Doogan said that given the colder weather north of the border, the change could push up bills for most Scots.

Posting on X, he wrote: "Wait a minute, on gas prices...

"By moving the cost of the standing charge on to the unit rate — has Andy Burnham actually just put up energy bills for the majority of Scots?

"We are colder country & have to put the heating on more, the more gas we use, the higher price we pay."

He added: "The Labour Party has just made our reality even worse — their plan will make Scots pay more for our gas.

"Westminster and Treasury are making billions from Scotland's gas while making our people pay more. Time to put Scotland's energy in Scotland's hands."

A Labour source accused Mr Doogan of hypocrisy as he had previously argued for moving standing charges on to unit prices.

In a Commons exchange in February 2024, the SNP MP said that "if costs and charges were redistributed to the unit price, consumers would be empowered to pursue reduced usage, knowing that that would translate into lower bills".

However, Tory shadow energy minister Andrew Bowie raised a similar concern to Mr Doogan, saying anyone who is a "higher consumer", using gas for heating, cooking and hot water, would see their bill go up.

Mr Bowie said: "Next week in large parts of the UK, the temperature is going to get close to freezing. Because of these changes, if you live there and heat your home with gas and want to turn on the central heating, Labour just made your life much more difficult and far more expensive."

Meanwhile, the End Fuel Poverty Coalition welcomed the move for the relief it could bring to low-income households that use little energy. But co-ordinator Simon Francis warned that "millions of households won't see a saving", and said ministers have "serious questions to answer" about how vulnerable households with high energy needs will be protected.

Octopus Energy chief executive Greg Jackson, who is quoted in the Treasury's release, said most customers would see a cut in their gas bills. He said those who could move to heat pumps would save even more.

Mr Healey said: "Gas standing charges are unfair and outdated so I am scrapping them to give millions of households a bit of breathing space."
