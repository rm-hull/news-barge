A Scottish retail chain has revealed a “modest” sales rise to almost £74 million.

The Glasgow-registered company that lists prominent businessman Stefan King as the ultimate controlling party and has around 50 stores also reported a drop in profit of £1m.

One-O-One Convenience Stores Limited reported turnover of £73.87m in the year to March 31, 2026, set against £73.76m the year before.

Gross profit rose to £18.2m from £16.4m, which the company attributed to changes in strategy across its stores. Profit after tax was £2.9m, compared with £4.2m in the prior period.

The firm is also “actively seeking out and considering new sites for potential investment” and “investing in existing stores”.

The directors said in their strategic report: “In 2026, the company delivered a robust financial performance in light of a challenging retail backdrop, underpinned by disciplined commercial management and operational efficiency.”

They said that “despite modest sales growth, we have encouragingly expanded both gross profit and gross margin”, the latter to 24.6% from 22.2%.

This was through “strategic change in our category sales mix”.

### **Legislative change**

The directors also said: “Performance in the year has been particularly impacted by legislative change, notably the ban in single use vape sales and illegal tobacco trading, the latter of which we are actively engaged with government on tackling.”

Operating profit in the year was £3m, set against £4m. The directors are “satisfied with the performance of the business”.

The directors said: “The management team is committed to further enhancing the company’s status in the sector by continuing to actively seek out and consider new sites for potential investment and, at the same time, investing in existing stores thus ensuring that the company’s portfolio maintains its appeal to customers.

“Based on the forecasting performed the directors are confident that the actions and strategies in place which will allow the business to continue to trade strongly. Further, the directors have assessed the business risks and are confident that actions and strategies are in place to mitigate these and ensure continued trading.”

The company said the principal risk to its business is competition from local supermarkets.

“The directors are committed to continuing the company's policy of regularly reviewing product mix and pricing to ensure that all customers continue to be provided with the best local products available and at a pricing level suited to all budgets and tastes,” the directors said.

“The company business has been operating under the same family ownership for 35 years,” they added: “In considering all key decisions, the preservation and enhancement of long-term asset value are priorities of the directors.”

The immediate parent undertaking is Scotsman Group Plc, the pubs and clubs company that recently reported a decrease in profit despite a rise in sales.

**The Scotsman Group Plc**, founded by entrepreneur Stefan King, posted recent results showing revenue was £61m in the last year, up from £58m the year before, a rise of over 5%, but there was an overall drop in profit from £2.4m to £427,528.

Its related subsidiaries include the Corinthian Clubs Limited, Iona Pub Partnership (Scotland) Limited, Café Drummond (**Aberdeen**) Limited and Scotsman Hospitality Limited.
