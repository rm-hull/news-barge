Volunteers who have helped transform the world’s oldest railway into one of the region’s leading visitor attractions have received County Durham’s highest civic honour.

Durham County Council has awarded the Chairman’s Medal to the dedicated team at Tanfield Railway during its 300th anniversary year, recognising their decades of service and commitment.

Tanfield Railway has been run by volunteers for over 50 years, with 218 people involved, aged between 14 and 94.

Durham County Council chairman Cllr Rodiss said: "I am delighted to recognise all those who help to preserve Tanfield Railway, particularly in this special anniversary year.

"Their extraordinary contribution to their local community, the wider county, and to the heritage sector is hugely appreciated, with their efforts helping to attract visitors from far and wide and boost the county’s economy."

The volunteers have helped transform the railway into a three-mile heritage line that now welcomes around 30,000 visitors each year.

It is now one of the county’s leading visitor attractions and one of the foremost industrial heritage railways in the country.

Cllr Rodiss also highlighted the broader impact of the railway, noting its role in supporting the health and wellbeing of volunteers, offering upskilling opportunities, and helping individuals find new career paths.

He said: "I’d like to share a special thank you to all volunteers, past and present, who have helped support the railway and our local community."
