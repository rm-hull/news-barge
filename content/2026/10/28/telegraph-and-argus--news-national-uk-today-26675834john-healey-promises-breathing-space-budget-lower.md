John Healey has said he will use his first Budget to give households and businesses “breathing space today” from everyday costs while taking action to bring down energy bills for good in the future.

The Chancellor is expected to announce a freeze in fuel duty as well as plans for longer-term reforms as he seeks to make Britain “more resilient” from global shocks.

In a video message before he delivers the autumn statement on Wednesday, Mr Healey insisted the Government would move to ease the cost of living “responsibly” by sticking to Labour’s self-imposed fiscal rules.

He said: “Breathing space for today and fresh hope for the future. That’s what today’s Budget will deliver.

“The world’s a highly uncertain place. Conflicts abroad are pushing up energy prices, and the cost of living here at home – piling pressure on families, on businesses, and on our public finances.

“But Britain’s economy is showing real signs of resilience: investments up, productivity is growing, and we had the joint fastest growth in the G7 in the first half of this year.”

Mr Healey added: “This Budget is designed to give households breathing space from some of the everyday costs, provide targeted support for businesses and self-employed people, and to start the long-term action needed to get energy costs down for good.”

The Chancellor will have limited scope for big giveaways because of the stretched public finances, made worse by international market turmoil caused by the Iran war, and little has been leaked ahead of his statement on Wednesday.

He is sticking with the rules set by predecessor Rachel Reeves, requiring day-to-day spending to be financed by taxes rather than borrowing by 2029/30 and that public debt as a share of the economy should fall in the same year.

But changes aimed at easing the cost of living are set to feature heavily, with Mr Healey expected to cancel January’s planned increase in fuel duty, keeping the rate of the levy frozen for the 16th year at 52p per litre, as first reported by The Sun.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has also ordered the Chancellor and Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh to examine scrapping standing charges from gas bills.

He wants to break the link to stop the price of wholesale gas setting electricity bills too, even though the fossil fuel only makes up a small percentage of total generation.

The Chancellor is also expected to end the Carers’ Allowance cliff edge which has penalised unpaid carers for going over their earnings limit, even by as little as a few pence per week.

As part of a series of measures billed as “everyday fixes,” ministers have announced plans to make a higher tax-free mileage allowance of 55p permanent, and to slash tariffs on a range of popular foods, including chocolate, beans and gherkins, to make them cheaper for shoppers.

The Chancellor is also set to announce a rise in the minimum wage by nearly 4%, while ending the trend of hiking the national minimum wage for those aged 18 to 20 by more than the rate for older workers, raising it instead roughly in line with the rate for over 21-year-olds, according to reports.

In his message ahead of the landmark statement, Mr Healey insisted that “in an uncertain world, we will strengthen national security and our defences, as well as our support for Ukraine”.

He said: “We’ll back British businesses and entrepreneurs, we’ll invest in the technologies that drive future growth, and we’ll build more homes, shift power out of Whitehall, so that leaders locally can drive growth in every postcode.

“We’ll do all of this responsibly, sticking within tight fiscal rules, and make sure every pound is well invested.

“Because this Budget is about getting through this difficult moment. But it’s also about putting Britain on a future path, a better future path.”
