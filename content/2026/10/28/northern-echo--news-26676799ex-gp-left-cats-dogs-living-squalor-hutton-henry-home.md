A former GP has been banned from keeping pets after inspectors found her cats and dogs living in squalor, with faeces, urine and rubbish littering the floors of her County Durham home.

Nicola Stirk, of Hutton Henry near Peterlee, has been disqualified from keeping cats and dogs for two years after failing to provide a suitable environment for her two dogs and two cats.

The RSPCA had previously warned Stirk about the conditions after they began to deteriorate. Although a follow-up visit found improvements, RSPCA Inspector Rowena Proctor visited on February 3 after concerns the situation had worsened.

She described a filthy property where a sofa had been chewed beyond use, rubbish was piled feet high in a bedroom, and a bath was black and brown with dirt.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641133.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The home covered in litter and faeces *(Image: RSPCA)*

The smell inside was described as “dreadful”.

In a written statement to the court, she said: “Upon entering the front door, the passageway was littered with faeces and rubbish. The sofa had been chewed by the dogs to the point it was unusable. The floors throughout, bar one small area in the living room, were completely covered in rubbish, faeces and urine.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641134.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The home covered in litter and faeces *(Image: RSPCA)*

“The pathway through to the kitchen and the kitchen itself was the same - faeces in almost every single place you could look and every surface and area was just filthy.”

The spare bedroom was unusable because rubbish was piled a couple of feet high, while a litter tray in the bathroom was surrounded by a build-up of faeces.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641132.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The home covered in litter and faeces *(Image: RSPCA)*

A vet later examined the animals and reviewed footage and images of the property, concluding they were suffering or likely to suffer if their circumstances did not change.

The four animals were removed and signed over to the RSPCA.

The cats, Charlie and Maddy, recovered and were rehomed by the charity’s Coventry and District Branch.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641135.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Charlie the cat *(Image: RSPCA)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641137.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Maddy the cat *(Image: RSPCA)*

Mabel the Doberman and Anya the Borzoi were taken to the RSPCA’s Felledge Animal Centre near Chester-le-Street.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641131.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mabel recovering at the RSPCA's Felledge Animal Centre *(Image: RSPCA)*

Anya needed extensive rehabilitation after arriving withdrawn and struggling with everyday life. Staff spent months building her confidence, with help from clinical behaviourists and specialist medication.

Deputy centre manager Luka Atkinson said: “It took a long time to see progress but with the help of clinical behaviourists and some specialist medication we got there. We’re over the moon at how far Anya has come.”

Anya now has a new home lined up with a male Borzoi she has already met several times, while Mabel was quickly rehomed.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641129.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anya recovering at the RSPCA's Felledge Animal Centre *(Image: RSPCA)*

Stirk was also handed a 15-month community order after admitting an animal welfare offence at Teesside Magistrates Court. She was also ordered to pay £900 in costs and a £114 victim surcharge.

The court heard in mitigation that she was suffering from mental health issues and wanted to get help.

Magistrates said they wanted her to receive support to help her lead a better quality of life.
