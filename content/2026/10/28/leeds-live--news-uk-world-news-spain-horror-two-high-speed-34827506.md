# Spain horror as two high-speed trains smash in rush hour with 46 among dead

## The two trains reportedly collided at around 9am this morning in Spain with carriages said to have derailed and fallen down an embankment

The Spanish authorities are reporting a major incident on the tracks this morning after two high-speed trains are said to have crashed during rush hour, with 'at least' 46 confirmed dead, and more than 175 people injured.

Two carriages reportedly plunged down an embankment following the horror smash at around 9am local time (8am UK time) this morning (Wednesday). The trains crashed as they travelled between the towns of Los Yébenes and Urda, in the province of Toledo.

The authorities warn the death toll could rise even further as emergency services continue to attend to the wreckage and the wounded. The death toll was confirmed to Sky News as emergency services continued to respond to the incident.

The accident involved an AVE high-speed train and an Iryo service. Spanish state broadcaster TVE reported that both trains were travelling in the same direction when one struck the rear of the other, knocking two carriages off the tracks.

Sources from the 112 emergency services said two carriages fell down an embankment following the collision.

Spain’s rail infrastructure operator ADIF confirmed the incident occurred on Wednesday morning, while rail operator Renfe announced that services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia had been suspended.

The trains involved were travelling to and from Seville, according to reports.

Manuel Galans, mayor of the nearby town of Urda, told TVE that passengers had been injured. Civil protection authorities had asked the town hall to prepare a sports pavilion to receive passengers affected by the crash.

Emergency medical support has also been sent to the scene. Antonio Sanz, Andalusia’s Minister of the Presidency, Health and Emergencies, announced on X that mobile intensive care units and a logistics support vehicle from the Andalusian Health Service (SAS) had been dispatched to help treat passengers.

A room is also being prepared at Madrid's Atocha train station to support relatives of potential victims and those injured in the crash.

Madrid Deputy Mayor Inmaculada Sanz said personnel from SAMUR, the city's emergency medical service, were on their way to the station to establish a support centre.

She said a similar arrangement had been put in place following the Adamuz train accident, when a dedicated room was used to provide care and psychosocial support to relatives and others affected. On that occasion, the room was located on the station's first floor, with access restricted to family members. It is not yet known whether the same arrangements will be used following Wednesday's crash.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said he was "deeply saddened" by the accident in Urda and expressed his "solidarity and support to the victims and their families" in a message posted on X.

He said emergency services were working at the scene to help those affected and ensure their safety.

The Spanish government has cancelled its schedule for Wednesday following the accident.

The crash comes after 45 people died in January when a train derailed in southern Spain and struck an oncoming train.
