**York Council criticised over empty homes and rough sleepers**

There is something absurd in the City of York Council report that rough sleepers could be housed in converted shipping containers and garages.

At this very moment a former care home off Walmgate is being torn down along with four garage blocks and a storage building with no prospect of new construction taking place soon after site clearance.

Willow House, barely 50 years old, was left empty and allowed to deteriorate for many years when it could have been utilised for housing rough sleepers until such time that funds were in place to proceed with a proposed new housing scheme on that site.

And speaking of new housing schemes there are over 100 brand new homes on Council sites at Burnholme Green and Duncombe Square which have long been completed but remain empty with no explanation other than “awaiting snagging”.

This is not good property management or a prudent attitude to taxpayers’ money. There is a distinct lack of joined up thinking at York Council.

*Matthew Laverack*

*Retired Architect*

*Lord Mayors Walk,*

*York*

**Gutter bliss**

I am sat in the bus shelter at Blossom Street. I had previously watched a cinema, nice film; waiting for a bus; but intrigued at the purple light that assailed my eyes opposite on the hotel window.

Abruptly my smell senses focused upon nothing! I am surprised because for years this revolting gutter smell from the sewerage at below street level was a vomiting experience.

An utter calm embraced me as I stepped upon my bus home.

My thanks to you York Council for fixing this multiple year-old problem.

**Single Market confusion**

I am confused. The news is reporting that Andy Burnham thinks a referendum would be needed for the UK to rejoin the European Union’s Single Market.

Why would that be? There wasn’t a referendum on leaving the Single Market.

Owen Patterson, a leading Tory Brexiteer said during the campaign “Only a madman would actually leave the market”.

Matthew Elliott, chief executive of Vote Leave, described the Norwegian or EEA option as potentially attractive. Norway is in the Single Market.

In the Telegraph, Boris Johnson wrote before the referendum “There will continue to be free trade, and access to the single market”.

No one ever voted on leaving the Single Market so why, now, would there need to be a referendum to take the UK back into the Single Market? I don’t get it. Anyone have any ideas?

*Christian Vassie*

*Blake Court, Wheldrake, York*
