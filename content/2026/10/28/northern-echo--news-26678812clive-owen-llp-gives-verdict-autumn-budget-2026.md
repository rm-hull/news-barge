A leading North East accountancy firm has welcomed the Chancellor's Autumn Budget as a "balanced" approach that could provide businesses with much-needed stability, despite concerns over fresh tax pressures.

Clive Owen LLP, which has offices in Darlington and Middlesbrough, has given its verdict on the Autumn Budget 2026, describing the announcements as "balanced rather than transformational".

David Baggaley, Head of Tax and Tax Partner at the firm, said the absence of many widely anticipated tax increases would be welcomed by businesses and individuals across the region.

However, he warned that changes affecting entrepreneurs, savers and employers could create additional challenges.

Mr Baggaley said: "The Autumn Budget is a significant one, being the first under Andy Burnham’s leadership, but I would describe it as balanced rather than transformational.

"Despite speculation ahead of the announcement about substantial changes to tax rates, many of those anticipated increases have not come about. That is welcome news and should reassure businesses and individuals alike."

Among the measures welcomed by the firm was an increase in business rates relief from £12,000 to £18,000, which Mr Baggaley said would provide support for businesses in the North East, particularly those operating in hospitality and leisure.

The Budget also included measures aimed at helping households with energy costs and increasing the minimum wage.

However, the firm warned that higher wages would inevitably add to the financial pressures facing employers already dealing with rising operating costs.

Mr Baggaley said: "There are positive measures for businesses and households, including support with energy costs and an increase in the minimum wage. However, the latter will inevitably place additional pressure on employers already managing rising operating costs."

The Clive Owen LLP tax expert also raised concerns about further tax increases affecting property and savings income, alongside changes to Business Asset Disposal Relief.

The government has announced a reduction in Business Asset Disposal Relief from £1 million to £500,000, a move Mr Baggaley warned could affect entrepreneurs considering selling their businesses.

He said: "At a time when we should be encouraging entrepreneurship and business growth, this setback will affect some business owners looking to sell soon."

However, the introduction of Founders Relief was identified as a potentially positive development for those looking to establish new businesses.

Mr Baggaley said the measure could offer future capital gains and income tax benefits, although further details would be needed to understand how the scheme would operate.

Despite the concerns, the firm said the relatively measured nature of the Budget could help restore confidence among businesses looking to invest and expand.

Mr Baggaley added: "Ultimately, what businesses across our region are looking for is stability. Confidence is essential to investment, and investment is what will drive sustainable economic growth.

"While this Budget may not deliver sweeping changes, its relatively measured approach could provide the certainty businesses need to plan ahead, invest and grow. That, in itself, is a positive outcome for the North East economy."
