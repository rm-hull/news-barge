A Yorkshire dales village shop has created its own cookbook to prove that home cooks do not need a nearby supermarket or takeaway to put everything from pies to a döner kebab on the table.

Sykes House Village Shop in Askrigg, Wensleydale, launched the cookbook to show just how far stock from a village store can go.

The 84-page Sykes House Village Cookbook contains 62 recipes using only ingredients found on the shop’s shelves—excluding meat, which can be ordered fresh for same-day collection.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21635140.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sykes House Village Cookbook *(Image: Supplied)*

Helen Oswald, co-owner of Sykes House Village Shop, said: "I grew up in Carperby before moving to Askrigg, and the village shop was always where you caught up on the news, saw your neighbours and picked up something for tea.

"We wanted to prove it can still be all of those things and more.

"Every recipe in this book comes off our shelves, and each time someone cooks from it, they're helping to keep a village shop open.

"A village shop keeps a village alive."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21635141.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sykes House Village Cookbook *(Image: Supplied)*

The recipes span a wide range of styles and cuisines, including Yorkshire favourites like Steak & Black Dub Ale Pie, Toad in the Hole and a rarebit made with Askrigg Ale.

There is also a World Food chapter with dishes from Hungarian goulash to Caribbean jerk chicken, as well as a section of 15-minute meals and a 'Fakeaway' chapter featuring chip-shop curry sauce, chicken katsu and a homemade kebab—ideal for a village without takeaway options.

The cookbook even explores unusual combinations, such as Marmite & Cheddar Sausage Rolls and pork escalopes coated in salt & vinegar crisps, inspired by the concept of mixing ingredients from different shelves.

Sykes House itself is a piece of village history.

The building has stood on Main Street for around 400 years, serving at various times as a guest house, a temperance hotel and a stop for cyclists.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21635142.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sykes House Village Cookbook *(Image: Supplied)*

The shop was established in the 1990s to ensure Askrigg would always have a village store, and Graeme and Helen Oswald took over in March 2025.

Mr Oswald said: "People told us a village shop can't compete with the supermarkets.

"We decided we didn't need to compete, we just needed to be useful.

"Nobody delivers a curry to Askrigg on a Friday night, so we wrote one you can make from what's on Main Street.
