**The number of vacant council houses in Edinburgh has fallen by 66 per cent in four years, as the capital tackles a serious housing squeeze.**

According to a Freedom of Information request, just 474 council houses were recorded as vacant during the 2025/26 financial year, down from a high-water mark of 1,386 in 2022/23.

Data shows 1,231 properties were recorded as vacant in 2023/24, a decline of 150. The trend intensified during the following financial year, when the number of vacant properties was cut almost to 672 before subsequently falling to 474.

The number of days a property had been empty before being let to a new household also declined.

The decrease appears to be the result of deliberate action by the council.

“Since the Housing Emergency Action Plan was approved we’ve made significant progress in bringing empty homes back into use and accelerating the turnaround of vacant properties,” a City of Edinburgh Council spokesperson said.

![The statistics, obtained by the Tories under a freedom of information request, reveal a dramatic rise in those seeking help with homelessness](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20535714.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 1,000 households were placed in unsuitable accommodation earlier this year. *(Image: Yui Mok/PA Wire)*

“Whilst factors such as utility issues and the need for both major and common repairs can affect turnaround times, we remain committed to making sure properties meet our standard of let before tenants move in and continue to focus on reducing void periods and improving turnaround times.”

In 2023/24, council properties sat vacant for an average of 135 days, a figure which rose to 173 days in 2024/25 before falling to 111 days last year.

While that is still higher than the 2021/22 average of 76 days, it represents a drop of nearly two months in just one year.

“We've been able to lift 350 households out of unsuitable accommodation because we made every single empty property that came back to us available as temporary accommodation,” Councillor Tim Pogson, the council's housing convener, told The Herald earlier this month.

Between April 2025 and October 2026, any property returned to the council after a tenant’s departure was prioritised for use as temporary accommodation, rather than being allocated to the social housing waiting list.

“Another really, really important consideration is that if a homeless person comes to us today, we must be able to offer them accommodation tonight,” the Labour councillor said.

“And it's on the record that we haven't been able to do that every time.

“That’s the sharpest, the most acute point in the whole system to say, we can't accommodate somebody who is homeless now.”

“That was a really difficult decision for the committee to make … but eventually we managed it.”

![Councillor Tim Pogson is the city's housing convener.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21532386.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Tim Pogson is the city's housing convener. *(Image: Tim Pogson/Facebook)*

Figures suggest that the number of homeless people in Edinburgh has grown in recent years.

Scottish Government figures show the council was unable to provide temporary accommodation to homeless households on 7,465 occasions in 2025/26, despite being legally required to do so.

That is an increase of 130% from the year before.

The capital saw an corresponding increase of 14% in homeless applications, rising to 4,435.

There were also 6,521 households living in temporary accommodation in March 2026, an increase of 83% in three years.

The Herald has also reported that 1,078 households were placed in unsuitable accommodation that month, a slight increase from the 970 households in March 2025, but a decrease of 43.2% from a peak of 1,544 households in September 2024.

Despite the grim figures, Councillor Pogson said work to cut down on the backlog has proven successful.

“In the last year, there were 1,450 new affordable homes built,” the housing convener said.

“There were 850 affordable homes purchased. As a result of that, we have lifted 350 households out of unsuitable accommodation.

“And the best part of it is that we have been able to basically end families living in unsuitable accommodation. A single person living in a hotel room for longer than seven nights is bad enough. But if it's a family with kids, that's really not good."

Councillor Pogson added: "We made an absolute cast-iron huge commitment in our last budget to spend £1.6 billion on housing over five years, which is enormous.

“We acknowledge that homelessness is the biggest challenge that the city faces and therefore we just need to invest.”
