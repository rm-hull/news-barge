**Barnet 3 York City 1**

York City coach Gary Elphick was left hugely frustrated by the manner of the team’s defeat at Barnet last night (Tuesday).

The game turned on the sending off of Hiram Boateng in the 52nd minute, when he was given a second booking for attempting a quick free kick.

And head coach Scott Lindsey was shown red following the final whistle.

It was York’s first defeat in six league matches, dropping them to fifth.

The visitors started brightly. Joe Grey’s left-footed effort was saved by Barnet goalkeeper Matthew Cox before Mark Kitching was flagged offside after tapping home from a subsequent cross.

Ollie Pearce’s low drive forced Cox into another save on the counter-attack midway through the first half.

York broke the deadlock in the 37th minute through Arthur Read’s left-footed strike into the bottom corner, but Barnet’s Kabongo Tshimanga equalised two minutes later after turning smartly and finishing past York goalkeeper Alfie McNally.

The hosts took the lead with ten minutes left following substitute Jake Reeves’ superb volley before Damola Ajayi’s run ended with his effort ricocheting off McNally and into the net for an own goal to seal a first victory in three games in the 87th minute.

York coach Gary Elphick was left infuriated by the referee decisions.

Elphick said: “We are very frustrated. It stemmed from the decision to award a throw-in, from which they scored.

“It was difficult to say it was definitely a throw-in to Barnet. I’m really upset for the fans who paid good money to come to London.

“I thought the first 45 minutes was the best of our season. We outplayed them at times, outfought them, moved the ball quicker and created chances. It made me proud to be part of this York side.

“When you’re officiating a big game like this, you’ve got to be on the money.

“They’re saying he was booked for a dive, but it was actually a shirt pull. You can’t be booking players like the referee has done tonight.

“To go down to ten men in a big game like this, especially the way he was sent off, was frustrating. It wasn’t a two-footed challenge.

“We have to bottle this up and use it as motivation. That’s my message to the boys. We need to get justice when Barnet come and play us at our place.”
