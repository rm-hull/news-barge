Households face a year of no real earnings growth, with the Office for Budget Responsibility warning that living standards will stall as the Iran war pushes up energy bills and inflation.

The Chancellor used the first Budget of Andy Burnham's premiership yesterday to try to offer some "breathing space", paying for it with a temporary raid on the banks and by spending part of the fiscal buffer built up by his predecessor, Rachel Reeves.

In the Commons, as he set out his tax and spending plans, Mr Healey said oil had risen from around $70 a barrel to well over $100 since the conflict in the Middle East began, and gas prices had almost tripled since February.

He told MPs: "Those are the storms we face. We cannot wish them away. We must choose the course we steer through them."

The closet the cautious budget had to a showstopper was a one-off £100 payment to help with energy bills for around 20 million households in council tax bands A to D in England .

It will be paid into bank accounts from January at a cost of £2.6 billion and funded by raising the bank corporation tax surcharge from 3% to 6% for 15 months from January 1, 2027.

The Scottish Government will receive a share through the Barnett formula and Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth has confirmed it will be passed on to householders north of the border.

The Treasury says the Scottish Government will receive an extra £600 million over three years, though Ms Gilruth said the budget would still be cut by around £650 million in real terms next year.

The Scottish Budget follows on Wednesday December 3.

Mr Healey also confirmed that the VAT cut on household electricity is extended into next summer, which the Treasury says will take around £50 off the Ofgem price cap in April. The 5p fuel duty cut is extended to July and a planned levy on gas bills to fund hydrogen projects is scrapped.

Gas standing charges will be abolished from 2027, but the change is revenue neutral, with the cost moved to the unit price.

The Treasury estimates 60% of households will save an average of £45 a year. That implies the other 40%, who use more gas, many of them in Scotland, will pay around £67.50 more, according to analysis by the Scottish Parliament's research service, SPICe.

The National Living Wage rises 3.9% to £13.21 an hour from April 2027. A new Pension Tax Allowance means pensioners with income up to the value of the full new state pension will not be brought into paying income tax by pension increases.

The triple lock on the state pension will be adjusted from 2030 so it rises with earnings and inflation, saving £15bn a year by the end of the 2030s.

Mr Healey stuck to Labour's manifesto pledge not to raise headline rates of income tax, National Insurance or VAT. But tax on property and savings income rises by two percentage points from April 2027 in England, Wales and Northern Ireland. He scrapped Ms Reeves's planned cut to the cash ISA limit, which stays at £20,000.

The Defence Investment Plan is fully funded, including £4.7bn previously unfunded, with a target of 3% of GDP on defence by 2030.

On the North Sea, the OBR forecasts North Sea tax receipts peaking at £5.1bn this year, then falling to almost nothing by 2031-32, even with Rosebank assumed. The Acorn carbon capture project has no confirmed grant funding.

In its forecasts published alongside the budget, the OBR said interest rates and energy prices have both risen significantly since March.

It expects no real earnings growth in 2027, and real household disposable income per person, its measure of living standards, is forecast to dip in the near term.

By 2030-31 it is only expected around 2% above pre-pandemic levels and about three-quarters of a percentage point below the March forecast.

Growth is forecast at 1.2% this year, up slightly from 1.1% in March, but next year's forecast is cut sharply from 1.6% to 1%. Inflation is forecast to average about 3.5% in 2027, against 2% expected in March, before returning to target in 2028 as energy prices fall back.

Borrowing is forecast to fall from 4.4% of GDP last year to 4.2% this year, and to 1.8% by 2030-31.

Mr Healey meets both of Labour's fiscal rules, but with less room than before. Headroom against the stability rule is £15bn in 2029-30, rising to £22bn the following year. He said: "That is what a buffer is for. Use it when you need it. Rebuild it when you can."

The OBR stresses that outcomes could be significantly better or worse. If energy prices reach a higher and more sustained peak, borrowing could be £17bn above its central forecast in 2027-28.

Emma Congreve, deputy director of the Fraser of Allander Institute, called it a Budget "more about rearranging rather than reimagining".
