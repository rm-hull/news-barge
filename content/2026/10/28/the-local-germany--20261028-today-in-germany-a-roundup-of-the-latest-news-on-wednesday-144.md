**Today's top story: Four injured after explosion triggers fire at apartment in Regensburg**

Emergency services are at the scene of a major fire at an apartment building in the city of Regensburg, Bavaria.

An explosion happened in the early hours of Wednesday. According to police, several residents reported hearing a loud bang around 4am.

Shortly after the residential building became engulfed in flames, a police spokesperson said. Police initially suggested a gas explosion might be the cause, however, it is still unclear.

At least four people have been injured and taken to hospital. Parts of the building's facade are completely destroyed.

There were no reports of fatalities early on Wednesday.

Emergency services have advised people to avoid the area in the south east of the city due to the heavy smoke.

**Antisemitism row hangs over Berlin's Left Party**

Berlin’s Die Linke (Left Party) is still stuck in a row over antisemitism and its position on Israel ahead of planned coalition talks with the Greens and Social Democrats (SPD).

The dispute centres on newly elected Neukölln MP Vanessa Emde, whose comments on Israel have sparked controversy. She now faces possible sanctions, including expulsion from the party’s parliamentary group.

On Tuesday, the executive committee of The Left’s parliamentary group met with Emde at the House of Representatives for consultations.

During the meeting, Emde was given the opportunity to present her position. It is unclear what the next steps will be.

Emde has previously defended her statements, including the argument that states do not have a right to exist, only people do.

The Left Party's leadership said last week that it no longer saw a basis for working with her after she criticised an anti-antisemitism declaration she had signed, which included a commitment to Israel’s right to exist.

The issue has been causing problems as The Left, which won September's state election, is seeking to build a coalition government in Berlin.

Three people were arrested on Tuesday after a car was reported to have deliberately driven into a group of people in Neustadt am Rübenberge near Hanover the night before.

The suspects were said to have been found at the address of the registered owner of the suspected vehicle.

"The extent to which these individuals are directly connected to the incident is currently part of the ongoing investigation," police said, according to a report by Tagesspiegel.

Police referred to the incident as an "attack".

Five people had been injured and taken to the hospital, but none were in life-threatening condition.

After the attack, police in the area had stopped the car by firing at it. The suspects then managed to flee the scene.

Several cars were said to have flat tires and bullet holes as a result.

**Investment in Germany remains stable despite political turmoil, says Merz**

Europe remained a stable destination for investment despite political turbulence at home and elsewhere, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said Tuesday, kicking off a three-nation Asian tour aimed at deepening ties beyond China.

Germany has been seeking to diversify its strategic and trade links to counter the perceived unreliability of the United States or China's growth as an economic rival.

Speaking in Singapore, the first stop of a visit that will also take him to Japan and South Korea, Merz said there were "no uncertainties in Europe".

"Under the federal government I lead, investment opportunities will be continuously improved," Merz said after meeting Singapore Prime Minister Lawrence Wong.

"All I can say is that I encounter a great deal of confidence here in Germany and in Europe as locations for investment."

The German leader's comments came despite fresh turbulence in Berlin, where his government has faced pressure -- including the resignation this month of a key minister from its junior coalition partner.

Elsewhere in Europe, France has seen widespread looting and protests over the past weeks.

But Singapore's Wong said investor confidence remained high in Germany and Europe.

"Our investors are long-term investors. They are not just investing for one election cycle," he said.

**EU wants to beef up border agency with deportation powers**

The European Union wants to beef up its border agency with powers to handle deportations under plans to strengthen the bloc's response to irregular migration presented on Tuesday.

The European Commission proposed giving the Frontex agency a central role in arranging the expulsion of people with no right to stay in the EU and to create a 17,000-strong "reserve" of officers ready to be deployed when needed.

"Europe will always stand up for the protection of its borders. Europe decides who comes to our Union and under what circumstances," EU chief Ursula von der Leyen said.

The reform plan is the latest in a series of measures aiming to tackle irregular migration amid a souring of public opinion that has fuelled far-right electoral gains across Europe.

Under the plan, Frontex's "standing corps" will triple to about 30,000.

**Bayern praise 'humble' Kane after Ballon d'Or win**

Bayern Munich assistant coach Aaron Danks on Tuesday praised Harry Kane after his Ballon d'Or win, saying the striker will already be focused on scoring his "next goal".

Kane, 33, was awarded the prestigious honour in his hometown of London on Monday evening, breaking through to win the award for the first time.

![England and Bayern Munich forward Harry Kane reacts after receiving the Ballon d'Or award for the best male footballer during the 2026 Ballon d'Or France Football award ceremony at the Palladium Theatre in London on October 26, 2026. ](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/AFP__20261027__D4KX74J__v1__MidRes__776576274.jpg)

Englishman Danks, who is in the dugout while head coach Vincent Kompany recovers in hospital from a virus, said receiving the game's highest individual award would not change the England captain's mindset.

"I'm really happy for him and proud of what he's achieved. I know Harry and his full focus will now be on his next goal, his next performances, his next games," Danks said at a press conference on Tuesday.

"He'll be straight back into that professional, humble mindset that got him to this level."

Kane had won several goalscoring honours and was runner-up in the Premier League and Champions League with Tottenham but did not break through for a first team trophy until after he moved to Bayern in 2023.

Since arriving in Munich, Kane has won two Bundesliga titles and the German Cup, along with making the Champions League semi-finals in two of three seasons at Bayern.

**Germany's Infineon to invest €1.1bn in Austria chip production**

German semiconductor maker Infineon says it would invest €1.1 billion in a next-generation chip production unit in Austria to meet rising demand driven by the AI boom.

The group, based near Munich, said it intends to create the "world's first high-volume manufacturing facility" for a specific kind of "next-generation" power semiconductor made using gallium nitride (GaN).

"Gallium nitride enables significant efficiency gains in numerous applications, such as AI data centres, energy infrastructure, e-mobility, robotics and consumer electronics," the group said.

The new investment will expand the production capacity of Infineon's Villach site in Austria by 2034, but the first chips are expected to be manufactured well before then, by the end of 2027.

Nearly 30 percent of the investment is financed by European subsidies under the European Chips Act, which aims to strengthen the EU's technological sovereignty.
