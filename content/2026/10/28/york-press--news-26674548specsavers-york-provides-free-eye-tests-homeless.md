A free eye test scheme has helped people get glasses and referrals to additional services.

Specsavers’ York home visits team partnered with Vision Care for Homeless People (VCHP) to deliver free eye tests to 16 residents at James House, York, earlier this month.

The drop-in clinic offered a safe space for individuals to discuss vision concerns, with 12 people — 75 per cent of attendees — receiving glasses on the day.

Georgia Thompson, retail director at Specsavers York Home Visits, said: "As optometrists, our role is to improve people’s lives through better vision and provide them with the care they require.

"Vision is a sense we often take for granted, but it’s fundamental to our daily lives; without it, simple tasks can become difficult, and for people experiencing homelessness, it could even put their lives at risk."

Kim Critchley, housing support officer at York Council, helped organise the session.

She said: "Vision care, like many other health issues, sadly gets put on the back burner when you’re trying to make ends meet during hard economic times."

Specsavers’ home visit team delivers routine eye tests and support to people unable to visit a store unaccompanied, including those eligible for NHS-funded support.
