Work on the long-awaited A66 dualling scheme will begin next month, but the major upgrade linking North Yorkshire and Cumbria will be delivered over nine years, the Government has revealed.

The confirmation, contained in the Budget 2026 documents published today (Wednesday, October 28), gives the clearest indication yet of when construction will begin on the crucial trans-Pennine route.

The Government has officially given the green light for main construction works to start in November 2026, with the project now set to be delivered through a revised nine-year programme.

The announcement ends months of uncertainty after Richmond and Northallerton MP Rishi Sunak branded the delays “unacceptable” and warned work at Scotch Corner and Ravensworth could be pushed back by two years.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21352664.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The A66 near Scotch Corner, North Yorkshire *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

The Budget documents say: "Giving the green light for main construction works to begin on the A66 next month, delivering a continuous 50-mile dual carriageway between the M6 and A1(M), strengthening connectivity between the North-East and Cumbria.

"The project will be delivered through a revised nine-year programme, with phased construction commencing in November 2026."

A further section of the Budget confirms: "The government will deliver a continuous 50-mile dual carriageway between the M6 (Penrith) and A1(M) (Scotch Corner), starting works from November 2026 and with a revised nine-year delivery schedule."

Construction is due to start within weeks, but the revised timetable could keep the project running into the mid-2030s.

But it remains unclear when work will start on individual sections, including the long-awaited improvements at Scotch Corner and near Ravensworth.

Mr Sunak said the timescale in the small print of the Budget was "disappointing news."

He added: “With the Government having already delayed starting the works, which were previously due to begin in 2024, it is frustrating that it is going to take another nine years to complete.

"Upgrading these 18 miles of dual carriageway as soon as possible is vital to improve journey times and, most importantly, safety.

“The last Government expected this upgrade to be completed over five years. If this Government had kept to our schedule and not delayed deciding on whether to go ahead with the scheme, the work could have been at halfway stage by now.”

As The Northern Echo previously reported, Mr Sunak raised serious concerns earlier this month about the lack of a confirmed construction timetable for the North Yorkshire sections of the scheme.

He said at the time: "I have been seeking certainty for residents in Ravensworth, the Laytons and Newsham since last May, when the Government announced it would proceed with the scheme which was shovel-ready in 2024.

![The A66 Scotch Corner and Penrith signs, near Scotch Corner, North Yorkshire](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21642484.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The A66 Scotch Corner and Penrith signs, near Scotch Corner, North Yorkshire *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

"The uncertainty this has left residents with is unacceptable, and I am deeply concerned about reports that the Ravensworth and Scotch Corner upgrades might not get underway for two years."

The Northern Echo also reported in August that the Government had reaffirmed its commitment to the scheme following questions raised with Chief Secretary to the Treasury Emma Reynolds during a visit to the region.

After the visit, the Department for Transport said it was "continuing to deliver the strategically and nationally important A66 Northern Trans-Pennine scheme."

The Budget gives the project a start month, but its nine-year timetable raises fresh questions over when motorists and communities will see the full benefits.

National Highways estimates the improvements could cut journey times by up to 12 minutes.

Safety concerns have driven calls for the upgrade, with at least 12 deaths reported on the A66 since 2023.

Supporters say the project will improve links between the North East and Cumbria, boost growth and attract investment across the region.

The A66 announcement came as the Government unveiled wider Budget 2026 measures, including £100 payments for millions of households facing soaring energy bills, an extension of the fuel-duty freeze until July next year and a 3.9 per cent rise in the national minimum and living wages for 2.3 million workers.
