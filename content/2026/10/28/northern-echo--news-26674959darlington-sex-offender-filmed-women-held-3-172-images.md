A sex offender found with thousands of indecent images of children also secretly filmed women having sex with him, without their knowledge.

Christopher Wilson was arrested last March after police received intelligence that he possessed indecent images of children.

Specialist officers from Durham Police's Digital Intelligence and Investigation Unit then examined his phone and other devices seized from his Darlington home.

Teesside Crown Court was told investigators found they contained 3,172 indecent images of children, including 371 category A images or videos, the most serious bracket.

There were also 1,173 category B images and videos and 1,628 category C images, while investigators also found two prohibited images.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21504113.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Teesside Crown Court was told Darlington sex offender had amassed thousands of indecent images of children on devices seized by police

While examining the devices, officers also found several videos of Wilson having sex with women which were filmed on a hidden spy camera in his home between 2019 and 2024.

Investigators tracked down the women, who said they did not know they were being filmed and had not consented.

The 33-year-old defendant, from Darlington, was charged with multiple voyeurism offences, possessing indecent images of children and making indecent images of children.

He pleaded guilty and appeared at Teesside Crown Court on Monday (October 26), when he received a two-year prison sentence suspended for 24 months.

Wilson was also ordered to complete 200 hours' unpaid work, made subject of a Sexual Harm Prevention Order and restraining orders banning contact with the women.

He was also placed on the sex offenders’ register for ten years.

Detective Constable Laura Fairhurst, from the Durham force's South CID, said: “Wilson carried out sustained and deeply invasive offending, abusing everyday technology to violate the privacy of his victims for his own sexual gratification.

“Wilson betrayed the trust of these women at a time when they should have felt safe.

"In doing so, he has shattered their lives and destroyed their confidence.

“I’d like to praise them for their courage in coming forward and supporting the police investigation, which has enabled us to bring a dangerous offender to justice.

"I hope the conclusion of this case allows them to rebuild their lives and move forward.

“Wilson poses not just a risk to women, but children too.

"The examination of his digital devices found he was in possession of thousands of still and moving images involving the sexual abuse of young children.

“This type of offending not only affects those children involved, but their families and wider communities too.

"That is why as a force we will continue to work tirelessly to disrupt this type of criminal activity, protect our communities, and put offenders before the courts.”
