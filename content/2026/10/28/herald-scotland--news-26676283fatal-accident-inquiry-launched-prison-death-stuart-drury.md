The death of a man who killed his former girlfriend in a frenzied hammer attack is to be probed in a Fatal Accident Inquiry.

Obsessive stalker Stuart Drury was jailed for life after he murdered mother-of-three Marilyn McKenna with a claw hammer in an attack near her Glasgow home in 1998.

Then 35, Drury had been in a relationship with Ms McKenna but began targeting her after it broke down.

He claimed he had been "provoked" into violence after seeing a man leave her home while he loitered outside.

His first conviction for murder was quashed after it was ruled that the trial judge had misdirected the jury. However, he was found guilty again at a retrial.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/10275456.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Drury died at HMP Edinburgh

Drury died in prison on August 3, 2023, aged 57. **The case led to calls for the tightening of anti-stalking laws in Scotland.**

Drury, who was being held at HMP Edinburgh, was in legal custody and under the care of the state at the time of his death.

Under Scots law, deaths that occur in legal custody must be investigated by the Crown Office and are subject to a mandatory Fatal Accident Inquiry.

A preliminary hearing in the case is scheduled to take place at Edinburgh Sheriff Court on November 24, 2026. The inquiry itself is due to begin on December 3, 2026.

The purpose of an FAI is to establish the circumstances surrounding a death, including the cause of death and whether any reasonable precautions could have been taken to prevent it.

The process may also identify measures that could reduce the risk of similar deaths occurring in future.

Procurator Fiscal Andy Shanks, who leads on fatalities investigations for COPFS, said:

"The Lord Advocate considers that the death of Stuart Drury occurred while in legal custody and, as such, a Fatal Accident Inquiry is mandatory.

"The lodging of the First Notice enables FAI proceedings to commence under the direction of the Sheriff."
