As Halloween approaches, County Durham horror filmmaker and radio film reviewer Dean Midas has picked out the films and TV shows he believes are worth settling down with this spooky season.

The award-winning filmmaker, who is based in Willington, specialises in low-budget horror and mobile filmmaking, while also reviewing films on BBC Radio Tees.

His latest short, The Stitcher, was filmed around Durham and released earlier this month.

![The Stitcher, Dean Midas, horror film, short film, County Durham, Durham, Halloween, independent film](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21628466.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The title screen for The Stitcher, the latest horror short from County Durham filmmaker Dean Midas *(Image: DEAN MIDAS)*

He is no stranger to sharing his Halloween favourites with Northern Echo readers.

In 2024, Midas selected classics including Halloween, Scream, The Exorcist and A Nightmare on Elm Street, while last year he and his daughters offered up a more family-friendly collection of seasonal favourites.

This time, his selection ranges from new television series and all-time horror classics to films suitable for the whole family.

Introducing his choices, Midas said: “As the nights draw in and October delivers its signature chill, screen programmers have unlocked the horror vault.

“Whether you are looking for edge-of-your-seat scares or cosy family viewing, there is a bumper crop of options this Halloween.”

## The New Big Hitter TV Series

**Carrie (Amazon Prime Video) (15)**: Adapting Stephen King is a gamble, but showrunner Mike Flanagan (The Haunting of Hill House) nails it.

Stretched across eight episodes, this series dives deep into high school cruelty and psychological dread.

It’s slick, deeply emotional, and builds to a genuinely explosive telekinetic payoff.

**Crystal Lake (Peacock / Sky) (18)**: A24 turns back the clock with this moody prequel to the Friday the 13th franchise.

Starring Linda Cardellini as Pamela Voorhees, it trades cheap jump scares for slow-burning, atmospheric mystery.

It offers a stylish, surprisingly grounded look at the dark origins of the infamous camp.

## Unmissable Horror Classics

**Halloween (1978) (18)**: John Carpenter’s original masterpiece remains the ultimate spooky season watch.

Cold, sharp, and brilliantly simple, Michael Myers’ haunting stroll through Haddonfield hasn't lost a single drop of its quiet terror.

**Scream (1996) (18)**: Wes Craven’s meta slasher reinvigorated the genre by poking fun at its own rules.

It’s witty, fast-paced, and packs one of the most iconic opening scenes in cinema history.

**The Stitcher (YouTube / Online) (15)**: If you want your scares rooted a little closer to home, do not miss my latest horror short, The Stitcher.

![The Stitcher, Dean Midas, Durham City, County Durham, horror film, short film, Halloween, independent filmmaker, mobile filmmaking](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21628467.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A scene from The Stitcher, Dean Midas's latest horror short, which was filmed around Durham City *(Image: DEAN MIDAS)*

Filmed right on our doorstep across Durham City, it’s our biggest production to date and, in true modern indie fashion, the whole thing was shot entirely on a mobile phone.

Give it a watch if you fancy seeing local landmarks turned into a setting for pure dread.

## Fun for All the Family

**Hocus Pocus (1993) (PG)**: It isn't officially Halloween until Bette Midler and the Sanderson sisters take to the skies.

A nostalgic, hilarious Disney staple that never gets old.

**The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993) (PG)**: Tim Burton’s stop-motion classic bridges the gap between Halloween and Christmas with brilliant songs and gothic charm.

**The Addams Family (1991) (PG)**: Creepy, kooky, and endlessly quotable, Anjelica Huston and Raul Julia deliver a masterclass in dark comedy that appeals to all ages.

**Casper (1995) (PG)**: Perfect for younger viewers who want a gentle touch of ghosts and haunted houses without the nightmares.

“Happy Halloween everyone,” Midas added.

His choices leave plenty of room to tailor a Halloween movie night to the audience, from slasher favourites Halloween and Scream to the gentler scares of Casper and Hocus Pocus.

And for anyone wanting to keep their Halloween viewing closer to home, The Stitcher brings the scares to familiar Durham surroundings.

The film is available through the Dean Midas Films YouTube channel, where Midas publishes his independent productions.
