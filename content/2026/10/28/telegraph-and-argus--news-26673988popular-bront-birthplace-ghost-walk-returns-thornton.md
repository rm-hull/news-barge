A ghost walk is set to examine Thornton's eerie past in a family-friendly evening of folklore and storytelling, taking place on Thursday, October 29, and starting from the Brontë Birthplace on Market Street.

Led by storyteller and historian Adam Sargent, who also runs the Haunted Haworth tours, the walk will lead visitors through the village’s historic streets and hidden corners, sharing tales of unexplained sightings and dark legends.

Anna Gibson, manager of the Brontë Birthplace, said: "I met Adam Sargent, who runs Haunted Haworth walking tours, a few years ago and ever since have been so keen to produce a similar walk around Thornton.

"It's a beautiful historic town with lots of fascinating buildings and a large conservation area.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637087.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Adam Sargent on the ghost tour in 2025 *(Image: Anna Gibson)*

### Dark tales from Thornton's past

"Adam was kind enough to agree, and jumped straight into research which has pulled up some incredible local stories that have become key features of the walk."

The ghost walk first launched last year to a sold-out crowd.

Stories include the legend of Fair Becca, ghostly hounds, and haunted sites tied to Thornton’s industrial heritage.

Ms Gibson said: "I actually think it's a rather undeveloped area.

"There are lots of local ghost stories, places that people used to play as children that have spooky energies and dark historical events that have taken on a folkloric tone over the years."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637084.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Adam Sargent on the ghost tour in 2025 *(Image: Anna Gibson)*

### The mystery of the 'ominous bearded man'

One story uncovered during research stood out to organisers.

A volunteer shared that his great-grandfather died in a freak accident at the Thornton Limestone Works, and a resident living on the site has reported seeing an "ominous bearded man" – a figure also seen by previous occupants.

While the Brontë sisters were born in Thornton, organisers have not uncovered any ghost stories directly connected to the family.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637086.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Adam Sargent with participants on the ghost tour in 2025 *(Image: Anna Gibson)*

However, Ms Gibson said they would likely have grown up around local folklore.

She said: "They may have been told that some becks run copper red because of the local legend of a bloody-mouthed hound, or heard the story of Fair Becca and her ill-fated love affair."

The walk is designed to be accessible and engaging for all ages.

Ms Gibson said: "There's nothing on the tour too spooky or too upsetting, and we didn't want to sensationalise history that matters deeply to local people."

The walk will begin at 6.30pm, with the venue’s tearoom open beforehand serving hot chocolate and seasonal treats.
