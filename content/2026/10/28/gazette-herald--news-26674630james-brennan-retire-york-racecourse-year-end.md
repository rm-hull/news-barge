One of the mainstays of York Racecourse has announced he is leaving after 28 years at the Knavesmire.

James Brennan, its head of marketing and sponsorship, is to retire at the end of the year.

Having turned 60 over the summer, James says it is "time to step back."

He confirmed: "This is a retirement, though I remain open to new challenges, I will not be taking on a role at another track—to my mind, there is nowhere better than here."




"It is an emotional decision, but I think the right one for me; as the wise say about the timing of these things—when you know, you know."

James has an extensive career in marketing, with roles at what is now GlaxoSmithKline plc, working on Aquafresh toothpaste.

He also worked for Leeds Building Society and before coming to York Racecourse, British Telecom.

"I have always been in marketing, with a bias towards local sponsorships and event management at BT in Leeds."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637584.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James Brennan at the Knavesmire in 2003 *(Image: York Press)*

James says he was attracted to York Racecourse because whilst everyone worked hard at BT, you did not have the sense of making a difference.

But at the Knavesmire, conversations and decisions will matter the following season.

It is a small team at the racecourse, James explains, but everyone works together, from the ground staff, the support staff, the front of house teams, right through to car park attendants and stewards.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637600.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James Brennan presenting the York Press Business Personality Award to Sarah Czarnecki in 2023. *(Image: David Harrison)*

But when there is a major event such as Ebor Week, there can be as many as 2,679 people working in various roles.

James continued: "It’s been an honour to be part of the team and we stand and fall together collectively."

"I like to think we have made some very happy memories over the years."

Times have changed too, especially with the use of technology.

The racecourse website that was simply a brochure when James came to York has evolved to feature digital ticketing, online booking and interactive mapping.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637643.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pictured is James Brennan from York Racecourse with Professor Robert Mortimer from York St John University at the York Press/ York St John University Top 100 in 2023. Picture by David Harrison *(Image: David Harrison)*

Phones can now broadcast Racing TV and also be used for betting.

James is grateful to the sponsors, noting John Smith's Brewery will be sponsoring the 67th John Smith's Cup next July.

And in turn, the Press is grateful to James and the racecourse for their support in our Business Awards, Community Pride awards, the Top 100, and now our new Food and Drink Awards, for which he has been a regular and popular presenter.

James continued: "The horses have always been at the heart of it, whether that was Gary Stevens booting home Royal Anthem in my first Juddmonte International in 1999, Sergeant Cecil, the unforgettable Frankel Day (in 2012), or the heroics of Copper Knight!"

The Juddmonte International is the Knavesmire's richest race and is worth £1.5 million, with independent judges saying it is the best race on the planet.

James also recalls with pride the Grand Départ in 2014 when the Tour de France came to Yorkshire.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637817.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James Brennan in 2024, when the Knavesmire won Racecourse of the Year *(Image: Pic supplied)*

And whilst there has been an increase in race days at the Knavesmire, some 800 other events have also become central to its success.

These include concerts and music events, either standalone or as part of a race day.

James recalls big names like Blondie and The Corrs appealing to a more mature generation, whereas his children will go for McFly.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21637845.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James Brennan and his team in 2024, when the Knavesmire won racecourse of the year. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

James says he and his family will be staying in the York area and whilst he is retiring, he is open to "other things that catch my interest or in making a contribution," saying he is still fit.

"But it won’t be another racecourse, there’s no better course than York."

He added: "I look back with huge pride, to have made these memories. I am grateful to everyone both inside and outside the organisation for the support they have given me."
