A traditional Teesside pub has reopened its doors after more than three years, following a major makeover.

The Green Dragon on Finkle Street, Stockton town centre, welcomed customers back from noon on October 28, ending a long wait for regulars.

The Samuel Smith pub had been closed since May 2023 after seven years in business.

Regulars voiced their disappointment at the time, with one describing it as their favourite pub and another saying they had marked major moments in their life there since 1990.

Earlier this year, new management took on the Green Dragon and unveiled plans to breathe new life into the venue while preserving its traditional character.

Work has since transformed the Finkle Street pub ahead of its reopening.

The team said it wanted to create one of the area’s best traditional pubs—a place to enjoy a drink, catch up with friends, meet people and feel at home.

The new-look venue is centred on conversation, darts and what the team calls a "proper pint in a proper pub".

The team said on Facebook that two last-minute problems had thrown "a couple of spanners in the works", but were fixed in time for the reopening.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21635792.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Green Dragon Yard sign *(Image: Freya Cain-Metcalf)*

The reopening also brings one of Stockton’s most historic buildings back into use.

The Green Dragon Inn is a Grade II listed building added to the National Heritage List for England in 1985.

Stockton’s heritage records describe it as a large three-storey building, built around 1666 as a house with an alehouse at the rear.

The Green Dragon sits next to the Georgian Theatre in its courtyard on Finkle Street, which has formed part of the wider Stockton Waterfront regeneration in recent years.

After more than three years closed, the Green Dragon is pouring pints again as it begins its next chapter.
