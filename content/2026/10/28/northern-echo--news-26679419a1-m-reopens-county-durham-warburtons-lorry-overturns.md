A HGV driver was taken to hospital after a Warburtons lorry overturned on the A1(M) near Durham, sparking hours of disruption and lengthy delays for motorists.

Emergency services were called to the motorway near Bowburn at 5.14am on Wednesday after a lorry overturned between Junction 60 at Bradbury and Junction 61 at Bowburn.

The vehicle came to rest across the central reservation, shedding its load across both carriageways and forcing major restrictions on one of the region's busiest roads.

Pictures from the scene showed the lorry lying on its side, spanning both the northbound and southbound carriageways, while debris was scattered across the motorway.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21639965.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

The driver, who was the only person involved in the crash, was taken to University Hospital of North Durham for treatment.

A spokesperson said: "Police were called just after 5.10am today following reports that a lorry had overturned on the A1(M) just after the Bowburn junction.

"The lorry has shed its load across both carriageways, resulting in a full closure of the northbound carriageway between Bowburn and Carville Interchange. The southbound carriageway is restricted to the hard shoulder only.

"The driver of the lorry has been taken to hospital for treatment."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643736.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Submitted)*

North East Ambulance Service dispatched a double-crewed ambulance, a clinical team leader and two hazardous area response team vehicles to the incident.

Firefighters from Durham and Spennymoor were also called to assist with the safe extraction of the driver.

National Highways warned that recovery was likely to be a lengthy operation after the lorry flattened the central reservation barrier and left a significant debris field across the motorway.

By 10.15am, delays of up to 50 minutes northbound and 40 minutes southbound were being reported.

A further update confirmed there was a "significant amount of debris" to clear before the road could reopen, while recovery crews worked to remove the overturned vehicle.

Just before midday, National Highways said the HGV had been righted by a crane, but warned that extensive clean-up work and barrier repairs were still required before traffic restrictions could be lifted.

Temporary repairs to the damaged central reservation continued into the afternoon.

After more than 12 hours of disruption, National Highways confirmed this evening that the A1(M) had fully reopened between Junctions 60 and 61.

Both carriageways are now open and traffic is moving through the area, although congestion remains on the southbound carriageway with delays of around 17 minutes still being reported.

The cause of the crash has not yet been confirmed.
