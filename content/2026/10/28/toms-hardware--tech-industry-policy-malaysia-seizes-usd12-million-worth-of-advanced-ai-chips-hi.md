Malaysian customs have seized over $12 million (49.5 million Malaysian ringgit) worth of advanced AI chips in a single bust, as the country clamps down on a suspected semiconductor smuggling syndicate. According to a *Reuters* report on October 28, officials found 30 servers containing the chips at Kuala Lumpur International Airport’s free trade zone on October 15th. Initial investigations revealed that the shipment originated in an Asian country and was bound for another undisclosed Asian country.

![Microsoft data center in Mount Pleasant, Wisconsin](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Vh4nY3pMCcmra2ymXah9S7-1200-80.jpg)

The airport’s customs director, Aspalila Ag Tuah, confirmed that the servers were falsely declared as ordinary electronic goods, saying the suspected scheme used Malaysia as a transit point to avoid restrictions on the goods’ onward movement. The Malaysian company involved in the shipment has been summoned to assist in the investigation.

Authorities suspect that the foiled smuggling attempt was carried out by the same group responsible for a similar attempt last June. On June 5, customs officers discovered 72 server units containing advanced AI chips at the Kuala Lumpur International Airport, valued at approximately $13 million. This shipment had also been falsely declared as computer components and was meant to be reshipped without the required permit. Aspalila said the network has been linked to seizures worth over $25 million so far across at least five cases. She declined to provide further information on the network or the current case, citing ongoing investigations.

Several key facts remain unknown, including the chip type, country of manufacture, final destination, and intended recipient — whether a company or a military organization. The U.S. had placed export controls on the movement of advanced Nvidia and other U.S.-origin AI chips to China, the most likely destination for the seized chips. Smuggling networks began using Malaysia, a U.S. ally not affected by the controls, as a hub, exporting the banned chips to the country before re-exporting them to China.

While the country’s geographic location and mature logistics infrastructure make it important for legitimate international trade, these characteristics also make it ideal for smuggling sensitive technology. To restrict this avenue, Malaysia subsequently imposed export controls on the ​movement of advanced AI chips of U.S. origin, at Washington’s request. Other Asian U.S. allies, such as Singapore and Taiwan, have implemented similar controls. Demand for powerful Nvidia GPUs remains high in China, fueling a booming market for smuggled hardware.
