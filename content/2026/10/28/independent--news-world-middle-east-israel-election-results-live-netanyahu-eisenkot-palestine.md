Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu is facing calls to step aside after exit polls predicted an election deadlock between his and a rival bloc.

Exit ⁠polls ⁠by major ​television networks ⁠showed neither Netanyahu nor ‌his main ⁠rival Eisenkot secured the 61 seats needed for a parliamentary majority. Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party and the rival "change" bloc, told party workers in Tel Aviv they "have made history".

"This morning the people of Israel went to a referendum on the leadership that will lead Israel in the coming years. Tonight we received an answer. The public decided in favour of change,” he said.

With actual results still being counted, the exit polls indicated that Netanyahu's bloc won 50 seats, the opposition won 52-53 and Arab parties won 17-18. The strong showing by Arab-led parties could allow them to decide the next prime minister, but both blocs have until now refused to partner with them.

Netanyahu, addressing a crowd in Jerusalem, said the election provided Israel a "historic opportunity" to eliminate the "Iranian threat" as he called for the formation of a "strong, right-wing" government.

## Over 1.6 million votes counted so far

More than 1.6 million votes have been counted so far, according to Israel’s Central Elections Committee.

Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud party remained in the lead with 22.24 per cent of the votes counted, while Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar party trailed with 15.41 per cent of the votes.

More than 5.69 million voters or 77.7 per cent of the eligible voters cast their ballots on Tuesday, according to the elections committee.

## Exit polls show no clear winner in Israeli elections. What happens next?

Israel’s election has ended in a deadlock as no party is on track for majority of the Knesset, exit polls have suggested.

The stalemate comes after three years of war waged in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran, which have plunged the Middle East into turmoil.

The election is widely seen as a referendum on Benjamin Netanyahu, who is seeking another term after a combined 19 years in power.

## Eisenkot calls on Netanyahu to quit politics

Gadi Eisenkot has claimed his bloc has "made history" and called on Benjamin Netanyahu to retire from politics, even as exit polls showed the two blocs neck and neck in the elections.

"This morning, the entire Israeli nation went out for a referendum. Tonight, we got an answer - the public has decided it wants change," Eisenkot told party workers in Tel Aviv.

He said Netanyahu's "fiasco" coalition has "lost their confidence".Eisenkot earlier ⁠warned Netanyahu not to use Israel's security for political purposes.

“Netanyahu is a ‌caretaker prime minister, without a mandate or the people's trust,” Eisenkot said, calling on the security establishment to “stand firm” against any attempt to put political interests ahead of Israel's security.

## Israeli national security minister: ‘The night is still young’

Itamar Ben-Gvir, the far-right national security minister in Israel, said, “The night is still young” as exit polls suggest a deadlock with prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his main opponent Gadi Eisenkot.

## Netanyahu calls for formation of 'strong, right-wing' government

Benjamin Netanyahu has called for the formation of “a strong, right-wing national government" after exit polls predicted a deadlock in Israel's election.

With actual votes trickling in, Kan 11 and Channel 13 exit polls had the blocs tied at 51-51, while Channel 12 gave Netanyahu's Likud-led bloc a narrow 52-49 edge.

None left either side with the 61 seats needed for a majority in the 120-seat Knesset.

## Emotions run high among some voters

In Kiryat Shmona, a Netanyahu stronghold near the border with Lebanon, one man kicked and tore down a poster of Eisenkot.

The election is Israel's first national vote since the Oct. 7 attack that sparked the war in Gaza and related conflicts.

In Tel Aviv, families of some killed on Oct. 7 or former hostages held a moment of silence.

“Remember all those whose voices are no more,” said Yona Adam, whose daughter Mapal Adam was killed.

Around 330,000 young Israelis, many facing compulsory military service under the shadow of war, are eligible to vote for the first time.

Eisenkot started the day by visiting communities attacked on Oct. 7.

“If someone in the government had a shred of responsibility, they would start the morning here” in the communities near Gaza, he said.

Standing in a cheering crowd, Eisenkot called on all Israelis to vote to get rid of “the worst government Israel has known.”

He noted that victory wasn’t certain, adding that “we are on perhaps the most critical day since the founding of the state.”

There were scattered allegations of irregularities and issues at polling stations, but Dean Livne, the acting director-general of the elections commission, said the number of complaints was so far much lower than in previous elections.
