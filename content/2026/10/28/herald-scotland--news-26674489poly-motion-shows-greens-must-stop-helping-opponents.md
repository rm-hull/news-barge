The most interesting aspect of Ross Greer's speech to the Scottish Greens' conference in Inverness at the weekend was the warning he gave to his party.

It reminded me of the one Patrick Harvie issued during his final co-leader's address in 2025 ahead of the party's leadership campaign.

It highlighted that a major challenge facing leaders of all parties is how to tell off members in public in as politest way as possible.

Mr Harvie's stern words were about anonymous briefings to the media against other senior figures as a "civil war" brewed.

The then co-leader did not mention the target of the negative talk but many thought it was a message to those leaking and briefing against Mr Greer.

Later that year Mr Greer told The Herald he had been contemplating resigning as an MSP at the time because of the impact of the situation on his health.

Back in his Spring 2025 speech Mr Harvie compared the party's drive to be "hopeful" and "constructive" to what was currently inside the Greens.

"I’m truly sorry to say that there have been times recently when I’ve had to ask if we really live up to those values? Times when instead of speaking up in an open and democratic way, a small minority of members have taken to anonymous leaks, smears, insults, undermining the work of fellow members and damaging our whole party and our reputation by doing so," he told members.

"I want to appeal to everyone, let's make sure that the next few months see a positive campaign that lifts our party up, one that lives up to the best of our values, not one that descends to the factionalism and toxicity that characterises too much of political debate."

Fast forward to October 2026 and with Mr Greer and Gillian Mackay now in charge, the former had a warning which also related to the party's internal politics and the acts and statements expressed by individual members.

He told his party not to give opportunities to their opponents to attack them.

“We are under constant attack," Mr Greer told his audience at Eden Court.

“Our opponents will take the opportunity to portray us as extreme as fringe or as un-serious or out of touch with people’s lives.

“We cannot afford to give our opponents those opportunities to attack us – 320,000 people voted for the Scottish Greens. They put their faith in us to change their lives.

“That makes the work of a Green MSP very hard, it is very personally exposing. We stand for a different way of doing politics. We face a level of scrutiny and often bad faith attacks that often aren’t faced by other parties."

The party co-leader did not cite particular examples of members giving opportunities to their opportunities.

But a couple of instances come to my mind.

First were the comments by the Greens' Q Manivannan on rape which emerged during the summer and had been made before they were elected in May this year.

The newly-elected MSP for Edinburgh and Lothians East, who is non binary, and uses the pronouns they/them, apologised after describing rape as being “on a spectrum” and that there were "different modes" of consent.

They had made the comments in The Subjective Space podcast recorded last year and which resurfaced on social media in August.

The fury their words sparked dominated the news headlines and clearly cut through to wider Scotland. I was on holiday at the time and people at a music event I was at in Kilmarnock were talking in horror about what Q had said.

Second is the motion on polyamorous relationships that made it onto the conference agenda for party's conference last weekend.

The motion, by the Greens of Colour group, calling for rights of people in polyamorous relationships was published on Thursday just ahead of the two-day event and dominated the coverage of the party's conference in the print media.

It caused something of headache for the party leadership whose leaders wanted to present a focussed message on party policies designed to combat the cost-of-living crisis and climate change and what it wants to do to make life better for struggling Scots.

But instead of talking about their promise of new rail card to cut fares for people aged from 31 to 49 -the only age group that is not eligible for a card to allow them discounted fares - Mr Greer and Ms Mackay had to answer questions from the media about the polyamorous rights call.

Reform UK and the Scottish Conservatives accused the party of being "detached from reality".

In the end Storm Boelo intervened and the motion - which was towards the end of a list ranked in terms of members' priorities - wasn't debated with the conference finishing early.

But for the Greens the damage may have been done.

Many Scots could be left with the perception they focus too much on fringe issues such as wanting to see people in multi-person relationship given the same rights as couples, rather than finding ways to alleviate the pressing problems of every day life.

If the Greens don't want further such controversies as they strive for greater influence and a bigger electoral presence, the party to my mind, will have to become more disciplined both in terms of vetting candidates - and selecting conference motions for debate.
