# 'I'm a fantasy author and I've reimagined what happened during Agatha Christie's disappearance, before she was found in Harrogate'

## It was a mystery that wouldn’t look out of place in one of her bestselling detective novels. Nearly 100 years ago, crime writer Dame Agatha Christie disappeared for 11 days, sparking a huge search effort involving hundreds of police officers and thousands of civilians.

![](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjkzM2NkM2MyLTg2MDktNDJkZi04YjA1LTE0Njg1ODY5MWFkZjphYzlkOTY2Yy05NzA2LTRiZjItODUyZC1lZDg3YzM2YmRhYTE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


It was a mystery that wouldn’t look out of place in one of her bestselling detective novels.

Nearly 100 years ago, crime writer Dame Agatha Christie disappeared for 11 days, sparking a huge search effort involving hundreds of police officers and thousands of civilians.

The Poirot and Miss Marple writer famously left her Surrey home on December 3 1926, and was later discovered staying under an assumed name at the Swan Hydropathic Hotel in Harrogate (now The Old Swan), appearing to have little recollection of how she got there.

![Author Fiona Maher.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjcxN2YwMzc5LTJmZTktNDY5OS05MDFkLTBjOGY5OGY1MGYxYzo0NTcwNGU1ZC04NjI4LTQ3M2UtYTJmOC05NzQzZWI0NDQyMGQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Although Christie’s literary career was reaching new heights at the time, she had been hit by a double blow in her personal life, with the recent death of her mother, and her husband, who had been having an affair, wanting a divorce.

Yet, whilst many theories about her vanishment have been suggested over the years, the true reason behind her disappearance remains a mystery.

What appears to be the case, however, is that Christie was hiding in plain sight.

Though she had checked into the hotel under a pseudonym, including the surname of her husband's mistress, it is reported that she spent her time in Harrogate mingling with other oblivious guests and exploring the town’s local shops.

Finally, on December 14, she was found, having been recognised by musicians at the hotel, who told police of a guest staying there that resembled the missing writer.

Fiona Maher, who has been researching the events for her latest novel, finds it all rather remarkable.

“I just don’t buy that it was so long before someone recognised the most famous woman, who was headlining all the newspapers,” reflects the 68-year-old fantasy and thriller author a century on.

“She was incredibly famous by then. And during the search, up to 15,000 people were looking for her. It became quite a thing to go out into the countryside to look for Agatha Christie - or her body…

“She was allegedly dancing every night. Sending out for clothes. She wasn’t exactly hidden away.

"Everybody was looking for her and yet it took them ten days to find her. There’s a gap there, which nobody seems to have questioned.”

North Wales-based Maher, who writes under F.R. Maher, has used that as inspiration for her new novel Sherlock Holmes and The Lady in Grey.

Rather than being in Harrogate for the time of her disappearance, Christie is placed in the novel as an assistant to Holmes as he sets to examine the case of a poisoned lady from Hay-on-Wye.

Maher mixes a fictional world involving Holmes and Dr Watson with real people and events, including Christie, and the tale of Herbert Rowse Armstrong, the only solicitor in the UK to ever be hanged for murder. She visited Hay-on-Wye to read about the case.

“I went to to all the bookshops in the town… and couldn’t believe it,” she says. “I really believe in signs and little nudges from the universe and above this shop door was written Christie and Doyle. It was a metaphysical sign that I should write this story.”

The book is a sequel to her previous novel Sherlock Holmes and The Man Who Believed in Fairies, which began life as a stage play.

That story sees Holmes clash with his creator Sir Arthur Conan Doyle over the real-life case of the Cottingley Fairies.

In 1917, Elsie Wright, along with her cousin Frances Griffiths, took photographs at Yorkshire’s Cottingley Beck near her home which appeared to show the two girls surrounded by fairies.

The pictures divided the country, with Conan Doyle among those insisting the images were real - evidence of supernatural phenomena – and others (rightly, it turned out) believing they had been faked.

Maher has a long-standing interest in folklore. Having grown up wanting to be an author (she says she got sidetracked “by the tricky business of actually making a living”), she self-published her first novel in 2013 after years of creative work as an artist, craft maker, and lecturer.

The Last Changeling was a fantasy tale exploring the fairies of old English folklore and a hidden story behind present day people and its success inspired Maher to go on to study Creative Writing at Liverpool John Moores University.

She has since established a storytelling career as a novelist, playwright, screenwriter, and folklorist, recently having become a member of the Crime Writers’ Association, and Maher is also the driving force and champion behind The Legendary Llangollen Faery Festival, an annual affair celebrated as the largest fairy-themed event in the UK.

“I set that up to publicise my (first) book,” she says. “It’s grown into this monster – we get thousands through the gates each August.”

Soon, it will be on to writing book three in her Sherlock Holmes series. “I’m looking for a real life, interesting case (for it),” she says. “I’d quite like to take Holmes to America but people typically want to read about him being in London or cosy English towns.”

Whatever she decides, Maher is drawn to stories where the extraordinary meets everyday life. And, for now at least, her attention is back on Sherlock Holmes and The Lady in Grey, the release of which comes just ahead of the centenary of Christie’s baffling disappearance.

“What really happened to that famous lady novelist who disappeared in December 1926?,” she asks, teasing: “Well, how about this for the answer.”
