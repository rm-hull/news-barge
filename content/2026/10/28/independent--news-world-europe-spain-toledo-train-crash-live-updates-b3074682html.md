At least 46 people are feared to be dead after two high-speed trains collided in Spain.

The two trains crashed just after 9am, causing two carriages to derail, a government spokesperson for the Castilla La Mancha region said. Spanish media has reported that at least 46 people have been killed in the crash with local authorities warning the number of casualties could rise.

A total of 544 people were travelling on the two trains impacted by the incident, which occurred between the towns of Mora and Orgaz, and involved a Renfe and an Iryo high speed train travelling from Madrid to Seville.

Footage from ⁠regional Castilla-La Mancha Television showed ​a derailed ⁠train with at least one carriage remaining on an elevated section of track while twisted metal, ⁠broken panels and wreckage could be seen strewn across an ​embankment ⁠beneath the line.

One survivor, Jesus Jeronimo, 54, told *El Pais* that “people were thrown from the train” during the “brutal” incident.

“We thought we had derailed. Then, little by little, we got out and saw that our train was tilted. There were many injured people and a lot of people shaken up.”

Prime minister Pedro Sanchez said that he was “shocked by the serious train accident in Toledo” as he passed on his “affection, solidarity, and support to all the victims and their families.”

## Express train halted at signal was struck by high-speed train

Details are coming in about what happened as two high-speed trains collided near Toledo.

An express train operated by Iryo was halted at a signal when it was struck by a high-speed train operated by Renfe, a person briefed on the disaster has said.

## 100 military personnel called in to help with Toledo incident

At least 100 military personnel have been called in to support authorities after a train accident in Toledo, the Emergency Military Unit has reported.

## 'Europe mourns with you' - Ursula Von der Leyen

Ursula Von der Leyen has expressed her condolences following a major train collision in Spain.

The president of the European Commission is one of many world leaders putting out statements following the incident.

“Just received the devastating news from Toledo on the collision of two high-speed trains. My heart goes out to the victims’ families and loved ones. Today, Europe mourns with you.”

## Emergency services issues call out for blood transfusions

Madrid’s emergency services has issued a call out for blood donations following the train accident in Toledo.

On social media, the emergency services said: “Urgent call for blood donations in the face of a possible sudden increase in care needs.”

## Spanish Royal Family speaks out after Toledo train accident

The Spanish Royal Family has expressed concern after the train accident in Toledo.

A statement from the family on social media read: “We are moved and concerned by the train accident in Urda (Toledo). We share in the sorrow of the families and loved ones of the deceased and send our affection to the injured and those affected.

“Our support also goes to the emergency teams and all those working in the area to help as much as possible under these circumstances.”

## At least 46 killed in train collision - report

At least 46 people have been killed after two trains collided near Toledo, *El Español* has reported, citing the Civil Protection authority.

Local authorities have said they believe the number could rise, which would make it one of the worst train accidents in Spain’s modern history.
