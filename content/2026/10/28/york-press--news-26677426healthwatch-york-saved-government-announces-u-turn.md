A York health watchdog service has been saved after the government dropped plans to axe it.

Health secretary Yvette Cooper has ditched plans to abandon Healthwatch England, meaning Healthwatch York is no longer at risk of closure.

Siân Balsom, Healthwatch York manager, said: "We are delighted about this announcement. This means that independent health and social care lived experience will continue to influence the quality and equality of care.”

Ms Cooper told the Guardian the government will retain Healthwatch England and the local Healthwatch branches, “while strengthening them to ensure patient voices are heard and can influence real change across health and care services”.

“Patients need a voice in our NHS,” she said. “I’ve always been clear on the importance of people and communities having a say in local services and that’s why I said I would review all reforms in this area.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21641262.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Health secretary Yvette Cooper *(Image: Jordan Pettitt/Press Association)*

Plans to axe the Healthwatch were announced by then health secretary Wes Streeting last year who said there were “way too many checkers and not enough doers” in the health system.

Under Mr Streeting’s proposals, responsibility for gathering feedback on health services would have passed from Healthwatch York to the Humber and North Yorkshire Integrated Care Board, which also plans, funds and oversees local health services.

The plans faced criticism from MPs across the political spectrum including York Central’s Rachael Maskell who said Healthwatch played a “vital role” in holding health services to account.

Ms Maskell said she was "really pleased by the news that Healthwatch will continue to operate and will not be abolished".

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21641250.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rachael Reeves, York Healthwatch Team Leader; Siân Balsom, York Healthwatch Manager; and Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central *(Image: Supplied)*

"I have been campaigning against its potential abolition, and raised my concerns whenever I have had the opportunity in Parliament and with relevant ministers," the Labour MP said. "I am so glad that government has listened and taken action to ensure that this service will continue.

"Local Healthwatch branches do incredible work within their communities and provide vital accountability within the health system. They ensure that local people have the ability to voice their concerns, shape the agenda, and call out failings. Its independence from government and from any other agencies ensures that there is necessary trust in their work.

"I have worked with my local Healthwatch branch on a number of topics, including concerns around mental health agencies, and I know just how invaluable their work is. It is clear that Healthwatch is an essential part of our health system, and I am so pleased that the government recognise this."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19696118.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Siân Balsom, manager of Healthwatch York *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Balsom said Healthwatch York heard from almost 10,000 local people last year, adding: “Clearly we have been doing something right.”

"We are so pleased that we can continue our work,” she said. “It comes at a time when Healthwatch York has become a trusted partner in the local health and care system.”

Healthwatch has been praised for its work highlighting issues in the health services, such as a recent report which found there is “a lot more that needs to happen” to give York women confidence that their health concerns will be taken seriously.

It also revealed earlier this year that people in York and North Yorkshire had been forced to “turn down” hospital appointments for “essential” treatment because they are no longer eligible for NHS-funded transport to get them there.
