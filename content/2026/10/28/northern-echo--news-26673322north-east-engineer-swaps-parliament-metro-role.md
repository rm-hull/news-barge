An engineer who helped keep Parliament running has swapped Westminster for the Tyne and Wear Metro.

Ben Brooks spent three years as lead electrical engineer at the Palace of Westminster before becoming Nexus’s head of engineering.

Mr Brooks worked across the parliamentary estate, including Big Ben, both Houses and the area where Guy Fawkes was caught during the 1605 Gunpowder Plot.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21636667.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Engineer Ben Brooks near Big Ben in London when he worked at the UK Parliament   *(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Brooks, 35, from Newcastle, said: "It was such an honour and a privilege to work at Westminster.

"I was delighted to join Nexus when we moved back up north to start a family, but I’ll always cherish my time down in London.

"Working in Parliament felt like I was really doing something for the good of the country, right at the heart of our democracy."

He worked on major projects including Big Ben’s £80 million refurbishment and preparations for Queen Elizabeth II to lie in state at Westminster Hall.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21636665.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ben Brooks *(Image: Supplied)*

He also helped renovate both Houses of Parliament and nearby offices.

Mr Brooks said: "I got to see every nook and cranny of the place.

"I saw areas behind the scenes the public don’t really get to ever go into.

"It’s one of the most famous buildings in the world.

"It’s a truly fascinating place to work.

"You can feel the history when you walk around the place.

"A real highlight for me was getting involved in the conservation of the Elizabeth Tower, which most people know as Big Ben.

"I got to work with the estate historian on that one, which was a particular privilege.

"The clock hands were returned to their original Victorian colour scheme.

"My role was to work on the lighting systems which light up Big Ben's clock face at night.

"We had to get the colour scheme absolutely right."

He also helped prepare for major national events.

Mr Brooks said: "When Queen Elizabeth II passed away, I was called to help with some of the work that was required at Westminster Abbey and at Westminster Hall, where Her Majesty was to lie in state.

"I did specifications and design work for the many temporary installations that were needed for this huge moment of national mourning."

Mr Brooks began his engineering career 13 years ago after graduating from Northumbria University and has since worked across the UK and overseas.

Mr Brooks said: "I would recommend a career in engineering to anyone.

"It’s brilliant. No two days are the same.

"I’m excited about this latest chapter, working for Nexus, and supporting the many public transport services we deliver, not least the Metro."

He urged others to consider an engineering career with Nexus.

Mr Brooks said: "We currently have five engineering jobs advertised at Nexus.

"It's a really exciting time to come and work for us.

"Anyone who is interested can get more information on our website, www.nexus.org/engineering-vacancies."
