A pharmacist has returned to the North East university where she trained to help students look after their health.

Donna Roberts, 39, graduated from the University of Sunderland with a Master’s degree in pharmacy in 2010 and has since supported communities across the region.

She returned to the university for its Feel Good in Sunderland event, organised by the CitySpace Wellbeing Team.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21636149.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pharmacist and University of Sunderland graduate Donna Roberts back on campus *(Image: Sunderland University)*

Ms Roberts, from Gateshead, said: "I want to make sure that everyone has access to healthcare and support, including students.

"A lot of students are young, potentially away from home, and can often be unsure how to access certain healthcare needs.

"If I can help make this access easier for them, it ensures they get the help and support they need."

Students received healthcare advice and could check whether they qualified for a free MenACWY meningitis vaccination.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21636150.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pharmacist and University of Sunderland graduate Donna Roberts back on campus *(Image: Sunderland University)*

The day brought together NHS services, community pharmacy teams and student support organisations.

More than 100 students attended and 66 received vaccinations.

The event gave Ms Roberts the chance to give back to the university that launched her career.

She began her career with the Co-operative Pharmacy, now Well Pharmacy, before becoming a relief pharmacist and branch manager in Seaham and Sunderland.

She later returned to the university to complete a postgraduate diploma in clinical pharmacy in 2014.

Ms Roberts later worked as a medicines optimisation and practice pharmacist in GP surgeries across Durham and Newcastle before becoming a locum pharmacist after the birth of her first child.

In November 2018, she and her husband, Derek, also a University of Sunderland pharmacy graduate, took over Riverview Health Centre Pharmacy in Sunderland.

The couple have since added two more pharmacies in the Durham area.

Derek manages medicines procurement and business operations, while Donna leads the clinical work.

She said: "Being part of the community is incredibly important to us and is something we will always strive to achieve.

"I hope people in the community feel comfortable in coming to us for support, not just for health needs but also for emotional support.

"It's not always the big things that make a difference; sometimes it's the little things like asking how someone's relative is after a hospital stay or helping to arrange appointments for people who are struggling."

Ms Roberts continues to support the university by offering placements to MPharm and Overseas Pharmacists Assessment Programme (OSPAP) students.

The placements give students practical skills for their future careers.

Ms Roberts said her undergraduate degree gave her insight into different areas of pharmacy, let her explore career options through placements and developed communication, teamwork and problem-solving skills she has used throughout her career.

Dr Adrian Moore, head of the University of Sunderland’s School of Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences, said: "It is wonderful to see our graduates making such a positive difference to the health and wellbeing of their communities.

"Community pharmacists play a vital role in providing trusted health advice and support, and initiatives such as Feel Good in Sunderland highlight the importance of multi-disciplinary teams working together to ensure students know where to turn for help.

"Donna’s journey from MPharm student to community pharmacist and business owner demonstrates the diverse opportunities a pharmacy degree can offer, while her ongoing support for students shows how our graduates continue to shape the future of the profession and make a real difference in their local communities."
