A leading North Yorkshire hospitality boss says today's Budget offers only limited relief for pubs and restaurants, as key cost pressures remain unaddressed.

While welcoming the increase in Small Business Rates Relief, Simon Cotton, managing director of the Fat Badger Group in Harrogate, said the hospitality sector in York and Harrogate continues to face "significant cost pressures" on multiple fronts.

Mr Cotton said: "Today's Budget contains some welcome measures for hospitality businesses, particularly the increase in Small Business Rates Relief, which will provide valuable support for many independent operators and smaller venues across the country.

"Any move that helps protect high streets, support local employers and encourage investment in town and city centres is a positive step.

"For hospitality businesses in York and Harrogate, however, the picture is more mixed.

"While some smaller operators will benefit from enhanced business rates support, many hospitality businesses continue to face significant cost pressures."

He pointed to the confirmed rise in the National Living Wage as a particular concern, saying it will "add substantially to employment costs at a time when wages, National Insurance, utilities, food and supplier costs are already placing enormous pressure on margins."

Although Mr Cotton said he supports "fair pay" for hospitality workers, he argued that wage increases must be matched with policies that enable businesses to "remain competitive, continue investing and create jobs."

He also criticised the continued rise in alcohol duty, warning that "eventually those costs have to be recovered somewhere, and that risks putting further pressure on prices for consumers."

The biggest disappointment, he said, was the lack of any mention of reducing VAT for hospitality.

Mr Cotton said: "Industry leaders have consistently argued that a targeted VAT reduction would be one of the most effective ways of stimulating growth, encouraging investment, supporting employment and strengthening Britain's tourism economy.

"Given the importance of hospitality to local communities and regional economies, it is disappointing that there was not even an indication that such a proposal would be considered in the future."

He described the Budget as "a welcome step in some areas, particularly around business rates," but not the "game-changing moment that many hospitality operators had hoped for."

Mr Cotton plans to discuss the Budget further with Harrogate & Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon during a breakfast meeting.

He said: "Constructive dialogue between government and industry will be essential if we are to ensure hospitality businesses can continue to play their vital role in the prosperity of York, Harrogate and the wider region."
