Fresh support for families, pensioners and first-time buyers in today's Budget have been welcomed in Bradford.

Chancellor John Healey’s plans include a £100 energy payment, changes to pension tax, a freeze on the cash ISA allowance and new help for first-time home buyers.

The Budget has been welcomed by politicians and business leaders in Bradford, who say it will offer much-needed relief to households and businesses during a difficult period.

Bradford South MP Judith Cummins called the Budget “welcome support” for families and businesses and said it would invest in the constituency’s future.

Ms Cummins said: "£100 towards energy bills for almost every household in my constituency will be a great help for families this winter.

"More than 44,000 households in Bradford South will benefit from the Energy Bills Breathing Space scheme announced by the Chancellor in his Budget.

"This comes in addition to the Government’s earlier removal of VAT from electricity bills and the promise to remove standing charges from gas bills next year.

"Conflict in the Middle East and Europe is hitting people here through higher energy costs.

"The Budget gives families and firms breathing space now and fresh hope for the future with plans to lower costs permanently.

"Taking more business out of paying business rates is great news for our local high streets.

"The Government is also delivering a real-terms business-rates freeze for eligible retail, hospitality and leisure properties for the next two years.

"This is a Budget that provides welcome support for families and businesses and invests in Bradford South’s future.

"It combines direct support for people now with a clear plan for good opportunities and future economic growth."

Victoria Wainwright, chair of West & North Yorkshire Chamber of Commerce, also welcomed several of the Chancellor’s measures, particularly those supporting small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).

Ms Wainwright said: "As a Chamber of Commerce, we were adamant prior to the Budget that there should be no new taxes on SMEs and it is pleasing that Government has heeded this message.

"The rise in the threshold that firms begin paying business rates is to be welcomed, especially for those working in retail and hospitality.

"Measures to prevent online marketplaces allowing users of their platforms to avoid paying VAT also has the potential to boost small businesses.

"Similarly, increased fiscal devolution to our mayors will mean greater decision-making powers in our communities.

"The £15 million in funding for Leeds College of Building will also make a huge difference to construction skill capacity for the whole region.

"However, there still remain significant challenges for employers that were not addressed in this Budget.

"Next year’s Spending Review needs to focus on reducing the significant cost pressures facing businesses if the country is to deliver the growth it needs."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21489596.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lee Bloomfield, chief executive of Manningham Housing Association *(Image: UGC)*

Lee Bloomfield, chief executive of Manningham Housing Association (MHA), welcomed the Budget’s focus on social and affordable housing; MHA manages more than 1,400 homes for over 6,000 residents in Bradford and Keighley.

Mr Bloomfield said: "I was pleased to hear John Healey repeat the Prime Minister’s pledge to deliver the biggest growth in affordable housing numbers since the Second World War.

"However, both the Chancellor and Andy Burnham must now be as good as their word.

"The Chancellor has committed an additional £1.5 billion for social and affordable homes, taking the overall investment to more than £40 billion over next decade.

"These are impressive figures but seeing homes come out of the ground is now the ultimate test."

Mr Bloomfield also welcomed the Government’s commitment to tackling rough sleeping.

He said: "I also commend the Prime Minister and Chancellor for making tackling rough sleeping a cross-Government priority under their watch.

"Manningham Housing Association stands ready to do everything we can to help achieve these worthy objectives.

"The pandemic proved that we could take people off our streets in the short-term.

"Now is the moment to deliver a durable, long-term solution."

The Chancellor’s wider package also includes plans to make the 55p-a-mile tax-free mileage allowance permanent.
