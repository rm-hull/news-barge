GRANTS are now available to improve the look of shopfronts in Skipton.

The Shop Front Improvement Grant Scheme, managed by Heart of Skipton Community Interest Company (CIC) , will support up to 20 businesses in the town centre with funding ranging from £1,000 to £3,000.

Edwin Rothwell, manager at Heart of Skipton CIC, said: "Regeneration is going to be a key driver for Skipton over the next 10 years.

"The Town Investment Plan is already underway and we need to strengthen our experience of community delivery, so we are ready to take advantage of the devolution opportunities that are coming.

"We have won investment here from the Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, David Skaith, and we will deliver lasting results with good quality improvements."

Built on a community-first model, the programme aims to keep skills and spending within Skipton by prioritising projects that use local contractors and craftspeople.

Additional points are awarded for using skilled trades such as joinery, traditional signwriting, and specialist glazing and heritage glass.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21641690.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hearts coffee shop in Swadford Street *(Image: Edwin Rothwell)*

Mr Rothwell previously presented the scheme to Skipton Civic Society and incorporated their feedback into the project criteria.

The programme also rewards businesses that involve apprentices or trainees from an accredited course, contractors working with a construction or built environment college programme, or those willing to let the scheme be used as a live teaching brief.

Points are available for improving accessibility features—such as step-free access and signage contrast and tactile elements—as well as for removing plastic signage or adding planting.

Applications are open to all businesses with qualifying shopfronts in Skipton, including national chains and franchises.

While independent businesses score higher in some areas, larger brands can compete by committing to local tradespeople, craft specialists, or educational partnerships.

A Gateway Exception Rule allows businesses outside the standard criteria to apply by contacting the CIC in advance.

Such applications are considered lower priority but are designed to ensure valuable projects are not excluded due to technicalities.

Applicants must contribute at least 10 per cent of total project costs, with higher contributions earning additional points.

After assessment, a summary will be published detailing the number of applications, funded projects, and how funds were distributed.

The selection panel is made up of a representative from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority (YNYCA), one from the voluntary and community sector, and a local business representative.

Panellists declare any conflicts of interest and are replaced for specific applications where necessary.

Scoring is based on a 135-point matrix, published in advance so applicants and the public can see exactly how decisions are made.

The scheme is part of a wider regeneration effort funded through the Vibrant and Sustainable High Streets element of the Mayoral Investment Fund, managed by the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

The broader project also includes upgrades to street furniture and wayfinding signage throughout the town.

The initiative was developed with support from North Yorkshire Council, whose planning officers advised on consent requirements for works in the town's Conservation Area.

Full eligibility criteria, application details, and the scoring matrix are available at heartofskipton.co.uk.
