A family has sold two long-standing Scottish city businesses as a key director has stepped back.

The sale of Reardon’s snooker clubs in Glasgow marks “a new chapter for the established venues”.

Acting on behalf of the Links family, broker CDLH said it was “delighted to announce the sale” of the two city clubs, as the “market remains very strong for licensed and leisure businesses”.

![club](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637809.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Family sells long-standing businesses as a director steps back *(Image: CDLH)*

A spokesperson for CDLH said: “The Links family has owned the businesses for more than 25 years, building a strong customer base and successful businesses.

“Having reached retirement age, the family’s principal director will now step away from the licensed trade and enjoy a well-earned rest.”

Reardon’s Southside, on Pollokshaws Road, was sold in early 2026 to licensed operator Paul Cardow. Following a high-quality refurbishment, the venue has been rebranded as Langside Social.

The city centre business has been sold to experienced licensed operators John Burns and Fraser MacIntyre.

It has “a superb trading history and strong profitability”.

![club](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637808.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Family sells long-standing businesses as a director steps back *(Image: CDLH)*

The spokesperson also said: “The purchasers plan to maintain and grow the current business.

“The sales mark a new chapter for both established Glasgow venues. CDLH wishes the new owners every success.”

Peter Darroch, director at CDLH, who handled the sale, said: “It was a pleasure acting for the Links family on the successful sale.

“The market remains very strong for licensed and leisure businesses with a track record of strong revenue and profitability.

"It is crucial that assets are packaged correctly, with sound financial analysis before marketing, to ensure full value is achieved.

"We have several purchasers seeking similar licensed and leisure assets."

Reardon’s was set up “in conjunction with six-times world snooker champion Ray Reardon".

The ﬁrst club was located in Trongate. “Reardons New City appeared some 15 years later and in 2005 moved to Hope Street, becoming Reardon’s Central, Scotland’s ﬁnest,” it recounted on its website.
