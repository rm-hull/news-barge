Scots are set for calmer weather after a battering of wind and rain from two autumn storms.

Mt Met office said that high pressure is moving in over the UK, bringing drier conditions in the wake of storms Austen and Boelo, which brought torrential rain and high winds.

Met Office forecaster Craig Snell said the UK would see a “big flip” over the next 48 hours.

He said: “We’re saying goodbye to the low pressure and saying hello again to high pressure.”

But he said the calmer conditions will bring a new hazard — with dense and stubborn fog threatening to disrupt morning journeys.

Mr Snell said Wednesday would still be “fairly blustery” with scattered showers before winds begin to drop in the evening.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637607.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An aerial view of Dumbarton Golf Club submerged by flood water following the recent storms *(Image: Getty)*

Two men were killed by falling trees on Sunday: a man in his 20s in Finchley, north London; and Richard Baines, 60, in Dumfries and Galloway.

A woman in her 50s died after being pulled from the sea off the Isle of Wight.

She was walking her dog when she was reportedly caught by waves on the revetment between Sandown and Shanklin on Monday, according to the Isle of Wight County Press.

Her dog survived, the newspaper reported.

Mr Snell said Storm Austen hit Cornwall and Wales hardest, while Boelo bore down on Scotland, Northern Ireland and northern England.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21637153.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The recent storms brought high winds and heavy seas *(Image: PA)*

Provisional Met Office data shows Storm Austen brought a maximum gust of 69 knots (about 79mph) to the Isle of Wight, while Storm Boelo saw wind speeds in some exposed areas reach the low 80s mph.

Heavy rain was also a feature of both storms, with Abbey St Bathans in Berwickshire recording 77.6 mm of rain during Storm Austen, while 134.4 mm was recorded in Ross and Cromarty during Storm Boelo.

Mr Snell said: “Many of us have certainly seen some very wet and windy weather.”

But next weekend is expected to be “very different” to the last.

However. the Scottish Government is urging those in coastal areas to remain vigilant as a yellow risk of flooding is still in place on Tuesday and through to Wednesday in northern parts of mainland Scotland and the Northern Isles.
