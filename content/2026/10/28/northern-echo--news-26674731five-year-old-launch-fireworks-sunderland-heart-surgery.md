A five-year-old girl will light up the sky at this year’s Red Sky Foundation Fireworks Extravaganza, when she launched the display at Ashbrooke Sports Club in Sunderland.

Ivanna Potts will captured hearts across the North East last year when her family raised more than £250,000 for life-saving surgery in the United States.

Ivanna was born with hypoplastic right heart and heterotaxy syndrome, a severe form of congenital heart disease.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21637692.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Following a successful 12-hour heart operation in March, Ivanna is now back home. *(Image: Red Sky Foundation')*

Jack Potts and Shannon Green, Ivanna’s parents, said: "Within weeks, your kindness helped us raise the funds Ivanna needed to receive specialist care and interventions in Boston.

"Now she is home, thriving, going to school, running around and enjoying childhood without constantly feeling breathless."

"There are no words for what this means to us.

"Thank you to everyone in the North East and beyond who donated, shared our story and supported us."

They added: "We are especially grateful to the Red Sky Foundation for standing by us and helping make this journey possible, including arranging our flights to Boston.

"Ivanna will still need ongoing specialist care and interventions here in the UK, including trips to London every three months, as well as continued support from her medical teams.

"It is a lot to take in, but today she is home and living the life every child deserves.

"From the bottom of our hearts, thank you for bringing our little girl home."

Last year, the Red Sky Foundation donated all general admission ticket profits from its 2025 Fireworks Extravaganza to Ivanna’s fundraising appeal.

The charity also arranged the family’s flights to Boston.

Thanks to the generosity of supporters, more than £250,000 was raised, allowing Ivanna to travel to Boston Children’s Hospital in March 2026 for a complex 12-hour heart operation.

The surgery was a success, and her family says it has transformed her future.

Dr (Hon.) Sergio Petrucci MBE, founder of the Red Sky Foundation, said: "Ivanna’s story has touched every single one of us at Red Sky Foundation.

"Last year, we watched a family fighting to give their little girl a chance at life, and we saw the most extraordinary response from people across the North East who came together to make that possible.

"To see Ivanna now, back home with her family and doing so well after everything she has been through, is absolutely wonderful.

"It’s the outcome we were all hoping and praying for."

They added: "When we supported Ivanna’s appeal through last year’s Fireworks Extravaganza, we could only hope that one day we’d be celebrating news like this.

"To now welcome her back to press the button and light up the Sunderland sky is going to be an incredibly emotional and special moment for everyone involved.

"Ivanna is a true little heart warrior, and I can’t think of anyone more deserving of the honour.

"It’s a reminder of what can happen when a community comes together to make a difference."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21637693.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ivanna Potts will press the button and officially launch this year's display. *(Image: Red Sky Foundation')*

Ivanna remains under specialist care, with regular appointments every three months in London, but is now able to enjoy a more active and fulfilling childhood.

The Red Sky Foundation Fireworks Extravaganza has become a staple in Sunderland’s events calendar, offering an evening of entertainment while raising funds for the charity’s life-saving work.

The event will take place on Wednesday, November 4, 2026.

Gates open at 4.30pm, with the fireworks display set to begin at 8pm.
