Brand told jurors he couldn't find anyone to give evidence on his behalf after 'devastating' Dispatches documentary

Earlier, Brand told the court that he had struggled to find anyone to give evidence on his behalf, saying the consequences of the September 2023 investigation by the Sunday Times, Times, and Channel 4 Dispatches were “devastating”.

His barrister, Ian White, asked: “Were you able to persuade a single person with relevant evidence to give to come to give evidence on your behalf?”

Brand said: “I have not.”

Winter asked: “Would they touch you with a barge pole?”

Brand said: “No.”

Brand later told the court he did not consider the Dispatches investigation a documentary, saying: “I call it a TV show.”

During cross-examination, Brand told the court that he “said very many foolish things as a young man”.

Responding to the prosecutor’s question about wanting to have sex with 5,000 women in his lifetime, Brand told the court that was like a riff of a joke made by American film-maker Woody Allen, who reportedly said the only advice his father gave him was about “F-word women”.

Continuing her cross-examination, Karmy-Jones asked Brand whether he had a “goal” of sleeping with 5,000 women. Brand told the court he did not.

Karmy-Jones continued: “You said that consent was fundamentally necessary and in many ways more important than sex itself, that wasn’t what you thought between 1999 and 2009 was it?”

Brand said: “Consent is absolutely non-negotiable.”

Winter moved on to the final count of sexual assault, involving a woman who said she met Brand while working on a project for Channel 4 in 2009. She alleged he demanded a massage and put his head into her groin area, which Brand denied.

Concluding his questions for Brand, Winter said: “Are you guilty or innocent of these allegations?”

Winter has now turned to count 6, in which a woman alleged Brand anally raped her in 2009 after watching his comedy gig at Hammersmith Apollo.

Brand told the court that he had a “striking” and “memorable” sexual encounter with the woman in a dressing room before the alleged incident. He had saved her number on his phone with the initials “AP”, which he told the court meant “anal play”.

Brand told jurors he was “excited and enthusiastic” about meeting her again.

Winter then went on to ask Brand about the third and fourth charge of sexual assault and rape, in which a woman alleged Brand grabbed her breasts before pulling her into a toilet at a bar in Soho and orally raped her in 2004.

Brand said he invited her to go into the lavatory to “fool around”. When asked by Winter whether she was happy to do so, he said “yes”.

He described the woman as “wide-eyed, enthusiastic and flirty”.

Winter asked Brand about the second count of attempted indecent assault, in which it is alleged he tried to drag a female MTV worker into a toilet in 2001.

Winter asked whether Brand “intended any non-consensual assault, sexual or otherwise” on the woman.

Winter is asking Brand about the first alleged complainant, a woman who alleged she was raped by the comedian after meeting him on the fringes of the Labour party conference in Bournemouth in 1999.

Brand told the court she had invited him to her hotel room, and that he “thought at the bare minimum it’s better than sleeping in shared accommodation” with his team, and that he “hoped and thought that there was a possibility of sex”.

'My entire life has been consumed by these allegations,' Brand tells court

Brand told the court that around the time of the Channel 4 Dispatches documentary, which reported the allegations as part of an investigation with the Times and the Sunday Times, his baby son had to have open heart surgery at the Great Ormond Street hospital in London.

He told jurors that the hospital refused to let him stay with his son “due to the materials published”.

Brand said his “entire life has been consumed by these allegations”.

He added: “I have devoted myself to these matters.”

Defence to begin presenting its case in Russell Brand's trial

The defence is to begin presenting its case in Russell Brand’s trial at Southwark crown court, where he faces allegations of rape and sexual assault against six women.

The prosecution’s case has drawn to a close after nearly two weeks of evidence, with the jury to hear from the defence today.

Russell Brand arriving at Southwark crown court on Wednesday. Photograph: Yui Mok/PA

In yesterday’s hearing, the jury was told by the judge, Mr Justice Bennathan, that the indecent assault charge against Brand has been amended to attempted indecent assault. It relates to an allegation that Brand grabbed a female MTV worker by the arm and tried to drag her into a toilet. Bennathan told jurors he had reached the conclusion that there was insufficient evidence to support the charge of indecent assault.

The court also heard Brand was interviewed by police on four occasions – in November and December 2023, January 2024 and July 2025. Brand’s pre-prepared statement to police in November 2023 was read out in court by the prosecutor, Riel Karmy-Jones.

In it, he said: “As I have repeatedly stated publicly and written about extensively, I led a very promiscuous life in the past, which I have spent years openly denouncing.

“I have never engaged in non-consensual sexual activity.”

Brand, 51, has been charged with three counts of rape, three counts of sexual assault and one count of attempted indecent assault, in allegations dating between 1999 and 2009. He has pleaded not guilty to all the charges.
