The York Board Games Convention (YBGC) celebrated its most successful event to date – welcoming a record number of tabletop enthusiasts over the weekend.

The event, held at St Peter’s School in York, was completely sold out, with the total footfall at this year’s convention increased by 44% compared to their biggest previous year.

The weekend was filled with gamers of all ages who enjoyed the vast board games library, an increased number of trade stands, and open gaming areas.

Highlights of the weekend included an extensive Tabletop Roleplaying Game (RPG) programme, featuring quirky fan favourites such as *Crash Pandas* (where players embody raccoons in a high-stakes drag race), package-delivering elves, and crime-solving grandmothers.

The weekend also showcased high-profile social deduction gaming, featuring plentiful sessions of *Blood on the Clocktower* and an exclusive preview of*Counter Espionage*, the upcoming social deduction title from game designer and former* Traitor*, Ivan Brett. Visitors also had the opportunity to sit down and play games alongside contestants from the hit TV series* The Traitors*, including Wilfred Webster and Jessie Roux.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/ybgc-2026-event-13.webp)

*Traitors* star Wilfred Webster at YBGC

Despite the rapid expansion, organisers stressed that community spirit remained the core focus of the weekend.

“We are blown away by the response to this year’s convention,” said Ryan Willis, convention director of York Board Games Convention. “To see footfall leap by 44% and sell out completely shows just how passionate and growing the tabletop community is, both locally in Yorkshire and across the UK.

“Beyond the record numbers, the real highlight was the atmosphere. Hundreds of people of all ages came together to share space, make new friends, and enjoy playing games together.”

“Year-on-year we’re continuing to build an event that’s open to all”, said Matt Coward-Gibbs, convention director of YBGC.

“Whether that is providing something as mundane as easy-to-read signage, increasing the heights of tables to allow wheelchair users the ability to join in games, or providing card-holders to help motor-support needs, we want everyone who wishes to be part of YBGC to have no barriers to entry.

“If you want to play tabletop games: York Board Games Convention is the space for you.”

Following the success of this year’s gathering, organisers have confirmed that the York Board Games Convention will return to St Peter’s School in October 2027.
