### TechRadar Verdict

There’s a lot to like about the Acer Swift Air 14, starting with its almost budget-level price. But it’s more than a cheap laptop, as it looks great in a sleek Ultrabook kind of way, and is about as portable as a 14-inch laptop can be. Plus, the battery life is about two days’ worth, which is certainly impressive. Of course, it’s not the most powerful machine, the audio quality is severely limited by its form factor, and, for some reason, both USB-C ports are on the same side.

### Acer Swift Spin 14 AI review: Two Minute Review

I really like the Acer Swift Air 14 and not just because of the price. It’s very portable, attractive in that clean minimalist way popularised by a certain Cupertino company, and comes with a battery life that’s as good as any other I’ve come across.

Acer has been impressing me with its recent Swift laptops, and this one is no exception. Of course, the price tag makes it a no-brainer for anyone wanting a solid, portable laptop at an almost budget price tag. It’s not sub-$500 / £500, but it’s close. I certainly think it’s one of the best laptops for the price.

It does, however, have some of the shortcomings that even the best Ultrabooks have. It’s not the most powerful laptop out there, by any stretch of the imagination – this is for people who just want to go online and do some office work or stream, not game or edit video. And the audio suffers from the laptop's smaller design.

Those things actually don’t bother me. That’s not what this laptop is supposed to be used for. But, Acer makes the same weird design choice that even Apple makes on more budget models, where there are two USB-C ports, both of which can be used for power, but, for whatever reason, they’re on the same side of the computer.

Still, quibbles aside, the amount of computer you get for the price in the Acer Swift Air 14 is pretty impressive.

### Acer Swift Air 14 review: Price & Availability

* **How much does it cost?** $699.99 / £749.99 (about AU$1,010)
* **When is it available?** Available now
* **Where can you get it?** Available in the US and UK

If you’re looking for the Windows version of the Apple MacBook Neo, the Acer Swift Air 14 offers just as much value. The base configuration might start at a higher $699.99 / £749.99 (about AU$1,010) price tag, but if you want to match the Swift Air 14’s 512GB of storage, then two laptops end up at the same price. Unfortunately, that currently excludes Australia as it hasn’t yet been released there.

Granted, you’re not getting the most powerful laptop out there. If you’re looking for something similar but better, the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI is that computer. Sure, it’s $1,499.99 / £1,399 (about AU$2,150), but its Qualcomm CPU has double the cores and triple the NPUs than the base version of the Acer Swift Air 14’s Intel Core 5 320 CPU. You can upgrade the Acer Swift Air 14 to a configuration with the Intel Core 7 350 CPU and 12GB of RAM, but it’s still not going to do the same heavy lifting. Plus, the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI has a 360-degree hinge, touchscreen, and stylus.

### Acer Swift Air 14 review: Design

* **Very attractive, portable design**
* **Keyboard and touchpad are good but not amazing**
* **Both USB-Cs are on the left**

The Acer Swift Air 14 is a gorgeous laptop. I’ve only seen the Sage Green color in person – the color of the test unit for this review – but the colors, lid etchings, and aluminum chassis all present a very aesthetically pleasing package. I imagine that the other three colors look just as good in that way that I think makes this an attractive alternative to the Macbook Neo.

Also in its favor is its utter portability. It weighs just over two and a half pounds. That’s about the same as an ankle weight. And it’s just over a half inch thick at its thinnest point. I don’t think you can really get any more portable without going down in screen size to a 13-inch laptop.

![The Acer Swift Air 14 is attractive in its various colors. The reviewed model came in Sage green.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DUULzf2xbdyB6BgDHbSK6B-1200-80.jpg)

Speaking of which, its 14-inch screen is a good-looking, if not amazing, non-touch display with a 16:10 aspect ratio and 1920x1200p resolution. It does get up to 300 nits of brightness – I typically have the brightness up only halfway when I’m using it.

I appreciate the 180-degree hinge, but that’s mostly because I feel like I don’t have to be as gentle with the Acer Swift Air 14 when opening it. I could see its use, though, if you want to share your screen with someone sitting across from you. If you do that, the viewing angle is more than enough for both people to see at 170 degrees.

![The Acer Swift Air 14 comes with a 180-degree hinge for a bit more flexibility.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/xyuPoDGsqfD93Fibcs6U2B-1200-80.jpg)

Moving on, the keyboard is pleasant to use if nothing spectacular. I used the Acer Swift Air 14 to write this review and didn’t feel fatigue from typing. At the same time, it’s a linear keyboard with a somewhat short travel.

The trackpad is fairly responsive and accurate, though I found it to be a little too sensitive at times when my thumb was a little too close to it and had trouble getting the cursor to cooperate.

![The Acer Swift Air 14&#039;s keyboard and touchpad are pretty good to use, if not mind-blowing.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/EH4rbctCJ3pW4Mcp2Ce6DB-1200-80.jpg)

The port selection is about as minimal as one would expect on a laptop like this. Along with a USB-A port and headphone jack are two USB-C ports. Unfortunately, they’re both on the left side, a design choice that I always dislike since I find myself regularly having to plug the computer in on the right side, sometimes from a distance too.

![The Acer Swift Air 14 has two USB-C ports for charging, both on the same side of the laptop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/9ptEckpDsUi8L6KMZCajDB-1200-80.jpg)

* **Design score:** 4.5 / 5

One of the downsides of a laptop built for a sub-$1,000 price tag is that either the design or performance (or both) suffer. The design on the Acer Swift Air 14 is pretty good... so it goes that the performance is what is throttled.

Now, Acer built this laptop to hit a price point, but the company also built it to an expectation. This is supposed to be an extremely portable laptop with long battery life for not much money.

With that in mind, Acer used the affordable Intel Core 5 320 six-core CPU with a speed of 1.5GHz and 20 TOPs (21 for the Core 7 350) and put only 8GB of RAM in the computer. At least the 512GB SSD storage is sufficient.

![The Acer Swift Air 14 can do some light gaming, but it&#039;s not a powerhouse.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/fMMcDLhHCED9jK4EBhQsnA-1200-80.jpg)

If you’re looking for a laptop to work on while in a cafe responding to emails in between social media side quests, then what the Acer Swift Air 14 does is more than enough. Even using the AI features like Acer PurifiedView and PurifiedVoice during video calls, both of which work well, or messing with CoPilot+ doesn’t tax this laptop.

But it’s not too hard to push the Acer Swift Air 14. When I’ve tabs open that require a lot of RAM and try to switch windows, I’ve experienced some freezing. And anything but the least demanding games are a jittery mess. I wouldn’t use this laptop for 4K video editing, either. A powerhouse this is not. Then again, it’s not meant to be.

As far as the sound quality goes, the Acer Swift Air 14 suffers from what ails just about all thin and light laptops. There’s just no room for any speakers capable of producing a full sound and it shows. There’s not much bass and the sound is on the thinner side. That said, it’s not harsh, so listening to music in the background while working is still pleasant.

![One of the Acer Swift Air 14&#039;s AI apps is PurifiedVoice, which can minimize background noise or focus a speaker&#039;s voice during a video call.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Fo7RKHacAwWEUtsdsV5R3B-1200-80.jpg)

* **Performance score:** 4 / 5

### Acer Swift Air 14 review: Battery life

* **Almost 17 hours of battery life**
* **Charges relatively quickly**

The new benchmark for a good laptop battery life these days is anything over ten to twelve hours. So, the fact that the Acer Swift Air 14 can hit almost seventeen hours in our benchmark is remarkable. Note that this benchmark requires the laptop to run for that entire amount unplugged.

I’ve been using this laptop regularly for a little over a week and I don’t think I’ve seen it go below 50%. I have charged it a few times, mostly out of habit, but I have yet to be compelled to find an outlet. Plus, it does a good job of holding its charge when the lid is closed.

Acer advertises that the Swift Air 14 has fast charging, which might be true on certain settings, but I found it to charge at a still-good 1% a minute rate when set to a balanced performance mode.

## Buy it if…

**You need the ultimate in battery life**  
The Acer Swift Air 14’s battery life is impressive to say the least. It can last a couple days without a charge, and charges back up relatively quickly.

**You need portability**  
Not only is this laptop attractive in that MacBook kind-of-way, but it’s very light and thin, making it perfect to just throw in a backpack and take it on the road.

**You’re on a budget**  
One of the best things about the Acer Swift Air 14 is its price. It feels like a much better laptop than what I would expect for $700 / £750.

## Don't buy it if…

**You want a lot of power**  
The Acer Swift Air 14 does what it does well, but it can’t handle demanding tasks. If you want or need a lot of power, there are plenty of less portable alternatives that might be a better option.

**You get irritated by having all the USB-C ports on the same side**  
I know it’s probably not a big deal, but it’s one of those design choices that actually affects day-to-day use if you’re plugging into random outlets. There will be times where running the cable behind the laptop to the other side gets in the way or shortens the distance that you can be from an outlet.
