**Water campaigners have called for tougher legal accountability for water companies, following a series of events with Yorkshire MPs on the River Nidd and River Ouse.**

Wild Waters Yorkshire held events in Knaresborough and York on Friday 23 October, calling for a stronger Clean Water Bill to address sewage pollution, protect public health and the environment, and hold water companies to account.

The campaigners met Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon at Blenkhorn’s Boats in Knaresborough, York Outer MP Luke Charters at York St John University Boat Club, and York Central MP Rachael Maskell at Rowntree Park.

The campaign comes as Yorkshire Water sets out details of an £8.3 billion investment programme across the region, including several projects in Harrogate and Knaresborough.

### Campaigners call for a change in the water industry

Wild Waters Yorkshire said its campaign was focused on strengthening legislation ahead of a Clean Water Bill expected to be brought before Parliament in the coming months.

Its proposals include a roadmap towards public ownership of water companies, stronger legal accountability for environmental damage, and tougher independent regulation and enforcement.

The campaign also surveyed 433 Yorkshire Water customers in September to gauge public attitudes towards sewage pollution and the water industry.

According to the campaign’s findings, 92% of respondents said they were very concerned about sewage in rivers and seas, with a further 4.6% saying they were quite concerned.

The campaign reported that 86% were very concerned about the bonus paid to Yorkshire Water’s chief executive, while 76% were very concerned about dividends paid by the company and 70% about the effectiveness of the Environment Agency.

Of those surveyed, 81.3% said water companies should be run in the public sector.

The survey figures were supplied by Wild Waters Yorkshire. The campaign said the survey was conducted largely through mainstream social media groups; it was not presented as a representative poll of all Yorkshire Water customers.

> **Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, said:**
>
> Yorkshire Water certainly seem to want a pat on the back about the fact that they are now trying to do works, but that is the result of grass roots campaigning over a number of years and being pressured into it by government too.
>
> Obviously no river, no coastline should still see the record levels of dumping that we see.

### Yorkshire Water outlines local investment

In response to questions from The Harrogate Informer, Yorkshire Water said the water industry operated on a five-year regulated investment cycle. The current cycle began in April 2025 and runs until April 2030.

The company said a number of projects in Harrogate and Knaresborough had recently started or were still in investigation, design and planning stages.

In Knaresborough, seven storm overflow projects are planned for completion by 2030. They cover Waterside 49, Waterside 48, Waterside 47, Abbey Road, Cragg Top, Spittal Croft and Boroughbridge.

Yorkshire Water said borehole investigations were due to begin at Waterside 49 ahead of potential work. However, it had not yet provided specific delivery dates for the seven projects or an estimate of how much the Waterside 49 scheme would reduce discharges.

The company is also carrying out a phased phosphorus removal scheme at Knaresborough wastewater treatment works, with planning for the second phase under way. The work is expected to be completed by 2030.

Separately, Yorkshire Water said it had completed a £1 million mains replacement scheme around Chain Lane in Knaresborough during the previous 12 months.

### Projects planned for Harrogate

In Harrogate, Yorkshire Water has recently begun a £5 million project at Harrogate North Wastewater Treatment Works intended to reduce the amount of phosphorus entering the River Nidd. The work is being delivered in phases and is expected to be completed in late 2029.

The company has also undertaken borehole investigations in Harrogate, including on The Stray, to support planning for up to 11 storm overflow discharge reduction projects.

Those schemes remain at the modelling and design stage, and Yorkshire Water said it could not yet provide defined start dates. It said all the work would be completed by 2030.

A separate £15 million project at Pannal is intended to increase capacity in the wastewater network to meet future demand associated with expected housing development. Yorkshire Water stressed that this project was not specifically intended to reduce wastewater discharges.

The company also reported that mains replacement schemes worth £1.2 million in Duchy and £1.8 million around Crescent Road were under way and expected to finish in the coming months. A £600,000 scheme on private land at Hampswaite is being delivered in phases, with further work in the highway planned for next year.

### Company says discharges are falling

Responding to concerns about sewage discharges, Yorkshire Water said discharges had fallen by 24.5% in the previous year and by 12% in the year before that.

The company did not specify the geographical area or individual storm overflows covered by those figures in its response, so they do not establish the scale of any reduction specifically in the River Nidd or the Harrogate area.

Yorkshire Water also said it was undertaking work beyond infrastructure improvements, including citizen science initiatives, identifying private misconnections and changes to its operational approach to pollution incidents.

> **A Yorkshire Water spokesperson said:**
>
> We understand the strength of feeling around our performance and are currently delivering an £8.3 billion programme of investment across Yorkshire to drive significant improvements for customers and the environment. This includes a number of projects in Knaresborough and Harrogate.

The spokesperson said the company had called for increased local decision-making and closer links with stakeholders in its submission to the Cunliffe Review.

Yorkshire Water said it was improving how it reported its performance, including through a six-monthly report on progress and areas where further work was needed, and by making storm overflow discharge information available through an online map.

The company added that it would continue engaging with stakeholders on proposed stewardship models and regulatory reforms.

### Questions remain over local outcomes

While Yorkshire Water has outlined a range of planned projects, several of the storm overflow schemes are still at the investigation, modelling or design stage, with individual delivery dates yet to be confirmed.

The company’s response also did not provide specific figures for changes in sewage discharges or river water quality in the River Nidd and wider Harrogate area over the past 12 months, or quantify the expected reductions from the individual schemes.

For campaigners, the debate is about whether investment and regulatory changes will deliver measurable improvements to water quality and stronger accountability for pollution.

The Clean Water Bill is expected to provide a further focus for that debate as proposals progress through Parliament.
