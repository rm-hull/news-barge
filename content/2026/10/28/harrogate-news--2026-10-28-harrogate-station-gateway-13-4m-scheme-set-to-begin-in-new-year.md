A £13.4 million scheme to improve the area around Harrogate railway station is set to begin in the New Year, following the appointment of contractors by North Yorkshire Council.

NY Highways has been appointed to deliver the Transforming Cities Fund project, which aims to improve access to the railway station and create a more attractive public space around Station Parade and the town centre.

The work is expected to be completed in autumn 2028.

The scheme is intended to improve access for pedestrians, cyclists and bus passengers, while encouraging more sustainable travel and improving connections between the railway station, bus station and surrounding town centre.

## What will the scheme involve?

The planned work includes improvements to Station Parade, Station Square and the pedestrian route beneath the One Arch railway bridge.

Proposals include improved paving and pedestrian crossings, traffic signal improvements, a southbound segregated cycle lane on Station Parade and a new bus lane on Lower Station Parade.

Station Square will be redeveloped to improve the public space, while cycle parking facilities will be provided at Harrogate railway station.

Additional traffic signal improvements are planned at junctions on the A61, including King’s Road and Ripon Road, and King’s Road and Cheltenham Parade.

## Businesses to be consulted before and during work

North Yorkshire Council has said it will engage with local businesses before construction begins and throughout the project.

This will include drop-in sessions and direct visits to businesses to explain how the work will be carried out and discuss concerns about potential disruption.

> **Councillor Malcolm Taylor, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for highways and transport, said:**
>
> After almost six years in the pipeline, we welcome the start of this scheme which represents a significant investment in Harrogate town centre.

> **Councillor Malcolm Taylor added:**
>
> We understand that any scheme of this scale can raise questions, particularly around how it will affect trade, so those ongoing discussions are important.

The council says starting work in the New Year is intended to avoid disrupting businesses during the busy Christmas trading period. It has also said it will seek to minimise disruption during construction.

The announcement has included supportive comments from representatives of two businesses with interests in Harrogate town centre.

> **Gary Millar of Sheet Anchor Evolve, which operates Victoria Shopping Centre, said:**

## A project years in the making

The Station Gateway project has been under development since consultations on congestion and sustainable travel in Harrogate and Knaresborough began in 2019.

The original proposals subsequently went through several rounds of consultation and revision. The scheme has proved controversial, particularly over proposals affecting traffic flow on Station Parade and the balance between road space and provision for walking, cycling and public transport.

Campaigners and some local businesses have challenged aspects of the project, arguing that the changes and the disruption caused by construction would not justify the benefits. The council has maintained that the scheme will improve access to public transport and create a more welcoming arrival point for the town.

Following a legal challenge concerning traffic regulation orders, the proposals were revised. The current scheme retains two lanes on Station Parade while including a range of pedestrian, cycling and public transport improvements.

## How the scheme is being funded

The project is predominantly funded through the Government’s Transforming Cities Fund, which supports transport infrastructure across North Yorkshire and the Leeds City Region.

West Yorkshire Combined Authority is contributing £11 million, with a further £1.35 million from York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority and £1.05 million from North Yorkshire Council.

The wider Transforming Cities Fund programme allocated £38 million for transport schemes in Harrogate, Selby and Skipton.

North Yorkshire Council says the project is intended to support more sustainable travel, improve connections to employment and education, and contribute to the future economic prosperity of Harrogate town centre.
