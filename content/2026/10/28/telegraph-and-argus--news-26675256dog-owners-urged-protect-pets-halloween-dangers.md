DOG owners are being warned of hidden dangers at pumpkin patches this Halloween.

Vets say pumpkins pose a choking hazard to pets, with certain parts of the fruit potentially causing serious health issues if swallowed.

Sharon English, MRCVS and veterinary regulatory adviser at VetUK, said: "Pumpkins aren't toxic to dogs, but the stems and vines have small prickly hairs that can irritate sensitive tissues like eyes and mouths.

"Raw pumpkin can be quite tough to chew – especially the skin – and swallowing large chunks could potentially cause choking, stomach upset or blockage of the gut, though this is unusual."

She also warned that rotten pumpkins could lead to digestive upset.

Ms English said the risk isn't limited to eating pumpkin, and that owners should also be mindful of their dog’s comfort during seasonal outings.

She said: "Pumpkin patches can get busy at this time of year – noisy environments with lots of adults, children, pets and sometimes farm animals nearby can cause anxiety in dogs.

"Look out for signs like lip licking, whale eye (whites of the eye showing) and a low tail wag that may suggest your dog is feeling anxious."

Data from the Veterinary Poisons Information Service (VPIS) shows that in 2025, October enquiries spiked 30% above the average monthly volume from January to September, making Halloween the busiest period of the year after Christmas.

Ms English also reminded dog owners to keep chocolate, grapes, raisins, onions, garlic, and products containing the sweetener xylitol well out of reach.

She advised always reading food labels and consulting a vet if unsure about what is safe for dogs to eat.
