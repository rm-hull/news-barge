Let's take a closer look at the slightly less exciting part of Apple's new flagship MacBook Pro — its price.

The 14-inch versions starts at $2,899 / £2,899 / AU$4,599, while the base 16-inch versions comes in at $3,399 / £3,399 / $5,499. You can pre-order today, with deliveries starting on November 6.

## About that touchscreen

![A person touching the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR Display](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/4D2TYs7wGHKLbfJc7TjLm-1200-80.jpg)

Apple has revealed a little more about the OLED MacBook Pro's touchscreen. It supports all the usual Multi-Touch gestures (scroll, pinch, rotate, draw and more).

Why? Apple says you can use it to manipulate 3D objects or play virtual instruments. There are also classic use cases like pinching-to-zoom on photos, docs or web pages, or sketching and annotating on the screen.

Apparently the touchscreen is on this very high-end MacBook so that designers can interact with 3D prototypes, or developers can test how touch feels in their iOS apps. All very nice, but these don't sound like revolutionary use cases for touch on a laptop — perhaps our hands-on demos will make us think otherwise...

## Why no M6 Pro and M6 Max chips?

![Nvidia RTX Spark and Apple M6 chip icons](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/QnT5zbqvcVbzAYMsL43AgL-1200-80.jpg)

The MacBook Pro released yesterday has Apple's M6 chip, but today’s touchscreen MacBook Pro is expected to get the M5 Pro and M5 Max, not the M6 Pro and M6 Max. What gives?

According to Bloomberg, Apple is working on AI upgrades in the M7 generation that it deems so important that it has decided to skip the M6 Pro and M6 Max (and M6 Ultra) and jump right ahead to the M7 Pro and M7 Max in late 2027.

That suggests the company ditched the high-end M6 chips and moved development resources over to the M7 equivalents, but presumably also decided it was worth launching the entry-level M6 anyway.

Confusing, we know. But here’s one way to look at it: like pretty much everything these days, AI is the likely reason.

## Can Apple convince the skeptics?

![The M5 MacBook Pro on a black background next to an M3 MacBook Air on a red cushion](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/4MFy266bjvSx8nqHbnq6dY-1200-80.jpg)

We've asked for your opinion on the concept of a touchscreen MacBook Pro in our poll below. But what do TechRadar's resident experts think of the idea?

I asked Alex Blake, an Apple veteran and TechRadar contributor, for his thoughts on what we might be seeing today.

"I’ve long been a touchscreen laptop doubter and agree with Steve Jobs’ pronouncement that they make your arm want to fall off. So I’m going to take some convincing that Apple’s latest effort is worth the outlay, especially when they’re rumored to cost an eye-watering $2,899 and up," he said.

"I’m also wondering why Apple is making this move now. The MacBook Pro is already the best laptop in the world without a touchscreen, so Apple must feel like it’s got something really special — as it often does — with this latest product," he added.  
"Perhaps if Apple had decided to make a 2-in-1 instead of a fixed laptop, I might be more tempted. But that’s apparently not what we’re getting — and if we were, the price would be even sillier than it’s already expected to be," he said.

## The Dynamic Island could be a dark horse

![The screen of an Apple MacBook Pro M5 showing a face](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/yZHqUnoRc69wcYxaLpy3xh-1200-80.jpg)

I’m a fan of the Dynamic Island on my iPhone 16 Pro, and despite its awkward name, it feels like a genuinely useful addition to my phone. It's a rumored addition to the new OLED MacBook Pro, and I’m intrigued to see how it plays out on a Mac.

Over the years, many developers have built their own versions of the Dynamic Island for macOS, and most of them have added handy functionality in one way or another. Now it’s Apple’s turn to show us its own take on the concept.

But really, all the Dynamic Island needs to be is an upgrade over the useless notch, which won’t be difficult. That alone could be enough to convince Mac fans of its utility, even if it doesn’t blow anyone away.

## OLED MacBook Pro: a pricing headache?

![An Apple MacBook Pro M6 on a green background showing the face of a woman wearing sunglasses](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ufDFag9KRjxa6asPVWm9VG-1200-80.jpg)

The MacBook Pro M6 is already expensive, starting at $1,999 / £1,999 / AU$3,199. So how much can we expect to pay for a touchscreen OLED model with an M5 Pro or M5 Max chip?

The latest speculation from Bloomberg suggests the 14-inch version (with M5 Pro) could cost $2,899, which would be the highest-ever starting price for a new MacBook model. The 16-inch version, meanwhile, is tipped to cost at least $3,399.

Of course, those are just starting prices, with extra like RAM upgrades taking the prices further skywards — Apple currently charges an extra $600 / £600 to double RAM from 24GB to 48GB, for example. In other words, we should expect this to be the premium iPhone Duo of the MacBook world...

## OLED MacBook Pro: what can we expect?

![The Apple MacBook Pro M6 on a black background in front of an M6 graphic](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/cdP8gBLBbBz7ShMJdkvV8W-1200-80.jpg)

Perhaps thanks to the legacy of Steve Jobs’ famous hatred of them, Apple’s never done a touchscreen laptop before. With a new one set to launch today, what should we be looking out for?

Obviously, there’s touchscreen functionality, and Apple is rumored to have built an updated version of macOS to accommodate touch gestures. We’re also expecting an OLED panel — likely dubbed Super Retina XDR by Apple — with a built-in Dynamic Island and thinner bezels.

There will likely be both 14-inch and 16-inch models and options for M5 Pro and M5 Max chips. Whichever size you buy, the chassis could also come in slightly thinner than existing MacBook Pro devices.

All very impressive, but this is also likely to come at a serious cost...

## What’s Apple announced so far?

![The iMac M6 next to a MacBook Pro M6](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/9hqLWZZwYnbnvxQJX3GywB-1200-80.jpg)

The touchscreen MacBook Pro won't be the first device Apple has set loose on the world this week. Yesterday we saw the entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro get a new version with the M6 chip that Apple says “delivers up to 30 percent faster graphics and up to 25 percent faster AI workloads” versus the M5. There’s also a new N1 wireless chip that enables Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6.

The iMac also got a spec bump with the same M6 and N1 upgrades as the MacBook Pro, and it can also support two external 4K displays at 165Hz instead of 144Hz.

Finally, Apple added AI features to its Creator Studio subscription, including Generate Fill in Pixelmator Pro and the addition of OpenAI models alongside existing Apple Intelligence options. Notably, Final Cut Pro got MotionVFX templates, graphics, transitions and more.

An impressive list, but Apple's biggest news is still likely to come...

## A 'Ternus teaser'

Apple CEO John Ternus posted this Batman-style teaser two hours ago — and it doesn't reveal a whole lot.

The biggest social media theory is that it points to the return of the glowing Apple logo, which we haven't seen on a MacBook Pro for over a decade.

That glowing logo was more of a translucent cut-out rather than a separate LED, but it appears that Apple's found a way to bring it back without compromising its flagship MacBook's structural integrity.

It'd certainly give this probably-very-expensive laptop an extra bit of stardust over the rest of the lineup...
