A new partnership aims to pave the way for breakthroughs in targeted drug delivery.

York-based Aptamer Group has announced a collaboration with Pharmahungary Group to develop and test targeted delivery vehicles for heart and skeletal muscle tissue using its proprietary Optimer technology.

The partnership will focus on creating preclinical evidence to support future pharmaceutical collaborations in delivering therapeutics to specific tissues, with particular emphasis on cardiac and skeletal muscle cells.

Dr Arron Tolley, chief executive officer of Aptamer Group, said: "Pharmahungary Group is a leading authority in cardiac miRNA, and we are delighted that it has chosen Optimer technology to tackle the delivery of its therapeutic payloads and meet a currently unmet need in the space.

"This collaboration represents excellent progress against our post-fundraising goals and is a capital-efficient way to generate the preclinical data packages partners need to evaluate our platform, while retaining full ownership of our Optimer delivery vehicle, and to develop licensable assets, which are core to our new strategy."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21636330.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aptamer Group CEO Dr Arron Tolley *(Image: Pic supplied)*

The cost-sharing agreement means Aptamer will fund the development and validation of the targeting system and manufacture the Optimer-conjugates.

Pharmahungary will fund and conduct the preclinical studies, which are expected to include both in vitro and in vivo testing.

The approach allows Aptamer to access valuable animal study data without additional financial burden, with a similar preclinical package typically costing between £300,000 and £500,000.

Aptamer will retain full ownership of its Optimer delivery vehicles and related intellectual property, and will hold a 50 per cent stake in any jointly developed Optimer-miRNA therapeutic.

Pharmahungary Group, founded by Professor Péter Ferdinandy, has more than 25 years of cardiovascular research experience and has completed over 350 preclinical projects for pharmaceutical and biotech companies.

Professor Ferdinandy, founder and chief executive officer of Pharmahungary Group, said: "We have over two decades of experience developing cardioprotective miRNAs and the preclinical models needed to test them.

"Delivering miRNA therapeutics into heart and skeletal muscle cells remains a key challenge for the field.

"We chose Aptamer because of its promising data on targeted oligonucleotide delivery, and because Optimer delivery vehicles, as oligonucleotides themselves, are a natural match for miRNA payloads.

"Our goal is to generate the in vivo evidence needed to take this combined approach to pharmaceutical partners."

Approved oligonucleotide drugs act mainly in the liver, and no targeted delivery system for cardiac or skeletal muscle cells has yet been approved.

Aptamer’s Optimer platform aims to address this gap by enabling targeted delivery to tissues such as the heart, kidney, and fibrotic tissue.
