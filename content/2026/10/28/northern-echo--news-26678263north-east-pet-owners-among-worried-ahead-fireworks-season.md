Fireworks season is becoming a source of stress for thousands of pet owners across the North East.

New research suggests many are worried about the impact that weeks of displays can have on their animals.

New research has found that North East pet owners are more anxious than the national average ahead of fireworks season.

69 per cent of pet owners in the region are worried about the effects fireworks will have on their pets compared to 64 per cent nationally.

More than three quarters of North East respondents told pet insurance company Animal Friends that they find fireworks season (October to January) stressful as pet owners.

Jen Hipwell, head of veterinary services at Animal Friends Pet Insurance, said: "For many pets and owners, fireworks are no longer confined to Bonfire Night.

"Displays can start around Halloween and continue right through to New Year, creating weeks of uncertainty for animals that are sensitive to loud noises and disruption."

Another 35 per cent of North East pet owners reported that they have stayed home when fireworks events were underway to support their anxious pets.

The RSPCA writes that "pets and fireworks aren't compatible," and that stress from fireworks can cause aggression, injury or death in animals.

Fireworks can also cause other serious health issues for pets beyond anxiety. Dogs' sensitive hearing can put them at risk of tinnitus, hearing loss and long-term hearing damage from repeated loud noises.

Interestingly, while 67 per cent of owners in the North East feel prepared for fireworks season, only 12 per cent said they have a detailed plan in place for their pets.

This planning gap reflects a broader national trend, with 71 per cent of owners across the UK reporting that they would like more guidance to help prepare their animals.

Animal Friends said respondents report taking various steps to help their pets cope, including keeping windows shut, closing curtains or blinds and playing music or television to mask the noise.

More than a third said they try to create a safe space for their pets to retreat to.

The pet insurance company said because of this they have launched a free online tool for pet owners, to see how prepared they are ahead of fireworks season.

The Fireworks Readiness Checker provides a personalised readiness score and tailored advice for pet owners, Hipwell said, adding: "Preparation doesn't need to be complicated.

"Whether it's creating a safe space to retreat to, adjusting walk times or recognising signs of anxiety early, small steps can have a big impact."

Jenny Baxter, an animal behaviourist at national pet charity Blue Cross, said there are practical ways to reduce pets' stress during fireworks, such as walking dogs earlier or keeping cats inside homes during displays.

Checking that microchip information is regularly updated was also advised in case pets flee during fireworks.

Baxter added: "Closing windows and doors and drawing the curtains or blinds, as well as turning on the TV or radio, not too loudly, to muffle the noise of the fireworks will also help cats and dogs."

For small animals housed outdoors, she recommends covering hutches with thick blankets to dampen the sound and block out the sight of fireworks, while ensuring ventilation.

Beyond individual households, Animal Friends reports that there is growing support for regulatory change.

Eighty-two per cent of pet owners nationally said they believe more should be done to make fireworks season safer for pets, wildlife and livestock, with 86 per cent of North East respondents being worried about the impact fireworks can have on wildlife and livestock.

Two thirds of North East pet owners support restricting fireworks to organised public displays, and 58 per cent back tougher penalties for misuse.

With fireworks season now stretching across several months, experts say preparation can make a significant difference for pets' wellbeing. Owners are being encouraged to plan ahead, monitor their animals for signs of distress and seek expert advice if they have health concerns.

The Fireworks Readiness Checker is free to use and can be found online.
