More than 400 people were arrested in France on Saturday after protesters supporting high school student grievances clashed with police.

Police fired teargas in confrontations with protesters, as demonstrations across France were boosted by other groups angry over high prices and the fraying of a social welfare system.

Hundreds of thousands of people took to the streets across the country, and tensions erupted on the fringes of the nationwide demonstrations over absent teachers, decrepit school buildings and police violence.

Late on Saturday, the interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, said there had been 400 arrests and most were adults and not part of the high school protests.

He said the official demonstration had been hijacked by *casseurs – “* opportunistic delinquents who had seen a chance to pillage and loot … we will find them and deal with them firmly”.

He said the state was not planning to declare a state of emergency but added: “I very strongly condemn the violence we have seen.”

In Marseille, protesters stormed the town hall, ramming the door with security barriers they removed from the street. They briefly managed to enter the municipal building, where a protester spray-painted an “A” for anarchy on a wall before the police removed them.

Around Place de la République in central Paris, a peaceful march turned violent, with police firing teargas at black-clad and masked rioters who threw fireworks and paving stones, attacked a bank and looted a sports shop.
  
  
“We’re taking the Bastille!” one demonstrator yelled.

Others set wheelie bins on fire and threw firecrackers inside or pushed over glass recycling bins and picked up the bottles inside to use as missiles. A lorry was set on fire and flames spread to a nearby building.

Along Boulevard Magenta, one of the main south-north axis north of Place de la République running through a mostly working-class area, dozens of shops were broken into and looted.

Residents in surrounding streets were forced to close windows and doors as huge clouds of teargas rose to roof level.

French authorities detained dozens of protesters across the country, with more arrests expected.

Saturday’s mobilisation followed calls on social media to revive the *gilets jaunes* (yellow vests) movement, which emerged in 2018 against social injustice.

The protests began last month at schools ⁠in the poor northern suburbs of Paris, where ⁠anger over teacher ​absences and shabby facilities quickly morphed into a nationwide movement against inequality, weak public spending and falling standards.

Saturday’s protests called for teachers and students, labourers, pensioners and farmers to join the protests and widen the movement.

French authorities said 140 protests involving 32,800 people had been recorded outside Paris by midday, blaming the unrest and violence on what they said was the “ultra-left”.

![French riot police detain a protester on the ground during a Marseille demonstration](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/f1ad1c5c8e22a4f019b8bca019ccb66f7b88ef30/309_0_4804_3845/master/4804.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The CGT, one of France’s biggest labour unions, has also backed the protests, calling for “higher wages and pensions, lower prices and the protection of public services that meet people’s needs”.

About 45% of French people are worried about the rising cost of living and inflation, according to a 2,000-person survey conducted in October.

Protesters ⁠in hi-vis vests blocked streets, an echo of the unrest that dogged President Emmanuel Macron’s previous term, and in Marseille rubbish bins were set ​alight.

Both the far left and far right in France sought to capitalise on the protest movement, with the left positioning itself on the side of the protesters and the right backing the police.

Jean-Luc Mélenchon, the leader of the far-left France Unbowed party, marched in a demonstration on Tuesday and has expressed support for student protesters.

A large police presence was visible in central Paris, especially near landmarks such as ⁠the Avenue des Champs-Élysées and the Arc de Triomphe, which were excluded from the routes of the two official demonstrations.

About 100 people walked towards the police at the beginning protest yelling, “all cops are bastards!”.

“We are fighting because the cost of living is ‌too harsh, because the price of ‌fuel is too high, because high school pupils are being violently attacked and hurt all over the country just for trying to ‌protest and ask for more teachers and decent learning conditions,” said a 19-year-old protester in Paris who gave her name as Lola.

Macron is entering the final stretch of his presidency, leaving behind a polarised electorate and a badly fragmented political landscape.

![Riot police with shields and batons push against protesters during a Paris demonstration](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/cb63d6040548e81d41340118751ddf6c394645ff/0_0_4650_3719/master/4650.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

On Friday, the president appealed for calm and respect for people and property as officials warned of possible violence.

“There is a constitutional right to protest and to express oneself, and I have always been vigilant and attentive that that right is fully respected,” he said. “But this right comes with the need to respect. Respect for people, respect for property, and therefore a commitment not to insult or destroy.”

Macron denounced as “unacceptable” the potential presence of people “solely to cause damage and create unrest”.

![A protester waves a French national flag in front of a fire in the street](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/dd33ce2bcb779aef16a8b31ffa158d4b9a3fd38b/179_88_3875_3100/master/3875.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Nuñez said hundreds of protest actions and more than 80 marches were expected nationwide, including spontaneous gatherings, street demonstrations and blockades.

Police banned some other proposed gatherings in central Paris. Many Métro stations were closed on Saturday at the police’s request, including those around the Champs-Élysées and near the presidential palace, parliament and government offices.

![A protester in a white hoodie smashes a window during a Marseille demonstration](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/f40c554f1c1e10cd3252bfca744f3242f2c9967b/276_0_3257_2605/master/3257.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

In recent weeks, there have been thousands of arrests, hundreds of injuries, and schools set on fire as the new school year descended into nationwide student protests that are influencing the country’s presidential election campaign and drawing international attention.

The police watchdog is investigating at least two dozen allegations of police violence, while the French prime minister said that more than 900 police officers had been injured.

Among protesters, particularly gruesome wounds suffered by some teenagers have been blamed on police riot-control weapons. Nuñez suspended the use of a type of police stun grenade against the young demonstrators after a 15-year-old lost a hand, but said that they could be used on Saturday.

On Tuesday, the climate and social justice activist Greta Thunberg joined students at a school in the Paris suburb of Saint-Denis.
