**The biggest week of the year for** Gaelic**language, music, and culture comes to a close in Glasgow today.**

A mainstay of the Gàidhealtachd’s social calendar since 1892, the **Royal National Mòd** ran from October 9-17.

**Read More: Royal National Mòd in Glasgow: Top 10 must-see events**

The Glasgow Mòd 2026 will hit its closing note on Saturday morning with the spine-tingling Massed Choirs event, as hundreds of choristers gather around the Donald Dewar statue at the top of Buchanan Street.

The spot will fill with Gaelic song for a showstopping performance of unity, officially rounding out the historic event.

## Here are some of the best pictures and highlights from this year's Mòd:

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21583409.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tuesday saw the junior competition programme reach its climax with the fluent learner events at the Royal National Mòd in Glasgow. In the Solo Traditional Singing Girls and Boys 13-15 categories, 15-year-old Flora Scott from Sir E Scott School in Harris, collected both the Traditional Silver Pendant and the Ann Kelly MacDonald Memorial Trophy for the highest marks in traditional solo singing. *(Image: Elaine Livingstone)*

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21590715.jpg?type=mds-article-620)As the world’s biggest celebration of Scottish Gaelic reaches a magnificent penultimate day in Glasgow, Gaeldom’s newest singing stars took their celebrations to new heights as they hopped aboard the Glasgow City Sightseeing Bus in the heart of Glasgow with their medals. The spirit of Gaelic culture and song triumphed at the Royal National Mòd in Glasgow as vocalists celebrated their winning moments. In the Traditional Gold Medal final - the solo Gaelic singing contest, which invites participants to perform traditional songs that they have carefully selected for themselves – Iain Cormack from the Isle of Skye and Glasgow’s Anna MacGregor were victorious. *(Image: Elaine Livingstone)*

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21584280.jpg?type=mds-article-620)TV personality and opening concert host Phil MacHugh and young performers say ‘fàilte’, ‘welcome’, to the Royal National Mòd as the world’s premier celebration of Gaelic language, music and culture gets set to take over the city for nine spectacular days. Around 10,000 competitors, performers and visitors are expected to soak up the scenes of celebration, camaraderie and creativity in the City of the Gaels – Baile Mòr nan Gàidheal – as the historic, multi-arts festival delivers a special Scottish cultural experience for all. An ambitious programme of almost 300 concerts, competitions, art exhibitions, sessions, film screenings, processions, literature events and sporting spectacles will run from Friday 9 – Saturday 16 October. *(Image: Elaine Livingstone)*
