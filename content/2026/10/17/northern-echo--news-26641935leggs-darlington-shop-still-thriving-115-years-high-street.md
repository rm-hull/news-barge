For more than a century, generations of Darlington families have walked through the doors of Leggs.

The independent clothing retailer, based on Skinnergate, first opened in the town in 1911. It remains one of the oldest businesses continuing to trade today.

When the shop welcomed its first customers, King George V had only recently come to the throne, the Titanic had yet to sail, and Darlington was a thriving railway town at the heart of the North East's industrial economy.

Over the decades that followed, Leggs witnessed some of the most dramatic changes in British history.

Two world wars, the Great Depression, post-war rationing, the rise of television, the growth of shopping centres, the arrival of online retail and changing fashion trends all transformed the way people buy clothes.

Yet through every era, the business endured.

From Edwardian tailoring and post-war formalwear to modern fashion trends, independent retailers such as Leggs have had to evolve continually to remain relevant.

What was once a town filled with locally owned shops is now a retail landscape dominated by national chains and internet giants.

Skinnergate itself has changed dramatically since Leggs first opened.

Horse-drawn vehicles gave way to motor cars, department stores came and went, and generations of shopkeepers watched the character of Darlington town centre evolve around them.

Despite those changes, the store has remained a familiar fixture. Few retailers can claim such longevity.

Many businesses fail within their first few years, while even nationally recognised chains have disappeared from Britain's high streets in recent decades.

Leggs, however, has continued to attract customers through personal service and a longstanding presence in the community.

The business is among a select group of Darlington traders that can trace their roots back more than a century.

According to records, it first opened in 1911 and has remained a town-centre retailer ever since.

Businesses that survive for so long often become part of a town's collective memory.

Grandparents who once shopped there as young adults may later have brought their children through the same doors, creating family connections that stretch across generations.

In many ways, the story of Leggs mirrors that of the high street itself.

The retailer has lived through periods of prosperity and hardship, adapting to changing customer habits while retaining its place in the heart of Darlington.

More than 115 years after it first opened, it remains a reminder of a time when independent shops formed the backbone of town-centre life.

As Britain's high streets continue to face new challenges, that longevity makes Leggs not just a shop, but a small piece of Darlington history.
