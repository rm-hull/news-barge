A developer has argued that “successful Bradford HMOs rarely feature lounges.”

The claim was made in an appeal that aims to overturn a Bradford Council decision to block plans for a 10-bed HMO to become a 12-bed HMO.

## Planning history

In July, Bradford Council refused planning permission to add an extra two bedrooms to 11-13 Cutler Heights Lane – a property near the junction with Sticker Lane.

Plans to convert the property from a house to a 10-bed HMO were approved by the council in late 2025.

Earlier this year, a Miss Shah applied for permission to amend the layout of the rooms in the property to create a 12-bed HMO.

The changes would see an existing kitchen/diner become a bedroom, with an existing living room becoming a kitchen/diner.

Another existing bedroom would be divided to become two smaller bedrooms.

On the first floor, two of the existing bedrooms would be reduced to create a small kitchen.

## Why was it refused?

Refusing the application, planning officers said: “In adding two bedrooms to the HMO, the proposal would result in the loss of a generous living room.

“The reduction of communal space is a particular concern, with the loss of a large communal living room only partly compensated for by the addition of a small kitchen.

“No other separate living room is shown on the proposed plans.

“The loss of a living room would noticeably reduce the opportunities for the HMO residents to socialise together should they wish to do so, and thus the proposal is considered to have a significantly detrimental impact on residential amenity.”

But now the applicant has appealed this decision, meaning a Government appointed planning inspector will have final say on whether the changes can go ahead.

## What does the appeal say?

The appeal criticised Bradford Council’s decision to refuse the plans, saying officers put un-due weight on the need for future residents to have space to socialise.

The appeal says: “The appellant strongly disagrees with the City of Bradford Metropolitan District Council's decision to refuse planning permission.

It says: “An HMO of this size and layout does not require a separate lounge area” and adds: “successful Bradford HMOs rarely feature them.”

The appeal claims the council has “failed to recognize that the installation of four independent kitchen facilities dramatically upgrades, rather than degrades.”

A planning inspector is expected to decide on the appeal in the coming weeks.
