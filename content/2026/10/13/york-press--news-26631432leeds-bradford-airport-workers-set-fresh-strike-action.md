Leeds Bradford Airport has condemned 'aggressive tactics' behind a fresh wave of strike action.

About 130 workers are set to strike between October 16 and 18, and again from October 23 to November 2 in an escalating dispute over pay.

Unite trade union said the airport was "expected to experience a shutdown" as essential workers, including air traffic controllers, security personnel, customer experience staff and cleaners, choose to strike.

Unite cited claims that Leeds Bradford Airport workers earn less than those doing the same roles at Newcastle Airport - based in a city with a lower cost of living - as the reason for the strike.

A spokesman for Leeds Bradford Airport said it respected the right of employees to participate in lawful industrial action but was disappointed by the escalation and did not believe it was the right way to resolve the issues that are still under discussion.

"The airport has made a fair and responsible offer that is significantly above inflation and reflects both the current challenging economic conditions and the need to ensure the long-term sustainability of the business.  
  
"Importantly, we currently have updated offers on the table, one of which is out to ballot having been recommended by Unite. We remain committed to working with Unite and our colleagues to reach a fair resolution through meaningful discussion with the remaining area.

"These aggressive tactics serve no purpose and only create uncertainty for the hardworking customers of Leeds Bradford Airport who are looking forward to enjoying time away with family and friends during the upcoming half-term break.

"We are disappointed that this approach has been taken at a time when positive discussions are continuing."

Despite the industrial action, the airport expects to maintain "near-normal operations".

The spokesman said: "Our priority now is to minimise disruption for customers, many of whom have worked hard and saved for important holidays, family visits, and travel opportunities.

"At this stage, we anticipate running near-normal operations during the periods of industrial action."

Leeds Bradford Airport is currently undergoing major upgrades as part of its LBA:REGEN project.

Recent improvements include a new central search zone and staff welfare facilities.

The airport emphasised its role as a key transport hub for the region and warned that strike action has the potential to cause inconvenience for thousands of customers and local businesses.
