**Why do I need to know* Herbstblues*?**

*Der Herbstblues* (pronounced like this) means the autumn blues: a mild, seasonal slump marked by lower motivation, a touch of melancholy and a craving for cosy comforts.

You’ll hear it in small talk, lifestyle pieces and even shop ads once the summer is over. It’s a handy everyday term – less clinical than *Herbstdepression* (seasonal depression), which would imply a diagnosable condition.

**READ ALSO: How to prepare for autumn like a GermanWhat does it mean?**

*Der Herbstblues* is a compound of*Herbst*(autumn) and* Blues*(borrowed from English), so literally “autumn blues.” It’s used to describe feeling a bit flat when the light fades, the* Nebel* (fog) creeps in and routines ramp back up after summer.

In Germany, the start of autumn can often bring with it the *Altweibersommer* – or Indian summer – complete with sunny days and dazzling blue skies. However, throughout September the days rapidly get shorter – culminating with the clocks going back in late October.

With the evenings closing in fast, and chilly days replacing warm summer evenings, many people find themselves in experiencing a *Stimmungstief,* or sudden low mood.

So, if your mood dips with the light and you find yourself reaching for a blanket and a book, *'der Herbstblues'* is the word that captures this feeling.

**Use it like this:**

*Ich hab gerade voll den Herbstblues. Mir fehlt die Sonne.*  
I’ve really got the autumn blues right now. I miss the sun.

*Ein Spaziergang im Wald hilft mir gegen den Herbstblues.*  
A walk in the woods helps me beat the autumn blues.

*Mit Tee, Suppe und guter Musik krieg ich den Herbstblues in den Griff.*  
With tea, soup and good music I keep the autumn blues under control.
