**Belgian police arrested ten people, including nine minors**, after clashes involving hundreds of young people near** Brussels-Midi railway station** on Monday, 12 October. Officers reported being targeted with stones and fireworks as unrest broke out on the sidelines of a national strike and a student gathering.

Police deployed **tear gas and water cannon** as groups moved between the station, Place Bara and the Cureghem neighbourhood, occasionally dispersing into Anderlecht and Saint-Gilles before returning. The Clemenceau metro station was temporarily closed, while a car, several scooters and street furniture were set alight. Officers also seized fireworks, flammable liquids and aerosol cans.

The violence came amid growing protests against **austerity measures, rising living costs and education cuts**. Since 1 October, secondary-school pupils have been demonstrating against plans to save**€500 million** from French-speaking education by 2029. Trade unions are also opposing Prime Minister**Bart De Wever's proposed €10 billion savings programme**.

In Liège, more than **200 arrests** have been reported since the student protests began. A separate anti-austerity march in Brussels drew around**30,000 people on Friday**, according to police, and was also followed by clashes.
