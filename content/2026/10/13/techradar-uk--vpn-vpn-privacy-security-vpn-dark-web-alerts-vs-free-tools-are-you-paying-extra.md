VPN providers are increasingly selling “dark web alerts” as premium security features, marketing them as essential protection against identity theft. This raises a key question: do paid alerts find unique threats, or just repackage data that's freely available?

With subscriptions to the best VPNs already costing up to $10-15 monthly, adding identity protection features can push bills toward $20 a month. If you’re essentially paying for data available at no cost, that’s money better spent elsewhere.

## How dark web monitoring works

Let’s clear up a common misconception first: VPN services aren’t sending security analysts to actively patrol the dark web hunting for your name. That would be impossible – the dark web is too vast and constantly shifting.

Instead, what most dark web monitoring services actually do is simpler: they cross-reference your information against massive databases of leaked credentials that have already been publicly disclosed. These databases contain billions of compromised accounts from data breaches spanning over a decade.

The monitoring happens automatically. You register your identifiers – such as your email address, phone number, or social security number – the service scans these databases periodically, and sends you an alert if a match appears.

## The free option: Have I Been Pwned

![Screenshot from Have I Been Pwned showing the data breach notification page](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/khvWxLqbBwQP6gu9vB6kvg-1200-80.jpg)

For free breach checks, Have I Been Pwned (HIBP) has become the industry benchmark. Created by security researcher Troy Hunt in 2013, this free service hosts nearly 18 billion compromised email addresses from hundreds of publicized breaches. Checking is free and requires no account.

Where it gets interesting for continuous tracking is Mozilla Monitor, which runs entirely on HIBP’s data.

Since launching in 2018, Mozilla’s free service has helped people in 237 countries identify exposed information. You can sign up for email notifications that ping you whenever your address shows up in a breach – essentially the core functionality paid services advertise.

Several VPN providers have acknowledged using this same foundation. ExpressVPN Keys explicitly integrates with HIBP for both email breach and password detection, while CyberGhost Identity Guard is described in their documentation as being “powered by Have I Been Pwned.”

Yet not all vendors are relying on public data alone. Domininkas Virbickas, Product Director at NordVPN, said they track “more than 40,000 sources around the clock” and that “most of this data never reaches the public breach databases people can check for free.”

## When to pay for premium alerts

That doesn’t mean paid services lack value. Having breach alerts built into an app you already use for VPN protection eliminates another password and dashboard to manage.

If you’re already paying for a VPN subscription, adding monitoring for free or minimal extra cost is efficient. You don’t need to remember to check another website or log into a separate service.

While HIBP updates regularly, paid services may offer faster notifications. Minutes matter when someone steals your credentials – they can try accessing accounts immediately.

That head start can mean the difference between resetting your password on your own terms versus dealing with account recovery after unauthorized access.

Finally, there’s the distinction between basic email alerts and full identity theft protection.

Free services like HIBP notify you about email addresses appearing in breaches. Paid identity protection packages often go much further, including SSN monitoring, credit file locks, bank account number tracking, and even identity theft insurance.

Coveron, which partners with NordVPN for identity protection on premium plans, adds credit monitoring, flags suspicious credit inquiries, and includes identity theft insurance.

Similarly, services like Norton’s LifeLock monitor multiple data types – phone numbers, SSNs, financial accounts – and provide guided recovery steps if something goes wrong.

## The verdict

Don’t pay extra for a VPN subscription to get basic email breach alerts. If you want to monitor whether your credentials have been exposed, free tools like Have I Been Pwned or Mozilla Monitor do the same job at no cost.

However, if you’re already paying for a VPN and the dark web monitoring is included at no additional charge, there’s no reason not to enable it. If you genuinely need identity theft protection, then premium services justify their cost with features free tools simply don’t offer.

So the question isn’t whether free tools work for basic breach checking – they do. It’s whether you need the expanded coverage, faster alerts, and recovery support that paid services provide.

For most people, starting with free monitoring makes sense. You can always upgrade later if your threat profile warrants it.
