The remake of Resident Evil 2 is a survival horror masterpiece, and the Resident Evil 2 Deluxe Edition runs consistently well on Nintendo Switch 2. It would’ve been neat to see some motion controls included for the Switch 2 release, but this edition is still a fantastic way to play what is arguably the best game in this legendary series.

The remake of *Resident Evil 2* is considered by many to be among the greatest games in Capcom’s legendary survival horror series, and it’s really not hard to see why. Stellar gunplay, immersive environments, a killer cast, and big scares…this entry has it all. And now you can experience it on the go like never before, with the game finally launching on Nintendo Switch 2 in its*Resident Evil 2 Deluxe Edition* form.

**Platform reviewed:** Nintendo Switch 2  
**Available on:** PS4, PS5, Nintendo Switch (cloud only), Nintendo Switch 2, Xbox One, Xbox Series X and Series S, PC, iOS  
**Release date:** October 16, 2026 (Switch 2 edition)

I’ve played the entirety of Leon and Claire’s stories, dabbled in some extra modes, and tried the title out in both docked and handheld mode.

So, if you’re wondering how the game runs and plays on Nintendo’s latest hybrid system, you’re in the right place. Here’s how I’d rate the *Resident Evil 2* remake on Nintendo Switch 2 after more than 20 hours of gameplay.

## Experience a legendary remake on the go

![Claire approaches the orphanage in Resident Evil 2 remake on Nintendo Switch 2](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/VryVtVcpf98hBPxJrL5PfQ-1200-80.jpg)

While I’m mainly here to talk about Nintendo Switch 2 performance, I’ll get this out of the way first: the *Resident Evil 2* remake is survival horror at its finest. This game conjures up a truly daunting atmosphere, with haunting and iconic areas like the Raccoon City Police Station and sewers translating beautifully into a modern, HD format. The gunplay is incredibly smooth and satisfying; you truly have to manage your resources carefully if you want to survive, and everything from random skirmishes through to major boss fights feels phenomenally tense. Pair that with a set of likeable characters, an ambient soundtrack, and rewarding exploration, and you’re looking at a true classic of the genre.

But how well does this top-tier Resident Evil title translate onto Nintendo Switch 2? Very well, thankfully.

First of all, let’s talk performance. While a platform like PS5 Pro may still offer a technically superior experience, I never felt like I was sacrificing much with this Switch 2 version. This version targets 60 frames per second (fps), and my frame rates were delightfully consistent, and gameplay felt smooth, even during higher-octane action segments — when docked or in handheld. Sometimes, the movement of distant enemies seemed a bit choppy, but this never affected my ability to blast them down, and I never realized any similar issues during crucial close combat situations.

It’s a similar story when it comes to visuals too. The game looks solid on Nintendo Switch 2 with HDR support. Dark and gloomy city streets and ambiently lit indoor areas appear detailed and faithful, while character models such as the Tyrant look polished and sharp. As you’d expect, some textures don’t look quite as refined in handheld mode, with elements like Claire’s hair slightly lacking in definition, but the game still looks clear with decent contrast and lighting.

![Tyrant model on display in Resident Evil 2 remake on Nintendo Switch 2](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/grKNDkjbwcooQg9dGoDVCR-1200-80.jpg)

**The moment in which the Tyrant, sometimes known as Mr. X, first appeared in Leon’s path made me jump out of my skin. They made this guy look incredibly daunting (and kinda dapper) for the remake. And I’m even getting chills thinking about the ominous sound of his footsteps as I type this review.**

*Resident Evil 2* also controls nicely on Nintendo Switch 2, with multiple schemes available through the Options menu. Whether I was using my Turtle Beach Rematch Wireless Controller or playing handheld, it was easy to switch weapons during combat, sprint past zombies, or sort through my inventory. This title also supports HD Rumble, which at times can feel a little overassertive, but overall adds to the immersion nicely. You also get virtual spatial sound, which works brilliantly, especially with headphones.

Something that this title does lack, though, is control schemes that make use of the Switch 2’s unique hardware. For instance, there’s no motion-controlled aiming, which would’ve been a nice touch for those who enjoyed it on *Resident Evil Requiem*, for instance. There isn’t any mouse mode functionality either, so you’ll be using traditional controls throughout the game. For me, this isn’t a huge problem — I prefer standard right stick aiming, but it would’ve undoubtedly been good to see this game make the most of the Switch 2’s hardware.

When all’s said and done, this is a very competent port of a fantastic survival horror game, and it only helps the Switch 2 establish itself as a major destination for third-party titles as well as big-hit Nintendo releases. Whether you’re new to this series or you’re a long-time fan, this is a great way to experience *Resident Evil 2* at home or on the go.

## Should you play Resident Evil 2 for Nintendo Switch 2?

![Zombie looking onwards in Resident Evil 2 remake on Nintendo Switch 2](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/P9dcnsevHRGfLii5wHX4DR-1200-80.jpg)

## Play it if…

**You want to take Resident Evil 2 on the go**  
You get smooth, consistent performance in this edition of *Resident Evil 2*, even if you’re playing in handheld mode. If you want to enjoy this classic on the go, then Switch 2 should be your go-to destination — there’s no need to play the cloud version on the OG Switch anymore!

**You’re new to Resident Evil**  
Many consider *Resident Evil 2* to be a strong starting point for series newcomers, and I’d be inclined to agree. You get to see the origin story of legendary gaming icon Leon Kennedy and the intuitive, modern controls are ideal for the uninitiated.

## Don’t play it if…

**You want peak performance**  
If you want to get the best performance possible, then you still may prefer to play on the PS5 Pro or PC. That being said, the Switch 2 version still looks good, especially docked, and its reliable framerate ensures smooth gameplay at all times.

**You’re expecting any major changes**  
If you’re looking for shiny new content or new ways to play *Resident Evil 2*, then this version is unlikely to spark your interest. There are no gyro aiming or mouse mode options, and although you’re getting the Deluxe edition here, there’s no brand new content to discover if you own the DLC on other platforms.

## Accessibility features

There are a number of ways to customize your experience in *Resident Evil 2*, with customizable camera options, three distinct control schemes, toggle or hold run modes, and a wide array of voice and text languages to pick from. In the display section, you can change the color of the reticle, laser dot sight, and dot display, alter HUD opacity, and even turn damage display levels down if you’d prefer.
