For many North East Harry Potter fans, a trip to Hogwarts is much closer to home than they might think.

From the ancient cloisters of County Durham to the Northumberland castle where Harry first took flight, the North East is packed with magical locations that played a starring role in the beloved Harry Potter films.

Durham Cathedral, Alnwick Castle and Goathland Station hosted some of the films’ most memorable scenes, letting fans step into Harry’s world without leaving the region.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21509113.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Tom Banks)*

Whether it's walking the same corridors as Hogwarts students, recreating a broomstick lesson or waiting for a steam train at the station that became Hogsmeade, these locations continue to draw visitors 25 years after the first film's release.

Durham Cathedral's Norman archways, built between 1093 and 1133, provided the atmospheric setting for many Hogwarts scenes.

The cloisters became the corridors where Harry, Ron and Hermione walked between lessons, and the Chapter House was transformed into Professor McGonagall’s Transfiguration classroom.

The Cloister Garth hosted the scene where Harry releases Hedwig into the snow, while the rarely opened Triforium became the setting for the trio’s discovery of Hagrid’s three-headed dog, Fluffy.

While Durham Cathedral does not offer Harry Potter-specific tours, its museum tour does include access to the Chapter House, usually closed to the public.

One tourist lined up scenes from the film series with their real-life settings, writing: "Walking through these historic corridors feels like getting a little glimpse into Hogwarts-no acceptance letter required."

Alnwick Castle in Northumberland is perhaps the most iconic of the North East locations, serving as the backdrop for Harry’s first flying lesson and his introduction to Quidditch.

The Outer Bailey was the site of Madame Hooch’s broomstick class, where Harry’s classmate Neville loses control of his broom and Harry takes to the skies for the first time.

A Hogwarts Express carriage set was also built inside the castle’s Guest Hall during filming.

Alnwick Castle has embraced its place in the Wizarding World by offering broomstick training sessions in the very spot where Harry and his friends took their first lesson.

Nearby, the Hallow and Crux offers unique Hogwarts-themed rooms for tourists to book, while Lundgren Tours runs family-friendly Harry Potter experiences at the castle. Further away, the Hog’s Head Inn takes its name from the pub in the series and offers fans a place to extend their stay.

Alnwick Castle also featured in Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets, including the Inner Bailey scene where the Weasley family’s flying car crash-lands.

Tourists describe Alnwick as a "must-visit" for fans of the series, praising both the cultural value of the filming location and the history the castle itself holds.

A little further afield, Goathland Station in the North York Moors became Hogsmeade Station in the first film.

![Goathland](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20906412.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Goathland Station, popular with Harry Potter fans for being Hogsmeade station. *(Image: CONTRIBUTOR)*

It’s here that Harry sees Hagrid once more before crossing the lake to Hogwarts.

Fans often visit to recreate the moment or to watch the North Yorkshire Moors Railway steam train arrive, just like the Hogwarts Express.

For fans of the franchise, these North East locations offer the chance to step into the world of Harry Potter without ever needing a ticket to London.
