A local council has published CCTV footage on social media of alleged fly-tippers in a bid to identify people illegally dumping waste.

Hillingdon Council, in west London, has shared images of individuals seemingly abandoning mattresses, furniture, construction rubble, and commercial waste on public land.

The initiative asks residents to identify those pictured through its portal, with leaders promising swift enforcement action in a renewed crackdown on fly-tipping.

Offenders can face fixed penalty notices of up to £1,000, while more serious cases referred to magistrates’ courts can attract unlimited fines or prison sentences of up to five years.

![Hillingdon Council has taken to posting CCTV images of suspected fly-tippers in a bid to crack down on illegal dumping of rubbish](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/9/28/Screenshot-2026-10-13-at-10-06-57.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a recent appeal published on Facebook, the council posted photos of suspected offenders alongside a warning: “While most residents dispose of their waste responsibly, a small minority continue to think they can dump rubbish and drive away without consequences. They're wrong.”

The local authority highlighted a recent incident where it said fly-tippers targeted St Anselm's Church car park, noting people “weren't as anonymous as they thought” and appealing for public information to help enforcement teams identify those responsible.

![The council issued a warning to alleged offenders, writing that they ‘weren't as anonymous as they thought’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/9/56/Screenshot-2026-10-13-at-10-07-16.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Officials have confirmed the campaign forms part of a wider enforcement push across the borough, where enforcement officers have already issued more than 300 fixed penalty notices totalling around £134,000 in recent months.

As part of the crackdown, the council has also started utilising statutory powers to seize and crush commercial vans linked to organised illegal dumping operations.

Newly released CCTV stills show alleged offenders pulling up along residential streets and rural verges at night before unloading bulky household items and driving away.

Council leaders urged residents to submit tips anonymously through the borough's portal, while issuing a fresh warning to householders about their legal duty of care.

![Council leaders have urged residents to submit tips anonymously](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/9/17/Screenshot-2026-10-13-at-10-07-25.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Under environmental regulations, residents remain legally responsible for ensuring any private rubbish removal firm they hire holds a valid environment agency licence. Householders whose waste is discovered dumped illegally face prosecution and fines, even if they paid a third party to remove it.

The push comes as municipal authorities across England struggle to manage a sharp rise in environmental crime, with local councils logging more than 1.25 million fly-tipping incidents in 2024/2025 – a nine per cent increase from the previous year.

The government spent £19.3 million on clearing large-scale fly-tipping incidents in England last year alone, up nearly 50 per cent from the previous 12 months. When factoring in clearance costs on private land and wider enforcement expenses, the overall toll on the UK economy is estimated to exceed £390 million annually.
