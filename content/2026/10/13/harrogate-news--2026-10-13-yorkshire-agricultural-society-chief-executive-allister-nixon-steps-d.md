Allister Nixon has stepped down as chief executive of the Yorkshire Agricultural Society following a period of absence due to health issues.

Mr Nixon is leaving after four years in the role, during which he oversaw the introduction of the Society’s values, a rebrand of the organisation and its family of businesses, and the refurbishment of Fodder at the Great Yorkshire Showground in Harrogate.

The Society’s Board of Trustees, led by chairman Paul Russell, will oversee the leadership transition.

Society Manager David Tite will oversee the charity’s operations, while commercial venues will be managed by Commercial Director Graham Thompson, supported by the senior management team.

> **Mr Nixon said:**
>
> My recent health issues have given me cause and opportunity for much reflection, and I have decided that this is the right time for me to move on to my next chapter.It has been a real privilege to lead this incredible organisation and work alongside such a dedicated team. We do amazing things here, and I am incredibly proud of what we have accomplished together during my tenure. I wish the organisation and everyone in it continued success.

Mr Russell said the Society was grateful for Mr Nixon’s commitment over the past four years and wished him well with his recovery and next chapter.

He added that the Board was confident Mr Tite and Mr Thompson, supported by the trustees and senior management team, would lead the organisation as it prepared for the busy Christmas season.

The Yorkshire Agricultural Society is a registered charity supporting farming, food and the countryside through events, education, training, grants, bursaries, scholarships, scientific research and practical support.

Its businesses include Fodder, the Great Yorkshire Events Centre and Harrogate Caravan Park, alongside the annual Great Yorkshire Show.
