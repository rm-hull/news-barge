Residents of a North East city are being asked for their views on renewing a scheme tackling anti-social behaviour and environmental problems.

The Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO), introduced in 2019, gives Sunderland City Council powers to tackle behaviour that harms people’s quality of life in public spaces.

It covers public drinking, dog control, nuisance bikes and scooters, car cruising, environmental offences and other anti-social behaviour.

Councillor Bill Blackett, Sunderland City Council’s cabinet member for environment, transport and enforcement, said: "We know the impact anti-social behaviour can have on our communities.

"It is one of the issues most frequently raised by residents and remains a key priority for the Council and our partners.

"The Public Spaces Protection Order provides important powers to help address behaviours that can make people feel unsafe, damage local environments, and affect people's enjoyment of public spaces.

"It is important that these powers remain effective and reflect the issues that matter most to our residents."

The current order came into force in 2024 and is now being reviewed for renewal.

The council wants to know whether the restrictions are still relevant and effective or need changing.

Anyone who breaches the PSPO restrictions can receive a £100 fixed penalty notice.

Councillor Blackett said: "This consultation is an opportunity for people to tell us whether the current measures are working and whether there are any changes they would like us to consider.
