### Braun OptiBrew Pro: two-minute review

The Braun OptiBrew Pro does even more than you might expect from a drip coffee maker. It makes five different coffee styles: Regular, Bold, Over Ice, Cold Brew, and Coffee Shot. There are seven different brew sizes: 8 ounces, 10 ounces, 12 ounces, 16 ounces, 20 ounces, a six-cup half-carafe, and a 12-cup full carafe. The Coffee Shot setting is not technically espresso but has a similar strength. This setting yields about three ounces of highly concentrated brew, perfect for making lattes and cappuccinos. A built-in milk frother tucks away when not in use.

The machine comes with a reusable filter, though you can use a cone-shaped #4 paper filter if you prefer. It also comes with a double-sided coffee scoop, so you can measure out the precise amount of coffee you need for each brew size and style.

The large, easy-to-read touch screen lets you set up your brew. You can adjust for high altitude and/or hard water if needed. Set up auto-brew for a specific time and you'll have freshly brewed coffee waiting for you when you wake up in the morning. The keep warm feature can keep your coffee warm for up to four hours at the temperature of your choice. You can also reheat cold coffee that way.

Cleanup is easy, and most of the parts are dishwasher-safe. The Braun OptiBrew Pro is easy to use, and it produces excellent coffee in a variety of styles.

I tested the brew pause feature by switching the lever from "coffee" to "stop" during brewing, poured myself a cup, and then switched the lever back to "coffee." Very little coffee dripped out while I was pouring a cup (maybe a drop or two) and when I switched the lever back, brewing continued as expected.

## Coffee shot and milk frother

To make a true espresso, you need an espresso machine, which uses pressure. This is a drip machine, so you can't make a real espresso. However, the Braun OptiBrew Pro does make an ultra-concentrated brew they call a Coffee Shot.

Frankly, I found it too strong to drink on its own, but it makes a great base for latte or cappuccino-style beverage. I used the milk frother and made myself a sort of cappuccino. Now, the frother only froths, it doesn't heat the milk, so the user guide suggests microwaving your milk first if you want hot foam. I did that and it worked out fine. I used 2% dairy milk and the foam was copious and quite sturdy. It even stood up to vigorous stirring. My drink was delicious! And the frother was easy to detach and wash afterwards.

## Buy it if

**You just want good coffee**

At its heart, this is a drip coffee machine that makes excellent drip coffee.

**You want a lot of different brew styles**

It doesn't just make a carafe of drip coffee, it makes a variety of single serve sizes, has two different methods for iced coffee, and even brews a highly concentrated coffee shot for espresso-style drinks. It also froths milk for a cappuccino type of beverage.

**You want some more fancy features**

Set your coffee up in advance with the auto-on feature. Keep it warm or reheat it with the adjustable-temperature keep warm feature. You can adjust for high altitude or hard water. Even change up beep tone if you like.

## Don't buy it if

**It's too pricey**

You can find a simple drip coffee maker without the bells and whistles for about a tenth of the price.

**You want true espresso**

Though the OptiBrew Pro makes a highly concentrated coffee shot that you can use like espresso for your frothed milk beverages, it isn't technically espresso. You need an espresso machine for that.

**You are looking for an automatic bean-to-cup machine**

There is no built-in grinder. You have to measure and scoop your ground coffee into the filter yourself. This is not an automatic or even semi-automatic machine, it's a manual machine.
