The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool began refilling Monday after months of construction delays, peeling paint and dropped vandalism charges.

A partially filled, navy blue basin was visible behind security fencing surrounding the pool. A sign posted at the perimeter by the National Park Service informed visitors: “The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool is being refilled as the National Park Service and contractors put the final touches on a historic effort to clean, repair, and beautify this iconic national landmark. As part of the normal refilling process, the water may temporarily appear cloudy before clearing.”

Contractor Atlantic Industrial Coatings, which received a no-bid contract for the project, updated its website on October 5 to announce: “All warranty repairs were completed Friday, 10/2/2026! It has been an honor to serve our great nation!”

However, journalists from *The Washington Post* observed workers at the site as recently as October 7 — a day after Trump stated the project was complete and four days after the contractor announced warranty repairs had wrapped up. Crews were seen spraying material onto two interior sections of the pool, while grates for the central drain remained uninstalled.

The refilling follows a second round of repair work necessitated by the flaking of the pool's new “American flag blue” coating, an issue the Justice Department attributed to a “botched” initial application.

![Months after peeling paint, construction delays and federal court dismissals stalled progress, the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool began refilling on Monday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/01/2026-10-12T193023Z_258533405_RC2T1OAAITD7_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-REFLECTING-POOL.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Internal reports submitted by Atlantic Industrial Coatings to the Trump administration admitted its own implementation had fallen short, noting that workers applied too little primer due to “human oversight” and used two incompatible chemicals during application.

President Donald Trump had announced the restoration initiative in the spring ahead of America 250 events planned for the National Mall. Initial estimates set the renovation cost at $2 million, but spending has since surpassed $16 million.

Shortly after work began, the pool experienced a large algae bloom followed by the structural coating failure.

![The refilling follows a second round of repair work necessitated by the flaking of the pool's new ‘American flag blue’ coating, an issue the Justice Department attributed to a ‘botched’ initial application](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/07/14/10/GettyImages-2279669701.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Though the site has faced recurring algae issues for decades, Trump claimed on Truth Social in July that the green blooms were caused by vandals who “dumped fertilizer in the water” and suggested the pool’s coating was “cut and powerfully lifted off the surface” with a box cutter.

Police records confirmed blade cuts along the pool’s foam layer. However, critics say the security fence — which could remain until early next year under federal contracts — is mainly being used to hide construction errors.

Federal agents were recently observed at the drained basin using laser-scanning and measuring equipment on the grimy floor. A spokesperson for the FBI Washington Field Office Evidence Response Team told *The Independent* that agents were “assisting the U.S. Park Police in its investigation of damage to the Reflecting Pool.”

![Atlantic Industrial Coatings announced that all warranty repairs were completed on October 2 following the pool’s coating failure. However, crews were still observed working at the site days later](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/01/2026-10-12T193029Z_1952195125_RC2T1OAIMQ8L_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-REFLECTING-POOL.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Meanwhile, ethics experts have questioned the ongoing security measures, with Craig Holman of Public Citizen telling *The Independent* that the chain-link enclosure represented an effort “to prevent curious citizens from gathering and displaying the fraying paint flakes.”

Despite contractor issues, Trump maintained that illegal activity contributed to the site’s complications, as reported by *The Washington Post*.

“The Reflecting Pool is totally complete, and we changed the old pipes,” Trump told reporters on October 6. “We wanted to get open for July Fourth, and now we went and got rid of all of the old pipes, which we were going to do later during the winter. We did it, and I think it’s almost complete. I’m going to visit.”

Addressing the site's history over the summer, Trump added: “We had vandalism there, and we have it about as vandal-proof as you can get. But only sick people do that, and we have their pictures.”

![President Donald Trump insisted that illegal activity caused the pool’s structural failures, citing alleged fertilizer dumping and razor blade cuts along the lining](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/13/01/2299058498...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Federal officials initially attributed the damage to intentional destruction. Several people were arrested, including former Olympian David Hearn after U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia Jeanine Pirro alleged at a press conference that he had “forcefully and violently” pulled at the lining.

Hearn denied damaging the site, stating he only touched a loose flap of coating. Federal prosecutors dropped the case in July, and a judge ruled on October 1 that charges against Hearn could not be refiled.

Visitors at the National Mall on Monday offered mixed reactions to the project's execution and oversight.

Jerad Johnson, a 46-year-old pastor visiting from Georgia, told *The Washington Post* that any alleged damage to the monument was “a disgrace.”

![Visitors at the National Mall expressed mixed reactions to the project’s execution, with some lamenting the rapid timeline and others calling the rising costs a waste of taxpayer money](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/05/15/29/GettyImages-2278647231.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The things that we’re trying to honor here are about more than an individual,” Johnson said. “Whether they appreciate or don’t appreciate our president, still respect the fact that this isn’t just about a person; it’s about our entire country and our country’s history.”

Regarding the structural flaws and rapid timeline of the repairs, Johnson added: “When you’re trying to rush to get a project done by a certain date, that happens.”

Other tourists criticized the cost and the management of public space in the capital.

“I think it’s a complete waste of taxpayer money,” Andrea Meisgeier, 53, of San Diego, told the publication.

Her son, 19-year-old college student Maxwell Meisgeier, reflected on the atmosphere around the National Mall.

“You feel like it’s his city when you’re walking around in it,” he said. “It’s not your city. It’s not the same.”

“It’s our city,” his mother added. “The citizens’.”
