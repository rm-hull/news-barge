!["Burnham ready to sign new EU deal next month - as he attacks Brexit 'damage'," reads the headline on the front page of The i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/b21d/live/15e0faf0-c682-11f1-9a39-31b474234113.jpg)

According to The i Paper, Andy Burnham is "ready to sign new EU deal next month - as he attacks Brexit 'damage'". Speaking ahead of a long-awaiting EU-UK summit on 20 November, the prime minister told MPs on Monday that a "deal is on the table", which would cover things like food and drink, carbon emissions trading, and under-30s migration. The paper says the under-30s migration is likely to be the most contentious part of any agreement, and negotiations on this "are still taking place".

!["Burnham: I'll let voters decide to rejoin Europe" reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ddd7/live/53b9fb80-c68a-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

The Independent paraphrases Burnham as saying "I'll let voters decide to rejoin Europe". It also carries a picture of actor Russell Brand, who has appeared in court for the start of his rape and sexual assault trial.

!["PM: Britain's relationship with EU 'not good enough'," reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/624/cpsprodpb/a0a7/live/cae68870-c682-11f1-9a39-31b474234113.jpg)

Burnham's statement that Britain's relationship with the EU is "not good enough" also leads the Guardian, as it says he promises "serious dialogue" over the UK's relationship with the bloc. Elsewhere, it reports that there will be a security review for high-profile public figures, including politicians and members of the royal family, after Ann Widdecombe's death.

!["'Dereliction of duty' bid to betray Brexit vote" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/d96b/live/08b53ee0-c682-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

The Daily Express says Burnham is making a "bid to betray Brexit vote". It also bids a "sad farewell to D-Day hero Henry Rice at 100".

!["Burnham in retreat over Russia Brexit 'lies'," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/2590/live/f7d6e5a0-c682-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

The Daily Mail writes that Burnham is "in retreat over Russia Brexit 'lies'." The prime minister had said that Russia interfered in the Brexit referendum but, it writes, "in an embarrassing U-turn", now the PM has "admitted he has no evidence".

!["Hand of Gord" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/1808/live/18ae5650-c683-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

The "Hand of Gord" leads the Daily Star. It says that former UK Prime Minister Gordon Brown "attended an exorcism to help Raith Rovers sign player". Brown has denied the accusations, with a spokesperson telling the paper: "Gordon is always happy to do what he can to support Raith Rovers, but he's never attended an exorcism."

!["Horrifying" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/f0a6/live/137e5d60-c683-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

"Horrifying" declares the Daily Mirror as Reform "bigwig" Robert Jenrick has said he would order the Royal Marines to use "lethal force" against migrants. The Mirror calls it a "new low in British politics".

!["Homes under the shammers" reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/7ead/live/e27027a0-c68f-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

"Homes under the shammers" is the headline of the Sun, as it says Manchester City footballers are "being used to flog a £3bn Abu Dhabi luxury housing development". This is happening, it writes, "despite the furore over the cheating scandal".

!["Putin breaking 'ceasefire' on energy targets, says Zelenskyy" reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a35d/live/0e191910-c682-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

The Financial Times covers comments from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky in its top story, who says Russia has been breaking a ceasefire declared by US President Donald Trump on energy targets. Meanwhile, it reports that hedge funds and banks have "hit out" at "Burnham's bid to ban non-compete clauses in work contracts".

!["We'd be ready to fight tonight" reads the headline on the front page of the Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/9934/live/b0310740-c681-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

"We'd be ready to fight tonight" Nato's Supreme Allied Commander Europe has said, in what the Metro calls a "stark warning to Putin". General Grynkewich has said Nato would destroy "anything and everything" used to assault a member nation.

!["Cut screen time in class 'for sake of pupil health'," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/2cf4/live/c0031940-c683-11f1-9a39-31b474234113.jpg)

The Times highlights a call to "cut screen time in class 'for sake of pupil health'" which comes from leading clinicians. The paper says experts "blame devices for stunting development". It also reports that disability benefits for 120,000 teens "cost taxpayers £1bn a year".

!["IMF warns against Budget giveaways" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/94a5/live/3c993900-c6b9-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

In the top slot for the Telegraph is a warning from the International Monetary Fund against "Budget giveaways". The IMF has said there is an "urgent" need to bring down borrowing, saying help for cost-of-living pressures should be used "sparingly" in all countries, according to the Telegraph.
