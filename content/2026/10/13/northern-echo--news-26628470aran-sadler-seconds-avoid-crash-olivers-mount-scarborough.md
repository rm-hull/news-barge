A 'gentleman' County Durham motorcyclist who 'lived and breathed' the sport had seconds to avoid a crash during a race, an inquest has heard.

Aran Sadler, 32, from Durham, died on April 11 this year during a race event at Oliver’s Mount in Scarborough, North Yorkshire.

An inquest at Northallerton Coroner's Court on Monday (October 12) heard Mr Sadler, an electrician by trade, was competing in the SuperSport A Race 1 at the Bob Smith Spring Cup on his green Kawasaki ZX636 bike when tragedy struck.

During the first lap another competitor, Joey Thompson, crashed off the circuit, but his bike remained on the track, the inquest heard.

Aran, who was behind Mr Thompson, came off his bike and collided with Joey's motor.

![Aran Sadler](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20840501.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aran Sadler *(Image: Aran Sadler Racing / Facebook)*

The 32-year-old was pronounced dead at the scene, despite numerous attempts by emergency services to resuscitate him.

Senior Coroner for North Yorkshire and York, Jon Heath, read statements which described the incident as "happening so fast".

Witness David Turrell said: "The first rider had crashed off the track and had managed to leap over the safety barriers, but his bike was still on the track.

"The other rider came round the corner, fell from his bike and was sliding along the floor and collided with the bike on the floor."

Joey Thompson, the other rider who crashed, said he had been travelling at 90mph when he lost control.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20840502.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aran on the racing track *(Image: Aran Sadler Racing / Facebook)*

"My front wheel had lost grip, and my bike came to a skidding halt on the first lap and fell from under me," he said.

"For people watching, it might have seemed fast, but these bikes are capable of high speeds, and I was going 90mph.

"I managed to avoid contact with other bikes and got out of the way. As I was there, I saw another rider lose control."

Mr Thompson added that there wasn't anything unusual or defective about the track surface.

The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) said race organisers had "taken all steps to ensure health and safety was maintained during the event" and no further action was taken by its inspectors.

Detective Sergeant Marie Williams from North Yorkshire Police told the inquest that police reviewed video and photo evidence and had concluded there was no third-party involvement and that, as soon as marshals became aware of the incidents, they raised the safety flags.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20840503.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aran gears up for a race *(Image: Aran Sadler Racing / Facebook)*

Aran's mum and dad described their son as a person who "lived and breathed racing" after getting his first motorbike aged 11 and starting racing at 20-years-old.

In a statement read by the coroner they said: "Aran was a shy child who always liked bikes. He was well liked by everyone who met him.

"He came out of his shell once he started working as an electrician and took over the business he was working for.

"As parents, we were proud of his business and everything he did.

"He was known on the circuit as the gentleman racer.

"Aran started racing bikes at age 20, and racing was his life.

"He was respectful to his family and friends and would do anything to help them.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20840504.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aran on the racing track *(Image: Aran Sadler Racing / Facebook)*

"He thought a lot of his family, and lived and breathed racing."

Mr Heath ruled Mr Sadler's death as an accident, saying: "On balance, this was an accident at an organised race. Another rider's motorbike was on the track and Aran collided with it after he'd fallen off his own bike.

"It can't be decided how or why Aran lost control, and it's not for me to speculate."

Aran's fiancée Lorna previously paid tribute to her "incredible" husband-to-be.

She wrote: "We are all devastated by Aran's loss and are struggling to come to terms with it. Although we were aware of the risks of his sport, we never imagined we would be doing this.

"Aran was a fantastic motorcycle racer with incredible raw talent, but he was also a son, brother, and fiancé.
