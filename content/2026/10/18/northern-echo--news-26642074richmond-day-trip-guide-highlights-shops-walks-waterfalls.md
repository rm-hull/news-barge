Richmond has long been one of North Yorkshire's most popular market towns, drawing visitors with its cobbled streets, Norman castle and views across the River Swale.

Beyond its headline attractions, it offers independent shops, historic buildings and riverside walks, making it ideal for a day out.

Those qualities have recently been highlighted by North East content account 'New Girl in Toon', who shared her guide to Richmond with thousands of social media followers, describing it as "perfect for a day trip".

Her itinerary begins in the heart of the town, where Richmond's independent businesses continue to flourish around the historic marketplace and surrounding streets.

Among the shops featured are All Things Bright in the Market Place, King St Kitchen & Gift and Pink Sundays on King Street, alongside Dots Place on Market Street and Wilfred Deli & Pantry on Finkle Street.

The content creator also recommends BB in Finkle Interiors, Greys Interiors, Caris Green and Everyday Style Story, highlighting Richmond's range of homeware, gift and lifestyle shops.

The Victorian Market Hall also earns a mention.

At its centre is The Book Stop, where visitors can browse hundreds of titles at low prices while supporting a local business.

Food is another major part of the Richmond experience, and the video sees the day start at The Little Drummer Boy, a town centre café that has become well known for its generous cake selection.

In the video, she jokes that customers will need "good luck" getting past the cake counter without taking a slice home for later.

History lovers have plenty to explore too.

One of Richmond's most celebrated attractions is the Georgian Theatre Royal, regarded as Britain's most complete working Georgian playhouse.

Visitors can take a guided tour, stand on the stage and explore backstage areas that are normally hidden from public view, including some of the country's oldest surviving theatre scenery.

The theatre sits just a short walk from Richmond Castle, the imposing Norman fortress that has watched over the town for more than nine centuries.

Perched above the River Swale, the castle remains one of North Yorkshire's most recognisable landmarks and offers panoramic views across the surrounding countryside.

Richmond's natural surroundings are another key attraction.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21588859.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Richmond Falls on the River Swale is one of the town's most popular beauty spots, drawing visitors with its dramatic cascades and scenic riverside views.

At Richmond Falls, the River Swale rushes over broad limestone shelves at one of the area's most photographed spots.

Just minutes from the marketplace, the waterfalls draw walkers, photographers and families throughout the year.

From there, visitors can continue to The Station, Richmond's restored Victorian railway terminus.

Once a transport hub, the building has found new life as a visitor attraction featuring a three-screen cinema, galleries, shops and places to eat.

For those wanting to stretch their legs further, the content creator recommends the Easby Loop, a 3.5km circular walk linking The Station with the ruins of Easby Abbey.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21588832.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Easby Abbey, near Richmond, is one of the highlights of the popular Easby Loop walk, attracting visitors with its impressive medieval ruins and riverside setting.

Following sections of a former railway line before returning along the riverbank, the route is widely regarded as one of the most scenic walks in the Richmond area.

The walk also passes the Easby Loop Café, where visitors can stop for coffee and cake on a terrace overlooking the River Swale.
