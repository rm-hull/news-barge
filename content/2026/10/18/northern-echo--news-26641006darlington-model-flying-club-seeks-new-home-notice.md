A model flying club with roots dating back to the Second World War is appealing for help after being told it may have to leave its home of almost 50 years.

Darlington and District Model Flying Club is searching for a new flying field after receiving notice that it could eventually have to vacate its long-standing base at Cowton Moor.

The club, which caters for everyone from young beginners to experienced flyers in their 80s, hopes a landowner, farmer or business owner may be able to offer a suitable piece of land and help secure its future.

Chairman Neil Dixon said members were determined to keep the club going but had to plan for a move.

Mr Dixon said: "For around 50 years, our current field has been more than just a flying site. It has become a meeting place, especially for many of our older and retired members, who come along not only to fly model aircraft but also to enjoy company, conversation and a cup of tea in our clubhouse.

"Our oldest member is about 88 and still flying, while our youngest is around eight and just beginning to learn. We also have lady flyers too, so it really is a family-friendly club with a passion shared across generations."

The club's history dates back to the post-war years, when members met at Darlington Library before later moving to the former Croft Aerodrome, where model aircraft were flown alongside full-size planes.

In the late 1970s, members were offered the opportunity to use a field on Cowton Moor and have remained there ever since.

Over the decades, the club has weathered its share of challenges. During the foot-and-mouth outbreak, members temporarily flew from Catterick Racecourse until restrictions were lifted.

It has also played host to some unusual moments, including a marriage proposal in which a model aircraft towed a banner reading: "Will you marry me?"

The club recently learned it may eventually need to leave its current site because of changing circumstances affecting the landowner.

While members understand the position, they are now looking ahead and hoping to find a new permanent base within the Darlington area.

Ideally, the club is seeking a field large enough to accommodate a grass runway measuring around 120 metres, preferably running east to west, with a clear and safe approach for aircraft.

The club also has a portable cabin which serves as a clubhouse and social hub for members.

Around 10 to 15 members regularly attend flying sessions in good weather, while winter months are often spent building new models and sharing advice over a cup of tea.

Mr Dixon added: "After more than half a century on our current field, we are now hoping for a little help to keep this much-loved club flying.

"We would be very grateful to hear from anyone who may know of a suitable piece of land, or who would simply like to help secure the future of model flying in Darlington for the next generation."

Anyone who believes they may be able to help the club find a new home is encouraged to get in touch with Darlington and District Model Flying Club.
