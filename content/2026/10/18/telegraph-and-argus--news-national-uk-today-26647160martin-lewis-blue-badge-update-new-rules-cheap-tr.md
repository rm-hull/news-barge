Money expert Martin Lewis has issued an update on Blue Badge rules, revealing how more people could qualify for discounted train travel following changes introduced earlier this year.

The Money Saving Expert founder explained that eligibility for the Disabled Persons Railcard has been widened, meaning people with certain long-term health conditions and neurodivergent conditions may now be able to benefit.

The railcard offers a third off rail fares and costs around £20 for one year or £54 for three years.

Speaking on the latest series of The Martin Lewis Money Show Live on ITV1 and ITVX on Tuesday, October 13, Martin highlighted the changes and urged viewers to check whether they could qualify.

Martin said: "Disability railcard, they have widened the eligibility. Earlier in the year, they said Blue Badge holders were added.

"Now if you have a long term health condition, mental impairment or neurodivergence that impacts your ability to travel, you can get one too.

"A third off rail fares, costs about £20 a year, or £54 for three years. So some more people may be eligible for that one."

The changes mean people who previously did not qualify for the railcard may now be able to access cheaper train travel, provided they meet the eligibility criteria.

### Blue Badge holders added to eligibility earlier this year

The Blue Badge scheme allows eligible people with disabilities or certain medical conditions to park closer to their destination.

Depending on the circumstances and local parking rules, a Blue Badge can allow holders to use designated disabled parking bays and park in some places where restrictions would otherwise apply.

The cost of applying for a Blue Badge varies across the UK. Applications cost £10 in England and £20 in Scotland, while the badge is free in Wales.

Martin explained that a change introduced on March 1 allowed Blue Badge holders to qualify for a Disabled Persons Railcard.

Further eligibility changes have since widened access to people whose long-term health conditions affect their ability to travel.

Anyone who thinks they may qualify should check the official Disabled Persons Railcard eligibility requirements before applying.
