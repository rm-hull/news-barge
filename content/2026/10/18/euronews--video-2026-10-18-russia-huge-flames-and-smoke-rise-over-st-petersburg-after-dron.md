Footage shared on social media showed a major **blaze** at a**warehouse** operated by**Ozon**,** Russia**'s second-largest online retailer, which said its logistics centre had been struck. Other images showed thick smoke rising from the area of the** St Petersburg Oil Terminal**, while an** oil refinery**and** depot** were also reportedly hit.

St Petersburg's governor, **Alexander Beglov**, confirmed that** air-defence systems**had been activated after a large-scale** Ukrainian drone attack**on the city overnight, while** Leningrad Region**governor** Alexander Drozdenko**initially reported that** 126 drones**had been shot down over the surrounding region. He later raised that figure to** 144**, with some reports putting it at** 145**.

The assault set the **Petersburg Oil Terminal** and the**Ruchyi oil depot** alight and struck infrastructure in several districts of the city. Beglov said**one person was killed** and**five others were injured**, while operations at** Pulkovo Airport** were temporarily restricted for safety reasons.

The strikes came as President **Volodymyr Zelensky** announced that Ukraine would resume attacks on Russian oil refineries after briefly suspending them following**Donald Trump’s declaration of an “energy ceasefire”** on 11 October. Neither side formally confirmed the arrangement, and Zelensky accused Russia of carrying out**76 attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure** over the following week, including strikes on substations in four regions that left tens of thousands of families without power.

The latest exchange underlines the continuing struggle over **energy infrastructure**, with both sides treating refineries, terminals and power grids as** central targets** in a war that shows no sign of abating.
