**Amber weather warnings have come into force in northern parts of the UK as Storm Boelo continues to move across the country, with wind gusts up to 80mph forecast in the worst-hit areas.**

Strong winds and heavy showers will intensify through Saturday night, with widespread gusts expected to hit Scotland, Northern Ireland and northern England.

The amber warning for Scotland and England will stay in place until 21:00 on Sunday, bringing an increased risk of travel disruption and potential risk to life.

The second named storm in as many days comes as National Grid continues to restore power to thousands of properties in south-west England after the area was hit hardest by Storm Austen.

The National Grid said on Saturday that more than 124,000 properties had been restored with power supplies since the beginning of the storm. However, hundreds were still without power on Saturday evening.

In north-west England, more than 2,000 properties were without power on Saturday night due to the storm.

Strong winds could cause power cuts, flying debris, damage to buildings and trees, travel disruption and danger to life from large waves, the Met Office said.

The strongest winds will affect western Scotland first towards the end of Saturday, before spreading eastwards overnight and through Sunday.

By Saturday evening, wind gusts of 75mph had been reached at South Uist Range, Western Isles, Highland and Eilean Siar, Scotland, on Saturday, according to the Met Office.

Amber wind warnings cover much of Scotland and the far north of England, with a separate warning for parts of Northern Ireland

The Met Office has warned that being outside in high winds could make people "more vulnerable to injury", stating: "Stay indoors as much as possible. If you do go out, try not to walk, or shelter, close to buildings and trees."

Parts of Northern Ireland have also been issued with an amber warning until 10:00 on Sunday.

Meanwhile, yellow rain warnings have been issued for the south-west, north-west and south-east of England, the West Midlands and Wales until 23:00 on Saturday.

Much of the north of England and Scotland has a yellow wind warning in place, which is set to last until 21:00 on Sunday, and Northern Ireland's yellow wind warning will be in place until midday on Sunday.

Events across the country planned for this weekend have been cancelled due to the weather, and a number of travel disruptions have been reported.

In Merseyside, a River of Light event has been called off for the second day in a row, and Knowsley Safari Park has announced that it will close on Sunday due to "high winds".

Several markets across the North West have also been cancelled due to the weather, including Quayside Makers Market in Salford and Macclesfield Treacle Market in Cheshire.

Dublin Airport in Ireland has cancelled several outbound and inbound flights and ferry providers such as StenaLine, Irish Ferries and P&O Ferries have cancelled a number of ferry crossings in the Irish Sea.

Jason Kelly, the Met Office's chief forecaster, said: "People should prepare for the potential for disruption, particularly to travel and power supplies. Secure loose items outside, plan ahead if you are travelling and keep up to date with the latest forecast and warnings for your area."
