# Six-year-old Yorkshire girl helps launch this year's Poppy Appeal

## A young girl from Bradford who is one of the Royal British Legion’s youngest fundraisers has helped launch this year’s Poppy Appeal.

This year’s Poppy Appeal has been launched by one of the Royal British Legion’s youngest fundraisers.

Six-year-old Bradford girl Ivy Knighton-Leake, who was picked for the launch in Manchester, received a special poppy, made from the metal of Second World War Spitfires, from Private Katie Brazier, 22, of the Royal Anglian Regiment.

Ivy is one of the country’s youngest Poppy Appeal collectors and takes part in the annual fundraiser alongside her father Steve Knighton, 41, who was supported by the Royal British Legion (RBL) in adjusting to life outside the military when he left the Royal Signals after six years.

![Ivy Knighton-Leake, 6, one of the UK's youngest collectors, holds one of four special poppies presented to her during the Royal British Legion Poppy Appeal 2026 national launch at Manchester Victoria Station, as she stands with her parents, Kimberley Leake and Steven Knighton.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjM0MWZiY2U3LWU4MGYtNDM3Yy05ODRhLTc0ZjI0ZmE0Y2I5ZTplZDhjNzgyNS1iNTc4LTQzMTctOGI1ZS0yNzlkMDRhNWMzNjM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Mr Knighton said: “Ivy got herself involved. I showed her that I was going to be going and collecting and she saw the stalls a couple of years ago and she said ‘can I join you, can I have a poppy shop?’

“As an ex-serviceperson it’s really important to me and I also benefitted from the services of the British Legion when I first left the military, so I feel like I owe a debt to them and I’m paying it back.”

Ivy’s mother Kimberley Leake said: “It’s so important to me: I had a father and grandfathers who served in the military and (it is) keeping that connection alive for the younger generation to know important the appeal is.”

Private Brazier it was “incredible” to meet Ivy and present her with the poppy.

This year’s appeal launch focuses on armed forces families and recognising the challenges they face to support loved ones who serve.

Representatives from the Royal Navy, Army and RAF delivered three more specially created poppies to forces families across the country yesterday, with launch events in North Wales, Shropshire and London.

This year’s appeal aims to raise more than £50 million to support serving personnel, veterans and their families. The appeal first took place in 1921.

Among the supporters is actress Dame Joanna Lumley, whose father served as a major in the 6th Gurkha Rifles.

She said: “Having grown up in an Army family, travelling with my father’s postings, I’ve seen first-hand how much military service shapes family life.

“When someone serves, their loved ones share in the uncertainty, the time apart and the sacrifices that come with it.

“I’d encourage everyone to get behind the Royal British Legion this year and show their support.

“Wearing a poppy is such a simple thing to do, but it means an enormous amount to those who serve, to veterans and, of course, to their families.”

Poppy Appeal director Lucy Inskip said: “This year, we’re proud to be highlighting that when someone serves, their family serves too, recognising the families behind those who serve and the unique sacrifices they make.

“We want to encourage the public to go out and show their support for the appeal this year.

“From short-term relief to lifelong support, your donation enables the RBL to help the armed forces and their families, whatever the need, whenever it’s needed.”
