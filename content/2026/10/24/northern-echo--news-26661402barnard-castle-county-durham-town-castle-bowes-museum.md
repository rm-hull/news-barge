Tucked away in the County Durham countryside is a historic market town where a magnificent French-style building and the ruins of a medieval castle stand just a short walk apart.

Barnard Castle sits above the River Tees, close to the Yorkshire border, and has attracted visitors for generations thanks to its history, independent shops and picturesque surroundings.

Its narrow streets, traditional buildings and independent businesses give it a character that feels far removed from the busy shopping centres of larger towns.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21618576.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Barnard Castle *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

One of its most surprising landmarks looks as though it belongs in France rather than the North East of England.

## A French-style palace in the Durham countryside

Built in the style of a grand French château, the Grade I-listed museum stands in landscaped grounds overlooking the town.

It was founded by John Bowes and his French wife, Joséphine, who wanted to bring art and culture to people in the North East.

The museum opened to the public in 1892, after both founders had died.

Its collections include paintings, ceramics, furniture, fashion and decorative arts from across Europe.

One of its most famous attractions is the Silver Swan, an extraordinary 18th-century mechanical automaton.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21618577.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bowes Museum, Barnard Castle *(Image: Chris Booth)*

The life-sized silver bird can move its head and neck in a remarkably lifelike way, making it one of the museum's most celebrated exhibits.

Visitors can also explore the museum's grounds, which offer another reason to spend time in the town.

## A medieval castle overlooking the River Tees

A short walk from the town centre stands the landmark that gave Barnard Castle its name.

The castle was established in the 12th century by the powerful Balliol family, who played an important role in medieval English and Scottish history.

Its ruins occupy a dramatic position above the River Tees, with surviving walls and towers offering a glimpse of its former importance.

The fortress was once associated with Richard III, whose emblem of a white boar can still be found among the castle's historic features.

Today, the ruins are managed by English Heritage and remain one of the town's main attractions.

## A marketplace with centuries of history

Barnard Castle's historic centre is another part of its appeal.

The Market Place is home to the Market Cross, commonly known as the Butter Market, an unusual octagonal building dating from the 18th century.

The landmark stands at the meeting point of several streets and is one of the town's most recognisable features.

Surrounding streets are lined with independent shops, traditional pubs, cafés and businesses selling antiques and second-hand goods.

The town has become particularly well known for its antiques shops, drawing visitors who enjoy browsing for unusual finds.

The mixture of historic architecture and independent businesses helps give Barnard Castle a distinctive atmosphere.

## Riverside walks and Teesdale scenery

The River Tees winds below the castle, providing scenic walks within easy reach of the town centre.

Visitors can explore riverside paths and enjoy views of the castle rising above the water.

The surrounding Teesdale countryside offers further opportunities for walking and sightseeing.

Not far away are High Force waterfall, one of the region's best-known natural attractions, and the landscapes of Upper Teesdale.

The combination of countryside, culture and history means Barnard Castle offers much more than a traditional shopping trip.
