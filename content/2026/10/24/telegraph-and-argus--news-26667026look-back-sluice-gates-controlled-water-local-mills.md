By Robin Longbottom

IN Yorkshire dialect sluice gates, for controlling the amount of water let into a mill pond, were called goit stocks.

The goit was the channel along which the water flowed and a stock referred to anything that could be opened and closed.

Small water mills usually relied on a single sluice whilst bigger mills often had very large double gates.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21626464.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Preserved sluice gates at Langcliffe Mill, Settle

Most are now completely ruinous and in danger of being lost to history.

However, a pair of steel gates have been preserved at Langcliffe Mill, near Settle.
