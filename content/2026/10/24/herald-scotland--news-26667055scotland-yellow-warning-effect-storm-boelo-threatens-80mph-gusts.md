A yellow wind warning has taken effect across Scotland as Storm Boelo threatens 80mph gusts this weekend.

The strong wind warning started at midday on Saturday, but at 9pm an amber alert comes into force, lasting until 9pm on Sunday, with only Orkney remaining under the yellow warning.

Forecasters have issued the warnings for much of Scotland on Saturday into Sunday as Storm Boelo hits the nation, the day after Storm Austen brought heavy rain to many areas on Friday.

The Met Office has warned of potential injuries and danger to life from flying debris as well as a chance of damage to buildings, power cuts and travel disruption.

“Very strong westerly winds associated with Storm Boelo are expected, with fairly widespread gusts of 60 to 70mph and gusts to 80mph in exposed coastal areas,” the Met Office said.

“The strongest winds will initially affect western Scotland and the far north of Northern Ireland on Saturday evening and then spread eastwards overnight and through Sunday.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21626535.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Storm Boelo weather warnings *(Image: Press Association Images)*

Rail lines in western and northern Scotland are set to close later as a result of the strong wind and heavy rain warnings across the country.

Services south and west of Kilwinning in North Ayrshire will stop from 9pm on Saturday while further north the Oban and Mallaig lines will close from about 10pm.

On Sunday, the line between Kyle of Lochalsh and Achnashellach in the Highlands will be closed from the start of service, as well as the railway between Wick and Helmsdale.

ScotRail said speed restrictions are also expected across much of the network from late Saturday night and throughout Sunday, with the potential for restrictions to remain in place on the Far North Line between Inverness and Wick into Monday morning.

ScotRail has advised people to check before travelling and to consider alternative arrangements where possible.

The storm has also affected ferry services across the country.

14 of CalMac’s 30 ferry services have been cancelled, some of them until Monday.

All other routes are facing disruption or last-minute cancellations because of the weather.

The Skye Bridge has also been closed to high sided vehicles.

Scottish ministers have activated the Scottish Government’s Resilience Room (SGORR) to prepare for Storm Boelo.

Social Justice and Housing Secretary Shirley-Anne Somerville chaired an SGORR meeting on Friday and said it will continue to “monitor the situation closely over the weekend and provide regular updates to the public”.

The City of Edinburgh Council said it will be closing Princes Street Gardens, Lauriston Castle Grounds and Saughton Winter Gardens on Saturday evening because of the forecast and will reopen them on Monday once it is safe to do so.

Edinburgh Castle will also be closed on Sunday, and visitors have been warned that the site may be delayed or closed on Saturday because of the weather forecast.

The Paisley Halloween Festival event on Saturday – one of Scotland’s best-known Halloween events, according to organisers – has also been cancelled due to the severe weather forecast.

The Enchanted Forest event at Pitlochry on Saturday has also been cancelled, and organisers have said it is possible Sunday’s event may be cancelled too.
