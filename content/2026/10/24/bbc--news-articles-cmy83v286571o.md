Arif Patel, who masterminded a massive VAT carousel fraud, remains on the run

**One of the UK's most prolific fraudsters, described by a judge as a "breathtakingly arrogant" and "totally dishonest" man, remains on the run in Dubai after stealing more than £33m of public money.**

Arif Patel, from Preston in Lancashire, was exposed following a complex investigation spanning years, led by His Majesty's Revenue and Customs (HMRC), which established that he had tried to steal as much as £90m.

Patel, who skipped the UK in 2011 never to return, was eventually convicted in his absence and in 2023 was handed a 20-year prison sentence.

BBC File on 4 Investigates has been on the trail of the man who stole from all of us - and seemingly got away with it.

"Al Capone was brought down by the taxman. This is the same situation and 20 years was more than a satisfying result," said Rod Stone, a veteran investigator of large-scale tax fraud, of Patel's conviction and custodial sentence.

The judge at Patel's sentencing hearing described him as the leader of a "fraternal organised crime group" with his brothers Munaf and Faisal who were "truly high-level criminals".

The fact that Arif Patel has not served a single day of his 20-year prison sentence clearly rankles with Stone.

For him and the dozens of others who were involved in bringing Patel down, there remains a strong sense that justice has only been served on paper.

![Rod Stone, who is wearing glasses and a white polo shirt, is standing in his living room and is smiling at the camera. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/d537/live/a173f2a0-ce1d-11f1-906e-e3128518d85d.jpg)

Former HMRC assistant director Rod Stone specialised in the detection of fraud and organised crime

In the early part of the millennium, Arif Patel appeared to be a businessman very much on the up.

His company, Faisaltex, headquartered in his home city of Preston, was known for manufacturing textiles, such as socks and nighties, which it supplied to retailers.

Patel was a fixture in the Sunday Times Rich List with his wealth in 2006 estimated to be £105m. He was also investing heavily in property across the city and beyond.

In 2007, his teenage son bought a minority stake in Preston North End Football Club. There was even a plan to build a £100m, 19-storey tower block - complete with botanical gardens and restaurant - in the centre of the city.

Behind the facade, though, something very different was going on.

In the early 2000s, organised crime groups had turned to large-scale attacks on the tax system through what became known as carousel fraud.

Chains of fake business transactions are created as a way of stealing VAT repayments from HMRC.

Goods move between different companies, into and out of the country, to generate false tax refunds.

The same shipments of goods are often moved around repeatedly, hence the name carousel fraud.

This type of fraud was costing taxpayers billions of pounds a year and experts like Stone were at the vanguard of the fightback.

This is how Patel's textiles empire appeared on his radar.

"When we started to look at them we did notice some very strange shipments of goods - a container load of hats to Switzerland seemed odd because they were actually Swiss hats," he said.

![A container ship container packed full of bundles of counterfeit clothes sits on a dockside, with its door wide open.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/06a2/live/decc62a0-84bc-11f0-85ca-c5c26b7f555d.jpg)

The fake clothes the gang were selling would have been worth up to £50m had they have been genuine

Most carousel fraudsters dealt in high-value, high-volume items like mobile phones or computer chips, for which investigators were on the lookout.

But Patel's enterprise had predominantly been dealing in textiles, making the scam harder to spot.

The team of investigators discovered Patel had successfully stolen more than £33m of taxpayers' money before HMRC cottoned on to what he was doing.

In total, he tried to steal more than £90m of public money - the rest of the payments were stopped.

Teams of investigators spent years working through vast chains of transactions until they were ready to make their move.

Patel was charged with a range of offences including conspiracies to cheat the taxman, money-laundering, false accounting and selling counterfeit goods.

But when he appeared at Bolton Crown Court in 2011, Patel asked the judge for permission to travel to Dubai on bail.

The judge agreed to this request and Patel left the UK, never to return.

In Dubai, he joined his brothers Munaf and Faisal, who had already left the country before they could be charged for their part in the conspiracy.

Over the next three years, 24 members of Patel's gang were convicted. Collectively, they were jailed for more than 116 years.

A judge ordered Patel's Ferrari Superamerica to be auctioned off

Efforts to have Arif Patel extradited back to the UK failed and he remained a free man in Dubai.

The BBC has heard claims he had been actively seeking to strike a deal with the UK authorities to settle the case against him.

"In my opinion, he thought he was above justice," said Graham Honey, another former HMRC investigator.

In 2020, he was working in the private sector when he said he was approached by Patel's representatives.

Honey said he was asked to open up a channel of communication with HMRC and the Crown Prosecution Service because Patel wanted to make a deal.

It soon became clear, however, that there would be a sticking point.

"It started to become apparent to me that Patel was not willing to come back," said Honey.

"He just thought he could get an agreement, pay some money, and this would all go away."

Honey claimed that while Patel was willing to admit his guilt and repay money, he was not prepared to face a criminal trial or the prospect of time behind bars.

He claimed it was not the first time that Patel had attempted to strike such a deal.

Honey said: "One of the officers told me that he had said 'I'm a tiger, not a caged tiger' meaning he was a big man, he'd done what he'd done, but in essence, he wasn't prepared to go to prison for it."

HMRC said it had seized more than £78m of the gang's UK assets

In 2023, Patel's trial got under way without him, something which is highly unusual and only done as a last resort.

He was found guilty of leading one of the UK's biggest carousel fraud gangs and received a 20-year jail sentence.

In 2025, he was subjected to a confiscation order to repay £90.5m through the sale of assets found to have been gained through criminality.

The funds raised were to be returned to the public purse.

But more than a year later, none of the money has been recouped because, despite being on the run overseas, Patel has been able to go to the Court of Appeal to object to the confiscation order.

That matter has yet to be resolved.

So where is Patel now and what might he have done with his stolen millions?

The BBC has discovered that over the last few years, an increasing number of websites, press releases and social videos have begun to appear, celebrating the achievements of a businessman called Arif Patel in Dubai.

This Arif Patel is variously a business owner, an investor and a philanthropist. The photographs on the sites are often stock images or, in at least one case, belong to a different British entrepreneur.

Many of the pages also suggest Patel has links to Preston and worked in the textile industry.

## 'Pillar of integrity'

The coincidences do not end there.

A story was published in 2024 in which Arif Patel denies allegations of fraud by HMRC.

The article quotes a "Sam Mackenzie", who it said was a close associate of Patel.

Mackenzie spoke out in support of Patel, whom he described as a "pillar of integrity".

The piece also said he had received backing from an "Andrew Fox", who the article described as having reviewed the claims and deemed them to be "unfounded".

It just so happens that the deputy chief constable of Lancashire police is called Sam Mackenzie, while Andrew Fox is a senior prosecutor with the Crown Prosecution Service.

Their names appeared in other stories too, including one which claimed Patel had made a sizeable investment in HMRC anti-fraud technology in Lancashire.

The BBC does not know for certain whether Patel is connected to these sites, but File on 4 Investigates has established some sites are linked to an online reputation management company based in Dubai.

Another story was published earlier this year, this time about a donation Patel had made to a charitable organisation which supports children with disabilities in Dubai.

It shared similarities with some of the other websites but had one key difference - it featured a genuine photograph of Patel.

## 'Should be punished'

With an up-to-date picture, facial recognition technology uncovered numerous other pictures of Patel posing at glamorous events in Dubai.

We showed everything we had learned about the case to the prime minister's anti-corruption champion Dame Margaret Hodge.

"I think this is a shocking story of somebody who deliberately acted fraudulently and laundered money and he should be punished," she said.

"He should not be beyond the reach of the British justice system.

"I think he is clearly abusing the justice system in a way that only rich people can.

"There's an intrinsic injustice in a system that allows those with money to delay the implementation of a punishment for a crime and to delay the confiscation of assets which was stolen as part of that crime."

The BBC contacted the Government of Dubai but did not receive a response.

The BBC also contacted Arif Patel via his Manchester-based solicitor - as well as his brothers Munaf and Faisal - but did not hear back.
