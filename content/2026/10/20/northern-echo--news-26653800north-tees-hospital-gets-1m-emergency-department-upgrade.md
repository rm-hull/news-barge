The University Hospital of North Tees will receive £1m in government funding to refurbish its emergency department and bring down waiting times for patients.

The two-year cash boost will go to refurbish the Stockton hospital’s emergency department entrance, reception and waiting areas.

It is part of a government investment into 14 urgent care schemes across the country, which was announced as the House of Commons is about to hear a debate on the hospital.

The refurbishment aims to “reduce overcrowding, improve patient flow and make it easier for people to be assessed and directed to the right care first time, including faster access to community-based services”, the government says.

It has announced an almost £140m investment in new urgent treatment centres, ambulance fleets and emergency department upgrades.

The University Hospitals Tees group, which covers both North and South Tees NHS Foundation Trusts including the North Tees hospital, said patients would benefits from “significant improvements” to redesign and enhance the department.

It says the investment will support the design and construction of improvements to create a more efficient and welcoming environment and reduce waiting times.

The plans, currently being developed with patients and staff, will focus on improving the experience of people attending the department from the moment they arrive.

Proposed improvements include expanding the waiting area with more space and comfort for patients and families, more capacity to ensure patients are assessed more quickly and enhancing reception for a “smoother patient journey”.

It may also add improvements requested by patients, like better access to drinking water and phone-charging, and improving digital information systems giving patients real-time updates and waiting times.

The improvements are designed to support the emergency department to meet national standards for patient care, including reducing the time patients spend waiting for assessment, treatment or admission.

The project is currently in the design phase, with works expected to begin in the 2027-28 financial year.

Steve Taylor, director of estates, said: “We are grateful for this funding, which will help us develop an emergency department that is fit for the future and able to meet growing demand.

“These improvements will help us create a more comfortable environment while supporting our clinical teams to assess and treat patients as efficiently as possible. We’re hard at work drawing up the plans for our enhanced emergency department waiting area.

“We’re keen to do all we can to improve the experience of our patients and their loved ones as they wait for emergency care.”

The University Hospital of North Tees’ buildings were previously branded “not fit for purpose”, were costing hundreds of millions of pounds to maintain, and were said back in 2023 to have only eight years’ life left in them.

A pledge to build a new hospital was part of Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen’s 2024 mayoral campaign.

Matt Neligan, University Hospitals Tees’ deputy chief executive and chief strategy officer, told Stockton Council’s adult social care and health committee in May they needed to develop businesses cases for redeveloping the North Tees estate.

He said: “It is essential, it is a high priority, that’s acknowledged in the region. And it’s going to be the major endeavour over the next 10 years that gets us into that position where we can ensure that we’ve got facilities that are modern and up-to-date and our residents can be proud of.”

Chris McDonald, Labour MP for Stockton North, said today: “This £1m investment is very welcome news for patients and staff at North Tees. It will improve the A&E entrance, reception and waiting areas, helping people access care more quickly and in a better environment.

“We all know the hospital needs much more significant investment in the years ahead. I have been making that case directly to government and will continue pushing for both the immediate funding needed to keep North Tees safe and effective today, and a long-term plan that gives our area the hospital facilities it deserves.

“The doctors, nurses and support staff at North Tees do an outstanding job. They deserve buildings and facilities that match their expertise and dedication, and this is an important step in that direction.”

Matt Vickers, Conservative MP for Stockton West, who has called a debate on the University Hospital of North Tees in the House of Commons on Wednesday, said: “Any investment that eases pressure on our hard-working hospital staff is welcome, and I’ll always back improvements to the hospital. But £1m for a refurbished entrance and waiting area is a sticking plaster, not a long-term plan.

“North Tees is an ageing, crumbling hospital, and local people have been clear that it requires a significant investment. Labour still haven’t committed a penny towards it.
