A large majority of Americans do not believe President Donald Trump would accept the results of the upcoming midterm elections if Republicans suffer losses, even as most voters retain confidence in the overall fairness of the vote, according to a survey conducted by *The Washington Post*, ABC News and Ipsos.

The skepticism crosses party lines, with seven in ten Americans overall lacking confidence that Trump would accept a defeat. That view is shared by vast majorities of Democrats and independents, as well as more than a third of Republicans.

Most Americans still express trust in how the election will be run. Six in ten respondents said they were confident the midterms would be fair, a view shared by 70 percent of Democrats and 67 percent of Republicans. Independents were more split, with 51 percent expressing confidence.

Americans also voiced faith in the vote counting process, with 65 percent confident that ballots would be accurately tallied. Democrats were the most confident at 76 percent, followed by 68 percent of Republicans and 55 percent of independents.

Trump has repeatedly raised doubts about electoral integrity over the years, maintaining unproven claims regarding his 2016 and 2020 election results. When asked by NBC News in February if he would trust the results should Republicans lose control of Congress, he said, “I will if the elections are honest.”

![Most Americans doubt President Donald Trump would accept the outcome of the upcoming midterm elections if Republicans suffer losses, even as a majority remain confident in the overall fairness of the vote, according to a recent nationwide poll](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/17/05/GettyImages-2178247386.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Courts and independent studies have consistently found no evidence of widespread voter fraud. Under the U.S. Constitution, states administer elections rather than the federal government, but Trump has voiced a desire to “nationalize the voting” and “take over” elections.

In a January interview with *The New York Times*, he expressed regret over not having military personnel seize voting machines following his 2020 loss. Local officials reported to* The Washington Post* that they were taking precautionary steps for potential federal interference or attempts to seize ballots.

Additionally, his administration has scaled back operations at federal agencies focused on voting safeguards and previously sought restrictions on mail-in ballots before being blocked by the Supreme Court.

![Trump has long questioned the integrity of US elections, continuing to make baseless claims about his losses and past contest results](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/14/2299457003...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

By comparison, voters expressed broader confidence in Democratic leaders. Sixty-one percent of Americans — including 88 percent of Democrats, 58 percent of independents and 36 percent of Republicans — said they were confident Democratic leaders would accept a loss. Among undecided voters and non-voters, only 37 percent felt confident Democratic leaders would accept a loss, while just 16 percent said the same for Trump.

Voters across the country offered differing views on the political landscape and election reliability.

Jonnie Smith, a 69-year-old human resources manager from Johnson City, Tennessee, who typically votes Republican but opposes Trump, doubted the president would accept a defeat.

“He has to throw dirt on it if it doesn’t come out his way, period,” Smith told *The Washington Post*. “He’s shown us that every time he loses.”

![Municipal election officials reported taking precautionary measures following Trump’s past statements expressing regret over not seizing voting machines in 2020](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/17/04/GettyImages-2175646399.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Gary Podbielski, a 67-year-old retired information technology manager near Houston who usually votes Republican but plans to support Democrats this cycle, agreed.

“He’ll fight it,” Podbielski said. “He’ll say something stupid.”

Other voters offered a more optimistic view of the process. Patty Nelson, a 58-year-old executive assistant in Jonesboro, Arkansas, who voted for Trump two years ago but does not plan to participate in the midterms, maintained trust in the system.

“I always do feel that the elections are fair,” Nelson said. “I feel like we have a [voting] system in America that works.”

However, Jennifer Fenderson, a 41-year-old nurse in Leesburg, Florida, who supported Trump in 2024 and remains undecided for this year’s governor and Senate races, voiced doubts.

“They’re never fair when it comes down to voting,” Fenderson said. “We just vote, but I don’t know if our vote really matters or not — or if they already have who they want selected.”

![Confidence among Republican voters has recovered significantly since late 2024, when fewer than half expressed trust in vote tallies](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/17/16/GettyImages-2182503639.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When asked about the House, 35 percent of voters predicted Democrats would capture the majority, compared with 16 percent who expected Republicans to maintain control. Another 28 percent viewed the chamber as a toss-up, while 21 percent remained undecided.

Republican trust in election accuracy has rebounded considerably compared to late 2024, when an ABC News-Ipsos poll showed just 49 percent of Republicans confident in vote counts.

Following the 2024 election, a Post-UMD poll indicated nearly all Republicans viewed Trump’s victory as legitimate, with 77 percent stating there was no solid evidence of fraud.

The poll surveyed 2,545 adults nationwide October 9-13 and has a margin of error of plus or minus 2 percentage points.
