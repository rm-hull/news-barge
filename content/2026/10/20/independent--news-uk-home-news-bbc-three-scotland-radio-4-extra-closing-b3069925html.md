The BBC will close BBC Three, BBC Scotland and BBC Radio 4 Extra linear broadcast services in spring 2027 as part of cost-cutting measures, the corporation has announced.

In addition, around 100 jobs will be cut at BBC News, as the corporation faces pressure to make savings of £500m by 2028.

A number of roles will also be cut as a result of the broadcast closures, including 10 in Scotland.

BBC Scotland will remain a channel on iPlayer, but will no longer be a broadcast channel on television. Content will also be available on BBC One and BBC Two in Scotland.

BBC Three content, which is aimed at 16- to 34-year-olds, will be available on BBC iPlayer and on other platforms, including YouTube.

![Phoebe Waller-Bridge’s hit show Fleabag first aired on BBC Three](https://static.independent.co.uk/2021/03/02/14/17661113-high-.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It is not the first time BBC Three has been cut from linear TV. It returned as a fully fledged TV channel in January 2022, six years after it moved online.

The channel has since been responsible for major hits including *Fleabag*,* Normal People*and* Killing Eve*.

Programmes from Radio 4 Extra will still be available on BBC Sounds – and The Daily Service will be on BBC Sounds and local radio.

In a note to staff setting out the changes, BBC director general Matt Brittin said: “We need to make every pound work harder for audiences as we respond to the immediate cost pressures and reinvent the BBC for a changing world. We all know that means tough choices.”

New director of BBC News Rachel Jupp will also lay out plans for further savings early next year, while other divisions will confirm savings plans in the coming months.

This follows earlier savings announced in BBC Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, Local and Speech and Sounds.

At the weekend the BBC confirmed its long-running home renovation TV series *DIY SOS* will be “rested” next year amid the corporation’s ongoing “savings challenges”.

The BBC also cancelled plans to build a new bespoke studio for *The Archers* at its Tea Factory in Digbeth amid its cost-cutting measures.
