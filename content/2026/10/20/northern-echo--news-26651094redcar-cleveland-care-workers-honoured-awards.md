Care workers who helped people communicate for the first time, supported residents with dementia and went above and beyond have been honoured at an awards ceremony.

The Caring Kind Champion Awards at the Mayor’s Parlour celebrated paid care workers whose dedication and compassion have transformed their communities.

Councillor Ceri Cawley, Mayor of Redcar and Cleveland, said: "It was a privilege to take part in this awards ceremony and celebrate those who make such a difference to the lives of people across our communities.

"Care workers provide far more than practical care and support; they offer kindness, companionship, dignity and reassurance to some of our most vulnerable residents.

"Every day, they help people maintain their independence, stay connected to their families and communities and live safely and comfortably in their own homes for as long as possible."

The awards covered four categories: Rising Star, Young Carer of the Year, Unsung Hero and Lifetime Achievement.

Grace McPherson of Clover Group won the Rising Star Award for care workers with less than three years’ experience who are already making a difference.

At 19, Ms McPherson was praised for building trusting relationships and helping people develop confidence, communication skills and independence.

She helped a non-verbal person communicate for the first time and supported others in reaching everyday goals.

Keith Hewitt of Caremark was runner-up for supporting clients and colleagues, taking people to concerts and community events, fundraising for local causes and mentoring new staff.

Alannah Adams of Housing 21’s Aldaniti Court won the Young Carer of the Year Award for care workers aged 25 and under.

Alannah was recognised for supporting residents, particularly those with dementia, running activities and building meaningful connections beyond her formal role.

Erin Smith of BJP Home Support was runner-up after joining the organisation in 2023 with no care experience and quickly becoming a valued member of the team.

Carly Smith of Clover Group won the Unsung Hero Award for creating a respite service that has "transformed the lives of families" with trusted, personalised care that builds independence and confidence.

Sophie Craddy of BJP Home Support was runner-up for giving up her own time to keep those she supports safe and comfortable.

The Lifetime Achievement Award was presented to Shirley Swales from BJP Home Support in recognition of her 23 years of service.

Ms Swales began as a carer in 2003 and later became a senior carer.

She has supported nearly 1,000 residents, reassured more than 2,000 relatives and trained or mentored over 250 staff.

Donna Brittain of Olam Care Services was runner-up for her hands-on work as a care manager and community and charity fundraising.

Councillor Lisa Robson, cabinet member for adults, said: "Our care workers support some of the most vulnerable people in our communities, usually during the most challenging periods of their lives.

"This event recognised and celebrated those who go the extra mile every day.
