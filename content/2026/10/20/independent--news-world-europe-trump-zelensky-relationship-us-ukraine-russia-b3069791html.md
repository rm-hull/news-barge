Relations between Donald Trump and Volodymyr Zelensky have reached a new low after the US president tore up sanctions on Russia and struck a highly controversial deal to purchase Russian diesel.

The latest fracture followed months of careful positioning by the Ukrainian president and his allies in Europe to avoid upsetting Trump and the US abandoning the country altogether.

But with Ukraine facing another brutal winter of Russian strikes, Washington’s commitment to sharing vital intelligence and interceptor missiles is again in question.

And where Zelensky would once have tried to court the American president with open displays of praise and gratitude, this time he has stood his ground.

As US special envoy Steve Witkoff meets the Ukrainian president in Oslo in a bid to repair ties, *The Independent* looks back on the key moments that have shaped the rocky relationship between the two leaders.

![Donald Trump and Volodymyr Zelensky during the Oval Office showdown last February](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/12/11/13/36/GettyImages-2201823715.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

### 2019: Phone call ‘favour’ threatens to bring Trump down

Shortly after Zelensky was elected in 2019, and during Trump’s first term in office, the US president called his Ukrainian counterpart to discuss a ceasefire proposal over the illegal Russian annexation of Crimea.

In the now-infamous phone call, Trump asked Zelensky to “do us a favour” and investigate then-former vice president Joe Biden, his son Hunter Biden, and the discredited theory that Kyiv colluded with the Democrats in the 2016 election to harm Trump.

That call later ultimately triggered the first impeachment case against Trump by the Democratic-controlled House in December 2019 on abuse of power and obstruction of justice charges. Zelensky later said Trump did not try to blackmail him, while Trump denied wrongdoing and described their interaction as a “perfect call”.

![Trump and Zelensky meet for the first time, in New York on September 25, 2019](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2019/09/25/19/front-trump-zelensky.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The timing of the July call, shortly after the United States froze nearly $400 million in aid to Ukraine, prompted concern that Trump used taxpayer money approved by Congress as leverage for his personal political gain.

Trump was ultimately acquitted by the Senate in 2020.

### 2022: Trump acknowledges ‘brave’ Zelensky after praising ‘genius’ Putin

In February 2022 Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine. It was Moscow's biggest attack on a European state since World War Two.

Trump, having by then been ousted from the White House, condemned the invasion and said he was praying for Ukrainians, after earlier in the week getting flak from Republicans for saying Putin’s attacks were “genius” and “pretty savvy”.

“The Russian attack on Ukraine is appalling. We are praying for the proud people of Ukraine. God bless them all,” Trump said, adding that he thought Zelensky was “brave” for staying in the capital.

![Trump and Putin, pictured in Alaska in 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/23/16/53/5784028-library-a99cee57.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump also began insisting that the “horrific disaster would never have happened if our election was not rigged and if I was the president”.

During his 2024 election campaign, Trump would vow to end the war within 24 hours, adding pressure on the administration and a desire to show progress after he missed his deadline.

### 2025: Trump and Vance ambush Zelensky in White House showdown

Trump won the 2024 election and returned to office in January 2025 with a new mandate and promises to end the war in Ukraine and avoid dragging the US into war.

Zelensky had been among the first world leaders to congratulate Trump and his “decisive leadership” when he won the election. But the two clashed dramatically at their first face-to-face meeting at the White House in February.

![Trump and Zelensky clashed in front of the world’s cameras in February 2025, shortly after the American president’s reelection](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/12/11/13/49/GettyImages-2201823904.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Ukrainian president had seen the meeting as an opportunity to convince Trump not to side with Putin. But Trump and his vice president JD Vance laid into Zelensky, saying he showed disrespect and had not thanked America for its support.

Zelensky was told to leave, a US official said, and a deal to jointly develop Ukraine’s rich natural resources was left in limbo. European allies rushed to Zelensky’s aid, but Washington within days had cut military aid and vital intelligence sharing to Ukraine, blocking billions in shipments.

### 2026: Trump signs diesel deal with Putin

The relationship between Zelensky and Trump steadily recovered after the White House showdown, with the pair meeting quietly on the sidelines of Pope Francis’ funeral in April last year to discuss peace plans.

Trump later restored intelligence sharing and the US resumed negotiations with both sides, including trilateral talks in Geneva in February 2026, though Russia kept close to its original demands for land and concessions.

![Trump and Zelensky met at the Vatican in April 2025 after the disastrous February meeting to get peace talks back on track](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/04/28/14/29/SEI248970808.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump had told Zelensky in the Oval Office that he “didn’t have the cards” in the war with Russia. But by August last year, Ukraine had developed the capability to start hitting oil refineries far across the border, dealing damage to the Russian economy and taking the fight closer to Moscow.

Trump in February 2026 began a costly war with Iran that contributed to rising energy prices in the run up to this year’s November midterm elections. But he directed much of the blame towards Ukraine and its strikes on Russian energy infrastructure, despite Russia’s attacks the other way.

It was against this backdrop that in October the US president announced a deal with Putin to allow Russia to sell diesel, undermining years of mounting pressure for little material difference to prices. When Russia ignored Trump’s ‘energy ceasefire’, Ukraine restarted its attacks across the border, and Trump hit out at Zelensky.

![Donald Trump meets with Zelensky during the 81st United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) in New York City, US, September 22, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/18/05/2026-10-17T100025Z_1149894844_RC2HONALMOW8_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-TRUMP-ZELENSKIY.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine subsequently reported difficulty with its Starlink communications service. Elon Musk deflected criticism, saying it was the US government that owned and controlled the military variant in use on Ukraine’s frontlines.

Ukrainian officials also said they were no longer receiving reliable US warnings of incoming Russian attacks, while the White House said there had been no changes to intelligence sharing with Ukraine.

Asked last week whether Trump could be using Zelensky as a scapegoat for his own domestic problems ahead of the November midterms, the Ukrainian president could only smirk and say: “This is my answer.”
