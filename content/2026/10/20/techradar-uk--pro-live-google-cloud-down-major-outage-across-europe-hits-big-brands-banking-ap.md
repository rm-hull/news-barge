Google Cloud is currently suffering what appears to be a major outage across most of Europe.

The company has confirmed "Multiple services are experiencing issues in the europe-west2 region" with user reports spiking on outage tracker site DownDetector.

It has since updated us to say a 'power event' had caused the issue, with mitigation going on as we speak. We're tracking the issue here, so stay tuned for the latest updates.

The end may very well be in sight - Google Cloud's latest update notes that things may be completely fixed within the hour.

"Full recovery of existing workloads should be completed, tentatively by Tuesday, 2026-10-20 06:00," the update page says.

The good news seems to be that most customers aren't suffering any adverse effects, as DownDetector graphs for other companies does appear to be on the slide.

Let's hope this is the beginning of the end now...

Google's latest update also provides some hope for mitigation - noting that while, "Google Cloud services are unavailable in europe-west2-b zone; some services in europe-west2 region may experience elevated errors and intermittent network issues."

"If feasible, the impacted customer may try to migrate their workload impacted to another available location (zone or region)."

Feeling concerned about the effect of the outage? It should be sorted soon, but here's some words of comfort from Dr. Pete Membrey, Chief Research Officer at ExpressVPN.

"Right now, a problem in Google Cloud’s London region is having a knock-on effect across banks, government services and retailers, because so many of them run on the same infrastructure underneath. When one part of that goes wrong, a lot of familiar apps and websites stop working at once."

"It’s too early to say what’s caused it, and people shouldn’t assume it’s a cyberattack. Most outages like this turn out to be technical faults rather than anything malicious."

"The main thing is not to panic. If your banking app isn’t working, it’s very likely the outage, not a problem with your account. There’s no need to reset passwords or reinstall anything. Be wary of any texts or emails claiming to be from your bank and asking you to confirm your details because of today’s disruption. Services should come back once the issue is fixed, so check official updates and give it time.”

A big update - Google has confirmed the cause of the issue - and it's one of the ideas we anticipated.

"We are experiencing an issue with multiple services in europe-west2 beginning on Monday, 2026-10-19 23:08 PDT," the company wrote.

"Due to a power event we had suffered an outage within europe-west2-b zone. The power event is mitigated. Partial mitigation to infrastructure services will be observed within the next 30 minutes and the remaining services will be mitigated there after."

The outage may have severe knock-on effects for some businesses, showing the need for effective backup systems.

“When a cloud outage leaves people unable to make payments or access everyday services, it exposes how much we depend on infrastructure we never see," Marijus Briedis, Chief Technology Officer at NordVPN, tells us.

“As Google is one of the world’s biggest technology companies, disruption to its cloud services can also affect businesses whose customers may have no idea they depend on Google at all.

“For everyday Google users, this dependence can mean several services they rely on suddenly being unavailable at the same time. Someone unable to pay through one banking app may find their alternative is affected too. Keeping a second payment option can help, although using a different bank does not guarantee it relies on different technology.

“Banks, retailers and delivery companies may look like separate businesses, but behind the scenes many rely on the same handful of technology providers. A problem at one supplier can quickly spread beyond its own customers.

“For businesses, the lesson is not that the cloud is unreliable, but that using a major provider does not remove the responsibility to prepare for disruption. Critical services need tested arrangements to switch to another system when something goes wrong.

“A backup is only useful if it can operate independently of whatever has failed. Businesses need to check whether their main service and their fallback rely on the same underlying systems, and test how quickly they can switch between them. Having copies of your data does not, on its own, mean customers can carry on making payments or accessing their accounts.”

It's too early to debate what might have caused the outage, but the limited reach of the damage (across European regions) could mean any number of things.

It's unlikely to have been a cyberattack, as user outage numbers are too low, so it could be an internal software or hardware issue, or even a power surge across the network.

Google Cloud is still set to give us an update soon, so we'll stay tuned for what they say.
