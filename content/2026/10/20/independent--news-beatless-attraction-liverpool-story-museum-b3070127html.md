Liverpool is set to become an even bigger pilgrimage site for Beatles fans, with plans for a major new attraction celebrating the band’s history and legacy to open in 2028.

The ambitious project aims to give the Fab Four a world-class visitor destination to rival Elvis Presley’s Graceland in Memphis, Tennessee, and Dolly Parton’s Dollywood in Pigeon Forge, according to its backers.

Apple Corps Ltd., the company that manages the Beatles’ business interests, announced on Tuesday that an expanded Beatles Story museum will open in central Liverpool. The launch is expected to follow the release of director Sam Mendes’s four biographical films about John Lennon, Paul McCartney, George Harrison and Ringo Starr, starring Harris Dickinson, Paul Mescal, Joseph Quinn and Barry Keoghan, respectively.

The new attraction will occupy the former St John’s Market site, covering 100,000 square feet opposite Liverpool Lime Street station. The Beatles Story currently operates from the city’s docklands, but the move will bring the museum closer to the heart of the city where the group began its journey.

Apple Corps said the new venue will use “immersive technology, original artifacts and spectacular reconstructed sets” to take visitors from the band members’ childhoods in postwar Liverpool through their hardscrabble years in the bars of Hamburg, Germany, to the explosion of “Beatlemania” and global fame.

![The former Radio City tower in Liverpool is to be transformed into a Yellow Submarine-themed viewing experience as part of plans for an ‘extraordinary’ new Beatles attraction](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/17/01M5D1XSTESCJVX9PD4HD7WQJ9.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Sets and props from Mendes’s movies will create versions of Liverpool’s Cavern Club, where the band got its start; the famous Abbey Road studios in London; and Shea Stadium, the site of their legendary 1965 performance.

The nearby St Johns Beacon, a 400-foot (122-meter) former radio tower, will be transformed into a tourist attraction inspired by the band’s song and animated movie “Yellow Submarine,” offering views over the city.

“Just like Graceland or Dollywood, it’s fitting that The Beatles will have a world-class destination for fans to visit in the center of the city where the story started,” Apple Corps chief executive Tom Greene said.

Apple Corps said a portion of the ticket revenue will support a charity set up to nurture new artists in Liverpool.

![Organisers hope the new Beatles attraction will create an experience to rival a visit to Elvis Presley’s home Graceland or Dolly Parton’s theme park Dollywood](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/20/17/01M5D1XZRM9W73YM4F97F7EWTA.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The two surviving Beatles welcomed the development. Paul McCartney said the band’s success “couldn’t have happened without Liverpool and I can’t wait for people to be able to step into our story and share the experience.” Ringo Starr said, “Liverpool was, and still is, music city.”

The announcement is the latest chapter in a Beatles boom, 56 years after the band’s breakup. Apple Corps has already announced that a new fan experience will open in late 2027 in the band’s former London headquarters at 3 Savile Row, whose rooftop was the site of The Beatles’ last public performance in 1969.

Mendes is directing a quartet of biopics starring Harris Dickinson as John Lennon, Joseph Quinn as George Harrison, Paul Mescal as McCartney and Barry Keoghan as Starr. All are due for release in April 2028.

The legacy of The Beatles has helped transform Liverpool into a culture and nightlife destination, reviving the city’s economy after decades of decline following the closure of its docks.

Mike Jones, a Beatles expert at the University of Liverpool, said the “double boost” of the films and attractions will bring in many more Beatles fans.

“I’m not sure that the city’s quite prepared for it,” he said, calling it both a risk and an opportunity. “What we have to resist is the Disneyfication of Liverpool, the Disneyfication of the Beatles."

That said, there's a lot more that people should know about the band and its legacy, Jones said.

“There’s a rich story to be told about the relationship between Liverpool and the Beatles, a very rich story," he said. "And I’m not sure that story’s been told."
