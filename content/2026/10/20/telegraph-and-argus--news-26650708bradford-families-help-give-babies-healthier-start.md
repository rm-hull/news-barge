A RESEARCH programme aiming to improve newborn health has reached a major participation milestone.

More than 1,500 women in Bradford have now enrolled in the Generation Study, which focuses on early identification of rare genetic conditions in newborns.

Led by research midwives and supported by maternity teams, the Bradford cohort is part of a national effort to improve genetic testing, diagnosis, and treatment for babies.

Jenny Butler, research midwife and principal investigator for the Generation Study in Bradford, said: "Enabling more than 1,500 women to take part in this study is a fantastic achievement for Bradford and the team.

"It demonstrates the commitment of both our team and the families who have chosen to take part.

"By participating in the Generation Study, families from Bradford can access genetic testing through the programme, helping babies receive treatment more quickly if a genetic condition is identified."

Babies involved in the study are screened for more than 200 rare genetic conditions that are treatable through the NHS.

A key factor in Bradford’s success has been its inclusive approach, with materials available in multiple languages and support for families whose first language is not English.

The Generation Study is open to pregnant women aged 16 or over who are expecting a single baby.
