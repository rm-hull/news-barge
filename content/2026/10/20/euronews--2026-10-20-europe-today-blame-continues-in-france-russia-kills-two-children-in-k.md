## Blame in France over protests continues as Sébastien Lecornu accuses the ultra-left of orchestrating the unrest. In Kharkiv, two children are among the victims of a Russian strike on a park as Putin reportedly floats a summit without Kyiv. And we interview with Ryanair CEO Michael O’Leary.

### On today's show:

**Estelle Nilsson-Julien** reports from Paris, where the political blame game continues over who is responsible for the weekend’s unrest. French Prime Minister**Sébastien Lecornu** has accused the “ultra-left movement” of seeking to “orchestrate deep unrest” in the run-up to next year’s presidential election.

We hear from **Audelia Sitbon**, a pharmacist near Place de la République in Paris. Last Saturday, rioters smashed their way into her shop, looted her stock and set it ablaze. Left with empty shelves, shattered glass and worries about how to support her three children, she shared her story with Euronews’s** Valérie Gauriat**.

We speak to **Patrick Molinoz**, mayor of Venarey-les-Laumes, as French retailers now face hundreds of millions of euros in damage. Shops of all kinds, from small boutiques to luxury stores on the high street, were targeted by thieves and vandals.

We speak to Ryanair CEO **Michael O’Leary** for the view from industry on Europe’s wave of protests, as people also take to the streets of Spain, Portugal and Italy.

**Sasha Vakulina** reports on the latest developments in Ukraine’s war, where two children were among the victims of a Russian drone strike on a park in Kharkiv yesterday. It comes as**Vladimir Putin** has reportedly proposed a summit with**Trump** and China’s**Xi Jinping** next month to discuss ending the war, potentially without Kyiv at the table.
