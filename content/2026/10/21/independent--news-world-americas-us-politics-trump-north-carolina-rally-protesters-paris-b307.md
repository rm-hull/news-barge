President Donald Trump compared hecklers at a rally he held in North Carolina to the violent riots in Paris that have erupted during nationwide protests.

Minutes into his rally in Greenville Tuesday night, Trump was heckled by two protesters in succession.

“They get home, and they get smacked in the face by the mother and the father, maybe a little harder by the father. And they say, ‘We voted for that man. We love that man. You did that — how disgraceful,’” Trump said, using the same jab he’s hit protesters with at rallies in Texas, Ohio and Alaska this month.

But then he took it a step further — likening the demonstrators interrupting his speech to the thousands of people who have been arrested for rioting and looting during ongoing protests in France against the rising cost of living and poor conditions in schools.

As Trump travels across the country to campaign for Republican candidates ahead of November’s midterm elections, he has struggled to get through a speech without getting interrupted by protesters.

![President Donald Trump has compared hecklers at a rally he held in North Carolina to the violent riots in Paris that have erupted during nationwide protests](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/0/55/GettyImages-2301222586.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“They get paid by the radical left lunatics that you watch taking place in France,” Trump said. “I would say this is a slightly lesser event,” he then admitted.

French President Emmanuel Macron blamed the violence in France on “seditionists from far-left fringe groups, joined by many criminals,” during his national address Sunday.

During another disruption later that night, Trump said, “Don’t hurt them,” but then mimicked wanting to punch them — a callback to his first presidential campaign in 2016 when he encouraged his supporters in Iowa to “knock the crap out of” anyone preparing to throw tomatoes.

Trump was in Greenville Tuesday night to stump for Republican Senate candidate Michael Whatley, whom he called “fantastic.” The president mentioned Whatley’s time as chair of the Republican National Committee with his daughter-in-law Lara Trump during the 2024 election cycle.

He went on a bizarre tangent about Lara’s body, telling the crowd, “She’s an extremely fit young woman. Have you ever seen her exercise?”

!['They get paid by the radical left lunatics that you watch taking place in France,' Trump said Tuesday night. 'I would say this is a slightly lesser event,' he then admitted.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/0/36/GettyImages-2300683951.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“She’s so fit it’s ridiculous,” he added. “But she’s also fit up here,” Trump said while pointing to his head. “The two of them were unbelievable.”

Whatley was trailing the state’s former Democratic Gov. Roy Cooper by more than 10 points in a recent poll. Fifty-two percent of voters in North Carolina said they’d vote for Cooper, compared to 39 percent who backed Whatley, according to the Elon University/YouGov survey.

Trump is known for commenting on the looks of people at his rallies, specifically women. But he recently made headlines for gushing over Republican Sen. Jon Husted’s butt at an Ohio rally last week.

“I’m studying his body. I’ve never studied the body of a man before. It’s not my thing,” Trump said Tuesday night as he recalled checking out the former college football star in Ohio. “But I looked at his a\*\*.”

The president then likened Husted’s butt to “a pile of muscle.”

Trump has been fiercely campaigning for GOP candidates in red states, where Republicans would normally win easily. But the party in power typically loses seats in the midterms, and Democrats are gaining ground in several of these states, such as Texas and Kansas.

As Trump spends his nights trying to boost Republicans’ chances in their races, telling his supporters to pretend that he’s on the ballot, the president is dealing with his own record-low approval ratings.

Just 33 percent of Americans approve of Trump’s job as president, while 66 percent disapprove, according to a recent *Washington Post*/ABC News/Ipsos poll.

The Trump administration has been dogged by an unpopular Iran war, stubborn inflation, and an immigration crackdown that critics believe is too aggressive.
