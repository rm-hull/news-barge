Andy Burnham has proposed removing standing charges from energy bills as he faces a grilling at prime minister’s questions on Wednesday.

His government’s decision to approve drilling in the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea has drawn criticism from Labour MPs, as well as climate activists, who are now pressuring the prime minister to reject the Rosebank oil and gas field project.

Zack Polanski described Wednesday’s move as “a betrayal of all of our futures” and said Labour had “abandoned all credibility in the fight against climate change.”

It comes after the prime minister said it would “protect hundreds of jobs and help keep millions of homes warm”, but stressed it would not hinder the government’s targets on renewable energy.

Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh has told Britons not to expect lower energy bills this winter, telling BBC that the “key consideration in coming to this decision was at a time when we are in an energy crisis triggered by the conflict in the Middle East.

“Energy security is really important and this will contribute to the homegrown sources of energy that we have,” she added.

## Ed Davey quizzes Burnham on plans for joining EU single market

*Political reporter Athena Stavrou writes:*

The leader of the Liberal Democrats used his first question at today’s PMQs to ask Andy Burnham about his plans to grow closer to the EU.

The prime minister has suggested another referendum to rejoin the bloc could be included in Labour’s next manifesto, but has so far committed to not ditching the party’s ‘red lines’ which include not rejoining the single market or customs union.

But Ed Davey asked the prime minister to speak about a “single thing” he could do to “fix growth” by taking the UK back into the European single market.

He asked Mr Burnham after “all he has rightly said about the damage that Brexit has done to our economy” whether he is still going to wait until after the next election to consider re-entering the single market.

The prime minister pointed to the upcoming UK-EU summit, calling it “an opportunity for this house to come together and get a good deal for food producers, for farmers in this country, and a good deal for Northern Ireland in relation to energy costs in Northern Ireland.”

## Burnham suggests government could scrap standing charges from gas bills

*Whitehall editor Kate Devlin writes:*

The PM made the announcement as he came under pressure from the Tories over today’s Jackdaw go-ahead.In a surprise move, he told MPs the decision would allow ministers “to do more to help people with bills”.

He added: “I can tell the House today that I have asked the chancellor and the energy secretary to look at whether we can remove standing charges from gas bills and to report back to this House at the earliest opportunity.”

Standing charges are a fixed daily fee added to energy bills and paid regardless of how much energy a household uses.

Ofgem estimates the average gas daily standing charge for those who pay by direct debit is currently 29.68p.

## Analysis: Burnham makes it clear that the door is open for Rosebank

Andy Burnham has upset the left of his party by giving the green light for drilling in the Jackdaw oil and gas field in the North Sea.

This is in reference to the Rosebank oil and gas field in the North Sea. It is clear he wants drilling to go on there.

Despite a jibe from Ms Badenoch that she thought she was listening to Mr Burnham’s predecessor, we are already seeing some changes with the Starmer government in the confidence Mr Burnham is approaching making big decisions.

## Burnham marks 60th anniversary of Aberfan disaster

Andy Burnham opened PMQs by marking the 60th anniversary of the Aberfan disaster, paying tribute to the "strength and spirit of a community that has stood together through adversity all of these years".

An entire generation was almost wiped out when 150,000 tonnes of coal waste slid down the hillside before engulfing Pantglas Junior School in Wales on October 21 1966, killing 116 children and 28 adults.

Addressing the Commons, the prime minister said: "Today also marks the 60th anniversary of the Aberfan disaster and I'm sure the thoughts of the whole house will be with the community today and with the people of Wales.

"I visited on Monday and heard how the injustices the people suffered at the time and in the aftermath of the tragedy at the hands of the state still hurt.

"This is a day to recognise that, say sorry for it, but also celebrate the strength and spirit of a community that has stood together through adversity all of these years."
