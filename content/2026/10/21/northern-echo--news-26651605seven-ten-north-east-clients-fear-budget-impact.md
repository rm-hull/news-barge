There is 'a high degree of nervousness' ahead of Chancellor John Healey's first Budget next week.

In a new survey, Active Chartered Financial Planners found that 71 per cent of clients believe the Budget will negatively impact their finances, while only 2 per cent expect to be better off.

Meanwhile, a survey by business advisory firm BDO says 'almost 90% of businesses believe devolving more spending decisions to local mayors will benefit their growth'.

Active has already written to Chancellor John Healey, raising concerns about frozen tax thresholds, inheritance tax, pensions, and the cost of employing staff.

Karl Pemberton, managing director of Active Chartered Financial Planners, said: “There’s clearly a high degree of nervousness ahead of this Budget.

“Seven in ten of the people we surveyed expect to be worse off afterwards and only a very small proportion believe their position will improve.”

The survey, conducted among a sample of the firm’s 4,000 clients, also revealed that 56 per cent feel less optimistic about the upcoming Budget, and 30 per cent have become less positive about their overall financial outlook.

When asked which taxes were most likely to affect them, 31 per cent cited capital gains tax, 29 per cent pointed to inheritance tax, and 22 per cent identified income tax.

The survey also showed limited support for further tax increases, with just 9 per cent backing the idea.

Instead, 46 per cent favoured reducing government spending to promote growth, while 37 per cent supported lower taxes.

In his letter to the Chancellor, Mr Pemberton raised concerns about the imminent inclusion of pension funds within estates for inheritance tax purposes.

He argued that frequent changes to pension and estate planning rules are creating uncertainty for people making long-term financial decisions.

He also called on the Government to reconsider the ongoing freeze on personal tax thresholds, which is pushing more individuals into higher tax brackets as their incomes rise.

Mr Pemberton said: “What comes through strongly is that people don’t simply want another round of tax increases.

“They need a degree of certainty if they’re going to make sensible long-term decisions about saving, investing, pensions, and their businesses.”

“Addressing the cost of living is understandably a major focus for the Government but businesses will want to see practical and meaningful support to help with the cost of doing business.
