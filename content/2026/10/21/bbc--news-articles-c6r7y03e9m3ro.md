Hackers stole information from Data Cymru's systems in 2024 which exposed the personal information of 3,300 people

**A cyber-attack stole information belonging to thousands of children by accessing an account that used the password "Password1", a data watchdog has said.**

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has reprimanded Ogi Networks Limited (ONL) and Data Cymru after the attack on 1 November 2024.

The hackers exposed personal information belonging to 3,300 people, mostly children, by using a test account, known as Test2, which had existed for more than 12 years and was protected by the password that could be compromised in less than a second.

ONL said the account "should have been removed long before criminals were able to use it", adding it had brought in independent cyber-security specialists.

The company, which provided IT services to Data Cymru, said it accepted the ICO's findings and had "acted on them".

Data Cymru merged with the Welsh Local Government Association (WLGA) in 2025 and now follows WLGA data protection protocols, including the use of "strengthened" passwords.

The WLGA said there was no evidence the stolen information was "shared further or misused".

ONL and Data Cymru were found to have breached UK GDPR rules, external by failing to properly protect their systems and by not regularly checking whether their security measures were effective.

During the ransomware attack, the hackers encrypted 182.7GB of data and stole 42GB from Data Cymru's systems. It exposed the personal information related to two projects supporting children and families.

David Doodson, head of investigations at the Information Commission's Office, said: "Password1 is not an appropriate password, it is a wide-open door to nefarious actors.

"This incident shows how a single weak password, attached to an account that was no longer needed, can leave the door open to a serious cyber-attack that can affect thousands."

Doodson added the responsibility for "these failures did not sit with one organisation alone".

According to the watchdog, ONL created a test account in 2012 and used the password "Password1" to protect it, failing to audit it or remove it for more than 12 years.

It also did not review local administrator accounts after they were first deployed, with accounts on three affected systems using the same password, for the same period of time.

The watchdog said Data Cymru failed to put a data protection agreement in place with ONL, and that there was a gap in responsibility between the two organisations.

It also said Data Cymru failed to ensure the dormant test account was identified and removed, or updated to meet stronger password requirements.

The ICO added it was a lesson for every organisation to "use strong passwords, protect remote access with multi-factor authentication, regularly review every user account, and be completely clear about who is responsible for cyber-security when services are outsourced".

Following the cyber-attack, both organisations introduced stronger security measures.

ONL introduced a process prompting customers to review active accounts periodically and providing recommendations about accounts that should be removed, the ICO said.

It also strengthened its account access controls, including the use of multi-factor authentication.

On 30 April 2025, Data Cymru and ONL entered into a legally compliant data processing contract.

Data Cymru also introduced periodic account reviews and strengthened account access controls, including using multi-factor authentication.

A spokesperson for ONL said: "Within a week of the attack, we'd worked with Data Cymru to check every account on its network and switched off the ones it no longer needed that same day.

"We also brought in independent cyber-security specialists to investigate."

They added: "Since then, we've protected remote access with multi-factor authentication and introduced regular account reviews for the customers whose networks we look after, so accounts that aren't needed are found and removed.

"This was limited to the server we hosted for Data Cymru. No other customer information held by ONL was affected."

A WLGA spokesperson said it remained "committed to maintaining robust information governance standards and continually reviewing the measures we have in place".

"We recognise the fundamental importance of strong information governance and of protecting the information entrusted to organisations working on behalf of local government.

"Since the incident, measures have been put in place to strengthen the arrangements identified by the ICO, including the removal of dormant user accounts, strengthened password and remote user access controls, and improved data processing arrangements to clearly define contractual responsibilities for security and audit activities," the spokesperson added.
