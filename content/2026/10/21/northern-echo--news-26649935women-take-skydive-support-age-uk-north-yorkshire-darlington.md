Two women have completed a charity skydive to raise funds for older people across the region.

The high-flying challenge was in support of Age UK North Yorkshire & Darlington, which is facing severe financial difficulties and could close without urgent funding.

If the charity shuts down, around 16,400 people per year could be left without access to vital support.

Sandra, one of the skydivers, said: "We wanted to do something that would really make a difference while challenging ourselves at the same time.

"Knowing that the money raised will support older people across North Yorkshire and Darlington makes every moment of the skydive worthwhile."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21601975.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sandra *(Image: Age UK/Sky High Skydiving)*

The funds raised will help the charity continue providing information and advice, befriending, wellbeing activities and practical support to help older people remain independent and connected.

Caroline, who also took part in the skydive, said: "It makes a change from the Kitchen, but we're excited to take the leap for such an important cause.

"We hope our efforts inspire others to support Age UK North Yorkshire & Darlington and help us reach our fundraising target."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21601977.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Caroline *(Image: Age UK/Sky High Skydiving)*

Anyone with even a pound to spare, is asked to help **save the charity here**, or is welcome to walk in to meet the team and donate in person at their office at Bradbury House, Beaumont Street West, Darlington, DL1 5SX.

Nicola Henderson-Begg, from Age UK North Yorkshire & Darlington, said the charity was extremely grateful to both women.

She said: "We are incredibly grateful to Sandra and Caroline for their courage, enthusiasm and dedication."

"Their skydive is an amazing way to raise awareness and funds for our work, and every donation will help us continue supporting older people across our communities."

A spokesperson for the charity said it relies on the generosity of supporters, fundraisers, volunteers and donors to keep delivering its services.

The charity continues to thank the public for ongoing donations, which come at a critical time for its future.
