RIBBLE Rivers Trust is calling on the public to get involved in its latest tree planting season.

Starting on November 4 near Settle, the scheme aims to plant tens of thousands of native trees across the Ribble catchment, with additional events planned in Hapton, Ribchester, Preston, and Colne.

The trust says tree planting offers a rewarding way to enjoy the outdoors, meet new people, boost fitness, and support the environment.

James Birwistle, conservation and volunteering officer at Ribble Rivers Trust, said: "For us, tree planting isn't just about creating woodlands.

"It's about creating healthier rivers.

"The trees our volunteers plant today will help improve water quality, support wildlife, and create better habitats for species such as salmon, trout, and eels.

"Every tree really does make a difference."

Volunteers will plant saplings just a few feet tall and no thicker than a little finger.

The trust says anyone who can climb a stile and walk across a field is likely able to take part.

The activity provides fresh air, gentle exercise, and the chance to connect with like-minded people, while contributing to a lasting environmental legacy.

While the focus is on tree planting, the trust says many volunteers also develop lasting friendships through the programme.

Riverside woodlands play a critical role in protecting waterways and wildlife.

They help filter pollution, reduce soil erosion, provide shade to keep rivers cool for fish, and offer habitats for birds, mammals, insects, and fish.

The benefits reach beyond wildlife.

Trees capture carbon, improve local landscapes, and slow water flow across the land, reducing flood risk and supporting natural river processes.

The success of the planting programme relies on cooperation from farmers and landowners who contribute land and work with the trust to develop new woodlands and habitats.

Ellie Schenk, senior woodland creation officer at Ribble Rivers Trust, said: "We're incredibly grateful to the farmers and landowners who work with us each year.

"Their support allows us to create woodlands in the right places, delivering benefits for farming businesses, wildlife, water quality, and local communities.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21610797.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ribble Rivers Trust volunteers working to maintain a new woodland at Salesbury. *(Image: Ribble Rivers Trust)*

"These projects are a fantastic example of what can be achieved when people work together."

No prior experience is needed and all equipment and training are provided on the day.

Anyone interested can sign up for the trust’s volunteer bulletin, which includes weekly updates on upcoming opportunities, as well as information on accessibility, parking, maps, meeting points, and contact details.

With the planting season about to begin, Ribble Rivers Trust says now is the perfect time to get involved and help create healthier rivers, larger woodlands, and a greener future.

To sign up, visit ribbletrust.org.uk/volunteer.
