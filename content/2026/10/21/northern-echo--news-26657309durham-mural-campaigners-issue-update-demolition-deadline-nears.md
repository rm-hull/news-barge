Campaigners fighting to save a historic mural from demolition have issued a fresh update, describing the project as being at a "crucial" stage.

The group behind efforts to preserve the iconic artwork at Durham County Hall say significant progress has been made since launching their campaign, but securing funding remains their biggest challenge as the building moves closer to demolition.

Campaign leader Professor Bennett Zon has updated supporters on plans to remove, restore and relocate the mural from Durham County Hall at Aykley Heads.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21612455.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A mural on the side of Durham County Hall in Durham which depicts the history of the city. The County Hall along with the mural is due to be demolished. *(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

The mural’s future was thrown into doubt when Durham County Hall’s demolition was confirmed, with campaigners told they may need up to £250,000 to save it.

Professor Zon said the campaign has won support from Beamish Museum, Durham University, the Twentieth Century Society, City of Durham Parish Council, conservation specialists and local residents.

Campaigners are also exploring new homes for the mural.

They are discussing moves to Beamish, The Light in Durham or a future redevelopment at Prince Bishops Shopping Centre.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21188693.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

Professor Zon said talks are continuing with those behind plans for Prince Bishops, which a new operator is due to take over next year.

But funding remains a major hurdle.

Campaigners initially hoped a £250,000 National Lottery Heritage Fund grant would cover removal, storage, restoration and relocation.

They are also asking whether demolition contractors could help fund the work through charitable support and are exploring matched funding.

To apply for a grant, the group needs a charity to sponsor and eventually own the mural, plus a committee experienced in managing major heritage projects.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21188694.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

Professor Zon warned that time is running short: demolition is expected to begin next year, but no contractor has been appointed.

Ceramic conservation expert Mike Rieveley has also backed the campaign and agreed to produce a new report on the mural’s condition and future conservation.

Writing in the latest edition of Glazed Expressions, the Tiles and Architectural Ceramics Society journal, Professor Zon called the mural a unique artwork charting Durham’s story from Lindisfarne and St Cuthbert to its mining heritage.

Campaigners say the mural is historically important and could help regenerate Durham’s culture if it is saved and relocated.
