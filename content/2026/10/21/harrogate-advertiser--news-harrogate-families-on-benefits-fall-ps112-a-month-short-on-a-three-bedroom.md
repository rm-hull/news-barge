# Harrogate families on benefits fall £112 a month short on a three-bedroom home

## New figures shine light on Harrogate housing rental market

Families renting privately in Harrogate who rely on benefits face a shortfall of £112 a month or £1,344 a year on a three-bedroom home, new analysis of government figures shows.

![The monthly shortfall between housing help and cheaper-end rents for a two-bedroom home in Harrogate. Source: VOA and DWP figures, analysis by Landlord Insurance UK.](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/jpim-static/image/ugc/2026/10/21/08/harrogate-advertiser-chart_1792522165_d5e9f285-bd5c4454a8d18e94.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=65)


In the Harrogate rental area, the most Universal Credit will pay towards a two-bedroom home is £736 a month.

The cheaper end of the local market is £818, leaving tenants £82 a month short, less than the England typical of £103, but still short.

That ranks 117th of England's 152 rental areas for the size of the gap.

The Local Housing Allowance (LHA) sets the most a private renter can get towards their rent through Universal Credit or housing benefit.

It is normally based on the 30th percentile of local rents, the cheaper end of the market where three in ten rents are lower.

The rates have been frozen since April 2024, and the Valuation Office Agency's 2026 figures for the 30th percentile, from rents collected in the year to September 2025, were not used.

How far help falls short in Harrogate, for every size of home:

* Room in a shared house: help of £421 a month against a cheaper-end rent of £475, £54 short.
* One bedroom: help of £595 a month against a cheaper-end rent of £648, £53 short.
* Two bedrooms: help of £736 a month against a cheaper-end rent of £818, £82 short.
* Three bedrooms: help of £875 a month against a cheaper-end rent of £987, £112 short.
* Four bedrooms: help of £1,275 a month against a cheaper-end rent of £1,396, £121 short.

Nearby rental areas, two-bedroom home:

* York: help of £775 against £873, £98 a month short.
* Leeds: help of £775 against £823, £48 a month short.
* Richmond and Hambleton: help of £550 against £623, £73 a month short.

Across England, the two-bedroom allowance falls short of the cheaper-end rent in all 152 rental areas, with a typical gap of £103 a month, or £1,236 a year.

"The 30th percentile is the cheaper end of what people are actually paying in Harrogate, and the allowance still falls short. Someone has to make up that difference, usually tenants on the lowest incomes, out of money they don't have. That puts tenancies under strain for renters and landlords alike."

Renters can check the figures for their own area, from a shared room to a four-bedroom home, on Landlord Insurance UK's Local Housing Allowance gap page.

The figures compare the DWP's Universal Credit Local Housing Allowance rates for 2026 to 2027 with the Valuation Office Agency's 30th percentile of rents.

Housing benefit uses the same rates set weekly, so the gap for housing benefit claimants is a few pounds a month larger.

Rental areas are the official Broad Rental Market Areas, which do not follow council boundaries exactly. England only.
