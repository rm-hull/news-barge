Britain would be welcomed back into the European Union, but there would be strings attached, the group’s former chief Brexit negotiator Michel Barnier has said.

The former French prime minister, once dubbed the bloc's “Mr Brexit”, pointed out that when the UK left the EU, “they left 600 international agreements.”

Were the UK to rejoin, it would be a case of “welcome back, but there are conditions attached,” he added.

![Former French prime minister and ex-Brexit negotiator, Michel Barnier](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/19/11/2245609544...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Andy Burnham recently said it was “possible” Labour’s next election manifesto could include an in-out referendum on EU membership, in his strongest signal yet of his intentions to repair the damage caused by the UK’s departure.

Former Labour leader Neil Kinnock has also said the PM would be the man to lead the UK back into the EU.

Support for rejoining the EU is at its highest level since the Brexit vote, at 61 per cent according to leading pollster Professor Sir John Curtice says, while Nick Bloom, a professor at Stanford University, has estimated that up to 8 per cent of GDP has been wiped off the UK economy through lost trade, increased red tape and delays in pushing the Brexit deal through.

But Mr Barnier also said that Parliament would have to have a say before Mr Burnham presses for a closer relationship with the EU.

He told POLITICO that leaving the bloc “was a sovereign decision by the British people, and so any reopening of talks has to be a sovereign decision of the United Kingdom.”

“Andy Burnham and his government need to clarify their intentions and debate them in the House of Commons and the House of Lords,” he added.

And he warned the UK must be clear-eyed about the long and “very complicated” path to accession.

![Prime minister Andy Burnham recently said it was “possible” Labour’s next election manifesto could include an in-out referendum on EU membership](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/24/16/2291311273...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

There would be “no single market à la carte,” he said. Customs union and single market members who are not in the EU cannot vote on European legislation that sets the rules.

He added, however, that he always “regretted” Britain’s decision to leave the EU.

“Brexit was a lose-lose game, and I would like us to somehow build a win-win game,” he said.

An upcoming EU-UK summit set for November 20 “illustrates the new dynamic encouraged by prime minister Andy Burnham's openness,” Mr Barnier said.

“Before talking about rejoining the bloc, there are lots of things we can do with the United Kingdom,” he added. “We can cooperate on foreign policy, on defense ... it’s even possible that the UK will be included in our big investments on artificial intelligence.”

“All that is open today,” he added.

However, he added: “It’ll be more complicated if the British diverge from European regulation. Currently it’s their right to do so… We need to tread carefully.”
