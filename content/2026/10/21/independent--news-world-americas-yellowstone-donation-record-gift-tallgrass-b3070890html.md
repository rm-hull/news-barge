Yellowstone National Park has received a $105 million donation, the largest single private gift in the agency’s history, from a Colorado company building an AI power hub near Cheyenne.

The park announced the donation Monday, establishing a conservation and stewardship fund with Yellowstone Forever, its nonprofit conservation partner, and Tallgrass, a Blackstone Infrastructure portfolio company.

The fund intends to serve as a permanent source of support for long-term stewardship, the groups said. The donation will establish the Tallgrass WYld Yellowstone Initiative, named for the company that has been operating in Wyoming for more than a decade.

Among the projects the effort will fund is a $35 million visitor center at Norris Geyser Basin, Steven Davidson, senior vice president at Tallgrass said in an interview with *WyoFile*.

“There’s no place for you to sit and engage with a ranger directly,” he said of the sprawling basin. The center will offer opportunities for experiences that are “a bit more interactive,” he said.

![Interior Secretary Doug Burgum called the investment historic and said it brings together ‘the best of American enterprise and conservation’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/17/2277213414...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The fund will advance conservation and research, invest in infrastructure and expand opportunities for Wyoming residents and students to visit the 2.2 million-acre reserve.

“The focus is for Wyoming,” Davidson said. “If you are a Wyoming kid, family, we are going to target these programs for you. Let’s be proud and selfish for Wyoming kids.”

Gov. Mark Gordon called the gift “extraordinary” and said it will help Wyoming students better connect with Yellowstone.

“Ninety-three percent of the park is in Wyoming, and so few of our school kids in Wyoming really know what a remarkable place that is,” he said, adding that it is because people in Wyoming who love the outdoors “already all have favorite places.”

“We’re blessed with beautiful mountains almost everywhere, and so unless you have a sort of a sense of the history and (of) some of the remarkable stuff about Yellowstone, why would you skip over the Winds or the Absarokas?”

More interpretation provided at a visitor center will empower visitors to talk about “more than just: You came and saw,” he said. “Talking about the uniqueness of that area is a valuable thing for visitors at the park generally.”

Interior Secretary Doug Burgum called the investment “historic,” and said it brings together “the best of American enterprise and conservation.”

![Established in 1872 as the world's first national park, Yellowstone National Park encompasses more than 2.2 million acres across Wyoming, Montana and Idaho](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/21/16/34/GettyImages-1241460725.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Blackstone said the gift “reflects our belief that infrastructure investment can and should be a force for good, helping us invest in Yellowstone’s future and the growth of the communities that surround it,” Sean Klimczak, the company's global head of infrastructure said in a statement.

## Period of change for Yellowstone National Park

Lisa Diekmann, president and CEO of Yellowstone Forever, called the gift a transformational investment: “It gives us the ability to meet urgent needs today while building enduring support for the research, education, stewardship and visitor infrastructure that will protect this park for generations to come.”

Tallgrass President and CEO Crystal Heter said the company is proud to strengthen the park's connection with the next generation.

The gift arrives during a period of change and challenge for Yellowstone. The Park Service estimates $1.2 billion in deferred maintenance and repairs at the park and Barclay Trimble just took over as Acting Superintendent of Yellowstone National Park. He replaced Cam Sholly, who left his post after eight years.

Tallgrass, a “multi-commodity infrastructure” company based in Colorado, operates more than 10,000 miles of pipelines. Early this year it received approval to build an AI data center south of Cheyenne. The $7 billion project is located on more than 1,000 acres in the Switchgrass industrial park south of Cheyenne.

The data center, which will span more than 700 acres, has faced pushback from rural neighbors in Laramie County who are concerned about lost views and potential water contamination.
