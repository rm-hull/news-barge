OpenAI recently introduced GPT-6, a new model of ChatGPT. You've probably heard about a lot of its new features but one that I don't think has had nearly enough attention, especially considering how much fun you can have with it, is Intelligent UI.

It allows ChatGPT to generate interactive experiences directly within a conversation. OpenAI explains: “With Intelligent UI, responses can include graphics, tappable buttons, forms, charts, and interactive experiences you can use directly in your conversation.”

What's interesting is there isn’t a single format. Which means, depending on what you ask, ChatGPT might create an interactive diagram, a calculator, a mood board, a map or even a little gaming console.

There are some genuinely useful applications here, from making complicated subjects easier to understand to building simple tools on the spot. But I was particularly interested in something a little less practical — games.

ChatGPT can now create games you can actually play within the chat window. And while there are all sorts of possibilities for educational games, quizzes and entirely new creations, I wanted to see what would happen if I asked it to recreate some old favorites.

So I started with five classic games, including *Snake*,* Pong*and* Tetris*. I gave ChatGPT simple prompts to see how faithfully it could recreate them. I then asked it to reinvent each game with new aesthetics, unexpected gameplay mechanics and a few deliberately chaotic twists. Think* Snake*in a neon cyberpunk world and* Tetris* with rules that change every time you clear a line.

Here's what happened when I put ChatGPT's new game-making abilities to the test.

## Snake gets a neon makeover

![A screenshot of the ChatGPT interface showing a snake game](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DjMgHJ9Z6QwfB7xDDUk83K-1200-80.png)

**The prompt:** *"Create a playable version of the classic Snake game directly inside this conversation."*

ChatGPT got the basics right straight away. The game was instantly recognizable, with responsive controls and even a slider to adjust the speed. It looked good and, importantly, it was actually fun to play for a couple of games.

I then asked it to give *Snake* a neon cyberpunk makeover, complete with moving obstacles, special power-ups and a speed increase every five points. Say hello to*Neon Snake.*

![A screenshot of the chatgpt interface after creating a neon game of snake](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/hFdmdAtfxgvyY8HwS2oCjX-1200-80.png)

There was one small hiccup. Instead of creating the new game directly in the chat window, ChatGPT initially gave me the code. Which wasn't much use when the whole point was to play it there and then.

I tried another prompt asking it if it could make it directly playable in the conversation and that worked. The results of that experiment were fun. The neon aesthetic looked good, everything was color-coded and the new mechanics gave the classic (and, let’s be honest, sometimes boring) game an extra layer of challenge.

## Tetris gets new rules

![A screenshot of the chatgpt interface showing a tetris game](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ZULrPBFKgPkjqcBZ7axjqg-1200-80.png)

**The prompt:** *"Create a playable game inspired by Tetris directly inside this conversation."*

This one worked really well from the start. I’d just assumed it would be a bit buggy and glitchy because there’s so much going on compared to *Snake*, but the controls were intuitive and responsive, and the classic block-stacking mechanic was all there.

But what if *Tetris* changed the rules every time you cleared a line? That was the idea behind my follow-up prompt.

![A screenshot of a new tetris game in the chatgpt interface](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/yv22cJErXHCXnhQckSD2o-1200-80.png)

I asked ChatGPT to introduce unexpected twists, which could include rotating the board to adding unusual block shapes or special powers that could destroy part of the grid.

And it delivered. The changing rules introduced a fun element of unpredictability to a game that's usually all about getting into a rhythm. I also came away thinking about how much further you could take this. There are so many opportunities to experiment with different rules, challenges and power-ups.

## Space Invaders becomes a First Contact situation

![A screenshot of the chatgpt interface showing a new take on the space invaders game](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/NCKXW9zLXnZXfLHfKdVZvC-1200-80.png)

**The prompt:** *"Build me a fully playable retro arcade game inspired by Space Invaders"*

Again, ChatGPT recreated the classic arcade experience surprisingly well. It looked and played like the original, with straightforward controls and all the fun of shooting at waves of approaching aliens.

But I wanted to see whether it could do something a bit more imaginative than adding extra enemies or making everything move faster. So I asked it to introduce a choice: what if I could defend Earth by fighting the aliens *or* negotiating with them?

![A screenshot of the chatpgt interface with a space invaders game in it.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/oz7pBRsUwZvJqHVxqABS9R-1200-80.png)

I wanted waves of pixel-art enemies, but also decisions between rounds that could unlock weapons, stop an invasion or make the next attack more dangerous.

ChatGPT called its reinvention *First Contact*, which I loved. Up until this point it was calling most of the games "chaos" or "chaotic" so this felt more inventive.

It was a fun way to introduce a new dimension to a familiar game. But did it work well? I don't know, I couldn't ever make it past the first level. This experiment really suffered with my poor alien-fighting abilities.

## Haunted Pac-Man

![A screenshot of a pacman-style game in chatgpt](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/WWhWmh5bn4LtsbYXoDrprE-1200-80.png)

**The prompt:** *"Create a fully interactive retro maze game inspired by Pac-Man that I can play directly inside this ChatGPT conversation."*

This was easily my favorite of the five, but I have always adored *Pac-Man*. ChatGPT's first attempt was a fun, responsive maze game with the familiar mechanics of moving around, collecting things and trying to avoid enemies. Bonus points for the fact it renamed it* Maze Muncher*.

For the reinvention, I decided to lean into Halloween. I asked it to turn the game into a haunted 1980s arcade, with me playing a little ghost hunter instead of Pac-Man.

![A screenshot of the chatgpt interface with a pacman-style game](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/zTNRkUAXGVLXYDngXDzPrM-1200-80.png)

I also wanted the maze itself to change. Every ten orbs collected would trigger a surprise, like shifting walls, new passages or an extra ghost. And I asked for temporary power-ups that would let me chase the ghosts for a change.

ChatGPT handled this well but didn't wait until I'd collected orbs. It was super fast-paced and there were new ghosts, secret passages and changing mechanics from the start. It was quick, easy to control and genuinely fun to play.

## Pong reinvented

![A screenshot of a pong-style game in the chatgpt interface](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Q2o5NF6CPKykkRGfnXkwWT-1200-80.png)

The prompt: Build me a fully playable version of Pong inside this conversation.

*Pong* might be the simplest game of the bunch, but ChatGPT made a really solid version. The controls were easy, the aesthetics were nice and it even offered easy, normal and hard difficulty settings.

I decided to ruin that simplicity and asked ChatGPT to create a more chaotic version where every point triggered a random modifier. The ball could split in two, the paddles could shrink, gravity could change or an obstacle could appear in the middle of the court.

The result definitely delivered on the chaos. The familiar back-and-forth gameplay was still there, but the changing rules made each rally a little less predictable. It was also a reminder of how much fun you can have with a simple idea. You don't need to reinvent every part of a game to make it entertaining.

![A screenshot of the chatgpt interface with a modified pong game](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/P5uYJjhVuf95ybUQpwbvBZ-1200-80.png)

I was genuinely impressed with how well all five games worked, especially considering how simple my original prompts were.

Yes, there were a few hiccups along the way, like ChatGPT deciding to give me code instead of a playable game a couple of times. But overall, the controls were responsive, the games looked good and the reinventions were more imaginative than I expected.

What I enjoyed most was how easy it was to experiment. You could take a game you already know, change the aesthetic, introduce an unexpected mechanic or come up with something completely ridiculous and see what ChatGPT made of it.

I can imagine this being a fun way to waste half an hour, especially if you loved these games growing up. Although, after all this experimenting, I do feel like I should remind you that the originals are still out there, and they're well worth revisiting too.
