## France will put soldiers on the streets ahead of more weekend protests, after days of violence and vandalism. Italy is giving police new powers, Albania's protests enter a second week, and Greek students march today. In Oslo, Steve Witkoff meets Zelenskyy before heading to Moscow.

## On today's show:

**Christophe Rouillon**, mayor of Coulaines, tells Euronews that curfews are bringing calm back to French streets, but that parents also bear a big responsibility.

Member of the EU Parliament **Fabrice Leggeri** (Patriots for Europe) speaks from Strasbourg, where far-right and far-left MEPs clashed over the French unrest. We ask him who is responsible, whether the army should be deployed, and how to tackle the root causes.

**Apostolos Staikos** reports from Athens, where teachers and students strike and rally today over the cost of living, housing and low wages, and whether the government is worried ahead of elections next year.

**Thomas Ossowski**, Germany’s ambassador to the EU, tells** Stefan Grobe** whether the EU is trying to build a budget it cannot afford, as Germany, its biggest contributor, sits at the centre of the fight.

**Sasha Vakulina** reports on the talks**Steve Witkoff** held with**Volodymyr Zelenskyy** and European leaders in Oslo, ahead of his visit to Russia today, and whether there is any sign of a breakthrough.

**Jozef Síkela**, EU Commissioner for International Partnerships, tells** Mared Gwyn** why the EU is unveiling a new Arctic strategy now, focusing on peace and security and pushing to keep oil, coal and gas in the ground.
