The French education minister said yesterday that 78 teachers and other education staff had been injured in the student protests so far, while in some cases, members of the public have also been caught in the fray.

But students have also accused police of brutality and using excessive, disproportionate force against them. Rights groups have also raised concerns over violent arrests.

Amnesty International said last week: “Images of high school students being subjected to violence by law enforcement are extremely concerning. Many of them are minors, children. They must be protected, not beaten with batons,” the human rights group wrote on X.

Along with this incident we’re hearing of in Lens this morning where a boy reportedly had their hand blown off by a police grenade, Amnesty last week reported that a 14-year-old had been hit by a grenade to the jaw where he lost several teeth, while other students had been arrested under chokehold techniques by police.

The French Human Rights League (LDH) on Monday also raised the alarm.

“We can see that in the instructions given to the police, there is no directive to show restraint, even though they are dealing with children,” LDH president Nathalie Tehio said.

A reminder that students are protesting en masse against the state of their schools – the overcrowded classes, run-down school buildings and shortage of teachers. The protests began on September 17 in the Parisian suburb of Creteil when students there joined their teachers who were striking over the lack of resources and staff.
