A real estate agent in California was arrested at LAX for allegedly spying for China, U.S. officials said.

Wanying “Heather” Zhang, 34, was detained at the Los Angeles airport just as she was leaving for China Sunday. The Californian is charged with acting as an unregistered foreign national.

The charge comes after Zhang travelled with an associate, identified as “Individual 1”, to Seattle in September 2025, according to a criminal complaint filed in federal court.

It is claimed that under the direction of Chinese officials, she conducted surveillance on a Taiwanese official’s relative, taking pictures and videos, and collecting vehicle plate details before passing it onto a handler.

![Zhang Wanying is a California realtor arrested for allegedly spying on behalf of China](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/05/8/30/Zhang-Wanying.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While the FBI didn’t name the alleged target, the *New York Post* reported it was Lai Ting-yu, son of Taiwanese president Lai Ching-te and an American resident.

“She coordinated the trip with an associate and used tradecraft to avoid detection,” First acting U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli said, adding she was due to appear before federal court in Los Angeles Monday.

The Taiwanese president’s son, an engineer in his 30s, lives with his wife and their U.S.-born young child. He works with the Dutch multinational electronics firm Philips.

According to the criminal complaint, security camera footage showed Zhang and Individual 1 waiting outside Lai’s home in a rented SUV.

![Zhang was detained just as she was about to board a flight to China, officials said](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/05/12/2026-10-05T030030Z_1088076430_RC2QWNAKAU5W_RTRMADP_3_USA-CHINA-AGENT.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When Lai or his family members arrived, they “quickly sat up within a black SUV, took out what appears to be their phones, and held their phones out in a manner indicating that they were taking pictures or videos of the family”.

"If the PRC wish to obtain leverage over the president of Taiwan during any potential conflict," FBI special agent Kevin Holt wrote in the complaint, filed on 3 October, referring to China by its formal name, People's Republic of China, “the location and pictures of the close relatives of a high-ranking Taiwanese government official, including pictures of their homes and cars, could be useful.”

Taiwan's presidential office described Zhang's actions as a “typical case of transnational repression” and thanked the U.S. government for protecting Taiwanese citizens.

![The realtor is accused of spying on the son of Taiwan president Lai Ching-te](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/05/12/2026-09-28T054722Z_1052634494_RC22SNAPWTJ5_RTRMADP_3_TAIWAN-DEFENCE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Our country expresses ​the strongest condemnation of such barbaric conduct that disregards human rights and the sovereignty of ​nations," spokesperson Karen Kuo said in a statement.

Zhang’s husband Edric Ding, also a realtor, told *CBS News* Sunday he did “not know anything yet”. It was not immediately clear if she had an attorney.

China has yet to comment on the arrest. The country is observing the weeklong National Day holiday, during which government offices remain shut.

Lai ⁠and his Democratic Progressive Party champion Taiwan's separate identity from China, a stance that angers Beijing, which sees the island as its sovereign territory. Lai says that the people of Taiwan alone can decide their future.
