Germany is at “danger ‌of becoming embroiled in a violent conflict with Russia” as Moscow intensifies an increasingly aggressive “shadow war” against European democracies, the head of the country’s foreign intelligence service has warned.

Martin Jaeger, head of Germany’s Bundesnachrichtendienst (BND), said the confrontation with Russia had entered a “new, more dangerous dimension”, as he addressed lawmakers at the annual public hearing of Germany’s intelligence chiefs on Monday.

“No other country currently poses such a direct, comprehensive and concrete threat to Germany,” Jaeger said.

“We did not seek this confrontation. Russia is forcing it upon us and sees us as a main adversary, because we refuse to accept its revisionist, expansionist policies and its war of aggression against Ukraine.”

Meanwhile, the Kremlin has threatened that Ukraine will “pay the price” after Volodymyr Zelensky said Kyiv would continue striking Russian oil refineries.

It came as German chancellor Friedrich Merz announced a further €1bn in military aid to Ukraine during a visit to Kyiv in a show of support for president Volodymyr Zelensky.

The military package includes long-range strike weapons, combat drones and satellite technology, "so that Ukraine can also target Russian launch pads," Merz said at a news conference.

## Estonia moves troops closer to border with Russia

Estonia is permanently relocating its troops closer to its border with Russia, according to a report, amid growing fears of a possible attack.

Officials told the *Financial Times* that Moscow is preparing for a potential conflict with the Baltic country - despite not wanting a wider war with Nato.

Colonel Tarmo Kundla, commander of the Estonian First Brigade, the country’s main fighting force, told the newspaper: “We have to start from the border and win from the first battles.”

Last week, the *Atlantic* reported that Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia are intensifying preparations for a potential Russian attack.

## Zelensky says Ukraine responded 'positively to negotiations'

Ukraine has responded positively to a US proposal to hold trilateral talks with Russia at a technical level by the end of October, Volodymyr Zelensky said.

Kyiv said the American negotiating team was currently in contact with both Ukraine and Russia over the proposed meeting.

“I spoke with the American negotiating team. Today, the American side is in contact with both Ukraine and Russia and is proposing a trilateral meeting at the technical level by the end of October,” Zelensky said.

“Yesterday, I confirmed to them that our side is ready to support holding such a meeting by the end of October. There is no new information here.”

Zelensky also signalled flexibility over where the talks could take place, saying it would be open to the United Arab Emirates or another country proposed by Washington.

“I know they will propose this to the Russian side as well, or may have already done so. We will wait for the response,” he said.

“We respond positively to negotiations.”

## India steps up Ukraine peace efforts as Ukraine calls proposal 'most comprehensive'

India has stepped up its involvement in efforts to end the war in Ukraine, holding talks with both Moscow and Kyiv on proposals ranging from protecting Black Sea shipping to halting attacks on energy infrastructure.

Foreign minister S Jaishankar said India had moved “beyond advocacy” in recent months and was now engaged in conversations with both Russia and Ukraine about practical steps towards reducing hostilities.

“We got a little bit more involved beyond advocacy in the last few months,” Jaishankar said on Sunday.

He said discussions had begun with the safety of shipping in the Black Sea before expanding to grain and energy exports and possible “non-attack” arrangements.

His comments come after Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha revealed that Kyiv was considering four separate ceasefire proposals, describing India’s as the “most comprehensive”.

“We have four proposals. The most comprehensive is India’s proposal,” Sybiha told reporters in Kyiv, adding that India had for the first time signalled a willingness to play a more active role.

The other initiatives have come from Turkey, Egypt and the US. Türkiye and Egypt have proposed a Black Sea truce aimed at facilitating Ukrainian grain exports, while Washington has pushed for an agreement to halt attacks on energy infrastructure.

Kyiv has said it is prepared to consider an energy and Black Sea truce, but insists the two must be combined.

“We are ready for an energy and Black Sea truce, but only in this combination,” Sybiha said. He said such an agreement would require a complete halt to attacks on energy facilities, including port infrastructure.
