A new singing project is bringing nursery children and care home residents together across Harrogate to help tackle loneliness.

Songbirds Music Harrogate has partnered with Harrogate Town Council to launch a series of sessions connecting different generations through music.

The programme, launched to mark the International Day of Older Persons on 1st October, gives children and older residents the chance to sing, move, share memories and enjoy musical activities together.

Children from Harcourt Nursery visited residents at Harcourt Gardens Care Home, while children from Performertots joined residents at The Granby Care Home.

The Mayor of Harrogate, Councillor Viv Poskitt, also attended the session at The Granby to celebrate the initiative.

Paul Forster, Events and Culture Lead at Harrogate Town Council, said:

> “We want to deliver a wide range of free events and activities that appeal to people of all ages and backgrounds across Harrogate.
>
> “This project is a great example of how we can create inclusive opportunities that bring different generations together, support wellbeing and ensure more residents can take part in positive community experiences, regardless of their circumstances.”

Songbirds is led by Frances Bryant, Sarah Lancaster and Lydia Buckle, who bring more than 20 years of combined experience of making music with children and families.

The team delivers singing sessions for children aged up to four and their parents and carers, alongside music sessions in nurseries, schools and community settings.

Lydia Buckle, Early Years Music Practitioner, said:

> “We are delighted to work in partnership with Harrogate Town Council in this new initiative promoting intergenerational music-making.
>
> “The project aims to foster community cohesion and help to tackle social isolation, whilst having a lot of fun!”

Participating care homes and nurseries hope to build ongoing relationships that continue beyond the initial sessions.

Familiar songs and shared musical activities are intended to spark memories, encourage participation and provide an enjoyable way for children and older residents to spend time together.

The project forms part of Harrogate Town Council’s wider efforts to promote community wellbeing, inclusion and social connection.
