**Realitea**, the Indian bistro and specialist tearoom based at 84 North Street, celebrated its twelfth anniversary this August. Founded in 2014 by directors Sarita and Paul McDermott, the venture has grown from a local coffee stop into a destination dining hub drawing regular clientele from Leeds, York, Harrogate, and international visitors from as far as New Zealand.

The business initially launched in Ripon by chance. Sarita explained: *“We were actually on our way to negotiate premises for a setup in Easingwold when we stopped off in Ripon for a break. When the Easingwold transaction subsequently fell through, our positive experience here made us refocus our commercial operations on Ripon instead.”*

## Overcoming Market Pressures

Launching a new business alongside navigating early parenthood presented significant operational challenges, but Sarita’s targeted menu design and premium tea selections quickly secured a dedicated regional following. That solid client base proved vital when the hospitality sector faced severe disruption during the 2020 pandemic.

> **Sarita said:**
>
> *Covid, combined with the continuously escalating overhead costs of doing business, have been our largest structural obstacles to date*.
>
> *However, being integrated into Ripon’s independent trading network has been a tremendous asset. Local business owners have routinely collaborated, cross-promoting services or sharing capital equipment and event assets during peak periods.*

## Cross-Industry Collaboration

Realitea’s current growth strategy relies heavily on cross-industry partnerships within the local economy. The bistro recently launched a series of joint evening events with local taproom **The Portly Pig**, providing curated picnic options directly to their patrons to drive dual-postcode footfall.

Beyond the restaurant floor, Sarita spearheads regional cultural initiatives, managing a Bollywood-style dance troupe that delivers community performances for Diwali across the Market Square, Ripon Library, and Spa Gardens. To support the upcoming winter menus, she is currently travelling to India to conduct culinary research and import authentic regional flavour profiles to the North Yorkshire network.

## Driving the Regional Economy

> Lilla Bathurst, Manager of the Ripon Business Improvement District (BID), emphasised the commercial impact of the venture on the city’s tourism profile:
>
> This moment of serendipity in 2014 led to Sarita establishing a fantastic, resilient business footprint on North Street. She stands as a prime example of the innovative business directors whose determination, vision, and commercial execution add considerably to what our city has to offer, making Ripon an exceptional place to invest, operate a business, and visit.
