A MAJOR railway station in West Yorkshire will be affected by four short closures before the long-running transformation of the facility concludes with a huge 38-day closure.

Huddersfield Station is being restored and remodelled as part of the Transpennine Route Upgrade (TRU).

A 16-day closure of the station has just concluded - and passengers are being warned of five further closures this year;

* Saturday 10 and Sunday 11 October 2026
* Saturday 14 and Sunday 15 November 2026
* Sunday 13 December 2026
* Sunday 20 December 2026
* A 38-day closure of Huddersfield station following the final service on Thursday 24 December 2026 until Sunday 31 January 2027, reopening on Monday 1 February 2027

The most recent batch of work took place between Saturday 19 September and Sunday 4 October, during which engineers worked around the clock to complete a series of major upgrades across the railway and at both Huddersfield and Deighton stations.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21539845.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the Transpennine Route Upgrade *(Image: TRU)*

During the 16-day programme of works, teams completed:

* Installation of two new lines of track through the Huddersfield area, including more than 2 km of new track, supporting future capacity improvements and enabling faster services to overtake stopping trains
* More than 1.3 km of track renewals and over 900 m of drainage renewals to improve the reliability of services
* Significant progress at Huddersfield station, including platform extensions, new restoration of the station's historic roof canopy and subway extensions
* Major upgrades at Deighton station, including work to extend the platforms and the installation of a new accessible footbridge which will have lifts once complete
* Installation of more than 50 overhead line equipment masts and over 50 steel structures to support future railway electrification
* Preparation works at A62 Leeds Road bridge ahead of its future reconstruction to accommodate railway electrification
* Signalling installation to improve railway performance and reliability

James Richarson, TRU Managing Director, said: “Completing this latest phase of work is down to the dedication, skill, and teamwork of our teams.

![Work on the Transpennine Route Upgrade](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21539846.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the Transpennine Route Upgrade at Deighton Station *(Image: TRU)*

From infrastructure delivery to planning and keeping passengers on the move during the disruption to their journeys, every part of the programme has played a role in this latest work.”

Liam O’Shaughnessy, TRU Programme Delivery Lead for Northern, said: “The work that has been completed over the last 16 days in the Huddersfield area is another crucial step towards delivering the benefits that the Transpennine Route Upgrade will bring for customers across the North.

“We have worked closely with our industry partners to keep customers on the move as these works have continued and we would like to thank our customers and local communities for their patience and cooperation whilst these improvement works have been taking place.”

![Work on the Transpennine Route Upgrade](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21539847.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the Transpennine Route Upgrade in the Hillhouse area of Huddersfield *(Image: TRU)*

Following this successful programme of works, attention will now turn to the next phase of delivery, including a 38-day closure of Huddersfield station starting after the final service on Thursday 24 December 2026 until Sunday 31 January 2027, reopening on Monday 1 February 2027, when engineers will carry out further transformative upgrades to the railway and station.

These upgrades form part of the wider multi-billion-pound TRU programme, which is transforming the railway between Manchester, Huddersfield, Leeds and York by improving capacity, reliability and accessibility while supporting the future introduction of cleaner electric trains.
