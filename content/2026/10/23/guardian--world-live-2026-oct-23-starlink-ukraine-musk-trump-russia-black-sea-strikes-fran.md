70,000 police officers and gendarmes to be deployed

The French interior minister says the government is mobilising 70,000 police officers and gendarmes- military officers who serve in the national police force – to city streets this weekend.

Riot squads and other elite, specialised units will be deployed. Even police trainees are being drafted in as support.

Laurent Nuñez said the government was aiming for an “extremely mobile, agile and effective” force. Some units will be stationed specifically outside government and public buildings as protection.

As I’ve noted earlier, several museums and tourist sites are closing this weekend – including Paris’s major museums the Louvre and Orsay – while shops and businesses are also boarding up.

Elite units like the GIGN – the National Gendarmerie Intervention Group- and RAID (Research, Assistance, Intervention and Deterrence) are being activated tomorrow in the protests.

These units more commonly specialise in counter-terrorism events, hostage rescue, protecting senior state officials.

Nuñez defended their involvement in policing these street demonstrations of students, workers and civil servants.

He noted calls on social media “from the far-left” to target ​public buildings.

"We are not talking about ⁠a peaceful, friendly demonstration,” Nunez ​told reporters. “We ​are ​ready tomorrow ​to be ‌extremely firm ​and ​not tolerate anything.“

Nuñez again condemned the vandalism and looting seen on the sidelines of protests last weekend – saying authorities would be “uncompromising” with “the vandals, with the looters and with all those who will commit atrocities”.

He also again repeated that some of this had been stoked by groups “on the far-left and radical extreme left” – but didn’t provide details.

“What happened on Octobe 17 is totally unacceptable. We will be fully in control to prevent these lootings, these attacks on public buildings” from occurring again, he said.

More than 200 people have already been jailed over last weekend’s violence.

French interior minister Laurent Nuñez is speaking now following a meeting of the Defence Council this morning, chaired by President Macron.

He begins by mentioning the shooting this morning in Trappes before going on to defending some of the blanket bans on demonstrations that have been issued in different regions across the country- saying each prefecture has the best idea of the security situation.

For example, until an hour ago, Marseille authorities had banned all demonstrations but the prefecture there has now allowed a trade union march to go ahead tomorrow.

Several local protest bans are also being challenged in last-minute court hearings today.

Nuñez will also be travelling to Orleans tonight to speak with law enforcement there ahead of the National Rally congress and counter-protests expected in the city tomorrow.

I’m going to track away from the French protests just slightly – although one of the demands of Parisian high schoolers is for air-conditioning and other basics in their classrooms – and highlight some shocking new numbers on just how many people died from the extreme heatwaves across Europe this summer.

People succumbed to heat at the highest rates inMalta, Italy and Hungary, while absolute death tolls were greatest in Italy, France, Spain, Germany and the UK.

The “staggering” death toll, modelled using historical relationships between heat and health in 854 cities, is the worst on record.

Scientists are blaming climate breakdown for two in every three of the deaths. The share of deaths due to climate breakdown has risen from about 40% at the start of the century to 65% this summer, the study found – leading to an extra 32,000 deaths.

Because death certificates rarely record contributing factors – such as dangerous temperatures that push a stroke victim or lung cancer patient past their limits – families rarely know that heat and human activity hastened their relatives’ death.

What protests are planned?
Both student groups and trade unions have planned rallies across the country. France’s ⁠main student union, UNEF, has called for demonstrations on Saturday and the Yellow Vest movement also filed plans to march through central Paris.

Marine Le Pen is also hosting the far-right National Rally congress in the central city Orléans, so we’re anticipating protests there with up to 30,000 counter-demonstrators expected.

What restrictions have kicked in?

There are already overnight curfews in place in Paris and many other cities.

In Marseille and Lyon, the second and third-largest cities, there is a general ban on demonstrations however some union groups have been authorised to hold marches.

The Marseille city government also initially said it would stop public transport running on Saturday; they’ve hastily reversed that severely unpopular decision this morning.

In Paris, protesters have been told they can only march a designated route between the Bastille and Nation squares.

Many shops and businesses, tourist sites and events are boarding up and closing for the weekend. Marseille’s business council advised shops to do so.

In Paris, the Louvre Museum will be shut while the Art Basel Paris art fair is also finishing up early.

What’s the government doing?

On top of the protest bans and restrictions in place, the government says they’ll deploy 20% more police onto the streets this weekend

Austria expels four more Russian diplomats suspected of spying

In Vienna this morning, Austria’s foreign ministry has announced it is kicking out another four Russian diplomats over “activities incompatible ⁠with their diplomatic status” – a euphemism usually for espionage.

Three of them worked at the Russian embassy while the other worked at the Organization ​for ​Security and Cooperation in Europe) in arms control, Die Presse is reporting.

They had all been monitored for some time by the Austrian security authorities.

You have to wonder how many Russian diplomats are left in Vienna – Europe’s espionage capital since the cold war, as my colleague Jon Henley puts it. Just a few months ago, Austria also expelled three Russian embassy staff on spying allegations, after a “forest of antennae” was detected at the Russian mission.

Austria has expelled now 18 Russian diplomats since 2020.

A war of words has erupted between the Trump administration, officials in Kyiv and Elon Musk over who is to blame for limiting Ukraine’s access to Space X’s military-grade version of Starlink.

The restrictions to Starshield have rendered many of the drones used by Kyiv to strike behind the frontlines inoperable and hampered air defence systems that stop Russian attacks, according to Ukrainian officials.

The US president said on Thursday that the service was running again at full speed, but a European official speaking to the Associated Press denied the service was fully functioning.
What has Trump said?

Trump initially denied any responsibility for the changes to Starshield, telling reporters last Friday: “I don’t know if it was anybody, but it wasn’t me, and I don’t think it was Elon,” referring to Musk, SpaceX’s CEO.

Trump’s cabinet backed him up, with secretary of state Marco Rubio saying on Wednesday: “What I can tell you with certainty is the president of the United States nor anyone in our system ever requested that any changes to that system be made.”

Rubio, however, also made a comment that threw suspicion on to Musk, the world’s richest man.

The president has nothing to do with the decisions that are made by Starlink and Starshield and by Mr Musk. That’s a private company. The president doesn’t run that company and can’t direct that company to do anything.

The Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday that the US president had asked Musk to restore the service, with Trump later telling reporters the system was “back”.

He refused to comment on whether he had spoken to Musk, but appeared to suggest the ultimate power to limit the service rested with the SpaceX CEO. “I guess the bottom line, he put it back,” Trump said, referring to Musk.

However, John Cornyn, a Republican who is in his final weeks as the US senator for Texas, told US media that he didn’t believe Musk would have acted “without at least the acquiescence of the White House”.

What about Musk?

Musk has supported Trump’s calls for Volodymyr Zelenskyy to be replaced and has been highly critical of the Ukrainian president. However, he was quick to blame the limits on Starshield on the Trump administration, writing on X last week that the systems were “owned and controlled by the US government”.

Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS.

Musk – one of the Republican party’s biggest donors and a Trump ally – continued to deny responsibility for the changes. However, on Tuesday the Wall Street Journal reported that Musk made the order to slow down Starshield, with the paper’s sources in disagreement over whether this was made in concert with Trump.

Vice-president JD Vance backed Musk on Thursday, saying the billionaire “doesn’t want to get in the middle of what is a war between two state actors without the United States government effectively directing him on what to do”.

So I don’t think this is about Elon. I think it’s fundamentally what is America’s policy in Ukraine and Russia going to be.

Amid the war of words, civilian casualties in Ukraine have surged, and the question over who is ultimately responsible for limiting Kyiv’s access has remained unanswered.
