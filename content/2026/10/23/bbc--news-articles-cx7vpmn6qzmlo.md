**Ukrainians held alongside freed US prisoner Stephen Hubbard have said they witnessed him being beaten and humiliated by Russian guards during his four-year detention.**

Dmytro Khyliuk first met Hubbard at the Novozybkov prison in Bryansk, not far from Russia's border with Ukraine, in September 2022.

"We heard it from the guards. They came during inspections and were asking: in which cell is the American? And then they used obscene words. They threatened him, they mocked him from the first days."

Ihor Shyshko also remembers Hubbard's arrival at the jail and says his nationality marked him out to the officers.

"It attracted such attention because they mocked him, forced him to learn the Russian language, sing the national anthem and repeat the rules of the prison schedule, which he could not read."

Hubbard was captured in April 2022, when Russian soldiers occupied the eastern city of Izyum, where he was living with his Ukrainian partner.

He was accused of serving in Ukraine's armed forces and convicted in a trial in Moscow before being sentenced to six years and 10 months. Shyshko and Khyliuk were held alongside Hubbard as he awaited trial.

![Ihor Shyshko looks directly into the camera. He is holding up and photos of himself pre-imprisonment in Russia to draw and contrast between his appearance then and now. He now looks older, thinner and has sustained some scars. His hair has turned grey. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/297a/live/a4044b50-ceea-11f1-bdca-23c64447299f.jpg)

Ihor Shyshko alleges that Hubbard was subjected to sexual humiliation and abuse for the entertainment of Russian prison staff

Shyshko said he witnessed and heard Hubbard being beaten by prison guards and electrocuted with a stun gun.

"They beat him on the legs, on the back, on the arms," he said. "Of course, they didn't give him food, they didn't give him medical care. They kept him in the cold.

"He was always treated more harshly, because he was an American. They claimed he was a mercenary, and said 'let's beat him'."

Shyshko, a captured Ukrainian soldier, alleges Hubbard was subjected to sexual humiliation and abuse for the entertainment of the prison staff.

Accounts from the two former Ukrainian prisoners corroborate statements made by Hubbard's family that he was tortured in prison.

The 74-year-old was released by the Kremlin after four-and-a-half years, on the day US special envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg to discuss the war in Ukraine.

Witkoff praised the Russian President as a "humanitarian", despite the ongoing full-scale invasion and daily Russian attacks across Ukraine.

US President Donald Trump said he had asked Putin to free Hubbard during a phone call the week before.

![Dmytro Khyliuk, 51, looks directly into the camera in this photo. He is wearing a yellow jumper. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/64a0/live/348d2de0-ceeb-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.png)

Dmytro Khyliuk says Hubbard was hidden in a Russian prison to conceal his condition from a team of International Red Cross visitors

Khyliuk, a journalist, was held in the earliest days of the invasion, when Russian troops advanced towards the capital Kyiv.

He said he knew Hubbard's health was declining over the months they were initially held in nearby cells because he heard his complaints and the guards calling him more often to see the prison doctor.

Khyliuk described broken windows and biting cold, with detainees using time out of their cells to huddle together in the bathroom for warmth.

"It was very difficult conditions in Novozybkov, worse than in the next prison. It was very cold there in winter, terribly cold," he said.

Both men encountered the retired English teacher again at their next prison, Pakino, where Shyshko says Hubbard's condition worsened.

"There was not a single spot on his knees or on his feet without rot," he said.

"Everything was rotten. We all had scabies. It was very, very difficult for him. I even bandaged him once at the medical centre, because the doctor didn't want to do it. He just couldn't do it himself, and I had to help. He was quite weak then and he almost couldn't move."

They detailed a harsh regime and a meagre diet of porridge, bread and potatoes, with a daily routine of beatings and humiliation, being forced to run or hold stress positions.

"There's a handler with a dog, who lets go so it can bite you on the buttocks, on the elbow, or bite your leg. Then these wounds fester very badly," Shyshko said.

Khyliuk claimed that soon after their transfer to Pakino, Hubbard was hidden to conceal his condition from a team of International Red Cross visitors.

"I would like to say that an international humanitarian organisation that receives a lot of funding could do better. They saw only what was shown to them and they were happy with that," he said.

The International Committee of the Red Cross said in a statement that "in any situation where the ICRC has been granted access, we request to see every individual and assess the conditions they are held in. We continue our discussions with parties to the conflict on this issue."

Khyliuk said Hubbard disappeared from the prison in 2024 and he thought he must have been released. Khyliuk was returned to Ukraine in August 2025 in a prisoner exchange.

He likened their treatment to the regime of Soviet leader Joseph Stalin.

"Russian captivity is a torture machine. From the first second of your detention, they do everything to suppress you, to intimidate you. This is torture in all its manifestations, from psychological to physical. Beatings, hunger, cold, lack of any information, lack of the opportunity to communicate even with other prisoners".

Khyliuk said he was pleased to see Hubbard finally released and heading to the US.

"I understand what it is like to be in terrible captivity. Moreover, it was not his war. He ended up in the wrong place and at the wrong time," he said.

But he described the timing as "shameful PR of the US president, who is losing votes in elections to Congress".

Shyshko said he hoped one day their torturers would face justice.

"Evil must be punished, and so it will be. I have hope for that".
