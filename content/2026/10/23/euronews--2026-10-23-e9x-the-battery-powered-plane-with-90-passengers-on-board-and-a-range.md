## Flying without kerosene is no longer just a dream, with pilots being trained to fly electric planes in the Netherlands. Before long, these battery-powered planes could be carrying 90 passengers and flying distances of up to 1,000 kilometres.

Johannes, Peter and Floris are thrilled. The pilots are carrying out the first test flights for the scaled E9X demonstrator. It’s a world premiere — and if all goes to plan, their electric model plane could soon become a big passenger aircraft.

After a soft landing, the aircraft is still in one piece — and now the real adventure can begin: analysing the flight data in the Elysian Aircraft laboratory, which sits beside Amsterdam’s Schiphol Airport.

Over the next two decades, the global number of air passengers could double. This is bad news for the global climate, so the young Dutch start-up Elysian is working on a solution.

## The world’s first battery-powered passenger aircraft

Daniel Jacobson is one of the founders of Elysian Aircraft. With ten million euros in start-up capital, his team now aims to develop one of the world’s first big, battery-powered passenger aircrafts.

“The design pretty much stands”, Jacobson tells Euronews in his workshop. “There will be 6 engines. And we have folding wingtips, so that we fit into the current gates’ infrastructure of the airports, because the wings are very large. We are talking about more than 50 metres.”

## Battery-packed wings

Jacobson shows Euronews the first full-scale model of the innovative wing. The battery packs are integrated into the wing’s structure. To give a better idea of the scale, there are roughly 40 battery packs across the entire wing, each pack weighing around 1000 kg.

“Yes, we are an obese plane”, the Elysian manager admits. Yet he smiles confidently: “We are always on a diet, trying to reduce weight. How can you use different materials, how can you combine things, how you do thermo-management. And it’s also IP (intellectual property) that we are generating here.” There are no further details — it’s top secret at this stage.

Battery-wings — a revolutionary idea, and the key to success. The co-founder and co-CEO of Elysian Aircraft sounds rather proud: “There was really this consensus in the academic world and in the industry that flying electrically with a large aircraft would never be possible. We have disproven that with our research on our design.”

## Reducing global aerospace emissions

But what are the benefits? “By being able to transport 90 passengers for initially 800 kilometres and eventually 1000 kilometres you can reduce emissions to zero, from half the world’s flights”, Jacobson says. “That is a very large amount of flight activity in the world. And you reduce total emissions from aerospace by 20%.”

The E9X is due to be operational by 2034. Europe is doing rather well in the race for the future of aviation, Jacobson believes. “But comparing it to the US or even China, we should fund start-ups and innovation much more aggressively when we want to keep our advantage”, the top manager urges with regard to the EU.

## Pilots prepare for electric take-off

We stop at the small regional airport of Teuge. Here, flying on electricity is already a reality. The E-Flight Academy trains pilots from several airlines in an environmentally friendly way at a low altitude.

Co-founder Merlijn van Vliet takes Euronews on a ride in a small 2-seater: “In this aircraft we have two batteries with 11 kilowatt-hours each. So, in total we have 22 kilowatt-hours on board. We can fly 45 to 50 minutes with them and still have ten minutes of reserve capacity.”

We follow the banks of a river through green meadows where Dutch cows are grazing. “We are now flying towards the old city centre of Deventer. They don’t hear us. So, we can fly wherever we want and they don’t hear us on the ground”, Merlijn beams.

What is his vision for the future? “In ten years, this will grow big and we will fly electric with 40 or 60 passengers — or even more.”

## Charging infrastructure

Jurjen de Jong has made a lot of money from charging stations for electric cars and is one of Europe’s pioneers in the field. He is now investing his profits into the development of a European charging infrastructure for electric aircraft.

“We will expand from the Netherlands to the Nordic States, to France, to Germany and Switzerland”, the co-founder of NRG2fly tells Euronews. “And then we will have a fully-fledged network of low-emission, low-noise electric mobility and that is really what we’re going for”.

Airports are surrounded by huge car parks. Solar panels, tens of thousands of electric cars and stationary energy storage systems could be connected to form a network — “the inter-net of energy”, as Jurjen calls it.

Teuge airport is testing the scaled concept. “We have 500 solar panels on the rooftop”, Jurjen explains, “their energy is stored in a battery. So basically, we have a whole energy hub which is providing energy instead of taking energy from the grid.”

NRG2fly co-founder Jurjen de Jong has a broader vision too: “My dream is to have all regional electric planes powered by the sun. And then we can leave a better world for me, my children and everybody.”
