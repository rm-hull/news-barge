Britain and France carried out their first joint exercise involving France's airborne nuclear deterrent, France's armed forces ministry said in a statement on Friday.

British Typhoon jets joined in a nuclear exercise alongside French Rafale warplanes on Tuesday evening, French armed forces minister Catherine Vautrin said.

“Having independent nuclear forces does not preclude coordination,” she said, adding: “Our nuclear cooperation with the United Kingdom continues to deepen.”

Ms Vautrin said the exercise showed the “coordination and commitment” of the two European nuclear powers “in support of Europe’s security and credible deterrence”.

![Europe's nuclear powers took part in joint drills earlier in the week, Ms Vautrin said](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/23/10/21/HVTp3ueWgAAYn9I.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The two countries last summer agreed to reinforce cooperation over their respective nuclear arsenals in response to growing threats to Europe and amid uncertainty over their US ally.

US president Donald Trump's criticism of the Nato military alliance and his overtures to Russia have led to concerns in Europe about Washington’s commitment to helping defend its European allies.

Europe's primary nuclear deterrence comes from the United States and is a decades-old symbol of trans-Atlantic solidarity.

France and Britain represent the two remaining nuclear powers in Europe outside of Russia, after Ukraine gave up its weapons in 1994 in return for security assurances from the US, UK and Russia.

The French defence ministry said earlier in October that it had carried out the first test launch of its latest-generation M51.3 submarine-launched ballistic missile, underscoring its nuclear deterrent.

![Vautrin said the exercise showed the coordination and commitment of the two European nuclear powers in support of Europe's security and credible deterrence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/23/11/40/HVTp3ufXsAAFtVA.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The missile, which was not equipped with a nuclear warhead, was launched off the coast of Brittany as part of an operation to certify the nuclear-submarine Le Vigilant's ability to deploy the weapon following a major maintenance overhaul, the ministry said in a statement.

It said the launch was conducted without a payload and in strict compliance with international obligations.

The announcement made no mention of Russia but came amid heightened tensions days after president Vladimir Putin warned the West not to threaten the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad, which Moscow has said it would defend with nuclear weapons if necessary.

Nato has said none of its “exercises or activities pose a threat to the territory of the Russian Federation”.

Putin also said Russia had no plans to attack any European country and wanted to coexist peacefully.
