A council is considering new powers to address the use of face coverings during criminal or anti-social behaviour.

South Tyneside Council is launching a public consultation on proposals that would give authorised officers the authority to require the removal of face coverings, such as balaclavas, if they are being used to conceal identity during the commission of a crime or anti-social acts.

The measure would form part of a Public Spaces Protection Order (PSPO), but would not apply to face coverings worn for legitimate religious, health, or work reasons.

Councillor Karen Pittuck, lead member for highways, neighbourhoods and community safety, said: "We want everyone to feel safe and confident in our towns, villages and public spaces.

"This proposal is focused on specific situations where a face covering may be used to conceal someone’s identity while committing crime or anti-social behaviour, or causing intimidation or distress.

"It is important to be absolutely clear that this is not a proposal to ban face coverings.

"We want to hear a broad range of views, including any concerns people may have about how the proposed power could be used or enforced."

The consultation will seek public opinion on whether the new power is needed and if it could help improve safety and confidence in public spaces.

The consultation launches on Monday 26 October and closes at 5pm on Sunday 22 November 2026.

Northumbria Police has expressed support for the conversation.

Chief Superintendent Darren Adams, the force’s anti-social behaviour lead, said: "We recognise face coverings being used to conceal someone's identity to commit crime, take part in anti-social behaviour or cause fear and intimidation is a concern for our communities."

The consultation is open to anyone who lives in, works in, or visits South Tyneside.
