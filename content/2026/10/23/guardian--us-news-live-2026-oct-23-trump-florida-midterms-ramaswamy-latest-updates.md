Justice department files preliminary lawsuit to challenge results of Alaska Senate race

The Department of Justice has filed a lawsuit in federal court against Alaska’s and its election division, to block the certification of the state’s US Senate result, claiming there could be “voter confusion” about the two Dan Sullivans on the ballot, which might influence the race’s outcome in favor of the Democratic nominee, Mary Peltola.

A reminder, the Republican incumbent Dan S Sullivan will be competing against Peltola and another candidate named Daniel J Sullivan, a retired schoolteacher also running as a Republican.

The incumbent senator will appear on the 3 November ballot in Alaska with the words “(Registered Republican) Incumbent” next to his name to make it clearer for voters.

Since the Last Frontier uses ranked-choice voting in their elections, Alaskans can rank up to five candidates. However, Trump has routinely disparaged the voting method, calling it a “scam” and encouraging voters to support a ballot measure in the state to repeal it.

Pivoting back to the lawsuit filed by the Department of Justice against the state of Alaska.

The federal government argues in the complaint that “the sole purpose” of Dan J Sullivan’s of candidacy is to “benefit the Peltola campaign by reducing the number of votes for Senator Sullivan”.

As a result, “there is a substantial likelihood that a certification of the election will reflect an electoral process that lacked integrity”, it writes.

The administration is calling for there to be a pause in certifying the vote in Alaska “pending an investigation” to determine whether votes that are cast for Dan J Sullivan were actually meant to be for the Republican incumbent.

Voting rights experts say, however, the basis for the lawsuit is thin given the fact that any meaningful probe into voters’ preferences after they have voted would be practically impossible to conduct.

In June, the Alaska supreme court ruled that the Dan J Sullivan could remain on the ballot, despite the frustration it has caused the Trump administration and national Republicans hoping that the incumbent Sullivan can win against Peltola, a moderate Democrat, in their bitter fight for a Senate seat that could deliver Democrats a key victory in their path for control of the upper chamber of Congress.

A reminder that Acton, the public health official who is leading Ramaswamy in polls, was removed from her mother’s care when she was 12, after her stepfather was charged and indicted for sexually abusing Acton. Her mother, Donna Arthur, fled the state with her husband, before his trial, and remained married to him until his death in 2004.

While several Republicans have disavowed the ad, where Arthur calls her daughter “a liar”, and urged Ramaswamy’s campaign to take it down, Trump has encouraged the opposite. “This is a person, a mother, a smart woman,” Trump added. “This has nothing to do with the stepfather or anything else … the mother of a person running for governor of one of the greatest states, where I set records, said that she’s a liar. Her daughter’s a liar.”

If Democratic candidate Mary Peltola were to win the election next month, she would be the first Democrat to win the seat in nearly two decades.

Majority of voters say they will trust midterm results, according to new poll

Most likely voters say they will trust the results of the midterm elections, according to the latest poll from the New York Times and Siena University.

While 74% of those surveyed said they would have confidence in the outcome, 23% said they would not.

Notably, 50% of voters said that that the Democratic party is “too far to the left”, while 51% feel that the GOP is “too far to the right”.

Meanwhile, the latest polling also shows that a majority (53%) are not confident that Republican candidates will accept the results of the midterm elections if they lose. This is compared to 28% of those who feel the same about Democrats’ response to the results.

Almost half of those surveyed said that they were worried about the possibility of Republicans trying to overturn the results if they lose. Only 31% had the same fear about Democrats.
