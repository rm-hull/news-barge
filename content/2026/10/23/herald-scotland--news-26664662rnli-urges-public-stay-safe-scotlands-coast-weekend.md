The Royal National Lifeboat Institution (RNLI) has issued a reminder to those along the coast to be aware of the dangers as Storms Austen and Boelo are expected to bring strong winds and heavy rain across Scotland this weekend.

The Met Office has issued an amber weather warning for much of Scotland, with widespread gusts of 60 to 70mph forecast and the potential for gusts of 80mph in exposed coastal locations.

The amber alert warns of potential injuries and danger to life from flying debris as well as damage to buildings and trees, travel disruption and power cuts.

The RNLI is encouraging people to exercise extreme caution if near the shoreline, especially along exposed cliffs, seafronts and piers.

Michael Avril, RNLI Regional Water Safety Lead, said: "Storm Austen and Storm Boelo are set to bring potentially dangerous conditions so we’re asking the public to stay vigilant and not take unnecessary risks by travelling to coastal locations. If you are near to the coast or a waterside location, we urge you stay a safe distance from the water as conditions could knock you off your feet or wash you into the sea.

"If you find yourself in trouble in the water, remember to Float to Live: Lie back in the water with your head so far back that your ears are wet, extend your arms and legs and try to relax as best as you can until you get control of your breathing before then calling for help or swimming to safety.

"If you see someone else in danger – do not enter the water yourself as you could also end up in serious difficulty. Instead, remember phone, float, throw – phone 999 or 112 and ask for the Coastguard; tell the struggling person to float; throw them something buoyant, like a life ring."
