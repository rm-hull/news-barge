The Bradford Bulls pathway has undergone significant development since November 2022, when key founder Ryan Hunkin became the club's head of youth.

And Bulls have delivered an update on its progress since it began four years ago.

Notably, it has evolved from a predominantly coaching-led structure into a comprehensive player development programme.

Supported by the club and Bullbuilder members, the programme has expanded its staffing from 14 to 42 listed roles, introducing specialist expertise to support players on and off the field.

The pathway now benefits from dedicated leadership across reserves, academy, scholarship, college rugby and player transition, highlighted by Jonathan Schofield’s appointment to a first-team role this month.

Talent identification has also expanded significantly, with the scouting network growing from three to 12 scouts, strengthening the club’s ability to identify emerging talent and create opportunities for local players.

Beyond expert coaching, physical performance and medical provision, the programme now offers nutrition, sports psychology, performance analysis and wellbeing, alongside internship opportunities across several disciplines.

The pathway connects community rugby league with Talent ID, scholarship, academy, reserves and the first team, providing a framework for players to develop and progress.

By investing in coaching, specialist support and talent identification, Bulls are strengthening the opportunities available to young players aspiring to represent the club at senior level.

The likes of Jamie Gill, Alfie Leake and Harvey Ward made their Super League debuts this year, while Riley Gelder has just been handed a professional contract aged 17.

Hunkin said: “When I came into the role in November 2022, we had a solid foundation to build on.”

“Growing from 14 to 42 listed roles shows the commitment to creating a stronger environment for player development.

“Coaching remains at the heart of what we do, but nutrition, physical performance, psychology, analysis and wellbeing are equally important in developing the complete player and preparing them for the demands of the game.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21612735.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alfie Leake has come through the Bulls' pathway and began to get regular game time towards the end of the Super League season. *(Image: Ed Sykes/SWpix.com)*

“The progress we’ve made reflects the commitment of the club, our staff, volunteers and Bullbuilder.

“Their support helps us build a better environment for young players and invest in the future of Bradford Bulls.

“Our job is to identify talent, develop players and give them the best opportunity to progress towards the first team.

“Seeing young players take that next step into the senior environment makes all the hard work worthwhile.

“We’re proud of how far we’ve come, but there’s still more to do.

“Our ambition is to keep raising standards and create a pathway where young players are supported, challenged and given the opportunity to represent Bradford Bulls at the highest level."
