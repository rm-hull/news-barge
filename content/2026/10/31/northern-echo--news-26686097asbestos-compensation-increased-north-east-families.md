Bereaved families across the North East are set to receive thousands of pounds more in compensation following a major change to the way victims of asbestos-related diseases are supported.

The UK Government has ended a system that paid reduced compensation to families of workers who died from asbestos exposure before making a claim.

Now, families will receive the full amount their loved one would have been entitled to.

Sir Stephen Timms, Minister for Social Security and Disability, said: "Losing a loved one to an asbestos-related disease is devastating.

"Families should not then face a financial penalty because of that loss.

"From today, bereaved families in North East England will receive the full compensation their loved ones would have been entitled to, ending a longstanding unfairness in the system.

"This is a long-overdue change that will provide greater support for families at a difficult time."

In 2025, 160 compensation awards were made in the region under government schemes for diseases like mesothelioma and pneumoconiosis.

Previously, if a worker died before making a claim, their dependants received around £10,700—about £7,000 less than what the worker would have received.

For a 60-year-old claimant, a dependant could now receive £56,656, compared to £24,526.

Sir Stephen said: "This is a long-overdue change that will provide greater support for families at a difficult time."

It follows decades of campaigning by groups such as the Asbestos Victims Support Group Forum UK and parliamentarians.

UK Government Minister for Scotland Melanie Ward said: "For far too long, the families of workers who have suffered from the devastating effects of asbestos and dust-related diseases have been short-changed by outdated compensation rules.

"I'm glad that from today, the UK Government is putting an end to this injustice—including for victims in Scotland—by scrapping reduced payments for dependants.

"Across the UK, this long-awaited reform will increase compensation payments for grieving families.

"I pay tribute to the campaigners who have fought for many years to make this change happen."

The new legislation is expected to cost between £2 million and £6 million per year.

Jane Lassey, director of regulation at the Health and Safety Executive (HSE), said: "Asbestos-related diseases continue to cause great suffering, and the effects of past exposure are still being felt by workers and their families.

"This change means bereaved families will receive the support their loved ones would have been entitled to.
