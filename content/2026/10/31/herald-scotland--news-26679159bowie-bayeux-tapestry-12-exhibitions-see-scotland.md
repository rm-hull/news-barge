## **David Bowie: On Tour**

### **V&A Dundee, Dundee, November 4 - February 15, 2027**

You possibly haven’t heard much about this wee exhibition that’s opening up in Dundee next week. There’s not been much fuss made about it, has there? Still, if you do fancy a quick swatch at around 100 items taken from the personal archive of one of the greatest artists of the late 20th century, then the V&A in Dundee may be able to help. We’re promised photographs, hand-written lyrics, annotated film scripts, Bowie’s own guitar and more.

Here’s your chance to venerate his relics and wish the man to whom they belonged to was still with us.

## **Harold Offeh: The Mothership Collective 2:0**

### **Tramway, Glasgow, until February 7**

And, post-Bowie (or pre-Bowie for that matter), if you want some more hazy cosmic jive, you could always check out the current exhibition at the Tramway in Glasgow. Co-commissioned with the Baltic in Gateshead, The Mothership Collective 2.0 sees artist Harold Offeh explore Afrofuturist ideas in this hands-on show which combines sound, text and objects to explore different visions of tomorrow. One for all you Starmen (and Starwomen and Starchildren).

## **Stanley Cursiter: Artist and Collector**

### **The National, Edinburgh, until December 6**

With the wealth of art on display at The National in Princes Street it would be easy to overlook this current display of the work of the National Galleries of Scotland’s last artist-director, Stanley Cursiter.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21643418.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Regatta, Stanley Cursiter, 1913. National Galleries of Scotland *(Image: Estate of Stanley Cursiter)*

The Orkney-born painter, who died 50 years ago in 1976, was in charge from 1930 to 1948. He famously painted miniature versions of paintings in the collection, some of which are on show here. But his own work had a brio and a swagger to it, too.

His 1913 painting The Regatta - inspired by the Italian Futurists - is a glorious thing.

## **Ade Adesina: Bard**

### **City Art Centre, Edinburgh, November 7 - April 11, 2027**

The work of printmaker Ade Adesina is full of beautiful delight and dangerous shadows. The Aberdeen-based artist draws on the classical traditions of landscape and still lifes, but infuses them with contemporary concerns, addressing climate change and colonialism. The resulting images are complex and beguiling.

## **Dear Earth**

### **St Andrews Museum, St Andrews, until March 6, 2027**

Ade Adesina’s work is also on display in St Andrews at the moment as part of an ongoing group show examining the impact of human activity on the health of the planet.

Featuring the work of artists Helen O’Brien, Jeni Reid, Jenny Pope, Naomi Mcintosh, Scott Hunger, Susie Johnston, Will Carey, Adesina and Zoe Hamill, the exhibition gathers up photography, prints, ceramics, found objects and installation work in a bid to both serve as a warning and maybe offer some reassurance.

## **Duncan Shanks: The Unseen Journey**

### **Hunterian Art Gallery, Glasgow, October 30 - May 2, 2027**

This new exhibition at the Hunterian provides a rare opportunity to examine the artistic process of one of Scotland’s most celebrated landscape artists. Drawing on a decade of sketchbooks, exploratory studies and finished works, this is an insight into the creative practices of the Airdrie-born artist.

It takes in the journey from sketchbook to canvas as Shanks seeks inspiration in the landscape of the Clyde Valley. The finished work hovers on the edge of abstraction and pulses with colour and light.

## **Bayeux on the Balcony**

### **Dovecot Studios, Edinburgh, November 21 - April 9, 2027**

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21643410.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bayeux on the Balcony *(Image: Dovecot)*

Listen, you could hustle and hassle to get a ticket to see the real thing at the British Museum and try and catch a glimpse as you battle your way through the crowds trying to do the same, or you could take a trip to the Dovecot’s balcony gallery to examine a full-sized print that will cover the length and breadth of the space.

True, you will not be in the presence of the historical artefact, but at least you will be able to examine its details without getting an elbow in your stomach. There will be additional interpretation from the Dovecot’s own weavers too.

## **Beyond the Bedspread: Sheena Norquay**

### **Inverness Museum and Art Gallery, Inverness, until December 5**

And if textiles float your boat then can I direct you towards Sheena Norquay’s richly detailed quilts on show in Inverness until the start of December?

Inspired by the coastlines and landscapes of Orkney and a smattering of Norse mythology, the punchy colours, textures and intricate details of Norquay’s quilts are well worth seeing in the thread, as it were.

## **Emily Sutton: Animals Everywhere**

### **The Scottish Gallery, Edinburgh, until November 21**

You can see the influence of Edward Bawden, Eric Ravilious and mid-twentieth-century children’s picture books on the work of Yorkshire-born Edinburgh College of Art graduate Emily Sutton.

But her paintings and embroideries are totally Suttonesque, as it were. They are, as the title suggests, full of fauna. But there’s plenty of flora too. This should be joyous.

## **Michael Agnew: A Real Kind of Fiction**

### **Burgh Halls, Linlithgow, until January 24, 2027**

More animals. The work of Aberdeen-based artist Michael Agnew RSA is all dense, dark detail and layered imagery that draws (ahem) on mythology, folklore, folk art, print ephemera and collective memory. Expect owls. Expect a lot of great owls.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21643427.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Postcards from the Line with Helen Acklam, 'Near Lunna' *(Image: Helen Acklam)*

## **Postcards from the Line**

### **The Line Gallery, Linlithgow, until November 22**

And while you’re in the town pop into the compact Line Gallery on the High Street for its annual exhibition of postcard-sized art. There are around 300 works to be seen, submitted as part of an open invitation.

Look out for the work of Anna Divers and Jade Stout in particular.

## **Time + Space**

### **The Collective Gallery, Edinburgh, until December 20**

This is the Collective Gallery’s first group show, bringing together four new-generation Scottish-based artists - Hetal Chudusama, Abi Lewis, Gianni Esporas and Huss Al-Chokhdar - who are all exploring ritual, myth and memory in their work for this show.

The exhibition contains painting, film, sculptures and site-specific installations.
