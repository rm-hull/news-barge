Community heroes from across a North East city will be recognised for their contribution to the people and places around them as an awards ceremony returns for its seventh year.

The Open Arms Awards, organised by Voluntary and Community Action Sunderland (VCAS), will take place at Sunderland Minster.

The event honours individuals, volunteers, trustees and organisations that strengthen Sunderland’s communities.

Janette Hilton, chief officer of VCAS, said: "The Open Arms Awards celebrate the people and organisations who make an extraordinary difference in our communities every day.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650844.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sunderland’s Open Arms Awards return for a seventh year at Sunderland Minster, recognising community heroes, volunteers and organisations across the city *(Image: Supplied)*

"Many work quietly behind the scenes, supporting others without seeking recognition, and these awards provide an opportunity for the sector to come together and celebrate their achievements."

This year’s ceremony will feature five new Community Champion Awards, sponsored by Sunderland City Council, honouring people who have made a significant impact in their neighbourhoods.

Fourteen organisations from the public, private and voluntary sectors are supporting the event, including Gentoo, Ben & Jerry’s, Groundwork North East, Sunderland BID, Sunderland Culture and Northern Powergrid.

Categories include health and wellbeing, community development, volunteering, partnership working, culture, sport and environmental action.

Ms Hilton said: "The voluntary and community sector plays a vital role in supporting residents, reducing inequalities, and strengthening communities across Sunderland.

"The awards highlight the incredible impact of our volunteers, trustees, community leaders, and organisations and recognise the difference they make to people's lives."

VCAS has supported Sunderland’s voluntary and community sector since 1973.

Students at Sunderland's Sandhill View Academy contributed 663 hours of voluntary service through the Duke of Edinburgh's Award over the previous year.
