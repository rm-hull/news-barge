Bradford Council has reminded residents that lampposts do not contain "surveillance equipment" after a number of street lights were cut down on the city's largest housing estate.

The damaged lampposts were reported last weekend near Holme Wood Road and Broadstone Way, prompting an emergency response from the council.

When the T&A visited the area earlier this week, the remains of one lamppost could still be seen, wrapped in blue tape.

Bradford Council said the site had since been made safe and an investigation into the vandalism was ongoing.

Councillor Andrew Judson (Keighley West, Reform UK), Bradford Council's Executive Member for Economy, Regeneration and Planning, said: "We are aware of the incident involving several street lighting columns that have been cut down in Holme Wood.

![Councillor Andrew Mark Judson Reform UK for the Keighley West ward](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20981511.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Councillor Andrew Mark Judson *(Image: T&A)*

"An emergency response crew attended the site to make the affected columns safe, and the incident is being investigated.

"The council is assessing the damage and will repair or replace the affected infrastructure as soon as possible."

## Councillor addresses social media speculation

Cllr Judson also addressed social media speculation surrounding the purpose of the lampposts.

He said: "The columns are fitted with smart lighting control nodes as part of the council's Smart Street Lighting Project.

"These devices allow remote monitoring and management of the street lighting network.

"The council is aware of social media speculation about the technology and would like to reassure residents that the nodes do not contain cameras, microphones or any form of surveillance equipment.

"Their sole purpose is to support the operation and maintenance of the street lighting system."

He also thanked residents for their patience while investigations and repairs continue.

## Vandalism has made the area 'less safe'

When asked about the incident and the council response, Councillor Ian McInnes (Green Party, Holme Wood & Bierley) said: "I’m glad the council acted quickly to make the damaged lampposts safe.

"These lampposts did not have cameras, microphones or any kind of surveillance equipment in them.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21282526.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Newsquest)*

"They are there to light our streets and keep people safe.

“Whoever cut them down has made parts of Holme Wood darker and less safe, just as the nights are drawing in."

## Previous incidents reported

The incident follows other cases of vandalism linked to surveillance equipment in Holme Wood.

In April 2025, the T&A reported that CCTV cameras installed by housing association Incommunities on Stoneyhurst Square were destroyed "within hours" of installation.

A similar incident occurred in March 2018 when some residents threatened council workers installing a surveillance camera on Broadstone Way.

Less than 12 hours later, a man used an angle grinder to cut down the pole.

On the latest incident, Cllr McInnes added: "Residents regularly tell Councillors Matt Edwards, Celia Hickson and me that they worry about crime, especially burglary and theft.

"This mindless vandalism will only add to that worry.

“If anyone has information, please contact West Yorkshire Police on 101, or Crimestoppers anonymously on 0800 555 111.”
