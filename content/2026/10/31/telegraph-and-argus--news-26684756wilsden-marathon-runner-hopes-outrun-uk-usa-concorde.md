THERE were plenty of interesting sports stories in the T&A on this day in 1983.

Bradford Northern had been spooked by a knee injury to star hooker Brian Noble, which looked set to rule him out of a key cup clash against Widnes, as well as putting his hopes of leading Great Britain's Under-24s out against France the following week in doubt.

And Bantams fans must have been feeling a little fearful, knowing they had competition to bring back their beloved striker Bobby Campbell to Valley Parade from his ill-fated spell at Derby County.

Campbell did choose a City return over Sheffield United after all and his goalscoring achievements in claret and amber speak for themselves.

But another (nearly) remarkable feat was also covered in the T&A on this day in 1983.

Married Wilsden distance runners, David and Joyce Johnson, had just taken part in the New York Marathon together.

But for husband David, he had a particular goal in mind.

David ran the event looking to beat the flight time of the Concorde he and his wife had taken to New York from London.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21651270.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Concorde first took off in 1969, with its final flight coming in 2003. *(Image: PA)*

Their trip to the Big Apple had been funded by the TNT shipping company, who employed David at their Birkenshaw depot.

The express flight took just three hours and 21 minutes to cover the 3,456 miles between Heathrow and JFK.

David fancied his chances, but as was so often the case with Concorde, few could compete with its speed.

Despite completing his fastest-ever marathon in wretched New York weather, David's finishing time of three hours and 28 minutes was not quite enough to outdo the supersonic airliner.
