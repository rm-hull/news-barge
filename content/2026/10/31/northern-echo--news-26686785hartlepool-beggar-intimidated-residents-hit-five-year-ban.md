An aggressive beggar who left people feeling "intimidated and fearful" has been banned from Hartlepool town centre.

Glen Moore targeted elderly residents, vulnerable individuals, and young female workers across the town, repeatedly engaging in threatening behaviour over many months.

A civil injunction was granted at Darlington County Court under the Anti-social Behaviour, Crime and Policing Act 2014 following evidence of persistent intimidation and abuse.

Councillor Amanda Napper, chair of Hartlepool Borough Council’s neighbourhoods and regulatory services committee, said: "This injunction sends a clear message that aggressive begging, intimidation, and threats of violence will not be tolerated in Hartlepool.

"Businesses, workers, and residents have the right to go about their daily lives free from fear, harassment, and abuse.

"We have worked closely with our partners to address this individual's behaviour through a range of interventions over a significant period of time.

"Unfortunately, those efforts have failed to bring about a change in behaviour, making court action necessary."

The injunction prohibits Moore from entering a designated exclusion zone covering the town centre and surrounding areas, except for pre-arranged appointments relating to healthcare, pharmacy services, emergency services or support services.

He is also banned from using or encouraging others to use abusive, insulting, or threatening language or behaviour; begging for money or other items; and refusing to leave publicly accessible premises when requested by authorised staff or security personnel.

A power of arrest has been attached to the order, which remains in force until October 23, 2031.

Moore continued his behaviour despite interventions including a Community Protection Warning issued in August 2025, multiple police attendances, arrests, and existing banning orders.

He was repeatedly reported for aggressive begging at locations including Asda on Marina Way, Mecca Bingo, McDonald's on Burn Road, Morrisons, Tesco, Marks & Spencer, and Middleton Grange Shopping Centre.

Concerns were raised about his daily presence at Asda since around March 2025, where he approached customers for money and refused to leave when asked.

Elderly customers at Mecca Bingo also reported feeling unsafe due to his regular presence outside the venue late in the evening.

Businesses across Hartlepool reported verbal abuse, intimidation of staff, and non-compliance with requests to leave their premises.

The council worked closely with Cleveland Police as part of the Hartlepool Community Safety Team to secure the injunction, with support from security staff at McDonald’s, Mecca Bingo, and Asda.

Anyone who witnesses it being breached should call Cleveland Police on 101 or 999 in an emergency.

A breach of a civil injunction by an adult constitutes contempt of court, and may result in imprisonment for up to two years, an unlimited fine or both.
