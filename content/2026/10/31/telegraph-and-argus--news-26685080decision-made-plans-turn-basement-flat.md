Councillors have voted that the basement of a terraced house is not suitable living accommodation.

An application to create a new basement flat at 27 Blenheim Road in Manningham was refused by Bradford Council Planning Panel at their last meeting after a debate over whether the basement would provide suitable living conditions.

A similar plan for the property was refused last year, with a subsequent appeal dismissed by a planning inspector, who said the basement flat would not provide “an acceptable standard of living” for future residents.

## What did the latest application say?

Earlier this year, Sonam Ali submitted a new application, that they claimed dealt with many of the previous reasons for refusal.

The plan included the replacement of a door, added to the front of the basement without planning permission around 2020, with a front bay window.

One of the previous reasons for refusal would be the lack of natural light entering the property.

The application claimed enough light would reach the apartment, but this would rely on the new front bay windows, a large lightwell and the glazed door installed.

## Why it was refused

Planners said this would be harmful to the St Paul’s Conservation Area, as it would require “digging out” of the property’s yard.

The report to members said: “The plans claim that the bay window would be a historical restoration, however there is no evidence to indicate that a bay window to the frontage of the basement was an original feature of the property.

"The provision of high-quality housing should consider the impact on the occupants themselves, and a dwelling that fails to deliver acceptable levels of natural light and outlook would be detrimental to the experience, and potentially wellbeing, of any occupant."

The planning panel were advised to refuse the application based on the impact of the works to the conservation area, the poor levels of natural light to the flat, and the lack of any outlook.

## Councillor wanted plan approved

At the meeting Councillor Muhammad Haseeb Khan (Manningham, Bradford Independents) spoke on behalf of the applicant, saying: “An independent assessment demonstrates that this will provide acceptable living conditions for future occupants.”

He said the works would “enhance, rather than harm” the building, as it would lead to the replacement of the unauthorised door.

Planning officer Andrew Moxon replied: “There shouldn’t be a door in the first instance.

“It is for members to decide whether the basement of a terraced house is suitable as accommodation.”

## How the panel voted

Agent Waqar Hussain pointed out that the lower floor did have some outlook, adding: “It is not fully below the ground.”

Mr Moxon replied “From the pavement you will be able to see through the entirety of the living space.

“The way to improve things would be to block off the door to return it to what it was before, rather than dig it out more.

“Anyone living here is going to have a very low level of light.”

Six members of the panel voted to refuse the plans, four voted against officers’ recommendations and one abstained.
