A Bradford mum has spoken out about the dangers of e-scooters after her 12-year-old son was seriously injured in a crash.

Gemma Moffatt, of Thackley, contacted the Telegraph & Argus after Tommie Tilley and another boy were injured when their e-scooter crashed into a parked car on Thornacre Road, Wrose, on Sunday, October 18.

Ms Moffatt said: “Tommie suffered a six-inch-deep laceration to his chest, below his rib cage.

“It was so deep, but it didn’t reach his organs.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653832.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The scar of Tommie Tilley's wound which he suffered in an e-scooter crash in Wrose

“He had a CT scan in hospital which confirmed it stopped short of his organs.”

A Yorkshire Ambulance Service spokesperson said: “We received an emergency call at 2.52pm on Sunday afternoon reporting a collision between an e-scooter and a parked car on Thornacre Road, Wrose.

"Two ambulances, a specialist paramedic (critical care) and a team leader were dispatched to the scene and one patient was conveyed to Leeds General Infirmary and one patient to Bradford Royal Infirmary."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653829.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ambulances were called to the scene after two boys were injured in an e-scooter crash on Thornacre Road, Wrose

Tommie is now recovering at home, and Ms Moffatt told the T&A: “He’s getting better and moving about a lot more.

Explaining how the crash happened, she said: “Tommie is not allowed an e-scooter of his own but he got on his friend’s electric scooter.

“They were going down a hill when they lost control and went into the front of a parked car.

“Tommie got a piece of scooter which was sticking out in front of the handlebars stuck into his chest, and his face hit the bonnet breaking his nose.

“I believe his friend suffered a concussion.

“I have no idea how fast they were going but it’s not a busy road.

“Tommie could have lost his life just doing something that a lot of kids do.

“I nearly lost my 12-year-old boy. His wound was horrendous.

“It’s the scariest thing I’ve ever experienced.

“I don’t want any other parents to go through what I did.

“He’s not done anything wrong, and I want to make it clear that not every kid on an e-scooter has a balaclava on.”

Ms Moffatt said she wanted to warn other parents about the dangers of e-scooters.

Ms Moffatt said: “I’d like to raise awareness for parents not to buy e-scooters for their children. They’re so dangerous.

“A lot of parents just don’t realise how dangerous they are.”

She also described the crash’s impact on Tommie.

“It’s not just physical, but psychological.

“Tommie is very cautious now.

“He doesn’t even want to go out on his bike.

“He feels like he’s reliving the crash.

“I think it’s important to tell his story to raise awareness.

“If it helps just a few people it makes all the difference.

“I hope it stops another family from having to go through the trauma.”
