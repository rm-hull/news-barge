# 'No banks, post office and full of empty units' - the Yorkshire suburb staggering along with diabolical parking

## Locals have spoken out

A area of Yorkshire full of empty units and charity shops is “staggering along with diabolical parking” say the locals.

Leeds City Council is launching a scheme to improve all 33 wards in the city called ‘Love Leeds’. It aims to bring each community together to target issues facing each ward.

Moortown has been chosen as the fourth ward in the scheme which aims to harness “community and volunteer action”. Examples of possible improvements could be intensive environmental clean-ups, weeding, cleaning, and graffiti removal.

The scheme also aims to help with improving highways, removing rubbish, work to tackle and prevent fly-tipping, clearing and cleaning community spaces, housing support, and health support and advice such as pop-up blood pressure checks.

The programme will follow a rolling operating model, starting with engagement and consultation with local groups, stakeholders and residents in a ward to listen, understand and identify priorities for improvement.

Retired sales manager Pam Thompson, 76, says there’s lots of “unsightly bins” by her estate in Moor Allerton that are brimming with rubbish which comes at odds with the “pristine” neighbouring area of Alwoodley. Speaking about Moortown Corner, Pam said: “It’s a shame there are so many second hand shops. There’s a lot of coffee shops now, they’re pleasant, I like to see those. I don’t like to see empty shops.”

Pensioner Moira, 72, is also concerned about the shopping area. She said: “There’s a lack of banks and post offices. You’ve got to look at the demographic. You do get a lot of elderly people, here, because of the housing. You need to cater for those and it seems as if there’s a lot of cafes and nail bars springing up but they’re catering for the younger end. Where are there businesses, look at it! Empty, empty! It isn’t buzzing at all. It’s staggering along.

“People would come just to use the bank, they look and the banks have gone along here and they won’t come again.”

Cathy Macpherson, has been the manager at LJWB Charity Shop for 10 years, thinks there “definitely needs to be more restrictions for parking on Moortown shopping parade” so more people can pop by and support the local economy.

Cathy explained: “The parking around the shops is actually diabolical. There’s nowhere for customers to park which means our footfall over the years has just gotten worse. Then, because some of the cafes have opened, they’ve taken some of the parking space for seats and benches. Some drivers park here all day and get a bus into town.”

Cathey had positives to mentioing, adding the area has changed in the past ten years with more families and more “diverse cultures” and it’s “opened right up”. She said the cafes and restaurants are “really nice” and top quality.

Nursery practitioner Amy Bennison, 24, has worked in the area for two years and is dismayed with the level of flytipping at Moortown Park. Amy said: “The park could do with a bit of upkeep. At the minute, there’s been a lot of flytipping. Especially where the nursery meets the park. People are cutting hedges and dumping it in massive piles in the park, there’s lots of rubbish, like pots and things. It’s not nice to see. The kids see it as well, so it’s not setting a good example.”

The Love Leeds scheme will be harnessing a range of methods to engage with the community. These include resident drop-ins and workshops, pop-up listening and engagement sessions and local volunteer and third sector groups. The aim is to provide local opportunities for people to have a stronger voice in how the area in which they live is looked after and improved.

Launching the programme, Leeds City Council executive member for environment Councillor James Gibson said: “We are delighted to be getting ‘Love Leeds’ underway, as it will bring services, community groups and residents together in a more integrated way than ever before to identify what people want to see and feel improved in their area, and to quickly get on and deliver it.

“The tagline ‘cleaner streets, stronger communities’ reflects the aim to not only make a difference residents will be able to recognise, but to strengthen relationships at all levels to provide a sustainable new way of working in localities across Leeds, with everyone pulling together to make things better. Let’s get started.”

Council services directly involved include Housing Leeds, parks and greenspace, highways, Leeds Anti-Social Behaviour Team (LASBT), public health, Active Leeds, community hubs, waste management, jobs and skills and volunteering. Partners include West Yorkshire Police and third sector organisations.
