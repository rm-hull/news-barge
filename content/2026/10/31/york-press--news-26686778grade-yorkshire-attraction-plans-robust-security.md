A "security breach" and customers entering without paying has prompted plans to improve protections at a popular Yorkshire

East Riding of Yorkshire Council is planning to install metal palisade fencing at Sewerby Hall and Gardens just outside Bridlington after existing wire mesh was cut and removed from a site access road.

Infill panels added to a gate across the road which links the estate to nearby Bridlington Links Golf Course are also part of the plans.

The Grade I-listed country house with 50 acres of parkland, award-winning gardens, zoo and clifftop views attracts more than 150,000 visitors a year.

The site is also popular with dog walkers, offering scenic views along the East Yorkshire coast.

Papers submitted with the planning application said gates on the access road were installed in response to an increase in visitors accessing Sewerby Hall and Gardens without paying for admission, together with people being on the road without permission, especially in the evenings.

![Free entry is now available to the grounds and gardens at Sewerby Hall and Gardens](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21601783.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Free entry om most days is now available to the grounds and gardens at Sewerby Hall and Gardens until late March, East Riding of Yorkshire Council said *(Image: East Riding of Yorkshire Council)*

A section of existing fencing was deliberately cut back using tools and vegetation was also damaged, which prompted plans to install more prominent 1.8metre tall metal palisade railings, according to the plans, which the council said would "provide a more robust deterrent against unauthorised access and vandalism, reducing the risk of further breaches."

Landscaping of the boundary was not considered due to ongoing issues with members of the public damaging hedgerows whilst attempting to gain access to Sewerby Hall, the documents said.

The area is not part of the visitor route and is used by staff and service vehicles, however the access road runs alongside, and the new palisade fencing would be put up on the other side of the walled garden.
