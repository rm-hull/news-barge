**Proposals to demolish part of Summerhall have been branded ‘insulting’ by artists working at the creative hub.**

Earlier this month, The Herald revealed property firm AMA’s ambitious plans for the complex, which would retain its status as an ‘arts quarter’, alongside a hotel and residential accommodation.

Developers have unveiled proposals to bulldoze several of the buildings on site, including the 1970s-era block on Summerhall Square and the so-called ‘cranked wing’ extension on Hope Park Terrace.

They argue that the poor condition of the buildings, which once housed Edinburgh University's Royal Dick Veterinary School, necessitates demolition.

Several public consultation events have been held ahead of an official planning application being lodged earlier next year.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21592318.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The buidings shaded in red would be demolished under plans for the future of Summerhall. *(Image: AMA Summerhall Consultation)*

However, residents at the complex told The Herald they were unimpressed with the level of engagement from the developers so far.

"If AMA had come to us and said: 'We know there's a creative community here. How do you envision a future where these two things could coexist?’ that would have been a good start,” designer Andy McGregor, who described the plans as a “failure of imagination”, explained.

“They just kind of seem to have run fairly roughshod over it and used a sort of cut and paste version from some kind of photo library of what artist studios and artist practice is all about."

Award-winning animator Iain Gardner, a longtime resident of Summerhall, added: "I also spoke to the developers and their idea of what an artist studio is falls significantly short of what is required.

“One of the architects I spoke to seemed to think that the best solution was to have a kind of row of glass-fronted studios for tourists still looking on. It's a patronising view of what art is.

“We're not part of Edinburgh's Disneyland.

"I challenged the architect. I said: 'You would not want me coming into your studio, nicking your ideas, and giving them to your competitors. We're actually doing serious work here.'"

![The unique anatomy lecture theatre could be lost, Sam Gough warned.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21568870.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Summerhall is home to a range of unique venues, including the Anatomy Room. *(Image: Summerhall Arts)*

According to one iteration of the plans, a “reimagined arts centre” would be retained within the complex’s main building on Summerhall Crescent, while a hotel would be built on Hope Park Terrace.

“A revived arts centre would include galleries, performance spaces and studio space,” the developers explained.

“Many of the ‘grand rooms’ in the main building would seem to be a perfect fit for these functions, while other spaces in the complex may be more suitable for artists’ studios and related business workspaces.”

The arts centre would “occupy parts of the main building”, while the exterior courtyard would be enhanced for year-round use.

However, documentary filmmaker Alice Nelson questioned AMA’s framing.

She told The Herald: "When they talk about the art centre, they're talking about a tiny fraction of the site. It's just the building at the front.

"AMA just seems to have completely ignored the fact that there's a little town of people making here.

"The way it's been described by AMA is really quite insulting. We seem to be an absolute afterthought on AMA's part.

“When they talk about an art centre, I think really all they're thinking about is a venue."

Photographer Peter Dibdin added: "They haven't had any dialogue with us.

“As everybody keeps on saying, this place needs investment and if they want to be investing in the future of arts in Edinburgh and in Scotland, then we should be part of the conversation.

“Until now we haven't been, until they set up the consultation downstairs. That was the first moment we had to meet them."

![Dr Ali Afshar, managing director of AMA Limited.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21592260.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dr Ali Afshar is the managing director of AMA Limited. *(Image: Supplied)*

A spokesperson for AMA said that the firm was “absolutely committed” to ensuring that arts remain at Summerhall.

"We know from our many conversations with the people who make Summerhall what it is just how important it is to Edinburgh's creative community,” they said.

"Our commitment to retaining and growing the arts provision at Summerhall is fundamental to our plans. Our current proposals would provide at least 17% more arts space than is currently available and used across the site, reflecting the importance we place on securing a strong future for the arts at Summerhall.”

The spokesperson said that decisions about how individual spaces are “allocated, managed and priced” would be taken by future leaseholders, not AMA.

“However, we want to ensure that the needs and realities of Summerhall's creative community are understood and recognised as the plans progress,” they added, saying that buildings are in need of “significant investment and repair”.

“This must go hand in hand with understanding and protecting the spirit of Summerhall: the creativity, independence and community that make it such a special place,” the spokesperson said.

"This is why we chose to open two consultation periods, giving people opportunities to engage with the proposals and help shape their development.

“The first consultation period closed last week, and all feedback received is being carefully considered ahead of our second consultation event next month.

"We are committed to working with the people who know and love Summerhall to create a future that celebrates its past, supports its creative community and allows the arts to continue to thrive here for generations to come."
