What did everyday life in York really look like during the 1940s?

We’ve delved into The Press archives to uncover 10 fascinating photographs from that decade.

Pickering’s recent 1940s Weekend attracted crowds of visitors and demonstrated the enduring popularity of stepping back in time so it seems timely to look back at life in York during that era.

From wartime York to the changing post-war city, these remarkable images capture people, places and moments of resilience, community, and change.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20085216.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1949 - Huge crowds eagerly await the royal visit. *(Image: The Press)*

* From footballers training at York City FC to crowds gathering for a royal visit in 1949, the images reflect a city shaped by global events but grounded in local spirit.

One striking photo from 1941 shows soldiers gathered at York Public Library, a reminder of the city's strategic importance during World War II. Another captures young women from the 1920 class lining up at York Employment Exchange to register for National Service, highlighting the vital role women played in the war effort.

Coney Street, a bustling hub then as now, features prominently. A 1941 image shows Lieutenant General Sir Ronald Adam, GOC Northern Command, taking the salute - a moment of military pride. Meanwhile, Leak & Thorpe, a well-known department store of the era, stands as a symbol of York’s commercial heritage.

The floods of 1947 also make an appearance, with dramatic scenes of waterlogged streets reminding us of the challenges faced by residents in the post-war years.

These evocative photographs are part of our weekly nostalgia supplement, available every Wednesday in The Press.
