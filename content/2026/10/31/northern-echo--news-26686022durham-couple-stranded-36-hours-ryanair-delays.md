A Durham couple were left stranded at an airport for more than 36 hours after their Ryanair flight was repeatedly delayed, missing out on two days of their holiday.

Edward Scott, 55, and his wife were due to fly from Leeds Bradford Airport to Warsaw at 6.05am on Sunday, October 25, but did not arrive in Poland until almost midnight the following day.

The couple, who were travelling to visit their family, had arrived at the airport at 4am, but their holiday quickly descended into chaos as strong winds disrupted flights.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653139.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lots of people were stranded in Leeds Airport *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Edward said: "We sat on the plane for three hours. I asked the stewardess if I could have some drinks, or refreshments under the EU directive, and she went, no, you have to pay for it."

He said passengers were eventually told they would not be taking off and had to return to the terminal.

"We purchased our own drinks and subsistence," he added.

The couple later received a £3.50 food voucher from Ryanair, which Edward said was barely enough to cover a bottle of water.

But after spending hours waiting at the airport, passengers were left confused when they were directed to another gate at about 5pm.

Believing they were finally about to board, the couple were left bewildered when they found themselves outside the terminal.

Edward said: “We went to gate 10 and spoke to a lady from Ryanair. We said ‘we’ve come for the flight to Warsaw.’ She told us to go down the corridor, so we did, and it took us outside the airport.

“We went back through the main entrance and spoke to a security guard. He basically said Ryanair would probably be in touch by text in the next 20 or 30 minutes to let me know what was happening next.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653140.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flights cancelled or delayed *(Image: PROVIDED)*

Passengers were initially told the airline was trying to arrange hotels, but Edward said he later received a message saying it had been unable to find accommodation and they would have to make their own arrangements.

The couple drove around for half an hour to find somewhere reasonably priced to stay, after being told their replacement flight would leave at 7.30am on Monday.

But by the following morning, the departure time had changed again.

Edward said: “I woke up the next morning, headed to the airport and then got a message saying, ‘Your flight’s been moved from 7am to 12 o’clock.’”

The departure time was repeatedly pushed back, first to about 4.20pm and eventually to 6.20pm.

Edward said the lack of information on the second day was particularly frustrating, with passengers left watching the departure boards and trying to work out what was happening.

He said: "There was no communication throughout the course of the day. There was no understanding of what was happening, there was no understanding actually whether we were going to fly out or not."

He tried contacting Ryanair through its online chat service but said the response was far from reassuring.

Edward said: “He said, ‘I know as much as you. I can’t tell you what’s going on because there’s nothing on my screen.’ He basically said, ‘On that note, I’m going to end this conversation,’ which I thought was quite abrupt.”

He also called the airline to complain about the lack of information but said he was promised someone would look into it and get back to him.

"They never did," he said.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653141.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flights cancelled or delayed *(Image: PROVIDED)*

He believes the airline should have been better prepared for the second day's disruption, particularly as the weather conditions had improved.

Edward said he spoke to a member of cabin crew on the eventual flight who told him she had been brought over from Manchester as a standby worker.

He questioned whether the airline's staffing arrangements had contributed to the prolonged delays.

He said: “I’m not blaming Ryanair entirely for what’s going on. On the first day, Ryanair and the airports were struggling because of the weather conditions, which is understandable. But by the second day, it seemed like it was foreseeable.”

The couple were travelling to Warsaw to spend time with Edward's wife's parents and had booked activities and theatre tickets which they missed out on.

The disruption also meant Edward arrived in Poland on his birthday.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653137.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lots of people were stranded in Leeds Airport *(Image: PROVIDED)*

He said the couple were so exhausted after the journey that they struggled to enjoy the holiday they had planned.

“By the time we got to Warsaw and then reached the hotel, it must have been quarter to 12 at night. We got up the next day but felt so drained. It had completely wiped us out, both physically and mentally.

“We basically missed two whole days of our holiday and, with my wife being a teacher, we couldn’t move the trip around at all.

"It is disappointing and has put a dampener on it all."

Edward has since submitted a claim to Ryanair for the cost of their hotel and food, but his separate claim for flight delay compensation was rejected.

The airline cited unforeseeable events linked to the previous day's weather conditions.

He believes passengers should still receive proper care and assistance when flights are disrupted, regardless of the cause.

Edward added: "This was not simply an inconvenience. We lost valuable time from our trip, incurred additional expenses and experienced considerable stress and uncertainty."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653138.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Flights cancelled or delayed *(Image: PROVIDED)*

He added: "Passengers should not have to argue for basic refreshments, accommodation, information or the rights they are already entitled to under the law."

And he warned: "I will certainly think twice before using Ryanair again."

A spokesperson for Ryanair said: *“This flight from Leeds Bradford to Warsaw Chopin (25 Oct) was delayed overnight due to strong winds as a result of Storm Boelo, which affected all airlines operating at Leeds Bradford Airport.*

*Passengers booked on this flight were notified of the delay via email, SMS and push notifications. Due to limited availability, passengers were advised that they could arrange their own accommodation and submit receipted expenses for reimbursement via* * Ryanair.com**. They were also offered the option to change their flight free of charge or apply for a refund.*

*This flight departed for Warsaw Chopin the following day (26 Oct) at 18:45pm local time.”*
