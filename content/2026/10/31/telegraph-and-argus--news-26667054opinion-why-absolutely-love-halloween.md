SUMMER is over, the leaves are changing colour and the weather at times somewhat irregular - Autumn is here.

I love to drop the T-shirts and trainers for flannel jumpers and comfy boots, order the hot drinks with fancy flavours and head off with cameras looking for that ‘perfect leaf shot’ before it gets too wet and muddy.

Before the weather gets a little too windy and wet, I always look forward to the end of October which celebrates the secular customs of Halloween. Its origins are over 2,000 years old, from the ancient Celtic festival of Samhain in Ireland and Britain where the Celtic people believed that the boundary between the living and the dead weakened and ghosts were allowed to roam the earth for one night. People dressed up in animal skins to confuse ghosts and offer food to the spirits.

This later broadened when the Christian church dedicated November 1 as All Saints Day (All Hallows) and November 2 as All Soul’s Day to honour saints and the dead. The night before however became known as All Hallows Eve and eventually Halloween. Today’s Halloween came from Irish and Scottish settlers who brought their traditions to North America in the 19th century and it spread in popularity.

I recently listened to a segment on the Jeremy Vine radio show which discussed people’s thoughts on Halloween and it was an interesting listen. There are those who view Halloween as the commercialisation of death, and some believe it has taken over Bonfire night as the autumnal event.

Respectfully, I like to think of myself as someone who tries to see more positives in these things as I was once a grave digger and this gave me a clearer perspective on life as I witnessed more families celebrate their loved ones in a happy manner than those who mourned silently. So for me, Halloween is a fun way to bring people together during a dark and cold time of year where for the most part we work hard, pay taxes and look forward to a two day weekend. Why not embrace every single chance given to live outside the box and be imaginative? Halloween is something that all cultures and creeds can enjoy together.

Besides, ‘Remember Remember does not fall on October 31, so don’t get ahead because one night of fun can become two nights instead.

As a child I would love dressing up and going out with my family on Halloween night, knocking on decorated doors and being greeted by people all dressed up handing out sweets for our goodie bags. It was exciting to see so many getting into the spirit which gave me such fond memories. In later years me and my brother would host Halloween parties at home with our friends and they were encouraged to dress up in costume. We would also go out on the town with like-minded people. When we eventually had families of our own we kept the traditions alive with them and they love it the same!

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21626511.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mark Thompson in a creepy costumer *(Image: Mark Thompson)*

The social fun of dressing up with others is wonderful but there are other parts of Halloween that I enjoy too. The movies, TV and music are brilliant. From scary screamers to the fun family genre, Halloween on the box can be a cosy and enjoyable experience. I’m a fan of Tim Burton, I love his whimsical surrealism and dark, gothic humour. Other notable mentions are the Ghostbusters, Beetlejuice, The Nightmare Before Christmas, Halloween, Hocus Pocus, American Horror Story, Wolfman and Dracula and of course The Addams Family (click click) to name but a few. I don’t seem to tire of them every year, even a little!

Decorations play a huge part in our life during October. From light up spooky candles in the garden to a tree arch filled with cobwebs, bats, lights and ghoulish trinkets in the living-room to hanging spiders in the light fixings, it’s all in great fun! The lights create a mystical atmosphere during the evening. We have created a miniature Halloween town on our bookshelf and their illuminations make me happy.

In North America they really go above and beyond with Halloween, from giant outdoor theme parks to scare houses and entire neighbourhoods taking part in trick or treating. I absolutely love it and I think that we are adopting some of the best bits for here at home.

It might seem strange to others that this time of year makes me feel warm and happy inside. This may be a combination of factors including the changing of the seasons (more colourful trees) and seasonal events (with spiced beverages) but perhaps it also comes from many years of wonderful memories made and experiences I had as a child.

Whatever the reasons may be, I am surely grateful that we get a seasonal event that not only embraces the joy of make-believe and dressing up but also gets people into the spirit of keeping old traditions, albeit modernised, alive and exciting.
