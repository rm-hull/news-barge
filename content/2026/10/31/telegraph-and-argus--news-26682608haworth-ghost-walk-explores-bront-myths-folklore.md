A ghost walk with a literary twist will explore the haunted moors that inspired the Brontë sisters.

The guided walk, taking place in Haworth on Sunday, November 1 from 11am to 1pm, will explore the folklore, hauntings and industrial history behind some of Yorkshire’s best-known literary works.

Led by poet and Penistone Hill artist-in-residence Kristina Diprose, the event is titled 'What Haunts the Moor?' and will take participants from Haworth to the Penistone Hill trig point.

### 'A ghost walk with a twist'

Ms Diprose described the event as a "A ghost walk with a twist.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21648439.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Penistone Hill artist-in-residence, poet Kristina Diprose on a moor *(Image: Jed Skinner)*

"We'll be going on a short walk from Haworth up to the Penistone Hill trig point, exploring what haunts the landscape from bog lore, its industrial past, and from the archives of the Brontë Parsonage Research Library."

The walk will explore stories of bog bodies, ghostly black dogs, forgotten industries, and the enduring mysteries of the moorland that helped shape the imagination of the Brontë sisters.

The event is inspired by Ms Diprose’s audio trail, How to Talk to the Moor, which was created during her residency on Penistone Hill.

Ms Diprose said: "I've been artist in residence on Penistone Hill from August to October and have loved learning about the hidden history of this iconic landscape.

### How the moors inspired the Brontës

She said "It offers a chance to share some of the stories and facts I've been inspired by in my writing, and to get to know Brontë country as you've perhaps never seen it before."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/17255060.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Branwell's portrait of the Brontes, with his face painted out. *(Image: Martin Greenwood)*

The walk will also touch on the moor’s literary legacy, including references to the supernatural black dogs of Yorkshire folklore, known as the barghest and gytrash.

Ms Diprose said: "I love how Charlotte Brontë weaves Yorkshire gytrash folklore into Jane Eyre, using the black dog myth to give Jane's first encounter with Mr Rochester a sinister edge of foreboding."

### 'Why these places are worth celebrating'

In addition to ghost stories and folklore, the walk will highlight the environmental importance of the Haworth moors as a carbon store and nature reserve.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20394830.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Penistone Hill artist-in-residence, poet Kristina Diprose on a moor *(Image: Bradford Council)*

Ms Diprose said: "The Haworth moors aren't just a historical or a literary curiosity – they're a vital carbon store and habitat, including for threatened species of ground nesting birds like the curlew.

"Art is one of the ways that we can shine a spotlight on why these places are worth celebrating, exploring and cherishing for future generations."

The two-mile walk is expected to take around two hours.

It is free to attend, but booking is required.

Walkers will meet outside The Old School Room in Haworth.
