A Durham prisoner who “reeked of violence” left his cellmate covered in blood when he went “well over the top” over his snoring.

David McLaughlin carried out a sustained assault on the cellmate at HMP Durham on the morning of February 27, 2024, at a time both were on remand at the prison.

McLaughlin, who has a history of violent offending, launched the attack after taking exception to the other inmate's loud snoring, Durham Crown Court heard.

Prosecutor Martin Towers said the victim was left covered in blood with wounds to his right forehead, the back of his head, behind his ear and chin, as well as mouth injuries caused by his own tooth.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647798.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham Prison inmate David McLaughlin's violent reaction to his cellmate's snoring was 'well over the top', court told

He also suffered a broken arm that needed surgery and two suspected fractured ribs.

In a victim statement, he described it as, “the worst experience of my life”, and said he had been left badly scarred.

He said he had suffered PTSD, nightmares and was in pain for several months, and also was threatened by other inmates for being “a grass”.

McLaughlin, 47, of Turnbull Close, Durham, denied wounding with intent, claiming self-defence.

He was cleared of that charge at trial last month but found guilty of the alternative of unlawful wounding.

The court heard his record of 85 previous offences includes 14 assaults. His most recent conviction was for putting someone in fear of violence in June 2024, for which he served 10 months in prison.

Rachel Kelly, in mitigation, said the victim believed a weapon had been used, but McLaughlin was never charged with a weapon offence.

She described it as a case of, “excessive self-defence”.

Miss Kelly said McLaughlin found stability after his release from his previous sentence in March last year, with a roof over his head and steering clear of drink and drugs.

The court was told McLaughlin had been back in prison on remand awaiting sentence since his conviction on September 17.

Judge Tom Mitchell said that whatever the jury thought about how the confrontation began, it ended in, “grossly disproportionate violence”.

The judge said McLaughlin appeared to “reek of violence” during his trial and had clearly gone “well over the top”, causing his cellmate “grave injuries”.

The judge imposed a three-year prison sentence, saying the assault was so serious that only immediate custody was justified.
