The former High Royds Asylum has long attracted tales of ghostly sightings and unexplained phenomena.

So when I came across dozens of online reviews questioning several aspects of a paranormal tour of the site, my curiosity was immediately sparked.

Was it a hidden Halloween gem or a waste of money? There was only one way to find out.

So, like Mulder and Scully, Daisy and I booked onto Mystic Nights' High Royds ghost walk, which promises an evening exploring the former West Riding Pauper Lunatic Asylum and its reportedly haunted surroundings.

Tickets cost £12.99 per person when we booked, although prices have since increased to £13.99 as Halloween approaches.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21652642.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Archive images of staff and nurses form part of an exhibition exploring High Royds' past. *(Image: T&A)*

Online, the event was described with suitably spooky accounts, referencing the asylum's long history and the patients who once lived there, many of whom are said to have never left.

However, the first challenge came before the tour had even begun.

We arrived at Menston Park, the advertised meeting point, but there was no obvious sign of a tour guide or group gathering. After wandering around for several minutes and spotting only a few scattered groups, we began to wonder whether the online criticism might have been justified.

It was only after messaging Mystic Nights through Facebook that we were directed to the actual meeting point, The Clean Inn, directly north of the park.

Once there, everything made a lot more sense. A sizeable crowd had gathered and was clearly waiting for the tour to begin. While the issue was quickly resolved, some clearer signposting would have gone a long way and may explain why some previous visitors believed the tour wasn't taking place.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21636439.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Visitors make their way through the grounds during the High Royds ghost walk on a damp autumn evening. *(Image: T&A)*

Once the walk got underway, it was clear the guide was passionate about the tour. He was friendly, approachable and made an effort to chat with people throughout the evening, helping everyone feel at ease.

As for the ghost-hunting itself, I enjoyed it, but perhaps not for the reasons I expected.

The group visited the treeline near the former asylum grounds and the graveyard, armed with dowsing rods and electromagnetic meters that were jokingly described as being "just like Ghostbusters". It was entertaining, there were plenty of laughs throughout the evening, and everyone seemed eager to get involved. However, I wouldn't describe it as genuinely frightening.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21652648.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Participants used dowsing rods while searching for signs of paranormal activity during the High Royds ghost walk. *(Image: T&A)*

Personally, the do-it yourself portion of the trip felt like an effort to fill the time, and despite our tools, no spirits came to us apart from the wind whistling through the trees.

My main criticism was that I wanted more stories. Some of the local legends and reported sightings were fascinating, particularly tales linked to the asylum's past, but they felt too few and far between. More detailed accounts, reported hauntings or historical case studies would have helped bring the locations to life.

This was definitely not helped by the tour guide mistaking the former asylum patients for prisoners more than once along the tour.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21652645.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Historic photographs on display inside the former asylum's chapel offer a glimpse into life at High Royds. *(Image: T&A)*

That said, the séance and some unexplained flickering lights certainly added a spooky edge to the evening. The on-site chapel was also fascinating, particularly the displays about former patients, although that felt more like a credit to the charity that allowed us access than the tour itself.

Despite being a sceptic, years of horror movies have taught me not to willingly invite spirits into my head.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21652643.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An aerial view of High Royds Asylum, which housed thousands of patients during its history. *(Image: T&A)*

However, all of that being said, I had fun.

Overall, if you're looking for a fun way to get into the Halloween spirit, I'd happily recommend it. But if you're expecting museum-level historical research or an experience that will leave you sleeping with the lights on, you may leave disappointed.

For me, the scariest thing all night was the mud slowly soaking through my trousers.
