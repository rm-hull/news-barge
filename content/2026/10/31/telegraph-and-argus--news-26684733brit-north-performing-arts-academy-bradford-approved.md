Plans for an “important regeneration project” for Bradford have been approved, and will bring over 550 people into the city centre to work and study.

Brit North will be a performing arts college built on the Jacob’s Well car park in Bradford city centre.

Plans for the school have been in the pipeline for years, with a planning application submitted last month.

Those plans, for a education campus complete with car park and multi use games area, have been approved by Bradford Council this week.

Officers said the school would boost the city centre’s regeneration and “increase footfall and activity within the area.”

Once up and running, 500 students will study at the school, with a further 50 staff. The aim is for Brit North to be operating by 2028.

The project is spearheaded by BPI (British Phonographic Industry) who are owners and organisers of the music showcase, the BRIT Awards.

Along with the wider recorded music industry, BPI helped to found The BRIT School in South London over thirty years ago which produced talents like Adele, Amy Winehouse, Tom Holland and Olivia Dean.

The facility was first proposed by BPI in 2023, with the Department for Education giving the green light for the new school to open in Bradford.

Earlier this year it was announced that Bradford Council would give the Jacobs Well Car Park to the DfE to allow the school to be built there.

![The site of the planned performing arts college](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21459423.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Jacobs Well car park site *(Image: T&A)*

The free-to-attend college will offer a vocational curriculum, providing young people with skills at the cutting-edge of industry practice.

The planning application showed that the building would include a theatre, set construction workshop, multi use games area, music performance spaces and a TV studio set, as well as the usual classrooms and college facilities.

There had been one objection to the plan, saying a taller building should occupy the site and that building the school “could limit future redevelopment opportunities.”

But planning officers that approved the application said: “The redevelopment of the site would secure the re-use of a vacant and underutilised brownfield site within a highly sustainable city centre location.

“The proposal would contribute towards the effective use of previously developed land whilst supporting the wider regeneration objectives of Bradford City Centre. Furthermore, the introduction of approximately 500 students and over 100 staff would increase activity and footfall within the city centre, supporting the vitality and viability of nearby businesses, services and community facilities.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21457251.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An artist's impression of the new Brit North school *(Image: BPI)*

“The inclusion of performance and creative arts facilities would also contribute towards the cultural offer of the city centre and support the growth of knowledge-based and creative industries.

“The redevelopment of the site therefore presents a significant opportunity to replace a visually weak and underutilised site with a building that makes a positive contribution to the character, appearance and function of this part of the city centre.

“The development would contribute towards the regeneration and continued diversification of the city centre, increase footfall and activity within the area, generate employment during both the construction and operational phases, and make effective use of a prominent brownfield site.”

After the plans were approved Dr Jo Twist OBE, CEO of BPI, said: “We’d like to thank Bradford Council, the Department for Education and the wider design team who have delivered another key milestone in this exciting project.

“BRIT North is a cultural and educational initiative, but it’s also an important regeneration project for Bradford.

“It successfully brings together significant investment from the private sector and central government, working in partnership with regional and local leaders, with an ambition to deliver for the city and the young people of Bradford and West Yorkshire.

“We look forward to celebrating more milestones in the coming months.”

Mark Casci, head of Policy and Representation at West Yorkshire Chamber of Commerce, also welcomed the approval, saing: “Bradford’s year as UK City of Culture demonstrated conclusively that the city is a significant national player as a cultural epicentre and is a region brimming with talent and creativity.

“Bradford is therefore the perfect location for Brit School North and will give our young people a significant springboard to performing arts greatness.

“This in turn will act as a magnet for talented people all over the UK who will hone their genius in the city and further enhance its prospects.”
