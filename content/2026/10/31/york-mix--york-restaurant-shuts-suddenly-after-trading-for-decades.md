A long-running and popular York restaurant has shut suddenly.

The Bengal Brasserie on Goodramgate is now closed.

All the furniture has been cleared from the main trading floor and the chairs are stacked up in front of the windows.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/bengal-brasserie-goodramgate-york-closed-2.webp)

The Indian restaurant has notes in the window saying: “Due to unforeseen circumstances, the restaurant will remain closed until further notice.

“Sorry for any inconvenience.”

***YorkMix*** understands the Bengal Brasserie closed yesterday (Friday), yet some staff still turned up that evening expecting to work a normal shift.

We have tried contacting the owners, but their phone appears disconnected and an email to one business address bounced back. We are waiting to hear back from an email sent to a second address.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/bengal-brasserie-goodramgate-york-closed-3.webp)

There is no mention of it being closed on the Bengal Brasserie website. It still appears to be taking online reservations or takeaway orders.

Bengal Brasserie opened on Goodramgate 34 years ago, in 1992. “Founded by Mohan Miah, our York restaurant on Goodramgate has been serving authentic Indian and Bengali cuisine in the shadow of the magnificent York Minster for over three decades,” the website says.

“As our founding location, York holds a special place in the Bengal Brasserie story. Every recipe, every tradition, and every aspect of our service philosophy was first perfected here.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/bengal-brasserie-goodramgate-york-closed-4.webp)

The last review published on TripAdvisor for York’s Bengal Brasserie was on 26 October. “Saf and the team were so welcoming and the food and drink very good,” the reviewer said.

It has a 4.6/5 rating on the site, and a 4.5/5 rating on Google Reviews.

The closure of the restaurant comes days after Tommy Banks, the founder of Roots York and the Black Swan at Oldstead, warned after the budget, that “we are looking at huge closures, huge job losses, and and potentially an industry that will be decimated forever” unless VAT was reduced for hospitality businesses.
