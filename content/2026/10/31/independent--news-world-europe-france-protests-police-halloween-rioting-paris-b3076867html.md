Police in Paris have fired tear gas during the latest anti-government protests in Frnance as more than 300,000 people march across the country.

Huge crowds have again taken to the streets for a third consecutive weekend of nationwide demonstrations, with authorities bracing for violence after nightfall. Demonstrations are also taking place in a number of cities in Germany and Albania.

Around 70,000 police officers and gendarmes have been deployed across France, with Saturday’s marches pushed away from central boulevards to the outer fringes of major cities.

French interior minister Laurent Nuñez has warned of a “very high risk” of coordinated disorder, cautioning that Halloween will not be accepted as an excuse to wear masks or trigger street violence.

![Protesters run amid tear gas, during a demonstration organised by labour unions in Paris](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/31/16/2026-10-31T162434Z_708492232_RC2GEOAQMR6O_RTRMADP_3_FRANCE-PROTEST.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The mobilisations follow two weekends of unrest that have seen widespread rioting, looting, arson, and mass arrests across the country.

At Porte de Bagnolet, on the eastern edge of Paris, several thousand people have gathered on Saturday afternoon behind a line of high school students leading the march hand in hand.

Stewards from the CGT union kept watch at the front of the procession, while riot police lined adjacent side streets.

Among those on the march was 21-year-old university student Corentin Cabreux, who said squalid conditions in student housing forced him to turn out.

![A pyrotechnic explodes next to police officers in riot gear in the French capital](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/31/16/2026-10-31T161157Z_1313864925_RC2GEOAX554X_RTRMADP_3_FRANCE-PROTEST.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I live in a state-run student residence where there are damp stains all along the bottom of my walls, whole sections peeling off, cockroaches everywhere, rats,” he said.

Mr Cabreux added that he hoped the turnout would remain peaceful to counter government depictions of the movement as mere cover for rioting.

In recent weeks, police crowd-control tactics have drawn fierce criticism from trade unions and legal monitors following the repeated use of tear gas, water cannon and dispersion grenades.

![Huge crowds turned out across France for the third weekend of demonstrations, including here in Marseille](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/31/16/2026-10-31T161057Z_1540280500_RC2FEOAN304W_RTRMADP_3_FRANCE-PROTEST.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Anger mounted earlier this month after a 15-year-old student had their hand blown off after picking up an explosive stun grenade.

While France previously suspended the use of stun grenades for student rallies, Mr Nuñez confirmed that all intermediate crowd-control weapons remain authorised if officers face targeted assaults.

Mass protests in the country began last month when secondary school pupils walked out over staff shortages, deteriorating school buildings and a university admissions system seen as unfair to disadvantaged students. The movement has since widened into a broader challenge to Emmanuel Macron’s government, drawing in trade unions and left-wing groups opposing economic reforms.

In an effort to prevent a repeat of previous evening unrest, the president ordered marches kept out of central commercial districts.

In Paris, police have barred access to Place de la République and Les Halles between 23:00 and 06:00, while unaccompanied minors face a 21:00 curfew around the Champs-Élysées.

The unrest in France is part of a widening pattern of youth discontent across Europe.

![A protester holds a smoke bomb with flames in the foreground during an anti-government demonstration in central Tirana, Albania](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/31/16/2302343659..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In Albania, violent clashes between young demonstrators and riot police have now entered their second week in the capital, Tirana, over soaring food prices, low wages and university fees.

On Friday, officers fired tear gas after government and education ministries were pelted with stones and pyrotechnics, leaving dozens detained and scores of protesters and police injured. There were further clashes in the country on Saturday.

In Italy, riot units remain on high alert across Milan, Turin and Bologna following recent clashes between student groups and law enforcement, with calls for fresh demonstrations circulating widely on TikTok over precarious job contracts and rising university costs.

In Germany, the youth campaign “Gen Z regelt” (“Gen Z’s got this”) has called for demonstrations across roughly 30 cities, demanding funding for education, healthcare and climate protection, while opposing any return to military conscription.

![German protesters facing riot police during a demonstration organised by "GenZ regelt!](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/31/15/2302332879..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Berlin police are expecting around 10,000 people at the Brandenburg Gate, while luxury retailers along the Kurfürstendamm boulevard have boarded up display windows ahead of the evening.

In Spain, regional police in Catalonia have activated their highest emergency alert in Barcelona after an anonymous TikTok network called Blocus urged fresh gatherings. T

he alert follows rioting last Saturday that resulted in roughly 130 arrests, mostly of minors, prompting officers to seal off access routes to major commercial districts.
