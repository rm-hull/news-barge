A County Durham family has transformed their home into a £2,000 Halloween spectacular, with thousands of lights synchronised to spooky music.

The Stonebank family’s annual light show has returned to Hunwick, near Bishop Auckland, for its sixth year, after months of work programming the elaborate display.

Dad Brad Stonebank, 53, spends months preparing the display, using specialist software normally found in TV studios, theatres and concert venues to synchronise thousands of LED lights to music.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652443.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Brad and Megan Stonebank at their Halloween house on Hunwick *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

What started as an idea spotted by daughter Megan, now 18, on YouTube during the Covid lockdown has grown into a major Halloween attraction, drawing families from across the area.

The 25-minute show features spooky classics, alongside new songs and light routines for this year's display.

Brad said: “There’s something about seeing the kids out there dancing and it makes you feel great, and we get to make a little bit of money for charity."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652445.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Hunwick Halloween House *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The family initially worried about how their neighbours would react, but the display has since become a fixture on the estate.

Brad said: “On the estate everybody looks forward to it.

“They come and speak to you and ask if you’re doing the lights again this year.”

Despite the thousands of lights and more than £2,000 worth of equipment, the show costs Brad an average of around £7 on his electricity bill.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652442.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Hunwick Halloween House *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The family also raises money for charity each year, with donation boxes placed in the garden.

The display is on Oakfields, Hunwick, with visitors able to catch the show from 5.30pm until 8pm tonight (October 31).

Visitors travelling by car are asked to park in the village and walk the short distance to the display, rather than block the street for residents.
