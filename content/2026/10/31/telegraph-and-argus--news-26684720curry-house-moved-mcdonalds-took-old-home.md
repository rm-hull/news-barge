A popular curry house which moved after McDonald's took over its former premises has been nominated for an award.

Kashmiri Aroma, on Northgate, in Baildon is among 10 finalists in the Telegraph & Argus Best Curry House 2026 competition.

Nadeem Akhtar runs the business with his four brothers.

## New chapter begins

The family-run restaurant started in Ilkley in 2008 before opening its Baildon branch at the start of 2023.

Mr Akhtar said: "Unfortunately, when our lease came up for renewal, we couldn’t compete with McDonald’s, who eventually took over the premises.

"After so many years in Ilkley, leaving wasn’t easy.

"We had put a lot of hard work into that restaurant, and it held many memories for us.

"But rather than close the business, we decided to relocate to Baildon and start the next chapter."

## 2 more restaurants

The move helped the family expand Kashmiri Aroma, which now has two more restaurants in Wakefield and Horbury.

Mr Akhtar said: "Restaurants have been part of our family life for as long as I can remember.

"We wanted to build something of our own, using the experience we’d gained over the years, while keeping it very much a family business."

He runs the Baildon and Horbury branches with his brothers Tariq Mahmood, Waheed Akhtar, Imran Khan and Majid Jahangir.

## 'Reputation means everything'

The family has long-standing ties to the restaurant trade and previously worked with the Aagrah group.

Mr Akhtar said: "We don’t try to overcomplicate things.

"We believe in good food, generous hospitality, and treating customers properly.

"When you run a family business, your reputation means everything."

## 'Real honour' to be nominated

He said being named a finalist in the T&A competition was a "proud moment" for the whole family.

Readers can vote for their favourite finalist using voting slips in the newspaper until November 7, with the winner receiving a special feature in the Telegraph & Argus.

Mr Akhtar said: "We’re absolutely delighted.

"When you’ve spent so many years working in restaurants, you don’t always get the chance to stop and appreciate what you’ve achieved.

"To be recognised by local people alongside some excellent restaurants is a real honour."

## 18 years and still going strong

He also spoke of the strong relationship the restaurant has built with its customers over the years.

Mr Akhtar said: "Our customers are the reason we’re still here after 18 years.

"We never want Kashmiri Aroma to feel like just another restaurant.

"We want people to feel comfortable, whether it’s their first visit or their hundredth."

He thanked the community for its ongoing support.

Mr Akhtar said: "On behalf of myself, my brothers, and all our staff, I’d like to say a big thank you to everyone who has supported Kashmiri Aroma over the years."
