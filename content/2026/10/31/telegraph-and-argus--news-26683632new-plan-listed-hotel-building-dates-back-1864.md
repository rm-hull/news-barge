A Grade II Listed hotel could be converted into a 21-room house, if new plans are approved.

Oakwood Hall on Lady Lane, Bingley, was built as a large Victorian family home in the 1860s, and has most recently been used as a hotel.

But now a planning application to change the use of the hotel to a single dwelling has been submitted to Bradford Council by Vikesh Tailor.

The application says the change will return the building to its original use as a grand mansion, and will reduce traffic in the area.

The council are expected to make a decision on the application next month.

## History of the building

Built in 1864, Oakwood Hall was constructed for Thomas Garnett, a prominent local cloth merchant who helped Bingley prosper in this period.

It was designed by George Knowles and William Wilcox, and despite the later conversion to a hotel, most of the original features remain.

The plans show the building will retain its large dining room, as well as a formal dining room, a kitchen and prep kitchen and 21 other rooms.

## What does the application say?

The planning application says: “The building will be occupied as one dwellinghouse, with its existing accommodation used for domestic purposes.

“The principal reception rooms, dining rooms and other communal areas will provide generous family living accommodation.

“Existing bedrooms and associated facilities will continue to provide residential accommodation appropriate to a substantial private dwelling.

“All existing historic architectural and decorative features will be retained, including the principal staircase, historic panelling, fireplaces, ornamental plasterwork and stained-glass windows.

“An important aspect of Oakwood Hall's significance is its original purpose as a substantial Victorian family residence.

“The building was designed to accommodate the domestic and social requirements of a prosperous nineteenth-century household, with generously proportioned reception rooms, formal dining accommodation, substantial bedrooms and elaborate circulation spaces.

“Its architectural composition and decorative interiors were conceived specifically for this domestic setting.

“Particularly important surviving features include the elaborate principal staircase with carved newel posts and decorative balustrading, the Morris & Co. stained-glass staircase window incorporating designs associated with Edward Burne-Jones, and the dining-room fireplace designed by William Burges.

“The stained-glass window is regarded as an exceptional example of early Morris & Co. domestic stained glass.

"The building's association with Burges and Morris is particularly significant, with their surviving work forming an important part of the building's original decorative scheme.

“The continued occupation of historic buildings is an important consideration in securing their long-term conservation.

"The proposed change of use will enable Oakwood Hall to continue functioning as an occupied building, with its maintenance and upkeep supported through private residential ownership.”
