## Jeremy Clarkson remembers Hollywood 'nice guy' with Top Gear tale after death

Jeremy Clarkson remembers Hollywood 'nice guy' with hilarious Top Gear tale *(Image: Newsquest)>*

Jeremy Clarkson has remembered Hollywood 'nice guy' Sir Patrick Stewart with a hilarious Top Gear tale after his death, aged 86.

The former Top Gear presenter shared the story following the death of Sir Patrick Stewart, who died at his home in Los Angeles on Wednesday, October 28, aged 86.

His family said he died following a “brief and poignant journey” with Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia.

Mr Clarkson, who lives in Oxfordshire and runs The Farmer’s Dog pub near Burford, remembered the actor’s appearance on Top Gear as the Star in a reasonably priced car.

Writing on X, he said: “Patrick Stewart. Turned up so early and so unobtrusively to be a star in the reasonably priced car, we mistook him for a delivery driver and asked him to go round the back.

“Took his hat off and said, ‘But I’m the captain of the Enterprise’. F\*\*\*\*\* g nice guy.”

Sir Patrick, who has West Oxfordshire links, was best known around the world for playing Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation, a role he began in 1987 and which propelled him to international fame.

He also became known to a new generation of moviegoers as Professor Charles Xavier, also known as Professor X, in the X-Men film franchise, starring alongside Sir Ian McKellen as Magneto.

Sir Ian, a close friend of the actor for decades, has been among those leading tributes.

The pair first met as young actors with the Royal Shakespeare Company around half a century ago and later became internationally recognised for their roles in the X-Men films.

In his tribute, Sir Ian described his friend as “a man to rely on, to admire and in my case, love”, adding that Sir Patrick was “the most respected and beloved actor of his generation on either side of the Atlantic”.

Other stars paying tribute have included William Shatner, who said Sir Patrick brought “intelligence, grace, and a deep sense of humanity to everything he did”, and Hugh Jackman, who called him one of the greats of stage and screen and a “tremendous friend and man”.

Sir Patrick enjoyed a career spanning more than six decades across theatre, television and film.

He received numerous honours during his long career, including Laurence Olivier Awards for his stage work and a Grammy Award for his spoken-word recording of *Peter and the Wolf*, while earning nominations for the Emmys, Golden Globes and a Tony Award.

He was knighted for services to drama in 2010 and remained closely associated with both Star Trek and X-Men throughout his later career.

His family said he was surrounded by family and friends during his final days, with “live music, prayer, tears, laughter” around him.
