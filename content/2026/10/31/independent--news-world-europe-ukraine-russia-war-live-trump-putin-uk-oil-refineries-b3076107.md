Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky has welcomed the sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 missiles to Kyiv while warning that the deliveries could take years.

Zelensky has said that the stockpiles were needed now to counter mounting Russian ballistic missile attacks as the war approaches yet another winter.

The US State Department on Friday approved the sale of 500 Patriot missiles to Ukraine for an estimated £1.5 billion in one of the Trump administration’s most significant steps yet to supply defensive weapons to Kyiv.

Zelensky praised the “strong decision" but added: "It has been stipulated that deliveries may begin ONLY IN A FEW YEARS." "Right now, the Russians are attacking our energy infrastructure every single day."

The missiles, which are yet to be manufactured, were not expected to be delivered until a couple of years into the 2030s, officials told Reuters.

The approval comes just days after Trump said that “it’s time for Ukraine to get a new president” in his latest dig amid a long-running feud with Zelensky.

## Could Oleshky pave the way for similar local ceasefires?

Kremlin statements suggest the operation could be a one-off — at least for now.

Asked if the operation meant Russia and Ukraine could find other areas where they could cooperate, Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said it was a specific and isolated case that, for now, did not have wider implications for the peace process.

The ceasefire, which was extended to the wider Kherson region, expires on Sunday. There has been no easing of fighting in the other three Ukrainian regions Russia says it has incorporated as its own.

After holding talks with US negotiators, Putin this month agreed to resume US-mediated peace talks with Ukraine next month.

But Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov, unhappy about attempted Ukrainian drone attacks on Moscow, has hinted at a possible rethink if the attacks continue, while Ukraine has resumed attacks on Russian oil refineries after a pause.

There has also been no let-up in Russian airstrikes on infrastructure targets across Ukraine.

## Desperate residents evacuate devastated Russian-occupied Oleshky by bus

Residents in the occupied Ukrainian town of Oleshky began a desperate evacuation on Friday as a localised ceasefire between Moscow and Kyiv held for a third day.

A fleet of 25 buses with at least 750 seats stood ready to withdraw locals seeking to leave, Kyiv's human rights ombudsman, Dmytro Lubinets, said. His Russian counterpart, Yana Lantratova, added that space would also be provided for their pets.

Residents who wanted to leave were given a four-hour window to do so, lasting until 2pm local time. Several hundred people are understood to have decided to stay.

## G20 photocall cancelled after Miliband refuses to be pictured with Russia

## Canada to provide €500m for Ukraine

Canada will contribute €500m to the EU's €90bn Ukraine Support loan, prime minister Mark Carney announced.

Canada's contribution is expected to bridge Kyiv's shortfall of at least €70bn in its budget.

European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen welcomed Ottawa's participation, saying it sent a strong message of solidarity to Ukraine.

“This sends a powerful message to Ukraine. Together, we are stepping up our support for Ukraine,” she said.

## US midterm elections could determine Ukraine’s future, Zelensky warns

Volodymyr Zelensky has said the forthcoming US midterm elections could impact the future of Ukraine, as Donald Trump’s Republican Party faces gloomy polls.

The Ukrainian President warned that “a lot of things” - including US economic deals with Russia - now depend on Tuesday’s votes, in an interview with *The Sun*.

“The result will have an influence either way,” he said. “It will influence a lot of different things, including Ukraine, including the war in Ukraine, including the attitude to Russians and different deals with them... Changes are understandable.”

“How about the condemnation?” he said. “If the United States, our strong partner, understands that Putin’s goal is to occupy us and to destroy us, they can’t be on Putin’s side.”
