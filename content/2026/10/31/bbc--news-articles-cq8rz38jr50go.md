Karl Bushby bears down on Hull, with his heavy duty gear trolley "The Beast"

**With his journey almost complete, Karl Bushby reflects on adventures that have taken him across four continents, as well as strained relationships and how life as he knows it is about to change dramatically.**

Back in 1998, the former paratrooper, then aged 29, set off from Punta Arenas, Chile, to walk an unbroken path to his home city of Hull, without breaking two self-imposed rules: not to return to the UK until he had completed his journey, and not to use transport to advance.

Now 57, he is embarking on his final few miles having hacked his way through the jungles of the Darién Gap, crossed the frozen Bering Strait and, just a few weeks ago, swum the English Channel.

At the end, he will be met by his 36-year-old son Adam, a 12-year-old granddaughter whom he has never met, his father and brothers. His mother will be waiting at his childhood home that he left 28 years ago.

![Karl Bushby, with a thick beard covered in snow and ice. He has a hood up. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/8ccf/live/c8ef1030-d462-11f1-ab12-eb0c94f5b382.jpg)

Karl Bushby crossed the Bering Strait in 2006

"We're definitely in *terra incognita* [unknown territory] right now, or pretty soon will be," says Bushby, as I join him in rural Lincolnshire.

"Right now, I'm comfortable because I'm doing what I do, but pretty soon that's going to screech to a halt."

A bit like "The Beast", Bushby's heavy duty hardcart.

Just a couple of miles into this leg, Bushby's handcart, which he uses to carry essential supplies, sustains a puncture.

"We haven't had one of these for a long time," he says. "This could be a pain. Damn."

As I watched him attempt a hasty roadside repair, he flashed a grin before adding: "If it was raining, I'd probably be moaning a bit more."

"The first few days that I was in Dover, it started raining. But we'd managed to just thread the needle as far as the weather was concerned. We had those perfect three days [for the swim]."

Cars thunder past us.

Bushby quips: "You guys know you drive on the wrong side of the road, right?"

His use of the word "you" speaks of his disconnection from home.

![A man in a mustard coloured fleece and shirt sits by the roadside repairing a punctured tyre on his handcart. The card is laden with essential equipment.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/31ab/live/683a2a30-d12a-11f1-9a61-7b8d75492b97.jpg)

Bushby makes a roadside repair after his handcart sustains a puncture in Lincolnshire

Bushby is well versed in overcoming physical barriers, such as a flat tyre.

But is he prepared for what is to follow?

"Everything that I am and everything that I know of me is going to go away," he says. "And that's going to be a problem. You're going to have the old identity crisis, because Karl is *this* [an adventurer] and has been for half his life.

"I will have to figure out who I am again. What is the meaning to it all? How do I find that meaning and purpose? How do I fill that hole I'm suddenly going to find myself in?

"Every fibre of my being, every day, has been what I'm doing right now. It has been the mission.

"I think the old adage goes... the only thing worse than a man who can't fulfil his dreams is a man who's fulfilled his dreams... because then what?"

Angela Maxwell, an American long-distance walker, joined him for the walk from Dover, where he landed after swimming the Channel.

"I think Karl might be surprised how much he'll miss the road," she says. "We joke sometimes that Karl doesn't love walking. But he loves the mission. He loves the passion, the purpose."

![A distance shot showing a man in a wetsuit wading out of the English Channel, the white cliffs of Dover in the background. Five people are on the sand to greet him.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/3ac6/live/4546a560-d12c-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.png)

Bushby emerges from the English Channel near Dover

Bushby has been surprised by the interest his return to the UK has stoked.

"It's been almost overwhelming. The amount of people that have recognised me along the way. It's cool. It gets a bit weird at times when people get really, really excited, and I just can't work out why. From my perspective, it just seems a little strange.

"I'm some guy who's just walking."

A car pulls into a lay-by and the driver asks if he is *the* Karl Bushby. The men pose for a selfie.

Bushby has consistently denied this is a spiritual undertaking; a journey of discovery.

But does he think the global interest in his story stems from an inner wanderlust present in many of us, but acted upon by few?

"I think that's it," he says.

"I've lived a million men's dreams. A lot of people would love to walk away from home and just keep going."

Bushby says his "opportunity to walk" came following the end of his marriage, with his wife returning to Northern Ireland with their son.

![A black and white image of British paratroopers marching with rifles.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/480/cpsprodpb/00f4/live/7c204120-cfa8-11f1-aadb-297fd0a130b9.jpg)

Bushby, pictured centre, at the start of his 12-year Parachute Regiment career

The reality of life on the road is not as rosy as it might first appear, it seems.

"I'm dealing with the day-to-day stuff - punctures, just trying to find a place where you can wash your underwear. That's my life. That's the mundane stuff, walking in the rain, it's never-ending kilometres.

"It's not as glorious as it looks."

Bushby says he has only spent about 15 of the 28 years walking, often retreating to Mexico, where costs are low, between stints.

The remainder of the time has been spent applying for visas and "dealing with geopolitics", he says.

Travel restrictions during the Covid-19 pandemic also resulted in delays.

Under the rules he set for his journey, Bushby could not return to the UK to meet his family.

On rare occasions, he met his son and parents, who are divorced, in other countries.

"Family is family, right?" says Bushby.

"They're always going to be there. But there will be strain. There will be distance. So much so that you've grown apart to some degree. You don't really know each other anymore.

"As far as a love life goes, standby. You're going to take a kick in the nuts. It's not going to work out for you."

![Karl Bushby, then in his late 20s, walking along a steep mountain dirt track.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e436/live/4c7bcf20-d066-11f1-bbf5-99ed69f0d81f.jpg)

He set off from Chile on 1 November 1998

Soon after arriving in Dover, he tasted fish, chips and mushy peas for the first time in almost three decades.

He said nowhere else in the world does the dish quite like the Brits.

But venturing beyond the superficial, he appears not to have missed the UK.

"I've had the whole world to play with, so I've been distracted by many shiny things."

Bushby believes he has not aged conventionally.

"I'm older, but I don't feel that old. I still feel way too young for my age, mentally. I still identify with that guy from the Americas way back when."

With a twinkle in his eye, he adds: "I have still got that sense of adventure."

His journey has also enabled him to meet many people.

"I personally have met the very best of humanity, and that's been a very uplifting thing. It's reaffirmed my belief and faith in humanity. The kindness of strangers made it all come together.

"I couldn't have done it without the world."

Before sponsors and a book deal, Bushby claims he was a "penniless backpacker" who relied on donations and even food discarded on roadsides to survive.

![A woman with light brown hair stands clutching a black wrought iron gate in front of a terraced home. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/3c9b/live/07bae0e0-d068-11f1-bbf5-99ed69f0d81f.jpg)

Bushby's mother, Angela, will be waiting for him at the gate to the home where she raised him

He has advice for others pondering an adventure.

"Take any given opportunity. You can do a lot more than you ever imagined. A little bit of self-confidence will take you an awful long way. You're not going to achieve anything by not doing anything."

What advice would he give to a young Bushby about to set off for Chile?

"He doesn't want to hear it. You don't tell him anything. Just grin at him and let him be."
