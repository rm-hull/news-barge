## Competing US, Russia resolutions on Western Sahara defeated in UN Security Council

Russia vetoed a US-drafted resolution on Western Sahara at the Security Council on Friday, leaving the UN peacekeeping mission there without a renewed mandate just a day before it expires.

The resolution would have extended the mission for another year while cementing Morocco's autonomy plan as the only ⁠basis for talks to settle the half-century-old conflict and scaling back its military component.

The council then defeated a rival Russian resolution to extend the mission for a year. That draft got three votes in favor and one against, with 11 abstentions, failing to get the nine affirmative votes needed to pass.

The rare showdown threatens the future of a mission the council has routinely renewed for decades and deepens a dispute over the territory, most of which is controlled by Morocco but claimed by the Algeria-backed Polisario Front, which seeks an independent state.

## Reeves urges EU to ‘act now’ and seize £175bn of Russian assets to protect Ukraine

Rachel Reeves has urged the European Union to seize €210bn (£175bn) of frozen Russian assets to help Ukraine defend itself against Kremlin aggression, warning that failing to act now would risk the continent’s security.

In her first significant intervention since she left government earlier this year, the former chancellor told Brussels that “the time to act is now” as Kyiv faces “a desperate winter”. She warned Kyiv is confronting a funding blackhole as Donald Trump seeks to pressure the nation “to cut a deal with Moscow”.

## Russian drone strikes knock out electricity supplies to 280,000 Ukrainian families

Russian drones struck Ukraine's energy facilities in overnight attacks, knocking out electricity supplies to about 280,000 Ukrainian families in the Kyiv and Dnipropetrovsk regions, president Volodymyr Zelensky said on Saturday.

## Humanitarian convoy ready for Oleshky

A humanitarian convoy for the stricken city of Oleshky has been set up in a nearby town.

It includes 25 buses, ambulances and other vehicles for the aid mission, a Ukrainian official said.

“We are bringing humanitarian aid to the local residents of the city of Oleshky,” Dmytro Lubinets, Ukraine's ombudsman and chief negotiator, said.

“Our main task is to ensure that this aid reaches every person who needs it. We pay special attention to the elderly, people with disabilities.”

## Zelensky berates Russia for continued attacks

Fighting continued elsewhere, however. Russia’s Defense Ministry said air defences downed more than 1,000 Ukrainian drones overnight, while Ukraine’s air force said Russia launched ballistic and other missiles, along with scores of drones.

A Russian drone attack on a town in northeast Ukraine has left one woman dead and eight people injured, Ukraine’s emergency services said Friday.

The strike occurred in the Sumy region in the town of Shostka, near Ukraine’s border with Russia.

The agency said rescue efforts were hampered by repeated air raid alerts.

In his nightly address, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky accused Russia of stepping up attacks designed to make ordinary Ukrainians suffer during another winter.

He did not mention the aid mission.

“The Russians have not taken a single step toward de-escalation in the energy sector and continue terrorist strikes on our cities,” he said.

“We will respond accordingly and justly.”

Zelensky's office also announced sanctions on individuals and entities involved in developing Russia’s Rassvet satellite communications system, which Kyiv says Moscow is scaling up for military purposes.

## Evacuated Oleshky residents begin long journey out of the war-ravaged region

Evacuees from Russian-occupied Oleshky began the long journey out of the war-ravaged region in bus convoys Friday, on the fourth day of the most significant truce since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine 4½ years ago.

The estimated 2,000 remaining residents in the city of Oleshky were offered the choice of traveling to Ukrainian- or Russian-held territory, or remaining in the city with replenished food and medical supplies.

Officials in Kyiv said the evacuated residents bound for Ukraine were traveling to the city of Anapa in southern Russia from where they would fly to Belarus before crossing the border into northern Ukraine.

The shortest route from Oleshky to Anapa is about 450 kilometers (280 miles) through Russian-held Crimea.Russia’s ombudswoman and lead representative on the mission, Yana Lantratova said buses also transported Oleshky residents who want to live in Russia.

Russia and Ukraine have not specified how many people chose to leave the city, but US first lady Melania Trump, whom the White House said helped broker the localized truce, posted on social media Friday that the convoy could safely evacuate more than 750 people.

After the convoys left Oleshky, Dmytro Lubinets, Ukraine’s ombudsman and chief negotiator, described the aid mission as “unique” and said he hoped the same model could be used to help other communities stranded by the war in Ukraine.

## Russia’s last independent newspaper critical of Putin's war in Ukraine shut down

Russia’s flagship independent newspaper *Novaya Gazeta* announced that it was closing.

It said it had been “killed” by state pressure after years of repression targeting its journalists whose reporting was critical of the Kremlin and its Ukraine war, AFP reported.

Novaya Gazeta was one of the last outlets to openly criticise Russia’s offensive.

The paper’s Nobel Prize-winning founder Dmitry Muratov told AFP the closure was “part of a giant military campaign being waged by the Russian Federation”.
