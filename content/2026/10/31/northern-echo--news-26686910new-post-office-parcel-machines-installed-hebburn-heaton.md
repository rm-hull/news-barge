Post Office drop boxes have arrived in Tyne and Wear as part of a national rollout to speed up parcel sending and reduce queues.

The new self-service machines have been installed at the Hebburn and Heaton’s Chillingham Road branches.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21654386.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Post Office)*

They are among the first in the country to receive the drop boxes, which allow customers to scan pre-paid parcels and deposit them quickly without waiting at the counter.

Richard Fleetwood, director of RF Retail Limited, which operates the branches, said: "It’s exciting for my branches to be among some of the first in the UK to benefit from this new technology.

"Customers have been impressed with how easy Post Office Drop Boxes are.

"It’s a doddle.

"It’s just scanning a barcode on a package or QR code on a phone which prints a label.

"Then it’s pressing a single button and putting the item in the box.

"Customers can get a printed receipt or email and customers also get a notification from their courier company.

"It’s not just youngsters who are tech-savvy using them, it’s all ages as it’s so intuitive how to use a Drop Box."

In the first full day around 100 parcels were self-scanned at Hebburn and Heaton’s Chillingham Road combined.

Post Office Drop Boxes were highlighted in the Chancellor’s Budget as part of wider support for the Post Office network.

The boxes are part of a national rollout of self-service technology designed to improve efficiency and customer experience.

Chancellor John Healey recently announced plans to install 1,000 drop boxes by the end of the financial year, with 500 set to be in place by the end of this calendar year.

Mr Healey said: "Rolling out hundreds more Post Office drop boxes before Christmas and bringing the total to 1,000 by the end of this financial year will make it easier and more convenient for people to send and return parcels without having to queue at a Post Office counter.

"This will help take some of the hassle out of everyday errands in the run-up to Christmas and going forward."

Postmasters will still offer traditional counter services for those who prefer face-to-face assistance.

However, the drop boxes are expected to shorten wait times and free up staff for other services.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21654388.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Post Office)*

Neil Brocklehurst, CEO of the Post Office, welcomed the investment and said it reflects ongoing Government support.

Mr Brocklehurst said: "It’s clear that the Government continues to back the Post Office network – both through the Budget and its new strategy to champion the UK’s high streets."

"As part of his Budget, the Chancellor announced that Post Office to roll out up to 1,000 new multi-carrier self-service Drop Boxes by the end of the financial year.

"Postmasters will now have the opportunity to bring the new technology into their branches, reducing their costs and giving customers greater choice and flexibility."

Mr Fleetwood confirmed that additional drop boxes are planned for other RF Retail Limited branches, including Sunderland City and South Shields, in mid-November.
