**The Jenners Building will shine once again this winter as external scaffolding is removed, revealing the extent of a major restoration project.**

The facade of the building will be seen for the first time in two years, as craftspeople and artisans continue to work to protect historic architectural features.

The Princes Street building, which has been closed since December 2020, will be redeveloped into a multi-use retail and hospitality site incorporating 20,000 square metres of space.

Restoration work was temporarily halted in 2023 after a fire broke out in the building, claiming the life of firefighter Barry Martin.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21652570.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the inside of the building will continue *(Image: Jim Stephenson)*

Recent efforts have seen the cataloguing of more than 600 windows and the refurbishment of the gold-lettered Jenners signs.

“Those of us working on the restoration have had the privilege of seeing the Victorian craftsmanship up close,” said Andrew Keith, managing director of The Jenners Building.

“Over the coming months the façade will gradually re-emerge.

“Edinburgh is getting The Jenners Building back, and people will be able to see the care that has gone into conserving it.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21652569.jpg?type=mds-article-620)34 statues and *(Image: Jim Stephenson)*

“It is a much-loved building that has always belonged to Edinburgh. There is still a great deal to do inside, but we are making good progress towards it once again playing a central role in the life of the city.”

Home to the Jenners department store for decades, the Princes Street building was purchased by Scotland’s richest man, Anders Holch Povlsen, in 2017.

Designed by William Hamilton Beattie, the building was completed between 1895 and 1905.

Work on the building's facade has included the refurbishment of 34 caryatids and statues by Ratho-based Graciela Ainsworth Sculpture Conservation.

“It was a genuine privilege to return to the female caryatids and statues at The Jenners Building in 2025, a full 30 years after our initial conservation of them in 1995,” Ms Ainsworth said.

“Today, we are reapplying breathable, sacrificial shelter coats to give these vulnerable statues another layer of defence. This project has been a career highlight, blending our original care with today’s best practices, and I’m immensely proud to have helped conserve these sculptures for a new generation.”

Falkirk’s Matheson Plumbing Company Limited, led work to repair the building’s gutters, dormers and ridges.

“As a company that has worked on many of Edinburgh’s historic properties, we are honoured to be involved with a building as iconic as this one,” said director Fraser Matheson.

“I have fond memories of visiting the building as a child, so now playing a part in its evolution is a joy.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21652568.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rainbow Glass Studios has restored all of the building’s decorative glass. *(Image: Jim Stephenson)*

Meanwhile, Rainbow Glass Studios, Scotland’s largest stained-glass specialist, led efforts to restore all of the building’s decorative glass.

Director Moira Malcolm said: “Working on The Jenners Building has been a privilege for our team. We have been restoring the heraldic glass windows and enjoying the unique opportunity to study historic photographs of the interiors.

“The 1895 images revealed more individual decorative lights that had been lost to time. Recreating these features from the photos and surviving examples has been immensely satisfying.”

Stonework restoration was led by Stirling Stone, whose director, John Dunn, said: “We have used Blaxter Sandstone, which is quarried in Northumberland and supplied by Dunhouse Quarry.

“This fine-grained, honey-coloured stone has been quarried since the 1890s and used for many prestigious buildings in Scotland.

“I’m looking forward to the wraps coming off and people being able to see the building’s exterior reinstated to its former glory.”

The Jenners Building is not expected to open before 2029.
