President Donald Trump is hunkering down in Mar-a-Lago this weekend for a blitz of telerallies to support struggling Republican candidates across the U.S. before Election Day next week.

Surface-to-air missile systems have been deployed near the president’s Florida compound and at the newly renamed Donald J. Trump International Airport at the request of U.S. Secret Service, according to federal officials.

The defenses are designed to combat potential aerial threats, including aircraft and drones, though officials have not revealed the exact reason for them.

Early voting has jumped in key battleground states as candidates begin a final weekend of midterm campaigns. Nearly 50 million Americans have already voted, and early voting has already outpaced the rate of the 2022 midterm elections

Changes to Trump’s campaign schedule this weekend followed a brief cancelation of rallies in Kansas and Iowa, though and the president will now rally in those states on Sunday after GOP officials pulled the plug on the in-person events.

“You know what the reason is? I don’t feel I have to go to Kansas,” Trump snapped at a Reuters reporter on Thursday. “I feel perfect. You are disgusting.”

The president rallied in Florida on Friday night, where the president lashed out a heckler within minutes of his remarks.

## Mary Peltola responds to accusations her campaign helped recruit same-name candidate

Mary Peltola, Alaska’s Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate, responded to a federal investigation and election lawsuit stemming from allegations that her campaign helped recruit Dan J. Sullivan to run against Republican incumbent Sen. Dan S. Sullivan.

“I am so deeply offended by these accusations about having any involvement with anyone else’s candidacy,” Peltola said on Friday.

"I'm not out soliciting more competition,” she added. “I do not need any help winning this race except by all of you in this room and the massive amounts of support we have across this state.”

Also on Friday, the FBI reached out to people who gave money to Dan J. Sullivan as its investigation into the candidate intensified.

## Judge blasts Trump team for threatening election workers in latest rebuke to midterm plans

A federal judge has blocked Donald Trump’s administration from threatening election workers with criminal prosecution for processing ballots — thwarting the government’s last-ditch attempts to toss out votes before Election Day.

The temporary restraining order prevents the Department of Justice and Homeland Security from demanding state and local election officials stop processing or setting aside ballots in the president’s crusade against alleged noncitizen voters.

## Trump plans to deport dozens of Afghans back to the Taliban in first large-scale removal flight

Donald Trump’s administration is imminently expected to forcibly remove dozens of Afghans from the U.S. to Afghanistan, marking the first large-scale deportation flight of Afghans back into the hands of the Taliban, aid groups and human rights lawyers warn.

Roughly 150 Afghans are reportedly scheduled for the removal flight, which is being routed from Louisiana to Pakistan, where officials are expected to expel them to their home country.

## Judge blocks Trump team from threatening election officials with criminal prosecution

In another major blow to the Trump administration’s midterm planning, a federal judge in California rebuked the government for its “campaign to intimidate, threaten, and coerce state and local officials and employees who are engaged in administering the ongoing midterm 2026 election.”

“Although Defendants claim that their actions are designed to halt unlawful voting by noncitizens, their actions are likely to have the effect of interfering with election administration and disenfranchising lawful voters,” U.S. District Judge Trina Thompson wrote Friday night.

The order commands the federal agencies to stop threatening election officials with criminal prosecution in its ongoing efforts to target alleged noncitizen voting.

Her order arrived hours after a federal appeals court in the state blocked the administration’s efforts to segregate 20,000 ballots, which another judge warned would wreak havoc” on elections in the state. That battle could soon head to the Supreme Court next if the administration wants to keep fighting.
