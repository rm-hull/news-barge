**'Why we should be paying less for the BBC each year'**

I MUST admit to having a preference to the BBC programmes on TV when it comes to the news, documentaries and plays, far better than the interruptions on commercial TV.

But I've noticed more and more that lots of programmes now have celebrities in their lineup, including the news or other popular shows, who are actually plugging their own books, music, plays or films.

The BBC is supported by us paying our licence fee and as such we should be paying less not an increase every year.

*D M Deamer,*

*Penleys Grove Street,*

*Monkgate,*

*York*

**National security should be the first priority**

FROM a defence perspective, the Government's current spending commitments do not go far enough to meet the challenges facing the United Kingdom. While the ambition to increase defence spending to 3 per cent of GDP by 2030 is a positive step, it feels too little, too late given the growing threats to our national security.

Defence procurement remains a significant concern. Too often, equipment programmes suffer delays and cost overruns, meaning our Armed Forces receive technology that is no longer cutting-edge by the time it enters service. Greater investment is needed not only in equipment but also in the industrial capacity required to build, maintain and support it.

At the same time, welfare spending continues to rise. While support must always be available for those who genuinely need it, there must be robust checks in place to ensure the system is fair and sustainable. Public confidence depends on taxpayers knowing that benefits are being directed to those who are entitled to receive them.

National security should be the first priority of any government, and I believe greater urgency is needed to ensure the UK is properly equipped to meet future challenges.

*John Jones,*

*Russet House,*

*Birch Close,*

*York*
