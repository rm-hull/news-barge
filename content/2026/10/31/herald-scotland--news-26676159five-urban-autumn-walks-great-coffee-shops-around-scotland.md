As much as I love rural gems, I would be the first to admit that there are some fabulous nooks to explore in and around Scotland’s towns and cities too. Here’s my pick of five gorgeous urban autumn walks, all with superb places for coffee or a bite to eat nearby.

## **Pittencrieff Park, Dunfermline, Fife**

Known locally as “The Glen”, Pittencrieff Park is great fun for a gentle daunder, with a waterfall, Italian garden and famed peacock sanctuary among its many attractions.

The tree trail has photogenic specimens such as monkey puzzle and giant redwoods. And not forgetting the Carnegie Oak which was planted by its namesake, the industrialist and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie, in 1904.

Another fascinating spot is Wallace’s Well, reputed to be where William Wallace sought refuge while fleeing English enemies in 1303.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21157176.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Pittencrieff Park *(Image: unknown)*

Pit stop: Brunch aficionados will adore Wynd (10 Cross Wynd). Prost Coffee (11 Maygate) is located within the 16th-century Abbot House, offering a fantastic selection of home-baking, including scones, biscuits and seasonal cakes.

## **Water of Leith, Edinburgh**

Described as “a silver thread in a ribbon of green”, the Water of Leith Walkway has myriad delights to enthral as it winds the 12 miles (19km) from Balerno to Leith Docks.

You don’t have to do the whole thing in one go. From Stockbridge to Leith is around 3.5 miles (5.6km). This can be undertaken in either direction, depending on your inclination.

Highlights include 6 Times, the sextet of life-size, cast-iron sculptures created by the Turner Prize-winning artist Antony Gormley, with four visible along this portion of the walkway.

Pit stop: The Pastry Section has shops in Leith (143 Great Junction Street) and Stockbridge (86 Raeburn Place). Both are a mere stone’s throw from the Water of Leith and ideal if you fancy a yummy brownie, cinnamon bun or lemon meringue tart.

## **Balgay Hill, Dundee**

Rising above the rooftops in the west end of Dundee, Balgay Hill is not only a terrific vantage point to enjoy scenic panoramas over the city below, but also the wonders of the night sky above.

Mills Observatory occupies a lofty perch on its wooded summit. Britain’s first purpose-built public facility of its kind, it opened in 1935. The 2026/27 programme of astronomy-themed and stargazing events is currently under way.

The surrounding paths are lovely for a stroll, with a 1.85-mile (3km) route providing glimpses of the playing fields at neighbouring Lochee Park, as well as the picturesque Victoria Park.

Pit stop: Leyline Cafe (98 Annfield Road) is just over half-a-mile (1km) away on foot. A cosy and dog-friendly haven, it is also a welcoming hub for board game fans. Perfect for coffee and light meals.

## **The Back Walk, Stirling**

Built between 1724 and 1791, the Back Walk lays claim to being the oldest, publicly maintained road in Scotland.

Beginning opposite Stirling Central Library, a 2.8-mile (4.5km) loop packs in history and charm, tracing the old city walls adjacent to Stirling Castle, running around the base of Castle Rock and up to the Old Town.

It also links well with the Gowan Hill Heritage Trail, which takes in landmarks such as Mote Hill, site of the Beheading Stone.

Pit stop: Vera Artisan Bakery and Kitchen (51 King Street) serves all-day, brunch-style dishes including eggs, pancakes, French toast and filled croissants. It has a dog menu too.

## **Dalzell Estate, Motherwell, Lanarkshire**

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21268889.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dalzell Estate, Motherwell *(Image: NLC)*

On the southern outskirts of Motherwell, the Dalzell Estate is a balm for the soul. I’m particularly fond of the magnificent arboretum and wider tree-lined trails, which take in the Covenanters Oak, reputed to be more than 800 years old.

Volunteers have worked tirelessly in recent times to rejuvenate the Japanese Garden which – originally laid out in the early 1920s to resemble those of the Temple of Buddha at Nagasaki – is looking spellbinding.

If you wish to further stretch the legs, the paths connect with the Clyde Walkway and RSPB Baron’s Haugh nature reserve (don’t be surprised if you see a lot of folk dressed in camouflage and carrying big cameras – it is popular with twitchers).

Options include the 2.5-mile (4km) Woodland Route and the 1.5-mile (2.5km) Wetland Route, which deliver exactly what they say on the tin.

Pit stop: Papercup Motherwell (77 Manse Road) is around half-a-mile (1km) on foot. The menu features hearty breakfast rolls and delicious topped toasts. Dogs welcome.
