# Devolution, AI and asylum accommodation: what the Budget means for Yorkshire

## Here’s everything in the Budget that affects Yorkshire.

### **Fiscal devolution**

This will not see mayors get any additional money at first, however it will give them greater flexibility over how they spend it. It also means they can borrow against future earnings, as they have a steady income stream, which will help major projects like the West Yorkshire mass transit and Doncaster Sheffield Airport.

### **£150m for asylum accommodation**

The Government is currently attempting to close all asylum hotels and move migrants into old military bases. The former RAF Linton-on-Ouse has been identified by the Home Office as a site for this - it appears Mr Healey has put the funding in place.

### **A66 dualling to start next month**

However, while the scheme was previously expected to be delivered over five years, it will now be spread over nine years, with the entire route not expected to be fully upgraded until 2035.

![The new walkway planned for Leeds Station. Credit: DfT](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/28/18/23/LESP-new-walkway.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


### **Additional £200m for Leeds Station upgrade**

Leeds Station has long struggled with capacity issues, and has been the worst in the UK for delays in recent years. The funding will help add three new entrances and exits to Leeds South Bank, the site of one of the Government’s new towns. It will also create better flow of passengers, with a number of additional bridges added.

### **Funding for Leeds College of Building**

Mr Healey also announced that £15m was going to the Leeds College of Building, to accommodate more than 2,000 additional students.

This was celebrated by the MPs which cover LCB’s two campuses. Leeds North West’s Katie White said: “This is exactly what the Prime Minister meant when he promised good growth in every postcode: investing in people, creating opportunity, and backing Leeds to succeed.”

Leeds Central and Headingley’s Alex Sobel added: “Leeds now has the opportunity to become the building capital of the UK.”

![Leeds North West MP Katie White meets with Sir David Attenborough in Parliament.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2025/09/09/14/56/Katie-White-MP-with-Sir-David-Attenborough.jpeg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


### **£1.5bn borrowing facility for mayors**

He said: “I am announcing flexible funding to invest in local economies, boosted by a further £1.5 billion of devolved loans for mayoral authorities—funds that will back projects such as … the reopening of Doncaster airport, led by the Mayor of South Yorkshire.”

### **Talks ongoing over mineworkers’ pensions**

The Government is in talks with coalfield MPs to reach an agreement to transfer the remaining surplus from mineworkers’ pension schemes.

In the Budget, Mr Healey, who represents a former mining constituency, said: “I’m guaranteeing that every penny of the pension fund surplus now goes to miners.” However, PoliticsHome has reported that trustees of the scheme are not content with the process.

### **£20bn to Leeds-based institutions**

The Chancellor is providing an additional £15.4bn of capital to the Leeds-headquartered NWF, which is to increase its focus on investing in projects and companies connected to defence, alongside critical minerals and artificial intelligence and its supply chains.

**Funding for Don Valley Corridor**

John Healey announced that £3m is going to match funding for specialist innovation space for early-stage companies in the Don Valley Corridor, in South Yorkshire. He said this would help unleash Britain’s innovation power. Mayor Oliver Coppard said this would support his region’s “budding, burgeoning tech sector”.

**AI funding**

Yorkshire’s three Labour mayors lobbied hard to receive £30m in funding to pilot local partnerships in the style of the Barnsley tech town in North, West and South Yorkshire.

Tracy Brabin said: “This funding will help us shape how AI is adopted across our region, supporting good jobs, more effective public services and helping unlock the next generation of growth for West Yorkshire's thriving digital and tech sector.”
