Comedy favourite Harry Enfield is bringing some of his best-known characters to Darlington and Stockton as part of a national tour looking back over four decades of his career.

The BAFTA-winning comedian will perform Harry Enfield and No Chums at Darlington Hippodrome on November 11 before appearing at The Globe Theatre, Stockton, on November 21.

He will revisitcharacters including Loadsamoney, Stavros, Wayne and Waynetta Slob and reflecting on the real-life inspirations behind them.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21372070.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Harry Enfield brings Loadsamoney, Stavros, Wayne and Waynetta Slob to Darlington Hippodrome on November 11 and Stockton’s Globe Theatre on November 21. *(Image: Supplied)*

Described as part autobiographical exploration and part character comedy, the show blends storytelling and classic sketches with social commentary on the changing British landscape.

Enfield, 64, is known for his collaborations with Paul Whitehouse and for creating characters that have become touchstones of British humour, such as Kevin the Teenager, Smashie and Nicey, and Mr Cholmondley-Warner.

The tour marks his first solo stage outing since 2015, when he last performed live alongside Whitehouse.

Mr Enfield said: "These folks from Fane Productions approached me. It sounded fun.

"You know, if people ask me to do something, I tend to do it.

"Otherwise, I'm just quite happy sort of pottering around, really."

He admitted to nerves in his early live performances but said his attitude has changed with age.

Mr Enfield said: "When I was young and used to do characters in clubs I was really nervous.

"That's why I stopped as soon as I could.

"Now I'm so old I don't care anymore.

"The tour with Paul was really fun, but we spent all our money on making it a slick show with loads of costumes and sets.

"I said to Paul afterwards people would love it if we just chatted and did a bit of this or whatever as a surprise.

"The next thing I know, he was doing that with The Fast Show reunion tour."

Fans can look forward to the return of many classic characters, including DJ Nicey, who now hosts a podcast described by Enfield as "very, very, very right-wing."

Other returning favourites include Angry Frank, as well as some new material.

He will also perform one of The Surgeons from his BBC series Harry & Paul, a character inspired by a real person.

Mr Enfield said: "I'll be doing one of The Surgeons that I did in the BBC series Harry & Paul.

"He's based on someone I met who told me stories about his life that were absolutely heartbreaking.

"His daughter died, and he said: 'and then she sadly died, ah boo-hoo.'

"There's a whole bunch of people in this country, sent to prep school aged seven and if they blubbed for their mummies they were beaten.

"So anything that's happened to them in their life, they just go, 'ah boo-hoo,' and carry on."

Enfield believes Loadsamoney remains as relevant as ever in today’s climate of financial inequality.

He said: "Yes, money is everything now.

"The rich have got richer.

"The poor have got poorer.

"I imagine that he now runs Pimlico Plasterers and he's gone to live in Dubai in case Labour tax him more."

Reflecting on his body of work while preparing for the tour, Enfield said he was pleasantly surprised by how well some of it has aged.

He said: "Yes. I thought they were really funny.

"Which surprised me, because at the time you do these things and you move on and don't really think about it anymore, but a lot of it I thought was really well written.

"We really got something right about a lot of things.

"And now I can see when I watch that stuff that Paul was a fucking genius."

He expressed particular fondness for Wayne and Waynetta Slob, who will also feature in the show.

"I love them all, actually. I'm very proud of them.

"I do a bit of Wayne and Waynetta in the show, they are probably great, great, great grandparents by now."
