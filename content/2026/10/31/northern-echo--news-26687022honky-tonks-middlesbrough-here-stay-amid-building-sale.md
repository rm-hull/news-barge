A country bar is ‘here to stay’ as the building hits the market - with bosses announcing two new floors are set to be revamped.

Texas-style bar Honky Tonk’s, which has been in Middlesbrough since September 2024, will be rebranding the top two floors of the Grade II listed building.

The venue, formerly Lloyds Bank and later the Ayresome Park pub, is working with Jordan Jefferson, the outgoing manager of The Middlesbrough Empire, on the project.

Honky Tonk’s bought the entire building in September last year before expanding and opening a sports bar upstairs in February.

Bosses said the building is being sold with Honky Tonk’s as sitting tenants, meaning the buyer ‘will have the pleasure of Honky Tonk’s for many years to come’.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641231.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Honky Tonk's, on Albert Road in Middlesbrough

‘We look forward to welcoming you all for many years to come,’ the venue said on Facebook.

Known for its live country music, line dancing, and cowboy hats, Honky Tonk's has grown in popularity in the last two years.

North East entrepreneurs Glenn Bartlett and Michael Kitson started the venture after Glenn visited a country music bar in Las Vegas.

Glenn previously said he had ambitious plans at the time to open as many as ten Honky Tonk’s and had his eyes on Darlington and Newcastle.

It previously had venues in both Guisborough and Stockton but decided to close them when it acquired the Middlesbrough building in a ‘strategic decision’.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641230.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Honky Tonk's, on Albert Road in Middlesbrough

Before Honky Tonk’s, the venue had several names, including the Mint nightclub and, before that, Haze.

The building is being sold freehold, so the buyer would own the property and the land beneath it indefinitely.

The listing describes it as an "exceptional opportunity" with "significant scale, character and flexible potential for commercial, leisure, hospitality or mixed-use redevelopment".

The building spans about 20,000 sq ft across five levels, including the Honky Tonk’s bar, an upstairs sports bar and a second-floor bar and dance floor.

The third floor contains accommodation, while the basement has extensive storage and cellar space serving the floors above.
