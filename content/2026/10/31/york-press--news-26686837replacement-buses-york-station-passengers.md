## Replacement buses for most passengers at York station for consecutive weekend

York Railway Station was virtually deserted at 8am this morning with buses replacement most departing services all weekend due to engineering works on key routes in the area *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Major engineering works mean buses are replacing the majority of trains from York for a second weekend.

Rail passengers were queuing outside the station this morning (Saturday, October 31) as works taking place on a key section between Leeds and York, and between York and Darlington, closed all lines on those routes.

TransPennine services between York and Scarborough will run normally.




Engineers are carrying out essential East Coast Main Line upgrades between York and Darlington and the Transpennine Route Upgrade (TRU) project in the Church Fenton area is also continuing.

Network Rail said the upgrade work includes track renewals – totalling almost 2.6km – between York, the Colton Junction, Holgate Junction and Skelton Junction.

![Fitting new tracks on the station bypass/freight line in Holgate](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21654315.jpg?type=mds-article-620)New tracks were being fitted this morning (Saturday, October 30) on the station bypass and freight line in Holgate *(Image: Haydn Lewis)*

There will also be upgrades to overhead line equipment between York and the Colton Junction and across York station platforms.

For today and tomorrow, National Rail said services are significantly impacted for passengers travelling on CrossCountry, Grand Central, LNER, Lumo, Northern, and most TransPennine Express routes.

LNER will run replacement buses between Doncaster and Darlington, and between Wakefield Westgate and York.

Northern services will be suspended between Moorthorpe, Micklefield, Selby, Poppleton, and York, with rail replacement buses running instead.

TransPennine Express will operate replacement buses between Leeds and York.

Passengers are advised to plan ahead and allow extra time for journeys.

Further details on specific routes and replacement services are available on train operators’ websites.

Passengers are encouraged to check before they travel using National Rail Enquiries or their train operator’s journey planning tools.

Network Rail and LNER’s joint operations director, Gunnar Lindahl, said the works will make the East Coast Main Line, between Edinburgh and London, “more reliable for passengers and freight customers”.

“These upgrades will help reduce the need for unplanned repairs, improve performance and support a more reliable railway for years to come”.

“We know these works will mean some journeys take longer than usual or involve a rail replacement bus. We’re sorry for any inconvenience caused and we thank passengers for their patience while we complete these important improvements.”

A spokesperson for the train operating companies said: “We’re working closely with Network Rail and industry partners to help customers travel during these important railway improvements.

“Anyone planning to travel should check before they set off, as many services will be amended and some journeys may take longer than usual or involve a rail replacement bus.”
