More than 100 workers are preparing to tackle snow and ice across County Durham as the council launches its winter roads operation.

Durham County Council has invested more than £5.2 million in its winter maintenance service this year to keep roads clear and safe during severe weather.

The operation runs from now until April and involves a team of 68 council employees and 44 contracted staff, including gritter and snow plough drivers, snow blower operators, duty managers, highway inspectors, and fleet mechanics.

Councillor Tim McGuinness, cabinet member for rural, farming, and transport at Durham County Council, said: "Winter maintenance is about much more than spreading salt.

"While many people see the gritters out on the roads, there is a much bigger operation working behind the scenes to monitor weather forecasts and decide what treatment is needed and when.

"By treating roads before ice forms and responding quickly when conditions change, we're helping to keep residents safe and the county moving."

The council’s gritting network prioritises the county’s busiest and most essential routes, including major roads, public transport corridors, and roads used by emergency services.

The aim is to keep roads accessible for commuters, school transport, businesses, and critical services throughout the winter months.

The wider winter operation also includes footpath gritter operators and weather forecasters who help inform and guide daily decisions.

Residents can access information about gritting routes, winter maintenance activities, and safety advice for travelling during severe weather by visiting www.durham.gov.uk/winterinfo.
