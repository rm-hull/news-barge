A lifeboat crew were left scratching their heads after deploying to investigate apparent lights shining from a yacht in North Berwick's West Bay on Friday night.

Volunteers from North Berwick RNLI launched the charity's D class inshore lifeboat, SUNIJO, at 10.03pm after concerns were raised about lights seen on board a vessel moored in the bay.

Police and Coastguard Rescue Teams initially responded to the reports and, after occasional sightings of what appeared to be a torch on board, the lifeboat was tasked to investigate.

The vessel's owner was confirmed not to be on board, prompting further concern and a search of the area.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21654716.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Volunteers at North Berwick RNLI launched the charity’s D class inshore lifeboat on Friday night *(Image: RNLI)*

Using powerful searchlights and night vision equipment, the crew carried out a thorough search of vessels moored in the bay, as well as the surrounding water and nearby rocks.

However, despite an extensive sweep of the area, no-one was found and there was no sign of anyone in difficulty.

RNLI volunteers later concluded that the mysterious lights had most likely been reflections from shore lights shining through the yacht's cabin windows, creating the illusion of torchlight as the vessel moved on the water.

With no casualties identified and no danger detected, the lifeboat was stood down and returned to station.

Matt Gibbons, helm of the lifeboat, said: "On the eve of a night traditionally associated with mystery and folklore there was lots of talk about Burns' 'Spunkies' or water-kelpies when we came back ashore. However in this case, a Halloween mystery at sea proved to be a trick of the light."

He added: "Thankfully, there were no ghosts or ghouls to contend with – just reflections. We'd like to thank the first informant for their alertness and remind the public that in any coastal emergency they should dial 999 and ask for the Coastguard."
