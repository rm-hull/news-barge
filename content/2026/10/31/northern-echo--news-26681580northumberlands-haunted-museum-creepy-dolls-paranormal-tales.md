A free museum dedicated to preserving the North East's paranormal history is attracting attention across the region through its eerie artefacts and creepy dolls.

Northumberland Haunted Museum, on Queen Street, Amble, is a free museum dedicated to preserving folklore, artefacts and spooky history across Northumberland and the North East.

The museum says many local ghost stories have been passed down through generations, but are at risk of being forgotten as families move away and memories fade.

Its displays bring together haunted artefacts, documented paranormal cases, personal stories and objects linked to unexplained creepy activity in the region.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646718.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The museum's *(Image: TRIPADVISOR)*

A key attraction is the upstairs Victorian children's room, which contains dolls and teddies with backstories and information about the tales surrounding them.

The collection also uses a traffic-light system, ranging from positive attachments to accounts described as particularly disturbing.

Another room contains a single doll, which the museum says is kept separately.

Other exhibits include Ouija boards, tarot cards, old documents and displays about witchcraft and folk magic.

A vampire-slaying kit, complete with a wooden stake, is also featured.

The objects are displayed alongside stories and explanations about their history.

North East lifestyle influencer Chloe, known online as New Girl In Toon, recently visited the museum and shared a video of her trip online, describing the attraction as a place for believers, sceptics and people who are unsure about the paranormal alike.

She praised the "real emphasis on the people and stories behind the objects," rather than the museum being "rooms full of creepy stuff" thrown together for maximum creepiness.

The social media creator also highlighted the museum's wider programme of activities, which includes ghost walks, paranormal investigations and evening workshops.

The museum describes its approach as combining history with the unexplained. It aims to offer an experience that is immersive, educational, respectful and interactive, while recognising the stories connected to each artefact.

Organisers say the project was built with support from crowd funders, local historians, paranormal investigators and families who shared their experiences and belongings.

They want to continue collecting stories from North East communities and provide opportunities for people to record their own accounts.

Visitors have also praised the staff and the balance between local history and supernatural stories.

One reviewer wrote: "If you're looking for a unique experience, I can't recommend Northumberland Haunted Museum highly enough!

"The staff were absolutely amazing- so welcoming, passionate, and incredibly insightful."

The museum is open to visitors Thursday to Monday 10am to 4pm, and closed on Tuesdays and Wednesdays.

With free daytime entry and a programme of ghost walks, paranormal investigations and workshops, the museum joins the ever-growing programme of events and experiences the North East has to offer this Halloween season.

Just don't be surprised if you find yourself hurrying past the Victorian doll room on your way out.
