Red has never been a timid colour, and it has never needed to be. It belongs to ripe winter fruit and sealing wax, to velvet theatre seats and ribbon-tied parcels, to the glow of a hearth at the far end of a room. It sits at the warm end of the spectrum, and the eye responds accordingly. Placed beside the face, in the soft focus of cashmere or lambswool, it lends colour to the complexion and lightness to the mood.

At Edinburgh Cashmere, our red scarves are handcrafted in cashmere and lambswool across a considered range of designs, from solid shades to Scottish tartan and check. Each is chosen for its softness and for the particular warmth that red brings to a winter wardrobe.

### **Why red suits winter so well**

Red earns its place in winter for practical reasons as much as poetic ones. Its saturation holds its own against the muted tones of the season, so it never disappears beneath a coat or a grey sky. Its warmth is visual before it is physical, and it brightens the face in low light. It is also remarkably versatile: in the right proportion, red settles into a wardrobe as readily as navy or grey.

A single red scarf, worn with an otherwise quiet outfit, becomes the focal point of the whole look, and a good coat and an unhurried walk are all it needs.

### **Choosing between cashmere and lambswool**

Both fibres suit red beautifully, and the choice is a matter of temperament. Cashmere is the more refined, with a lightness and softness that make it a natural partner for tailoring, evening wear and anything that rewards a fine finish. Lambswool is the more companionable, with a cosy, slightly sturdier character that suits everyday wear, long walks and layers over chunkier knits. Many wardrobes find room for both, a cashmere red for the days that call for polish and a lambswool red for the days that call for comfort.

### **The way a red scarf is worn**

A scarf is as much about gesture as fabric, and red rewards a little attention to how it falls.

**The loose drape** - Draped long over the shoulders with the ends hanging free, a red scarf lends an easy, unstudied elegance, particularly over a tailored coat.

**The single loop** - One loop at the neck, with the ends left to fall. It is the simplest arrangement and often the most flattering, a line of colour that frames the face.

**The Parisian knot** - Perhaps the most elegant way to wear a scarf in the cold. Folded in half lengthwise and draped around the neck, the scarf has its loose ends threaded through the loop and gently drawn snug. The result is neat, close and wonderfully warm, with a small, sculptural knot of red at the throat. It suits lambswool especially well, as the fibre's cosy body holds the knot beautifully.

**The knot at the collar** - Folded once and tied loosely, a scarf becomes a small point of focus at the collar, sitting neatly beneath a jacket or coat.

**Wrapped against the wind** - On the sharpest mornings, a scarf can be drawn up over the nose, mouth and ears, which is when its warmth truly earns its place.

### **Colours that keep red company**

Red is a generous colour. It flatters its neighbours, and a handful of pairings in particular bring out its best.

**Camel** - Red and camel are a classic winter combination, warm against warm, the red adding a spark to a soft neutral coat.

**Navy** - Against deep navy, red takes on a crisp clarity, a clean line of colour at the collar.

**Charcoal and black** - Red is at its most vivid against dark wool, a single bright note that draws the eye and holds it.

**Denim and white** - For weekends, a red scarf over a white shirt or a denim jacket lends an easy, relaxed polish.

**Grey** - Grey knitwear gives red room to glow, and the combination feels at once quiet and confident.

**Deep green** - Red beside evergreen is the palette of holly and berries, familiar and festive, and entirely at home from the first week of December.

### **Red through the season**

Red moves easily through the winter months. In the first cool weeks it echoes the last of the turning leaves. By midwinter it keeps company with evergreen and candlelight, at the theatre, at a long dinner, on the walk home beneath the streetlamps. And in the long grey stretch that follows, it offers a welcome lift when the days begin to feel endless.
