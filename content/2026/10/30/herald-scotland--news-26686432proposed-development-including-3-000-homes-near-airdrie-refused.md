North Lanarkshire councillors have refused planning permission for a proposed development including thousands of homes near Airdrie.

The council held a planning hearing concerning Macrocom’s plans to construct up to 3,000 homes, as well as large storage and distribution units, four mixed-use centres with retail, commercial, leisure and community uses, a primary school and nursery, a hotel and various types of infrastructure on a 370-acre site near Airdrie and the villages of Plains, Wattston and Stand.

Earlier versions of the proposals also included a drive-through restaurant but it was clarified that this element had been deleted. Some 150 of the proposed homes would be affordable housing.

A report on the application recommended it be refused for multiple reasons, including contravention of green belt and countryside policy, the design of the town centre, high probability that residents would be poorly served by public transport and therefore reliant on cars and generating excessive amounts of traffic, and that allowing this development would undermine the council’s efforts to promote the regeneration of brownfield sites.

These traffic concerns were reflected in an objection from the council’s transport officers, especially as it was proposed to build some of the new development before the East Airdrie Link Road is completed. Additionally there were 18 objections from the public, citing impacts on the amenity of local residents, the environment, traffic and the sustainability of the proposal.

A representative for the applicant said that the site could be classed as brownfield due to an extensive history of mining works, and that it was “low quality” countryside already allocated for major industrial development. He added that the area was being transformed with the development of the East Airdrie Link Road and the Monklands Hospital replacement, and that in these situations case law allowed councils to support development not included in the local plan.

He added that the proposed housing would be important for people working at the new hospital and that development could be phased in agreement with the council while environmental harm would be limited and no objections had been received which could not be resolved through further discussion and conditions, including the concerns over transport, for example by funding bus services.

Other benefits highlighted by the representative included decentralised energy networks, as well as the creation of 2,200 jobs and a 70 hectare nature reserve.

No objectors attended the hearing to speak against the proposals and the applicants’ representative said that public consultations had been encouraging with the majority of people either supporting the development or raising no concerns.

The session concluded by moving into a special hearing of the full council, in which Councillor Barry McCulloch (Labour, Cumbernauld East) moved to refuse the application, citing the reasons given in the report.

Councillor Michael McPake (Labour, Gartcosh, Glenboig and Moodiesburn) called for the application to be granted, saying the council needed to be bold and the East Airdrie Link Road was one of the biggest projects for North Lanarkshire under the City Region Deal.

“Whether this application today goes through or not, that whole area is going to be developed,” he said. He said the applicant had presented this application very well and he felt it had ticked all the boxes.

The council voted to refuse the application with 37 votes to 18.
