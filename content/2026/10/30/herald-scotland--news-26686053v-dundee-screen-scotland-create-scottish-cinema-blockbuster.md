V&A Dundee is to become home to a major celebration of Scottish cinema, The Herald can reveal.

The project, which is being described as “an ambitious celebration of the story of cinema from and of Scotland”, is being developed under a new partnership with the Scottish Government’s film and TV agency.

The collaboration is expected to lead to V&A Dundee staging a blockbuster exhibition charting Scotland’s big screen heritage within the next two years.

V&A has revealed first details of the project ahead of the launch next week of a new exhibition celebrating the life and work of David Bowie.

Special events, conferences and screenings are expected to be held at the waterfront museum as part of the cinema project, which is expected to take shape in the run-up to V&A Dundee’s 10th anniversary in 2028.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20820030.jpg?type=mds-article-620)V&A Dundee director Leonie Bell says the museum is working on an 'ambitious celebration of the story of cinema from and of Scotland'. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

V&A Dundee says the project, which is expected to run until early 2029, will explore “the cultural and historical relationship between Scotland and cinema”.

Screen Scotland told The Herald it had committed initial funding of £111,000 to support the development of the film project.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21653471.jpg?type=mds-article-620)V&A Dundee is working on a major celebration of Scotland's cinema history. *(Image: Hufton & Crow)*

The museum has started recruiting for a programme leader with “major exhibition” experience for what it is describing as “a major national partnership project” with Screen Scotland.

The celebration of Scottish cinema at V&A Dundee is expected to be the biggest new exhibition launched at the venue since its celebration of the history of tartan in 2023.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20820474.jpg?type=mds-article-620)V&A Dundee director Leonie Bell says the museum is working on an 'ambitious celebration of the story of cinema from and of Scotland'. *(Image: Julie Howden)*

The partnership has emerged months after V&A revealed plans to “reimagine and remodel” the museum in time for its 10th anniversary year.

New star attractions are expected to come to Dundee from the V&A’s long-running museum in South Kensington in London and other collections around the world.

More than 2.6 million visitors have been recorded since V&A Dundee opened its doors less than eight years ago, with nearly 330,000 visits recorded during 2025 alone.

Tourism visits to Dundee are said to have increased by more than 61 per cent, to around 1.4 million annual visits, since the building welcomed its first visitors in September 2018.

V&A Dundee hopes to raise more than £5m for the 10th anniversary revamp, which is aimed at creating a “more dynamic visitor experience” throughout the building, which was designed by Japanese architect Kengo Kuma.

The UK Government has already committed £2.6m in funding to help pay for the forthcoming transformation of V&A Dundee, which gets £4m in annual funding from the Scottish Government.

V&A Dundee has previously announced plans to honour Scottish screen star Alan Cumming in the new-look museum by displaying one of his tartan outfits won in the American version of The Traitors.

Screen Scotland’s partnership with V&A Dundee has emerged months after the Scottish Government confirmed it planned to remove the screen agency from Creative Scotland and relaunch it as a stand-alone body.

Screen Scotland is already leading efforts to create a new event to “replace” the annual TV Festival, which was staged during Edinburgh’s summer festivals for 50 years, after it announced it would be relocating to Greater Manchester.

Baby Reindeer and Half Man creator Richard Gadd launched the new venture at the new headquarters of the Edinburgh Festival Fringe Society.

The plans for a “major exhibition” at V&A Dundee are understood to have been under discussion between the attraction and Screen Scotland for at least six months.

V&A Dundee’s recruitment advert for the new role to lead the film project states that it would welcome applications from people currently working within the Scottish screen industry.

However, it said it was looking for people with experience of planning and delivering “complex, multi-partner programmes of public activity, such as exhibitions, festivals, events or learning programmes”.

Applicants should have “genuine interest in V&A Dundee's work and in Scottish film and screen culture”.

The recruitment advert states: “We are Scotland’s design museum. Designed for everyone, firing imaginations and sparking curiosity in design across Scotland and around the world. At V&A Dundee we are on a mission to inspire and empower through design.

“Design shapes our world, it is part of everyday life and it is everywhere.

“We are a museum with energy and have a vision of the future where everyone is inspired through design and recognises its far-reaching impact on our lives.

“The post will lead the delivery of a major national partnership project exploring the cultural and historical relationship between Scotland and cinema.

“V&A Dundee and Screen Scotland are seeking a programme lead who will strategically drive the national partnership opportunities, manage stakeholders and oversee the operational aspects of the project's development.”

V&A Dundee director Leonie Bell said the partnership project with Screen Scotland was still at an "early stage".

She told The Herald: “We are delighted to be working in partnership with Screen Scotland to develop a national project exploring the cultural and historical relationship between Scotland and cinema.

“The job we’re jointly advertising will be integral to shaping this project.

“We look forward to engaging audiences across Scotland, and at V&A Dundee, on an ambitious celebration of the story of cinema from and of Scotland.”

V&A Dundee's partnership with Screen Scotland has emerged days after Scottish Government culture secretary Màiri McAllan hailed the museum as "an absolutely outstanding organisation".

Speaking at Holyrood's culture committee, she said: "I have been a great fan and enjoyer of the V&A since before I came into post.

"Since becoming culture secretary, I have seen cutting-edge work at the V&A. It makes me so proud to visit it, to see the beautifully regenerated area at the waterfront and the wonderful array of exhibitions that they put on.

"I cannot recognise the V&A thoroughly enough. That is reflected in in the fact we have provided increased funding for the museum.

"We know that significant national and international activity is required to sustain something of the V&A’s scale and offer."
