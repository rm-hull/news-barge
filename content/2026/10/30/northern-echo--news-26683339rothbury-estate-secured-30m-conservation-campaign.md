Sir David Attenborough has thanked thousands of people who backed a £30m fundraising campaign to save a vast Northumberland estate, after conservation charities completed a landmark deal to secure its future.

The renowned naturalist and broadcaster hailed the successful purchase of the 9,500-acre Rothbury Estate as an opportunity to create "something very special for generations to come".

The Wildlife Trusts and Northumberland Wildlife Trust confirmed on Friday (October 30) that they had completed the purchase of the entire estate, bringing to an end a two-year fundraising campaign backed by more than 25,000 people.

The deal means the charities can now press ahead with ambitious plans to restore wildlife habitats, support nature-friendly farming and establish a 40-mile nature corridor stretching from the Northumberland coast to Kielder Forest.

![Sir David Attenborough](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20560499.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir David Attenborough *(Image: BBC/PA Wire)*

Sir David, who threw his support behind the campaign last year and previously described the landscape as a "very special place", praised those who had helped make the purchase possible.

He said: "The Wildlife Trusts called for your support and you answered in your thousands.

"The appeal to secure the future of The Rothbury Estate in Northumberland has been successful and now the opportunity to bring the vision of a landscape where nature and people thrive side by side can become a reality.

"This will be a place where nature, community, farming and heritage can combine to create something very special for generations to come.

"Thank you very much."

The estate, which includes the Simonside Hills, was put up for sale three years ago by the family of the Duke of Northumberland, raising concerns about the possibility of the land being divided into multiple ownerships.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21649380.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lordenshaw Hill Fort on the Rothbury Estate *(Image: The Wildlife Trusts/Press Association)*

Conservationists launched an ambitious campaign in 2024 to secure the entire landscape and protect its future.

Sir David's intervention proved a major turning point, generating more than £1 million in donations in just one week.

In total, members of the public contributed more than £17 million, with donations coming from across the UK and more than 50 countries, including Finland, Barbados, Kenya and New Zealand.

Local organisations including Rothbury WI, the Cycling Cranks cycling club and Curvy Sounds guitar shop also helped raise funds.

A £5 million grant from The National Lottery Heritage Fund in March took the campaign beyond its halfway point, while the Government pledged a further £1.94 million this month.

The Government announced its support at the COP17 global biodiversity summit, recognising the estate's potential contribution towards the international target of protecting 30 per cent of land and sea for nature by 2030.

The charities have also taken out a low-interest loan to cover a short-term gap between pledged funding still to be received and the amount needed to complete the purchase.

With the entire estate now secured, attention will turn to transforming the landscape into a major centre for nature recovery.

The Wildlife Trusts hope to restore habitats across the estate and link them with other important wildlife sites in Northumberland, creating a continuous corridor for nature stretching approximately 40 miles from the coast towards the Scottish border.

Longer-term ambitions have included the possible reintroduction of species such as beavers, bison and golden eagles, although no firm plans for their return have been confirmed.

The charities say they want to work closely with local communities and farmers to develop a landscape where wildlife recovery and sustainable agriculture can operate alongside one another.

However, concerns have previously been raised locally about how the conservation plans could affect existing farming operations.

Mike Pratt, chief executive of Northumberland Wildlife Trust, described the purchase as a defining moment for the region.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21649381.jpg?type=mds-article-620)River Coquet on the Rothbury Estate *(Image: The Wildlife Trusts/Press Association)*

He said: "What a remarkable achievement. The completion of The Rothbury Estate purchase is a defining moment for Northumberland and the North East.

"Opportunities of this scale and significance are incredibly rare. By securing this extraordinary landscape, we've the potential to create a flagship for nature recovery supported by sustainable farming, whilst delivering lasting benefits for wildlife, local people and the rural economy for generations to come."

He added: "It has been truly humbling to witness the support behind this vision. From individual donors to major funders and partners, people have come together in a shared belief that we can create something exceptional here."

Eilish McGuinness, chief executive of The National Lottery Heritage Fund, welcomed the deal, describing the estate as a nationally significant landscape.

She said: "It is fantastic news that The Rothbury Estate has been successfully acquired by The Wildlife Trusts thanks to money raised by National Lottery players.

"By working with local communities to develop new approaches to land management, this transformational project has the potential to deliver nature recovery at a grand scale, helping to reconnect people to the unique natural and cultural heritage of this remarkable and nationally significant landscape."

Work has already begun on parts of the estate purchased by the charities in October 2024, with volunteers, archaeologists, birdwatchers, academics and community groups helping to survey its wildlife and heritage.

Further projects are due to launch in November, including reptile surveys, soil health testing and acoustic monitoring to better understand the landscape and guide future restoration work.
