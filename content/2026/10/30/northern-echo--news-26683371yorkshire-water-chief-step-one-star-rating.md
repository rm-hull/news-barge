The top boss of Yorkshire Water has announced she will step down, just 24 hours after the company was handed its lowest-ever environmental performance rating by the Environment Agency.

Chief executive Nicola Shaw said she was "extremely proud" of what she'd achieved during her time in charge of the utility firm, despite it being branded a "poor performing" water company by the Environment Agency on Thursday (October 29).

The firm receiving just one star out of four in its annual Environmental Performance Assessment (EPA).

Shaw will leave her role after four-and-a-half years, with Nicola Medalova appointed as CEO designate ahead of taking over permanently on January 1, 2027.

Yorkshire Water said the succession plan forms part of an "orderly transition", with Medalova joining the company on November 2 and working alongside Shaw during a handover period.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21649428.jpg?type=mds-article-620) Nicola Shaw *(Image: SUBMIT)*

The regulator recorded 13 serious pollution incidents involving Yorkshire Water during 2025, the second-highest total in England behind Thames Water, while the company also reported 335 pollution incidents overall during the year.

Yorkshire Water was one of four companies to receive the lowest possible environmental rating, alongside Southern Water, South West Water and Thames Water.

Despite the timing of the leadership announcement, Yorkshire Water said the change had been planned as part of a structured succession process.

Paying tribute to Shaw, Yorkshire Water chairman Jon Lewis said she had made an important contribution during her time in charge.

He said: "Under her leadership, the company secured £500m of new shareholder equity, with a further £600m committed, enabling us to embark on the largest investment programme in our history and accelerate work to strengthen the resilience of Yorkshire's water network.

"She leaves the business with strong foundations for the next phase of investment and improvement."

Lewis said Medalova was an "accomplished and inspiring leader" with significant operational experience and a strong track record of delivering major infrastructure programmes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21649423.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nicola Medalova *(Image: YORKSHIRE WATER)*

He added: "The board believes she is the right person to lead the company through its next phase, improving service for customers, reducing pollution and delivering the investment needed to modernise Yorkshire's water network."

Shaw said it had been a privilege to lead the company and thanked colleagues across Yorkshire for their support and commitment.

She said: "Although there remains a huge amount of work to do, I am extremely proud of what our colleagues have achieved, putting the business on a stronger footing for delivery across all aspects of its work.

"This now feels like the right time to begin an orderly transition to the company's next chapter."

The outgoing chief executive said she looked forward to working closely with Medalova over the coming months to ensure a seamless handover.

Medalova joins Yorkshire Water from National Grid, where she held a number of senior leadership positions, most recently serving as Chief of Staff to the group's chief executive.

She previously spent time as Chief Operating Officer of National Grid New England, overseeing a 4,000-person business and helping deliver a major operational turnaround while preparing the organisation for billions of pounds of investment.

She has also served as a non-executive director on Yorkshire Water's board, giving her direct experience of the challenges facing both the company and the wider water industry.

Speaking about her appointment, Medalova said: "Yorkshire Water faces huge challenges but also great opportunities for continued improvement, and I look forward to working with Nicola Shaw, the leadership team and colleagues across the business as I get up to speed.

"My immediate priority will be to listen and learn, while maintaining a relentless focus on delivery."

The leadership announcement follows renewed scrutiny of Yorkshire Water's environmental performance after this week's Environment Agency assessment.

Campaign group Save Our Swale said it was "not surprised" by the company's downgrade, pointing to a rise in pollution incidents across Yorkshire's rivers and questioning how customer bills had increased while environmental performance had worsened.

Yorkshire Water has acknowledged its performance "was not good enough" and said it has already changed the way it tackles pollution incidents through greater operational focus, improved asset management and earlier identification of problems.
