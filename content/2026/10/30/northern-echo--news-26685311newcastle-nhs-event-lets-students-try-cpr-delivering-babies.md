A hands-on careers event has inspired the next generation of health and social care workers.

Hundreds of students from across the North East took part in the third annual NHS careers event this month, designed to showcase the wide range of roles available within Newcastle hospitals.

The event aimed to inspire young people in Key Stages 4 and 5 by connecting classroom learning with real-world career opportunities.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652132.jpg?type=mds-article-620)This event gives the Trust a rare opportunity to engage directly with its future workforce. *(Image: NE1)*

Two students from Jarrow School, Eadie and Niamh, said: "We came to the event to learn about careers in Health and Social Care and find out about all the jobs and professions in the NHS.

"We are interested in midwifery and nursing and really enjoyed all the activity sessions, especially the CPR and intubation stations where we put tubes into the throat of a dummy.

"The event has really helped us to understand the different roles in the NHS and find out what a typical day looks like in some of these jobs."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652130.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The event highlighted the wide range of career opportunities available across Newcastle Hospitals. *(Image: NE1)*

Organised by NE1 as part of its NE1 CAN programme and held in partnership with Newcastle Hospitals, the event welcomed around 500 students from 20 education providers.

Attendees came from across Newcastle, Durham, Sunderland, Northumberland, and North and South Tyneside.

Kerry McCabe, business network manager at NE1 Ltd, said: "NE1 can offers Newcastle’s business community the rare opportunity to get in front of their potential future workforce and help shape the job candidates of tomorrow.

"These tailored and targeted sessions inform the next generation about the career opportunities available across different business sectors.

"The Big One is the largest event in our calendar delivered in partnership with Newcastle city centre’s largest employer, Newcastle Hospitals.

"The young people who attended the event were engaged and enthusiastic, and all said they were inspired by the NHS staff they met and the opportunities available."

With around 16,000 employees, Newcastle Hospitals is the largest employer in Newcastle city centre.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652124.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The sessions centred on 20 stations, with participants alternating between practical, hands-on activities and interactive discussions. *(Image: NE1)*

The event featured 20 interactive stations, combining practical activities with career talks.

Students took part in hands-on sessions such as delivering a baby using simulated models and learning CPR from trained nurses.

Caroline Docking, director of communication and corporate affairs at Newcastle Hospitals Trust, said: "Newcastle Hospitals is one of the biggest employers in the city and we have an extraordinary range of careers available.

"Events like The Big One give us a fantastic opportunity to open young people’s eyes to those possibilities, from clinical and scientific careers to roles in digital, engineering, finance, communications, estates and many more."

Educators praised the event for its real-world impact.

Janet Leigh, careers advisor from Lord Lawson Academy in Gateshead, said: "NE1 CAN’s Big One event provides a fabulous way to bring STEM to life for our students, giving them practical, hands-on, interactive sessions and the chance to speak directly with professionals from different departments within the NHS."

"These first-hand conversations and insights are really inspiring for students and can help shape the rest of their lives."

Rebecca Thompson from Thorpe Academy in Ryton said: "This is the second time we have attended the BIG ONE event with our students studying Health and Social Care.

"It was wonderful to see our students engage, ask questions, participate in interactive sessions, and learn about the range of opportunities available to them."
