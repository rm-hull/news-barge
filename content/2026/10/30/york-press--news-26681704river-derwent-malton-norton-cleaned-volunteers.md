## Shopping trolleys, bikes and traffic cones recovered from River Derwent

Shopping trolleys, bicycles, road signs, traffic cones and dozens of bags of litter have been hauled from the River Derwent as part of a major community clean-up in Malton and Norton *(Image: Nick Fletcher)*

Shopping trolleys, bicycles, road signs, traffic cones and dozens of bags of litter have been hauled from the River Derwent as part of a major community clean-up in Malton.

Cllr Keane Duncan took to the water with Malton & Norton Canoe Club on last week, while volunteers from Malton & Norton Tidy Group and North Yorkshire Council’s Streetscene team supported the operation from the riverbank.

The three-hour clean-up focused on the stretch of river between County Bridge and Railway Bridge, with a van-load of debris removed from the water and surrounding riverside.

It follows weeks of complaints from residents about debris exposed by the recent low river level, with volunteers and council operatives joining forces to take action.

The operation marks the start of a wider push to restore community pride in the River Derwent, with Cllr Duncan bringing key partners and local organisations together to draw up on an action plan of further improvements.

Cllr Duncan, who represents Norton on North Yorkshire Council, said: “This was a real community operation, with volunteers and the council responding to concerns and joining forces to give the Derwent a much-needed clean.

“It has been such a shame to see our river in its recent state. In just three hours, we were able to tidy the area between the bridges, removing a van-load of debris from the river, from shopping trolleys and bikes to road signs, cones and bags of litter.

“A huge thank you to Malton & Norton Canoe Club, particularly Paul Lister for inviting me into his canoe, the council’s Streetscene team who helped hoist the heaviest items out of the river, and volunteers from Malton & Norton Tidy Group who litter-picked the riverside.

“Our river is looking much better now, with this effort representing a good step forward in restoring community pride in our river. We are now looking to deliver further improvements to the waterway itself, as well as the nearby riverside areas.”

Paul Lister, of Malton & Norton Canoe Club, said: “Malton & Norton Canoe Club has been active on the river in Malton for many years and we regularly see litter in the water and on the riverbanks.

“We try and pick up anything we come across whenever we can. Lots of plastic bottles and drinks cans, but also things like shopping trolleys, bicycles and traffic cones are amongst the things we find.

“A group of us spent a couple of hours on the water in club Canadian canoes armed with litter pickers and rubbish bags, which we quickly filled. Paddling the river means we can see and reach things that others on the banks can’t.

“We were more than happy to help alongside the Tidy Group and North Yorkshire Council. We are always happy to get new members and enjoy the river, which should be enjoyed by all.”

Antony Croser, of Malton & Norton Tidy Group, said: “Volunteers from the Tidy Group were delighted to help with collecting litter from the riverbank and removing the debris gathered up by members of the canoe club.

“The afternoon was a fantastic demonstration of volunteers working alongside North Yorkshire Council to make a positive difference to the community’s environment.”
