Crime victims across County Durham and Darlington will get help through a single service after a shake-up of support, it has been announced.

Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen has launched the Victim Care and Mentor Service, combining the Victim Care and Advice Service with the Community Peer Mentor Service.

The move aims to make support easier to access, including for people who have not reported crimes to police, with practical, emotional and confidential help available.

Ms Allen said: "Victims and their voices are at the heart of everything I do.

"Our dedicated support teams have helped individuals and families navigate some of the most challenging experiences of their lives and many have told us they owe their lives to these services."

The free, independent service helps people cope with the immediate and long-term effects of crime.

It also offers mentoring and practical help to rebuild victims’ confidence and resilience after traumatic events.

Ms Allen said: "We recognise that being a victim of crime can have a profound and lasting impact, emotionally, physically, and psychologically, and has a ripple effect on the wellbeing of our communities.

"For many people, it can be devastating, affecting confidence, mental health, and their overall sense of safety."

She believes timely support can make a crucial difference to a victim’s recovery.

Ms Allen said: "Appropriate, timely intervention can empower victims to cope, recover, and move forward with their lives.

"This belief underpins everything we do and drives our continued commitment to strengthening and improving our service to meet the needs of those we support.

"This is why I am proud to bring these services together under one identity to continue delivering the high-quality care, support, and guidance that victims deserve."

The service is one of the PCC’s six priorities in the Police, Crime and Justice Plan, under the heading “Championing Victims”.

Ms Allen has pledged to give every victim consistent, high-quality support throughout their case and uphold their rights under the Victims’ Code.

A dedicated Roads Policing Unit worker will continue providing specialist support to road traffic collision victims.

The worker provides informed, compassionate support to victims and families during some of the most traumatic moments of their lives.

The PCC’s office will also continue supporting people affected by anti-social behaviour (ASB).

The service helps victims understand their options, cope with the ongoing impact of ASB and access the ASB Case Review Scheme.

The scheme lets victims request a formal review if they believe reports of persistent ASB have not been properly addressed.

Ms Allen and her partners have signed the ASB Help Pledge, promising support for victims of long-term and repeat anti-social behaviour.

The PCC aims to strengthen and unify support for victims across County Durham and Darlington through the new service.

Anyone affected by crime in County Durham or Darlington can access the service, whether or not it has been reported to police.

Support is free, confidential and tailored to each person’s needs.

Contact the Victim Care and Mentor Service on 0800 138 2020 or at [victimcare@durham-pcc.gov.uk.](mailto:victimcare@durham-pcc.gov.uk.)

Find out more at www.victimcareandmentorservice.co.uk.
