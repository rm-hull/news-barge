A towering crane has moved onto a Darlington housing estate as work continues to remove a huge tree which crashed through the roof of a newly-built family home during Storm Boelo.

Contractors were on site at Havelock Park, on Antiquary Drive, on Friday as efforts got under way to carefully dismantle and remove the tree which came crashing down onto the detached property last weekend.

The dramatic incident happened at about 9am on Sunday, October 25, leaving the roof of the newly-completed home severely damaged just weeks before its new owners were due to move in.

When reporters visited the scene, workers were unwilling to comment on the operation, but activity around the property had clearly stepped up as specialist teams arrived to tackle the fallen tree.

A large crane towered above the development, with contractors using it to lift branches from the damaged property.

Several vans belonging to private contractors, including Branch Walkers NE Landscapes, were parked nearby as crews worked around the cordon.

Workers at the scene indicated the job could take as little as a couple of hours, although they warned it could continue throughout the day depending on how the removal operation progresses.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650445.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Storm damage crews begin removing a huge fallen tree from a newly built Darlington home after it crashed through the roof during Storm Boelo. *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

The full extent of the damage remains unknown until the tree has been completely removed.

Once clearance work is finished, inspections will be carried out to determine the scale of repairs required to the property.

The detached house, part of Homes by Esh's Havelock Park development, was unoccupied when the tree came down during the height of Storm Boelo.

Neighbours previously said the new owners had been expected to move into the property next month.

Photographs taken after the collapse showed a large section of the roof torn away, with debris scattered across the site.

A neighbouring fence was also damaged and roof slates landed on a nearby vehicle, smashing its windscreen.

Earlier this week, Darlington Borough Council confirmed the fallen tree was not council-owned and stood on land belonging to the developer.

Homes by Esh said it was working with teams to remove the tree safely before assessing the damage and deciding what to do next.

Thankfully, nobody was injured in the incident.
