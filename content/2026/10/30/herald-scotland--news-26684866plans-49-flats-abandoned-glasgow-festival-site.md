An abandoned Glasgow site, which has been lying empty since the Garden Festival, could be transformed into flats.

The proposed development of 49 homes in Pacific Quay would offer two and three bedroom properties with balconies and a roof terrace for residents.

The bid to build on the rough grassland plot in Govan has sparked 12 objections as locals are worried about traffic, parking and overlooking among other concerns.

Designs show a ground floor shop unit as well as 32 car parking spaces and 60 cycle spaces with two blocks of flats reaching six and five storeys.

A council report said the site has remained “undeveloped since the closure of the Glasgow Garden Festival in 1988” while the wider Pacific Quay has been regenerated with the development of the BBC and STV headquarters as well as the Glasgow Science centre, hotels, offices and homes.

Objecting to the application, MSP Paul Sweeney said the proposal for “island-style pavilions surrounded by surface car parking is completely inappropriate in this inner-city urban location and continues the trend of incoherent planning of Pacific Quay, which is one of the worst-designed waterfront developments in Europe.”

He calls for a “traditional enclosed tenement perimeter block” instead of the two proposed blocks arranged in an L-shaped layout around landscaped grounds.

The site was subject to a previous planning application that was approved.

A design report from the applicant said: “This application does not seek a radical design departure from the prior consented scheme submitted in 2022 and mirrors the massing and footprint of the approved submission.”

Councillors are due to make a decision on the planning application from Surplus Property Investments next week. Glasgow City Council officials are recommending approval.

A council report said the “principle of residential-led mixed-use development on this vacant brownfield site” is considered “acceptable.”

It added: “Overall, the proposal demonstrates a design-led approach that responds positively to its context whilst delivering a high-quality residential-led mixed-use development.

“The scale, layout, massing, architectural treatment, landscaping and material palette are considered appropriate for this prominent location and would contribute positively to the evolving character of Pacific Quay.”
