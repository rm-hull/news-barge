Core. has been, in less-than-hushed tones, the best way to experience Glasgow's heavy, abrasive and off-kilter music since its first weekend in 2023.

That first year's line-up snagged American heavy hitters like Deafheaven and recent noise rock scene favourites Chat Pile, but in 2026 that first line-up feels almost quaint. This year the line-up bolsters acts that have shaped the entire subgenres it draws from, bringing legends as well as promising upcomers from around the world to Maryhill and Glasgow city centre.

Friday is anchored by a UK exclusive performance from screamo legends Saetia. The influential New York cult heroes reunited several years ago after becoming dormant at the turn of the millennium, making their rare appearance in Glasgow all the more special for long-time devotees. Joining the headliners on Friday is Texan trio Portrayal of Guilt, whose fusion of hardcore and black metal will likely make for the most positively punishing sounds of the evening.

Saturday leans into the hardcore and punk side of things, with the tough throwdown rage of Incendiary and the versatile punk rock of Angel Du$t leading the bill. They will also be joined that evening by London punks Petrol Girls and the burqa-sporting Moroccan punks Taqbir.

Sunday leans more into sludgy darkness where the festival’s metal and grindcore acts are free to roam. The main headliner is New Orleans sludge metal legends Eyehategod, but not before the audience witness sets from grindcore masters Pig Destroyer and Singapore’s Wormrot.

![wormrot](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21653759.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Singaporean grindcore band Wormrot performing for Biquette the Punk Rock Goat *(Image: Wormrot)*

Beyond the touring and international acts, Core. has also filled its set times with some of the best domestic punk and metal bands Scotland has to offer. Don’t miss Dundonian veterans Kaddish, who take the stage early on Friday. Saturday sees a wealth of local talent throughout the day, with rural feminist punk sisters Bratakus, noise punk screechers K U T E, Dundee screamo natives Knives Chau, and the emo-indie Flakes returning after a seven-year hiatus. Also on Saturday is the Girls Rock Glasgow Takeover, exhibiting the developing talents of young girls who have picked up the guitar and will likely see themselves booked for Core.’s line-up in the coming years. And on Sunday don’t miss the noisy and brutal death metal outfit Unmaking nor the blackened hardcore stylings of Dundee’s Bed of Wasps.

Alongside the hours and hours of live ear-bending sounds will be an independent merchandise market, artist exhibitions and various community showcases. Such displays of music and community matter greatly at a time when small venues and independent promoters operate on the thinnest margins, with Core. helping to make the case for an infrastructure that supports and nurtures all of these acts.

Core. runs from Friday 13 to Sunday 15 November across The Art School, Nice N Sleazy, 1990 and Garnethill Multicultural Centre. The venues are close enough together that wandering between rooms and buildings becomes a key part of the fun, so make sure to dip in and out to discover what could be your next favourite band.
