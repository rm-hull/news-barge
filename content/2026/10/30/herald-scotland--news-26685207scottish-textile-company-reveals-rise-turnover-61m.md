A historic Scottish textile manufacturer has unveiled a rise in turnover of £61 million.

The Forfar-registered company that traces its roots to 1792 also reported a loss of £1.35m, narrowed from £6.85m.

Don & Low Limited, which makes industrial fabrics, carpet backing, carpet yarns and geotextiles, also moved to end the manufacture of artificial grass products.

Gross profit was £5.8m, set against £3.1m, against an operating loss of £3.9m in 2025 compared with £8.5m in 2024. Turnover increased 9.4% from £56m to £61m.

Exceptional reorganisation expenses of £295,000 in 2025 and £192,000 in the year before “represent redundancy and related costs as a result of the group’s restructuring process”, with a reduction in staff numbers of 338 from 344 recorded in the last year.

An exceptional impairment of stock relates to a “business decision to cease manufacturing artificial grass products and the subsequent write-down to the recoverable value of the grass inventories”. This was listed as £191,000 last year and £1.1m the year before.

The main activities of the group and the company are the manufacture and marketing of woven and nonwoven polypropylene textiles. The main woven products include geotextiles, industrial fabrics, carpet backing, and carpet yarns. The nonwoven products are mainly used in the areas of construction, medical, furniture and bedding, and filtration.

The directors said the business model is “clearly defined strategic plan that is designed to take the group into the future on a sustainable and profitable path”.

The directors said: “The group has undertaken a major capital investment programme in recent years that has expanded and upgraded production facilities across the business and there was further, but more modest, investment during 2025.”

It has generally invested in “relatively capital-intensive processes to improve long-term competitiveness”.

The report states that the sales and production mix continues to be modified “in response to market developments”.

### **Challenging**

The directors said that “economic conditions continued to be challenging”.

There was some, but not much, respite on raw materials and a watching brief was flagged on war in the Middle East.

The directors added that there was an improvement in sales volumes and revenues after a direct-to-market sales strategy in specific markets, and a focus on improving product mix.

“External supply and demand effects remained significant, general cost-of-living pressures suppressed demand in several sectors, and surplus worldwide capacity affected volumes and market price level,” the directors said. “Compared with recent years, there was less volatility in the major cost categories, for example raw materials, electricity and transport, but these continued to be at historically elevated levels.”

The company, now a wholly owned subsidiary of Greek group Thrace Plastics, has “sufficient available funds for operations and planned expansion”.

The directors said: “In summary, 2025 financial performance, although in line with planned performance for the year, and improved compared with the previous year, did not recover to the full extent the group plans to achieve in the short to medium term.

“The long-term investment plan remains the correct approach and the strategic plan is focused on the correct actions. All the factors affecting performance, whether internal or external, continue to be monitored closely by management.”
