Some disabled Calderdale residents are waiting more than two years for an accessible shower despite legislation that caps the wait at 12 months from approval.

Last week, Calderdale Council Interim Director of Adult Health and Wellbeing Anne Flanagan told the Scrutiny Committee there are more than 1,000 overdue reviews for grants designed to fund adaptations in people’s homes.

Ms Flanagan said: ‘A review should be carried out within a 12-month period. Those big numbers are largely overdue by more than a year. We have a smaller number overdue by more than three years, and a very tiny number overdue beyond that.

‘It’s an area I am very concerned about.’

According to the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, Disabled Facilities Grants (DFGs) – which are means-tested for over 18s and can be up to £30,000 – should be approved or refused in six months and the works completed with 12-months from approval.

Adaptations include level access showers, stair-lifts, through-floor lifts, widened doorways, ramps, and extensions.

But Ms Flanagan said: ‘The need for that service vastly outstrips the available resource to provide it.

‘We have, for example, increased the threshold where people don’t have to go through the very complicated application process.

‘We do perform much better on those lower-level needs, lower-level equipment and adaptations.

‘Where we have got very significant weights is for major adaptations.

‘At the moment, there’s around a two-plus year wait for a level access shower.

‘Those waits will continue unless there’s a change in funding allocation. The Disability Facilities Grant is allocated centrally, and we do not have control over the level of grant.

‘It’s a very difficult position. There are very long waits.’

In 2026/27 Calderdale has been allocated £3,763,476 for DFGs, while Bradford got £6.6m, Leeds £10.3m, Kirklees £4.7m, and Wakefield £5.4m.

Ms Flanagan also partly blamed the waiting times on a ‘recording issue’ that will only be fixed once the Council completes a move to its new digital social care system (Mosaic) in January.

Calderdale’s three-year corporate plan, published in September, has prioritised ‘helping people live well in their own homes’, and implementing Mosaic within the first year.

Calderdale’s adult social care provision was scored 70 out of 100 by the Care Quality Commission (CQC) in October 2025, putting Calderdale joint third in the region and in the top third of local authorities across England and Wales.

While seven areas were marked ‘Good’, the Council was told it ‘Requires Improvement’ in ‘Assessing Needs’ and ‘Safe Pathways and Transitions’.

The watchdog report found 272 people were waiting for an occupational therapy assessment, with a median wait time of 56 days and a maximum wait of 990 days, based on figures from January 2025.

On Thursday (22 October), Ms Flanagan revealed the number of people waiting for an OT assessment is now ‘in the region of 300 people’, with the longest waits in learning disability and neurodiversity.

Ms Flanagan said the recruitment of four new occupational therapists has brought down waiting times, though they remain ‘very high’.

‘If I had unlimited money, I would throw a lot at occupational therapy because they do such amazing preventative work,’ she added.

Cllr James Cook (Reform, Elland) asked how the waiting list compares to neighbouring boroughs, but Ms Flanagan said recording practices differ across borders.

‘We are not comparing apples with apples,’ she said, adding that recording practices are due to be standardised.

According to 2024/25 data from Foundations, the government-funded national body for Disabled Facilities Grants, the total average time for completing a grant was 247 working days while 98 per cent of councils completed the work within the 12-month deadline.
