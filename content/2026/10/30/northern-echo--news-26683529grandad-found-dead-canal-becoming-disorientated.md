A grandad whose body was recovered from a canal died after he became disoriented and lost while on his mobility scooter, an inquest has heard.

Shaun Barton, 60, was seen on his mobility scooter in Ripon on September 12 before becoming lost, an inquest at Northallerton Coroner's Court heard on Friday (October 30).

His body was found in the city's canal basin the following day by a member of the public.

Assistant Coroner Sarah Middleton said: "On the September 12 this year, Mr Barton was reported as disorientated and lost. His body was recovered on September 13."

A provisional cause of death was given as hypertension and immersion in water.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21649625.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mr Barton with daughters Kim and Gemma *(Image: SUBMIT)*

Police were called to Boroughbridge Road at 8.41am on September 13, where a member of the public had reported finding a body in the water.

The inquest was adjourned to a date to be fixed.

Following his death, his family remembered the Ripon resident as “the clown of the party”.

His daughter Gemma Cox paid tribute to him, describing a man full of laughter and kindness.

Ms Cox said: "He was really funny, always there for people, and the clown of the party.

"He was a very loving person, and well-known in Ripon and in Harrogate.

"He loved his squirrels; he had a family that would come and visit, and he'd feed them every day in the garden.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21649624.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mr Barton with two of his granddaughters *(Image: SUBMIT)*

"I was in my garden the other day, and a squirrel came out of nowhere, that put a smile on my face knowing he's around."

She said Mr Barton "adored his grandkids," and was especially proud of his family.

Ms Cox, who lives in Dorset, said her father had always supported her through difficult times.

She said: "Even before all of this, I was struggling with mental health, and I was struggling financially, and my dad would always call and check in on me.

"I can't come to terms with it, I can't face reality.

"Even my kids don't know yet, my eldest is autistic - I don't know how to tell them."

Mr Barton leaves behind two daughters, a son, a brother, and six grandchildren.
