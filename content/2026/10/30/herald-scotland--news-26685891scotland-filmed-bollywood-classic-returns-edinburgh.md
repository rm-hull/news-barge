A Bollywood classic partly filmed in Scotland is set to return to the big screen in Edinburgh to mark its 30th anniversary.

*Kuch Kuch Hota Hai* will feature at the 2028 Edinburgh International Film Festival (EIFF), with director Karan Johar expected to attend the celebration.

The film marked Johar’s directorial debut and helped strengthen the connection between Scotland’s landscapes and Indian cinema.

First Minister John Swinney and Screen Scotland interim executive director David Smith extended the invitation during a recent meeting at Dharma Productions’ office in Mumbai.

Discussions also explored opportunities for collaboration between Scotland and India in film and television production, including co-productions, talent development and cultural exchange.

Released in 1998, *Kuch Kuch Hota Hai* was the first major Indian film to shoot extensively in Scotland. Its locations included Eilean Donan Castle, Glen Coe, Tantallon Castle and Inchmahome Priory.

The anniversary celebration is planned for EIFF’s 81st edition in 2028.

The festival’s 80th edition will take place from August 12 to 18, 2027, with submissions due to open in January. Further details are expected to be announced in the coming months.

EIFF was established in 1947 as the International Festival of Documentary Films and describes itself as the world’s oldest continually running film festival.

Following its 2023 edition, a new organisation was established under chairman Andrew Macdonald to lead a revitalised festival from 2024. Paul Ridd was appointed CEO and festival director in December 2023.

The festival is supported by Screen Scotland, which provides funding and strategic guidance to Scotland’s film and television industry. Screen Scotland operates as part of Creative Scotland, with support from the Scottish Government and the National Lottery.

First Minister John Swinney said: "It was a pleasure to meet Karan Johar in Mumbai, and I am delighted that he has accepted the Edinburgh International Film Festival’s invitation to celebrate the 30th anniversary of KUCH KUCH KOTA HAI which holds a special place in the hearts of communities across Scotland and beyond. I look forward to welcoming him to Edinburgh.

"During my visit to India, I met with people from the Indian film industry, including representatives from Dharma Productions, to discuss opportunities for future collaboration between Scottish and Indian filmmaking.

"Scotland’s screen industry has tremendous potential as a creative partner for Indian film and television, offering the infrastructure, expertise and world-class filming locations to support a wide range of productions."

Paul Ridd, CEO and festival director of EIFF, said: "At EIFF we are always thinking about the future and what’s next.

"So we jumped at an opportunity like this, to plant the seeds for a celebration of a legendary, much-loved film at our Festival, a film featuring Scotland and some of the most iconic stars of Bollywood cinema on screen.

"We can’t wait for what promises to be something truly special for our 81st edition in 2028.

"But in the meantime, watch this space for more news of our 80th next August."
