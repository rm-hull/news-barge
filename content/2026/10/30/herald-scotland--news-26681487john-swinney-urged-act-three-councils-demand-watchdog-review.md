Scotland’s ethics watchdogs are facing a growing councils revolt with the First Minister asked to intervene amid warnings that councillors are afraid to speak out and challenge decisions in the public interest in a new wave of freedom of speech concerns.

A third local authority has now demanded action, with East Renfrewshire Council unanimously backing a motion calling for an independent review of the Standards Commission for Scotland (SCS) and the Ethical Standards Commissioner (ETH) Labour councillor Katie Pragnell Lees, who brought forward the motion, said elected members must be able to ask difficult questions regardless of their political affiliation.

The decision adds to pressure on the bodies responsible for investigating complaints against councillors, following a Court of Session ruling that the Standards Commission acted unlawfully in its handling of evidence in the case of Glasgow Labour councillor Fiona Higgins.

Councillor Pragnell Lees said: “Many of us will have followed Fiona's case when the SCS was questioned, and it still feels very defensive. And a lot of us don't have faith in the process.

"I think it this sends a message that we believe there is something has to be done about the Standards Commission and the Ethical Standards Commissioner, and that government need to intervene.

"There seems to be no rhyme or reason to when councillors do get censored.”

She said the motion was also about "solidarity" with other councillors who have gone through the ethics system and are speaking out about a "climate of fear" over doing their job in representing the public about local concerns.

Dumfries and Galloway Council has called for an independent review, while Edinburgh councillors have voted to express no confidence in the SCS.

The three councils have taken different approaches, but each has raised concerns about the operation of the bodies responsible for enforcing standards among elected representatives.

The issue has come to a head following the legal battle brought by Ms Higgins, who challenged the commission after it refused to allow certain documents to be used in a misconduct hearing against her.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21576152.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lorna Johnston, the Standards Commission for Scotland's executive director *(Image: NQ)*

In August, Lord Sandison ruled that the Commission had acted unlawfully by refusing to admit seven documents which Ms Higgins wanted to rely on in her defence. The judge said the decision had prevented her from properly presenting her case.

In his written judgment, he said: “By refusing to allow the productions in issue to be used at the substantive hearing, the commission was not allowing the petitioner to be properly heard. It had shut its ears to her proposed submissions and in so doing acted unlawfully.” The SCS is appealing.

The case arose from social media comments Ms Higgins made about proposed cuts to teaching posts at Glasgow City Council. After obtaining internal council emails through freedom of information legislation, she accused a former council finance director of misleading councillors and the public over the proposals and called for an investigation.

The Standards Commission was considering whether her comments breached the councillors’ code of conduct. Ms Higgins argued that seven documents, including council emails and budget material, were relevant to her defence and to her right to freedom of expression.

Councillor Pragnell Lees said the issue was not confined to one political party or local authority. East Renfrewshire’s motion states that the current arrangements must command the confidence of elected members, councils and the public.

It calls on John Swinney and the local government minister Jenny Gilruth to support an independent review of the Standards Commission and the Ethical Standards Commissioner. It also asks the Convention of Scottish Local Authorities (COSLA) to continue engaging with the Scottish Government on the issue.

The proposed review would examine how the bodies operate and whether their procedures are fair, transparent and proportionate.

The standards system exists to ensure councillors are held accountable for their conduct. Elected members must follow a statutory code governing their behaviour, and complaints can be investigated by the Ethical Standards Commissioner before cases are referred to the Standards Commission for a hearing.

Where a breach is found, sanctions can include censure or suspension, depending on the circumstances. The system is intended to protect public confidence in local government, but the latest calls for action centre on whether its procedures strike the right balance between accountability and the ability of councillors to scrutinise decisions.

Ms Higgins said she welcomed the call for an investigation into the actions of the ESC and SCS.

She said: "An independent review must examine the quality and rigour of ESC investigations.

![Fiona Higgins](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20192291.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fiona Higgins *(Image: Newsquest)*

"The National Records of Scotland investigation into the destruction of councillor case files highlights the urgency, particularly with three court cases concerning human rights protections and procedural fairness still live.

"I am also concerned by reports that the SCS has refused to fulfil Subject Access Requests from councillors seeking information about themselves to understand how decisions affecting them were made. Those reports require examination and a clear explanation.

"Bodies entrusted with upholding standards must themselves demonstrate fairness, transparency and accountability. Councillors and the public deserve confidence in both the investigations and the decisions that follow."

Mrs Pragnell Lees said there were also concerns about the practical difficulties of defending cases, particularly where legal representation was required.

She said: “If you need to employ a private solicitor, many of us can't afford to do that, and it's not accessible.”

The cost of challenging a decision can be significant, as the Higgins case has demonstrated through the need for court proceedings to contest the commission’s decision on evidence. The eventual legal costs of that dispute have been a separate issue from the judge’s finding on the lawfulness of the commission’s approach.

![Ian Bruce became Acting Commissioner in April 2021 and was formally appointed as Ethical Standards Commissioner in March 2023.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21580817.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ian Bruce became Acting Commissioner in April 2021 and was formally appointed as Ethical Standards Commissioner in March 2023. *(Image: NQ)*

East Renfrewshire’s motion expresses concern about the operation of both standards bodies and argues that a review is needed to maintain confidence in the framework. Its unanimous passage means the call has support across the council’s political groups.

Mrs Pragnell Lees said the aim was to ensure elected members could carry out their duties while maintaining proper accountability.

Standards Commission convener, Suzanne Vestri said: "Councillors are entitled, within their debates, to raise their concerns and to discuss the importance of being able to scrutinise their organisation's performance and the actions of council officers.  
"The Standards Commission believes that it is possible for all elected councillors to carry out their vital scrutiny role without breaching the Councillor's Code of Conduct.

"The Code states that councillors must treat everyone with courtesy and respect, and that they must not undermine any individual employee or group of employees, or raise concerns about their performance, conduct or capability in public.

"We maintain that any councillor can scrutinise effectively without breaching these two elements of the code, and indeed the evidence shows the vast majority do so.

"The Standards Commission would, of course, be happy to contribute to any future review into Scotland's ethical standards framework and the work of both the Ethical Standards Commissioner and the Standards Commission."

The ESC declined to comment.
