A County Durham restaurant boss has been fined £6,000 after three rats were found in the cellar where food was kept.

Dirty sinks, cobwebs on the ceiling, and built-up grease were among the hygiene failings inspectors found at Lebaneat, on North Bailey in Durham.

Two dead rats and one live rat were found in the cellar, run by Ahmed Sayed, where the restaurant’s food and drink are stored.

This is not the first time the owner of Lebaneat has found himself in hot water for food hygiene breaches, with him and another director left facing a bill of more than £16,000 in May over a string of food hygiene issues and staff right-to-work checks.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20990687.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lebaneat *(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Following the most recent inspection, Sayed, the sole director of 47 Northbailey Limited, the company behind the restaurant, agreed to shut so it could be proven there was no rat activity.

However, just two days later, despite Sayed, 42, saying a deep clean was carried out, a freshly dead rat was found in the cellar.

Sayed requested to reopen the restaurant as it had high bookings. However, he was told the restaurant must remain closed until effective pest control treatment had been carried out.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650049.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rats found inside Lebaneat in Durham *(Image: DURHAM COUNCIL)*

Officers returned three days later and were satisfied there was no more rodent activity and cleanliness had improved, and the business was allowed to open.

Sayed pleaded guilty at Peterlee Magistrates Court to three charges of breaching food safety and hygiene regulations.

He was ordered to pay a £200 fine, an £80 victim surcharge, and £1,330 costs. A fine of £4,800 was also imposed on the company 47 Northbailey Limited.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650051.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rats found inside Lebaneat in Durham *(Image: DURHAM COUNCIL)*

The Lebaneat restaurant now no longer appears on the firm’s website, and is listed as “permanently closed” on Google.

Lebaneat has faced a turbulent year, having been fined in May for the conditions in its restaurant and Meadowfield warehouse that were a “serious risk to public health”.

Officers visited the restaurant in May 2025 and found two people working illegally in breach of their immigration conditions.

Another illegal worker was found at the restaurant during a visit in May this year.

In August, The Home Office then launched a bid to strip Lebaneat of its alcohol licence.

The restaurant was fined £45,000 in 2024 and lost its licence - the second time in five years after also losing its licence in 2019 over the similar issue.

Mark Anslow, Durham County Council’s health and environmental protection manager, said: “Food businesses have a legal responsibility to maintain safe, clean premises and always comply with food hygiene regulations.
