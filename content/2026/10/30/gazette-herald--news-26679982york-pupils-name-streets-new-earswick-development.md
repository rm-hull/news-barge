Students have helped shape a new housing development by naming its streets and house types.

The Joseph Rowntree School pupils visited the Willow Bank site in New Earswick, after taking part in workshops to name parts of the scheme.

Six students from years eight and 10 toured the development on Thursday, October 22, meeting senior representatives from JRHT and Esh Construction, including Julian Hartley, CEO of JRHT, and Rich Thomas, director of assets and investments.

Rosie Johns, pastoral manager for year eight, said: "The entire experience from the street naming to the ceremony has been so inclusive, exciting and valuable.

"We welcome the partnership between JRHT and the Joseph Rowntree School as one community working for families and the next generation."

In May, Year eight students entered a competition to name the house types at Willow Bank, with winning entries including The Burrow (by Annabelle), The Dam, The Den, and The Hideout (by Riley), and The Nest (by Talia).

In a separate workshop, Year 10 students created street names inspired by the history and environment of New Earswick.

Five names were selected by JRHT’s sales team and submitted to City of York Council for approval.

Rich Thomas, director of assets and investments at JRHT, said: "It was a pleasure to meet the students who named Willow Bank’s streets and house types, and to show them how the homes are taking shape next to their school.

"Their ideas will be part of this neighbourhood for years to come, long after the building work is finished."

Willow Bank will provide 117 new homes for social rent and shared ownership.

Megan Simpson, social value manager at Esh Construction, said: "It's been great to work alongside JRHT to get students from The Joseph Rowntree School involved, who now leave their mark on the development's history."
