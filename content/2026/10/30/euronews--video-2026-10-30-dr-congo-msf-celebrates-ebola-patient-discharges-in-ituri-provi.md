Health workers and local residents danced and sang outside an **Ebola treatment centre in Mongbwalu**, in the** Democratic Republic of Congo's Ituri province**, to celebrate the discharge of its** last remaining patients**. In footage published by** Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) on 28 October**, staff and members of the community gather outside the facility, moving to the rhythm of the celebrations.

According to the medical charity, it is the **first time since May** that the centre has had**no confirmed Ebola patients under treatment**. The joyful scenes mark a welcome moment for healthcare workers and residents in a region that has been grappling with the deadly virus for months.

The celebrations come despite the **continuing Ebola epidemic** in the region. On**27 October**, Congolese health authorities reported that Africa's latest outbreak had** surpassed 10,000 confirmed cases**, the vast majority recorded in the** Democratic Republic of Congo**.

Ebola is a severe viral disease that can cause life-threatening illness and spreads through direct contact with the bodily fluids of infected people. Although the discharge of the final patients represents an **important milestone for the MSF facility in Mongbwalu**, it does** not mean the outbreak has ended**or that new cases cannot emerge. The charity's announcement concerns patients being treated at** this particular centre**, rather than the wider situation across** Ituri province** or the country.
