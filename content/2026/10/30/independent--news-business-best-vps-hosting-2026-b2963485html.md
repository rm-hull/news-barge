Providing all the benefits of having your own private server located elsewhere, there’s a number of reasons why you might be thinking about investing in a VPS (virtual private server). For instance, you might want to run your website on one, deploy applications such as a game server, or simply experiment with it if you're a developer.

But with so many options out there, it isn’t obvious which VPS to choose. That’s where I come in. I’m a tech journalist with more than 15 years of experience, so I know how to cut through the noise to extract what’s helpful in making that decision.

For this review, I’ve spent time researching the best VPS hosting for many different situations. I’m focusing on the hosting side of VPS here, but it’s always good to know there’s flexibility with any additional features. Below, you’ll find the best VPS hosting options I’ve chosen, from my extensive time testing them.

## The best VPS hosting services in 2026 are:

* **Best VPS hosting overall** – Hostinger: From £4.99 per month, Hostinger.com
* **Best budget VPS hosting** – Ionos: From £2 per month, Ionos.co.uk
* **Best three-year plan** – Bluehost: From £2.29 per month, Bluehost.com
* **Best high-end VPS hosting** – GoDaddy: From £7.49 per month, Godaddy.com

## How I tested

I spent time signing up to each service, setting up a VPS, then trying out many of the features available – such as installing operating systems, installing a game server, or simply setting up cPanel ready for a new website.

I also looked at anything else provided via the VPS and how well it links into the hosting company’s control panel and other available services. I focused on the strength of features offered and how intuitive each VPS is so that novices could figure things out too. You can see my full testing criteria at the bottom of this article.

## How the best VPS hosting was tested

While spending time with many VPS hosting providers, I considered certain criteria. Below is what I took into consideration each step of the way:

* **Ease of use:** A VPS can feel confusing to figure out, especially if you’re new to VPS hosting. I looked at how easy it was to set up a VPS, whether any onboarding processes were done in a logical and simple-to-understand way, and what technical knowledge was needed to achieve anything. I also looked at the little things that add up, like whether it’s easy to change or cancel a plan.

* **Features:** I looked at the features of each VPS host. There was definitely overlap but I wanted to see how many different elements of a VPS host could be one-click installed and what else is available through each provider. I compared these to the competition but also evaluated whether there were features that you would actually use regularly.

* **Value for money:** This doesn’t translate to the cheapest VPS hosting but the one that represents the best value. Sometimes, it’s smarter to pay a little extra for additional features, so this was a very important one when weighing up which were the best VPS hosting providers.

* **Performance:** I didn’t want to have to wait around for my VPS host to do things, so I made sure to track performance and speed. When it came to setting up websites through the VPS, I wanted to check that the resulting website loaded quickly.
