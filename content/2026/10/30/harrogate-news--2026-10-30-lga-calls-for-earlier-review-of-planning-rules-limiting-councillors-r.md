**The Local Government Association (LGA) is calling on the Government to review new planning rules a year earlier than planned, warning that changes could weaken the role of elected councillors in local planning decisions.**

The new National Scheme of Delegation rules come into force on 31 October 2026 and will limit councillors’ ability to call in planning applications for consideration by a planning committee.

The LGA says the changes risk undermining local democratic accountability and argues that councils should have greater flexibility to decide how planning decisions are handled in their own areas.

The Government has committed to reviewing the changes in 2028. The LGA wants that review brought forward to 2027 so that any impact on local democracy can be assessed sooner.

### Concerns over local decision-making

The LGA said a standardised approach might not adequately reflect the differences between local planning authorities, particularly those covering large geographical areas or communities where developments can have significant local impacts.

It argues that councils should be able to establish planning governance arrangements that reflect the needs of their own areas, while still working towards a more efficient planning system.

Councillor Carl Cashman, chair of the LGA’s Inclusive Growth Committee, said councillors were concerned about the potential effect on their democratic mandate.

> **Councillor Carl Cashman, chair of the LGA’s Inclusive Growth Committee, said:** Councillors are concerned this could negatively impact their democratic mandate and ensure their views about how their area is developed are taken into account. We support creating a more efficient and effective planning system, but we do not believe the government’s plans address this.

He also emphasised the importance of elected representatives in the planning process.

> **Councillor Carl Cashman, chair of the LGA’s Inclusive Growth Committee, said:** Elected councillors are the backbone of the English planning system.
>
> Government needs to review the impact of this change earlier to ensure it does not come at the expense of local democratic accountability.

### Implications for North Yorkshire

The changes could be relevant to planning decisions across North Yorkshire, where housing developments and other proposals can have significant effects on individual communities.

Under the new arrangements, the circumstances in which councillors can bring applications before a planning committee will be more restricted. The precise implications for individual applications will depend on the rules and the arrangements operating within the relevant planning authority.

The LGA’s call is for an earlier review of the changes, rather than their immediate reversal.

The association has published a position paper on the reforms to planning committees, alongside guidance from the Planning Advisory Service.
