A prisoner “swilled” a fellow inmate at a women’s prison with boiling water in a Christmas Day attack over the loan of some tweezers.

Jessica Blenkiron was on remand at Low Newton Prison, Durham, last December over an unrelated knife incident in public, for which she has since received a suspended prison sentence.

Newcastle Crown Court heard that both women were on C-wing and fell out over tweezers the other prisoner had loaned Blenkiron.

Prosecutor Kevin Wardlaw said Blenkiron told the woman she could not find them when the other inmate complained they had not been returned.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641372.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jessica Blenkiron given a three-year prison sentence for 'swilling' a fellow inmate at Low Newton Prison, Durham, with boiling water in a dispute about the return of borrowed tweezers

The woman said Blenkiron “blanked her” when she spoke to her on Christmas Day and so she called her a “f\*\*\* ing freak”.

Blenkiron returned to her cell, then shouted for the woman to come and collect the tweezers a short time later.

When she reached the cell, Blenkiron threw boiling water over her face, neck and arms, causing immediate burning and throbbing pain.

Blenkiron closed her cell door as prison officers rushed to the victim’s aid.

Mr Wardlaw said she suffered second-degree burns to her ear, hand and wrist, but was treated with dressings in prison and did not need hospital treatment.

She wore bandages for a fortnight and took painkillers, but Mr Wardlaw said her injuries appeared to have healed without long-term physical harm.

The victim said she felt “stupid” for allowing it to happen, but had not had any previous problems with the defendant.

Blenkiron, 23, of Shaftoe Road, Sunderland, declined to be interviewed at the time, but pleaded guilty to causing grievous bodily harm with intent at a June court hearing.

The court heard Blenkiron has six previous convictions for 13 offences, including unlawful wounding and possessing a bladed article.

She received a nine-month sentence, suspended for two years, for those offences in April.

That offence was committed several weeks before the Christmas Day attack and led to her being remanded in custody at the time.

Sentencing was adjourned so the Probation Service and a psychiatrist could prepare reports, alongside an assessment by a mental health liaison service.

The court heard she has complex PTSD and bipolar disorder.

Sophie Allinson-Howells, in mitigation, said Blenkiron was “a complex individual” with a lengthy history of involvement with mental health services in the community and secure settings.

She said Blenkiron was “very guarded” with professionals, but she was unsure of the root cause.

Miss Allinson-Howells said substance misuse issues had made matters worse, “further muddying the waters”.

She added that Blenkiron was back in custody at Low Newton Prison after a warrant was issued for previously failing to attend court for sentencing.

Recorder Jamie Hill KC said it was an unpleasant incident, but the burns could have been more serious.

He told Blenkiron: “I understand and appreciate that relationships between inmates in prison can be difficult and it has been additionally challenging for someone like yourself who has been struggling with mental health issues.

“But what you did was very dangerous and had very unpleasant consequences in the short term for the victim, but it could have been so much worse.

“Thankfully, she has made a good recovery.”

Recorder Hill imposed a three-year prison sentence, saying the offence was “too serious” to be suspended.

He told Blenkiron: “I can only hope you continue to receive assistance in prison and that you accept that assistance during that period.”
