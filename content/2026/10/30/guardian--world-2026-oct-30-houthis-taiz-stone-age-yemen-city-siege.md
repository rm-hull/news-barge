The Houthis are trying to send the city of Taiz back to the stone age, the city’s governor said in a briefing designed to urge the international community to do more to lift the month-long siege.

Nabil Shamsan said massacres were taking place in Yemen’s third city, that oxygen and water supplies were running out and that rubbish was piling up in the streets due to landfill sites being either full or inaccessible.

The city’s daily minimum water needs are estimated at about 35,000 cubic metres, he said, but no more than 1,000 cubic metres was being allowed to reach it each day and that water shipments were being stopped at Houthi roadblocks surrounding the city.

![A padlock secures a brass outdoor water tap in Taiz ](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/bcb18f807bc344138bb67241ed61f06bf447898a/343_0_3433_2747/master/3433.jpg?width=120&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

With food stocks depleted and the price of wheat soaring, he said “citizens are heading back to the stone age”. Restaurants had closed due to the lack of fuel.

Shamsan said that as many as 1,800 people were in need of dialysis treatment but were unable to access supplies, that 120 health centres had been suspended and schools had closed, affecting hundreds of thousands of children.

He said 64 people had been killed, including civilians, from August to 20 October 2026 and a further 173 injured. More than 6,400 newly displaced families, each averaging seven members, had come to the city to flee the fighting.

“We are not talking about living, just surviving,” he said.

His account contradicted one given by the Houthi leader, Abdul-Malik Badr al-Din al-Houthi, who said: “The way is open for food, medicine, and humanitarian aid to enter the city and even for Saudi-backed groups to do so. Anyone who wants to verify that there is no siege on the city of Taiz can go to the city and see.”

![A healthcare worker attends to a baby in an incubator in a neonatal intensive care unit](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/65c89a6f7eb4ca95ad5f26a932657877103ed8f4/560_0_5600_4480/master/5600.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Shamsan said that Houthis forces had a near-total grip on the roads into the city, adding the group had issued a choice to the more than 800,000 inside the city “either to leave or to die”.

He accused the Houthis of not waging a military battle but a battle to change the identity of Yemen, with captured villages being taken over by new religious leaders with education and religious curricula being changed. “This is not a military war but a war of vengeance,” he said.

People in occupied territories were being required to show loyalty to the Zaydi religion and to sign forms affirming they had joined the Houthis. “They are not starting a war but an revolution in identity,” Shamsan said, adding that scores of children from each village were being forcibly ordered without their parents’ consent to join the frontlines in Saada and elsewhere.

Vowing the people of Taiz would resist until the end, he said statements of solidarity were of no use and that what was needed was access for aid agencies, including the UN, to deliver supplies to the city.

![A huge pile of rubbish bags fills a street in Taiz](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/40d8e06cd7f342bc526498eeb563c93bab8a3886/977_0_5600_4480/master/5600.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Shamsan was reluctant to divulge details of the state of the military campaign, but said shelling of the city was becoming more frequent and that communication between the city and rural areas had broken down. Houthis now controlled the mobile networks and were sending messages to citizens to end their resistance, while eavesdropping on all conversations, he said. Mobile networks were “completely disrupted”, he said, adding that he was speaking only 2-5km from the frontlines.

Most independent agencies agree that life inside the city is rapidly deteriorating. Niku Jafarnia, a Yemen researcher at Human Rights Watch, said: “Renewed efforts by the Houthis to cut off food, water, and medicine to Taiz show their utter disregard for civilian lives.

“Throughout the 12-year-long siege of Taiz, the Houthis have bombed, starved and denied humanitarian aid to a million residents of the city with scant adherence to international law.”
