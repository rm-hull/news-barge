# The Royal Whitby: Yorkshire hotel invites guests to wake up to the view that inspired Bram Stoker’s Dracula with dramatic coastal views

## Visitors have the opportunity to stay at The Royal Whitby hotel that overlooks the harbour that inspired Bram Stoker’s Dracula.

Guests looking for a Halloween experience can stay in the spot that inspired Bram Stoker’s Dracula, looking out over Whitby Harbour and the town’s ruined abbey.

More than 135 years ago, Bram Stoker spent time in the first-floor reading room of The Royal Whitby, BW Premier Collection by Best Western, looking across the dramatic Yorkshire coastline as he developed his well known vampire story.

![Whitby harbour at sunset.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjRiNzkwNjI0LTZiOTItNDcwYS05OWE5LTU2YjEwZmMyZWE5MjpiOGFlOTVhOC03YTM1LTQ3YzAtOTQyZC01YzA2ZGM4MmJkZGQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Today (October 30), that former reading room has been converted into bedrooms, allowing guests to experience the atmospheric views that helped inspire one of the world’s most famous horror novels.

The scene includes Whitby Harbour, where Dracula famously arrives in England aboard a shipwrecked vessel before escaping ashore in the form of a black dog.

While visitors come to explore Whitby’s Gothic history, hotel staff say there is one question they hear over and over again - where is Dracula buried?

General manager of Royal Whitby, BW Premier Collection, Andrew Lee, says the fascination with the fictional vampire continues to surprise him.

“You’d be amazed by the questions we get about Dracula. People ask us where he’s buried, whether Bram Stoker stayed here and even which room he used to sit in,” he said.

![Dracula arrives.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmY2MWM4NGRiLTc1NGMtNGZlNS1iYmUyLTNlY2Q5MjYxMDcyODphNjdhYzY2Yy05ZDVmLTQ1NTctYjViMC1lYTQ1ZjE3Y2Q2NjA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“We explain what we know about the history, but there’s a real fascination with the legend. People come to Whitby wanting to experience a little of that Gothic atmosphere for themselves.”

The interest goes beyond Halloween, with Whitby’s connection to Dracula attracting visitors from across the world, including America, Japan and China, eager to explore the places associated with the tale.

Bram visited Whitby in 1890, and its dramatic coastline, ruined abbey, churchyard and maritime history helped inspire his book. The story’s famous arrival of Dracula is linked to the real-life wreck of the Russian schooner Dmitry in 1885.

The hotel also displays a portrait and plaque presented by the Dracula Society.

But one popular assumption needs to be corrected: there is no established evidence that Stoker stayed at the hotel. His documented lodgings in Whitby were at 6 Royal Crescent, and Dracula himself certainly isn’t buried in the town’s churchyard.

The legendary author helps to boost one of Whitby’s biggest weekends of the year. Last October, Royal Whitby took more than £60,000 over the three-day Goth Weekend - around twice its usual earnings for the same period - as visitors arrived in elaborate Gothic outfits to enjoy live music, food and late-night celebrations.

With Halloween falling on a Saturday this year, Whitby Goth Weekend returns from October 30 to November 1, 2026, with the hotel preparing for another busy few days.

This year, it is also introducing a Gothic afternoon tea featuring black cakes and Gothic-inspired cocktails.

Beyond Halloween, a larger medium event is planned at the hotel for January, white Whitby’s Krampus parade in December brings monster costumes and a darker twist to the Christmas season.
