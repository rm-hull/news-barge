UK house price growth has slowed to its weakest rate since January 2024, with prices remaining flat month on month in October, according to the latest figures from Nationwide.

The building society’s latest house price index, shows annual house price growth fell to 0.5% in October, down from 0.8% in September.

The average UK house price stood at £273,566, compared with £274,251 the previous month.

The outlook for the housing market remains subdued in the near term. Surveyors have reported weak new buyer enquiries, while consumer confidence fell in October as households became less optimistic about the economy and their own finances.

Robert Gardner, chief economist at Nationwide, said: "October saw a further slowing in UK annual house price growth to 0.5%, from 0.8% in September.

"That is the weakest growth since January 2024. Prices were flat month on month, after taking account of seasonal factors. Market activity and house price growth have slowed in recent months, in part reflecting the uncertain economic backdrop.

"Geopolitical tensions remain high, with the conflict in the Middle East exerting upward pressure on energy prices, fanning inflation concerns.

"This in turn has led to mounting financial market expectations of Bank Rate increases, which have maintained upward pressure on the market interest rates which underpin mortgage pricing.

"Forward-looking indicators suggest further softness in the near term. Surveyors report that new buyer enquiries remain weak. Moreover, consumer sentiment dipped noticeably in October, with households becoming less confident about the outlook for the general economy as well as their own financial situation in the year ahead.

"Once the energy shock fades, there are good reasons to believe the market will regain momentum. The broader economy has proven surprisingly resilient to date, with annual economic growth accelerating in recent quarters.

"Employment is holding up well, as are real incomes, while household balance sheets are solid, with debt levels still close to 20-year lows relative to household disposable income."
