More than 1,800 young people in York have helped shape new recommendations to improve community safety.

Their views were gathered through the latest Big Conversation led by the York and North Yorkshire Youth Commission and were presented to decision-makers at a recent conference.

Youth commission members, aged 10 to 25, were consulted on issues including online safety, gender violence, discrimination, drugs, and their relationships with police and fire services.

Jo Coles, deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime in York and North Yorkshire, said: "A huge thanks to everyone who has taken part in this work over the last year.

"The scale of engagement from over 1,800 young people across our county is incredible and key to understanding how Mayor David Skaith and I can help make them feel safer in their communities."

Their members' recommendations included more visible patrols in public spaces, especially around schools and the night-time economy, and improved support for young victims of crime.

Kaytea Budd-Brophy, senior manager at Leaders Unlocked, said: "It’s inspiring to hear the Youth Commission present the findings from their peer-led research directly to local decision-makers and partners from across the region."

The commission’s final report will be published later this year.
