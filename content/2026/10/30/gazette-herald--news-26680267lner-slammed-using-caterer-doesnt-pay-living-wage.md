An MP has said she is "deeply concerned" that a York-based rail firm is using a catering company that no longer pays its staff the Real Living Wage.

Rachael Maskell has written to LNER's managing director, David Horne, over its catering contract with gatesolutions, urging him to "restore pay on this contract to the level of the Real Living Wage" (currently £14.10 per hour outside of London).

LNER said work has begun on a tender process for its catering supplier "which will include a requirement to properly consider payment of the Real Living Wage to employees".

A gatesolutions spokesperson said it is working to reach an agreement with the RMT union, adding: "Our priority is to ensure our employees benefit from an agreed pay settlement as soon as possible."

Ms Maskell said some of the pay rates she has seen from gatesolutions, an arm of a Swiss multinational, are "shockingly level with the statutory minimum wage" (currently £12.71 per hour).

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644912.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Horne, managing director of LNER *(Image: Newsquest)*

She said the case showed the "false economy of outsourcing", arguing that LNER, which operates the East Coast Main Line and has been under public ownership since 2018, would "have far greater control of the quality and real cost of the service if it ran it in-house".

The Labour MP for York Central also told Mr Horne the outsourced workers "have a statutory minimum pension scheme, statutory sick pay and no decent holiday arrangements comparable to your directly employed [LNER] staff".

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19715684.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central *(Image: UK Parliament/Press Association)*

"During a worsening cost of living crisis, this act will inflict real hardship on low paid, disproportionately BAME [black, Asian and minority ethnic] workers," Ms Maskell wrote to Mr Horne.

"While these actions are being taken by gatesolutions, we are clear that responsibility lies with LNER.

"This is your contract and payment of the Living Wage can be directed by the client. Indeed, LNER already makes this link explicit in its own promotional literature.

"LNER claims to be 'on a mission to be the UK's most responsible rail train operator. The business is making this ambition a reality through both its corporate activities and its engagement with the supply chain.

"You explicitly claim that payment of the Living Wage by your suppliers is one of the key measures of the Social Value you claim to be delivering. By the same token, permitting one of your contractors to cease paying the Living Wage is a conscious choice on your part."

An LNER spokesperson said: "We continue to work closely with our catering provider and we encourage all sides to work together towards a resolution.

"LNER is committed to ensuring employees of its partners are paid fairly. Work has begun on a new tender process for our catering supplier, which will include a requirement to properly consider payment of the Real Living Wage to employees.

"Plans are in place to make sure our customers continue to receive a high-quality catering experience when travelling with us."

The gatesolutions spokesperson said: "We cannot comment on ongoing negotiations.

"Our priority is to ensure our employees benefit from an agreed pay settlement as soon as possible.

"We remain committed to working with the RMT and hope to reach an agreement soon."
