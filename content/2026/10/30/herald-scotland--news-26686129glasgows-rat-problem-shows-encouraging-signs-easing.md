Glasgow’s rat problem shows “encouraging signs” of easing as fewer reports of rodents have been made according to a senior councillor.

Councillor Laura Doherty made the comment as she responded to a question from councillor Robert Mooney asking what is being done to tackle the issue as public concern grows.

Pointing out the work taking place, councillor Doherty said: “Recent investment in pest control and cleansing services including increased capacity and repeat visits where required has strengthened the council’s ability to respond to reports and target problem areas.

“While it is too early to conclude that there has been a reduction in the underlying pest control population the latest service data provides encouraging signs.

“In September this year reports of rats in living areas fell by 26 per cent compared with the same period last year from 237 reports to 176 while reports in non living areas fell by 16 per cent from 714 reports to 600.”

The SNP convenor for neighbourhood services and assets said the council can’t tackle vermin alone and pointed out housing associations, factors, businesses and property owners have a role to play in stopping the conditions that allow rats to thrive.

That includes disposing of waste, property maintenance, drainage issues and land management according to councillor Doherty.

Speaking at a full council meeting yesterday, the SNP councillor added: “The council has therefore working with our housing associations and other local partners to develop a more coordinated and preventative approach, which focuses on tackling the underlying causes of infestation as well as responding when they do occur.”

She was replying to a question from Labour councillor Mooney who asked “in light of growing public concerns about rat sightings in communities across Glasgow, whether they believe current cleansing and pest control arrangements are sufficient, and what additional measures are being considered to tackle the problem.”

Referring to a major issue in the local area, which he represents, councillor Mooney said: “Unfortunately the largest amount of sightings is in my ward – the Canal ward.”

He asked if the large number of missed uplifts is the problem. Councillor Doherty said was available to contact directly to discuss local issues.
