Members of a drugs gang have been jailed for smuggling cocaine inside wheels of cheese.

The network, made up of 18 members including a man from Brighouse and another man from Halifax, was responsible for trafficking 4.5 tonnes of cocaine with a street value of more than £450 million.

The drugs were hidden inside shrink-wrapped pallets of cheese and brought through Dover before being distributed across the north of England, including a site in Cleckheaton and a car wash in Halifax.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21650901.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police discover cocaine inside a wheel of cheese *(Image: PA)*

Three of the 18 convicted individuals were sentenced at Leeds Crown Court on Friday, with four others jailed earlier in the week.

Reporting restrictions had been in place until the conclusion of two trials, which began last year.

West Yorkshire Police launched the investigation after seizing £2 million worth of cocaine and ecstasy in Leeds in 2023.

Detectives uncovered a sophisticated drug distribution network that had imported more than 123 tonnes of cheese between December 2022 and April 2023.

The cheese was transported as commercial freight through Dover and delivered to a legitimate distribution centre in Derby.

From there, refrigerated lorries took the pallets to industrial units in Huddersfield, Cleckheaton, Halifax and Blackburn.

Two of the sites were managed by Brighhouse man Aiden McCullagh, while a third was run by Saleem Chaudhri.

Ikraam Safdar had rented a unit at a car wash on South Parade in Halifax, which was also used in the operation.

At these locations, McCullagh, Chaudhri, and Craig Asquith unpacked the cheese wheels to extract the cocaine, which was then repackaged in cardboard boxes and black bin bags.

The drugs were then distributed through a network of couriers from West Yorkshire, Lancashire, the West Midlands and the North East.

Lewis Ockenden and Umar Farooq acted as "lieutenants" at the Huddersfield and Cleckheaton hubs, meeting couriers and managing the handover of vehicles.

Chaudhri operated a similar system in Blackburn, either loading drugs into couriers' vehicles himself or meeting them nearby to complete the handover.

In May 2023, Lancashire Police raided Chaudhri’s Blackburn unit and found 217kg of cocaine, some of it still hidden inside blocks of Gouda cheese.

Each block of cheese contained two "bricks" of cocaine, concealed by hacking a space into each one.

Chaudhri was found to have agreed to supply more than 2,000kg of cocaine worth over £70 million between September 2022 and May 2023.

He was sentenced to 27 years and six months in prison at Preston Crown Court in April 2024.

After the Blackburn raid, the gang stopped using cheese to hide the drugs and switched to chocolate spread and frozen fish mince.

Craig Goulding was linked to both the West Yorkshire and Lancashire hubs through phone evidence.

Messages showed he was paid thousands to dispose of waste food and played a leading role in moving money between the sites.

Eight people had already been jailed before this week’s sentencing.

Aiden McCullagh, 58, of Brighouse, and Craig Goulding, 39, of Wakefield, were each sentenced to 25 years in prison in May.

Craig Asquith, 44, of Wakefield, received a 10-year sentence.

Umar Farooq, 37, of Halifax, was jailed for 15 years.

Lewis Ockenden, 36, of Wakefield, was sentenced to 13 years and four months.

Ikraam Safdar, 44, of Halifax, was sentenced to eight years and nine months.

Driver John Whittell, 43, of Wakefield, received 12 years and nine months, and David Jackson, 48, of Manchester, was jailed for 14 years and four months.

This week, Harry Johnson and Darren Johnson, both of Caledine Road, Leicester, were jailed for 16 years and 15 years respectively.

Jordan Miles, 32, of Whinchat Close, Coventry, was sentenced to 12 years and eight months.

Arran Gowans, 28, of Yarm Road, Darlington, was jailed for five years and 11 months.

On Friday, three couriers were sentenced for their roles.

Brandon Eley, 31, of Tamworth, Staffordshire, was jailed for 10 years.

Aaron McNally, 38, of Manchester, was sentenced to 10 years and nine months.

Antony Etheridge, 70, of no fixed address, received 18 months.

Three other defendants—Mark Briggs, 31, of Norham Avenue, South Shields; Sammy Stapleton, 42, of Peel Hall Road, Manchester; and Nathan Reilly, 26, of Oxton Avenue, Wythenshawe—are due to be sentenced at a later date.
