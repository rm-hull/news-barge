TRIBUTES have flooded in from the worlds of television and film following the death of Sir Patrick Stewart at the age of 86.

Born in Mirfield, Sir Patrick, a Shakespearean actor who went on to star in major Hollywood franchises Star Trek and X-Men, lived in Los Angeles, but for 20 years, until he put it up for sale in 2016, he had a 'getaway' home in Hebden, in the Yorkshire Dales.

He was an advocate of Yorkshire, lending his support to a new Welcome to Yorkshire tourism trail around the film and television locations of the region in 2012, describing how the atmosphere of the Yorkshire Dales and its surrounding area made it a perfect location for film and TV work.

And, in 2011, he came to Skipton to view Craven Museum's copy of Shakespeare's First Folio, which had gone on display for the first time earlier in the year and for which he recorded an accompanying voiceover.

During his visit, he was interviewed by Adrian Braddy, the then editor of the Telegraph & Argus's sister paper The Craven Herald, , and talked about his love of the Yorkshire Dales, how he first visited the area as a child and his life on the stage, in television and in film.

He also popped into Bizzie Lizzie's for some fish and chips, as fish fryer Paul McGranaghan later told the Craven Herald.

*Here, we reproduce the first of three features printed in the Craven Herald of October 2011.*

Sir Patrick Stewart said he "got goosebumps" when he was allowed to hold a rare volume of Shakespeare's plays at Skipton's Craven Museum.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21652003.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Patrick Stewart with the Shakespeare First Folio *(Image: Adriian Braddy)*

The Hollywood actor, who is well known for his Shakespearean performances, visited the town to view the prize exhibit, which recently went on display for the first time.

Sir Patrick first learned of the volume's existence when he was asked to record an accompanying voiceover.

The acclaimed actor told the Herald he was shocked to discover there was a copy of the Folio so close to his home in the Dales.

"It's thrilling," he said.

"And I love telling people that we've got a First Folio just down the road.

"When I was first approached I was astonished and doubly astonished that I didn't know about it.

I was unaware that this part volume of the Folio existed, and I didn't know that it existed not far away from where I've been living for 20 years.

"I thought, well there's the Folger [in Washington DC], there's Stratford-upon-Avon and there's one in the British Museum, I think... and now Skipton.

Isn't that great?

It's a fantastic story.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21652004.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Patrick Stewart being presented with a copy of a page from the First Folio, printed using the original Craven Herald printing press. Also pictured is museum volunteer, Brian Aldred, who restored the printer *(Image: Adriian Braddy)*

"When the museum approached me about wanting to put on a special display and they wanted a narrator, I was delighted to be asked, because Shakespeare, of course, has played a huge part in my life.

Without him I'd have been unemployed for a large part of my career.

"As a local person, a Yorkshireman, it seemed absolutely the right thing to do."

When he saw the exhibit for the first time, he admitted: "I get goosebumps whenever I get close to a Folio."

Sir Patrick explained why the book is so important.

"The First Folio was the first complete edition of Shakespeare's plays," he said.

"Everything that we know for certain he wrote is there.

It was the first time that a writer's exclusively dramatic works had ever been printed before, so it's a very, very important volume.

Without the Folio, half of Shakespeare's plays would be unknown.

"So, I would say, along with the King James Bible, it's the most important piece of publishing in this country."

The Folio lay undiscovered for many years in the museum's archives and Sir Patrick said there was a chance more copies could be discovered.

"There are approximately 500 copies missing from the published list and we should all take that attic apart," he said.

"I've actually been doing that today, but everything that I had I know was mine, so it's unlikely that I was going to turn up a First Folio.

"They were significant volumes and it's a little surprising that such a small percentage of them still exist."

During his visit, Sir Patrick was shown the original Craven Herald printing press, which is on show at the museum, and was presented with a facsimile of a page from the Folio, reproduced using the old press.
