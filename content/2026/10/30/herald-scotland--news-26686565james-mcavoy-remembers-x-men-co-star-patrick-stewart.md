James McAvoy has paid tribute after the death of his X-Men co-star Sir Patrick Stewart, saying he was “always lovely to me”.

Sir Patrick, famed for his role as Captain Jean-Luc Picard in The Star Trek: The Next Generation, died at his home in Los Angeles on Wednesday. He was 86.

The two actors portrayed older and younger versions of Charles Xavier in X-Men: Days Of Future Past in 2014.

Scottish actor and director McAvoy shared several photos on Instagram of them together, writing: “Wherever you’re going I know you’ll go boldly… I was lucky enough to work with Sir Patrick a number of times and he was always lovely to me.

“First as a fan and then as a colleague, I count myself lucky to have gone with you for a short time.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21649090.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Patrick Stewart died at his home in Los Angeles on Wednesday

Hercule Poirot star Sir David Suchet, a close friend of Sir Patrick’s, also paid tribute, saying he would be “so much missed”.

Sir David told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “It’s a great loss but he leaves the most fantastic legacy in all his work in radio, film, television and theatre.

“He covered all bases and has done great performances in each of the media. He will be so much missed.

“I was on stage yesterday all afternoon and evening and I heard of his death in the interval and it came as a huge shock.”

Downing Street said Sir Patrick “brought joy to millions”.

Former artistic director of the Royal Shakespeare Company Sir Gregory Doran also paid tribute, after he worked with Sir Patrick on a production of Antony And Cleopatra in 2006 and Hamlet in 2013.

He said: “I think it’s something about the very firm classical foundations that he had that gave him the ability to, whether it was X-Men or Shakespeare on stage, that just gave him that grounding.

“He was a magnificent Antony in Antony And Cleopatra, a part many people don’t want to play because they think it’s Cleopatra’s play. He brought an intense forensic analysis to the role.”

Sir Gregory added: “He was really democratic, no airs and graces, real fun. It was always fun in rehearsals with Patrick.”

Born in Yorkshire, Sir Patrick became a member of the RSC in 1966 and found international fame in Star Trek: The Next Generation.

He was also known for appearing in the X-Men franchise and films including Excalibur (1981), Dune (1984) and Jeffrey (1995). He was knighted in 2010 for services to drama.
