A University of Dundee researcher has received a major international award recognising her work to improve the lives of people living with Parkinson’s.

Dr Esther Sammler, based at the university’s Faculty of Life Sciences, has been named the 2026 recipient of the Tom Isaacs Award.

Presented by Cure Parkinson’s and the Van Andel Institute, the award recognises researchers whose work has a significant impact on people with Parkinson’s and who actively engage with the patient community.

Dr Sammler leads a translational research group at the University of Dundee and combines laboratory research with her work as a neurologist and clinical trials researcher.

She also leads trials of potential Parkinson’s therapies at Ninewells Hospital, where patients can take part in studies. Her work includes collaboration with advocacy groups and charities.

The award recognises her commitment to involving people affected by Parkinson’s in research and connecting scientific discoveries with clinical practice.

Dr Sammler was also recently named as part of a collaborative effort to develop a more holistic approach to Parkinson’s care.

Dr Esther Sammler said: "The Tom Isaacs Award came as a complete surprise and means so much to me because it recognises the importance of working alongside people living with Parkinson’s.

"Dundee is a special place to do this work.

"I feel very fortunate to lead a translational research group at University of Dundee, surrounded by outstanding basic science into Parkinson's, while also working as a neurologist and leading clinical trials.

"This allows us to bring discoveries from the laboratory into studies with people living with Parkinson’s, and questions from the clinic back into the laboratory."

Helen Matthews, CEO of Cure Parkinson’s, said: "We are delighted to present the Tom Isaacs Award for 2026 to Esther.

"In both her consultant neurologist and researcher roles she has shown an outstanding commitment to bettering the lives of those affected by Parkinson's.

"Dr Sammler has also shown a real dedication to the Parkinson's community beyond her day-to-day roles, working with advocacy groups and charities, as well as mentoring junior colleagues."
