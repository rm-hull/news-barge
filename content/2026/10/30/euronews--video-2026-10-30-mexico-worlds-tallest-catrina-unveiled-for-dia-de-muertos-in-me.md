The monumental **female skeleton**, roughly the height of an** 11-storey building**, surpasses the** previous record of 28.15 metres**set in** Puerto Vallarta in 2023**.

Created by around **140 artists and technicians** from**Taller Xibalbá**, the figure wears a** purple dress stretching more than 27 metres**. Visitors gathered beneath the installation to take photographs as workers put the finishing touches to the display. Facing the** Metropolitan Cathedral**, the** Catrina**stands at the centre of an enormous** ofrenda**, a traditional altar honouring the dead, surrounded by a carpet of some** 70,000 marigold plants**grown in** Xochimilco**.

The installation pays tribute to Mexican artist **José Guadalupe Posada**, whose** 1913 illustration**of a skeletal woman became the inspiration for** La Catrina**, now one of the most recognisable symbols of** Day of the Dead**.

The elaborate display also features coloured sawdust carpets and figures inspired by **axolotls and alebrijes**, Mexico's fantastical folk-art creatures. Celebrated on** 1 and 2 November**, Day of the Dead brings families together to remember deceased loved ones through photographs, food, flowers and offerings. Marigolds, known as** cempasúchil**, are traditionally believed to guide spirits home through their colour and scent.

The record-breaking Catrina and its illuminated altar will remain on display until **2 November**.
