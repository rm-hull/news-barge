Email hosting services let you use your company name, instead of a generic one, at the end of your email address. This can count for a lot, helping you to create a good first impression when contacting customers and so on. These services also provide better security, AI tools, templates, and more, so they’re certainly worth considering.

But which email hosting service should you choose? As a tech expert with years of experience, I spent time testing and comparing different email hosting services to help you pick the right option for you.

I’ve focused on dedicated email hosting plans here, but many of the companies also offer web hosting. As such, there's room to expand your vision with a website that can help you gain visibility online and further promote your business or personal project.

Below, you’ll find my pick of the best email hosting services, including some popular web hosting names, such as Hostinger and Ionos, and some dedicated email-only options. Along the way, I looked for a good price, speedy service, reliability, and strong security. You’ll find more details about my testing process below.

## The best email hosting services in 2026 are:

* **Best email hosting overall** – Ionos: From £1 per month, Ionos.co.uk
* **Best budget email hosting** – Hostinger: From 39p per month, Hostinger.com
* **Best email hosting for security** – Proton: From free per month, Proton.me
* **Best email hosting for storage** – Google Workspace: From £2.95 per user per month, Workspace.google.com

## How I tested

Like most people, I’ve used email a lot over the last couple of decades. Over the years, that’s extended to dedicated email hosting. With the entries below and the alternatives I considered, I spent a number of days trying them out and seeing how well they worked and what made the services stand out.

Besides daily use, I also looked at prices and made comparisons to competitors, as well as evaluating features and potential bonuses that might be included with one but not another. I also wanted to make sure that each email host was simple to sign up to and use.

Ionos email hosting is refreshingly simple to set up. It only took me a few minutes to set up an email hosting account, create a domain, and get everything ready for my new email address. In the control panel, you can instantly see your account along with any additional features you may have purchased. From there, you can see how much storage you have available – with 2GB being the bare minimum you get, depending on the plan you sign up to. The inbox is clearly laid out and I’d argue it’s more appealing to look at than something like Google’s Gmail.

![Ionos email hosting](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/13/21/Ionos-email-hosting.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

It all works quickly with a spam filter included as standard, email templates, and a free domain. Those are the core essentials but you can also purchase various add-ons. For example, you can buy extra storage, virus protection, email archiving, and an AI email assistant. Add all those on and Ionos can get pretty expensive. On the other hand, few people are likely to need all of these features so instead, you can stick with paying for exactly what you need and nothing more.

Ionos is a tad in your face about add-ons but it doesn’t take much to dismiss these options and move on. Also, you don’t have to pay for anything truly vital as things like two-factor authentication are still available for free. I was more than happy enough with the spam filter without needing to get extra virus protection. This is a solid option for you and your business or project.

I find that Hostinger is a reliable bet for pretty much any hosting need and that’s certainly the case with its email hosting. It took me a few minutes to set up an account, connect my domain, and instantly access my email. Even better, it’s very cheap, particularly if you opt for a 48-month plan. It does get pricier if you go for shorter durations but, arguably, when dealing with email, you probably want a long-term solution.

![It takes a few minutes to set up an email hosting account with Hostinger (Jennifer Allen/Hostinger)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/03/9/27/Hostinger-email.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

As standard, you get at least 10GB of storage, 10 or 50 forwarding rules, email aliases, and up to 3,000 emails per day per mailbox (depending on your plan). There’s a free domain included with the two pricier plans as well, along with AI support. This AI support will help you with writing or summarising your emails and also crafting outreach emails. I appreciated how it could quickly triage and summarise a busy inbox, saving me time, while its marketing advice was handy too.

All plans come with spam, virus, and phishing protection, and there’s end-to-end encryption too. Rather usefully, Hostinger also guides you through everything you need to know when it comes to connecting your email to other devices or apps. It’s excellent value for money and surprisingly cheap too.

For anyone who wants their emails to feel truly secure, there’s Proton. Remarkably, depending on your needs, it could be free for you. Free users get 1GB of storage space while anyone paying gets at least 15GB of storage, extra email addresses, support for a custom domain, and unlimited folders and labels. Pay for the more premium Proton Unlimited plan and you also get a VPN, encrypted cloud storage, and password manager which could potentially save you money overall.

![With Proton Unlimited, users also get a VPN, cloud storage and password manager (Jennifer Allen/Proton)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/03/9/40/Proton-email.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Whatever you do sign up for, Proton is very straightforward to use. Sign-up only takes a couple of minutes. Make sure to save your recovery details while you’re going through the stages. Then you’re confronted with a pleasant-enough looking inbox. A choice of colour schemes awaits you along with guidance for connecting your email to apps. You can also download the Proton desktop app, although it’s far from vital.

If you’re looking for a business address which will also send out mailers to many people, this isn’t really it. It’s more for individual needs. There is AI support if you want it to create an email for you but you’ll need to pay extra rather than it be included like with Hostinger and others. However, for security features like its password protection and end-to-end encryption, you can’t really do better than Proton.

If you have a Gmail account, you’ll be very familiar with Google Workspace. It’s actually a service I use on a daily basis because it gives me a huge amount of storage space for the price, and it just works without any hassle. Sign-up is predictably easy with the only downside being that you’ll need to buy a domain for an additional fee as one isn’t included. That won’t take you long but it’s something to consider if your budget is tight. Similarly, the pricing plans aren’t the cheapest just for email but you do get a lot of storage space with 2TB easily acquired for under £10 a month at the moment.

![Google Workspace email hosting](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/13/39/Google-Workspace-email-hosting.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

The inbox is easy to figure out with plenty of useful features like filters, unlimited folders, and everything else you could want to organise your emails. There’s Google’s Gemini AI assistant to help you along the way, while you can also use Google Workspace for video meetings, creating videos, and even booking appointments depending on the plan you choose.

It’s a little more than just email, but if you’re looking for a home for a lot of emails and files, you can’t go wrong with Google Workspace. Its straightforward nature is a big selling point as well.

## The verdict: Best email hosting 2026

If you’re seeking email hosting, there’s a varied choice of options at your disposal. I’m a big fan of both Ionos and Hostinger’s approach to doing things. Both plans are inexpensive, include a domain, and take moments to set up. Both providers have simple inboxes to navigate with all the essential features you need. If you do choose to switch to full web hosting, it’s easy to add on to your existing package as well so there’s longevity here.

If you want to focus on security, Proton is a well-respected name in the field and near flawless security wise. It can be more expensive than competitors and it lacks some AI features (without paying), but if you simply want somewhere secure to store your emails, it’s the ideal choice.

Then for ease of use, don’t overlook Google Workspace. It’s definitely not the cheapest plan but you’ll almost certainly already know how to use Gmail and its other services. Also, you can get a lot of storage space at your disposal at all times. That’s great for anyone keen to utilise cloud storage.

## How I tested email hosting services

While I was testing the best email hosting, I paid attention to some key factors that are crucial to providing you with a good email host. These included things like price, ease of use, any extra features, and speed. Here’s a deeper look at what I evaluated:

* **Ease of use:** I sought out email hosting which is simple to set up, easy to use, and straightforward to change or cancel plans. I wanted to find email hosting that anyone can use and benefit from, steering away from options that are overly complicated for the sake of it.

* **Features:** It’s important to find an email host that has all the features you need and also one that’s not too complicated. In reality, many of the best email hosting providers need relatively few features. I considered what services did which roles well.

* **Value for money:** I looked at my choices, compared with the competitors, and evaluated what constituted good value for money. That didn’t always mean choosing the cheapest option but it did mean finding the ones that are worth investing in. I also kept an eye on any potential added costs over the original plan prices.

* **Performance:** I wanted the email hosts included here to be quick to use and browse through different parts of the interface, but also receive emails quickly. Time can be money and at the very least, it can be very inconvenient if emails come through slowly. I wanted to confirm that the services were quick and smooth.
