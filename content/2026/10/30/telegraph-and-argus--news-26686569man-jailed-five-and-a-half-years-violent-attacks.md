A violent man whose “uncontrollable anger” caused him to assault a man in the street and smash up his partner’s home has been jailed for five-and-a-half years.

Kyle Roper was locked up on his 33rd birthday.

Roper was warned by a judge at Bradford Crown Court that his prison sentences “may get longer … if you continue with this kind of destructive, aggressive behaviour in future.”

The court heard how Roper, of no fixed abode in Bradford, had been drinking at Olicana House on Chapel Street with three others in the early hours of April 13, 2024.

Prosecutor Beatrice Allsop said Roper and two others went to buy more alcohol and when they returned the fourth man asked why he had been left behind.

Roper punched him in the face and then, when he fell to the ground, kicked him twice to the face.

The victim, who woke later in hospital, suffered fractures to his face as well as an injury to the brain.

Nine months later Roper smashed up his ex-partner’s home.

And in April of this year, he broke into a house where he believed his ex-partner was present and carried out a “terribly frightening” attack that left all the downstairs windows smashed as well as the television.

The court heard that Roper had 55 previous convictions for 126 offences dating back to when he was a youth including for battery, criminal damage, breach of restraining orders, harassment, burglary, and assault occasioning actual bodily harm.

Mitigating, barrister Rodney Ferm said Roper, who appeared via video link from HMP Leeds, had been on remand for exactly six months.

He said: “He knows that he is about to receive the longest sentence that he has received so far.

“It is also his 33rd birthday today. Neither his mother nor his aunt who are present [in court], and who are concerned and anxious for him, could have wished to be here on his birthday for sentence.

“Today is in no way a celebration for any of the family, or for him.”

He asked the court to consider “moderate” punishment so that Roper could see “some light in the longest tunnel in terms of prison”.

His Honour Judge Colin Burn said the sentences reflected “the real difficulty that you seem to have and, judging by your record, seem to have had for much of your adult life in being able to control yourself.

“The evidence seems to suggest uncontrollable anger, which leads to threats of serious violence and actual serious violence both against people and against their possessions.

“You have got a very bad record of causing damage to people’s possessions. You also have previous convictions for violence, as well.”

He sentenced Roper to three years and six months for GBH with intent in 2024, plus a further two years, to run consecutively, for burglary on April 11 this year.

He also imposed a restraining order banning him from any contact with the victim of the GBH for ten years.

He said Roper would serve two-thirds of his sentence — around 44 months — in custody less time spent on remand and would be subject to licence on release.
