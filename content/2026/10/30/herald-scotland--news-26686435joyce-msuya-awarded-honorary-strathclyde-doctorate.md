A University of Strathclyde graduate who has held senior roles at the United Nations has received an honorary doctorate in recognition of her work in humanitarian action, international development and environmental leadership.

Joyce Msuya, a senior UN official from Tanzania, was presented with the degree by the University of Strathclyde in recognition of more than 25 years of service.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21653716.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Joyce Msuya. *(Image: Guy Hinks)*

She studied biochemistry and immunology at the university, graduating in 1992, before completing postgraduate studies in Canada.

Ms Msuya joined the World Bank in 1998, going on to hold senior leadership positions across Africa, Asia and North America. During her time there, she helped establish the World Bank Group’s first office in the Republic of Korea and led major development programmes in East Asia and the Pacific.

In 2018, she took on a leadership role at the UN Environment Programme, first as interim and later as deputy executive director, helping to lead its turnaround.

She moved to the United Nations Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) in 2022, becoming assistant secretary-general for humanitarian affairs and deputy emergency relief coordinator. In the role, she helped oversee humanitarian operations in more than 60 countries.

In 2024, she served as acting under-secretary-general for humanitarian affairs and emergency relief coordinator, advising the UN secretary-general and the organisation’s 193 member states during a period of significant geopolitical and humanitarian challenges.

Ms Msuya has maintained close links with Strathclyde, receiving its Alumna of the Year award in 2019 and supporting the university’s Centre for Sustainable Development through international collaboration and knowledge-sharing.

Joyce Msuya said: "To be honoured by Strathclyde is a privilege.

"This University was my springboard.

"I widened my imagination about the world, what I might become and about what an education is for.

"But Strathclyde taught me something more enduring – knowledge has a purpose."

Professor Stuart McIntyre, who conferred the degree, said: "A degree, earned here at Strathclyde, became the foundation for a career that has influenced international policy and improved responses to some of humanity's most pressing challenges.

"Her achievements embody the very best of a Strathclyde education: knowledge applied to the world's most pressing challenges, leadership in the service of others, and a determination to make a positive difference in the world."
