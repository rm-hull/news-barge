A PIONEERING clinic is marking 10 years of protecting children’s sight and shaping the future of eye care.

The University of Bradford’s Myopia Management Clinic, one of the first of its kind in the UK, has over 100 patients aged from four to their mid-twenties, offering treatments to slow the progression of myopia.

Better known as short-sightedness, myopia is a growing global issue with potentially serious long-term eye health risks.

Kathryn Webber, clinical teaching fellow and lead for the clinic, said: "When we established the clinic ten years ago, myopia management was still a relatively new area of clinical practice in the UK.

"We were among the early adopters, helping to build awareness of approaches that could slow the progression of myopia in children and young people.

"What has been remarkable is the pace of change.

"Ten years ago there were relatively few treatment options available.

"Today we have a range of evidence-based interventions, including specialist spectacle lenses, contact lenses and, most recently, low-dose atropine eye drops that have recently become available in the UK."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21645499.jpg?type=mds-article-620) Bradford Eye Clinic *(Image: Supplied)*

The clinic offers a range of evidence-based treatments, including specialist contact lenses, spectacle lenses, and low-dose atropine eye drops.

These interventions aim not just to correct vision, but to reduce the risk of serious complications in later life, such as glaucoma, retinal detachment, and myopic maculopathy.

Ms Webber stated the clinic’s goal is to protect eye health in the long term.

She said: "The aim is not simply to correct a child's vision today, but to help protect their eye health for decades to come.

"That's why early intervention is so important."

The clinic also supports students in the university’s School of Optometry and Vision Science, which will celebrate its centenary in 2027.

Ms Webber said: "The clinic sits at the intersection of patient care, teaching and research.

"Our students gain experience in an area of practice that is expanding rapidly, while patients benefit from some of the latest evidence-based approaches to myopia management."

Parents have praised the clinic for its patient-centred care.

Stephanie Whaling, whose daughter Elsie has attended the clinic for several years, said: "Finding the Myopia Clinic at Bradford University gave me knowledge, reassurance and most importantly the feeling that there was something proactive we could do to help.

"We travel quite a distance for her appointments, but I wouldn’t take her anywhere else."
