A plan aimed at giving children and young people with special educational needs and disabilities earlier, better support has been approved in North Yorkshire.

The Department for Education approved the scheme, developed by the North Yorkshire Local Area SEND Partnership.

The plan aims to improve support, inclusion and access to services for children and young people with SEND.

Councillor Janet Sanderson, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for children and families, said: "This is good news for children and young people in North Yorkshire.

"It is an important milestone in developing the offer of support for children, delivering that support earlier and building greater confidence in our local offer.

"Our ambition is to work collaboratively to ensure more children with additional needs can achieve and thrive in local mainstream schools, rooted in their own communities alongside their friends.

"We know that change to the education system for children and young people with additional needs may be concerning for some families.

"The partnership is committed to coproducing change with children, young people, parents and carers.

"The approval of the plan is one important step in that journey."

The three-year programme will focus on earlier intervention, inclusion in mainstream education, specialist support and closer work between health, education and social care.

The plan also sets out the “Experts at Hand” model, giving schools faster access to educational psychologists, speech and language therapists, occupational therapists and specialist teachers.

Cllr Sanderson said: "The aim is to identify needs earlier and support more children in our local schools and settings.

"We are strengthening access to specialist practitioners including educational psychologists and therapists as well as embedding the use of our Inclusive Mainstream Toolkit that has been coproduced with our Parent Carer Forum."

Other measures include expanding specialist bases in mainstream schools and widening alternative provision, with more outreach support and places for primary-aged children.

El Mayhew, the council’s corporate director for children and young people's services, said: "This plan is about building on what is already working well in the county while responding to what children, young people and families have told us they need.
