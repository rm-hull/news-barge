Two more drug dealers have been removed from the streets of Bradford.

Jim Whiteley and Ryan Kenyon, who peddled cocaine, have been jailed for four years and three years, respectively.

Whiteley, 31, was described at Bradford Crown Court as having an operational and managerial role within a chain.

Kenyon, 28, was dubbed “an enthusiastic and co-operative employee”.

The court heard how Whiteley was a passenger in a VW Golf that was stopped by police on May 7, 2025, following a chase.

He was found with three snap bags of cocaine and two mobile phones.

More snap bags and weighing scales were found during a search of his house.

Messages offering to sell drugs were found on one phone after it was downloaded as well as photographs of suspected cocaine packages and “copious amounts of correspondence” between Whiteley and Kenyon.

Prosecutor Celine Kart told the court that Kenyon was asking for wages and offering to “work the estate”.

She said both Whiteley and Kenyon were working together.

A total of 179.1g of cocaine was seized by police, which a drug expert said had a street value of up to £3,380.

Miss Kart said three dealer lines were found on the mobile phones and that there was “a close nexus” with the original source of the drugs.

She said both Whiteley and Kenyon had operational or management functions within a chain as they were running, storing, or weighing up cocaine and selling direct to users.

She said there were children in Kenyon’s property where drugs were stored, and a crossbow was seized from an address linked to Whiteley.

Police also recovered almost £1,000 in cash.

The court heard that Kenyon had three previous convictions for three offences including possession of Class A drugs.

Whiteley had four previous convictions for five offences but that they were “not of significant relevance”.

Both men appeared in court via video link from HMP Leeds.

Mitigating for Whiteley, barrister Jessica Heggie accepted that he used mobile phones to street deal drugs but that there was one drugs line rather than multiple.

She told the court that Whiteley had experienced a number of “life-altering attacks” due to mistaken identity but he “nearly lost his life in 2017”.

She said injuries he received had led to PTSD, severe anxiety, and depression and that he turned to using cocaine.

She said self-medicating with cocaine had in turn seen him turn to dealing Class A drugs.

She said that the possession of the crossbow was linked to significant anxiety about being attacked.

The court heard that Whiteley had spent 330 days on remand.

Mitigating for Kenyon, Syam Soni said he had suffered abuse during childhood, had been diagnosed with PTSD, anxiety, and depression and had neurodiversity needs.

He was described as being of a low risk of re-offending with Mr Soni adding: “This offending was committed whilst he was at a particularly low ebb.”

He had spent 42 days in custody.

Sentencing the pair, Mr Recorder Patrick Palmer referenced the “terrible trade” of drug-dealing and how the supply of Class A drugs to addicts “ruin their lives and the lives of their families.”

He said both Whiteley and Kenyon held significant roles in the drugs chain with Whiteley “clearly in an operational and management role”.

He said Kenyon was “an enthusiastic and co-operative employee”.

He told both men that they would serve off their sentences in custody minus time spent on remand and then be released on licence.

He ordered the forfeiture and destruction of the drugs seized.
