IT's a bright autumn day in Glasgow and three senior UK ministers are speaking to the media about what Andy Burnham's government is doing to put Britain on a war footing.

The Chancellor John Healey, joined by Defence Secretary Wes Streeting and Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander have been visiting BAE Systems in Scotstoun where HMS Glasgow and HMS Cardiff are nearing completion.

The two Type 45 frigates are among eight of these type of vessels being built for the Royal Navy and are designed to support anti-submarine warfare.

By Nato's reckoning they could well be needed soon.

Military chiefs have been warning that Russia is building up its military capabilities and would be in a position to attack western Europe by 2030.

Last weekend Mr Streeting said Britain must be ready for war in 1,000 days and that the UK must "be ready with our allies to fight and win".

The stark message came days after the government launched its "Be Ready" campaign, advising households to stockpile non-perishable food, bottles of water, blankets and radios in the event of a cyberattack or severe weather incident that could shut down critical national infrastructure.

Clearly, Russian President Vladimir Putin has been closely briefed on developments in Britain.

At a press conference on Thursday in Moscow, he told journalists: "Many people keep talking about preparing for war by 2030. Is that right or wrong?".

"First of all, we are ready, but we don't want any war with Europe. As for them, if anyone wants it, let them prepare. But they have not achieved their goal of inflicting a strategic defeat on Russia. It's unlikely that this will be possible by 2030. And if things go too far, there will be no winners at all. Let no one forget that."

![Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey (left) and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting (right) on board HMS Glasgow during a visit to BAE Systems Scotstoun in Glasgow on Friday.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21653775.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey (left) and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting (right) on board HMS Glasgow during a visit to BAE Systems Scotstoun in Glasgow on Friday. *(Image: Jane Barlow)*

In Edinburgh, First Minister John Swinney has made a series of interventions where he has raised fears over how well-protected Scotland is by the UK military.

He first raised the matter publicly in January this year.

"The United Kingdom has chosen not to base a single offshore patrol vessel, frigate or destroyer in Scotland – requiring vessels to be dispatched from the south coast of England when the flagship of the Russian Northern Fleet arrived off Scotland," he told the audience at Edinburgh University's McEwan Hall.

He stepped up his concerns earlier this month at the SNP conference saying: “In this uncertain world, what is certain is that the UK is failing to keep Scotland secure" adding that "Scotland’s vast coastline is left dangerously exposed.”

And on Thursday he told Holyrood: "Our country is being left defenceless by the appalling approach to defence taken by the UK Government."

![First Minister John Swinney has raised concerns that Scotland is not being sufficiently protected by the UK miitary.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21653801.jpg?type=mds-article-620)First Minister John Swinney has raised concerns that Scotland is not being sufficiently protected by the UK miitary. *(Image: Gordon Terris)*

Mr Streeting was asked about the First Minister's concerns by journalists gathered in Scotstoun on Friday afternoon.

In response the Defence Secretary accused Mr Swinney of being "irresponsible", that his claims were "unfounded" and that he was "playing party politics with national defence and security".

Mr Streeting pointed to the deployment of the aircraft carrier the HMS Prince of Wales as part of a Nato operation in the North Atlantic as “very visible proof that what the First Minister said is both irresponsible and unfounded”.

![Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey (right) and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting (left) on board HMS Glasgow during a visit to BAE Systems Scotstoun in Glasgow.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21653809.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey (right) and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting (left) on board HMS Glasgow during a visit to BAE Systems Scotstoun in Glasgow *(Image: Jane Barlow)*

But doubts remain.

Last year defence analyst Ed Arnold, then a senior fellow at the Royal United Services Institute, and now a Senior Advisor in Defence at the D Group consultancy, warned that Scotland would be a top target for Russia.

“RAF Lossiemouth hosts all of our Poseidon P-8 maritime patrol aircraft – effectively a lot of Nato’s capability to track Russian submarines in the North Sea and the North Atlantic comes from Lossiemouth,” he said.

“Lossiemouth is absolutely a target of the Russians, whether that is through a strike, which is more difficult, or more likely, a direct attack from the land.”

He added: “If it is Kremlin deliberate policy to go attack Nato, the Kremlin will want to do it when it is to their advantage so they will strike first.

“And if they strike first, Faslane and Lossiemouth at a minimum would be their first targets, over London."

At a conference on Scotland's role in UK security, held in Edinburgh earlier this month, Professor Kristen Harkness, of the School of International Relations at St Andrew's University, warned that Scotland's large coastline and numerous island communities made it particular vulnerable in terms of potential Russian sabotage of sub sea cables which carry power and communication links from the mainland.

"What is of most concern to Scotland is the intensification of sabotage of European under sea critical infrastructure," she told the gathering.

She pointed to a possibility that because Scotland is geographically close to where Russian has been attempting to damage sub sea cables off Norway and in the Baltic, Scotland could be a "target of ease".

The Herald asked the Defence Secretary if he believed Scotland would be a "top target" for Russia, pointing out that Putin said last night he was ready now.

Mr Streeting did not answer the question directly but underlined the work going on to improve our military capability and the resilience of society of the whole of the UK.

"The people of Scotland and right across the United Kingdom can draw comfort and security in the knowledge that we have some extraordinary capability at our disposal, across our armed forces as well as our special forces and the protection of the Nato alliance, that principle that an attack on one is an attack on all," he told The Herald.

"I don't think Putin would be foolish enough to seek a direct war with Nato today but what I can’t be certain of is at some point in the future he might not try, and that is Nato’s threat assessment and that is why we are building our capability, building our defence industry, including here in Scotland, and building our alliances, so that we are strong and therefore able to deter any potential Russian threat”.

Mr Streeting said such investment is being made “because we can see in Ukraine that the nature of warfare is changing, the nature of the threat is rising, so we can’t afford to stand still”.

He insisted: “The people of Scotland and people right across the United Kingdom can draw comfort and security in the knowledge that we have some extraordinary capability at our disposal across our Armed Forces, as well as our special forces, and the protection of the Nato alliance.”

Mr Streeting was also asked to give more details on the work to be done over the next 1000 to put the UK in a position where it could with Nato allies win a war against Russia.

"We are investing in our capability, we are investing in our defence industrial base and we are investing in our alliances, those things make us stronger," he said.

"The stronger we are, the stronger our deterrents and the less likely we are to find ourselves at war. We don't seek confrontation with with Russia. We have no quarrel with the Russian people. But what we can't avoid is the hard reality that at the same time President Putin says he is not interested in war in Europe. He is currently waging one. He gave the Ukrainians those assurances, that he had no plans to invade, only weeks before the Russian invasion took place."

In his Budget on Wednesday the Chancellor underlined the government's commitment to take defence spending up to 3.5% of GDP by 2035 although delayed until next year how he would raise the extra spending.

General Lord Dannatt, the former head of the Army, was critical: “Wes Streeting said we will be at war in 1000 days. But is it not irresponsible of the former defence secretary, now the Chancellor, to squander 365 of those days to say they will map the path to getting to 3 per cent of GDP in 2030 next year? Are we fiddling while Rome burns?”

At Scotstoun on Friday Mr Healey insisted he was serious about delivering the resources needed to beef up the Armed Forces.

"I resigned as Defence Secretary because we weren't able to make the commitment that I felt were required to keep this country safe," he said.

"I did this in the Budget this week, the points I resigned are the points that I resolved this week."
