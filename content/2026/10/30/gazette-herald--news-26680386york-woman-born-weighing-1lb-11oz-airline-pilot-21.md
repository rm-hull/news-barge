*Meet the York woman born weighing 1lb 11oz who is now a commercial airline pilot at 21*

*By MAXINE GORDON*

'Little Lucy' Atkinson is flying high as she reaches the milestone of her 21st birthday.

Born extremely premature and weighing just 1lb 11oz, Lucy defied the odds to survive.

And she has beaten them again to achieve her dream of becoming a commercial airline pilot.

Lucy was dubbed 'Little Lucy' when she was born at 26 weeks and was so tiny she could have fitted in her mother Fiona's hand.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644920.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Little Lucy - born at just 1lb and 11oz

Lucy, a former pupil of St Peter's School in York, got the flying bug after being invited to sit in the cockpit on a holiday flight as an eight-year-old girl.

A flying lesson at the age of 15 sealed her fate.

Speaking to The Press as Lucy celebrated her 21st this week, her mum Fiona said: "She is a miracle. It is like a dream!"

Lucy beat the odds not only to survive but to become a commercial pilot. Only one in five airline cadets make the grade and, globally, only eight per cent of pilots are female.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644922.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lucy Atkinson - former York school girl - now a commercial pilot. Image supplied

When Fiona's waters broke just six months into her pregnancy, medics warned her the baby might not survive.

"They told me the baby wasn't very viable and when she was born they didn't know if she would live or not. She needed three blood transfusions and had an infection.

"It was horrific; she was wired up to machines and you didn't know what was going to happen.

"She weighed just 1lb 11oz and was as big as my hand. She couldn't even wear a premature nappy, they just put some cotton wool under her bottom."

"It was a case of one step forward, one step back. We didn't know what the outcome would be.

"She could have been blind, deaf or had spina bifida. She has got nothing wrong with her at all. She is very, very fortunate."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644924.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lucy Atkinson as a little girl; she is now a commercial pilot

Fiona said she hoped Lucy's story would help other families coping with premature births.

When Lucy came home from hospital, it was a huge milestone, but the challenges didn't end there, recalls Fiona.

"She remained dependent on oxygen for another three months, and there was always the worry that she might suffer a setback. Yet throughout every challenge, Lucy continued to do what she had done since the day she arrived so unexpectedly – she kept fighting.

"Thankfully, Lucy grew into a happy, healthy little girl with a larger-than-life personality," said Fiona, who lives at Deighton, near York.

As a thank you, the family have undertaken various fundraisers over the years in aid of BLISS, which supports premature and sick babies.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644921.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Photo of Lucy as a child - if the cap fits!

And reflecting on Lucy's milestone birthday, Fiona said the cherry on the cake has been witnessing her daughter achieve her dream of becoming a pilot.

After completing her A-levels, Lucy left Yorkshire for London at the age of 18 to turn her passion into a profession, enrolling on a pilot training course.

Following her ground-school training, she travelled to the United States to begin the practical flight training required to become a commercial pilot.

After seven months, she continued her studies in Cork, where she gained her airline pilot qualifications.

Lucy secured a role at Leeds East Airport, the very place where her aviation journey had first begun five years earlier.

As she celebrated her 21st birthday this week she also began her career as a commercial pilot.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644923.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lucy Atkinson - former York school girl - now a commercial pilot. Image supplied

Fiona said: "Statistically not a lot of girls end up being airline pilots. She's done extremely well. We are very proud of her."

She added: "Twenty-one years ago, we were facing uncertainty, fear and countless unanswered questions. Today, that same little girl is living her dream.

"For any parents currently sitting beside an incubator and wondering what the future may hold, or for any child with a dream that feels out of reach, our message is simple: never underestimate what can be achieved through hard work, determination and perseverance.

"Sometimes the very best things really do come in little parcels."
