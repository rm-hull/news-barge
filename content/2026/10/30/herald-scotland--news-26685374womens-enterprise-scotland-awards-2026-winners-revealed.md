A Stirling jewellery business has taken two top prizes at an awards ceremony celebrating women-led companies across Scotland.

Lily Blanche was named Outstanding Business of the Year as well as Digital Business of the Year at the Women’s Enterprise Scotland Awards 2026. The awards were presented before a sell-out audience of more than 250 guests at the voco Grand Central Hotel in Glasgow, with established and emerging businesses recognised across ten categories.

Orkney-based Sheila Fleet Jewellery was named Rural Business of the Year, while Inverness-based Highland Campervans received the Strategic Growth Business of the Year award.

Working With Me Limited, a workplace technology and consultancy company also based in Inverness, was named Business to Watch of the Year. Pitlochry-based Consent Collective, a social enterprise based founded by psychologist Dr Nina Burrowes, won Purpose-Led Business of the Year.

Other winners included 24 HR Pharmacy, which took Innovation Business of the Year; Flourish with Deborah Limited, which was named Start-up Business of the Year; and The Astute VA, which won Service Excellence Business of the Year.

DEL FIN Group received the Inspiring Leader of the Year award.

Rachael Arnold, founder of the global Creative Entrepreneurs Club and Entrepreneur in Residence at the Royal Conservatoire of Scotland, was presented with the Lifetime Achievement Award.

“Huge congratulations to all last night’s finalists and winners," said Carolyn Currie, chief executive of Women's Enterprise Scotland. "We admire the determination they’ve shown in reaching their goals.

“Scottish businesses have faced mounting pressures in recent years. Women-owned and women-led businesses have also had to fight for a fair and proportionate share of business growth support, so it’s right that we celebrate the innovation, talent and vision behind their success.

“Our awards showcase women leaders who reflect the diverse, innovative and progressive business community Scotland has to offer. These ambitious and resourceful women are exactly the role models who will inspire our next generation of business leaders.”

She added: “With our economy under increasing pressure, unlocking women’s economic potential has never been more important. Unlocking the multi-billion-pound economic opportunity from closing the persistent gender gap in Scotland’s business landscape must be a priority.”

Women’s Enterprise Scotland was established in 2012 as a not-for-profit community interest company working to close the gender gap in enterprise participation.
