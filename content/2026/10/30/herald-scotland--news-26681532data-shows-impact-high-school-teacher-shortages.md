A survey of secondary schools has revealed the significant impacts of teacher shortages, shining a light on the consequences for pupils across Scotland.

The data, gathered by School Leaders' Scotland, reveals that students’ access to the curriculum is being restricted as provision is cut and subjects are removed.

## **Vacancies**

Nearly two-thirds of respondents said that their school is currently dealing with teacher vacancies. The report also notes that this data does not reveal the full scale of gaps in secondary schools, because in some cases subjects that have now been entirely removed from the timetable will not be classed as a formal vacancy.

## **Supply teachers**

Despite repeated government pledges to increase teacher numbers, the availability of supply staff - which is crucial for secondary schools - remains a serious problem.

## **Reasons for shortages**

Survey respondents highlighted various factors driving the shortage of teachers in many Scottish secondary schools. A lack of qualified applicants was the most common complaints, although this was closely followed by a lack of applicants generally.

Many schools in rural and island locations are also struggling to recruit the right number of specialist teachers, with headteachers mentioning problems with housing and transport in their more detailed responses.

School leaders also felt that perceptions of how difficult teaching has become, as well as the scale of the workload crisis facing the profession, are barriers to attracting more teachers into schools.

## **Impact of shortages**

Schools' inability to secure sufficient numbers of specialist staff is having a major impact on the educational experiences of pupils.

Nearly two-thirds of respondents said that their school has had to reduce provision in some specific subject areas, and more than 40% said that they now offer a reduced range of subjects as part of their overall curriculum.

Another common consequence of teacher shortages is a need for classes to be taught by staff who lack the required subject specialism: for example, a number of schools have reported being forced to ask science teachers to run maths classes in S1-3.
