According to the organizers, approximately 350 events will take place at more than 115 locations in the capital.

Berlin's Festival of Science and Culture runs for ten days. (File photo)

Ten days of science: Berlin Science Week begins on Sunday. Through November 10, research centers, museums, libraries and cultural venues will open their doors.

Most of the events at the Science Festival are free and open to anyone interested. Advance registration is recommended for some events. Visitors can, for example, get hands-on with experiments or take part in discussions and performances. According to the organizers, this year’s theme, "In Touch," is meant to put people at the center of the festival. It explores questions such as how research impacts our lives and how it connects people.

The ten-day program is varied. For example, anyone interested can put their knowledge of quantum physics to the test in an escape room at the Einstein Center for Digital Future or see just how well artificial intelligence tracks their movements during an AI Dance Challenge at the Jülich Research Center. Other events take participants on a tour of the historic Dahlem research district or explore the question of how research is transformed into art.

In addition to the scientific and cultural venues throughout the city, there is also a festival hub at the Haus der Kulturen der Welt (HKW). From November 6 to 8, the venue will host live talks, workshops, film screenings and performances. Anyone wishing to attend these events must register online for a free festival pass. At the opening event on November 6, for example, Canadian computer scientist and Turing Award winner Yoshua Bengio will speak about trust in artificial intelligence.

According to the organizers, this year for the first time there will be a dedicated program for families and children at the festival center, featuring live shows and hands-on activities for children ages 4 and up. In the hall of the German Aerospace Center, the youngest visitors can experiment with thermal imaging cameras or train for a flight into space. Berlin Science Week is organized by the Falling Walls Foundation and supported by, among others, the Berlin Senate Department for Science and Research, Health and Care.
