There was a time when creating a website was a bit of a headache. It involved either coding it from the ground up or paying for software that was complicated and expensive. Nowadays, thanks to the best website builders, these roadblocks are long gone – it's quick and easy to get a website up and running, regardless of previous experience.

But which website builder should you use? As a tech journalist, I’ve spent some time researching and using many of them, narrowing down the list to a handful of the best options. The names that made the list are heavyweights you will have heard of: Wix, Hostinger, Squarespace and Shopfiy. The ones that stood out were easy to use and had extra features, such as help with SEO. Ultimately, the one you choose should depend on what you personally prioritise – whether that's affordability, or the best tools for ecommerce.

Read on and I’ll take you through my experiences with each website builder, so you’ll know what your money will get you.

## The best website builders in 2026 are:

* **Best website builder overall** – Wix: From £9 per month, Wix.com
* **Best budget website builder** – Hostinger: From £1.49 per month, Hostinger.com
* **Best creative website builder** – Squarespace: From £12 per month, Squarespace.com
* **Best ecommerce website builder** – Shopify: From £19 per month, Shopify.com

I spent considerable time checking out each of the entries below (and others not featured). I went through each step of the process from signing up to creating a basic website through each website builder’s method, and then seeing how easily I could tweak and customise it.

During this testing time, I also looked at what features each website builder provides and evaluated how useful they actually are. I researched prices for each plan and learned the market so I could determine what was good value. There are more details on the testing process at the bottom of this article.

Wix is that rare thing – a website builder that I’m confident you could hand over to anyone and they could figure it out. It’s the most beginner-friendly option out there. Starting out, you can follow some AI insights to create a website but I preferred the design site option where you can drag and drop elements to create your site. Whichever method you use, it’s very intuitive and logical.

![Wix website building testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/15/52/Wix-website-building-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Even better, Wix isn’t just about static websites. It easily slots in plugins such as video content, a bookings system, or an online store. It’ll happily make suggestions based on your plans too so if you forget something, Wix will likely remember it for you. Another useful feature is its Quick Actions panel which means you can quickly create a blog post, add other content, or even set up marketing campaigns. Interestingly, you can also get it to create AI-generated content.

Wix isn’t perfect because it’s rather limited. Unlike a conventional web hosting service, you can’t upload your own website nor can you use WordPress or other services. You’re tied into the Wix way of doing things, which is good but could feel restrictive eventually. It’s also a little more expensive than most but I think it’s ideal if you want to launch your website quickly (and for it to look great).

Hostinger is one of the best web hosting services around, and it also has a very comprehensive handful of website builders. Rather than just focusing on one website builder, it has three options if you sign up to its web hosting. There’s the WordPress-based blog approach which uses AI prompts to create a neat looking template for your site. It also suggests plugins that may be relevant to your needs.

![Hostinger website building testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/15/18/Hostinger-website-building-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Additionally, there’s the dedicated website builder which creates things based on prompts and Horizons which is a truly advanced model. The latter takes a little time as you watch the code and design be made in your browser based on the information you give it. You can provide Horizons with a lot of insight into what you want and it does a pretty good job of translating that into something that looks attractive. Customisation is as simple as telling it to change a colour or move something around.

Away from its website builder elements, Hostinger is also a very useful web host provider. You can easily upload your own files, and develop your site in a more traditional way. If you want to learn website design but get something online quickly, it’s a good mixture of elements so that both can be achieved on a timeline that works for you.

Squarespace works a lot like Wix in that it’s a dedicated website builder that takes out the technical side of things. It produces some beautiful and elegant results with you simply typing in a few prompts and Squarespace guiding you through the steps of building a website. It offers a solid free trial that means you get to see most of what’s to come too, so you know exactly what you’re getting.

![Squarespace website building testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/15/09/Squarespace-website-building-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Squarespace leans a little bit into the idea that your website is to sell something, with various plugins added to help you promote and sell your products, but you can also use it as a portfolio. In either case, choose a template or get the Blueprint AI builder to create something more bespoke. Once you’ve finished the basics, you can edit the site through the drag-and-drop interface to get things looking just how you want them. Squarespace also offers up an extensive to-do list reminding you to tweak SEO features and do various things that mean you’ll be noticed and your site will look more professional.

It’s a very neat system but one that has the same issues as Wix. It’s fairly restrictive compared to conventional web hosting which may or may not be a problem, depending on your intentions. It’s also generally more expensive than Wix and slightly more expensive than regular web hosting so it’s good to be aware of that before committing.

Shopify is a comprehensive website builder for small businesses. Its sole focus is on making it easy for you to sell your products, either online or in-store. Rather than working as a conventional website builder, you’re left to customise your store’s template which works pretty well. It’s reminiscent of the WordPress way of doing things but more stylish most of the time. Editing is easy to do and AI assistance is always readily available to help you achieve your aims.

![Shopify website building testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/15/54/Shopify-website-building-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Adding a product is just as easy. If you’ve ever used eBay to list anything, it’s a very similar process so no technical knowledge is needed. Just make sure your product photos look great. You can also add customers and companies (such as the clients you deal with), while there’s areas for marketing and applying discounts, too. It’s all very shop-front focused and that’s why I like it. Analytics and Shopify’s finance section means you can quickly see how well your store is doing, and leaves you in control.

If you want an online presence only online, you don’t need Shopify. However, if you want to use a social media presence to channel people to your store, this is the builder you’re going to want. It truly is packed with every feature imaginable. It’s a little pricey at first glance (but a free trial exists along with offers), and if you want to make money online, this is a good route to pursue.

## The verdict: Best website builders 2026

The best website builder for most people is undoubtedly Wix. It’s incredibly easy to use even if you’re not great with technology, and its results typically look great. You’ll have a website up in no time and Wix offers plenty of ways to help you refine it and also improve your SEO – which is crucial for being high on Google search rankings.

However, if you want flexibility, go for Hostinger. It is also fantastic and it’s much more affordable in many cases. Its website builders are still very good (if not quite as attractive as Wix’s offerings), and being able to upload files via the file manager and create your website the traditional way is useful.

If you have more specific needs such as highlighting an attractive portfolio of work or selling products, that’s where Squarespace and Shopify come in. Shopify is the website builder that any online store needs, while Squarespace looks beautiful. Both cost more than either Wix or Hostinger, but you get good features in exchange.

## How the best website builders were tested

When spending extensive time with the best website builders, I had to consider a few key elements to make sure that they were tested the right way. Here are the factors I considered while evaluating what constituted the best website builders:

* **Ease of use**: Potentially the most important thing about a great website builder, you need it to be easy to use. Drag-and-drop options are a big deal here but you also want it to be simple enough to make changes to your domain, as well as set up ecommerce elements and other arguably trickier parts of web design. I looked at all this and also looked at how easy it was to make changes to my plan, and add a domain to my website. I also evaluated any insights or advice given throughout the process from each website builder.

* **Features**: Tied into ease of use, I wanted to check that each of the best website builders had all the features you need for a website. That can be as simple as a good-looking site, but I also checked for plugins such as ecommerce elements, video carousels, how easy it was to add blog posts, and everything else imaginable. I looked at the ideal options for the new website but also checked that there was room for expansion when it comes to your business or personal project growing.

* **Value for money**: I wanted to see which website builders represented good value for money. That doesn’t automatically mean these are the cheapest, but that they’re worth your financial investment. I looked out for any potentially hidden charges and also compared them to the competition to check you’re not paying over the odds for what you get.

* **Performance**: I wanted a website builder that was quick to enact my ideas, and I also wanted a resulting website that loaded quickly. I looked at reliability figures and speed. That came down to server performance and potentially-messy code. Basically, I wanted everything to run smoothly for me and my userbase.
