A senior police officer only discovered his force was taking a man to court for picking up a dead deer from the A66 when he read about it in The Northern Echo.

Church magazine editor Guy Carpenter has been embroiled in the deer drama since he scooped the carcass of the A66 at Greta Bridge, near Barnard Castle, on June 7.

The 41-year-old photographer was taken to court over the ordeal earlier this year, months after being stopped in his tracks by blue-light officers and told he was breaking the law.

Last week, Guy was invited to Barnard Castle Police Station for a "friendly chat", where a superintendent told him the case would be dropped due to the lack of public interest.

The senior officer even told him he found out about the case from The Northern Echo.

Guy said he was "grateful" - but argued it "should never have gotten to that point” and that it has been a "complete waste of time, money, and effort".

It came as the self-confessed nature lover's out-of-court resolution deadline loomed - meaning if it had not been agreed, he would be due back at Peterlee Magistrates' Court on Monday.

Today, Guy, editor of the magazine for the Holmedale Benefice Churches in the Richmond area, has spoken out about how the ordeal “ruined his year” and was “complete waste” of resources.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21426577.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Guy Carpenter *(Image: CONTRIBUTOR)*

“I am relieved and grateful, but I am frustrated that it had to be in the circumstances that it was,” he told The Northern Echo. “It should not have been brought forward.

“It was never in the public interest. It should never have gotten to that point. It ruined my year, it has made my life partly miserable.

“It has been a source of my stress for people around me and who worry about me. I am still processing that.”

Guy said the case was dropped last week after he got a call from a sergeant asking him to meet a superintendent for a "friendly chat”.

He attended Barnard Castle Police Station with a retired judge friend of his.

“Right at the outset, he said they were dropping the case because there wasn't enough public interest," he said. “His awareness came from the Echo’s reporting of it.

“He spent the rest of the meeting explaining their reasoning behind it - but he pointed out that I had committed an offence under The Deer Act.”

He added: “I don't quite understand why it took nearly five months, a superintendent officer, and the Echo’s coverage in order to get anyone to have seen any sense.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21426576.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Guy Carpenter *(Image: CONTRIBUTOR)*

The case was officially withdrawn by the court on October 20.

A force spokesman said: “Following a review, officers met Mr Carpenter and informed him that an application would be made to the court to have the case against him withdrawn.

“There is a common misconception that deer carcasses can be removed from the roadside without permission of the landowner. It is, in fact, an offence to do so under the Deer Act 1991.

“However, in this specific case, we now understand that Mr Carpenter had a genuinely-held belief that he was allowed to remove the animal from the roadside and an application is therefore being made to withdraw the case”.

Durham County Council deputy leader Darren Grimes was among those questioning the public interest of taking it to court, arguing that he did not think the public interest test had been “properly applied" in his case.

Benjamin Elks, grassroots development manager at the TaxPayers' Alliance, said that taxpayers would “struggle to understand why scarce police and court resources are being spent pursuing a case like this”.
