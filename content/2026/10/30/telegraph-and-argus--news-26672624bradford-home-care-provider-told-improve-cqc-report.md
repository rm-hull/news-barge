A home care provider supporting more than 140 people across the Bradford district has been told it must improve after inspectors found failings that put service users at risk of harm.

Care 24-7 Limited, based in Shipley, was rated Requires Improvement overall following an inspection by the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

While the provider received Good ratings for Safe, Caring and Responsive, inspectors rated Effective and Well-led as Requires Improvement.

Care 24-7 has disputed the findings, saying it does not believe the report accurately reflects the care it provides and that it plans to challenge the inspection outcome.

## Staff knowledge concerns raised

Inspectors found some staff did not have the correct knowledge for the job.

The report said: "Staff did not always have the appropriate training or knowledge to fulfil their role. Medicines documentation at times, lacked important information. This put people at risk of harm."

The service, which provides personal care to people in their own homes, was supporting 143 people at the time of the inspection.

## Safety culture criticised

The CQC found the service lacked a positive safety culture and said trends and learning relating to risk were not always identified, including safeguarding concerns.

Inspectors noted that while appropriate referrals were made to other services to help manage risks, these actions were not always fully documented.

Concerns were also raised over medicines records, with inspectors finding important information was sometimes missing from documentation.

## Families report inconsistency

People and their relatives generally spoke positively about staff, but concerns were raised about inconsistencies in care.

One relative told inspectors: "I do feel safe with some of them, but some people don't know what to do and I tell them."

The report also noted that professionals, staff and people using the service raised concerns about the consistency of carers.

## Governance failings identified

Inspectors found the service did not have robust systems and processes in place to support good governance.

Although leaders had auditing systems in place, these failed to identify key omissions in care records and documentation.

The CQC said people's records did not always include important information about their needs and health conditions.

As a result, the provider was found to be in breach of regulations relating to governance.

## Positive feedback despite concerns

Despite the areas requiring improvement, many people praised the care they received.

One relative told inspectors: "[My relative] is getting a very good service and we are quite satisfied. [The staff] are very nice and give a wonderful service."

Most people said staff were caring and compassionate, while service users also reported feeling able to raise concerns with management.

One person told inspectors: "I can't really fault the management."

## Improvement plan underway

The inspection took place between August 26 and September 7, with the report published on October 20.

The CQC noted that the provider had responded positively to the findings and had already started work to address the concerns identified during the inspection.

An action plan has been requested setting out how the service intends to improve its governance processes and address the shortcomings highlighted by inspectors.

### Care provider to challenge findings

In response to the report, Care 24-7 said it was disappointed with the rating and intended to challenge both the inspection findings and elements of the CQC's methodology and process.

A spokesperson said: "We are disappointed by the Requires Improvement rating and intend to challenge both the inspection findings and elements of the CQC's methodology and process through the appropriate review channels, not least because of significant procedural irregularities in the production of the report itself and our view that the report is not reflective of the service we provide.

"We would like to reassure service users, relatives and stakeholders that we remain committed to providing safe, responsive and compassionate care.

"We are also engaging with them to address any questions or concerns arising from the report."
