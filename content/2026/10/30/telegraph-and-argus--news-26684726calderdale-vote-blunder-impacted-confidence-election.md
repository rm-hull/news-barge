A blunder during Calderdale’s local election count ‘impacted confidence’ in the process, according to the elections watchdog.

An Electoral Commission report published last month said Calderdale Council’s returning officer ‘did not meet elements of our performance standards’ after some votes were initially tallied wrong in the Rastrick ward at the ‘all-out’ local elections on May 7 and 8.

The Commission concluded: “The errors did not change the election result. The correct candidates were declared elected but the published candidate totals were wrong, and this had an impact on confidence in the way the count was conducted.”

Though he did not challenge the result, losing Labour candidate Peter Judge complained to the Council’s returning officer Robin Tuddenham about what he labelled a ‘flawed’ process.

The error affected: Liberal Democrat Andrew Menzies (declared at 946 votes but received 195); Green Teddy Morgan (declared at 90 votes but received 540); and Conservative Chris Pillai (declared at 659 votes but received 960).

It did not affect Reform UK’s Simon Briggs (1,644 votes), David Small (1,551 votes) and Jackie Wilson (1,523 votes), who were all declared correctly and duly elected.

Councillors on the Governance and Business Committee have now received a report from the returning officer revealing what went wrong.

In the report, Mr Tuddenham explained: “The verification of ballot papers for the Rastrick Ward was completed satisfactorily. However, an error occurred during the count process, which was not identified until the RO declared the results.

“The error in paperwork enabled the ‘block votes’ cast for Teddy Morgan, a Green Party candidate, to be added to non-block votes cast for Andrew Campbell Menzies, a Liberal Democrat candidate.

“As there were only two Liberal Democrat candidates, it was not possible for either of these candidates to accrue block votes.

“Once this issue was identified, the matter was reviewed, and the RO corrected the result. This issue did not affect the outcome of the election of candidates in Rastrick.”

A ‘block vote’ occurs when a voter casts all of their available votes for candidates from the same political party.

After Mr Judge’s complaint, Peter Stanyon, Chief Executive of the Association of Electoral Administrators (AEA), was invited to review the Council’s processes and made 18 recommendations.

The transparency reforms include making the process to validate provisional results the same in every ward; sharing summaries of block and non-block votes with interested parties; and giving candidates ‘sufficient time’ to check provisional results are accurate before the formal declaration.

The recommendations also say ‘IT matters’ should be resolved ahead of next year’s elections and any Count Team Supervisors ‘should be fully trained’.

The Council has agreed to all the recommendations or noted where it will build on existing practice.

Mr Tuddenham, Calderdale Council’s Returning Officer, told the LDRS: “This was a very complex election. Fifty-four seats were contested in the first ‘all out’ election held in Calderdale since 2004. The overall process was handled effectively, and the hard work of the election team was commendable.

“However, there was an administrative error made during the count for the Rastrick ward. This meant the number of votes allocated to two candidates at the declaration was incorrect. We identified this issue at the count and, after an immediate review, I confirmed the correct figures for each candidate in a second declaration.

“The outcome of the election was not affected, as the votes cast for the three winning candidates elected did not change.

“The Council engaged with the Electoral Commission following the elections, and a review and learning exercise was commissioned from the Association of Electoral Administrators, personally undertaken by their Chief Executive.

“As advised to our Governance and Business Committee earlier this week, we are enacting the recommendations from that review, in time for the next set of local elections in May 2027.”
