**Edinburgh University managers have been urged to halt a redundancy consultation affecting the institution’s study abroad team.**

Writing to principal Sir Peter Mathieson, university secretary Leigh Chalmers and Edinburgh Global director Alan Mackay, a group of staff warned that the cuts are likely to impact the university’s international reputation.

Sixteen of 28 roles in the Study and Work Away (SWAY) team could be cut, sources told The Herald, leaving staff fearing for their jobs amid a £140m budget savings drive.

The team coordinates study abroad arrangements for more than 1,400 students each year, and has developed the university’s response to the return of the Erasmus+ programme from 2027.

The letter states: “We are [worried] by the fact that the changes seem to be steered by an abstract, and we would say, ideological, vision of efficiency through a distributed model of service.

“Without the central support of SWAY’s expert team, and oversight over partnerships, inbound and outbound mobilities, such a distributed model is likely to create more work for everybody, not less, at a time when professional services teams in schools have been shrunk and remaining colleagues have unmanageable workloads.

![University College Union (UCU) members and student supporters from universities across Scotland take part in a rally against cuts and job losses in universities outside the Scottish Parliament.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21529900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)On Friday, the UCU trade union completed five weeks of industrial action over 90 programme closures and £140m in budget cuts. *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

“Without expert support we can anticipate major problems on all fronts of mobility, and a significant loss in effectiveness and efficiency.”

Yesterday, The Herald revealed that academics claimed the cuts could put as much as £9.6m in annual tuition at risk.

“There are currently up to 480 overseas fee-paying students (in years 1 to 4) who either have returned from exchange (4th years), are on exchange (3rd years), are applying for exchange (2nd years) or are planning to do so (1st years), and who may not have chosen Edinburgh if they had thought exchange opportunities were limited,” the group explained.

Assuming the average international student brings in around £20,000 in tuition, if all 480 were missing, the university would lose £9.6m per year.

Even if the number dropped by just a quarter, the university would be out £2.4m, three times the amount of savings managers could expect to make through the restructure (around £800,000).

However, a university spokesperson pushed back against the figures, stating that the projected loss of income is "entirely unfounded and unequivocally rejected" by the institution's leadership.

Staff also spoke out about the potential impact of the cuts on students from working-class backgrounds.

“We wish to reiterate that we oppose the shrinking of the service to one exclusively concerned with student exchanges,” the letter adds.

![Sir Peter Mathieson was criticised by speakers at the rally.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21540957.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Peter Mathieson. *(Image: Paul Dodds)*

“The removal of the widening participation subteam, and the associated shrinking of opportunities for international exposure for less wealthy students, is a particular concern. So is the silence regarding staff mobility.

“We see the combination of cost concerns and abstract restructuring principles as highly damaging for the university’s commitment to international exposure and mobility.

“We urge you to pause the consultation on redundancies currently underway and reengage with the SWAY team and with us.”

The intervention comes as Sir Peter told staff in an email that “difficult but strategically important decisions need to be taken now”.

“Uncertainty and pessimism in the current circumstances are fully understandable. Misinformation and disinformation are not acceptable,” the principal added.

A spokesperson for Edinburgh University has defended the proposed restructure.

“There is no intention to withdraw from nor diminish our commitment to student exchange,” they remarked.

"A central team will continue to support student exchanges, which will remain an important component of the curriculum. The ambition is to develop a service that is sustainable and integrated into a wider institutional approach to international learning.

“Undergraduate students will continue to go abroad on exchange, and students will continue to come to Edinburgh through reciprocal exchange arrangements.

"Widening participation, inclusion and access will remain important considerations. The proposed model does not seek to reduce the number of outgoing exchange students nor fee-paying visiting undergraduates.”
