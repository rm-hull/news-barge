ILKLEY Civic Society has lodged a strong objection to plans for a new public toilet block in the town's central car park, arguing the proposals represent a "missed opportunity" to improve a key gateway into the town centre.

The plans, which would replace the existing toilet building, have been submitted by Ilkley Town Council to Bradford Council for planning approval and are intended to provide modern facilities, including a Changing Places toilet.

While the society accepts that the current building should eventually be demolished, describing it as structurally and visually poor, it says the proposed replacement falls well short of what could be achieved at such a prominent site within the Ilkley Conservation Area.

The society says the design amounts to "replacing a flat roofed box with a pitched roof box".

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21653230.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The current toilet block in Ilkley's main carpark *(Image: submitted)*

It argues the site should have been used as the catalyst for a wider redesign of the central car park area, creating a more attractive and legible route between the car park and The Grove.

The society said: "As proposed, the form of the proposed building is utilitarian - a stone shed with a pitched roof. It may help it to blend in with its surroundings, but it could offer so much more.

"It has the opportunity to be a positive contribution to the space, an attractive eye-catching feature that signals arrival in Ilkley."

Among its concerns are the lack of a covered transition space outside the toilets, the placement of doors opening directly onto a pedestrian route, limited natural light inside the building and the decision to position the Changing Places facility in a separate structure.

In its objection, the society said: "The proposed block ignores this need for a welcoming transition space: a series of doors open directly on a main thoroughfare without offering any privacy. This is highly undignified and inappropriate."

The society also criticised what it describes as a lack of consideration for the wider public realm around the toilets, including pedestrian movement between the car park and town centre.

It said opening toilet doors onto a public thoroughfare and towards outdoor café seating was "not good design" and questioned whether alternative locations for the facility had been properly considered.

Conservation concerns also feature heavily in the objection.

The society argues that planning documents fail to adequately address the site's location within the conservation area and do not properly consider recommendations contained within the conservation area appraisal.

It noted that the appraisal identifies the central car park as making a "negative contribution" to the conservation area and highlights opportunities for enhancement.

Other issues raised include the appearance of the proposed stonework, the use of stainless steel doors, landscaping details not shown on submitted plans, and inaccuracies or omissions in application documents relating to waste, recycling, trees, floor area and ventilation.

The society also questioned sustainability aspects of the scheme, saying supporting documents focus on maintenance efficiencies while failing to address issues such as material reuse and grey-water recycling.

Concluding its submission, Ilkley Civic Society said the proposals "do not improve the preservation and enhancement of the conservation area" and recommended that planning permission be refused.

The group has also raised concerns outside the planning process, questioning whether spending almost £250,000 on the development represents good value when the building stands on a 50-year lease and calling for greater clarity over future operating cost savings.

The application will be determined by Bradford Council following a consultation process.
