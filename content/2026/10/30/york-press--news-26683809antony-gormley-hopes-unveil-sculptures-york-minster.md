## Creator of Angel of the North hopes to unveil two new sculptures at York Minster

The Angel of the North *(Image: Owen Humphreys/ PA)*

The celebrated artist behind the iconic Angel of the North could create two new sculptures for York Minster.

The Chapter of York is exploring a collaboration with Sir Antony Gormley as part of the Minster's 1,400th anniversary celebrations.

The proposed works, Meet and Close, are subject to planning approval.




One of the sculptures – Meet – will need City of York Council consent, as it is external work, York Minster said.

![The sculptures, Meet and Close, would be subject to planning approval, including from City of York Council for external work, York Minster has said](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/20770026.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The sculptures, Meet and Close, would be subject to planning approval, including from City of York Council for external work, York Minster has said *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Speaking about this, Anthony said: “For me, this project is a precious opportunity to make a new work for the Minster, an evolving human project that has been both host and catalyst for human imagination for millennia.”

Born in London in 1950, the artist quickly gained recognition for his work in contemporary art and was won the prestigious Turner Prize in 1994.

Four years later, Antony’s Angel of the North - a 65ft steel sculpture seen by 33 million people each year – overlooking the A1 near Gateshead was unveiled.

The Very Reverend Dominic Barrington, Dean of York, said of the proposed sculptures, said: “We are immensely excited by the opportunity to work with Antony Gormley, celebrating the centuries of art that come together to form this extraordinary place.

“The proposed works would offer a contemporary examination of the body, which features prominently throughout York Minster, most famously in the pre-Reformation Kings’ Screen, but will also offer a fresh lens for us to examine the fundamentals of what make us human.”

![Sir Antony Gormley, the artist behind the Angel of the North, is hoping to unveil two new sculptures at York Minster](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21649968.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Antony Gormley, the artist behind the Angel of the North, is hoping to unveil two new sculptures at York Minster *(Image: PA Media)*

The Minster’s 1,400th anniversary will take place next year and the sculptures are hoped to form a part of its annual programme of events and exhibitions in 2028.

Before it is unveiled, the Meet and Close project requires consent under the Care of Cathedrals Measure.

The news comes four years after a full-length stone statute of Queen Elizabeth II was unveiled by the King at the cathedral’s west front in 2022.

Created by Richard Bossons, part of the Minster’s masonry team, the statute – which divided opinion at the time, depicts the Queen wearing Garter robes and the George IV State Diadem and holding the orb and sceptre.
