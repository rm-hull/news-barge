I’M A big fan of Halloween. I love seeing pumpkins glowing on gateposts, windows draped with cobwebs and bats hanging from porches. I miss the parties we used to have when my daughters were growing up – we would transform the garden into a ghostly playground, with all manner of spooky objects emerging from hedges, bushes and trees.

My husband would wear a scary mask and a tatty old coat and hide at the bottom of the garden, leaping out and terrifying the kids as they approached. It was great fun.

I don’t even mind a bit of Halloween tat – the shops are full of it, with Halloween-themed gifts including tumblers, candle holders and cheap jewellery.

But, while I love it, the seasonal event is getting out of hand. It’s getting more like Christmas, with themed gift-giving for both adults and children. Then there’s all the decorations – no longer will strips of old bedding or sacks from the garage suffice. Shops are bursting with Halloween products worthy of a Disney-style theme park, many of them with eye-watering price tags.

In one store I visited this week an inflatable clown the size of a caravan will set you back £100, a sparkly skeleton £90, and a set of three witches £55.

You can buy Halloween-themed bedding, complete with duvet covers and pillowcases, alongside ghostly-themed cushions, lampshades and rugs emblazoned with special 3D images of monsters emerging from holes in the floor. You can even buy Halloween curtains. Who goes to such lengths?

What in my day was simply about dressing up in an old sheet with eyeholes and doing a bit of apple bobbing, All Hallows' Eve has become a huge commercial concern, putting pressure on families to part with money they may not have, just two months before Christmas.

This year I encountered so-called boo boxes or boo baskets – spooky-themed boxes filled with Halloween goodies and given to someone as a gift. And they are not only for kids. Some of these boxes – available in shops and online – are aimed at the adult market. They arrive filled with a mixture of beauty or grooming products, luxury chocolates, Halloween-themed fashion accessories, and even alcohol.

Were I to present a basket full of such things to my husband on October 31, he would think I’d lost my mind.

Boo baskets are yet another import from across the Atlantic, taking off in America in the 1980s, when neighbours would secretly leave a basket of treats with a ‘You've Been Booed’ note attached, on a doorstep. The recipient would keep the gifts and continue the chain by ‘booing’ someone else. Thankfully, we Brits haven’t gone the whole hog and adopted such sinister goings on.

I’m by no means alone in feeling that Halloween has exploded into a commercial monster. There has been much condemnation on social media, from the cost of costumes in a society where few people make their own, to over-priced ‘pumpkin patches’ which charge a fortune for families to walk across a muddy field and pick up a pumpkin.

It also begs the question, where do people store all this stuff? Most families have a Christmas box containing the tree decorations and other random bits, kept in the loft or understairs cupboard and dragged out once a year for the festivities. Now, it’s necessary to make space for a second receptacle - preferably a shipping container - for all the Halloween clobber.
