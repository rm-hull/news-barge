## Two teenagers among five killed in Russian strikes on Ukraine

Two teenage girls were killed on a central street in Chernihiv in ‌northern Ukraine, and three people died in strikes on residential buildings in the central city of Kremenchuk.

In Chernihiv, in addition ​to the two dead teenagers, 23 people including four children were wounded in a drone attack on a busy downtown street.

"Ordinary, ‌peaceful people were attacked who were just going to work, going ⁠about their daily business, making plans for the day," said Dmytro Bryzhynskyi, head of the city's military administration.

In Kremenchuk, strikes on ⁠a residential block and private buildings killed three people and wounded 32, the regional governor said. Russian drones also struck city buses in three separate attacks on southeastern Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia, wounding six, ‌officials there said.

Thursday's strikes came amid ​renewed efforts to revive US-backed peace talks to end Europe's deadliest conflict ‌since World War Two.

New three-way talks are expected to take place ​next month, with Ukrainian officials, including top presidential adviser Kyrylo Budanov, urging pressure on Moscow from Kyiv's partners.

## Bodies scattered across platform in Dnipro

A Russian jet-powered drone had struck a platform at the Dnipro's main station, ⁠where a commuter train was preparing to depart, said state railway Ukrzaliznytsia.

Mangled bodies lay scattered across the shrapnel-marked pavement, where patches of bright red blood had pooled up. Police filled black body bags amid the wail of an air-raid siren.

"And for what? What goal are they (Russians) trying to achieve?" said Svitlana Shcherbak, 67, as she sheltered underground at the station.

## Russian drone strike kills 14 on Ukrainian rail platform

A Russian drone strike killed at least 14 people on a crowded rail platform in the Ukrainian city ⁠of Dnipro on Thursday, while five died in daytime strikes on two other provincial cities and a late-evening missile strike killed one person in the capital Kyiv, officials said.

The string of attacks jolted Ukrainian cities as a local truce in the southern region of Kherson raised cautious hopes for a resumption of diplomacy.

"Russia is deliberately hunting civilians: on trains, at stations, in residential buildings," Ukrainian prime minister Sergiy Koretskyi said on the Telegram app.

## Putin says aid is reaching Oleshky civilians due to ceasefire

President Vladimir Putin told ‌a gathering with African nations in Moscow that relief supplies would begin reaching Oleshky civilians on Thursday and Friday, "with the support, incidentally, of the first lady of the United States".

The truce applies only to ‌Oleshky and the wider Kherson region, which has suffered years of near-daily ⁠drone and artillery attacks. Some 1,500 of the town's prewar population of 24,000 people are believed to live among the ruins, under regular shellfire amid ⁠severe shortages of food and medicine.

Ukraine has long accused Russia of refusing to let aid in or people out. Russia says it is Ukrainian mines on the approach roads and Ukrainian shelling that have ‌prevented the delivery of aid or the safe evacuation ​of residents.

## Ukraine pauses strikes on Russian refineries after pressure from Trump – reports

Ukraine has halted its strikes on Russian oil refineries after pressure from US president Donald Trump, the *Wall ⁠Street Journal* reported on Thursday, citing people with knowledge of the operations.

Ukrainian units carrying out long-range strikes were told in mid-October that the campaign against refineries ⁠was effectively on hold ​once missions ⁠already in motion were completed, the *Journal* reported.

The unofficial pause is expected to ⁠last until after the US midterm elections and ​potentially ⁠deeper into November, it added. ‌Russia has meanwhile stepped up attacks on Ukrainian energy and civilian infrastructure.

The news of ‌a pause comes amid renewed efforts to revive US-backed peace talks to end Europe's deadliest conflict since World War ⁠Two.

Relations between Trump and Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky have taken a sharp downward turn in recent weeks, with Trump growing increasingly frustrated over Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy infrastructure as diesel prices hit record highs.

Ukraine had refused to halt ‌the strikes, saying its long-range drone campaign ​is its only viable response to ‌years of Russian bombardment.

That ⁠prompted Trump to announce a deal to ⁠buy diesel from Moscow and waive sanctions on Moscow authorised ‌by a ​bill he signed into ‌law in September.

## Russia, Ukraine say Oleshky demining operations complete, humanitarian operation to proceed

Russia and Ukraine said on Thursday that demining operations around the Russian-held town of Oleshky in southern Ukraine had been completed and that an operation to bring aid and allow residents to leave was ready to begin.

Efforts have been underway for several weeks to organise ⁠a relief operation for the acutely deprived town's residents, which Russian forces seized soon after Moscow's February 2022 invasion of its smaller neighbour.

Both sides have praised US first lady Melania Trump for helping broker a truce in a town where at least one resident who escaped said those remaining feared death by starvation.

![Deminers of Ukrainian Emergency Service walk past a crater following the explosion of anti-tank mines and other explosive materials, found in the town of Bucha](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/01/10/1251776625.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Demining operations of routes have been completed for the humanitarian mission in Oleshky," the two sides said in virtually identical statements issued by Yana Lantratova, Russia's human rights commissioner, and Dmytro Lubinets, her Ukrainian counterpart.

"The humanitarian convoy is ready for departure."

The convoy would ⁠head for the district hospital and residents wishing to leave would be permitted to go within a specific time frame. Aid packages would then be ​distributed, the statements said.

The ⁠operation aims to address a crisis in a town ‌marooned inside a war zone since 2022. But it is also part of a diplomatic dance where both sides want to show US President Donald Trump that they back his peace efforts, albeit for different reasons.

Lantratova, Russian human rights commissioner, said she was taking four journalists from the conservative US outlet Newsmax to the area. Lubinets ‌made no reference to specific reporters.

## Aid mission set to enter Oleshky as a localized Russia-Ukraine truce holds for a third day

Humanitarian aid is poised to roll into the stricken city of Oleshky, negotiators said Thursday, as a localized ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine stretched into a third day and military units cleared landmines.

Moscow and Kyiv have observed the truce in and around the devastated southern Ukrainian city since Tuesday, but remain otherwise locked in the war that began with Russia’s full-scale invasion more than four years ago. Previous ceasefire talks either failed or the truces quickly fell apart.

“Demining of the designated routes has been completed, and the humanitarian convoy has been assembled and is ready,” ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets, who heads Ukraine's negotiating team, said Thursday. He said aid deliveries were due to begin Friday.

Elsewhere, deadly attacks continued and Ukraine's central bank warned that damage to industrial targets could push the country into a recession.

The planned six-day ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky has brought a rare and welcome stillness for civilians in the nearby city of Kherson, which is held by Ukraine and has also been wracked by the war.

Russian and Ukrainian officials head to Oleshky for aid delivery

Both sides thanked Melania Trump for what the White House called her help in brokering the ceasefire so food and medical supplies can be delivered to about 2,000 people trapped in Oleshky. Pope Leo XIV also pressed for a truce.
