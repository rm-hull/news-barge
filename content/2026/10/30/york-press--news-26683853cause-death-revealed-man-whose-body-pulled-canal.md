The cause of death has been revealed for a “happy and jolly” man whose body was pulled from a canal in North Yorkshire.

Shaun Barton, 60 died from acute alcoholic toxicity followed by immersion in water and heart disease before his body was recovered from Ripon Canal last month, an inquest heard.

Shaun’s inquest was opened on Friday (October 30) and was told he became lost in the city on his mobility scooter on Saturday, September 12, with his body found in the canal the following morning.

Coroner Sarah Hudson adjourned the inquest for a full hearing to take place at a later date.

Shaun’s daughter, Gemma Cox, said her father had been at Ripon Bowling Club with friends the night before his death.

She said the family does not know why Shaun, who relied on walking aids following a motorbike crash around seven years ago, visited Ripon Canal after leaving the bowling club at 11pm on September 12.

After Friday's inquest opening, Gemma told The Press she is still waiting for the results of post-mortem and toxicology reports which she hopes will provide answers “about what happened to my dad in his final moments”.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21530275.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shaun Barton with his daughters Kimberley Barton, left, and Gemma Cox, right *(Image: Family)*

She described Shaun, a father of three and grandfather of six from Ripon, as a “loving, funny, down-to-earth man who would do anything for anyone”.

“He was my best friend, my rock and someone I could turn to about absolutely anything.

“He always made sure me and my sister were alright, and he was always there for his family and friends.”

Gemma, 38, a mother of two originally from Ripon and now living in Dorset, said her father was a "well-known person in Ripon" and "very liked".

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21530277.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shaun Barton whose body was recovered from Ripon Canal on September 13 *(Image: Family)*

Shaun had a painting and decorating business in Ripon and worked installing stage lighting prior to that.

"He was just happy and jolly constantly," Gemma said. "He was a good person to talk to."

Gemma launched a fundraiser to cover Shaun’s funeral costs and said he was given the “send-off he deserved” on Monday, October 19, at Maple Park Crematorium, near Thirsk.

But she said: “I am still struggling to pay the outstanding funeral costs. I still have a significant amount left to pay, and I've had to continue asking the public for help through my dad's GoFundMe.

“The heartbreaking reality is that I cannot bring my dad home with me until the outstanding costs are paid.

“All I want is to be able to have my dad's ashes home where they belong, with his family.

“I'm not asking for sympathy, and I don't expect anyone to give what they cannot afford. Even sharing the GoFundMe or donating whatever someone can manage would mean more than I could ever explain.

“I just hope people can understand that behind every fundraiser there is a family going through something they never expected to face.”
