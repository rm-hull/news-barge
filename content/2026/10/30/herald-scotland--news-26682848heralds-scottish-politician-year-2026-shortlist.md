The leaders of the SNP, Reform UK and the Scottish Greens will battle it out for the top prize at The Herald's Scottish Politician of the Year in association with ScottishPower.

First Minister John Swinney, Reform's Malcolm Offord and Scottish Greens co-leaders Ross Greer and Gillian Mackay have all made the shortlist after their parties' respective success at May's Holyrood election.

Judges praised Mr Swinney, who won the award last year, for returning the SNP to power and securing his own mandate, while Mr Offord was credited for taking Reform from one MSP before the election to winning 17.

Mr Greer and Ms Mackay are nominated jointly for leading the Greens to a record number of MSPs, including winning Nicola Sturgeon's former Glasgow Southside seat and ousting Angus Robertson in Edinburgh Central.

![The Herald Politician of the Year 2025 , First Minster John Swinney](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20228257.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Herald Politician of the Year 2025 , First Minster John Swinney *(Image: Stewart Attwood)*

The Best Scot at Westminster award, sponsored by Diageo, recognises the Scottish MP with the biggest impact and influence in their role.

The nominees are Labour's Scott Arthur and the Conservatives' Andrew Bowie and Harriet Cross.

Dr Arthur was recognised for steering his Rare Cancers Bill through parliament, which will incentivise research and investment into treatments for rare types of cancer. The act defines "rare cancer" in law for the first time, meaning any cancer where fewer than six in 100,000 people are diagnosed with it each year.

Ms Cross was praised for her campaigning on the farm tax, her contribution to the oil and gas debate and her ground presence in Aberdeen South. Judges were also impressed by her speedy ascent through the Tory ranks.

Mr Bowie was shortlisted for his work as a member of the shadow cabinet, where judges felt he had made a strong impact, and for his policy work on energy. He was also recognised for his role in the Tory victory at the Aberdeen South by-election.

Committee MSP of the Year honours a backbencher who has shone in scrutinising legislation and holding ministers to account. The nominees are the SNP's David Linden and Clare Haughey, and the Lib Dems' Willie Rennie.

![The Herald Scottish Politician of the Year Awards](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18797646.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Herald Scottish Politician of the Year Awards *(Image: Gordon Terris/Herald)*

Mr Rennie's nomination centred on his convenership of the transport committee, where he has questioned Stephen Flynn over ferries. Nominators said members of all parties speak of his fairness in giving them time for their questions.

Mr Linden impressed judges in his role as convener of the Justice Committee, where he pressed John Swinney hard on justice issues rather than offering the softer questioning often expected of SNP conveners.

Ms Haughey was recognised for steering the Health Committee's work on the Assisted Dying Bill, a huge workload that included going through the amendments. Judges praised her balanced and professional handling of a deeply controversial issue.

The Community MSP of the Year award recognises a politician who has fought hard for their constituency or region. The nominees are Labour's Paul Sweeney, the SNP's Ben Macpherson, and the Conservatives' Douglas Lumsden and Jeremy Balfour.

Mr Sweeney was described as the most vocal pro-Glasgow MSP on saving the city's architecture, including the Queen's Park Glasshouse and the former Hillhead Baptist Church, and was quick off the mark over the Union Place fire.

Mr Macpherson was cited for his closeness to the knife crime campaign in Leith and for giving up a ministerial role partly to focus on his constituency.

Mr Balfour was recognised for campaigning on disability issues and securing Changing Places toilets funding in last year's Budget, while Lumsden was nominated for his dedication to the oil and gas sector.

The Donald Dewar Debater of the Year, sponsored by the Advertising Association, can be considered on a one speech performance, which is why all three of this year's nominees made the shortlist.

The nominees are the Lib Dems' Duncan Dunlop, the Greens' Lorna Slater and the SNP's Karen Adam.

![The Scottish Politician of the Year Awards 2025](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20228131.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Herald Politician of the Year 2025 *(Image: Stewart Attwood)*

Five MSPs are nominated for the One to Watch award, sponsored by ScottishPower: the SNP's Hannah Mary Goodlad and Jack Middleton, Reform's Thomas Kerr, the Lib Dems' David Green and Labour's Jenny Young.

Ms Goodlad was cited for going straight into ministerial office and for being confident on a wide range of tricky topics.

Mr Green was described as the most impressive of the new Lib Dems in the chamber and in committee.

Mr Middleton was praised for strong contributions since being elected and for being more likely than other new SNP MSPs to speak out and challenge his party.

Ms Young was recognised for incisive questioning on Holyrood's culture committee, notably when she pressed Culture Secretary Mairi McAllan over the Historic Environment Scotland affair.

The Politics in Business Award, sponsored by Charlotte Street Partners, is contested between Labour's Daniel Johnson, the SNP's Ms Goodlad and Reform's Offord.

Mr Johnson was cited for consistent engagement with firms large and small and for being a prominent voice making the case for industry. Ms Goodlad's nominations pointed to the significant business experience she has brought to parliament and used in her ministerial role, including her work with businesses on non-domestic rates. Mr Offord's work since the election engaging with firms, including at a local level, impressed judges.

The shortlist for the ScottishPower Green Champion — supported by ScottishPower — is the SNP's Stephen Gethins, the Greens' Ms Mackay and the Lib Dems' Alex Cole-Hamilton.

Mr Cole-Hamilton was recognised for his sewage campaigning, while Mr Gethins was recognised for pushing through decarbonisation as the minister responsible.

Ms Mackay was recognised for her campaigning on the energy demands of data centres.

Public Campaigner of the Year recognises an individual or group who has engaged with the political system to change legislation. All three of those shortlisted have just done just that.

The nominees are Lynn McKenzie, for the Stacy's Law campaign for GPS protection for domestic abuse victims; Lisa Petrie, for the Leith bleed kit campaign; and James Bundy, for the 24/7 thrombectomy campaign.

The Scottish Local Politician of the Year, sponsored by Boralex, recognises outstanding and innovative public service in one of Scotland's 32 councils.

The nominees are Fiona Higgins of Glasgow City Council, Bill Lobban of Highland Council and Elen Forsan of Clackmannanshire Council.

The awards, which are also supported by partners Women in Public Affairs, will be announced at a sold-out ceremony at **Edinburgh**'s Prestonfield House on Thursday, November 26.

Keith Anderson, ScottishPower chief executive, said: "The decisions being taken by politicians today shape Scotland's economy, communities and opportunities for years to come.

"Good politics requires leadership, ambition and constructive debate, and these awards provide an important opportunity to recognise those making a genuine contribution to Scotland's public life."

Chris Walker, Director of Public and External Affairs at the Advertising Association, said: "The Advertising Association is proud to support The Herald Scottish Politician of the Year Awards and we extend our congratulations to all of this year's finalists.

"Good politics and good advertising share some common traits: they are trusted, they are engaging, and they speak to people in a way that resonates with them. We look forward to celebrating all the finalists and the winners at the awards in November."

Malcolm Robertson, founder of Charlotte Street Partners, said: "We're pleased to be supporting one of the mainstays of Scotland's political calendar.

"While there is certainly room for improvement in the way our politicians engage with the businesses that will deliver the growth on which the Scottish Parliament's aspirations depend, it is always worth stepping back and celebrating the best of our political talent. We wish all the finalists the very best of luck."
