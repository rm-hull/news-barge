Finance experts are warning thousands of North East music lovers to watch out for scammers if they missed out on Sam Fender tickets this week.

The concern comes after North Shields-born Fender’s presale was delayed following what promoters described as a “sophisticated bot attack” for the presale of his St James' Park tour dates next year.

Experts warn that fans who were unable to buy tickets may now look online for last-minute offers, which fraudsters could now exploit to deceive desperate concertgoers.

Scammers may use confusion from the original ticket sale to appear convincing, finance experts at price comparison site Money Expert said, with concertgoers being urged to take care when viewing resale listings, particularly if sellers ask for direct bank transfers or demand payment immediately.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/16874096.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The presale for Sam Fender's opening shows for his 2027 Proper Massive Stadium Tour was due to begin at 9.30am on October 28, but was suspended after high levels of suspicious activity affected the website.

Money Expert have shared major warning signs for people buying online, to ensure they do not fall victim to inauthentic sellers in the hunt for access to one of the North East's biggest music events.

Offers at unusually low prices should be treated with care.

Ticket prices for sold-out events are often pushed up by demand, so a very cheap offer may be designed to attract buyers quickly, with experts warning scammers may take payment and then disappear or block contact.

Pressure to pay quickly is another warning sign.

Fraudsters may claim tickets are “selling fast” or that an offer is “only available for a few minutes," in an attempt to stop buyers from checking if the seller, website or ticket details are genuine.

Buyers should be cautious about unusual payment methods.

Genuine ticketing services usually use secure payment systems.

Bank transfers, cryptocurrency and gift cards often prove difficult to trace and may not offer protection if something goes wrong, so can be a clear sign that the seller is not legitimate.

A legitimate seller should be able to provide clear evidence of ticket ownership.

Information such as a booking confirmation, order reference or screenshot should be able to be checked for authenticity independently.

A seller who refuses to provide proof of booking, or gives vague answers, may not be trustworthy.

Fake resale websites are another risk. Some websites ran by scammers can be made to look like established ticket platforms.

Fans are advised to check website addresses carefully, look for mistakes in branding and avoid unfamiliar sites that appear poorly designed.

Liz Hunter, director at Money Expert, said concertgoers should be vigilant when buying online tickets, especially for upcoming high-demand events like Harry Styles' 2027 tour.

Hunter also advised people to use a credit card where possible, adding: "If purchasing online, always pay by credit card as this offers important protection under Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act, which covers purchases between £100 and £30,000.”

She said this could help if tickets were not delivered, proved invalid or the seller failed to complete the transaction.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640316.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fans on Wednesday (October 28) reported being unable to access the site, being removed from queues and losing tickets from their baskets.

The presale for Sam Fender's opening shows for his 2027 Proper Massive Stadium Tour was due to begin at 9.30am on October 28. It was suspended after unusually high levels of suspicious activity affected the website.

Fans reported being unable to access the site, being removed from queues and losing tickets from their baskets.

Fender later said on Instagram that the pre-sale would move yesterday morning (October 29) so “real fans get the tickets".

Experts say the disruption and continued demand could leave fans more exposed to fake offers as they search for tickets.

Anyone who believes they have been scammed should contact their bank immediately, change any shared passwords and report the incident to Action Fraud.
