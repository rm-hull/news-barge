More than 1,600 people die on UK roads every year and speeding remains one of the biggest factors in serious and fatal collisions.

Speed cameras keep popping up across the country, but many motorists still do not fully understand how they work.

From beliefs about a “10pc plus 2mph” grace margin to claims that speed vans must be visible, a number of myths continue to circulate among drivers.

Road safety experts say relying on rumours or supposed loopholes could prove costly, with penalties ranging from fines and licence points to court action and driving bans.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20583092.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

## What speed triggers a camera?

There is no universal “10pc plus 2mph” rule written into law.

While the National Police Chiefs’ Council recommends that margin to allow for officer discretion, it is not a legal threshold and can vary depending on the enforcement area or the officer reviewing a case.

Experts from BigWantsYourCar.com said: “The idea that you’re safe doing 35mph in a 30mph zone is one of the biggest myths we hear.

“Technically, you’re liable for a fine the moment you go even 1mph over the limit.”

The RAC added: “The law states that you are liable for a speeding fine as soon as you exceed the limit, so if you’re doing 31mph in a 30 limit or 71mph on a motorway, you’re breaking the law and could receive a fine.

“The best advice is to not speed full stop.”

## Do speed vans have to be visible?

No. There is no law requiring speed enforcement vans to be visible to drivers.

However, operators say visibility is often used deliberately because it can encourage motorists to slow down.

Go Safe Casualty Reduction Officer Gareth Thomas said: “Legally, we don’t have to be visible.

“I could camouflage myself if I wanted to – but it’s all about being fair, education and preventing an accident.”

## Is flashing to warn drivers illegal?

Flashing headlights to alert other motorists to a speed van could be an offence.

Under section 89 of the Police Act 1997, it is illegal to wilfully obstruct a constable in the execution of their duty.

But Mr Thomas said proving the offence can be difficult, adding: “It doesn’t bother me that people flash to warn them of the speed van – I just want to educate people and the van to act as a speed deterrent.”

## What happens if you’re caught?

The minimum penalty for speeding is a £100 fine and three penalty points.

In some cases, drivers may be offered a speed awareness course instead, depending on the speed recorded, their licence history and whether they have attended a course in the previous three years.

Courses are generally available to drivers who were travelling at no more than 10pc plus 9mph above the posted limit.

For example, a driver recorded at more than 86mph on a motorway would not usually be eligible.

## How do modern cameras work?

Today’s camera networks use a range of technology, including radar, road sensors and average-speed systems capable of tracking vehicles over several miles.

Many devices now operate digitally and automatically record suspected offences, with no need for manual intervention.

Road safety experts say the safest approach remains simple: drive within the posted limit and adjust speed for road and weather conditions.
