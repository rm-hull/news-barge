A knifeman who stabbed another man in the neck in an argument over paying at a North Yorkshire country inn has been jailed.

Gary Holdsworth, 52, was originally charged with attempted murder following the incident last December.

The victim’s partner told Leeds Crown Court that the victim was a “kind, loving and caring” man who had nearly lost his life that evening in the George and Dragon Inn in Melmerby, near Ripon.

“This was not just an attack - it was something that could have taken him away from all of us forever,” she said in a personal statement.

Holdsworth, of Melmerby near Ripon, denied all charges and was convicted by a jury at Leeds Crown Court of wounding with intent and carrying a knife in public.

He was jailed for six and a half years and banned from contacting the victim and the victim’s partner for the rest of his life.

Leeds Crown Court heard that Holdsworth and the victim were in the pub shortly before midnight on December 4 last year.

There was an argument over payment that turned physical and carried on outside the pub, where the victim was stabbed.

He was taken to hospital and underwent surgery, but did not receive life-threatening injuries, police said.

The victim's partner’s statement said: "Since that day, my life has completely changed.

"I live with fear, anxiety, and constant worry about what happened and what could have happened. The images and emotions stay with me, and it is something I cannot escape.

"This incident has deeply affected not just me, but our entire family. It has taken away our sense of safety, peace, and normal life."

The senior investigating officer for the case, Detective Chief Inspector Cheryl Quinn, said: “I welcome the sentencing of Gary Holdsworth who has been held accountable for his shocking actions that night. The use of a knife to inflict such a serious injury is a deeply concerning act of violence with the potential to cause life-threatening harm and devastating consequences for victims and their families.

"My thoughts remain with the victim as he continues to deal with the physical and emotional impact of this incident. We recognise the lasting effects that offences of this nature have on individuals, families, and the wider community.

"This case serves as a reminder of the serious consequences of carrying and using knives. Violence of this nature will be treated with the utmost seriousness, and we remain committed to working with our partners and the community to prevent knife crime, protect the public and bring offenders to justice.”
