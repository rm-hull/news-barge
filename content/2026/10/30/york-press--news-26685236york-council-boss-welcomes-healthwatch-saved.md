A leading councillor has hailed the government pulling plans to axe a health watchdog service “an absolutely enormous victory for York”.

Cllr Lucy Steels-Walshaw, City of York Council’s executive member for health, said she “couldn’t be happier” after health secretary Yvette Cooper ditched plans to abandon Healthwatch England, meaning Healthwatch York is no longer at risk of closure.

"We fought for Healthwatch, we made our voices heard, and we've won,” Cllr Steels-Walshaw said.

"For months, people across our city have been standing up for something that really matters: the right of patients, families and carers to have an independent voice.

"Healthwatch York has spent years standing up for people who feel ignored, who have struggled to get the care they need, or who simply don't know where else to turn.”

Siân Balsom, Healthwatch York manager, said: "We are delighted about this announcement. This means that independent health and social care lived experience will continue to influence the quality and equality of care.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19696118.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Siân Balsom, manager of Healthwatch York

York Central MP Rachael Maskell, who had campaigned against the proposed closure of Healthwatch, also welcomed the news, saying: "I am so glad that government has listened and taken action to ensure that this service will continue.”

Plans to axe Healthwatch were announced by then health secretary Wes Streeting last year who said there were “way too many checkers and not enough doers” in the health system.

Under Mr Streeting’s proposals, responsibility for gathering feedback on health services would have passed from Healthwatch York to the Humber and North Yorkshire Integrated Care Board, which also plans, funds and oversees local health services.

But Mr Streeting’s successor, Ms Cooper, told the Guardian earlier this week the government will retain Healthwatch England and the local Healthwatch branches, “while strengthening them to ensure patient voices are heard and can influence real change across health and care services”.

“Patients need a voice in our NHS,” she said. “I’ve always been clear on the importance of people and communities having a say in local services and that’s why I said I would review all reforms in this area.”

Healthwatch has been praised for its work highlighting issues in the health services, such as a recent report which found there is “a lot more that needs to happen” to give York women confidence that their health concerns will be taken seriously.

It also revealed earlier this year that people in York and North Yorkshire had been forced to “turn down” hospital appointments for “essential” treatment because they are no longer eligible for NHS-funded transport to get them there.
