Citizens Theatre artistic director Dominic Hill has been awarded an honorary doctorate in recognition of his contribution to the performing arts in Scotland.

Mr Hill, who has led the Glasgow theatre as artistic director and joint chief executive since 2011, received the honour from the Royal Conservatoire of Scotland (RCS) during its autumn graduation ceremony on Thursday, October 29.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21651105.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dominic Hill, artistic director and joint CEO of the Citizens Theatre in Glasgow. *(Image: Supplied)*

He was recognised for his influence on Scottish theatre, including his leadership during the Citizens Theatre’s largest-ever redevelopment.

Completed in 2025, the transformation has revitalised the historic building for audiences, artists and the wider community.

Hill has also built an extensive directing career, working on co-productions with organisations including the National Theatre of Scotland, Royal Lyceum Theatre, Bristol Old Vic and Scottish Opera.

His other credits include work with the Royal Shakespeare Company and Regent’s Park Open Air Theatre.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21651106.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dominic Hill, artistic director and joint CEO of the Citizens Theatre in Glasgow. *(Image: Supplied)*

Before joining the Citizens Theatre, he was artistic director of Edinburgh’s Traverse Theatre and joint artistic director of Dundee Rep. His directing work has taken him to theatres across the UK, including London.

The Royal Conservatoire of Scotland is a centre for performing and production arts, with a history of training artists and practitioners across the creative industries.

Dominic Hill, artistic director and joint chief executive of the Citizens Theatre, said: “Since the beginning of my directing career, the Conservatoire has always been an important part of my working life.

“It is where I have taught, learnt and met many of the people with whom I consistently collaborate today.

“I am therefore delighted to have been offered an honorary doctorate.”

Professor Jeffrey Sharkey, principal of the Royal Conservatoire of Scotland, said: “Dominic Hill has made an outstanding contribution to the performing arts in Scotland through a distinguished career as a director and artistic leader.

“His vision kept the Citizens Theatre going through an extended renovation period to have it re-emerge as a great theatre for the city and nation.

“He has played a leading role in shaping the country’s cultural landscape and has been a passionate advocate for the power of theatre to enrich lives and strengthen communities.”
