Chancellor John Healey and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting are promising workers, businesses and apprentices in Britain will benefit from major changes to procurement rules being announced during a visit Glasgow today.

Under a new "offsets policy" overseas companies that win UK defence contracts of more than £50 million will have to invest benefits back into the UK up to the same contract value, such as jobs, apprenticeships, skills and industry.

Ministers say they are prioritising buying British defence contracts wherever possible, but that where it must buy defence equipment from overseas, the new rules will ensure UK workers and businesses still benefit.

They also argue that the move will encourage overseas defence suppliers to build enduring partnerships with UK industry, helping UK small and medium sized enterprises to access global supply chains and create new opportunities for growth.

Mr Healey and Mr Streeting will outline the measures on a visit to BAE Systems Scotstoun, Glasgow, with the policy being delivered through the government's Back British approach set out in the Budget.

"We are putting British jobs and British businesses at the heart of how government spends taxpayers’ money," said the Chancellor.

"When a big contract goes overseas, these changes will ensure 100% of the value creates new opportunities for workers and businesses here at home, strengthening our industrial base and supporting growth across Britain.”

![Chancellor John Healey delivering his Budget to the House of Commons on Wednesday.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21644985.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chancellor John Healey delivering his Budget to the House of Commons on Wednesday. *(Image: House of Commons/Press Association)*

Mr Streeting added: "Every pound we invest in defence should be judged by what it does for Britain. That’s why we are backing British by buying British wherever we can.

“Even when that is not possible, we are acting to ensure that any investment brings jobs, skills and investment back home, strengthening our national security by boosting our domestic industry.”

The UK joins allies which already use similar offset type polices to boost the economic benefit from defence procurement, such as Poland, Norway and the Netherlands.

A new central Offset Authority will agree, value and monitor delivery through dedicated Offset Agreements, ensuring consistency across the lifetime of each arrangement.

The defence economy in Scotland is worth £2.5 billion and directly supports 12,200 jobs, backed by a recent £10 billion Norway export deal for Type 26 Frigates supporting 2,000 local jobs and a £453 million Typhoon radar system supporting 300 roles.

Since 2016, more than £35.2 billion has been spent on defence contracts and services with industry overseas, representing 11% of total Ministry of Defence direct expenditure with industry and commerce between 2016/17 and 2025/26.

Last year, the Ministry of Defence's investment supported 274,000 jobs in UK industry, up 10%on the year before, with £34.1 billion going to businesses based in the UK - and 85% of MOD expenditure already goes to UK firms.

The government’s fully funded Defence Investment Plan - backed by £298 billion over the next four years - will deliver stronger front-line military capability, supporting jobs, businesses and economic growth across the UK.

The policy delivers key commitments from the Defence Investment Plan and Defence Industrial Strategy, and forms part of the UK Government's wider Back British approach through the Budget.

It follows the Chancellor’s announcements on Wednesday confirming a new commitment for the National Wealth Fund and British Business Bank to deploy up to £5 billion over the next four years into defence, dual use and other sectors that support national security alongside the commencing of treaty negotiations with the Netherlands, Poland, and Finland on the Multilateral Defence Mechanism to procure at scale and stockpile vital equipment which the UK has access to.

Secretary of State for Scotland Douglas Alexander welcomed the new procurement policy.

He said: “Scotland already has a thriving defence sector, with UK Government defence spending directly supporting over 12,000 jobs. But these new rules mean if an overseas company wants a major defence contract they have to invest back to Britain, with Scottish workers and businesses standing ready to benefit.

"This is another example of Scotland's defence dividend from UK Government in action, which makes a significant contribution to our economy and national security."

In his Budget statement on Wednesday Mr Healey committed the UK to be spending 3% of GDP on defence by 2030 paving the way for cuts to other departments.

The Chancellor said he will set out the plan for reaching the target at next year’s spending review, as well as how he will hit the Nato target of 3.5% of GDP by 2035. 2030 is the year when ministers believe Russia could attack Nato.

The government is currently spending 2.6% of GDP on defence and had previously committed to spending 2.7% by 2030.

According to the Institute for Fiscal Studies (IFS), reaching 3% by 2030/31 will require about £11bn of additional spending in that year.

Kemi Badenoch, the leader of the Conservatives, and James Cartlidge, the shadow defence secretary, said Mr Healey’s announcement was a “plan to have a plan”.
