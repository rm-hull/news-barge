A PHOTOGRAPHIC exhibition celebrating the engineers shaping our world will open at the National Science and Media Museum in Bradford.

Titled 50for50: Portraits of Engineers, the exhibition marks National Engineering Day and features powerful portraits of 50 engineers whose work has transformed life in the UK and beyond.

Commissioned by the Royal Academy of Engineering as part of its 50th anniversary celebrations, the exhibition will travel to venues across the UK.

A spokesperson said: "Located in the museum’s foyer and free to visit, the exhibition features powerful portraits by acclaimed photojournalist Jude Vidal, capturing a diverse group of engineers whose innovations have helped tackle some of humanity’s greatest challenges.

"From advances in telecommunications, satellites and wind energy to breakthroughs in artificial intelligence, healthcare and computing, the exhibition shines a light on the people behind the technologies that shape our everyday lives."

Among those featured is Professor Catherine Noakes OBE FREng, based at the University of Leeds, whose research into the transmission of airborne diseases has shaped national and international policy, particularly during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Also included is Professor Sarah Hainsworth OBE FREng, Pro-Vice-Chancellor (Research) at Durham University, who grew up in Baildon and Richmond, North Yorkshire.

Her forensic engineering expertise contributed to the identification of King Richard III’s remains in Leicester in 2012, including analysis of weapon marks on the skeleton.

Jude Vidal, the photographer behind the exhibition, said: "The engineers I’ve had the privilege of photographing are some of the most remarkable people I’ve ever encountered.

"Doing relentless, world-shifting work, and doing it so quietly, so modestly.

"Their stories deserve to be told.

"The world needs the kind of hope that lives inside those stories.

"And young people need to see themselves in these portraits, to understand how big, how expansive, how genuinely exciting engineering is as a life."

The Royal Academy of Engineering, founded in 1976, brings together leading engineers and innovators to advance engineering for public benefit and promote a sustainable, inclusive future.

The exhibition aims to inspire visitors of all ages by highlighting the vital role engineering plays in addressing global challenges and improving everyday life.

Professor Noakes, Professor Hainsworth and Jude Vidal will be visiting the museum from 10.00-11.00 on Wednesday 4 November, with an opportunity for interviews and photography.
