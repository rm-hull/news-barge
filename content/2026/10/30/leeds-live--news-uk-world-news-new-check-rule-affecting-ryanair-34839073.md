# New 'check-in rule' affecting all Ryanair passengers from November 10

## Travellers with hold luggage will need to reach Ryanair’s airport desks earlier from November 10

Ryanair passengers travelling with a suitcase will face an earlier airport deadline from November 10, with a 20-minute change that could disrupt poorly timed journeys. The airline is moving its airport check-in and bag-drop cut-off from 40 minutes before departure to 60 minutes.

That means someone booked on a 10am flight must have completed the airport desk process by 9am, rather than 9.20am under the current rule. Passengers who arrive too late could find the desks closed and risk being unable to travel, even if they have a boarding pass on their phone.

Arber Abdullahu, a travel expert at TripSIM, says holidaymakers should check their transport plans now, especially if they have arranged a shared airport transfer. He said online check-in and handing over a suitcase are separate steps, and completing one does not mean the other has been dealt with.

"Your boarding pass confirms you have checked in. It does not mean your bag has been accepted or that you can turn up at the desk whenever you like," Mr Abdullahu said.

The journey from a car park or transfer drop-off to the bag-drop desk can take longer than expected, particularly at busy airports. Travellers may need to allow time for a shuttle, locating the correct terminal and queues, so arriving just before the deadline is a risky plan.

Return flights also need checking, as the new deadline applies to flights departing on or after Tuesday, November 10. Anyone heading away before that date but flying home later should confirm the return arrangements with their hotel or transfer provider rather than assuming the same timings will work in both directions.

"Work backwards from the time you need to have handed over your bag. If your transfer gets you to the terminal close to the deadline, you need to rethink that journey," Mr Abdullahu added..

Ryanair says the earlier cut-off is designed to give passengers checking in luggage more time to get through security and passport control and reach their gate before boarding. The airline estimates that around one in five passengers checks in a bag, while those travelling without checked luggage can continue checking in online and going straight to security.

Online check-in remains a separate process and closes two hours before the scheduled departure time. Passengers should check their booking and Ryanair’s latest instructions before travelling, and make sure they know the online check-in, bag-drop and boarding deadlines rather than treating them as interchangeable.
