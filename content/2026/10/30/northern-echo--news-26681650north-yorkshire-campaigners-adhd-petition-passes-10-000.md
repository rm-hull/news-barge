A North Yorkshire campaigner excluded from mainstream school as a teenager has passed a major milestone in his bid to make ADHD and neurodiversity education compulsory.

Ryan Swain, 36, from Malton, won more than 10,000 signatures within two weeks, triggering a formal written response from the Government.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647425.jpg?type=mds-article-620)John Senior MBE DL and Norton-on-Derwent mayor Dinah Keal joined Ryan Swain at the final event of his ADHD awareness tour in Pickering. *(Image: Supplied)*

His campaign calls for all schools to teach pupils about ADHD and other forms of neurodivergence in an age-appropriate way.

Mr Swain said: "When I was at school, I was labelled naughty, disruptive and difficult.

"Nobody explained ADHD to me.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647417.jpg?type=mds-article-620)After being excluded from mainstream school at 14, Ryan Swain is campaigning to ensure future generations receive earlier understanding and support. *(Image: Supplied)*

"Nobody helped me understand why I struggled with things that other children seemed to manage.

"I was excluded from mainstream education at 14.

"It took years for me to understand myself."

Mr Swain grew up with undiagnosed ADHD and Tourette’s syndrome. He says the conditions contributed to behavioural difficulties and his eventual exclusion from school.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647437.jpg?type=mds-article-620)John Senior MBE DL and Norton-on-Derwent mayor Dinah Keal joined Ryan Swain at the final event of his ADHD awareness tour in Pickering. *(Image: Supplied)*

He believes earlier understanding and proper support could have changed his childhood.

Earlier this month, he joined discussions on diagnosis, early intervention and education at Westminster’s All-Party Parliamentary Group for ADHD.

He said: "Reaching 10,000 signatures is incredibly emotional because this campaign has never been about me.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647430.jpg?type=mds-article-620)After being excluded from mainstream school at 14, Ryan Swain is campaigning to ensure future generations receive earlier understanding and support *(Image: Supplied)*

"It’s about every child who feels misunderstood, every parent fighting to be heard and every teacher who wants to help but hasn’t been given the knowledge or resources.

"We teach young people about physical health, relationships, drugs and alcohol.

"Why shouldn’t they receive age-appropriate education about ADHD, autism and other forms of neurodivergence?"

Mr Swain also founded You, Me & ADHD, which gives free ADHD awareness talks to schools, colleges and community groups across Britain.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647428.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Supplied)*

During ADHD Awareness Month in October, he gave more than 25 talks and reached more than 3,000 pupils and 250 education staff by mid-month.

He said: "Understanding ADHD doesn’t mean excusing every behaviour.

"It means recognising the person behind the behaviour and giving children the opportunity to thrive.

"Early intervention is prevention.

"Education creates understanding, and understanding can change lives."

The petition says neurodiversity lessons would reduce stigma, encourage earlier support, prevent unnecessary exclusions and make schools more inclusive.

At 100,000 signatures, the petition could be considered for debate in Parliament.

Mr Swain said: "More than 10,000 people have now supported that message, and the Government will have to respond.

"But this is only the beginning."

After his October tour, Mr Swain plans to continue giving talks through November and December, with more sessions booked for 2027.

He said: "This isn’t where the work ends.

"Imagine what could change if every child grew up understanding that different brains work in different ways.

"We might not prevent every challenge, but we could prevent years of unnecessary misunderstanding.

"No child should have to grow up believing they’re broken simply because they learn, think, or experience the world differently."
