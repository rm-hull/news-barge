SSE’s proposed Coire Glas pumped storage hydro project in the Highlands could generate £1.5 billion in economic value for the UK and support almost 3,000 construction jobs, according to a new study.

The independent economic impact assessment, carried out by BiGGAR Economics, estimates the 1.44GW scheme could also generate £1.94 billion in business spending if approved.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21651517.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SSE’s Coire Glas could support almost 3,000 UK construction jobs and nearly £2bn of spend with UK businesses. *(Image: Supplied)*

Of the projected economic value, £656 million would be generated in Scotland, where the project could support 1,180 construction jobs and deliver £1.35 billion in spending with Scottish businesses.

Located in the Great Glen between Fort William and Inverness, Coire Glas would provide 45GWh of long-duration energy storage, enough to power nearly five million homes for 30 hours.

The scheme would work by pumping water from Loch Lochy to an upper reservoir, where it would be stored before being released to generate electricity during periods of high demand.

The study estimates the project could generate £216 million in economic value for the Highlands, support 460 construction jobs at peak and deliver £635 million in spending with local businesses.

During its operational life, Coire Glas could support 60 permanent jobs across the UK and generate £7 million in economic value each year. In the Highlands, it could support 20 jobs annually and contribute £2 million in yearly economic value.

SSE has already invested more than £100 million in the project, including construction of a 1.2km exploratory tunnel.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21651516.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SSE has already invested over £100m in Coire Glas, including a 1.2km exploratory tunnel to help inform the project’s final design.  *(Image: Supplied)*

Ofgem included Coire Glas in its “Minded-to-Decision” list for potential investment support through its Long Duration Electricity Storage (LDES) cap and floor framework in June 2026. The scheme was one of 16 projects under consideration.

However, the projected economic benefits depend on SSE securing an investable outcome through the framework, with the company saying further detailed arrangements must be resolved before the project can progress.

SSE has also proposed wider commitments for the Highlands, including apprenticeships, specialist training, knowledge-transfer programmes and a £10 million Hydro Community Fund.

Stephen Wheeler, managing director of SSE Renewables, said: “Coire Glas has the potential to be one of the most important infrastructure projects built in the UK in a generation, delivering major economic benefits while strengthening the country's energy security.

“This study demonstrates the scale of that opportunity, with the potential to generate £1.5 billion of economic value across the UK, support almost 3,000 construction jobs at peak and create significant opportunities for Scottish and British businesses throughout the supply chain.

“Unlocking that opportunity now depends on securing an investable outcome through Ofgem's Long Duration Electricity Storage framework.

“A number of detailed arrangements need to be resolved to secure an investable outcome so a project of this scale and complexity can progress.

“If that can be achieved, Coire Glas could deliver decades of benefits for workers, consumers, communities and the wider economy, during construction and throughout its operating life.”
