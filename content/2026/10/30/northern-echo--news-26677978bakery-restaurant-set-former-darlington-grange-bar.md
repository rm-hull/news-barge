Plans have been lodged for a new bakery, restaurant, and bar in the courtyard of a former Darlington night spot.

The Coal House and The Bake House will soon be taking over the outdoor space of the former Grange bar on Grange Road, which closed in August 2024 after 15 years.

The units and outdoor courtyard, which was used as an outdoor bar, will be made up of a bakery, restaurant, and bar once transformation works are complete.

It is understood that the site will be run by the team behind The Chopping Block butchers at Darlington Market, which opened in April.

The main building of the old Grange bar has already been given a huge makeover into state-of-the-art offices for ELG Planning.

And, as work continues on revamping the outdoor space, new licensing plans have been submitted to Darlington Borough Council.

The Coal House wants to operate a restaurant that sells alcohol inside and has seating outside, as well as an on-site bakery, where recorded and live music would be played.

![Inside the former Grange bar](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21589908.jpg?type=mds-article-620)One of the units *(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

The venue wants to be able to play live music - both indoor and outdoor - between 12pm and 10pm and recorded music indoors from 9am to 11pm and outdoors from 12pm to 10pm daily.

It also wants to be able to sell alcohol from 9am to 11pm, both inside and outside, with opening hours between 9am and 11pm.

Despite the licensing application, it is understood that the restaurant will be open from Wednesday to Sunday between 4pm to 11pm.

![Inside The Grange in Darlington 20 months after closure](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20832642.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The old Grange bar *(Image: AF)*

ELG Planning, which owns the site and moved into the offices earlier this month, bosses said there were hopes that The Coal House and The Bake House could open next month.

Director Jeremy Good told the Echo in September how the bakery and coffee spot will be located in a shopfront that was uncovered during restoration of the building.

![Inside the new ELG Planning office at the former Grange bar](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21589843.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new ELG Planning office at the former Grange bar *(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

Believed to have been a toy shop, the store was covered behind a mural in the courtyard and is understood to stretch back to the 1950s and 1960s.

Mr Good said he expects the restaurant to have 30 seats both inside and outside and will feature open-fire cooking.

ELG Planning moved from Consicliffe Road to its new offices on October 19, which is now home to a number of meeting rooms and office space.

Over the years, ELG Planning has had several offices in the town, including on Duke Street and at Morton Park before moving to its current home, where it has been for nearly 15 years.

The planning practice, which also has an office in Harrogate, has worked on a range of projects across the region and the country, as far south as London and north as Scotland.
