THE future of a former farm attraction that welcomed more than 1.6 million visitors over two decades is set to change after approval was given for its sale.

Bradford Council has approved the disposal of St Leonards Farm, Hollins Hill, Esholt, as part of its Asset Disposal Programme and Medium-Term Financial Strategy. The decision was made by the council's Interim Strategic Director of Corporate Resources and became effective on October 24.

The site is best known as the former St Leonard's Farm Park, a popular family attraction run by James Wainhouse and his wife Denise Wainhouse. The attraction opened to the public in 1995 and went on to welcome more than 1.6 million visitors before closing in 2019 when Mr Wainhouse retired.

Over the years, the attraction became a favourite destination for families, school visits and holiday clubs, offering opportunities for children to learn about farming, meet animals and experience life in the countryside. The farm also won a prestigious White Rose Award for Best Newcomer to Tourism shortly after opening.

Council documents describe the property as being centrally located within Esholt village and comprising a four-bedroom farmhouse, a substantial range of traditional and modern farm buildings and around 80 acres of grazing land.

The farmhouse, which extends to around 1,500 sq ft, is of traditional stone construction with a stone slate roof but requires upgrading and redecoration.

The farm has been let under the provisions of the Agricultural Holdings Act 1986 since December 1994 and currently generates an annual rent of £8,075.

According to the council report, the range of traditional and more modern farm buildings was "formerly used as a dairy farm and as a visitor attraction" but has been redundant since trading ceased in 2019. The authority notes that the traditional buildings could offer scope for redevelopment or change of use, although site access would need to be improved.

The surrounding land is all grass pasture, covering approximately 80 acres, and is currently sub-let by the tenant through two grazing licences.

An alternative option considered by the council was retaining the property and continuing to manage it as part of its non-operational estate, but the authority ultimately decided to proceed with a sale
