The next generation of North East film and television talent is being given the chance to learn the tricks of the trade directly from industry experts.

Connected Campus+, run by The Northern School of Art and North East Screen, began its second year at The Northern Film & TV Studios on October 14, 2026.

The scheme gives students hands-on experience and direct access to industry professionals as they begin careers in film and television.

Catriona McAvoy, education partnerships manager at North East Screen, said: "Connected Campus+ is an opportunity to have masterclass sessions with industry experts from the film and television industry in pre-production, production, and post-production.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645022.jpg?type=mds-article-620)North East Screen Operations Director Gayle Woodruffe at the Connected Campus+ launch *(Image: Supplied)*

"We are also looking at employability skills and transferable skills that will take students into careers right across the creative industries."

This year, the programme is open only to students at The Northern School of Art.

After a successful pilot, the programme offers workshops and real-world experience to bridge the gap between education and industry.

Millie Crompton, a BA (Hons) film, TV, and theatre production student, said: "I think what stood out the most was just the variety of things you get to learn.

"It’s so varied between the storyboarding and the production meetings.

"It’s all incredibly hands-on as well.

"I think that’s one of the best bits."

Gayle Woodruffe, operations director at North East Screen, said the programme turns classroom learning into practical experience.

She said: "The beauty of Connected Campus+ is that it takes everything that the students are learning within their various different courses, and it lets them look at what those skills look like when they transfer into a career in the film and TV industries."

The scheme also helps students build connections with working film and television professionals.

Film student Saffron Livsey said: "I think what stands out to me most about the programme is the opportunity to work with other creatives and industry professionals, which you wouldn’t get without an opportunity like this.

"It’s truly amazing, honestly."

Ms Woodruffe said the sessions are practical and engaging.

She said: "The most important thing is that this can be really hands-on and workshop-led for the students, which is great.

"There is no death by PowerPoint.

"There’s lots of exciting stuff coming up.

"It’s all led by industry professionals who are not only coming to share their knowledge and do workshops with the students, but they are also people who are potentially going to work on high-end TV dramas and blockbuster feature films, and who might potentially be looking for trainees.

"So, it’s the ideal opportunity for students to get in front of those people, learn from them, but also demonstrate what they can do."

Ms McAvoy said students face a packed programme of practical learning.

She said: "As part of the programme, students will get to work with a production manager, doing some really fun activities to understand the lingo on set and set etiquette.

"We are also going to look at storyboarding and storytelling with industry experts, so learning how to build a character, develop a character, follow a story arc and lay out a screenplay.

"Later in the year, we are going to have a brilliant camera workshop where the students will work with heads of department from camera, sound and directing, and with real actors on set, recreating a real-life scene from a TV show."

Jess Tones, employability manager at The Northern School of Art, said the programme connects students with industry professionals and practical sessions to support their future careers.

Ms Crompton said the experience had already changed how she viewed the industry.

She said: "It makes the world you are trying to get into incredibly accessible, which is a fantastic place to start."
