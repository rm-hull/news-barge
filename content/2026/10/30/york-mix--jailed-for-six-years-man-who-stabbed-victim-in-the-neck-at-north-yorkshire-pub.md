A man has been jailed for six years and six months after stabbing a man in the neck.

Gary Holdsworth, 52, of Melmerby near Ripon, was sentenced at Leeds Crown Court today (Friday) after being convicted of wounding with intent and possessing a knife in a public space.

On the evening of Thursday 4 December at 11.41pm at The George and Dragon Inn in Melmerby near Ripon, a verbal argument over payment took place.

#### The George and Dragon Inn, Melmerby

It turned physical, progressing outside the pub where the victim, a 46-year-old man, was then stabbed in the neck by Holdsworth.

He was taken to hospital for surgery to his injuries.

Holdsworth fled the scene after the attack, but was arrested at his home address shortly afterwards.

He pleaded not guilty to the two offences on 28 April, but was found guilty by jury on 4 August and was remanded in custody until his sentencing today.

As well as the jail sentence, Holdsworth was also handed a restraining order preventing him from contacting the victim and their partner until further order.

## ‘My life has completely changed’

In her Victim Personal Statement the partner of the victim said: “My boyfriend is a kind, loving, and caring man.

“On that day, he was stabbed in the neck and nearly lost his life. This was not just an attack – it was something that could have taken him away from all of us forever.

“Since that day, my life has completely changed.

“I live with fear, anxiety, and constant worry about what happened and what could have happened. The images and emotions stay with me, and it is something I cannot escape.

“This incident has deeply affected not just me, but our entire family. It has taken away our sense of safety, peace, and normal life.”

Detective Chief Inspector Cheryl Quinn said: “I welcome the sentencing of Gary Holdsworth who has been held accountable for his shocking actions that night.

“The use of a knife to inflict such a serious injury is a deeply concerning act of violence with the potential to cause life threatening harm and devastating consequences for victims and their families.

“My thoughts remain with the victim as he continues to deal with the physical and emotional impact of this incident. We recognise the lasting effects that offences of this nature have on individuals, families, and the wider community.

“This case serves as a reminder of the serious consequences of carrying and using knives. Violence of this nature will be treated with the utmost seriousness, and we remain committed to working with our partners and the community to prevent knife crime, protect the public and bring offenders to justice.”

Holdsworth’s co-accused, a 41-year-old man was charged with affray, but was found not guilty.
