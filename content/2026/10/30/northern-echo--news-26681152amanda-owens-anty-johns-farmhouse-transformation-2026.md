Amanda Owen's ambitious restoration of Anty John's farmhouse in the Yorkshire Dales is approaching another major milestone after years of work to bring the derelict property back to life.

The Yorkshire Shepherdess and her family have spent several years transforming the remote farmhouse, with the project forming the centrepiece of Channel 4's Our Farm Next Door: Amanda, Clive and Kids.

Now, as the fourth series approaches its final scheduled episode, viewers are set to see the interior of Anty John's beginning to resemble a family home.

It marks a significant change for a property which viewers first saw in a considerably more dilapidated condition.

Situated close to the family's famous Ravenseat Farm in Swaledale, Anty John's was purchased with the aim of providing more space and opportunities for the Owen family's future.

However, bringing the building back into use has proved far from straightforward.

The remote location, changing Yorkshire Dales weather and the demands of restoring an old property have all presented challenges.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21368043.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anty John's *(Image: CHANNEL 4)*

During the fourth series, Amanda and eldest son Miles were seen painting walls after the plaster had finally dried.

The family also tackled problems with the farmhouse's water supply, while work continued on the flooring and upstairs accommodation.

In one particularly unusual decorating development, the family used gallons of tea as part of the restoration work.

The latest episodes have also shown the upstairs space being divided into individual rooms, giving viewers a clearer picture of how the building could eventually be used.

The transformation has taken place alongside the family's continued responsibilities at Ravenseat, where livestock and the changing farming seasons remain central to daily life.

For Amanda, the farmhouse represents more than simply another renovation project.

It is part of the family's plans for the future, with the nine Owen children growing older and developing different interests.

The project has also brought the history of the property into focus, with Amanda investigating the lives of people who previously lived there.

As the fourth series approaches its conclusion, the arrival of furniture represents one of the clearest signs yet of the progress made.
