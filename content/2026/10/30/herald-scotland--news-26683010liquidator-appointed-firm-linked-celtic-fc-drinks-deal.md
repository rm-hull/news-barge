A liquidator has been appointed to a company linked to a drinks deal with the Scottish Premiership league champions.

The Glasgow firm connected to the three-year deal with Celtic FC has been placed into voluntary liquidation.

The Gorbals Drinks Co Limited passed a resolution to be wound up last month.

An official listing states: “That it has been proved to the satisfaction of the meeting that the company cannot by reason of its liabilities continue its business and that it is advisable to wind up same and, accordingly, that the company be wound up voluntarily."

![Gorbals Drinks Co via YouTube](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21648869.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Company linked to Scottish Premiership champions drinks deal placed into liquidation *(Image: Gorbals Drinks Co via YouTube)*

It also agreed that Ian Wright and Brian Milne, of Quantuma Advisory, should be appointed liquidators.

Now, the appointment of Mr Wright and Mr Milne has been documented.

Celtic announced its three-year deal with the Gorbals Drinks Co in January 2023 as its “official spirit partner”.

Celtic said in a statement at the time: “Their core range includes Graft Gin, Good Rum and New Vodka, which will be available at Celtic Park soon.

“Gorbals Drinks Co exists to create a wide and adventurous range of drinks that are open to everyone, fearlessly taking inspiration from every corner of the globe.

“Their growing range of products are guided by honesty, hard work and a healthy dose of risk-taking - all in the spirit of the Gorbals.”

Gorbals Drinks Co has publicised another signature drink not named by Celtic, called Pure Venom, alongside its Graft Gin, Good Rum and New Vodka

The Pure Venom trademark is registered to Gorbals Drinks Co Limited.

Gorbals Drinks Co posted a YouTube video against the background of its marketing material with Celtic player Jota, or João Pedro Neves Filipe.

Gorbals Drinks Co Limited was established in 2022 and is described as a "wholesaler of wine, beer, spirits and other alcoholic beverages".

The drinks company also established a retail presence at Silverburn shopping centre at one stage.

The Herald has reported on the backdrop of a short-term rise in the number of creditors’ voluntary liquidations in Scotland, some involving drinks-related businesses.

Insolvency Service figures show that in September, there were 117 company insolvencies registered in Scotland, 14% higher than the number for the same period last year.

The number of company insolvencies was made up of 68 creditors’ voluntary liquidations, 38 compulsory liquidations, 10 administrations and one company voluntary arrangement.

There were 73 company insolvencies registered in Scotland in August. The total number of company insolvencies consisted of 30 creditors’ voluntary liquidations, 35 compulsory liquidations and eight administrations.
