The chief executive of Yorkshire Water, Nicola Shaw, has stepped down after the company was given the lowest possible environmental rating following a rise in pollution incidents.

Ms Shaw announced on Friday (October 30) she would be ending her tenure at Yorkshire Water after almost five years and will be replaced by Nicola Medalova, the former chief operating officer of National Grid New England and a non-executive director of Yorkshire Water.

Ms Medalova will join Yorkshire Water on Monday and will officially take up the chief executive role on January 1.




It comes after Yorkshire Water on Thursday was given a one-star (poor) rating by the Environment Agency (EA), meaning it would automatically be blocked from giving out bonuses to bosses under new rules introduced last year.

New figures published by the EA showed Yorkshire Water was responsible for 260 pollution incidents in 2025, up from 151 the year before. This is a rise of more than 70 per cent and the highest total since 2011.

A Yorkshire Water spokesperson said its environmental performance last year was “not good enough and we understand that customers will be disappointed by this result”.

“In particular, we need to reduce pollution incidents and improve the reliability of our environmental performance."

The spokesperson also said Ms Shaw decided earlier this year it “would not be appropriate to receive a bonus due to our performance in the previous financial year, which was not good enough – particularly in relation to our environmental impact”.

It follows Ms Shaw facing backlash from regional leaders after it was revealed she had received an extra £1.3 million from Yorkshire Water’s offshore parent company, Kelda Holdings, since 2023.

Ofwat had already **blocked bonus payments from Yorkshire Water, as well as five other water companies,** over failings.

The water regulator, after Ms Shaw’s extra pay from Kelda Holdings was revealed by the Guardian, **announced plans to force water firms to report pay received from parent companies**.

Environment secretary Dame Angela Eagle has summoned water bosses to Whitehall to explain their failures and outline immediate action.

Announcing her resignation, Ms Shaw said the "time was right for a smooth transition".

"It has been a privilege to lead Yorkshire Water and serve the people of Yorkshire," she said.

"Although there remains a huge amount of work to do, I am extremely proud of what our colleagues have achieved, putting the business on a stronger footing for delivery across all aspects of its work.

"These jobs are challenging but fulfilling and I am especially grateful for the dedication and commitment of colleagues at all levels throughout the business across this county – thank you all.

"This now feels like the right time to begin an orderly transition to the company’s next chapter.

"I know Nicola Medalova will do an outstanding job leading the company and I look forward to working closely with her over the coming months to ensure a seamless handover."

Jon Lewis, chair of Yorkshire Water, thanked Ms Shaw for the "important contribution she has made to Yorkshire Water".

"Under her leadership, the company secured £500m of new shareholder equity, with a further £600m committed, enabling us to embark on the largest investment programme in our history and accelerate work to strengthen the resilience of Yorkshire’s water network.

"She leaves the business with strong foundations for the next phase of investment and improvement.

"We are delighted that Nicola Medalova will become our next chief executive.

"She is an accomplished and inspiring leader, with deep operational experience and a strong track record of delivering major capital programmes in complex environments.

"She is passionate about delivering for customers and brings a strong understanding of the environmental responsibilities that sit at the heart of Yorkshire Water’s role.

"Importantly, she also knows Yorkshire Water well from her time as a non-executive director on the board.

"The board believes she is the right person to lead the company through its next phase, improving service for customers, reducing pollution and delivering the investment needed to modernise Yorkshire’s water network."

Ms Medalova, a chartered environmental health officer and a registered specialist in land condition, said she was "delighted to be joining Yorkshire Water".

"Yorkshire Water faces huge challenges but also great opportunities for continued improvement and I look forward to working with Nicola Shaw, the leadership team and colleagues across the business as I get up to speed," she said.

"I’m looking forward to getting to know Yorkshire Water, its people and the communities it serves.

"My immediate priority will be to listen and learn, while maintaining a relentless focus on delivery.

"Yorkshire Water will ultimately be judged by what it delivers for customers, communities and the environment, and I’m excited about the opportunity to help shape its future."
