Hundreds of people joined a lantern parade at Bradford’s Lister Park this evening as the Festival of the Dead returned.

Schools, community groups, artists and residents from across the district gathered for an event focused on life, remembrance and community.

## Lanterns light up the park

Visitors began arriving from 4.30pm, with crowds meeting around Cartwright Hall before the main procession.

As darkness fell, participants carried handmade lanterns through the park, creating a display of light along its paths and open spaces.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653664.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park *(Image: Newsquest)*

The annual event invites schools and community groups to design and make their own lanterns.

This year’s parade included a wide range of creations produced by people of all ages from across Bradford.

![Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653667.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park *(Image: Newsquest)*

Families could also take part in lantern-making workshops inside Cartwright Hall.

Participants made hand-painted lanterns which they could carry during the parade.

## Music and workshops for families

Live music and performances took place throughout the evening. Peace Artistes, Drum Machine and Banda da Rua were among those performing as visitors watched from around the park.

![Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653668.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park *(Image: Newsquest)*

Food stalls served hot drinks, burgers and sweet treats.

Cartwright Hall also stayed open later than usual, giving visitors the chance to explore its galleries during the celebrations.

Organisers say the Festival of the Dead brings communities together through remembrance, creativity and shared experiences. It gives people an opportunity to reflect, remember loved ones and celebrate life in a welcoming community setting.

![Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653669.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park *(Image: Newsquest)*

The project is delivered through a partnership between the University of Bradford, Cecil Green Arts and Bradford Death Cafés. It is supported by Arts Council England, Bradford Museums & Galleries and Bradford Council.

For many visitors, the lantern procession was the main highlight. Hundreds of illuminated creations moved through the park as the parade wound its way around the grounds.

![Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653670.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park *(Image: Newsquest)*

The event has become an increasingly popular part of Bradford’s calendar, combining art, music and remembrance in a community celebration. Its organisers use creative activities and performances to encourage people to meet, take part and share experiences.

## Festival heads to Northcliffe Park next

The celebrations will continue with a second Festival of the Dead event at Northcliffe Park in Shipley on November 14.

![Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21653671.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Festival of the Dead Show in Lister Park *(Image: Newsquest)*

Further details about the Northcliffe Park parade are expected to be announced nearer the event.

Organisers hope the Shipley gathering will build on the success of the Lister Park celebrations.

The festival’s events are intended to provide space for remembrance while also bringing communities together.

Visitors can attend as families, join workshops, watch performances or take part in the procession.

The Lister Park gathering ended with the illuminated lanterns moving through the grounds, marking another year of the Bradford event.
