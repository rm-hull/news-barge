Digital sovereignty today is less about where data sits and more about who controls it. It is the ability for organizations to govern their data, infrastructure and operations, while retaining the flexibility needed to innovate.

Sovereignty is about having true control over your data and knowing where it lives, how it is regulated and exactly how it can be accessed at all times. Organizations can approach sovereignty through balancing regulatory requirements, operational resilience and innovation.

Different industries face vastly different pressures when it comes to data sovereignty. Banks, insurers, and healthcare providers must protect sensitive data while staying agile, while tech companies or logistics firms often require a greater level of flexibility.

In plain terms, data sovereignty is impacted by how data is governed by the laws of the country or region it is physically stored and/or accessed in. Data ownership and control are crucial, but current conversations about sovereignty have become a catch-all for resilience and security – which goes beyond data residency alone. It is never just about the physical location of the data.

The main challenge organizations face now is how to determine the right level of sovereignty for their regulatory, operational and innovation specific needs.

## Sovereignty across sectors

When it comes to regulation, different industries face entirely different regulatory and operational pressures, with no single universal mandate applying across all sectors.

In highly regulated industries like healthcare, government and financial services, organizations are required to meet strict local data controls to comply with sector-specific regulations, one example being NIS Regulations within the EU. Organizations working with sensitive data must ensure they are protecting personal and financial data while they remain operationally flexible.

For example, a global bank holds highly sensitive customer data like account details and transaction history and would therefore need to operate under dense and overlapping regulatory frameworks. Both customers and regulators expect strict control over the data, requiring the organization to uphold a specific and tightly governed sovereignty framework in its foundations.

Organizations in less regulated industries like tech or retail can often leverage global cloud provider infrastructure more freely, without needing to carefully balance performance or innovation.

Sectors that don’t require high levels of sovereignty have to ensure they do not overdo the concept as it often leads to slower development and wasted resources.

## Finding your place on the spectrum

Think of sovereignty as a dial which your organization uses to find your place on the spectrum. The dial moves depending on how much control you need, over what data and under which jurisdiction, to find the exact level for your organization and potentially per project or service. This method lets you examine your business in accordance to the regulatory requirements for your sector and location.

There are three main questions you need to ask to guide your decisions. Ask what your actual regulatory obligations are and where operational resilience requires local control vs where it demands global flexibility. Then think about where, in your business, does innovation depend on the free movement of data.

Once you have answered the above questions, you’ll know what level of sovereignty applies to your organization and how and when it might be subject to change. It is important to remember that your sovereignty decision must be revisited as your business scales, enters new markets and jurisdictions and as regulation evolves in your sector.

## Why your database is a sovereign decision

Your sovereignty decision is essentially ensuring you understand the importance of security, compliance, accountability and explainability when it comes to your organization's internal business controls and regulatory requirements.

In light of this, database infrastructure is taking on an elevated role and supporting enterprises across industries who choose and tailor the level of sovereignty they require and that’s just right for them.

Modern database software platforms can support enterprises across the spectrum, with strict geo-fenced deployments with data localization and flexible multi-cloud database architectures for more global businesses. To become a strategic decision, sovereignty has to be considered at the data layer of your infrastructure.

Technical capabilities like end-to-end encryption, strict audit and access controls and scalable support across the globe are what often matter in a decision about sovereignty. The right model of infrastructure gives organizations the choice to tailor their level of control to their actual needs.

## Sovereignty is a strategy

Sovereignty must be viewed as a spectrum that an organisation can choose from. It cannot be treated as a linear standard across industries and organizations.

Enterprises who treat sovereignty as a deliberate strategy are the ones getting sovereignty right. This outlook forces organizations to be precise about the exact thing they are protecting, at what cost and why they are protecting it.

Before asking if your organization is sovereign, think about whether you are sovereign in the ways that actually matter for your business and sector.
