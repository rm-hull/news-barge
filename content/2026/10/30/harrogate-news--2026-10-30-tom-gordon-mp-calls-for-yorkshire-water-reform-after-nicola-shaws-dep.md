**Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon has welcomed Nicola Shaw’s resignation, criticising her leadership of the Environment Agency over river pollution and the management of the country’s natural resources.**

In a statement issued on 30 October, Gordon said Shaw should have left her position earlier, pointing to concerns over river pollution, water bills and the prospect of hosepipe bans.

Shaw’s departure comes amid continued scrutiny of the water industry and the Environment Agency’s role in protecting the environment. Gordon criticised what he described as poor management of natural resources, arguing that the public was paying the price while senior figures received substantial salaries.

> **Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, said:** Good riddance. Nicola Shaw oversaw abysmal management of our precious natural resources, trashing our rivers while receiving an eyewatering salary.
>
> She should have stepped down much earlier instead of hanging on to receive the lowest possible Environment Agency rating.

He also criticised the situation facing households dealing with rising water bills and concerns about water supplies.

> **Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, said:** Her replacement needs to understand that residents are at their wits end, paying ever-rising bills for polluted rivers and threats of hosepipe bans while someone at the top rakes in millions.

### Call for changes to Yorkshire Water

Gordon used the announcement to renew his call for Yorkshire Water to be converted into a public benefit company, arguing that its legal obligations should place greater emphasis on environmental protection and reducing leaks.

> **Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, said:** If the Government had any sense they’d convert Yorkshire Water into a public benefit company now, legally required to prioritise environmental goals and reduce leaks.

A public benefit company model, as proposed by Gordon, would seek to give environmental objectives a formal role in the company’s responsibilities. His statement did not set out detailed proposals for how such a change would be implemented.

The MP’s comments reflect wider political debate over water company accountability, sewage pollution, infrastructure investment and the balance between shareholder interests and environmental responsibilities.

Gordon’s criticisms of Shaw and the Environment Agency were made in a statement issued by his parliamentary office. The statement did not include a response from Shaw or the Environment Agency.
