If you want to create a website, it’s a good idea to get a domain name. This is essentially the name (followed by ‘.com’, ‘.co.uk’ or another ending) that people will search to find your business website or online portfolio. While you could also set up a website under a free platform subdomain, this might not deliver the professional first impression that you want to give.

You don’t even need to get a domain with a view to use it. You could get a couple and have them on standby. In fact, some people make money by collecting domain names and then reselling them. Ultimately, though, it’s useful to be able to connect your personalised domain name to your business website, or at least some dedicated email hosting.

There’s a number of domain hosting providers available and it can be difficult to know which one to go for. To make your search simpler, I’ve done the research for you. I looked at different options and examined their pros and cons, before narrowing them down to my top picks, which I’ve shared below. While the right service for you will depend on what you want to get out of it, I personally recommend all of the options listed here. Keep reading for my full review of Namecheap, Hostinger, GoDaddy and Bluehost.

Hostinger gets straight to the point, ensuring that domain name registration is very simple. Just enter the domain name you want and Hostinger comes up with what’s available along with some suggestions of something similar. It has an AI domain generator for anyone who’s not sure where to begin. In all cases, you get free Whois privacy protection bundled in.

![Hostinger testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/30/8/07/Hostinger-testing-(1).png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Talking of bundles, Hostinger does the smart thing of appreciating that you want to keep your business name safe. It offers up bundles for a discount. That typically includes .co.uk, .online, and .org to get you started with protecting your brand’s online presence. The best prices come from committing to a three-year term with some domains costing only 1p. That price steadily increases if you only want the domain for a year. I can’t see many people only needing a domain name for 12 months though, so it’s not much of an issue unless you’re playing around with ideas.

Once set up, the Hostinger dashboard has all you could need. It arranges transfers well but also has a great area for your domain portfolio. You can switch on auto-renew, check out privacy protection settings, enable domain forwarding, and lock your domain so it isn’t going anywhere. In the same dashboard, Hostinger also suggests other similar domain names you might want. It’s a useful service overall and competitively priced.

Ever wanted to type in the name of a domain, press ‘add all’, and then own a frankly ridiculous number of domain names? If that’s your dream, then you will love Namecheap. I was quietly in awe of Namecheap’s Beast Mode which comes up with every domain name possible. There are literally hundreds of options and if you want, you can add them all at the touch of a button. Then there’s just a short wait while Namecheap processes everything.

![Name cheap testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/30/8/02/Name-cheap-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Whether that’s your aim or you just want one domain name, Namecheap is very easy to use. It offers domain privacy for free as standard, with a free trial for web hosting available, too. Listed prices are for the first year too with a one-year plan being standard, so there aren’t any hidden costs or surprises. Namecheap doesn’t even enable auto-renew by default but be aware that you’re paying in USD dollars which may incur extra fees with your bank.

Once set up, the dashboard is a little messy but has everything you need. That includes details about your privacy being protected, nameservers, the option to set up a redirect, and ways to share access to the domain with others such as a business partner. It’s all simple to figure out and I really appreciated the lack of hidden costs.

![GoDaddy testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/30/8/11/GoDaddy-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

By buying a domain, you still get access to a free plan which covers the basics and instantly gives you a presence online. This could be overkill if your aim is to collect many domain names but for a personal project, it’s useful. When searching for a domain name, GoDaddy finds a lot of different options like .co.uk to .sucks and some other fun ones. Individual years tend to cost more so it’s worth keeping an eye on what the prices actually break down to. For a quick way to make your mark online, GoDaddy hits the spot well despite the sometimes vague pricing.

Bluehost starts out very well. Search for a domain name and if it’s already taken, it’ll suggest a (usually) good alternative. That’s without using the AI suggestion box which goes even further. If you’re not sure where to start, it’s a great place to brainstorm ideas. From there, find the domain name you want and instantly be pleased by the fact that the price is the same for the first year whether you commit to three years or just one. The downside? Whois privacy protection is an additional fee which also bundles in advanced security. That’s an annoying one if you want to keep your business or home address private, as you’ll almost certainly have to pay the extra.

Once signed up, Bluehost’s domain list is very simple to manage. An overview gives you insight on when it expires, how protected it is, whether the domain lock is active, and more. It also suggests similar domain names that you might want to buy while you’re committing to your brand name.

If you need to change DNS records, update nameservers, or anything else like that, it takes moments to do. I liked how simple but effective the Bluehost dashboard is at helping you achieve those aims. It’s a good domain hosting service despite the additional fee involved in protecting your privacy.

## The verdict: Best domain hosting services

When looking for a domain hosting service, you only really need one provider. That provider should almost certainly be Hostinger. It’s affordable, takes moments to set up, and is ideal for your long-term goals. Other domain hosting providers like Namecheap and Bluehost are also good to experiment with.

A strong alternative is GoDaddy with its instantly-created landing page. I found that great for anyone short on time as you can still achieve a lot quickly. It’s not always the cheapest, especially if you just want to commit to a one-year plan, but it’s good for a quick solution.

Ultimately, each of the domain hosting services listed here are worth checking out. Do some comparisons, take a look at their respective AI searches, and go from there.

## How the best domain hosting services were tested

When testing the best domain hosting services, it was important to consider some key factors so that I could directly compare each service fairly. Here’s a look at what I focused on while trying out many different domain hosting services:

* **Value for money**: There’s a lot of competition out there so I kept an eye on the prices of domains. That mostly meant comparing prices, but also looking for any hidden extras that might increase the price. I also looked at any introductory pricing that would lead to a significant price hike in the future.
* **Ease of use**: I wanted a domain hosting service that was quick to set up and ideally had an attractive dashboard to look at what you’d just purchased. I also looked at how easy it was to cancel renewal policies and how simple it was to find other domain names.
* **Features**: A domain name is a fairly straightforward thing to purchase, but I wanted to see what other features such services provide. That could be anything from making it easy to attach web hosting or email hosting to it, or simply how easy it was to have your domain name choice evaluated.
* **Variety**: There are a lot of different domain names out there and I wanted to see which hosting service provided the best variety. While some domain name suffixes might not be vital, I appreciated being able to see so many options provided to me, and also some similar twists on the name I originally had in mind.
