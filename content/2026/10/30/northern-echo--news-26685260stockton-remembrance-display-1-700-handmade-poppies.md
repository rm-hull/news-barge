More than 1,700 handmade poppies have filled a Teesside shopping centre ahead of Remembrance Sunday.

Stockton Borough Council has praised the colourful display at Wellington Square, created in remembrance of those who served in the Armed Forces.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652122.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Handcrafted poppies *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

The Royal British Legion’s Stockton and Yarm branch, Wellington Square and Stockton-on-Tees Community Wellbeing Champions joined forces for the project, with charities, businesses and residents donating hand-crafted poppies.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652123.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Royal British Legion members alongside Community Wellbeing Champions, together creating a Remembrance display for Stockton. *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652112.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Handcrafted poppies *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

What started as an idea for a smaller display soon grew into a much bigger community effort, bringing together more than 1,700 poppies.

The council shared pictures of the finished display on social media, thanking everyone who helped bring the tribute together.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652113.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Handcrafted poppies, including the purple flowers honouring animals that served and died in the war. *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652118.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Handcrafted poppies *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

The poppies will remain on display at a pop-up unit in Wellington Square until Saturday, November 7.

The Community Health and Wellbeing Champions said the project would support the Royal British Legion’s Stockton and Yarm branch and boost its fundraising.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21652115.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Handcrafted poppies *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

It comes as communities prepare to mark Remembrance across the country.

This year's Remembrance Sunday falls on November 8, with Armistice Day following on November 11, marking the anniversary of the end of the First World War in 1918.

The poppy has become a familiar symbol of remembrance, with the Royal British Legion's annual Poppy Appeal supporting serving and former members of the Armed Forces and their families.
