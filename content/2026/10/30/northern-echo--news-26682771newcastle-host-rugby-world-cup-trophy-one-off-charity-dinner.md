The Rugby World Cup trophy is returning to the North East for a one-off charity event, its first visit in more than a decade.

It will be displayed at a special dinner in Newcastle on December 3, hosted by tech start-up KLIK SaaS.

Ben Cohen, who was part of England’s 2003 World Cup-winning squad, will be guest of honour.

![Rugby cup](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648597.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Guest speaker Ben Cohen MBE with the prized Webb Ellis Cup *(Image: Premiere Pro)*

Mr Cohen said: "The Webb Ellis Cup represents one of the greatest moments in English sporting history, and having been part of that incredible journey, it will always hold a very special place in my heart.

"I’m really looking forward to joining KLIK SaaS at Dakota for what promises to be a fantastic evening.

"It’s not often you get the opportunity to experience something like this in such an intimate setting, with the trophy itself taking centre stage.

"It’ll be a chance to share some great stories, relive a few unforgettable moments, enjoy some wonderful company and, most importantly, raise valuable funds for causes that really matter.

"I’m delighted to be part of it."

![Rugby cup](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648596.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kelly Whitfield, who launched technology company KLIK SaaS in 2023, is organising the event *(Image: Premiere Pro)*

The event at the Dakota Hotel on Newcastle Quayside will include a drinks reception, dinner, charity auction and Q&A with Mr Cohen.

Guests will also be able to have their pictures taken with Mr Cohen and the Webb Ellis Cup.

It will be the first private function in the region to host the Rugby World Cup trophy and only the third time the cup has visited the North East.

The last visit came during the official trophy tour before the 2015 tournament.

Money raised on the night will support several charities, including Champions 03, which helps rugby players adjust to life after professional sport.

Other causes include the Lewis Moody Foundation, which raises awareness of brain tumours, as well as motor neurone disease (MND) and early-onset dementia, which Mr Moody and Steve Thompson were diagnosed with in 2025 and 2020 respectively.

North East entrepreneur Kelly Whitfield, who launched technology company KLIK SaaS in 2023, is organising the event.

KLIK SaaS helps organisations build engaged communities through online forums, groups, events and other tools.

Ms Whitfield said: "This is going to be an incredible evening – and what a coup for Newcastle to be the first city in the North East to host the cup.

"KLIK SaaS is all about community, not only in business but in life and sport.

"So it was a natural fit for us to want to bring together the region’s rugby fans, of which I am most certainly one, and offer this once-in-a-generation opportunity.

"Having Ben with us will be brilliant.
