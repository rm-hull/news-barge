Plans for hundreds of homes at Skerningham Garden Village would have been refused if the council had come to a decision on time.

Skerningham Estates Ltd wants to build 441 homes to the west of the East Coast Mainline and up to 400 homes on fields north of Darlington Golf Club and south of Brampton.

Both plans form part of the wider Skerningham Garden Village, which spans 487 acres and is intended to see the construction of around 3,700 homes.

But three applications on the contentious project are being appealed by the developer after the time limit for Darlington Borough Council to decide on them lapsed.

A public inquiry on the plans is now set to take place on March 2 next year.

A council planning meeting on Wednesday (October 28) decided that two of the applications for the homes would have been thrown out, if a decision was made on time.

![Plans show how the initial 450-home layout could look](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/18450678.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The first phase of homes for Skerningham Garden Village *(Image: Skerningham Estates)*

A third application for a bridge over the River Skerne and an access road to allow for phase one of homes to be built would have been approved.

The homes plans were refused largely over plans for developer’s contributions to the area, also known as Section 106 (S106) Agreements.

The agreements ensure the right amount of facilities are provided to “make the area pleasant for everyone who will live or work there”.

But councillors heard how the agreement had not been agreed and completed with the current promoters of three sites within the garden village, including Skerningham Estates Ltd.

Planning officers said it had not been demonstrated that infrastructure will be delivered within the required timeframe to avoid “severe adverse impacts” on nearby roads.

Neil Westwick, senior director at Lichfields, on behalf of the developer, said it was “committed” to providing the infrastructure but that it was “complicated” due to the involvement of multiple other development parties.

![The planning meeting at the Darlington Borough Council chamber](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646997.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The planning meeting at the Darlington Borough Council chamber *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

“Essentially, we have had constructive discussions with the council on this but we haven’t managed to reach a conclusion on that,” Mr Westwick said.

“Not because there is a problem with delivery, or payment, but we just haven't managed to reach a conclusion.”

Mr Westwick argued the sites would deliver “much needed affordable housing” and “significant investment” in local infrastructure and was an "important development”.

But College Ward Cllr Bryony Holroyd argued that because the agreement hadn't been signed that it was “incredibly risky” and that we “shouldn’t touch any of this with a barge pole”.

Concerns were also raised over a pub being in the plans for phase one, given nearby venues had “struggled over the years”, car dependency due to local school places, ecology, and reports of methane and carbon dioxide gas from the nearby Brampton Quarry.

Councillors refused the second homes plan - which included plans for up to 400 homes, a neighborhood centre, healthcare, sports pitches, and allotments - similarly for the lack of the Section 106 agreement.

However, plans for the bridge and temporary haul road Barmpton Lane and Bishopton Lane, to serve the construction of phase one, would have been approved.
