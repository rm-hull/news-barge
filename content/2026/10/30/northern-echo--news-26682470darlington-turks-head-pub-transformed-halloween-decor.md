Skeletons, spiders and sinister creatures have taken over a Darlington pub as its new managers prepare to celebrate Halloween in spectacular style.

Paul Corbett and Gina Saint, managers of The Turk’s Head, have splashed out on their decorations for this Halloween weekend.

While this is their first year at The Turk's Head, having managed a pub in Leeds for the previous two, this is the third year running that they’ve transformed their pub for the spooky season.

![Paul Corbett and Gina Saint have transformed The Turk’s Head in Darlington with skeletons, spiders, pumpkin figures and other decorations ahead of Halloween celebrations at the pub](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648306.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Turk’s Head in Darlington has been given a spooky makeover for Halloween

In the last two years, they won a regional competition for Best Decorated Pub.

Gina’s inspiration comes from her father, who put on what she called “the best Halloween party on the street” when she was younger.

Since then, she has carried the spirit of going “above and beyond” into her work.

For her, it is all about “having fun and enjoying life”.

![Paul Corbett and Gina Saint have transformed The Turk’s Head in Darlington with skeletons, spiders, pumpkin figures and other decorations ahead of Halloween celebrations at the pub](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648291.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Paul Corbett and Gina Saint outside The Turk’s Head in Darlington, which they have transformed for Halloween

She said: “We all work so hard,” and added that it is important to her to give people “something to look forward to”.

The pub is decked with human-sized skeletons, spiders, and pumpkin men, many of which are mechanical installations.

Paul said the “skeleton on the front was about £200”.

But given the size of the pub on Bondgate, **Darlington**, they had to find creative ways to make it cheaper.

The walls are covered head to toe in weed fabric to create a black-out effect, and the clouds spanning the entire ceiling were sourced from several duvets.

The transformation is so thorough that it looks more like a haunted house than a pub.

![Paul Corbett and Gina Saint have transformed The Turk’s Head in Darlington with skeletons, spiders, pumpkin figures and other decorations ahead of Halloween celebrations at the pub](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648292.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Elaborate Halloween decorations fill The Turk’s Head in Darlington

Paul and Gina said: “We are hoping everyone’s going to join in and dress up.”

Gina added: “I want it to become a tradition, not just for the pub, but for the community."

She noted that one of the pubs nearby has not followed suit by decorating, adding: “I thought it was funny, but that’s doing the opposite of what I wanted…I want people to get involved.”

Paul and Gina have poured a lot of themselves into their pub, from recreating family memories to hanging up Leeds United and Newcastle United football flags on the ceiling.

Gina said that business and personal “go hand in hand”, and that “if you don’t put the effort in, [customers] are not going to enjoy it”.

![Paul Corbett and Gina Saint have transformed The Turk’s Head in Darlington with skeletons, spiders, pumpkin figures and other decorations ahead of Halloween celebrations at the pub](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648293.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Black coverings and homemade clouds help create the Halloween atmosphere inside The Turk’s Head

The Turk’s Head is hosting a rock night tonight (Friday, October 30) and a live singer on Saturday to accompany the celebrations.

“We like to have live entertainment”, Paul said.

Moreover, as part of a national costume competition hosted by Proper Pubs, well-dressed customers could have the opportunity to win a trip to Butlins.

Due to the success they’ve seen this year, Paul says the brewery is spending more money on the pub in the new year, with plans to build a stage in the garden.

As this weekend is likely to have a big turnout, the message from Paul and Gina is: “Why go small when you can go big."
