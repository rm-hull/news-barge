A drug-dealing duo were caught hiding under a trampoline and in a stream after doing a runner from police who stopped their taxi.

Mackenzie Holmes and Ethan Hannon tried to flee but were soon detained, Teesside Crown Court heard.

Prosecutor Joe Culley said police tracked Hannon by drone and arrested him hiding in a nearby stream.

Holmes stayed in the taxi at first, handing over cocaine and crack cocaine before running off, the court heard.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643406.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mackenzie Holmes

“He jumped over domestic fences through gardens and was found hiding underneath a trampoline,” he said.

Mr Culley said police found cash, cocaine and crack cocaine in Hannon’s jacket, while a further £3,000-plus and more drugs were recovered from his home.

Police found a knuckleduster when they searched Holmes’ home, he said.

Holmes was already on bail after police stopped him on the A19 in June 2025 and found drugs and a mobile phone containing drug-dealing messages.

Mr Culley said Holmes was also arrested in March after police found him carrying two bags of cocaine and cash.

Holmes, of The Greenway, Middlesbrough, admitted possessing crack cocaine and cocaine with intent to supply, supplying cocaine, being concerned in supplying cannabis, and possessing cocaine with intent to supply between June 2024 and March 2026.

The 21-year-old also admitted possessing cocaine and crack cocaine with intent to supply, plus possessing a knuckleduster, from February this year.

His barrister, Robert Mochrie, said Holmes accepted he had made his position worse by committing further offences while on bail.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643407.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ethan Hannon

Hannon, of Hershall Drive, Middlesbrough, admitted possessing cocaine and crack cocaine with intent to supply, and possessing criminal property, from February.

His lawyer, Dan Wilberforce, said Hannon dealt drugs for only 45 days, mainly transporting and preparing Class A drug packages to pay off his debts.

Recorder Peter Makepeace KC jailed Holmes for four years and six months. He said: “The reality is you have forced my hand in this case by committing offences repeatedly whilst on police bail – that was a huge error on your part.”

The judge sentenced Hannon to 18 months, suspended for two years. He said: “Your offending, in contrast to Holmes, was over a short period time and it was an isolated event in what otherwise has been a positive life.”
