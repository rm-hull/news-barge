More than 1,800 young people have highlighted their concerns about crime and safety North Yorkshire, calling for more police patrols and better support for victims.

The findings came from the York and North Yorkshire Youth Commission’s Big Conversation, a youth-led consultation involving people aged 10 to 25.

Young people raised concerns about gender-based violence, online safety, drug use, county lines activity and their relationships with the police and fire services.

The recommendations included more visible patrols in public areas, schools and the night-time economy, along with more inclusive youth spaces and better support for young crime victims.

Jo Coles, York and North Yorkshire’s deputy mayor for policing, fire, and crime, said: "A huge thanks to everyone who has taken part in this work over the last year.

"The scale of engagement from over 1,800 young people across our county is incredible and key to understanding how Mayor David Skaith and I can help make them feel safer in their communities.

"The recommendations the commission have made as a result of all that input will now form a vital part of the way in which I will hold the police and fire service to account as well as the commissioning work that takes place to support victims and to prevent crime.

"I will continue to work closely with them and other partners across York and North Yorkshire to implement their suggestions wherever possible."

Youth Commission members presented their findings and peer-led research directly to police, fire and local authority leaders at a recent York conference.

Workshops focused on turning the young people’s recommendations into action.

Kaytea Budd-Brophy, senior manager at Leaders Unlocked, said: "It’s inspiring to hear the Youth Commission present the findings from their peer-led research directly to local decision-makers and partners from across the region.

"The insights shared highlighted the issues that matter most to young people and showcased the passion and commitment of the Commission's members.

"We hope the recommendations spark meaningful action and strengthen collaboration between organisations and young people to create positive change across York and North Yorkshire.

"Congratulations to everyone involved for their hard work and dedication."

The Youth Commission’s final report, containing detailed findings and recommendations, is due later this year.
