A farmer has been banned from driving after failing to provide a blood specimen for analysis.

Ashley Caton, 49, initially co-operated with police after being involved in a collision with another car in Threshfield, and being suspected of drink-driving, heard Skipton Magistrates Court.

But, he became "belligerent" at Airedale hospital, Steeton, when he was asked for a blood sample, the court heard on Friday.

The court heard Caton had provided a positive roadside breath test for police of 80 microgrammes of alcohol per 100 millilitres of breath, the legal limit is 35.

But at the hospital, where he was taken following the collision, Caton refused to provide a sample for the evidential test, asking to see his lawyer.

## What was said in court

Caton, who admitted failing to provide a specimen for analysis on May 14, was of previous good character, the court heard, and had displayed symptoms of a possible concussion following the collision.

He had not intended to drink alcohol on May 13, the court was told in mitigation.

He had gone to a cricket match, but that had been cancelled, the court heard.

## The crash

Police had gone to Threshfield after receiving a call from a member of the public at 10.55pm about a road traffic collision.

Police stopped Caton's Land Rover as it was being driven away from the site of the collision and Caton admitted he had been the driver when there had been the collision.

## 'Will now be reliant on his wife to drive him'

Caton's advocate in court on Friday, Mr Iqbal, said the loss of his licence would have "huge ramifications" on his work as a farmer.

"He is a fourth generation farmer and a lot of his land is away from his home; he won't be able to drive now and will be reliant on his wife to drive him if there is any emergencies with the animals," he said.

Mr Iqbal said Caton had been driving along a road he knew well when he swerved to avoid something in the road and collided with another vehicle.

He had been violently sick at the hospital, which could have been down to his head injury.

Caton apologised for what he had done, he had let himself and his family down, and there was now a stain in his character, added Mr Iqbal.

Caton, of Otterburn, Bell Busk, near Skipton, was banned from driving for 17 months, the minimum ban for the offence.

He was also fined £960 and ordered to pay a surcharge of £384 and costs of £85.

If he completes a drink-driver rehabilitation course, his ban will be reduced by 17 weeks.
