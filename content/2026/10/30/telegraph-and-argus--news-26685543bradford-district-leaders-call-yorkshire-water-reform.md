Political leaders are demanding urgent reforms at Yorkshire Water amid growing public outrage and environmental concerns.

Their calls come after the sudden resignation of chief executive Nicola Shaw, which followed the company receiving a one-star environmental rating from regulators.

Ms Shaw said: “Although there remains a huge amount of work to do, I am extremely proud of what our colleagues have achieved, putting the business on a stronger footing for delivery across all aspects of its work.

She added: "This now feels like the right time to begin an orderly transition to the company’s next chapter."

The downgrade has reignited criticism of Yorkshire Water’s handling of sewage pollution, water leaks, customer service and rising bills.

## Change of leadership comes during critical period

Yorkshire Water is one of four companies in England to receive a one-star rating this year.

The Environment Agency recorded 260 pollution incidents linked to the company in 2025, marking a more than 70 per cent increase on the previous year and the highest figure since 2011.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/16833546.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nicola Shaw - Yorkshire Water CEO *(Image: Press Association)*

A joint report by industry regulators said environmental performance across the sector had fallen to its lowest level on record.

The report highlighted persistent issues with pollution incidents and permit breaches.

## MP requests 'urgent meeting' with new CEO

Keighley and Ilkley MP Robbie Moore (Conservatives) said: "After yesterday's news that Yorkshire Water has been given the lowest possible environmental rating by the Environment Agency of one star, Nicola Shaw's resignation as chief executive could not have come quick enough.

"Figures published by the Environment Agency showed that Yorkshire Water was responsible for a shocking 260 pollution incidents in 2025, a more than 70 per cent rise compared to the year before and the highest amount since 2011.

"This has been completely unacceptable."

Mr Moore said he had already requested an urgent meeting with incoming chief executive Nicola Medalova, who is set to take up the role on January 1, 2027.

He said: "I receive countless complaints about Yorkshire Water's terrible communication with its customers, its mismanagement of roadworks, shoddy workmanship and this, coupled with their ongoing sewage pollution incidents and continuous water leaks going unfixed for months, change at top has been long needed.

"I have therefore already requested an urgent meeting with the new CEO designate Nicola Medalova to understand how she plans to improve Yorkshire Water's performance at speed.

"The status quo cannot continue."

## Mayor calls for 'a radical new approach'

West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin echoed calls for reform and said the company must use the leadership change to rebuild public trust.

Ms Brabin said: "As Yorkshire Water enters a period of new leadership, I'll be holding the board to account on how it plans to improve and rebuild the evaporated confidence of the public.

"That has to include a radical new approach to transparency and investment.

"The company simply cannot continue to siphon off millions of pounds in hidden bonuses and shareholder payments, all while dumping sewage at record levels and asking the public to pick up the cost of repairing infrastructure.

"I will also be asking the Prime Minister and the Environment Secretary for the public control we need to truly hold Yorkshire Water to account, including having a real say over its operations and the ability to impose and direct fines, so we can support those communities hit hardest by pollution."

She also called for stronger regulatory powers and criticised the privatisation of the water industry.

Ms Brabin said: "More broadly, the privatisation of water has been a disaster.

"Regardless of your politics, it simply does not make sense to privatise an industry where there is no competition and where the public are a captive market."

## MP wants 'complete change of direction'

Bradford South MP Judith Cummins (Labour) said that leadership change alone would not be enough to address deep-rooted problems.

Ms Cummins said: "A new face at the top cannot wash away years of Yorkshire Water failing to put customers first.

"Yorkshire Water needs more than a change of leadership.

"It needs a complete change of direction.

"There must be a wholesale shift in culture from the top that finally prioritises customers.

"Only then can the company begin to restore public trust."
