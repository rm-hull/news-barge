A drug pusher who dealt in cocaine and cannabis to raise money to be able to move away from Holme Wood is beginning three-and-a-half years in prison.

Bradford Crown Court heard how, following threats, violence, and reprisals against him, Tommy Haigh was operating at street level and supplying drugs to users.

Prosecutor Daniel Ingham told the court how Haigh, now 24, was spotted by police officers in the passenger seat of a car on the Holme Wood estate on the morning of August 19, 2025.

After the car was signalled to stop Haigh got out and walked away "at pace," discarding a cannabis joint as he did so.

Officers struggled to handcuff him and search him under the Misuse of Drugs Act.

During the incident Haigh said: "I will break your jaw" and "When I get out of handcuffs, you're dead."

When other officers arrived, the car was searched.

Tammy Glen was found inside the car.

An iPhone found in the car contained messages consistent with drug-dealing between June 9 and August 16.

They showed Haigh had been directing Glen for cannabis order drop-offs.

Haigh was searched and denied having illicit drugs in his possession.

At the police station he was found with six bags of cannabis.

Glen was found with eight zip lock bags of cannabis.

Further cannabis was found at their home address.

The value of the drugs seized was £485.

In interviews both answered "no comment."

On January 5 this year police stopped a VW Golf on the A650 near Morley.

Inside was Haigh with another person.

An iPhone was seized that contained messages related to a drugs line offering "rapid drop-offs."

Police searched a property on Lansdale Court in Holme Wood and seized Class B drugs, scales, and paraphernalia.

After a further warrant was issued officers discovered cocaine, cannabis, a machete, and £970 in cash.

Two iPhones were seized from Glen's address, and the numbers appeared to be operated by Haigh.

Messages asking for drugs were found on them.

In their interviews both Haigh and Glen gave "no comment" answers.

Mitigating for Haigh, of no fixed abode but formerly of Farway, Holme Wood, and who appeared via video link from HMP Leeds, Saf Salam said he was operating at street level supplying to users but not operating at the higher end of the chain.

He said there was no evidence of significant financial gain.

He told the court that there had been a long-standing history of intimidation following his mother's involvement in a murder trial, and there had been threats, violence, and reprisals against Haigh.

He said he had made "a serious and misguided decision" in attempting to escape the environment and that his principal motivation was to raise money to move away from the Holme Wood area.

Mr Salam said: "His offending is connected to his immediate circumstances. His family have relocated out of Bradford. He also intends to move away when released."

Mitigating for Glen, 33, of Rushmoor Road, Holme Wood, Bradford, barrister John Bottomley said she had been "living in fear" after a car had been rammed and there had been issues involving people using baseball bats.

He said she too was raising money "to get out of Holme Wood" and that messages showed Haigh telling her where to deliver cannabis.

The court heard that both Haigh and Glen were of previous good character.

Sentencing the pair, Mr Recorder Jeremy Hill-Baker said Haigh, having been stopped by police for trading in cannabis, which involved Glen, then went on to be the operator of a drug line that supplied both cannabis and cocaine.

He jailed Haigh for three-and-a-half years.

Recorder Hill-Baker described Glen as "very much under the influence of Mr Haigh" and handed her an 18-month community order.

He told her: "You see what happens to people who get themselves involved in serious drug-dealing. Don't let yourself fall into that trap."

Recorder Hill-Baker ordered the forfeiture and destruction of the drugs, paraphernalia, phones, and machete, and the forfeiture of the cash seized.
