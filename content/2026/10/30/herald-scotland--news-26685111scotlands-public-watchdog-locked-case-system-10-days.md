Scotland’s public sector ombudsman has been unable to access its case management system for the past 10 days due to a technical glitch, The Herald can reveal.

The Scottish Public Services Ombudsman (SPSO) is the Scotland's independent final stage for complaints about public services.

Established under the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman Act 2002, it investigates complaints from members of the public who claim to have suffered injustice or hardship because a public body delivered a poor service or made administrative errors.

As a result of the technical issue, the watchdog said it is currently unable to progress existing cases or provide updates on them.

According to the SPSO, the problem arose after the supplier of its case management system experienced an issue with one of their separate clients, which triggered a precautionary shutdown of their system for all clients.

Despite the technical issue, the SPSO said none of its data has been accessed or lost.

A spokesperson for the SPSO told The Herald: “The supplier of our case management system experienced an issue with one of their separate clients triggering a precautionary shutdown of their system for all clients.

"Following forensic investigation, the supplier has confirmed that no SPSO data has been accessed or lost.

"We continue to accept new complaints but have been unable to progress existing ones.

"The system is currently in the process of being restored and we will be able to progress all complaints again shortly.”

![Paul McFadden, Scottish Public Services Ombudsman,](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21646120.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Paul McFadden took on his role as Scottish Public Services Ombudsman in October 2025 *(Image: Scottish Public Services Ombudsman)*

Commenting on the technical glitch, Scottish Conservative MSP Alexander Burnett, who serves as the Shadow Cabinet Secretary for Local Government, told The Herald: “It is deeply concerning that systems for those managing complaints against SNP-run public services have been down for over ten days.

“Serious questions must be answered as to why that is the case. We know there have been a record level of complaints made about the state of public services in Scotland, so have the systems buckled under this pressure?

“SNP ministers must work closely with the SPSO and ensure they have the resources to get back up and running as quickly as possible and that people receive timely responses to their complaints.”

News of the technical issue comes in the wake of a new report which shows that complaints to the SPSO have increased by almost one third in a single year.

The SPSO released its annual accounts on Thursday, October 29, showing 6,588 complaints were made in the 2025-26 financial year, up 31% compared to the previous year.

The number of whistleblower complaints also increased by a third, rising to 177 in the most recent year.

Paul McFadden, took on his role as Scottish Public Services Ombudsman in October 2025, said: “SPSO has responded effectively to the challenges placed on our service, delivering record levels of complaints decisions and demonstrating significant resilience and agility to continue to change and evolve how we deliver our service.

“However, the upward trend in complaints is continuing through 2026-27 and brings risks to delivery and growing wait times.

“As these pressures increase, we continue to evolve how we work, focusing our resources and new technologies where there is the greatest need, the most significant public interest and the highest risk of injustice.

“In the midst of these pressures, we must not lose sight of SPSO’s wider value to public service improvement with insights from complaints having an essential contribution to make to prevention and seeking to retain the trust and confidence of the public.”

The report warned of the need to increase resources available to the SPSO as a result of the increasing demand.

“We continue to adapt our approach through improved triage and impact-based proportionality, further efficiency measures, and exploration of digital and AI enabled tools.

“However, without additional investigative resource, the sustainability of our financial and operating models and delivery of our statutory functions is at risk, with serious implications for access to justice and public confidence.”
