A Teesside family has traded mainland life for one of the UK’s most remote island communities.

Katy Phenix, 33, partner Josh Moore, 35, and their children, Hetty and Teddy, left Redcar this summer for Fair Isle, a Scottish island 24 miles south of Shetland Mainland.

Fair Isle is Britain’s most geographically isolated inhabited island, with just 50 residents and more birds than people.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644981.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Katy Phenix and daughter Hetty *(Image: Katy Phenix / SWNS)*

Katy said: "We had a really large mortgage in England but now pay peanuts in comparison to live in one of the most beautiful areas of the UK.

"It feels like we've won the lottery. I don't think you could get a bigger change of lifestyle, but as a family we are all loving it."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644976.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Fair Isle *(Image: Katy Phenix / SWNS)*

Katy spotted an Instagram advert seeking new residents in March and applied “on a whim”.

At the time, both Katy and Josh were working 12-hour days and felt they were passing ships in the night.

After a rigorous application process involving the National Trust for Scotland and island residents, they were offered the move in May.

By July, they had sold their three-bedroom new-build in Redcar and spent £8,000 to move north, taking their four cats and three dogs with them.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644986.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Teddy, 11 *(Image: Katy Phenix / SWNS)*

Their new home, rented from the National Trust for Scotland, is a five-bedroom house with 17 acres of land and five outbuildings.

Life on Fair Isle is a world apart from the mainland, with everyday tasks requiring careful planning.

Ordering a takeaway is one of the family's biggest challenges. Getting a curry from an Indian restaurant in Shetland involves a taxi ride to the ferry, a sea crossing and reheating it on arrival, with the whole process taking around seven hours.

Bad weather can also cut off the island, cancelling ferries and flights and delaying deliveries.

But the family says the lifestyle is worth every challenge.

Early years practitioner Katy now works around four hours a day, Monday to Friday, while electrician Josh is the island’s handyman.

Katy said: "He's been out today fixing someone's washing machine. He's re-plumbed our house, he's done everything."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644978.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Josh with sheep on Fair Isle *(Image: Katy Phenix / SWNS)*

They have also embraced crofting - taking on 30 sheep and a ram with help from the former tenants.

Teddy, 11, helps out with his shepherd’s hook, while Hetty, six, looks after the hens and sheep.

The children quickly settled in, finding instant friends in other youngsters on the island.

There are seven children on Fair Isle: five at the school, one in nursery and a baby. Two teenagers also attend high school on the mainland.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644977.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hetty, 6, helping out with the sheep *(Image: Katy Phenix / SWNS)*

Despite its size, Fair Isle has a shop, post office, nurse and seasonal pub at the bird observatory.

In an emergency, a helicopter from Shetland can reach the island in around 15 minutes.

Katy said: "I think the massive misconception is that it's lonely and there's nothing to do. That couldn't be further from the truth.

"Our lives haven't actually changed that much. We've got something on everyday."

She has even taken up pilates and yoga, and is now learning traditional Fair Isle knitting.

The biggest change is the time the family now spends together.

Katy said: "I remember Teddy once said 'oh mum you're so much nicer in the school holidays.' It was a throwaway comment; he didn't mean anything by it.

"It stuck with me. I never wanted to be that mum but it was kind of the way life pans out and you accidentally get there.

"This has just given us that time to breathe and be as a family. We do everything together and that's made it totally worth it."
