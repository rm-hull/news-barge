With more than 20 years’ experience of using different web hosting companies, I know the best features to look for. The home of your business or personal project, your website (along with social media) is a showcase of who you are and what you offer, so you want it to run smoothly. On your end, you want a dashboard and control panel that makes it easy to implement updates, ideally without the need for advanced technical knowledge.

To help you choose the best web hosting platform for your needs, I’ve set up, used and then compared the best options on the market, to bring you my honest opinion on each, highlighting any issues or benefits worth knowing about before committing to one. I was interested in customer support, cost, features and what knowledge level, if any, was needed to use them effectively.

After extensive research, I’ve rounded up the best web hosting services to sign up for in 2026.

## The best web hosting services in 2026 are:

* **Best web hosting overall –** Hostinger: From £1.49 per month, Hostinger.com
* **Best budget web hosting –** Ionos: From £1 per month, Ionos.co.uk
* **Best website builder:** Wix**–** From £9 per month, Wix.com
* **Best UK-based web hosting –** Krystal: From £7 per month, Krystal.io

## How I tested

I’ve used a lot of different web hosting services over the years. I spent many days with each of the entries below. During this testing time, I set up a basic website to test out the functionality of each hosting provider and do some tweaking to check out ease of use.

In addition, I evaluated other features included with each web hosting service and whether you’d need to pay extra to get everything you need. I also looked at how simple it was to get started and how intuitive the web host was, ensuring you don’t need expert technical knowledge. You can see my full testing criteria at the bottom of this article.

Hostinger is incredibly versatile, which is a huge strong point. Its web hosting plans will suit most people, although you can get a VPS or cloud-based server if you prefer the ultimate wealth of options. Whatever you go for, the custom hPanel is your new home. It’s fairly intuitive if not the most attractive of interfaces. It guides you through things you may want to set up starting out before making it simple to tap on your new website.

As someone who used to code websites from scratch, I appreciated being able to upload files via the hPanel’s file manager, but it’s also good to see that Hostinger has plenty of website design options. You can easily set up a WordPress-based site via some useful prompts which include relevant plugins. There’s also an AI-based website builder, which makes it simple to create a website simply by typing in your vision and seeing what it comes up with.

![Hostinger testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/10/19/Hostinger-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

All plans come with a free domain for the first year, along with SSL, regular backups, and free email mailboxes. There are ecommerce features bundled in as well, courtesy of WooCommerce, so it’s a good option for anyone looking to launch an online store. That all means you don’t really need to pay for much extra on top of the standard plan you sign up to, which is always appealing.

There’s a comprehensive knowledge base for any queries you may have, along with an AI bot to assist with most solutions. Hostinger only really lacks the option of phone support, but that isn’t a dealbreaker.

It all makes Hostinger an appealing option for web hosting. I’d happily recommend Hostinger to any friend looking for web hosting, as I know it’ll grow with them. Whether you want to launch a small personal project or you have bigger business aims, Hostinger has a solution available.

Ionos has been around in some form for a long time and its expertise is pretty strong. Even better, it’s possible to sign up to a cheap plan – with options starting from just over £1 per month. You’ll need to tie yourself in for a while, but – as with most web hosting – you may want a site for a long time, so it makes sense.

I found the control panel to be fairly clear and focused on a few more visual elements than some others. Besides standard hosting, you can sign up for a VPS or cloud server as well as a dedicated solution, so there are plenty of ways to upgrade.

Rather than AI-based solutions, Ionos focuses on making it easy to set up a site using WordPress. It only takes a few minutes and there are plenty of ways to develop your site further. Alternatively, you can use its file manager to upload the site directly. There’s also free SSL, a malware scanner, regular backups, and DDoS protection as standard.

![Ionos testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/11/36/Ionos-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Ionos goes above and beyond with its support. There’s a good knowledge base, and a support ticket system – but the highlight is being able to seek direct advice over the phone. That’s perfect for inexperienced users who need some guidance. In a similar vein, Ionos is keen to hook you up with a design team if you’d prefer someone else to develop your site.

Ionos keeps its prices low by offering a handful of things for an additional fee, so you’ll want to keep an eye on what you actually need. On the other hand, that means you never pay for anything you don’t require, so if you’re shrewd, it can work out great value.

Wix is the simplest way you could possibly create a website. I’m convinced anyone who has ever used a PC could figure out how to make a good-looking website with it. It has a drag-and-drop website builder which is as simple as it sounds. Just move an element around on the screen and Wix does all the coding in the background.

It also offers some powerful AI tools – so if you don’t want to drag things around for yourself, Wix comes up with some templates that can be customised according to your needs, choice of colour scheme, and so on. You can make a site within a matter of minutes, although you’ll likely want to adjust things as you go along.

![Wix testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/11/51/Wix-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Wix is particularly good for businesses as it has many plugins and add-ons for booking systems, enquiries, and buying online directly. It takes out all the hard work and also deals with important factors like security, so you won’t have to worry about doing anything wrong. Its SEO tools and AI insights should help you be noticed, too.

The downside? Wix is powerful as a website builder but if you have any other plans, it’s very restrictive. It’s not conventional web hosting so you can’t upload your own website via a file manager and nor can you use it as a regular server. It’s also a little more expensive than other web host providers, although I found a deal landed in my inbox after signing up a day earlier.

I’ve used Krystal Hosting a lot over the years and I really appreciate its more ethical approach. It’s powered 100 per cent by renewable energy and one tree is planted somewhere in the world every month that you’re signed up for. It’s also based in the UK which is nice to see. Somewhat understandably, it costs a little more than other options – but if you’re considering sustainability, it’s worth spending the extra.

![Krystal testing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/23/11/51/Krystal-testing.png?quality=75&width=1216&auto=webp)

Its control panel is fairly logical, sticking to a more traditional layout – which is still welcoming enough. For the standard web hosting, your first port of call for a quick design is to set up WordPress. That doesn’t take long at all and Krystal suggests the best plugins to get you started. It’s a good opening gambit. If you want to use Krystal’s dedicated website builder, you’ll need to sign up to the specific plan that uses it.

I’ve found WordPress is more than good enough throughout. You can also upload files via the file manager and there’s free migration between web hosts if you’d prefer someone else does the hard work for you.

Support is via submitting an online ‘ticket’, live chat or a knowledge base and these options work well. Ultimately, Krystal’s biggest selling point is its environmental impact – but that’s an important thing to consider with any kind of technology.

## The verdict: Best web hosting service 2026

All of the above web hosting services made the cut because they have great features. My personal favourite is Hostinger because it covers so many areas of web hosting. I love how easy it is to create a website using the AI function, and I also appreciate being able to set up a VPS server (for a price) and have even more control over my online presence. Ionos offers a very similar service, although I did find it slightly less clear when it came to costs and I generally preferred Hostinger’s control panel.

Wix is almost my guilty pleasure as it’s pricier than regular web hosting, but it’s so convenient and makes building a website a lot of fun. Its results are generally some of the prettiest website builder-based sites around too. For a small business, its bookings plugin is a huge bonus. And then there’s Krystal, which makes the ethical part of me very happy to see. It isn’t the cheapest but I know it’s doing its best to offset my contribution to environmental issues, plus it offers speedy websites.

With all things considered, you can’t go wrong with any of the options listed here. Hostinger is my go-to for most people. But if you can afford more, Wix is great if you have limited technical knowledge and want a good-looking site with minimal effort, while the other two are also strong options too.

## How the best web hosting services were tested

When testing these web hosting services, I considered a range of different things – from ease of use and price to how long you need to commit to a plan, as well as extra features and speed. With each web hosting service, I looked at the following:

* **Ease of use:** I noted how easy it was to do anything. This could be as simple as checking you can cancel a plan quickly or how easy it is to set up a website. I focused on how simple it is to create a website without coding knowledge, and I also looked at how easy it is to upload files.

* **Features:** I looked at all the features each web hosting provider offers. I checked the essentials were there as well as if any extras were worth highlighting. I compared them to competitors to check these were the features you genuinely need. I focused on making sure these features mean that you have a full solution rather than needing to search for other tools.

* **Value for money:** I looked at how much each provider costs and whether that represents good value for money. That didn’t mean I focused on the cheapest plan, but the ones that represent good value for their price. I looked at anything that might incur extra charges too.

* **Performance:** I checked how fast each web hosting service is. I looked at how quickly I could set up a server and how speedy it was for me to move between control panel pages. I also looked at how quickly a finished website loaded, too.
