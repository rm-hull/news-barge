Almost 150 e-bikes and e-scooters were seized by police during a week-long blitz on their illegal use.

The crackdown saw officers working across the country, recovering a total of 99 e-bikes and 43 e-scooters during the week beginning Monday, 19 October.

Officers made seizures in most divisions across Scotland, with the highest number recorded in Greater Glasgow, where 54 vehicles were recovered.

All of the vehicles will be destroyed.

Police Scotland has already seen a notable increase in seizures, with more than 1,000 of these vehicles, including 561 e-bikes and 441 e-scooters, recovered in 2026 up to Friday, 16 October.

Superintendent Andy Barclay, deputy head of Road Policing, said: "We know this is a growing issue across Scotland and this week of action shows how seriously we are taking it.

"We are very clear that illegally modified e-bikes are dangerous and not suitable for use on public roads. They pose a risk to their riders, emergency service personnel and other road users, particularly pedestrians.

"We also want to make sure that people are aware of the legislation and their personal responsibilities when using an e-bike or e-scooter.

"Please be mindful of where you can and can't use vehicles of this type, and take responsibility for your own actions and the safety of other road users."

E-bikes and e-scooters are becoming increasingly common in towns and cities across Scotland.

Most are used appropriately and in line with legal guidelines. However, some vehicles are modified to exceed speed and power limits, which can reclassify them under motorcycle legislation.

E-bikes can legally be used on public roads if they meet specific requirements, including that the motor must not be able to propel the bike when it is travelling at more than 15.5mph and must have a maximum power output of 250 watts.

Vehicles that exceed these limits are classed as motor vehicles and are subject to the Road Traffic Act. This means the rider must hold a full UK driving licence, have insurance and wear an approved helmet. Those who fail to comply with these legal requirements could face enforcement action under the Road Traffic Act.
