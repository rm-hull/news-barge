A Darlington mum feared her tot was dying when she 'stopped breathing for nearly three minutes' after eating an Aldi Halloween sweet.

Marie Johns bought her two-year-old daughter Mia Parkinson a Halloween-themed cat bag from Aldi on the North Road Retail Park in Darlington earlier this month, which contained multiple packets of jelly sweets.

The 45-year-old said she gave Mia one of the sweets while they were decorating the house for Halloween on Saturday, October 17, when she suddenly began wheezing and 'turned purple'.

Horrified, the mum-of-two realised her daughter was choking and began delivering back blows to help clear Mia's airways.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650956.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Marie Johns, 45, and daughter Mia Parkinson, 2, who choked on an Aldi sweet. *(Image: KENNEDY NEWS AND MEDIA)*

But the tot was still gasping for air when Marie then performed stomach thrusts, before frantically dialling 999.

The singer and songwriter said Mia was unable to breathe for an agonising two minutes and 49 seconds before the skull sweet came flying out of her mouth.

Little Mia was rushed to hospital by ambulance and was checked over by staff before being discharged later that day when doctors found no internal damage.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650952.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mia in the ambulance. *(Image: KENNEDY NEWS AND MEDIA)*

Aldi said the £2.49 Trick or Treat Sweet Bag features a label on the outer felt bag warning it's not suitable for children under three years old 'due to small parts, which may pose a choking hazard and loops which may pose a strangulation hazard'.

However photos show this warning is not on the individual packets of sweets inside, which the manufacturer of the sweets confirmed

Manufacturer BIP Candy & Toys UK Limited said the age warning is displayed on the outer retail packaging of the product, which is required by law, and that covers the whole product and its contents.

Despite the outer packaging carrying an age restriction warning, Marie claims the individual 25g sweet packets inside the cat-themed bag don't and therefore shouldn't be sold to children.

The former teaching assistant claims Mia would've died without her assistance, and is warning other parents about the potential dangers of sweet Halloween treats.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650958.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mia Parkinson *(Image: KENNEDY NEWS AND MEDIA)*

Marie said: "We'd been painting and decorating some Halloween stuff we'd bought. We were decorating the house.

"I bought these jelly sweets as a treat for Halloween, they were skulls and teeth. I wasn't concerned when I opened them up.

"I gave Mia a sweetie, she ate one and that was okay. Then I noticed she was wheezing, I span around and she was purple.

"I started doing back thrusts. I rang 999, she couldn't breathe.

"It took me two minutes and 49 seconds to dislodge the sweet. I was whacking her back and doing stomach thrusts when it came flying out.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650955.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The bags of Halloween sweets. *(Image: KENNEDY NEWS AND MEDIA)*

"She nearly lost consciousness, it was horrendous. Two seconds more, she'd have blacked out I think."

Luckily, the 1.5cm-sized sweet came flying out of Mia's throat before she was rushed to Darlington Memorial Hospital when paramedics found fluids on her lungs.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650953.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The skull sweet that Mia choked on. *(Image: KENNEDY NEWS AND MEDIA)*

Now, Marie has banned the Aldi sweets from her home, and believes the treats shouldn't be given to any child.

Marie said: "It was very scary. Luckily I saved her life, otherwise she probably wouldn't be here now.

"At the time, I was crying my eyes out, screaming. I wouldn't wish it on any parent ever.

"She could've died, I think I only had a minute before she stopped breathing all together.

"As soon as we got home, I put the sweets in the bin. I don't think they should be sold.

"I don't think they should be given to any kid. I think a lot of parents would be buying these for Halloween, which makes me worried.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650957.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Maria and daughter Mia. *(Image: KENNEDY NEWS AND MEDIA)*

"I'm very lucky. Now she's not having any sweeties. She'll only be getting chocolate buttons for Halloween because they melt.

"She'll never be allowed sweets again after that traumatic experience."

Aldi said the £2.49 Trick or Treat Sweet Bag features a label on the outer felt bag warning it's not suitable for children under three years old 'due to small parts, which may pose a choking hazard and loops which may pose a strangulation hazard'.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651046.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aldi at the Darlington North Road retail park. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

However, photos show this warning is not on the individual packets of sweets inside.

A BIP Candy & Toys UK Ltd spokesperson said: "We are very concerned to hear about this reported incident and relieved to understand that the child was discharged from hospital.

"Our thoughts are with the child and her family following what must have been a very distressing experience.

"The product tag does include several warnings stating that the product is not suitable for children under three years of age and contains small parts which may pose a choking hazard.

"The individual sweet packets do not carry a separate age warning.
