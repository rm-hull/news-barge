Dying before justice is exactly what every victim of abuse will fear.

To live your life battling trauma for decades only to die before you are able to see your attacker forced to answer for their actions is unacceptable.

But for some of those who endured the abuse between the 1960s to the 1990s, time has run out. The story of Paul Overton is a tragic reminder of that.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21622220.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Natalie and Kirsti Overton from Newcastle with a photo of their dad Paul Overton *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Paul was one of hundreds who were attacked at the Yarm “hell hole”, and it took him decades to open up to his family about his ordeal. He turned to alcohol to help mask the pain, and his daughter Kirsti believes the abuse significantly contributed to his death.

This story, this family, and their trauma is a stark reminder of the reality facing many victims. Everyday they are fighting for answers and someone to be held accountable.

This is why it is paramount that Operation Magnolia, which has been ongoing since 2014 but has not seen one former officer charged, is urgently prioritised by Cleveland Police.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21104327.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HMP Kirklevington in Yarm *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Almost 1,000 victims have reported their abuse to police and at least 21 suspects have been interviewed, but we are still no closure to any punishment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20906498.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HMP Kirklevington *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Seven officers are already dead. Victims are dead. And with every day, it feels like the likelihood of ever getting justice is slipping away.

Paul may be gone, but his family are continuing to fight on his behalf so he can rest in peace.

It’s time to act now, before it is too late for another family. Don’t let another victim die without justice.
