Andy Burnham has blamed Britain’s migrant crisis on Brexit, arguing that leaving the EU had left the UK with “weaker control”.

The prime minister said on Monday that he wants to see Britain rejoin the European Union in his lifetime, after he reopened the Brexit debate earlier this year.

Mr Burnham ruled out a return for the UK to EU freedom of movement rules without a referendum, after he signalled he could include the move in Labour’s next election manifesto.

But the prime minister said he did not think “things have worked out as people said they would at the time”, highlighting immigration and the small boats crisis.

“I don't think things have worked out as people said they would at the time, particularly when it comes to immigration,” Mr Burnham said.

“I think we've got weaker control now. The reason the small boats crisis has arisen is because we can't return people, and people know we can't do that in the same way that we used to be able to do.”

More than 200,000 migrants have arrived in the UK after crossing the Channel since data began on January 1 2018.

The record for the largest number of migrants to arrive in the UK in a calendar year is 45,774, which was set in 2022.

The number rapidly increased over the Covid-19 pandemic, but in the first six months of 2026, the number of people arriving in the UK by small boat fell 41 per cent and the number of boats 47 per cent compared to the previous year.

The prime minister admitted on Saturday he is open to reintroducing freedom of movement in an intervention that has boosted hopes that the UK could look to rejoin the bloc after the next general election.

It comes just weeks after Mr Burnham said the UK could go “all the way” and rejoin the bloc, but insisted the British public will “be the boss” of its own future.

But he added on Monday: “I’m not proposing any changes in this parliament. I’m not going to move away from the red lines that were in the Labour manifesto.”

Asked whether he would allow the UK to join the EU freedom of movement scheme without a referendum, he said: “No, I mean I wasn’t saying that yesterday. I was asked about how things have changed in the 10 years since Brexit, and the truth of the matter is we’ve lost immigration control in the way that we once had.”

And asked whether he would like to see the UK rejoin the EU, he said: “In my lifetime I would like to see that happen but I am not going to move ahead of the public, I am not going to dictate.
