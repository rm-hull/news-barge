A UK construction firm has ceased trading after collapsing into administration.

Torsion Projects Limited, based in Leeds, specialised in the delivery of care home schemes across the UK.

It was incorporated in 2015 and employed 54 members of staff, having been a part of the wider Torsion Group.

Now, the company has collapsed, with administrators now having been appointed.

Mounting financial difficulties have now led to the collapse, with all sites handed back to developers or funders before the formal appointment of administrators.

James Clark and Rick Harrison of Interpath were appointed as joint administrators.

Mr Clark, managing director at Interpath, said: "The financial challenges that confronted Torsion Projects were insurmountable and, despite efforts by the Directors to identify fresh investment, there was little option but to begin the winding down of the business.

"We have a specialist team deployed to support staff through this difficult period."

Torsion Projects faced a sustained period of cashflow pressure following the termination of multiple contracts, which left the company unable to cover its costs.

All 54 employees have been made redundant.

The business is part of the wider Torsion Group.

Administrators were appointed in July to Torsion Construction Limited, which provided contractor services to Torsion Developments — a business operating in the purpose-built student accommodation sector (PBSA).

Torsion Developments Limited and Torsion Care Limited are not in administration.

## UK companies that have closed or entered administration/liquidation in 2026

It has been a tough year for the UK high street, with several retailers entering administration or liquidation and others announcing widespread store closures.

Major high street brands Claire's and Quiz have been forced to close all their remaining stores after falling into administration.

UK fashion retailer Leading Labels also closed its remaining 15 stores after falling into liquidation.

Wynsors World of Shoes is shutting 17 stores across the UK, with closing down sales already underway at several affected branches.

UK entertainment retailer Zavvi has ceased trading after nearly 20 years, having collapsed into administration for a second time.

Australian stationery retailer Typo is also set to pull out of the UK in 2027, closing all its stores and ending online trading.

Food companies have also been affected, with frozen food company Quality Bites, which is stocked in more than 1,000 independent supermarkets, closing down, too.

Crisp company Glens of Antrim Potatoes, which supplied potatoes to retailers across the UK and Ireland, has also fell into administration after 54 years.

Its crisps were sold in Tesco, Sainsbury’s, and other major supermarkets.
