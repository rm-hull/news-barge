A County Durham farmer who has transformed part of her family farm into an outdoor classroom for children has been shortlisted for a national award.

Jemma Clark, founder of Bowlees Education Farm near Barnard Castle, is shortlisted for the Community Impact Award at the UK Women's Business Awards.

Jemma runs the farm with her husband, John, a full-time farrier, and their children, drawing on 15 years in education, specialising in special needs and challenging behaviour, to teach youngsters about farming and rural life.

After being given the opportunity to convert a barn at the farm into an educational classroom, Jemma turned her attention to teaching from the family farm.

The outdoor classroom now welcomes schools, youth organisations and disadvantaged groups, with visits offered free to schools.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21632407.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jemma Clark, founder of Bowlees Education Farm near Barnard Castle

Free sessions are also provided for children from care homes and disadvantaged families.

Children can collect eggs, care for hens, feed lambs and cattle, groom donkeys and ponies, and learn how hay and straw are produced.

They can also learn about animal welfare and where their food comes from, giving youngsters first-hand experience of subjects they might otherwise only encounter in a classroom.

Jemma said: "We are incredibly honoured and thrilled to be recognised as a finalist at The UK Women's Business Awards.

"Being named a finalist means so much to me and to Bowlees Education Farm.

"I'm proud of the learning opportunities we offer and the positive impact we aim to make through our sessions.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21632406.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jemma Clark, founder of Bowlees Education Farm near Barnard Castle

"This recognition is a special milestone for the farm and encourages us to keep building on that work."

Jemma's work has now secured her a place among finalists from across the country at the UK Women's Business Awards.

A spokesperson for the awards said: "The standard of entries this year has been incredibly high.
