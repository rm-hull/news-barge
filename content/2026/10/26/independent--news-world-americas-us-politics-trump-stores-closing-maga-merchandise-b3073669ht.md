Rob Cortis has faith in President Donald Trump - and in the MAGA-themed store he owns in Michigan.

“There’s still the chance that Trump always pulls rabbits out of his hat,” he said. “Is he gonna run in 2028?”

Cortis runs the Trump Unity Bridge Shop in the Detroit suburb of Livonia. And, unlike the several Trump-themed stores shutting their doors across the country, he has no plans to close. “It's always busier during the elections, and then it slows down in between,” he told *The Independent.* “It’s getting very busy now during the election again.”

Trump merchandise accounts for about 80 percent of the store’s sales, Cortis said, with hats remaining a strong seller. “MAGA hats are still moving pretty good…ICE hats are selling,” he said.

But other retailers are not so optimistic and find themselves confronting a challenging political reality: the president is an unpopular lame duck with no reelection campaign on the horizon to fuel sales.

![Over the past year, at least four Trump stores nationwide have shut their doors, including locations in Pennsylvania and Tennessee, according to NPR](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/09/09/630428402...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Roughly 20 stores nationwide sell Trump merchandise, according to Ron Solomon, who heads the online retailer MAGA Mall. But over the past year, at least four stores nationwide have shut closed up shop, including locations in Pennsylvania and Tennessee, according to NPR.

“He's no longer a viable business model for entrepreneurs because he has peaked,” Wes Alker, a Trump supporter who frequented the Pennsylvania store, told the outlet.

The closures come amid a broader sales slump for MAGA merchandise. Retailers selling hats, T-shirts and other items bearing the president’s likeness and slogans have seen sales fall sharply, *The Wall Street Journal* reported last month. Some blamed economic conditions, with inflation driving up prices even for official Trump merchandise.

Trump’s approval rating has fallen to record lows in recent months amid voter frustration over the cost of living and the ongoing Iran war, which has no clear end in sight.

On January 5, the Trump Store in Bensalem, Pennsylvania, announced it was shuttering its brick-and-mortar operations after six years in business. “Come in tomorrow until January 31st for store-wide savings,” the store said in a Facebook post.

Owner Mike Domanico told CBS News that he opted to shut it down because he’s semi-retired and focusing on other business ventures.

“The store has kind of run its course,” he said. “You know, it's been six years and the elections are over. Trump's not gonna be in another election, even though he'll be part of it.”

David Russell, who frequented the store, added: “Most of the stuff they sell was to promote Trump. He's already in this last term. You can't do much more for him than he's already gotten.”

A Trump superstore outside Knoxville, Tennessee, also closed this summer. Owner Bill Hays told NPR that some of the president’s policies, including DOGE’s spending cuts, had hurt business. He also noted that some customers were choosing to have their purchases bagged rather than putting on their new Trump apparel in the store.

“People were not wearing their colors, so to speak, like they did before the [2024] election," Hays said, noting that sales had fallen to $20,000 a month from a high of $200,000.

“After the inauguration, I think a lot of people felt that their job was done,” he said.
