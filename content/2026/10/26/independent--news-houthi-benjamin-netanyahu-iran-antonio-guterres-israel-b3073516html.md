The Saudi-led coalition battling Yemen's Houthi rebels said Monday that it has degraded their ability to strike targets inside the kingdom after destroying tunnels used to store ballistic missiles and missile launchers.

In Israel, the country's defense minister strongly denied that the military has clashed with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu over his reported instructions for an “action” against Iran ahead of national elections on Tuesday.

Here is the latest news from the Middle East on Monday. Read our full coverage here.

Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed Houthi missile launchers, storage facilities

The spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition fighting the Iran-backed Houthis said an operation in Houthi-controlled territory in Yemen destroyed five tunnels used for storing ballistic missiles, four ballistic missile launchers and another missile storage facility.

Spokesperson Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said in a post on X that the operation also aimed to cut the Houthis' supply lines between areas under their control and targeted rebel fortifications and military equipment along the front lines inside Yemen.

Al-Malki said the coalition would continue to use “deterrent operational measures” to protect against the Houthis' “brutal attacks” inside the kingdom and in Yemen.

Explosions heard in Yemen's capital and on its Red Sea coast

Explosions were heard Monday in Yemen’s Houthi-held capital, Sanaa, and along the country’s Red Sea coast. Houthi-controlled media said airstrikes hit a solar power system and a water well in the Sanaa district but provided no evidence.

Residents in western Sanaa reported hearing at least four explosions, but it was not immediately clear what had been hit. A woman living near a Houthi base said a loud explosion shattered windows in her home and nearby houses. She spoke to The Associated Press on condition of anonymity for fear of reprisals.

In the port city of Mokha, recently seized by the Houthis, resident Khaled Adib said a rocket struck a four-story residential building. At least 10 other houses and Mokha General Hospital were damaged, according to Adib and another resident, Ahmed Omran.

— By Ahmed al-Haj in Aden, Yemen

Houthis say they fired missiles at Yemeni government forces

The Houthi rebels claimed Monday that they struck military gatherings and weapons depots belonging to Saudi-backed government forces in Aden and Lahj provinces.

The Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement that the rebels fired ballistic missiles at the sites, inflicting losses. The claims could not be independently verified.

The Houthis continue to target government forces and their allies inside Yemen. The Yemeni government accused the rebels late Sunday of targeting displacement camps on the outskirts of the southern port city of Aden. The government didn’t provide evidence to back this claim.

Israel’s defense minister denies clash with the army over Iran

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz on Monday dismissed reports regarding rifts between the political leadership and the Israeli military over Iran as “completely baseless and false.”

In a post on X, Katz also said Israel had no intention to strike Iran before Tuesday’s national elections but that it would respond “with great force” if Iran attacks first.

Israeli Channel 12 reported Sunday evening that Netanyahu and Israel's military chief of staff clashed over the Israeli prime minister's instruction to carry out an “action” that the army believes could quickly reignite the conflict with Iran. The report did not specify the nature of the action. Netanyahu’s office later described the report as “fake news.”

Pakistan's military chief meets with Iran's political and military leaders

Pakistan’s army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman and Turkish intelligence chief İbrahim Kalın are meeting with Iran’s top military official, Maj. Gen. Mohsen Rezaei, in Tehran, a Pakistani senator said Monday.

Senator Sehar Kamran said in a post on X that constructive engagement by Iran could help address threats to Saudi security and reduce risks to vital energy chokepoints, from Bab al-Mandab to the Strait of Hormuz, as oil prices continue to rise.

Munir returned to Tehran for talks with Iran’s top political and military leaders after visiting the capital last week as part of efforts to revive U.S.-Iran talks and discuss ongoing attacks on Saudi Arabia by Iran-backed Houthi rebels.

Guterres expresses concern over Israeli settler violence

U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres said Monday that he was “deeply alarmed” by surging Israeli settler violence and settlement expansion in the West Bank.

Speaking in Cairo on one of his final trips as U.N. chief, Guterres said Israeli restrictions on humanitarian aid to Gaza must be lifted and obstacles to the Security Council’s peace plan must be removed with “the specter of ethnic cleansing looming ever larger.”

He said lasting peace in the region depends on Palestinians’ right to self-determination, and that a two-state solution, with Jerusalem as the capital of both, is the only viable path.

Guterres also called for restoring stability around Iran and the Strait of Hormuz and for ensuring safety around Yemen’s Bab al-Mandeb Strait.

Egypt's parliament approves help to Saudi Arabia

Egyptian lawmakers on Monday approved a request by President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi for support to Saudi Arabia to help the kingdom counter Houthi attacks.

The decision comes amid growing public concern over the prospect of Egypt becoming involved in Yemen’s long-running conflict.

Egypt’s highest security body previously pledged to provide “all necessary and possible forms of support” to Saudi Arabia to defend its “security and stability,” protect its resources and territorial integrity, and safeguard Islamic holy sites.
