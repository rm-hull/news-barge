A North East dad who left his baby permanently disabled after shaking him in a cocaine-fuelled rage has had his jail term increased at the Court of Appeal.

Christopher Yates, 30, drank 11 pints of Foster’s lager before he shook his six-month-old son with the force of a “high-velocity car accident”, causing severe brain injury, blindness, and a likely inability to learn to walk.

Yates admitted causing a child to suffer serious physical harm and was jailed for four years at Newcastle Crown Court in July, but the Solicitor General referred the sentence to the Court of Appeal.

On Thursday, judges increased the term to six years.

Increasing the sentence, Lady Chief Justice Baroness Carr, sitting with Lord Justice Jeremy Baker and Mr Justice Wall, said: “The sentence imposed by the judge was not only lenient but unduly so.”

Gemma White, for the Solicitor General, said in written submissions that Yates, from Gateshead near Newcastle, shook the baby five times, yelling “shut the f\*\*\* up”, at around 2am on October 5 last year, but did not contact the ambulance until 8.15am, claiming the child had fallen from his chair.

The baby had been taken from his mother into foster care, but Yates gained custody three weeks before the incident despite his heavy drinking and “red flags” raised by his family, the court heard.

Family members were concerned Yates was not being supported by professionals and became easily frustrated with the child, was not clothing him properly, and said his flat was a “mess” with dirty nappies and empty alcohol cans.

When challenged about why he was drinking heavily when he worked so hard to get custody of the baby, Yates told a relative “I can do whatever I want”, the court was told.

Ms White told the court in London that the sentencing judge had not properly reflected the level of harm caused to the baby and had reduced the sentence too much to reflect Yates’s ADHD, autism, depressive disorder and traumatic upbringing.

She said: “There was very significant use of force against a six-month-old baby which resulted in catastrophic injury.”

Rachel Hedworth, for Yates, said the sentencing judge had rightly reduced the sentence to reflect his mental disorder.

She said Yates had discovered he was a father through a paternity test after the baby’s birth but had “put himself in the ring” to be the sole carer of the baby.

She said the baby was put in his sole care despite the fact he was “self-medicating” with alcohol during the assessment process and authorities being aware he had been doing this “for over half his life”.

Ms Hedworth said that despite family highlighting these “red flags”, he received “very little” assistance from professionals.

She said: “Had his behaviour and his care of that child been monitored at a much earlier stage, then perhaps with some more advice and guidance he may have been helped.”
