A journey that began with heartbreaking loss has brought two North East friends together with a shared mission to improve physical and mental wellbeing across the North East.

Kristie Graham and Natalie Wiley from South Tyneside have turned tragedy into a force for good, founding a community interest company (CIC) focused on helping others improve their health, emotional wellbeing, and overall quality of life.

Ms Graham, a gym owner and fitness coach, lost her fiancé Robbie Robson in 2022.

Mr Robson was killed by a colleague while working on a gas rig off the coast of Qatar, leaving Ms Graham to raise their two children—14-year-old Willow and five-year-old Sefa.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21639598.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kristie Graham and Natalie Wiley at the Fitfam gym, South Shields *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Graham said: "I couldn’t take in what had happened.

"The grief hit me so hard, and at the same time I was trying to hold things together for our children, Willow and Sefa, who’d just lost their dad.

"Fitness has always been a big part of my life, so I threw myself into training.

"It gave me some structure and kept me going, but on its own it couldn’t touch the grief.

"I was exhausted, I was losing weight, and I was still sinking. I’d had therapy through the NHS, but it just wasn’t enough.

"It’s a service that is already struggling to cope with demand and long waiting lists.

"I felt completely lost until I started working with Natalie."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21639600.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kristie Graham and Natalie Wiley at the Fitfam gym, South Shields *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Wiley, a hypnotherapist, massage therapist, and 9D breathwork facilitator, also lives in South Shields and had known Mr Robson through her brother.

When Ms Graham reached out for support, Ms Wiley was determined to help.

She said: "Grief can crush people and it was clear that Kristie was trying her best, but needed some additional support.

"We started by working on her breathing and helping her to relax.

"Exercise is brilliant for both body and mind, but when someone is carrying something as heavy as grief, it often needs to go hand in hand with support for what’s happening underneath.

"We look at the root of what someone is going through and work on that.

"Whether it’s grief, stress, or other challenges, it’s about understanding what’s really going on and helping them through it."

As Ms Graham began to recover, the two friends started talking about how they could combine their skills to help others.

Ms Wiley began attending Fitfam, the gym Ms Graham runs with her twin sister Carla, and together they developed the idea for a new CIC.

Fitfam x Realign was launched in April.

The company combines physical movement, emotional wellbeing, and nervous system support to help individuals, young people, and workplaces thrive.

It offers a range of services including hypnotherapy, breathwork, emotional regulation, and customised fitness programmes.

Ms Wiley said: "Primarily, we want to help people, but there are also major benefits for businesses in supporting their employees to become healthier and happier.

"We can help businesses reduce sickness absence, improve productivity and create a happier working environment.

"We work with people of all ages and tailor our support to what is likely to benefit them most.

"Young people, for example, are still dealing with the longer-term effects of Covid, with many experiencing anxiety and other challenges several years on."

For Ms Graham, the project is also a tribute to Mr Robson’s memory.

She said: "Robbie would light up a room when he walked in, and all of this has been inspired by him.

"Losing Robbie was incomprehensible, and it had a devastating impact on so many people.

"He inspired me to help others, and he inspired my sister Carla to pursue her dream of becoming a firefighter.

"He loved life, and he’d love knowing that good, positive things have come from him.

"His mantra was always ‘don’t just exist’."

Fitfam x Realign also gives businesses the chance to "pay it forward" by funding treatment and support for individuals who might not otherwise be able to access it.

Ms Graham and Ms Wiley recently spoke at a South Tyneside Pledge Networking event, where they explained how their combined skills are helping them deliver more holistic support to people in the area.

The South Tyneside Pledge encourages businesses to spend in, recruit from, and support the borough, building stronger connections between the private and voluntary sectors.

Councillor Elliot Curry, lead member for economic growth, regeneration, and investment, said: "I think people in the borough will remember the heartbreaking story of what happened to Robbie.

"It shocked the whole community and I can’t imagine the impact it must have had on those who loved and knew him best.

"However, it is incredibly inspiring to see something so positive come from such a tragedy.

"I wish Kristie and Natalie every success and I’m sure anyone who engages with their support will benefit greatly from their care and expertise."
