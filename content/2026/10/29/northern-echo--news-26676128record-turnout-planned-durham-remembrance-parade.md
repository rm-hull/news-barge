Durham City is set for its biggest Remembrance parade yet, with more Armed Forces representatives taking part than ever before.

Durham’s annual Remembrance Parade will be held on Sunday, November 8, starting with a service at the cathedral at 10.15am, followed by a two-minute silence at 11am and wreath-laying at 11.10am.

Cllr Elizabeth Scott, armed forces champion for the City of Durham Parish Council, said: "The City of Durham Parish Council is once again honoured to organise and host the Durham City Remembrance Parade on behalf of our community.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21639652.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A previous Remembrance Parade in Durham *(Image: Supplied)*

"Remembrance is an important moment in our civic life – a time when we gather in unity and gratitude to honour the service and the sacrifice of our Armed Forces, past and present."

The parade will feature a record number of Armed Forces representatives, including detachments from The Rifles, the Royal Lancers, the Light Dragoons and 607 (County of Durham) Squadron, Royal Air Force.

County Durham Lord-Lieutenant Brigadier Michael Butterwick CBE DL will take the salute in the Market Place as the parade passes through the city centre.

The parade will set off from the cathedral at 11.30am, pass through Durham Market Place and finish around noon.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21639653.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A previous Remembrance Parade in Durham *(Image: Supplied)*

The event will also feature all five Foot Guards regiments through the Guards Training Company at the Infantry Training Centre.

Cllr Scott said: "The City of Durham Parish Council is honoured to welcome representatives of all five Foot Guards regiments through Guards Training Company of the Infantry Training Centre, alongside a detachment from 1st Battalion Scots Guards."

The five regiments are the Grenadier Guards, Coldstream Guards, Scots Guards, Irish Guards and Welsh Guards.

They serve in ceremonial roles and on operations around the world.

Three bands will provide the parade’s music, including Esh & Bearpark Brass Band.

The parish council urged people to line the route and remember those who served and sacrificed.

Later, Redhills Durham Miners Hall will host a Remembrance concert called Under Earth, Under Arms, We Remember.

The 3pm to 4.30pm concert will feature the Durham Miners Association Brass Band and Lirica, honouring mining communities’ resilience and sacrifice during conflict.

Details and tickets are available on the Redhills event website.
