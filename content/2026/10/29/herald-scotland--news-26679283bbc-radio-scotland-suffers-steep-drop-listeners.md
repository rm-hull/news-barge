BBC Radio Scotland’s weekly audience has shrunk by more than 90,000.

RAJAR figures released on Thursday show BBC Radio Scotland had a weekly reach of 753,000 listeners between July and September this year. This was down from 844,000 the previous quarter - a fall of 91,000, or 10.8%.

Its share of radio listening was down slightly from 5.7% to 5.3%.

The station previously hit a peak of 921,000 at the start of 2026. But the BBC does not issue individual ratings for each show, making it difficult to say if the rise was due to Victoria Easton-Riley, the newly arrived head of audio and events.

Ms Easton-Riley, who took up her post in August 2025, hailed the 921,000 figure at the time as **“hugely positive”**.

She has faced a backlash from listeners for axing well-known shows and hosts including **Shereen Nanjiani**,** Billy Sloan**, and Michelle McManus, while bringing in presenters from the commercial sector.

An internal analysis seen subsequently by The Herald suggested much of the rise was due to sport  - particularly football - and Michelle McManus’s show. **McManus alone was thought to have added more than 50,000 listeners in six months.**

Responding to the analysis published today, a BBC Radio Scotland spokesperson said: "This latest RAJAR report shows that in an increasingly competitive market, BBC Radio Scotland remains the biggest Scottish station, and has seen a quarter-on-quarter rise in the average amount of time listeners spend with us.

"We're delighted that audiences continue to come to us in significant numbers for their favourite shows from agenda-setting news and sports programmes, to entertainment shows such as Breaking the News through to our night-time music offering which includes Travelling Folk, Another Country with Ricky Ross, and Roddy Hart's Mix Tape."

In other quarterly results from RAJAR, the official body in charge of measuring radio audiences in the UK:

The number of Clyde 1 listeners dropped from 687,000 to 648,000 (-5.7%)

Forth 1 listeners went from 339,000 to 378,000 (+11.5%)

Smooth Radio Scotland remained broadly steady, down from 324,000 to 323,000 (-0.3%). Figures also show 87% of the adult population tune into radio each week while 40% say they listen to at least one podcast every month.

UK-wide, Radio 4 went from 8.9 million listeners to 8.7 million (-2%)

Radio 5 Live was also down, from 5.5 million to 5.2 million (-5.5%)

Times Radio was up from 564,000 to 660,000 (+17%).

The sharp drop in listeners comes at a difficult time for BBC Scotland staff.

Last week it announced its dedicated linear TV channel would close next spring.

Director Hayley Valentine said 90% of the channel’s content would still be available on the iPlayer, BBC One and BBC Two.

More than 70 jobs are to go at BBC Scotland, including ten as a result of the channel closure and the loss of the News at Seven programme.

Speaking on BBC Scotland’s Scotcast, **Ms Valentine said there were more cuts to come.**

“We're in a really difficult financial position at the moment and we need to resolve it,” she told Scotcast presenter Martin Geissler.

“There is a £500m savings target and BBC Scotland has to play a part in that,” she added. “We are making some difficult choices across the board in terms of how we contribute to that. But I'm trying to do it in a way that keeps as much of the content as possible and gets it out to the audience in the places that they are increasingly consuming it in.”
