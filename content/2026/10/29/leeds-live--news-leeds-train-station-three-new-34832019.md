# Leeds train station to get three new entrances in '£200m gateway to West Yorkshire' pledge

## The upgrades are set to be delivered by 2030 after government promised more funding

Leeds train station is set to receive three new entrances and nearly 40% more ticket barriers, the Government has announced, alongside a further £200 million in funding for the project.

The additional investment was revealed in the Budget on Wednesday, bringing the total funding for the transformation of the West Yorkshire station to more than £300 million. The upgrades, which are due to be completed by 2030, will also include the construction of a new central overbridge and expanded concourse space, according to the Department for Transport.

It comes following major disruption at the station since 2022 as part of a £46.1m upgrade to the main entrance area.

The station currently welcomes more than 27 million passengers annually, with 80% of West Yorkshire services either beginning or terminating there. Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander said: "Leeds station is the gateway to West Yorkshire, and this extra £200 million investment will turn it into a station this city can be proud of: modern, welcoming and built for the people who rely on it every single day."

She added: "The great cities of the North deserve better transport and that's exactly what we're delivering.

"We're paving the way for Northern Powerhouse Rail, forging stronger connections across the region and backing the infrastructure that creates jobs, builds homes and puts passengers first as we work to set up Great British Railways."

Leeds ranks as the UK's third busiest station outside of London, with passenger numbers forecast to rise by almost 12 million by 2043, the department confirmed. Tracy Brabin, Mayor of West Yorkshire, commented: "Leeds station is the beating heart of our region's transport network, connecting millions of people to jobs, education and opportunity every year – and it's only going to get busier."
