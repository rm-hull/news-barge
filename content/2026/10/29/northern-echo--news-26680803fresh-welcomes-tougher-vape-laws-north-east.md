The North East tobacco control agency Fresh has welcomed tough new laws aimed at stopping children getting hold of vapes and other nicotine products.

Fresh said the restrictions would help prevent under-18s from accessing nicotine and vaping items, including nicotine pouches and nicotine-free vapes.

The changes, introduced under the Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026, came into effect today, Thursday October 29, and strengthen existing rules by extending age-of-sale protections to cover all non-medicinal nicotine products.

Ailsa Rutter OBE, director of Fresh and Balance, said: "Tobacco smoking is our single biggest preventable killer, and it is vital we encourage adults who smoke to switch completely to vaping.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645570.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ailsa Rutter, Director of Fresh and Balance *(Image: Supplied)*

"However, it is important the law is tightened up to reduce the appeal and the access of children to nicotine products.

"We support these steps to address the promotion and marketing of vapes that have made some products too appealing to children, while also preparing for the world-leading Smokefree Generation law which will make it illegal to sell lethal tobacco products to anyone born after 1 January 2009, enable millions of children to grow up without the harm of lethal tobacco products."

The new laws make it illegal for adults to buy or attempt to buy vaping or nicotine products on behalf of someone under 18, and ban promotional giveaways, substantial promotional discounts, and vending-machine sales of such items.

Trading Standards will lead retail enforcement, backed by up to £30 million in annual funding until 2028–29.

This includes up to £10 million per year for Trading Standards and the recruitment of 120 specialist apprentices to help tackle illicit and underage tobacco and vape sales.

The Government’s 10 Year Health Plan for England includes a target of achieving a smoke-free UK, and the new measures are part of the phased rollout of the Tobacco and Vapes Act.

Further steps will follow, including a ban on tobacco sales to anyone born on or after January 1, 2009, which takes effect from January 1, 2027.

A comprehensive advertising and sponsorship ban is expected to come into force from June 1, 2027.

Other proposals include restrictions on product packaging, flavours, displays, and the introduction of more smoke-free and vape-free public areas.

Consultations on these additional measures have concluded, with further announcements expected in due course.

A spokesperson for Fresh said the North East remains committed to its Declaration for a Smokefree Future, backed by all local authorities, hospital trusts, and the North East and North Cumbria NHS Integrated Care Board.

The declaration aims to end tobacco-related death and disease and improve health and economic outcomes in the region’s most disadvantaged communities.

Fresh continues to offer support for smokers looking to quit, directing people to advice, resources, and local stop smoking services through FreshQuit.co.uk.

Public health guidance remains clear that vaping is significantly less harmful than smoking and can support adults to quit tobacco, but it is not risk-free and should not be used by children or non-smokers.

The new restrictions do not prevent public authorities, including stop smoking services, from providing vapes or nicotine products to support smoking cessation.

A limited exemption also applies to vape vending machines in adult inpatient areas of mental health hospitals in England and Wales.

The Tobacco and Vapes Act forms a central part of the UK’s efforts to prevent long-term sickness and promote better health through prevention rather than treatment.
