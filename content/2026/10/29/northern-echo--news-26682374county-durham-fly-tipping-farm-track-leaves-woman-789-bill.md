A woman has been left with a bill of more than £700 after her waste was found dumped on a farm track.

Household rubbish was discovered in Burnhope, County Durham, in January, prompting an investigation by Durham County Council officers.

Evidence at the scene led them to a 34-year-old Rebecca Wilson, from Park Road in South Moor, Stanley.

The council hand-delivered a letter to Wilson asking her to make contact but received no response.

In February, officers then issued a formal section 108 notice, also hand-delivered, requiring Wilson to attend an interview later that month.

She did not attend and did not attempt to contact officers.

Ian Hoult, neighbourhood protection manager at Durham County Council, said: "This case is a prime example of why it’s important to take extra care when asking someone to get rid of your waste.

"Make sure they are a legitimate business, ask where they are taking it to, and make sure to get a receipt.

"If it’s your waste, it is your responsibility."

Wilson later pleaded guilty to the offence.

At Newton Aycliffe Magistrates’ Court, she accepted full responsibility for the fly-tipping.

The court heard that she had instructed someone to deal with the waste, and had acted in good faith, with no intention of dumping it unlawfully.

It was also made clear that she did not receive any letters as she had moved sometime in February.

Wilson was fined £120 and ordered to pay a victim surcharge of £48 and costs of £621, bringing the total to £789.
