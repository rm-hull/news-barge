A Washington dog grooming business could expand to offer dog daycare services under new plans.

The application to Sunderland City Council’s planning department is for 3-4 Heworth Road in the Concord area.

There is an existing dog grooming business at the site and new plans from applicant Alicia Thorp are seeking permission to offer extra services and accommodate more customers.

This includes diversifying the site to create a new dog day care facility within the existing commercial premises, whilst retaining the established dog grooming business.

As part of the plans, the existing dog grooming facilities would be relocated into a smaller room within the building, with the main floorspace used to accommodate the proposed dog daycare use.

Planning documents state the development would “accommodate a maximum of 6–7 additional dogs for daycare, alongside the existing 3–4 daily grooming clients”.

An accompanying planning statement notes the hours of operation are limited to daytime hours and “do not extend into early morning and later evening periods where residents can reasonably expect peace and quiet”.

It was also noted that there would be two collection periods which would help manage parking and vehicle movements around the site.

![3 4 Heworth Road In The Concord Area Of Washington Credit LDRS (2)](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644999.jpg?type=mds-article-620)3 4 Heworth Road In The Concord Area Of Washington Credit LDRS (2)

The planning statement goes on: “The introduction of a dog daycare facility alongside the existing dog grooming business will diversify the range of available services within the Concord District Centre, reducing the need for residents to travel further afield for dog daycare services and improving local access to this service.

“The proposal supports the vitality and viability of the district centre, in accordance with Policy VC1, by meeting local needs, expanding the range of services available within the centre, and remaining compatible with the established commercial uses within the surrounding area.”
