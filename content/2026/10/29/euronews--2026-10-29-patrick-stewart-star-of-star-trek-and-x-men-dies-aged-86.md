## The 86 year old acclaimed Shakespearean British actor died in Los Angeles on Wednesday following "a brief and poignant journey" with Alzheimer's disease.

The legendary British actor Patrick Stewart has died. He was 86.

Best known to millions around the world for portraying Professor Charles Xavier in the *X-Men* films and Captain Jean-Luc Picard in the sci-fi television series*Star Trek: The Next Generation* and subsequent films, Stewart spent much of his early career on stage.

A post on the actor's official Instagram and X accounts said: "We announce with great sorrow the passing of Sir Patrick Stewart yesterday at his home in Los Angeles, in the loving care of his beloved wife Sunny, following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's Disease and Lewy Body Dementia."

"He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter, and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to us all."

## A career spanning seven decades

Stewart's career on stage and screen spanned nearly seven decades. It began in Mirfield, northern England, before he trained as an actor and later joined the Royal Shakespeare Company.

His theatrical roles ranged from Claudius to Macbeth. He also frequently performed a one-man adaptation of *A Christmas Carol* and appeared alongside his longtime friend Ian McKellen in West End and Broadway productions of Harold Pinter's*No Man's Land* and Samuel Beckett's*Waiting for Godot*.

Stewart received numerous honours throughout his career, including a Grammy and two Laurence Olivier Awards, as well as Emmy, Golden Globe and Tony nominations. He was also honoured with a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2010.

As Captain Jean-Luc Picard, Stewart appeared throughout the seven-season run of *Star Trek: The Next Generation* between 1987 and 1994. He also starred in four Star Trek films before returning to the role on the small screen in*Star Trek: Picard* from 2020 to 2023.

## Tributes paid to the internationally acclaimed actor

In a post on Instagram, actor George Takei, who also starred in the Star Trek franchise, wrote: "As Captain Jean-Luc Picard, Patrick Stewart took the Starship Enterprise to places it had never gone before, and he inspired a whole next generation of star-gazers who looked longingly ahead to a brighter future for all humanity."

"Our hearts are heavy, indeed. Rest in the heavens, Captain."

Actor Hugh Jackman, who starred alongside Stewart in the X-Men films, described him as a "tremendous friend and man". In a post on Instagram, he wrote: "We lost one of the greats of stage and screen today."

"I learned so much from him. I will miss you, Patrick Stewart, all of my days."

Marvel Studios also paid tribute to Stewart, saying he brought Charles Xavier to life with "extraordinary grace, intelligence, and humanity, defining the character for generations of fans, from X-Men to Logan to Avengers: Doomsday."

The post continued: "He was a singular talent, a generous collaborator, and a true gentleman. Our hearts go out to his wife Sunny, his children, his friends, and the countless fans around the world whose lives he touched with his art."

William Shatner, who starred alongside Stewart in *Star Trek Generations*, also paid tribute to him on X. He wrote "Captain Picard stood for the best of us, and so did the man who played him. I was filled with affection and admiration for him and his work."

The UK's Defence Secretary Wes Streeting also honoured Stewart. In a post on X, he wrote: "He never forgot his roots, nor did he shy away from using his voice to stand up for others, particularly those affected by domestic violence."

He was also a prominent campaigner against domestic violence, speaking publicly about his childhood experiences of witnessing his father abuse his mother and supporting organisations working with survivors.

Stewart is survived by his wife, Sunny Ozell, and his two adult children from his first marriage, Daniel and Sophie Stewart.
