Police were spotted on a Sunderland street today as part of an "ongoing investigation" as a cordon was put up near a restaurant.

Northumbria Police and Northern Gas Networks attended the Everest Mountain Daisy, on Hylton Road, in the Millfield area on Thursday, October 29.

The force confirmed that planned activity was being carried out on Hylton Road, and a cordon was put up. The road has since reopened to traffic.

The investigation led to the discovery of a gas leak at a property.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646485.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police were carrying out planned activity at a premises on Hylton Road in the Millfield area of Sunderland when a gas leak was discovered at the property. *(Image: MC Turtle)*

A spokesman for Northumbria Police said: "As part of an ongoing investigation, we are carrying out planned activity at a premises on Hylton Road in the Millfield area of Sunderland.

"There is no risk to the wider public and anyone with concerns is encouraged to speak with an officer on duty.

"Members of the public can also get in touch by sending a direct message on social media, or by using live chat and report forms on the Force’s website.

"Those unable to make contact in these ways can call 101."

Dozens of police officers could be seen inside the restaurant, while a number of police vehicles were stationed outside.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646486.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: MC Turtle)*

While police were at the scene, a gas leak was identified.

Northern Gas Networks said it was contacted by police at 8am.

A spokeswoman for Northern Gas Networks said: "Our teams responded to a call at 8am this morning (29 Oct) from the Police regarding a potential gas escape at a property they were attending in the Hylton Road area of Sunderland."

"Gas supplies were secured and made safe so the property owner can organise a repair to the pipe and their gas supply can be safely restored."

The cordon was put in place as part of the police investigation and not because of the gas leak.

Anyone who smells gas or suspects a carbon monoxide leak is urged to call the National Gas Emergency Service on 0800 111 999.
