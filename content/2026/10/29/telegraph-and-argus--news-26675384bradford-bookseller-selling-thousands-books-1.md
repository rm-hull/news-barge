A Bradford man has built a bookselling business that now reaches readers across the country.

The 42-year-old runs Stephen White Books and recently launched UK Bookfest, a series of bargain-book events that have drawn thousands of visitors.

The T&A visited his latest festival at Albion Mills, which runs until Saturday, as he prepares to expand the concept to more cities across the UK.

For Stephen White, of Ravenscliffe, it is the latest chapter in a journey that began more than 20 years ago, selling books online from his university bedroom.

"I've always loved books," he said.

"I was the kid who got told off for reading too much rather than the kid who needed to be forced to read constantly."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638759.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stephen White outside the Albion Mills site *(Image: T&A)*

## From Uni bedroom to Bradford warehouse

What began as a way to earn some extra money soon became something much bigger.

"All I really needed to do was make enough money to match my student loan so I could afford to live," he said.

"That was my only objective. I wasn't looking to make a massive business."

As the business grew, so did the piles of books.

His stock first filled his room, then one garage, then another, before expanding into industrial units and eventually a large base at Albion Mills.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638761.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UK Bookfest at Albion Mills *(Image: T&A)*

"Basically it's just got more and more and more out of hand every year," he said.

"I took on a space in Albion Mills right in the heart of Bradford.

"It felt massive. Like, 'I'll never fill this space, what am I going to do with it?' Then a few years later, it's like, 'I can't move'."

The business kept growing, and Mr White eventually doubled the space he rented at the mill.

## Expansion from online to in-person sales

For years, the company sold exclusively online, but processing the growing stock was taking more and more time.

A suggestion from a friend in America prompted him to consider selling in person.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638763.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UK Bookfest at Albion Mills *(Image: T&A)*

## 40,000 children's books at £1 each

When a temporary unit became available at Albion Mills, he took a chance. That gamble led to UK Bookfest.

The first event, held in February 2025, was devoted entirely to children's books.

"I think we had 40,000 children's books, they were all £1 each and it was just sort of, let's just see what happens," he said.

"The response was really encouraging."

The success spurred him on to expand the idea.

An adults’ and children’s sale followed on Knowles Lane in October 2025 and was another hit.

The events are built around cheap books sold in volume.

Children’s books stay at £1, while adult titles start at £3 and get cheaper as the festival continues, until shoppers can fill a bag for £10 on the final day.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638766.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UK Bookfest at Albion Mills *(Image: T&A)*

"Some of these books are worth £20, £30 or £40, but I don't have the time to release that stock, so you can come and find a bargain," he said.

## Bradford is 'heart of the operation'

Following the success of the Bradford events, Mr White has taken the format on the road.

Bookfest visited Birmingham earlier this year, with future events planned for Newcastle and Liverpool.

Despite the national expansion, he insists Bradford remains at the centre of everything.

"I just started selling books in my bedroom. I've been able to give a dozen jobs to people. There's money going into the local economy," he said.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638764.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UK Bookfest at Albion Mills *(Image: T&A)*

"Bradford is still the heart of the operation."

He believes Bradford’s appetite for books has been underestimated.

"People were like, 'Oh, you couldn't run a business selling books to people in Bradford. No one reads in Bradford.'

"But there's been a real interest and it's been really great to see.

"It's not just the one demographic you'd expect. We get people from across the board, old, young, every background. It's really nice."

## Focus on low prices

The business has also expanded from second-hand titles into new books.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21638768.jpg?type=mds-article-620)UK Bookfest at Albion Mills *(Image: T&A)*

One weekend each month, part of the Albion Mills warehouse opens for a sale with every new book priced at £5.

He believes the low prices have struck a chord as household budgets remain under pressure.

"Everything's expensive, isn't it? Cost of living is up. Books are expensive," he said.

"I don't begrudge the price tag of £10 or £11 on a new paperback, but everyone's bottom line is tight."

He added: "We're giving a life to stuff that wouldn't get another life anyway.

"I love seeing the kids' books go back out to another kid who's going to appreciate them."

Looking ahead, Mr White hopes to take UK Bookfest even further, with ambitions to expand into London, Wales and other parts of the country.
