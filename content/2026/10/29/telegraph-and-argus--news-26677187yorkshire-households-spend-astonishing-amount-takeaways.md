HOUSEHOLDS in Yorkshire and the Humber are spending an average of £260 a year on takeaways.

That’s the second-highest total in England, according to new figures released by the Office for National Statistics.

The data, analysed by kitchen specialists Prestige, shows that households in the region spend around £5 a week on takeaway meals eaten at home.

Only London recorded a higher figure, with households in the capital spending about £322 a year.

Olivia Disley, from Prestige, said: "It's easy to see why takeaways appeal when you've had a long day, especially if you're getting home late and don't fancy spending another hour preparing dinner.

"But those convenient meals can become a regular expense without you really noticing, particularly if ordering food becomes part of your weekly routine."

Households in the North West and South East spent the least on takeaways, at £198 per year.

The England average stood at £229, meaning Yorkshire households spent around 14 per cent more than the national figure.

Ms Disley believes a well-organised kitchen can make home cooking easier and more appealing.

She said: "A well-organised kitchen can make whipping up a quick meal far less stressful, which may help make cooking feel like the easier choice."
