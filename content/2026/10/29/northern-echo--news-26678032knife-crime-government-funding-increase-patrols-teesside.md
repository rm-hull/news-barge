Extra government funding will boost efforts to tackle knife crime across Teesside, with more police patrols for hotspot areas.

The money from the Government’s Knife Crime Concentration Fund will increase Cleveland Police patrols in five areas of Stockton and Middlesbrough.

In Stockton, the investment will mean more patrols in the town centre and Newtown, as well as helping to fund measures including additional CCTV cameras.

Police say high-visibility patrols deter criminals, disrupt offences and reassure communities worst hit by knife crime.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20322046.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cleveland Police and Crime Commissioner Matt Storey *(Image: Supplied)*

Assistant Chief Constable Mark Hall said: "We know the devastating impact knife crime has on our communities, and we are determined to do everything possible to prevent it and arrest those we believe to be responsible.

"Whilst enforcement plays a huge part in tackling knife crime, it’s crucial we continue to work with our partners to address underlying causes to achieve lasting changes in our communities.

"We welcome this investment, which will help us focus activity in hotspot areas which are impacted the most."

The funding will also support long-term efforts to prevent knife crime by bringing councils, community groups, businesses and other organisations together to tackle violence at its source.

It builds on Cleveland Unit for the Reduction of Violence (CURV) initiatives, including targeted deterrence and direct work with people most at risk of violent crime.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20831296.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Detective Chief Inspector Chris Pringle, knife crime lead for Cleveland Police with Head of CURV (Cleveland Unit for the Reduction of Violence) John Holden *(Image: Cleveland Police)*

Cleveland Police and Crime Commissioner Matt Storey said CURV, which operates under his office, will manage services paid for by the funding.

He said: "I welcome the additional funding and plans to target very specific areas of Cleveland to drive down the number of people carrying and using knives.

"Evidence shows that focusing prevention and enforcement on small, hyper-targeted areas is the most effective way to tackle this problem.

"However, enforcement alone will never be enough.

"We need to get upstream of the issue, find out why it’s happening, tackle the root causes and provide viable alternatives so young people don’t get caught up in a mindless – and deadly – spiral of violence and criminality."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20456522.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cleveland Police and Crime Commissioner Matt Storey *(Image: Supplied)*

The investment comes as the wider North East steps up its fight against knife crime.

In September 2025, The Northern Echo relaunched its multi-agency Knife Crime Taskforce, bringing together about 30 political, police, community and charity leaders.

The taskforce called for knife-crime education in schools, changes to legislation and sentencing, and public access to mapped life-saving bleed cabinets through a 999 call.

In September 2026, Middlesbrough and Redcar and Cleveland were chosen for new Young Futures Hubs, to give young people earlier access to mental health, employment, training and crime prevention support, while helping to prevent serious violence before problems escalate.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19992901.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anna Turley, Redcar MP, speaking about knife crime in the North East as part of the Northern Echo's anti-knife crime campaign. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Stockton councillor Norma Stephenson said: "We welcome this funding which will help police crackdown on knife crime.

"This investment will mean police patrols in Stockton town centre and Newtown will be increased, providing reassurance to our residents and businesses.

"It will also help fund measures such as additional CCTV cameras which will help keep our communities safe."

Councillor Ian Blades said the investment could save lives.

He said: "The consequences of knife crime are devastating for both victim and perpetrator, and for the wider community, and we all have a responsibility to do everything we can to tackle it.

"This important funding will support the vital ongoing work across Middlesbrough and Stockton, and it really does have the potential to save lives."

Police urged residents to keep reporting concerns and sharing information to help crack down on knife crime and violence in Teesside.
