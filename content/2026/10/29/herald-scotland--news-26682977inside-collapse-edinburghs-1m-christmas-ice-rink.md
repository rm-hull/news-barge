Organisers of Edinburgh’s Christmas festival have revealed its axed ice rink was costing more than £1m to create - after council chiefs suggested it was scrapped because it was not making them enough profit.

The producers who stage the seven-week event on behalf of the council have blamed a “domino effect” of problems for the collapse of plans to bring back the attraction, which has been a fixture of the festive season in the city for nearly 30 years.

The Herald can reveal that other sites across the city centre were explored as part of efforts to try to rescue the ice rink, however an alternative could not be confirmed in time.

The city council has admitted it was told in the summer that the ice rink would not be returning to George Street – even though its absence was only revealed this week by The Herald and the attraction was still being promoted on the city’s official Forever Edinburgh website.

The local authority has blamed the ice rink’s demise on “insufficient profitability”, despite the attraction drawing more than 46,000 skaters last winter.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21629890.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Edinburgh's Christmas festival ice rink has transformed part of George Street in recent years. *(Image: Edinburgh's Christmas/Andy Catlin)*

It has insisted it played no part in the decision to pull the plug and was not asked to bail out the ice rink, which has not had any funding support from the council for almost a decade.

Producers of the Christmas festival are expected to run the festival on a commercial basis under the terms of their contract with the council.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19244334.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Edinburgh's Christmas festival was said to be worth £166m to the city's economy last year. *(Image: Stephen Bridger/Getty images)*

However, the George Street ice rink is said to have become “unsustainable” because of the rising costs involved and the “substantial financial risk” which borne by the festival’s current organisers, Unique Events and Assembly.

The companies were said to be “actively sourcing” new funding and seeking an alternative location for the ice rink when it became clear that a return to George Street was “increasingly unviable”.

Unique and Assembly revealed plans to expand the Christmas festival to Bristo Square in September, when it was announced that it would host to pop-up Fringe venue Palais du Variéte throughout the event’s run.

The decision to drop the ice rink was revealed by The Herald as work started to install infrastructure for the Christmas festival, which is due to open on November 14.

Its absence has sparked calls from business leaders for a full review of the future funding of the Christmas festival, which will still use part of George Street for two pop-up bars.

City centre business group Essential Edinburgh, which revealed it had “lobbied hard” for the ice rink to be located on George Street, has suggested the current funding model for the festival “does not work”.

New research published in May valued the Christmas festival as being worth more than £166m to the economy.

The Christmas festival’s sites and attractions were said to have attracted an overall audience of 2.9 million visitors last winter.

A report for the city council on last year’s event revealed it had generated £776,846 for the local authority from the rental of key sites, including parking spaces, as well as fees for licensing, planning permissions, building warrants and traffic orders.

Unique Events and Assembly are in the final year of a joint contract to produce the Christmas and Hogmanay festivals, with the council having the option to extend the contract by a further two years. Although the contract is currently worth £812,456 annually, the funding is ringfenced to help meet the costs involved in putting on the four-day Hogmanay festival programme.

The ice rink has been one of the main features of Edinburgh’s festive season programme since it was first created for East Princes Street Gardens in 1998.

It was subsequently relocated to St Andrew Square Garden and then George Street, but has raised concerns from heritage groups including the Cockburn Association and Edinburgh World Heritage in recent years due to its impact on the New Town areas.

The city council has previously suggested it wants to see a greater spread of Christmas festival events "across the city centre and the wider city".

The Unique-Assembly partnership previously suggested that “real uncertainty” over the knock-on effects of the Princes Street fire in early July was partly to blame for the decision to scrap the ice rink.

However, the companies have also highlighted “a lack of funding support” for the Christmas festival, including via Edinburgh’s new visitor levy.

A spokesperson for the city council told The Herald: “Unique-Assembly informed the council prior to the summer festivals of their intent not to host an ice rink for the 2026 winter festival due to insufficient profitability of the attraction.

“As with any attraction, the risk remains with the contract holder and it is not the remit of the council to be involved in such decision making. No requests for support of the ice rink were made.

“It is the role of the contract holder to communicate with businesses and the role of Essential Edinburgh to communicate with those within the new town business improvement district.”

A spokesperson for the Unique-Assembly partnership said: “Edinburgh’s Christmas ice rink is a high-quality attraction which in 2025 cost more than £1 million to host on George Street, with the aim of spreading visitors and economic spend to businesses on the western side of the city centre.”

“Conversations are held annually with partners to review the finance and viability of all attractions, including the ice rink.

“We informed the council prior to the summer festivals that it was unlikely the ice rink would be located on George Street.

“The domino effect of ongoing financial review, sourcing new income, and not knowing whether George Street would be available as an event location due to the fire, whilst continuing to seek out alternative locations for other attractions, made it clear we had to look at what was achievable and viable for Edinburgh’s Christmas as a whole for 2026.

“There are no income funding streams available to Edinburgh’s Christmas from the council to support event delivery, as our contract dictates. In conversations with event partners, no other opportunities were identified to us.

“George Street businesses in the immediate vicinity were informed of all of the Edinburgh’s Christmas activity taking place on George Street on October 2.

“Essential Edinburgh was kept across ongoing plans in advance and we continue to work with them.”
