The mum of a teenager who lost his sight when a firework exploded in his face has recalled how she watched as his eye "leaked" down her son's cheek.

College student Aleks Stasiukiewicz joined friends after an organised display and suffered life-changing injuries after going over to a firework which hadn't gone off straight away.

The 17-year-old said: "I remember someone lighting a firework, but it didn't go off as intended so I went over to relight it.

"It exploded instantly, knocking me to the floor."

His mum Julie rushed to the scene in his hometown of Hartlepool after receiving a call from one of his friends.

She said: "I thought he was joking, but he wasn't.

"When I arrived, Aleks was already lying in the ambulance, I didn't take my eyes off him, I was devastated.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646791.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aleks Stasiukiewicz in hospital after his firework incident. *(Image: FAMILY/PR)*

"I had to watch as his eye leaked down the side of his face."

He was taken to James Cook University Hospital in Middlesbrough after the incident in November 2024, where doctors confirmed he had lost his left eye and part of his eyelid.

He also suffered burns to his arm and leg and a broken front tooth.

Specialist oculoplastic surgeons removed firework debris from his eye socket and fitted a ball-shaped prosthesis to allow for realistic movement.

Mr Stasiukiewicz and his mother are now working with University Hospitals Tees to raise awareness of firework safety.

The teenager said: "When people say don't play with fireworks, listen.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647405.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aleks Stasiukiewicz *(Image: FAMILY/PR)*

"I know it can be tempting to break the rules and have a bit of fun but fireworks are incredibly dangerous.

"They are not something you should mess around with as they can cause serious, life-changing injuries.

"I'm now much more cautious when attending firework displays because I never want to take the risk of something like this happening again."

His mother said she is extremely grateful for the care he received.

She said: "The hospital was incredible and I cannot thank them enough for all they have done to give Aleks some normality, in what can only be described as a tragic incident.

"Aleks underwent surgery the next day and now, thanks to the team's expertise, he still has movement within his eye socket.

"He has lost a lot and there will be things he can't do but we feel lucky. It could have been so much worse."

Consultant oculoplastic and orbital surgeon Gar-Yun Wong, who helped treat Mr Stasiukiewicz, said: "Bonfire Night is always a worrying time as an eye consultant.

"Accidents can happen in an instant and can result in life-changing eye injuries or even permanent sight loss."

He urged the public to prioritise safety.

He said: "We strongly encourage people to attend organised firework displays and never return to or handle a lit firework."
