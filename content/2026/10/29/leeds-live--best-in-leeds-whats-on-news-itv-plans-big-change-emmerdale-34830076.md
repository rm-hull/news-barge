# ITV plans big change for Emmerdale visitor tours in Leeds

## ITV Studios has applied to Leeds City Council to continue running popular Emmerdale visitor tours

The producers behind the hit TV series Emmerdale are looking to extend the popular guided tours of their production set, which bring in millions of pounds annually.

ITV Studios has submitted an application to Leeds City Council seeking permission to continue welcoming fans to the Harewood filming location once existing planning approval lapses.

The renowned site near Leeds attracts over 42,000 visitors each year, injecting £7.72m into the local tourism economy. ITV has also submitted a separate application to secure permanent filming rights at Stub House and Burdon Head Farm on Harrogate Road, with current permissions due to expire next June.

According to a planning document: "The Emmerdale Village Tour is a popular tourist attraction." It continued: "It provides fans of the popular television continuing drama the opportunity to visit the exterior, particularly the core set at Emmerdale Village."

The document explained that the tours offer guests an exclusive glimpse into the show's iconic Yorkshire setting and depiction of rural life.

The report stated: "The Emmerdale Village Tour is intrinsically linked to the production set, providing viewers with the opportunity to go behind the scenes and experience the production set in person."

It added: "The visitor tours are well established, having been in-situ for over 10-years, attracting over 42,000 visitors per annum and generating approximately £7.72m of gross visitor expenditure per annum."

A comprehensive change of use planning application is currently open for public consultation until 13 November. According to the report, visitors would be able to reach the tour by car, bicycle, via a public footpath or by being collected from a nearby bus stop, eliminating the necessity for coach transport.

ITV emphasised that its planning application was not intended to boost visitor numbers to Emmerdale, which attracts millions of viewers across 120 countries.

The report stated: "Two coaches operate in rotation to facilitate the maximum of two tours per hour whilst allowing for reasonable boarding, alighting and travel between locations, in line with the planning conditions."

It added: "This application seeks to remove the requirement for coach travel as visitors will travel direct to site."
