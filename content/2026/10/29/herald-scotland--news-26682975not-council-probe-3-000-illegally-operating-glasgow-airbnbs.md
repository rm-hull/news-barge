The investigation of an estimated 3,000 illegally operating Airbnbs in Glasgow does not lie with the city council, a meeting heard.

Councillor Christina Cannon said Police Scotland are responsible for any probes into unlicensed holiday lets as the council does not have the ability to do so.

The problem was discussed at a Glasgow City Council meeting on Thursday as councillor Martha Wardrop called for the consideration of a short term let control zone, which would mean operators would require planning permission to convert properties into holiday rentals.

Councillor Martha Wardrop asked for an update on the number of short term lets offering rooms without a licence in the city.

Responding at today’s full council meeting, SNP Councillor Cannon said the council does not hold that information as the local authority is only responsible for processing licence applications and ensuring operators meet conditions.

She said concerns about properties acting as holiday lets without authorisation should be passed on to Police Scotland.

SNP councillor Cannon said current legislation does not give the council the powers to “investigate or enforce unlicensed short term lets”.

The convenor for open government said short term lets operating without a licence is a “criminal offence” and responsibility for dealing with the matter sits with Police Scotland.

SNP Councillor Cannon said she would take on board the consideration of a short term let control zone for the city after the suggestion was made by Greens councillor Wardrop who said it is estimated there are 3,000 short term lets operating without a licence listed on Airbnb.
