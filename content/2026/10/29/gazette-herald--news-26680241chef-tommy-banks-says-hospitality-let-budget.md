Top North Yorkshire chef Tommy Banks says “hospitality was let down” in yesterday’s budget, adding Andy Burnham also “failed to deliver” in supporting the sector, something the PM said he would do.

Mr Banks, whose venues include The Abbey Inn at Byland, the General Tarleton at Ferrensby and the Black Swan at Oldstead, took to social media to air his views last night.

His criticism adds to others in the sector including Simon Cotton, managing director of the Fat Badger Group and several local pubs.

The celebrity chef posted online: “Hospitality was let down today. But we cannot let it die on our watch.

“Since 2021, the minimum wage has risen by 48%. The employer’s National Insurance bill for one full time minimum wage employee has risen by 162%. Altogether, the basic cost of employing that person has increased by 55%.

“Food prices have risen by around 40%. Average commercial electricity costs have risen by around 65%, and for the smallest businesses they have more than doubled.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644793.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tommy Banks has responded to yesterday's budget, following earlier calls for a cut in VAT for hospitality. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

“Businesses cannot keep absorbing these costs, but customers cannot afford endless menu price increases either.”

Mr Banks continued: “Then there is VAT. On every £120 restaurant bill, £20 goes straight to the government before we have paid for the food, our team, energy, rent or rates.

“There is a reason France, Spain and Italy charge hospitality VAT at 10%. The model simply does not work at 20%.

“You can run a busy, successful, high turnover pub or restaurant and still lose money. That is not necessarily because you are bad at what you do. It is because the model is broken.”

Mr Banks welcomed support on business rates relief for some small businesses, but said this did not cover such recent added costs.

He explained: “Much of it (the support) is temporary and it completely ignores the structural problem.”

“Andy Burnham said he would argue for hospitality VAT to be brought into line with Europe. He told our industry that the cavalry was coming. Today, he failed to deliver.

“So we go again, and this campaign must now step up.”

Mr Banks added: “If you work in hospitality, have family in the industry, had your first job in it or simply value your local pub, restaurant or café, we need you with us.

“It is now or never. We must force change before we lose our industry forever.”

North Yorkshire pubs have also expressed their views on social media.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/16096792.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Marcia Inn at Bishopthorpe has spoken out at the budget on Facebook *(Image: NQ)*

Among them, The Marcia Inn at Bishopthorpe posted on Facebook: The UK has lost 296 pubs already this year, more are sure to follow.

The British pub industry have campaigned and lobbied to the government to do more on tax relief and in particular lower the VAT to 10% from its current 20%.

“Andy Burnham stated that “ the cavalry is coming “ to support our great British pubs. Well back that up PM and ensure the chancellors budget statement reflects your promises !

“The great British pub is more than a drinking hole, it is a vital part of our social wellbeing and to some people a third place after home & work. It is a huge proud part of the British culture and also plays a massive part in the UK economy generating almost £40 billion annually.”
