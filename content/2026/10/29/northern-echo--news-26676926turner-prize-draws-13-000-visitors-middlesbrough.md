Thousands of visitors have flocked to the North East in the first month of the Turner Prize, giving the region’s cultural scene a "major boost".

Around 13,000 people have visited Middlesbrough since the Turner Prize exhibition opened at MIMA, with guests travelling from across the UK and beyond—including Helsinkini and New Delhi.

Dr Laura Sillars, dean of culture and creativity at Teesside University and director of MIMA, said: "This has been a fantastic start for the Turner Prize.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641140.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Turner Prize has put Middlesbrough in the cultural spotlight, attracting visitors from across the world *(Image: Teesside University)*

"I was always confident that Middlesbrough would rise to the challenge of hosting the Turner Prize but it is fantastic to see this delivered and people coming here from across the country to witness the cutting edge of British art.

"The response from critics has been incredibly positive, but just as important is the opportunity to introduce thousands of children and young people to the Turner Prize.

"With the schools programme fully booked until January, the Turner Prize’s impact will continue well beyond the exhibition."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641141.jpg?type=mds-article-620)he Turner Prize has put Middlesbrough in the cultural spotlight, attracting visitors from across the world *(Image: Teesside University)*

This is the first time the Turner Prize has been hosted by a university, with this year’s exhibition featuring work by Simeon Barclay, Marguerite Huemau, Kira Freije and Tanoa Sasraku.

The opening weekend attracted dozens of journalists from major national and international media outlets, earning high praise from critics.

The Guardian called it "the most exciting Turner Prize in years," while The Independent described the exhibition as an "otherworldly show" and "one of the best in recent memory."

Beyond visitor numbers and media attention, the Turner Prize is also making a mark through a dedicated schools programme.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641143.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Thousands of visitors have flocked to MIMA in Middlesbrough for the Turner Prize *(Image: Teesside University)*

The first school group from Brambles Primary Academy attended on October 1, and the programme is now fully booked until January.

Following earlier outreach with more than 3,000 children, a further 10,000 pupils are scheduled to visit before Christmas.

Councillor Philippa Storey, deputy mayor and executive member for education and culture at Middlesbrough Council, said: "These early visitor numbers are excellent.

"It’s wonderful to see people travelling to Middlesbrough from far and wide to enjoy the Turner Prize.

"I know Middlesbrough children are also having a wonderful time as part of the schools programme.

"It’s so important that we can inspire young minds and show them the possibilities of creative and artistic careers."

The Turner on Tees programme is running alongside the main exhibition, bringing additional events, artwork and activities to venues across Middlesbrough, Hartlepool, Redcar, Darlington and Stockton.

The exhibition continues until March 29, 2027.

The winner will be revealed during a star-studded ceremony at Teesside University’s Digital Life building on December 10.
