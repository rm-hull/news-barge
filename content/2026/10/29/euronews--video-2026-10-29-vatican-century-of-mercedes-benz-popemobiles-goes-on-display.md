The exhibition brings together **vehicles used by successive popes**, from an opulent 1930 limousine to modern models designed to bring the pontiff closer to crowds while protecting him.

The oldest is a **Nürburg 460** presented to**Pope Pius XI on 23 May 1930**, featuring a luxurious armchair upholstered in raspberry-coloured silk brocade. By 1960, the** 300 d Landaulet**built for** John XXIII**had removable rear side windows, allowing him to interact more easily with crowds. Five years later, the** 600 Pullman Landaulet**introduced a raised roof and folding soft top, enabling** Paul VI** to stand and greet pilgrims more directly.

The modern popemobile took shape in **1980**, when** Mercedes-Benz developed the 230 G for John Paul II's visit to Germany**. Its** tall transparent enclosure**offered the pontiff protection while keeping him visible to the public. Later models, including the** ML 430 from 2002**and** ML 500 from 2012**, reflect the continuing evolution of papal transport, with security features such as** thick bulletproof glass**.

The Vatican's relationship with Mercedes-Benz has also entered the electric era, with a **modified G 580 becoming its first fully electric popemobile in 2024**. Although other manufacturers have supplied vehicles to popes, this exhibition focuses exclusively on** Mercedes-Benz**. Vatican Museums director** Barbara Jatta**says the exhibition aims to showcase a century of papal journeys. Entitled***A Century of the Popemobile***, it runs until** 31 January 2027**.
