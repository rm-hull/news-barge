A County Durham man who took up rowing after his Parkinson’s diagnosis is backing a campaign urging men to improve their physical and mental wellbeing through exercise.

Sean Bell, 56, is backing Durham County Council’s ‘he thrives’ campaign, which runs throughout November and promotes physical and social activity for men of all ages and abilities.

Mr Bell was diagnosed with Parkinson’s disease in December 2022 but had always kept active, working out regularly and competing in cycling events.

He started indoor rowing with Lambton Rowing Club through the ‘he thrives’ programme last year and now trains twice a week.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640997.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sean Bell says indoor rowing has helped him feel energised and motivated following his Parkinson’s diagnosis *(Image: DCC)*

Mr Bell said: "During a session I feel energised and motivated.

"There is something about the rhythm and repetition of rowing that demands concentration and commitment."

The club’s social side has also kept him motivated, he said.

Mr Bell said: "I am not the man with Parkinson’s who has turned up to exercise.

"I am a member of a rowing club.

"Parkinson’s may be part of my life, but it doesn’t have to dictate what I do with it."

The ‘he thrives’ campaign follows the council’s successful ‘she thrives’ initiative earlier this year and coincides with International Men’s Day on November 19 and Movember, which raises awareness of men’s health.

Activities including rowing, golf, table tennis, walking cricket, squash, indoor bowls, hockey and dragon boat racing will take place across County Durham throughout November.

Cllr Karen Allison, Durham County Council’s cabinet member for leisure, tourism, regeneration and high street, said: "The ‘he thrives’ campaign aims to highlight the positive impact physical activity can have on physical, mental and social wellbeing.

"By sharing the stories of people like Sean and providing opportunities for men to get together and try out new activities, we can support more men to make movement a part of their daily lives.

"With such a varied range of activities on offer this year, there really is something for everyone and I would encourage men of all ages and fitness levels to get involved."

Durham County Council has teamed up with clubs and organisations to run sessions at leisure centres and community venues across the county.

Other events and offers in November include free seven-day leisure-centre passes, men’s health MOTs with the NHS Adult Wellbeing Service and suicide-awareness training from If U Care Share.

‘Dads and Kids’ sessions will give fathers and children the chance to get active together through circuit training, bowling, soft play, gym sessions and swimming.
