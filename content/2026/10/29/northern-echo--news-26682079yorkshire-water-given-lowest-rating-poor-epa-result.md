Yorkshire Water has been downgraded to the lowest possible environmental performance rating after being classed as a "poor performing" water company by the Environment Agency.

The company received just one star out of four in the regulator's Environmental Performance Assessment (EPA) for 2025, down from two stars the previous year and marking the first time it has received the lowest rating since the assessment was introduced in 2011.

Yorkshire Water was one of four companies rated as "poor performing" in 2025, alongside Southern Water, South West Water and Thames Water.

Environment campaigners, Save Our Swale, says it's "not surprised that Yorkshire Water has been downgraded to the lowest possible rating by the Environment Agency, since pollution incidents in Yorkshire's rivers have risen by 70 per cent, which is the highest total since 2011."

Across England, the nine water and sewerage companies achieved just 16 stars out of a possible 36, down from 19 in 2024 and the lowest total since the EPA began.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647703.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The River Swale *(Image: SUBMIT)*

Environment Agency chair Alan Lovell said the findings "expose the scale of underperformance across the sector".

He said water companies continued to fall "well short of the standards rightly demanded by both us and the public".

The Environment Agency recorded 74 serious pollution incidents nationally during 2025, with Yorkshire Water responsible for 13 of them, the second-highest total in England behind Thames Water's 24.

Together, Thames Water, Yorkshire Water, Southern Water and Anglian Water accounted for more than 80 per cent of all serious pollution incidents recorded across the sector.

Yorkshire Water also recorded 335 pollution incidents overall during the year, making it one of the poorest-performing companies nationally on that measure.

Responding to the findings, Yorkshire Water accepted its performance had fallen below expectations.

A spokesperson said: "Our environmental performance last year was not good enough and we understand that customers will be disappointed by this result.

"In particular, we need to reduce pollution incidents and improve the reliability of our environmental performance."

But Save Our Swale says, "What is totally staggering is that whilst Yorkshire Water fails to keep our waterways clean, customers' bills go up and up."

The group added: "How is it possible that our bills have risen by over 35 per cent since April 2025 and yet there fails to be any improvement in the amounts of pollution incidents in our rivers?"

The EPA assesses companies against six measures, including total and serious pollution incidents, self-reporting of incidents, discharge permit compliance, sludge use and disposal, and the delivery of environmental improvement schemes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647704.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sewage discharge *(Image: PA MEDIA)*

While Yorkshire Water's overall rating fell to one star, the company performed better in some areas.

The regulator found Yorkshire Water was one of only two companies in England, alongside Severn Trent, to achieve a green rating for compliance with wastewater discharge permits.

However, it failed to complete five environmental improvement schemes within the required deadlines, achieving a completion rate of 93.2 per cent.

Yorkshire Water said it was already working to improve its environmental record and was not waiting for regulatory assessments to drive change.

The spokesperson said: "We have already changed the way we are tackling pollution, with greater operational focus on preventing incidents, improving the health of our assets, and identifying problems earlier.

"Alongside those operational changes, we are delivering Yorkshire's largest environmental investment programme, which will bring improvements in the long-term."

The report also revealed Yorkshire Water was prosecuted twice during 2025 over pollution incidents, resulting in £1.215 million in fines.

Elsewhere, Northumbrian Water, retained its two-star rating and remains classed by the Environment Agency as a company that "requires improvement".

The North East supplier recorded three red, one amber and two green scores across the six EPA measures. It reported 150 pollution incidents from sewerage assets in 2025, up from 117 the previous year, while the rate increased from 39 to 50 incidents per 10,000km of sewer, above the sector average of 41.

It also recorded two serious pollution incidents involving sewerage assets.

The publication of the report has prompted Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle to summon water company bosses to Whitehall and demand improvement plans from firms that have fallen short of environmental standards.

Under rules introduced last year, Yorkshire Water and the other companies receiving one-star ratings will automatically be prevented from awarding performance-related bonuses to senior executives.

Yorkshire Water said upgrades had already been completed at almost 300 locations in the past six months, while plans are in place to transform Scarborough's wastewater network and upgrade more than 450 storm overflows by 2030.

A spokesperson added: "There is much more to do, and we will be open about where we are making progress and where we are not.

"The test for us isn't simply how much we spend. It is whether customers and the environment see better results."
