Schoolchildren have taken to the streets in a North East neighbourhood to urge speeding drivers to slow down as part of a crackdown on neighbourhood concerns.

Pupils from Linthorpe Community Primary School joined council officers and Cleveland Police for a community speed watch on Burlam Road, in Middlesbrough, during a week of action in Linthorpe.

The Year 5 pupils, who serve as junior road safety officers, held signs urging drivers to slow down in the 20mph zone.

Several vehicles were recorded travelling at 30mph or higher.

The speed watch was part of a wider campaign led by the council’s north neighbourhood team, with support from Cleveland Police, volunteers and residents.

Councillor Philippa Storey, deputy mayor and ward councillor for Linthorpe, said: "It has been brilliant to work alongside neighbourhood team staff during a week that has been welcomed by our residents in Linthorpe.

"We've spoken to residents about parking, held our first ever community skip day and worked with the neighbourhood team and police about concerns over crime and antisocial behaviour.

"We also had the absolute privilege of working with our junior road safety officers from Linthorpe Community School who made posters for the speed watch to remind cars to reduce their speed near schools."

Council staff and police also held a drop-in session at Linthorpe Community Centre to hear residents’ concerns about crime and antisocial behaviour.

The campaign also held the ward’s first community skip day at Westbourne Park, with support from Thirteen Housing.

Neighbourhood teams regularly run similar week-long programmes across Middlesbrough’s north, south, east and west.
