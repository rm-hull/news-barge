Rail passengers travelling to and from Bradford may have noticed some services being delayed, altered or cancelled this week due to what operators describe as "slippery rails".

The phrase has appeared on disruption notices across parts of the rail network, prompting confusion among some passengers.

On X, Northern has now explained that the issue is linked to autumn weather conditions, which have been made worse by recent storms.

## **What are slippery rails?**

During autumn, falling leaves can create significant problems for rail operators.

When leaves land on railway tracks and are repeatedly crushed by passing trains, they form a hard, slippery coating on the rail surface.

This reduces the grip between train wheels and the tracks, making it more difficult for trains to brake and accelerate.

As a result, services may need to run at reduced speeds, while some routes can face delays, alterations or cancellations.

## **Storms have made the problem worse**

Northern says recent stormy weather has added to the challenge facing rail operators.

Strong winds have blown additional leaves and debris onto tracks across the network, increasing the likelihood of slippery rail conditions and disruption to services.

The operator said that autumn conditions, combined with the impact of storms over the weekend, have led to cancellations and timetable changes on some routes.

## **How could Bradford passengers be affected?**

Passengers travelling through stations, including Bradford Interchange and Bradford Forster Square, could experience delays or changes to services while rail operators work to manage the seasonal issue.

Anyone planning to travel is being advised to check journey information before setting off and allow extra time where possible.

## **What is being done to tackle it?**

Rail operators carry out a range of measures each autumn to reduce the impact of slippery rails.

These include clearing leaves from tracks and using specialist treatment trains that clean rail surfaces and improve grip for train wheels.

However, periods of severe weather can still affect the network, particularly after storms and heavy winds.

## **Check before you travel**

Northern is encouraging customers to keep an eye on the latest service updates before travelling as autumn conditions continue.

While the term "slippery rails" may sound unusual, it is a well-known seasonal problem on Britain's railways and one that can have a significant impact on train performance during the autumn months.
