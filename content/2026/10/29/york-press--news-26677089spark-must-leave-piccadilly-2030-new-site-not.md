**Spark must leave Piccadilly by 2030, new site or not**

Matthew Laverack is entirely right to question the future of Spark:York, but the community must now demand an unequivocal undertaking from City of York Council: Spark must be obliged to remove its containers from Piccadilly at the end of the latest lease in 2030, whether they have a new site or not.

Spark has enjoyed 13 years of operations at the expense of York taxpayers and to the suffering of local neighbours.

Those who argue that city-centre residents should simply 'accept noise' ignore the vast difference between normal urban life and a commercial, open-air venue playing amplified music with a capacity of over 500 people with the nearest neighbours mere 2m away.

This favouritism cannot continue forever; Spark is owed absolutely nothing.

Crucially, any delays in a future housing scheme must not be used as an excuse to let Spark remain. Converting the site into a temporary car park would generate an income to council coffers more than ten times what Spark currently pays, without the nuisance to residents.

Furthermore, the claim by E Hume that the council has 'given its word' to provide a site for Spark is simply unfounded and must be firmly challenged.

No such obligation exists. Any vague talk of a future relocation to York Central appears to be a total illusion; the land Spark claims to be targeting is actually designated as public parkland and green infrastructure within the recently approved phase one masterplan.

The council must state categorically right now that come 2030, Spark will be required to clear the site completely and that no more Piccadilly extensions will be considered.

*Franz Wallmann,*

*Nelsons Yard,*

*York*
