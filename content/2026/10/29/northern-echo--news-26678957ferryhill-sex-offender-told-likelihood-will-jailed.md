A paedophile convicted of possessing thousands of indecent images of children faces another likely prison sentence after breaching court orders.

Neil Kitchen was jailed for 12 months at Teesside Crown Court in May 2017 after admitting three counts of making indecent images of children, plus possessing extreme pornography and possessing prohibited images of children.

He was jailed after a judge rejected his suggestion that he was a paedophile hunter who used the images to try to trap child abusers.

When asked how many times he had reported sex offenders to police he conceded he had never done so.

As part of the sentence he was made subject of a ten-year Sexual Harm Prevention Order (SHPO) and ordered to register as a sex offender for the same period.

The now 63-year-old defendant, of Skipton Close, Ferryhill, appeared back at the court on Wednesday, (October 28), by video link from Holme House Prison in Stockton, where he has been held on remand.

He admitted breaching both the SHPO and his registration requirements when he appeared before magistrates on September 30 and was due to be sentenced.

Both offences were said to have been committed in Middlesbrough on August 6.

But when he appeared for sentence he was unrepresented.

But Recorder Simon Kealey KC said he ought to have legal representation before being sentenced.

“These are serious offences and custody is at the forefront of the court’s mind.

“But you don’t have anyone to speak on your behalf by way of mitigation.

“I can’t force you, but the fact is I know nothing about you and there’s no pre-sentence report in this case.

“As it stands the court is looking at a fairly significant sentence.

“It’s not for me to give you advice, however, and you can speak for yourself if you choose to do so.”

Kitchen agreed to seek legal representation before being sentenced.

Recorder Kealey said the court would also benefit from a background report before the rescheduled hearing.

He adjourned the case to allow time for the report to be drawn up by the Probation Service and for Kitchen to find a lawyer.

Remanding him to remain in custody, the Recorder added: “I’m giving you no indication as to the likely sentence but the likelihood is it will be a prison sentence."

The sentencing hearing was adjourned until December 16 at the court.

Kitchen's previous case involved more than 120,000 indecent images, although police were said to have examined only a sample of about 2,500 due to time constraints.
