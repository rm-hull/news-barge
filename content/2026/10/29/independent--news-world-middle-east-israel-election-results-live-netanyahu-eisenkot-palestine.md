Donald Trump has not yet decided whether the US will intervene in efforts to assemble an Israeli governing coalition, a US official has said.

The official told The White House that reports that Washington preferred a specific coalition were “false and premature”.

Trump has given his backing to Benjamin Netanyahu and his Likud party, who earlier claimed that the Tuesday elections were "stolen" by Arab parties after falling short of a governing majority.

The claim appeared to be a direct play from the Trump playbook. The US president infamously refused to concede the 2020 US election to Joe Biden and routinely attacks the results of free and fair elections, with no evidence.

The unsubstantiated allegations attack an Arab electorate that took just 19 seats in the 120-seat parliament — a small minority of the total vote.

The prime minister’s bloc remains on course to win 54 seats, while main challenger Gadi Eisenkot’s grouping trails at 49, with 92 percent of votes tallied.

The results could still change as counting continues, but a victory for Eisenkot appears increasingly out of reach as his anti-Netanyahu coalition underperformed pre-election polls.

## Caretaker role could limit Netanyahu but not rule out war with Iran

Benjamin Netanyahu is expected to face more constraints as Israel's caretaker prime minister after Tuesday's election, but experts say his government could still retain significant powers including when it comes to waging war on Iran.

Neither Netanyahu’s right-wing coalition nor an opposition bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot is projected by Israeli media to win a majority in the country’s 120-seat legislature. Eisenkot has acknowledged the prime minister was still in office as a caretaker but issued a warning that Netanyahu might use security for political ends.

Netanyahu's office has previously rejected accusations he is using the Iran conflict for political gain. His office did not immediately return a request for comment on Wednesday.

“If you look at the letter of the law, a caretaker government has the same authority, has the same powers as a regular government,” said Guy Lurie, a research fellow at the Israel Democracy Institute, adding however that a caretaker government “has a duty of restraint” and more limited discretion to act in some areas.

A caretaker government faces limitations when it comes to issues such as appointments, but has leeway to act in matters where there is a public interest, analysts say.

## Who won the Israel election? Live results as vote counts continue

Results are still pouring in from Israel’s election yet it remains unclear whether Gadi Eisenkot or Benjamin Netanyahu will lead the nation.

Israel's Central Elections Committee is continuing to tally votes, with expectations that definitive results won’t be drawn up until Thursday. With 92 per cent of the votes tallied, neither side appears on track to win an outright majority in the 120-seat Knesset, Israel’s parliament.

Israel’s longest-serving prime minister was on course to win 53 seats in parliament between his Likud party and allies on the right, well ahead of the 48 forecast for the opposition bloc of rival Gadi Eisenkot.

## How could Eisenkot rescue a coalition government and still win?

Israel’s longest-serving prime minister is on course to win 53 seats in parliament between his Likud party and allies on the right, well ahead of the 48 forecast for the opposition bloc of rival Gadi Eisenkot.

Parties representing Israelis of Arab descent were meanwhile set to win an unprecedented 19 of 120 possible seats, following high turnout from passionately motivated voters in the first elections since the October 7, 2023 attacks and ensuing regional conflicts.

If neither bloc can form a coalition government, the existing cabinet with stay on until another election can be called.

The results could still change, but a victory for Eisenkot looked out of reach save for a deal to accept broad backing from the Arab parties, which many Jewish leaders reject.

In 2021, Netanyahu was ousted from the top office by a broad coalition, which for the first time included an Arab party, Ra’am. But the government soon destabilised, making way for Bibi to return barely 18 months later.

## Netanyahu bloc cannot be defeated 'without Arab citizens,' says Israeli politician

Ayman Odeh, leader of the Hadash-Ta’al list alongside Ahmad Tibi, said Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc would have secured an outright majority if not for the turnout of Palestinian citizens of Israel.

"Without the Arab citizens, Netanyahu, Ben-Gvir, and Smotrich would already be celebrating today," Odeh wrote in a post on social media.

Odeh added that while Arab voters held the balance of power, broader cooperation across the political spectrum remains necessary to break the right's grip on power: "The truth is simple: even the Arab citizens alone cannot bring about the change, and even without us, it’s impossible to defeat them."

"Without a genuine and equal Jewish-Arab partnership, it’s impossible to build a better future here for all of us," he said. "And we all deserve such a future."
