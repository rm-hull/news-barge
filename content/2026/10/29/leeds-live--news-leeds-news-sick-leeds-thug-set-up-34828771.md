# Sick Leeds thug set up CCTV to watch partner and went through her phone daily

## He has avoided prison

A Leeds thug who set up internal cameras inside his home and stayed on a video call with his partner for 19 hours so he could track her movements has avoided prison.

Andrew Harrison, 41, of Nowell Parade, led a campaign of terror against his then-partner as she lived at his home, Leeds Crown Court heard on Wednesday, October 28. Prosecutor Victoria Ball told the court the couple were together from November 2025. She said the woman has told how "from the start he exhibited controlling behaviours."

Harrison added his fingerprint to the woman's phone so he could look at her messages. Ms Ball said: "She did protest, but when she did he would accused her of cheating. She felt it was easier to let him have access."

Harrison - who would check the woman's phone daily - would block contacts, including her mental health and alcohol support worker at Forward Leeds. Ms Ball said: "She felt unable to do anything or have any freedom because of it. The defendant would demand her to be on video call when he was not in the house. He would do this when at work and it would last hours. On one occasion it happened for 19 hours. It was so he knew she wasn't speaking to anyone else.

"He set up internal CCTV in the living room and would use it to watch her. Even when she took phone calls out of the room he would accuse her of hiding things."

Harrison also made the woman change her Forward Leeds appointments, ensuring they would take place at her address after he was told he could not attend. That way he could watch the appointments through the camera system.

Ms Ball said: "It exacerbated her mental health issues and made her feel suicidal."

Each month, Harrison would make the woman transfer her full benefits amount, claiming it was her contribution to bills. On one occasion, he ordered her to lend his friend money, but kept it when the friend paid it back.

On June 5 this year, Harrison went one step further and threatened the woman with a blade. Ms Ball said: "She was in the kitchen preparing dinner. She says he suddenly walked into the kitchen and picked up a blade from the draining board and held it to her forearm. He told her he was going to slice her arm and 'no one would believe it was me because you self-harm.'

"He walked away and continued watching TV in the living room."

On June 8, following a verbal argument on the phone the woman told Harrison she was leaving and left the property. She was on a video call to her friend on Harehills Road, heading to her house when he started to drive alongside her.

The court heard: "He grabbed the phone from her hand and pulled her engagement ring from her finger. She ran away and he followed her to the petrol station. Her friend, having heard the initial interaction, called the police.

"The defendant did persuade her to go back to the address and told her to lie to any officers if they arrived. They did and the defendant stated he had come home from work due to her having a seizure. He was arrested and taken to custody."

Harrison denied any wrong doing in his interview but went on to plead guilty on the second day of his trial at Leeds Magistrates' Court to controlling and coercive behaviour and threatening a person with a bladed article in a private place.

In two victim personal statements, the woman told how she has had to move to a different area out of fear and has tried to take her own life. Ms Ball said: "She says she is unable to sleep and unable to rest at night and still finds it hard to trust people and keep relationships."

Mitigating, Paul Canfield said a pre-sentence report had been uploaded for the court to see. He said: "He has no previous convictions and his most recent caution from some time ago was for common assault.

"He has been remanded in custody since the 10th of June 2026. He has struggled to accept any wrongdoing and does lack insight. This is his first experience of custody."

His Honour Judge Tom Bayliss made Harrison the subject of a 21-month sentence suspended for two years, during which he must complete a probation requirement. He also made Harrison the subject of an indefinite restraining order, prohibiting him from contacting the victim in the case.
