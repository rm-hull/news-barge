It’s *do what Addams do or die* in York this week as Wharfemede Productions stage this musical favourite that’s spookily spectacular.

Following their productions of *A Little Night Music* and*Little Women*, Wharfemede Productions return to Theatre@41 – and what better show for Halloween week than comedy musical* The Addams Family*.

Filled with heart, humour, and spooky spirit, *The Addams Family* is from Marshall Brickman and Rick Elice, with music and lyrics by Tony Award-nominated Andrew Lippa, based on the characters created by Charles Addams.

As we enter the aptly decorated auditorium, we are about to witness a dinner party that would make *Come Dine With Me* look like a walk in the park.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/the-addams-family-wharfemede-c-Simon-Charles-Photography-4.webp)
![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/the-addams-family-wharfemede-c-Simon-Charles-Photography-5.webp)

When Wednesday Addams falls in love with a ‘normal’ young man and invites his respectable parents to dinner, chaos, secrets, and hilariously macabre mayhem unfold in true Addams style.

Director Bells Spencer has done an applaudable job with her cast, as each and every member is fully committed in their characterisation of this unorthodox family.

Zander Fick as Gomez is perhaps the role most perfectly suited to him – as he’s able to fully express his comedic and vocal talent on stage. Gomez would absolutely win any ‘Dad of the Year’ award and Fick plays him as the doting dad he is, with love charm and humour. Opposite him, Henrietta Linnenman is a suave and sophisticated Morticia, but who is no less committed to her family. Linnenman makes the strangely uplifting ‘Death is Just Around the Corner’ a standout number.

Abbie Law as Wednesday and Dan Crawfurd-Porter as Lucas Beineke are earnest in their portrayal of young love, with Law having particularly strong vocals and adding an endearing innocent quality to the eye-rolling Wednesday.

Matthew Warry as Pugsley – playing another pesky brother after his excellent turn in Next to Normal – is a standout vocally, and Rosy Rowley as Grandma brings some of the biggest laughs of the evening.

Connie Howcroft belts out some beautiful notes as Alice Beineke, and Chris Gibson as Lurch manages to do so much while saying so little to great comedic – and poignant – effect.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/the-addams-family-wharfemede-c-Simon-Charles-Photography-2.webp)
![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/the-addams-family-wharfemede-c-Simon-Charles-Photography-3.webp)

The influence of commedia dell’arte was very effective for the ancestors, who glide and gallop across the set with masks and all-white costumes, and who perform Connie Howcroft’s choreography with gusto – while filling the auditorium with impressive harmonies.

Musical director James Robert Ball leads the energetic band skilfully, and got the whole theatre clicking along from the first notes of the overture.

There were some mic and sound issues throughout, and transitions from each scene weren’t always the smoothest, this doesn’t take away from the enourmous effort that has gone into this show.

Wharfemede Productions have put on a slick and polished performance, without taking away any of the kooky sides of the show we know and love – this is a production you’d be dying to miss.
