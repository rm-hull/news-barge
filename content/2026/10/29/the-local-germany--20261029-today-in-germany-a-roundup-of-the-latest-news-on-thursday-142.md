**Thursday's top story: Merz calls for new global trade alliance**

On his trip to Asia, Chancellor Friedrich Merz proposed the establishment of a "global political alliance for rules-based trade".

The aim of such an alliance should be to defend, strengthen and further develop rules-based trade "including the WTO", he said in Tokyo.

Merz pointed out that 70 percent of world trade is still conducted according to the rules of the World Trade Organization (WTO).

On the second part of his trip to Asia, Merz met with Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi.

At the meeting of the governments of the world's third and fourth largest economies, Merz described Japan as a "neighbour" and partner of values, despite the geographical distance.

In Tokyo, Merz said that a new alliance could provide political impetus and also react together "if the rules-based trade order is endangered".

Together we could insist that other states refrain from illegal tariffs or coercion, Merz added, alluding to the USA and China.

On Tuesday in Singapore, the first stop on his trip, the Federal Chancellor had already spoken out in favour of an EU-ASEAN free trade agreement.

**Child seriously injured in wolf attack at wildlife park**

An eight-year-old girl is seriously injured following an attack by a wolf in a wildlife park.

The incident happened in Johannismühle wildlife park in Baruth, Brandenburg, south of Berlin, on Wednesday.

The child was taken to hospital by rescue helicopter.

"The girl was attacked and seriously injured by at least one wolf within an animal enclosure while she was there with two other children and a staff member," police said.

They added in a statement that "the precise circumstances of the incident are the subject of an ongoing police investigation".

The park keeps brown bears, lynx and grey and arctic wolves, according to its website, which said that the facility was temporarily closed without providing further details.

The district now wants to review the safety requirements and operational processes in the wildlife park.

One day after the huge explosion in a Regensburg residential building, two people are still missing.

So far, the emergency services have not been able to enter the house, which is in danger of collapsing.

The police have not yet provided any information on the identity of the missing persons. However, both are suspected to be in the building.![An apartment building in Regensburg where an explosion went off.](https://apiwp.thelocal.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/628814431.jpg)

After a gas explosion destroyed a large part of the apartment building early on Wednesday morning, four people were taken to hospital, one of them with more serious injuries. A policeman also suffered smoke poisoning.

It has not yet been clarified if the explosion was an accident. Specialists from the Bavarian State Criminal Police Office are investigating.

**Munich considers opening Oktoberfest to more breweries**

Munich city councillors from the Greens, SPD and CSU are preparing a motion to allow more local breweries to supply beer at Oktoberfest.

Currently, only six traditional Munich breweries (Augustiner, Hacker-Pschorr, Löwenbräu, Paulaner, Spaten, and the state-owned Hofbräuhaus) are authorised to serve beer at the festival, where 6.7 million litres were served this year.

Under the strict rules, the beer served at the festival must comply with both the Munich Purity Law of 1487 and the German Purity Law of 1906, and be brewed within Munich's city limits.

The proposed changes could benefit breweries like Giesinger Bräu, which meets the requirements and has been campaigning for permission to serve its beer at Oktoberfest, as well as Münchner Kindl.

A billion-dollar Porsche write-down, weak business in China and costs for restructuring pushed Volkswagen deep into the red in the third quarter.

Group profit after tax was around negative 5.82 billion euros in the months of July to September, the carmaker announced.

In the same quarter of the previous year, VW had also reported a deficit, at that time it was negative 1.07 billion euros.

A write-down on the goodwill of the sports and off-road vehicle subsidiary Porsche has had a particularly strong impact.

In terms of sales, from July to September, VW delivered 1.89 million vehicles - almost 14.3 percent less than a year earlier.

VW is under considerable pressure, especially in the important market of China

Germany's Deutsche Bank reported Wednesday a record profit for the third quarter in a row as it expressed growing confidence in its home German market.

Net profit came in at €1.85 billion in the three months to end-September, up two percent from the period last year.

Overall revenue rose four percent to €8.34 billion, driven by growth for both its private banking and corporate banking divisions.

Revenue at the private bank rose eight percent to €2.60 billion and profits ticked up two percent to €699 million.

Revenue at the corporate banking division, which lends to businesses, rose 9 percent to €1.98 billion, with profit up 11 percent to €743 million.

"We see clear signs of growing confidence in our conversations with German clients," Deutsche Bank's financial chief Raja Akram said.

"Corporate sentiment is improving and that is translating into investments," he added.
