*(Image: Damian Shields)*

Gavin and Stacey star Alison Steadman, comics Frankie Boyle, Greg McHugh and Fern Brady, artist David Shrigley, Still Game favourite Gavin Mitchell and trailblazing poet Kae Tempest will be taking to the stage of Pitlochry Festival Theatre when a new chapter opens at its long-running winter arts festival.

Best-selling authors Joanne Harris, David Nicholls and Matt Haig will be joined by Scottish literary favourites Val McDermid, Denise Mina, Amy Liptrot, Jenni Fagan and Louise Welsh when the event returns in February.

Their appearances have been announced by poet and novelist Michael Pedersen, the new programmer of the Winter Words Festival, which has been running for more than 20 years in the Perthshire town.

His inaugural line-up will also feature Scottish stage and screen stars Shauna Macdonald and Gerda Stevenson, Scots Makar Peter Mackay, poets Len Pennie and Jackie Kay, former First Ministers Nicola Sturgeon and Humza Yousaf, and Mhairi Black, the former SNP MP who has ventured into stand-up comedy and acting.

Pedersen was asked to lead the event by the venue’s artistic director, Alan Cumming, after the actor led the programming of the 2025 and 2026 editions of the literary-themed event, which featured appearances from Andrew O’Hagan, Kirsty Wark, Douglas Stuart, Ian Rankin, Sally Magnusson and David Eustace.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18597254.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Frankie Boyle will be appearing at the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry in February. *(Image: PA)*

Pedersen, the current Edinburgh Makar and a former writer-in-residence at Edinburgh University, came to prominence in the Scottish capital’s cultural scene after forming the Edinburgh arts collective Neu! Reekie! with fellow poet Kevin Williamson in 2010.

Pedersen was interviewed by Cumming at Winter Words in 2025, while the roles were reversed at the latest edition of the event earlier this year.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20547182.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Gavin and Stacey star Alison Steadman will be among the special guests at the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry in 2027. *(Image: Supplied)*

Speaking to The Herald about his plans for the festival, Pedersen said he has drawn on his personal experiences of the event and the town, as well as the numerous events he has organised, to inspire his first Winter Words programme.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20554156.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Cumming and Michael Pedersen have previous appeared on stage together at the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry. *(Image: Olivia Attwooll-Keith)*

He said: “The brief has really been to create a great festival for the people of Pitlochry and beyond, which will be a real celebration of words and ideas.

“I pretty much feasted on every element of the festival in my previous visits. I really want to build on the reputation of what I’ve seen while putting my own stamp on the event.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18090079.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Artist David Shrigley will appearing at Pitlochry Festival Theatre's Winter Words Festival.

“Alan told me he didn’t me to programme a festival that he would book, but to draw on all my experience and love of book festivals and literature events to create something special.

“Book festivals are a celebration of literature and people who investigate all manner of brilliant storytelling. But, beyond that, there are also people who have a fervour for language, despite them being better known for something else.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19294249.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Comedian and author Fern Brady will be among the special guests at the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry in 2027. *(Image: na)*

"Literature is such a core part of their practice. They are just as worthy of a place at a book festival as anyone else, provided there is a great balance of guests and I think we have that.”

Next year’s Winter Words will open with a special live edition of Sara Pascoe and Cariad Lloyd’s Weirdos Book Club, which Only Child and Gary: Tank Commander star Greg McHugh will appear at.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18042635.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Author David Nicholls is expected to be among the main draws at the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry in February.

Other special events will include acclaimed stand-up Christopher McArthur-Boyd interviewing fellow comics Fern Brady and Frankie Boyle about their forays into writing books, Gavin Mitchell interviewing David Shrigley about his career hand-drawn art, Nicola Sturgeon interviewing Joanne Harris and David Nicholls about their novels.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21643654.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Special guests lined up for the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry next year include Sara Pascoe, Alison Steadman, Joanne Harris, Humza Yousef, Frankie Boyle, Cariad Lloyd, Matt Haig, David Shrigley, Jackie Kay, Val McDermid, Jo Sharp, David Nicholls and *(Image: Pitlochry Festival Theatre)*

Writer Alan Bissett will be reviving his one-man stage show When Billy Met Alasdair, which imagines an encounter between Sir Billy Connolly and Alasdair Gray at the launch of the writer’s novel Lanark at the Third Eye Centre in Glasgow in 1981.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20554156.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alan Cumming and Michael Pedersen have previous appeared on stage together at the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry. *(Image: Olivia Attwooll-Keith)*

Mhairi Black, who is about to appear in new BBC Scotland legal drama Counsels as a Glasgow detective, will be interviewing Humza Yousaf about his new memoir.

James Robertson, Alan Riach, Gerda Stevenson and Jim Mackintosh will be appearing in a special event staged to mark the centenary of the Hugh MacDiarmid poem A Drunk Man Looks at the Thistle.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19906501.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Poet and author Michael Pedersen has programmed the next edition of the Winter Words Festival in Pitlochry. *(Image: Supplied)*

Shauna Macdonald, who is best known for her horror film roles, will be interviewing Jenni Fagan about her new book The Fall of Frankenstein.

Pedersen said: “Near enough everyone I asked to come to the festival said ‘yes’ pretty quickly.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/12911159.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Actress Shauna Macdonald, who is best known for her roles in horror films like The Descent, will be interviewing author Jenni Fagan about her new Frankenstein novel at the Winter Words festival in Pitlochry.

“I wrote quite an effervescent email saying how much fun there is to be had in Pitlochry. I think it was very different from the kind of email you normally get from a book festival.

“Since Alan has arrived in Pitlochry, there has been quite a lot of gasping about the names that he has brought there. A lot of people have appeared in Pitlochry for the first time and Pitlochry has really been put on their radar. I knew I wanted to do that with this edition of the festival.

“We have definitely got a lot of Winter Words favourites coming next year, but there are so many names that have never been in the theatre before.”

Pedersen urged people who had not previously been to Winter Words to make a weekend of it at the festival, which will run from February 18-21.

He added: “I’ve always loved the type of book festivals that are quite immersive, where a great amount happens on these magical weekends that you cannot plan for.

“If people are coming to Pitlochry from further afield they should give themselves plenty of time and not make a hurried journey.

“They should leave time to discover a new writer they weren’t expecting to or go on a stroll between events. I love being ambushed at festivals and Winter Words definitely has the potential to do that.

“It will be a veritable feast for the senses and the emotions, and there will be sustenance for the soul and the body.”
