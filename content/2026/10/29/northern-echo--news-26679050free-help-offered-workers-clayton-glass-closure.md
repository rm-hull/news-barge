Workers who lost their jobs when a County Durham factory suddenly closed are being reminded that free help remains available as they look for new employment.

Former staff at Clayton Glass in Annfield Plain can access ongoing support from Durham County Council’s Employability Durham team, which has been offering assistance since the factory’s unexpected closure last month.

The team provides help with CV writing, job searches, interview preparation, and training opportunities to boost skills and confidence.

Cllr Darren Grimes, deputy leader of Durham County Council, said: "It’s now just over a month since Clayton Glass employees showed up for a day’s work only to discover the factory had been closed without notice.

"This came as a terrible shock for these workers and their families.

"Many Clayton Glass employees had been with the firm their whole working life, be that ten, 20 or 30 years, and the jobs market has changed a great deal in that time.

"We want to remind anyone impacted by the Clayton Glass closure that they are not alone.

"We’re still here to help, and I would encourage anyone who is feeling overwhelmed or uncertain of what to do next, to contact our Employability Durham team on 03000 262009."

Employability Durham also signposts individuals to training and skills opportunities.

The council’s Business Durham team is also offering support to businesses affected by the closure, including supply chain companies.

Cllr Karen Allison, cabinet member for leisure, tourism, regeneration, and high street, said: "When a large company goes into administration, the impact can be felt far beyond its own workforce.

"Businesses in the supply chain can face significant drops in revenue, resulting in growing financial pressure and uncertainty over the future.

"We would encourage companies affected by the closure of Clayton Glass to contact Business Durham.

"The team is there to help and can provide a range of specialist support to get you through this difficult time."

Business Durham can assist with identifying new contracts, exploring market opportunities, and offering advice on marketing, investment readiness, and workforce development.

Workshops and webinars on business strategy, costing, and content creation are also available.

Cllr Grimes said Clayton Glass is not the only major employer to close or reduce its workforce in County Durham recently.

He said: "Sadly, Clayton Glass isn’t the only firm to have closed in County Durham.

"Other major employees including Erwin Hymer Group in Consett, Crafter’s Companion in Newton Aycliffe and NSK in Peterlee have either gone into administration or are cutting their workforces.

"Last month, Greggs also announced it is to begin consultations on the future of its factory in Seaham.

"We’re committed to doing everything in our power to support those affected through our Employability Durham and Business Durham services.

"We are also lobbing the Government and the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority to invest in protecting existing jobs and industries in our area.

"And we’re pressing for a sustainable employment support funding settlement, which recognises the need within County Durham and enables us to support people when job losses are unavoidable."

Employability Durham can be contacted on 03000 262009 or by emailing [employability@durham.gov.uk.](mailto:employability@durham.gov.uk.)
