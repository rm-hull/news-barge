After the high drama of last year’s Autumn Statement when the Office for Budget Responsibility (OBR) stole then chancellor Rachel Reeves’s thunder by releasing its economic and fiscal outlook before she stood up in the House of Commons, this week’s Budget was relatively low-key and, some might say, dull.

Chancellor John Healey struck a confident pose in the House of Commons, oozing confidence as he spoke about providing “breathing space” for businesses and families and delivering lots of “small consequentials”, as one panel member at The Herald’s Budget Briefing in Glasgow put it today, that would “make people feel positive”.

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch didn’t hold back, summing up her view of the Chancellor’s performance: “Most people in the country listening to his Budget will be thinking to themselves: is that it?”

Mr Healey spoke about “steering us through the global economic storms today, to chart a new course for Britain in the years ahead”. There was no chaos in the chamber, no grand announcements – rather a steady-as-she-goes Budget offering a string of low-cost measures to persuade voters the government cares about the cost of living challenges they are facing.

So, what did Scotland’s business community make of the Chancellor’s first Budget? At The Herald’s Budget Briefing in the offices of Nationwide in Glasgow, one of today’s sponsors along with Mitchells Roberton, chairman Brian Taylor, Herald columnist joked: “Not every Budget is preceded by the Prime Minster telling us to stock up", referring to extensive headlines the day before when the Chancellor confirmed that import tariffs will be removed from more than 100 everyday food items – gherkins, marmalade, baked beans and chocolate, for example.

The veteran political commentator went on to expertly unpick the key points with the help of a distinguished panel: Stephen Boyd, director, IPPR Scotland; Craig Wilson, head of FX sales and trade finance (Business Banking), Nationwide; Andy Williams, director of ADS Scotland, part of ADS Group Ltd; and Derek Hanlan, tax director, Mitchells Roberton.

Asking the panel for their general views to kick off the session, Nationwide’s Mr Wilson suggested that the Chancellor had “achieved his mission”, adding: “The Budget had to calm the markets and that has been achieved.”

He added that a highlight for him was the announcement that there will be increased funding for the British Business Bank and the National Wealth Fund, with Mr Healey pledging £20.4 billion to the two institutions. “This supports businesses and I think it’s very positive,” Mr Wilson said.

Mr Williams said that from the perspective of ADS Scotland, the trade association for the aerospace, defence, security and space industries, confirmation on defence funding was a “big thing for the UK and Scotland”. Mr Healey said that at next year’s UK Government Spending Review, he will confirm details of how the country will meet its Nato commitment to spend 3.5% of GDP on defence by 2035, with a target date for 3% in 2030.

The Chancellor also confirmed that the UK will provide £10bn of additional economic and military support to Ukraine.

Meanwhile, Mitchells Roberton’s Mr Hanlan said that while there were lots of “small consequentials” that all add up, he felt the Chancellor was at times “tinkering around the edges”, adding: “Businesses need stability but when you look at the details, how is this Budget going to affect your clients or your business?”

The Institute for Public Policy Research’s Mr Boyd was much more stark in his opinion of the Budget, pointing to the “grim” outlook on the economy portrayed in the OBR’s report earlier this week. The government’s independent forecaster showed a sharp downgrade for growth in 2027, affecting the public finances and household disposable incomes – with the crisis in the Middle East contributing to that.

“One of my great frustrations is that we talk about economic strategy without looking at the wider geopolitical – China, for example,” he said. “It’s frustrating that we don’t engage with geopolitics.”

Later in the session, Mr Boyd admitted he was “pessimistic” when he considered the OBR’s outlook. He said the Budget hadn’t “made things worse and consumer sentiment might not be so bad”, but he was adamant there had to be a “national debate” about taxation. “If we avoid this, things will get worse,” he added.

Alluding to the University of Strathclyde’s respected Fraser of Allander Institute which recently described the Scottish economy as “resilient”, Brian Taylor asked the panel if they agreed. “The outlook’s not great but Scottish businesses typically get stuck in,” Mr Hanlan pointed out. “Scottish businesses are resilient – they keep investing and employing people.”

Nationwide’s Craig Wilson agreed: “Scottish businesses have had 20 years of turmoil and yes, they have been resilient. Fiscal discipline was one of the core points of the Budget and I think the additional funding support for the British Business Bank and National Wealth Fund is really positive – it will help businesses grow in Scotland and the UK and provide facility.”

On the resilience question, Andy Williams said there were “small shoots of positivity” that he did not want to be overshadowed by the need for clarity on the defence spending investment announced by Mr Healey. “We still need long-term certainty for defence companies,” he added.

Stephen Boyd, discussing taxation specifically, gave his view that taxation in the UK is really badly designed. “The relationship between tax and the economy is complex,” he said, agreeing with Andy Williams that “one of the best ways to improve tax is to create jobs”.

Giving his input to the conversation, Derek Hanlan said people had to understand the relationship between tax allowances and tax take – the revenue the government collects from taxes. “Tax take is always going to go up as a consequence of fiscal drag,” he pointed out, adding that the Chancellor did not increase the personal allowance in his Budget, rather he created an additional allowance for those who only have their state pension.

As Scotland anticipates the content of Deputy First Minister and Cabinet Secretary for Finance and Local Government Jenny Gilruth’s Scottish Budget on December 3, the panel universally welcomed the Chancellor’s announcement that £18 million has been earmarked for advanced chip design led by Edinburgh University, and £6m for Glasgow’s Scottish Event Campus.
