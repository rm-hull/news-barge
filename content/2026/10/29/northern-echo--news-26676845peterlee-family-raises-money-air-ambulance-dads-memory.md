The family of a man who tragically passed away nearly nine years ago are continuing to honour his memory by raising money for the air ambulance charity that tried to save his life.

The family of Stephen Lee, from Peterlee, are raising funds for the Great North Air Ambulance Service (GNAAS), which responded to his cardiac arrest in December 2017.

Mr Lee was 61 when he collapsed at home on December 13, and despite the efforts of emergency crews, he sadly died.

Sophie Lewis, Mr Lee’s daughter, said: "My dad became very poorly very quickly.

"The paramedics were called and began working very suddenly on my dad and at this point GNAAS arrived.

"We remember the air ambulance staff being so professional that night.

"Dr Mike spoke with so much compassion and was very knowledgeable about what he was doing.

"There was so much equipment that was brought to my dad’s aid.

"Despite the best efforts of all the team my dad sadly passed away.

"We were heartbroken as a family; we did everything together."

Described as a devoted family man, Mr Lee was known for his kindness towards his elderly parents and mother-in-law.

He enjoyed regular holidays with his wife Denise and daughter Sophie, especially to Salou, as well as trips to Torquay, Blackpool, Skegness and Whitby.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640543.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Denise and Stephen *(Image: Family photo provided)*

Ms Lewis said: "My Dad was a family man, he did everything for his family.

"He enjoyed his sports, especially when he was younger.

"He was a long-distance runner and every sport he put his mind to he was always top of the class.

"He loved watching the football especially Sunderland playing.

"He particularly used to enjoy watching the football with his son-in-law Cameron, however he was a Newcastle supporter."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640544.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cameron Lewis *(Image: Family photo provided)*

Following Mr Lee’s death, Ms Lewis struggled with anxiety, depression and later PTSD, while her mother became deeply depressed and his parents were left devastated.

She said: "As time went on it was so difficult to find a purpose and a reason to get up on a morning, so we decided to get a dog.

"We got Leo in February 2019 and he was a beautiful Shar Pei puppy.

"Shar Pei’s are typically a stubborn dog but the most loyal loving dogs with their families and we thought Leo would fit him perfectly as that was my Dad’s childhood nickname.

"We have had Leo now nearly eight years and he is the best dog we could wish for, he gave us a reason to smile and laugh and he is so similar to my Dad in so many ways."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640545.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cameron Lewis at the Great North Run *(Image: Family photo provided)*

The family has since taken Leo to several dog shows, including GNAAS’ Whirlybird Woof Fest, and together they have raised thousands of pounds for the air ambulance charity.

Ms Lewis said: "As a family we try to raise as much money as we can for the charity because they gave my dad a chance, a small chance at survival, and for that we will always be grateful to them."

GNAAS is reliant on charitable donations and currently need to raise £9.6 million each year to remain operational.
