A Scottish tent and outdoor equipment company has returned to profit.

The company behind brands including Vango, Force Ten and Wayfayrer returned to profitability despite a near-£1 million drop in turnover.

AMG Group Limited, also behind Black’s of Greenock and Lichfield and acting as UK distributor for Rossignol, Bollé, Radix and Trangia, reported a rise in gross profit of £800,000, or 9.6%, to £9.4m. Gross margin increased from 30.4% to 34.6%, its highest since 2021.

Profit after tax was £7,789 for the year ended December 31, 2025, compared with a £580,000 loss in 2024. Turnover fell £980,000, or 3.5%, to £27.3m.

The Port Glasgow company says its roots go back to 1847 “crafting ship sails on the River Clyde”.

Now, its key activities are the “production, marketing and wholesale of outdoor leisure equipment and the distribution of a portfolio of premier outdoor and snow sports brands”.

The directors said in their business review that the company “continued to navigate a challenging trading environment during 2025 as consumer confidence remained subdued from continuing macro-economic and inflationary pressures”.

Market research data indicates a decline in outdoor industry spend across the last two financial years, both in the UK and the wider European market, "reflecting the industry requirement for discretionary spend against challenging economic headwinds on household spending”.

The company said consumers are “increasingly focussed on lower price pointed product and this has been reflected in the company’s product developments, ensuring ‘value for money’ price points are achieved”.

The board “continues to believe that brand strength and trust is, and will continue to be, a key driver of consumer spend”.

The directors said: “With a portfolio of brands rich in heritage, the board considers that maintaining pricing which reflects the quality and innovation of our products and restricting discounting to selective products is key for brand credibility.

“Despite the challenging backdrops, the company delivered a resilient performance in 2025.

“Although revenues fell by 3.5% to £27.3m, the company’s gross profit % increased from 30.4% in 2024 to 34.6% in 2025. This level of gross profit % was last achieved in 2021.

“Consequently, total gross profit increased by £800,000 to £9.4m in 2025.”

The directors also said: “Notwithstanding the impact of higher delivery costs and property rental costs, the company prudently managed its operating cost base to record an operating profit of £300,000 in 2025 versus a £300,000 operating loss in 2024.

“While slightly below expectations for the year, the board are pleased to report the return to an operating profit.”

During the year the firm continued its actions to reduce the company’s stock holding, reducing the volume and value of purchase orders placed with its suppliers and temporarily delaying the introduction of certain new products.

As a result of these actions, the company’s stock holding decreased to £9.8m, down from £13.4m at the start of 2025 and down from £18.4m at the start of 2024.

The directors said: “The Board considers this to be a significant achievement. The company was cash generative again in 2025, ending 2025 with a net cash surplus of £1.8m versus the £900,000 net borrowing position at the start of the year.”

Looking forward, the company said retailers are starting to invest in their showrooms, websites and content creation and are reporting a promising start to the 2026 season.

“This, coupled with the uncertainty around overseas holidays following events in the Middle East, offers opportunities for the company.”
