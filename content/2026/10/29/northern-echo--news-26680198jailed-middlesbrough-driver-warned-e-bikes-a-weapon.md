An e-bike rider who smashed into a boy, seven, leaving him with a catastrophic brain injury has spoken from prison to warn e-bikes are ‘a weapon’.

The boy ran out onto Westminster Road in Middlesbrough seconds before Michael Hill rode past on an almost-silent Surron e-bike on August 21, 2025.

The youngster was left "drifting in and out of consciousness in the road" before Hill fled the scene.

The boy suffered life-changing injuries and spent three months in an induced coma, and became dependent on his parents, who travelled to the RVI in Newcastle daily to visit him while caring for his siblings and continuing to work.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20222883.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Michael Hill *(Image: Cleveland Police)*

Hill, a disqualified driver, had been driving round for hours before the crash on a scooter and e-bike.

Now, speaking from prison, Hill has issued a stark warning about the dangers of e-bikes.

“I wish I didn’t go out on the bike that day…they’re very dangerous, they’re a weapon," he said.

"People just jump on them and think that they're fun.

"It's affected me, it's affected the child, it's affected his parents. I can't sleep on a night - I still get dreams about what happened, what I'd done.

"It is just a blur truthfully, it all happened so fast."

He added that he took off because 'he was scared'.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645308.jpg?type=mds-article-620)CCTV footage of the incident *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

The boy’s father has also made an emotional plea for people to think about the consequences before getting on an e-bike.

Speaking via an actor, he said: “Even if one rider just stopped and thought….I’m not going to do this….I’m not going to cause harm to the people around me….it will mean a step in the right direction.

"My son has always been full of life. It's really difficult to see that his childhood has been impacted so much.

"After that accident he had a traumatic brain injury which meant he was in an induced coma for three months. He can't do anything on his own now, we need to constantly supervise him. His short term memory and his general quality of life has now been diminished and he no longer has that quality of life he did before it all happened.

"Every day for us now is a constant struggle, he struggles in his day to day life, he can't do anything by himself. He's lost his independence and he hates the fact he can't go to school or play like his brothers do."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645309.jpg?type=mds-article-620)CCTV footage of the incident *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

On the day of the collision the boy was playing with his brother at his cousins house with it being the summer holidays, after that he was on his way home and that was when he stepped out into the road and was hit by the bike.

Teesside Crown Court heard last November how Hill was helped to flee the scene by Lee Oliver.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20222882.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lee Oliver *(Image: Cleveland Police)*

Harrowing footage shown to the court captured the boy running into the road just seconds before Hill struck him on the e-bike.

Prosecutor Jo Hedworth told the court the boy’s uncle came outside to find him lying in the middle of the road in a foetal position.

“The boy was making no noise, appeared to be drifting in and out of consciousness and was not responding to any verbal command.

“It was clear that the limping male was trying to leave the scene.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644966.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Michael Hill speaking from prison *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

Hill suffered a broken arm after being thrown from the e-bike, which was too badly damaged to ride. Oliver returned on his scooter to collect him before the pair fled, leaving the injured child behind.

The court also heard Hill had tested positive for cannabis and cocaine at a probation appointment just two hours before the collision.

Judge Richard Bennett described their decision to flee as an “act of cowardice”, warning the boy would almost certainly depend on others for his daily care for the rest of his life.

Hill, 24, of Raby Court, Middlesbrough, pleaded guilty to causing serious injury by driving while disqualified, having no insurance, failing to stop and failing to report the crash.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645306.jpg?type=mds-article-620)CCTV footage of the incident *(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

He was jailed for a total of 55 months and banned from driving for 46 months.

Oliver, 31, of no fixed abode, pleaded guilty to assisting an offender, driving while disqualified and using a motor vehicle without insurance.

He was sentenced to 12 months in prison for his role in the incident and banned from driving for 23 months. He had already been recalled on licence.
