Campaigners have claimed victory after winning their battle to stop six giant wind turbines being built in an East Yorkshire beauty spot immortalised by David Hockney.

Planning chiefs have thrown out plans for the 492ft-high structures in the Yorkshire Wolds, citing threats to historic landmarks, homes and even military radar.

“We are all so relieved — the phones in the village were alive when we heard the decision,” said local resident Rob Booth, 58, who had campaigned against the proposals for Woldgate, near Burton Agnes, west of Bridlington.

“We did so much work to raise our concerns about this wholly inappropriate development, so it's a very welcome decision — but it's still not properly sunk in yet.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21226994.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rob Booth, left, and Brendan McEvoy who opposed the wind turbines being built

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21226991.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The site in Woldgate Woods which was painted by David Hockney in a piece entitled the Arrival of Spring

East Riding of Yorkshire Council refused the application by Three Oaks REP Extension Ltd on Monday (October 26).

The proposals would have seen six turbines built at Thornholme Fields.

A 300ft-high meteorological mast, underground cables, transformer kiosks, crane pads and access tracks were also included.

The Three Oaks Renewable Energy Park Extension would have sat beside an approved 161-acre solar farm and battery storage project.

Developer Ridge Clean Energy said the 25.2MW scheme could generate electricity equivalent to the annual needs of around 24,000 households.

But in a report setting out five reasons for refusal, council planners warned the development would cause "a high level of harm" to some of the area's most treasured landmarks. These included Burton Agnes Hall, its Grade I-listed gatehouse, All Saints Church in Rudston and the ancient Rudston Monolith.

Rob, a member of the Keep Wind off Woldgate campaign group, said: "The field that would have hosted the wind farm bordered Woldgate, an ancient lane made famous by David Hockney who painted it many times when he lived here.”

He said Hockney, who died in June aged 88, would have been “very relieved — like us — that the plans are not going ahead”, adding: "It's a really special little area and the windfarm would have blighted it for generations to come."

Hockney previously objected to wind farm proposals in the area, describing the turbines as “big ugly things”.

"I certainly wouldn't paint them,” Hockney once said of wind turbines. “They are unnatural.

"People should stand up for themselves and say no to these things if they don't agree with them."

The Bradford-born artist created a series of paintings of nearby Woldgate Woods which he regularly passed on the way to Bridlington, where he had a studio.

One piece, titled Woldgate Woods, sold for £9.4 million in New York in 2016.

Local councillor Denise Howard said Hockney would be "heartbroken" to see six giant turbines on the landscape that inspired his works.
