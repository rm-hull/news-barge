## Yorkshire students say practical skills remain vital despite rise of AI

Students at Bishop Burton College develop practical skills in subjects including agriculture *(Image: Supplied)*

Students say 'AI can’t deliver a lamb,' as they champion the future of more hands-on careers.

Vocational students at Bishop Burton College remain confident that practical skills will stay in demand despite advances in artificial intelligence, with one student pointing out that some jobs simply can't be automated.

Their views follow a new survey commissioned by the college, which found that 77 per cent of parents are worried about AI’s impact on their children’s job prospects.

Danny Metters, principal and chief executive at Bishop Burton College, said: "We absolutely embrace AI and new technology at Bishop Burton College because our students need to understand and use the technology they will encounter throughout their careers.

"That applies right across our curriculum.

"We want our students to understand how technology can help them do their jobs better.

"But technology doesn't remove the need for skilled people."

Second-year agriculture student Katy Cranston echoed this sentiment, saying: "AI won’t be impacting farming as quickly as some other careers, so I can see why parents and people my age are reconsidering their career choices and perhaps looking at agriculture.

The college, based in East Yorkshire, has specialised in vocational and technical education for more than 70 years.
