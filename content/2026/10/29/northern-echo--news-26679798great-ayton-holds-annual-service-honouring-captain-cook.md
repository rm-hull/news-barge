Dignitaries and residents gathered at All Saints’ Church, Great Ayton, on Wednesday, October 28, for the annual Captain James Cook Service of Thanksgiving.

The service celebrated the life and achievements of one of Britain’s most famous explorers.

The service was led by the Reverend Jon Dean.

![](https://www.darlingtonandstocktontimes.co.uk/resources/images/21644233.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Great Ayton remembers Captain James Cook with a wreath at his High Green statue *(Image: Supplied)*

He welcomed Councillor John Ritchie, vice-chairman of North Yorkshire Council, and the Mayor of Whitby, Councillor Sandra Turner.

Gail McCracken, a trustee of the Captain Cook Schoolroom Museum Trust, spoke about Cook’s achievements and posed the question of whether he should be regarded as a hero or a villain.

In his own day, Cook was undoubtedly a celebrated figure and a national hero, although his legacy continues to prompt debate today.

![](https://www.darlingtonandstocktontimes.co.uk/resources/images/21644233.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Great Ayton remembers Captain James Cook with a wreath at his High Green statue *(Image: Supplied)*

Following the service, a wreath was laid at the statue of Captain Cook on High Green, in tribute to the explorer and his enduring place in the history of Great Ayton.

Born in 1728, Cook spent ten formative years in Great Ayton, from 1735 to 1745, before leaving the area and eventually going to sea from Whitby.

![](https://www.darlingtonandstocktontimes.co.uk/resources/images/21644236.jpg?type=mds-article-620)All Saints' Church *(Image: Supplied)*

His connection with the village remains an important part of its heritage.

The annual service is held close to Cook’s birthday and will take on particular significance in 2028, when the 300th anniversary of his birth will be commemorated.

The occasion provided an opportunity for the community to reflect on Cook’s extraordinary achievements, his complex historical legacy and the village’s enduring connection with the explorer.
