Workers in Scotland's local authorities have been left in a state of "uncertainty" with questions over their jobs and timescales for reform "unanswered", ministers have been warned.

John Swinney’s administration has proposed a major shake-up to replace Scotland's 32 local councils with a two-tier system consisting of six to 10 regional authorities and 120 to 160 smaller community bodies, alongside bespoke arrangements for island areas.

It is part of a wider plan, announced by First Minister John Swinney in his Programme for Government, to change the face of public services in Scotland which also includes reducing the number of health boards from 14 to two, and a substantial cut in the number of public bodies.

The re-organisation comes as the Scottish Government faces a £4.7 billion black hole in its finances by the end of the parliament - a situation experts and trade unions could lead to the loss of 20,000 public sector jobs.

In a new statement the Convention of Scottish Local Authorities (COSLA) along with local government trade, unions, Unison, Unite and the GMB warned that the model being proposed "is creating uncertainty for the council workforce".

It said that key questions, including about the future of jobs, transfer of staff, and potential impacts on terms and conditions, "remain unanswered" and required thorough consideration.

"Scotland needs public sector reform which improves outcomes for people, strengthens prevention, supports local democracy, creates sustainable public services, and truly values the workforce that delivers them," the statement added.

"Reform must start with outcomes, not structures, and must be underpinned by a strong evidence base and a meaningful engagement process.

"Close involvement of the workforce, recognised trade unions, and local authority employers must be central to this. Change cannot be imposed from the top down, but must be informed by the voices of those who truly understand the realities of delivering local services".

It continued: "There is also a need for realism about timescales: radical reform of local structures, services, and workforces is incredibly complex and would require detailed planning and many years of sustained work alongside significant resource."

The organisations pointed out that the plans for reorganisation process must be carried out with "meaningful consultation with staff " and respect for collective bargaining.

They said that local government has "borne the brunt of cuts over many years, and workforce capacity has not kept pace with rising demand for services".

The statement added: "If we are to create public services which can meet the needs of communities now and in future, reform must be matched with the investment required to support long-term sustainability."

Commenting on the statement, Cosla's resources spokesperson, Councillor Kelly Parry said: “Public service reform must be evidence-based and underpinned by meaningful engagement with the Local Government workforce, realism about timescales and the complexities of reform, and the investment required to support the long-term sustainability of services.

"Our joint message as Local Government employers and trade unions is clear and we stand ready to engage constructively with the Scottish Government as equal partners.”

Scotland’s 32 council leaders will meet again on Friday to consider their response to a Scottish Government’s three-month dialogue on the future of local governance and what it means for councils.

The public services shake-up has already come under robust attack by the trade unions.

Ahead of the Holyrood election Unison's Scottish Secretary Lilian Macer called the plans "dangerous".

Speaking exclusively to Ms Macer rejected claims by ministers that the job cuts would not damage frontline services as she insisted they would have a "catastrophic" impact on sectors already struggling.

"The proposals in the Scottish Government's public services reform strategy are unrealistic, they are unworkable and they are frankly dangerous and are disconnected from reality," she said.

"Pretending there will be no impact on the quality of service delivery is wrong and delusional and that needs to stop. We are sleepwalking into a crisis."

Earlier this month Public Service Reform Secretary Ivan McKee faced a brutal backlash against his plans at the SNP conference, with party councillors accusing the minister of sidelining communities.

Earlier this week a coalition of more than 50 organisations warned that care has only “limited recognition” in the Scottish Government’s reform strategy, arguing that its absence risked undermining ministers’ ambition to shift public services towards prevention.

Ms Macer told The Herald: “For any public sector reforms, you cannot fix or try and fix health without looking at social care. It cannot be separated.”

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: “Public service reform will help to ensure investment is focused on frontline delivery to empower communities and deliver real improvements in public services.

"Some decision-making is best delivered at national level and regional level while others better sit closer to communities and we must get that balance right.

“Our proposals provide a starting point to facilitate debate during three months of dialogue with partners, including councils and trade unions, to inform the next steps.

"No decisions have been taken on future structures. Once formal reform proposals are developed, appropriate consultation will follow in the usual manner.”
