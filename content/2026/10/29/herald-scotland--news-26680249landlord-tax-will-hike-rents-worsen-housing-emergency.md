Jenny Gilruth has been warned copying John Healey's new tax on landlords will hike rents and exacerbate Scotland's housing emergency.

In his first Budget on Wednesday, the Chancellor of the Exchequer increased the rates of income tax applied to property income by 2%, adding to the 2% rise announced by Rachel Reeves last year.

The move will apply to landlords in England, Wales and Northern Ireland and will take effect from 6 April 2027.

It will not apply to landlords in Scotland, as taxes on property income and income tax is devolved to Holyrood.

It will be up to the Deputy First Minister to decide whether to bring in the new landlord tax when she unveils her first Budget as Finance Secretary on December 3.

The Scottish Association of Landlords (SAL), which represents landlords and letting agents, said it believed "any increases in property tax will impact negatively on tenants as their costs increase and fewer properties are available for rent as landlords leave the sector and fewer are willing to join.

The SAL said the Office for Budget Responsibility’s Economic and Fiscal Outlook had warned that the tax would risk increasing rents.

It pointed to the OBR warning which states: "These measures…represent a material reduction in returns to private landlords. This follows various measures over the past 10 years that have also reduced private landlord returns. These measures collectively risk leading to a steady rise in rents through two effects.

"First, potential landlords are less likely to enter the market, reducing long-run rental supply, which risks a steady long-term rise in rents if demand outstrips supply – which we think is more likely than not.

"Second, landlords are likely to pass on more of these costs to renters in the long-run through a combination of some current landlords leaving, thereby giving more price setting power to remaining landlords, and greater long-run flexibility to raise rents.”

SAL chief executive John Blackwood said: “Introducing a property income tax in Scotland could be the final straw for many private landlords.

"The increased level of financial strain makes operating margins increasingly fragile for those investing in the private rented sector. With around 90% of landlords in Scotland owning just one or two properties, this might mean that being a landlord is no longer financially viable. If landlords choose to sell up, it will put the supply of much needed rented housing in jeopardy."

![Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey delivering his Budget in the House of Commons on Westminster.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21644985.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chancellor of the Exchequer John Healey delivering his Budget in the House of Commons on Westminster. *(Image: House of Commons/Press Association)*

He said any new landlord tax could be introduced in addition to new rent controls which will limit annual rent increases to inflation plus 1%, with a maximum cap of 6% in areas officially designated as "rent control areas".

Ministers decide which areas are designated rent control areas for up to five years after councils assess local rent conditions. Decisions are expected within the next two years.

Mr Blackwood added that a new landlord tax along with rent controls "will put further pressure on landlords".

He added: "The Scottish Government now needs to consider whether to follow suit and introduce similar tax changes in Scotland. "However, such a move will prove equally unpopular with already nervous investors and no doubt further exacerbate Scotland’s housing emergency.

“Now, more than ever, the Scottish Government needs to work with landlords to keep them in the sector, rather than frightening them off with punitive tax hikes.”

The Scottish Government declared a national housing emergency in May 2024 after representatives from the housing sector, local government, and the trade union movement wrote an open letter to all MSPs urging them to come together to declare a housing emergency and commit to taking action to end it.

Campaigners want more affordable homes to be built to tackle the emergency.

The Scottish Government has pledged to build 110,000 affordable homes by 2032, but official statistics show the target is significantly off track, with just 32,479 homes delivered to that target so far.

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: “Decisions on taxation will be set out as part of the Scottish Budget on 3 December.”
