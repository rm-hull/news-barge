Poundland is on the brink of administration, putting around 12,000 jobs at risk, it is understood.

The retailer, which has stores at The Broadway, Forster Square, Enterprise Way in Idle and elsewhere in the Bradford district, is set to file a notice of intention to appoint administrators, which will give the business 10 working days of breathing space from creditors to secure a potential rescue deal.

High street discounter Poundland has filed to appoint administrators, threatening the future of around 12,000 workers.

Court filings show that the retailer has filed a notice of intention to appoint administrators on Thursday afternoon, which will give the business 10 working days of breathing space from creditors to secure a potential rescue deal.

Advisers are still in talks with parties interested in buying the brand and swathes of stores.

Poundland managing director Barry Williams said: “Despite enormous efforts it has not been possible to identify an acceptable offer for the business as a whole.

“The process has however attracted several established retail operators who have made formal offers.

"Collectively these cover a significant part of the business, and we believe offer the best available prospects for shareholders, creditors and to preserve jobs.

“We recognise any sale process creates uncertainty and particularly when set against a challenging UK economic environment.”

He added that the retailer will be working closely with its advisers to “identify and deliver the best possible outcome”.

It is understood corporate advisory firm AlixPartners will be appointed as administrators.

The discount chain is due to enter insolvency after owner Gordon Brothers rejected a takeover offer from Irish real estate firm Martin Property Group, which management named as their preferred bidder earlier this month.

Gordon Brothers – which only bought Poundland last year for £1 – launched a sale process for the retailer at the start of September.

Wilko and The Range owner CDS Superstores, Poundstretcher owner Fortress and TG Jones owner Modella are all understood to be interested in buying parts of the Poundland business.

It is understood the retailer has appointed advisers at AlixPartners to oversee the insolvency process and assess offers to buy all or parts of the business.

Gordon Brothers had overseen a major turnaround programme at Poundland that included closing more than 100 shops over the past year in an effort to shore up its finances.

Poundland stressed that all its stores remain open and trading as normal despite the insolvency process.
