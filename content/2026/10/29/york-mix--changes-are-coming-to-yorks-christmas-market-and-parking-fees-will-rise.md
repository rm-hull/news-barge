Changes are coming to York’s Christmas Market this year.

The market, which gets underway in November, is set to close on Tuesdays to help manage the impact of a ban on city centre traffic including on Blue Badge holders.

Parking charges in City of York Council-run car parks and for on-street spaces will be set at ten per cent extra events rates for the duration of the market, including on Tuesdays.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, the council’s Labour transport spokesperson, said they were contacting affected residents and businesses so they could plan ahead for the festive period.

It comes ahead of the market’s opening on Thursday, 12 November, with the event set to run until Monday, 21 December.

## Parking charges

Parking charges in council-run car parks and for on-street spaces, except suburban ones, will rise to £5.50-an-hour for the first two while the market is on.

Stays of up to five hours will costs £21.10 until the market is over.

## Traffic ban

Blue Badge holders and other drivers, other than a few exceptions such as emergency services vehicles, will be barred from driving into York’s footstreets while the market is open.

The restrictions come as part of an Anti-Terror Traffic Regulation Order (ATTRO) which will be active during the market’s opening hours from 10.30am to 7.30pm.

## Tuesday closures

On Tuesdays, the market will close and usual footstreet hours of 10.30am to 5pm will apply.

The Goodramgate, King’s Square, Colliergate loop will be available for Blue Badge holders and city centre shops, attractions and businesses will be open as normal.

Blue Badge holders will be able to access the footstreets on Tuesdays through the bollards at the Goodramgate and Deangate junction.

Temporary hostile vehicle measures will be in place in Low Petergate King’s Square and Church Street, meaning the latter will not be open to Blue Badge holders including on Tuesdays.

The Blake Street loop will also be closed to Blue Badge holders during footstreet hours on Tuesdays and until 7.30pm every other day.

## Police powers

Trial arrangements for this year follow a decision in September to make powers temporarily used during last year’s Christmas Market permanent.

The ATTRO covering York city centre mostly lies dormant but the council, North Yorkshire Police and others can activate it on a case-by-case basis during events or in response to specific threats.

Tuesday rest days were proposed this year after disabled people said last year’s measures restricted their access to the city centre in the run up to Christmas.

Police officers said the measures were needed to deter potential vehicle attacks targeting mass gatherings which have been seen elsewhere.

Market stall holders have previously warned closing the event on Tuesday would cut into their earnings and potentially threaten jobs.

Cllr Claire Douglas, the council’s Labour leader, said in September the issue was difficult and sensitive and a balance had to be struck.

Cllr Ravilious said this year’s arrangements aimed to make access easier for everyone and to support people who live and work in the city.
