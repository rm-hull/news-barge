Key bullet points for the region include:

* All business rates to be retained locally by the end of the decade, with mayors to keep 20% of revenue from next year
* A share of income tax receipts to go to mayors from 2028
* Regional bodies and mayors in England to get greater powers over spending, starting with £285m next year for things like housing, transport and High Streets
* Government to work with mayors to design a £1.5bn borrowing facility, enabling investment in larger projects

John McCabe, chief executive of the North East Chamber of Commerce, said: “This first Budget from the new Chancellor takes small steps rather than big leaps towards unlocking the North East economy.

We are pleased to see our main ask – no new taxes on businesses – heeded by the Chancellor. The retail offer to households may help offer working people and families with some of the breathing space we know they need. But for now, the government has potentially offered stability at the expense of dynamism and this may be a missed opportunity for our region.

“On behalf of our members, we have consistently called on the government to reduce the cost of doing business and create an environment which gives businesses the confidence to invest, innovate, export and create good jobs. Interventions on regulations and tariffs, financial support to help young entrepreneurs and female business founders, and support for our region’s strengths in clean energy are welcome, as is the continued business rates relief targeted at our high streets. We look forward to hearing more on skills and economic inactivity in the forthcoming reports later in the year.

“There were also some notable wins for the North East specifically in this Budget. The government prioritising delivery of mature carbon capture, usage and storage and hydrogen projects at Teesside will boost these important regional projects. Funding for the regeneration of Gateshead Quays, the £85 million investment to transform the Offshore Renewable Energy Catapult’s facilities in Blyth, and the announcement that Gateshead will be hosting the government’s International Investment Summit are all wins.

“We will be looking at the Fiscal Devolution Roadmap, launched alongside the Budget, to better understand the big announcement on local retention of business rates and income tax receipts. Unlocking this investment must offer a net benefit for both our local authorities and our Mayoral Strategic Authorities to deliver good growth right across the North East – but only if genuine rebalancing is achieved both between and within regions.

Mark Stephenson, Managing Director of Stephenson-Mohl Group in Newcastle told BUSINESSiQ: “As expected, details on areas such as defence and welfare spending have been deferred to the Spending Review.

“Defence presents a huge opportunity for North East and Northern businesses, particularly in terms of inward investment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20677519.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mark Stephenson, Managing Director of Stephenson-Mohl Group *(Image: Stephenson-Mohl)*

“The housing measures are very positive, although there is a danger that sales will dip in the meantime as buyers wait for new packages of support to arrive. Pace will be important.

“There were also some pragmatic measures on critical infrastructure, including data centres and co-located generation, alongside exciting ambitions for business rates.

“In short it was a Budget constrained by global economics and politics but delivered by a Chancellor who is clearly pro business, pro wealth creation and pragmatic to his bones. It will therefore help foster investor confidence at all levels of the private sector.”

Nik Tunley, a partner and head of corporate at Ward Hadaway, said business owners across the North East will be weighing up the pros and cons of John Healey's first Budget, such as Business Asset Disposal Relief and a new founders’ relief:

“Today’s cut to the Business Asset Disposal Relief threshold, from £1m to £500,000, will make a difference to when, and how, small business owners decide to sell. The Chancellor says three in four claimants will see no change, but for those affected, a gain of £1m or more now means around £30,000 more tax, which matters far more to the seller of a smaller business than it does on a £50m deal.

“There appears to be a positive in the new founders’ relief, a 10% rate on lifetime gains up to £10m for the founders of Britain’s most successful firms. We will need to assess the detail, but hopefully it will support our entrepreneurs to build and grow. The work we’re doing with our clients tells us there’s still an appetite to do deals, so what matters now is that the UK stays an attractive place to build, buy and sell businesses.

Owners want to be rewarded for the risk they’ve taken, and the more that holds true, the more deals will be done here.

“This isn’t just about today’s announcement; we are continually talking to clients about the need to plan ahead. What business owners need to do is to think about where they want the business to be in five years and plan for it now, rather than waiting for announcements and being forced into a decision.”

On devolution, Alexander Rose, Partner, Public Funding, said: “North East businesses will be watching how fiscal devolution for Metro Mayors translates into investment on the ground. The Budget is a ‘give and take’ package, with devolution being the area where ‘give’ is the most visible.

“There were commitments to individual regeneration projects but the moment where ‘a new wave of devolution’ was genuinely unleashed was the commitment to fiscal devolution for Metro Mayors, starting with 100% retention of business rates growth by the end of the decade, but also a share of income tax receipts.

“The main surprise was the lack of additional scrutiny accompanying this money, to make sure every pound spent on regeneration achieves ‘bang for its buck’, but perhaps that will be set out in due course. The real test for the regions will be whether the office tax and the surcharge change the appetite to lend and invest outside London.”

**Dan Brookes, partner in Yorkshire and the North East at BDO, told us:** “Confidence, stability and investment is what the region’s businesses were looking for today. According to BDO’s Mid-Market Tracker, 71% of Yorkshire’s businesses went into this Budget with confidence that the Government would reflect the current and future needs of their business in today’s statement.

“In Yorkshire, 71% were also upbeat on the positive impact on their business over the next three years about giving regional mayors and local authorities greater control over locally raised tax revenues, through a share of income tax and business rates. As the Chancellor announced “Good Growth Funds" for mayors, and "incubator capital" for areas without mayors to support local communities, Yorkshire business leaders will welcome this news.

“As well as investment in the region, access to capital to expand, and new announcements such as the founder’s relief, entrepreneurs need the government to build on today's announcements by simplifying taxes so they can focus on delivering innovation and driving growth.”

**Zoë Billingham, director of IPPR North, the leading think tank in the North of England, says:**

”This Budget is a vote of confidence in our regional and local leaders. We’ve taken a jump ahead on devolution, but to keep up with our peers this will need to be the beginning of radical change to rewire the state.

“The government has stuck to its promise to speed up progress on fiscal devolution with commitments on devolving business rates and a share of income tax to England’s mayors. Additional regional investment through £285million of good growth funds, funding for places without a mayor, alongside £1.5billion borrowing facilities for building infrastructure backs up the government’s words with actions.

“IPPR North urges the government to ensure that income tax devolution is ambitious enough to truly make a difference in peoples’ lives. Five per cent of income tax devolved to mayors would see an additional £4billion held by mayors.

“When the devolution agenda is felt by our communities across the country we know it will have succeeded.”

Stephen Slater, managing partner at Gosforth-based RMT Accountants & Business Advisors, which is part of the Sumer Group, says: “The Prime Minister has been clear in his desire to get out of the blocks as quickly as possible in delivering the change that he’s promised and the breadth of initiatives announced in the Chancellor’s Budget speech show how this is going to happen in practice.

“With the retail, hospitality and leisure sectors being so important to the overall success of the North East economy, there will be appreciation of the 50 per cent increase in rate relief, though this clearly won’t address the challenges they’re facing in full.

“Many of the cost of living support measures that were announced in the Budget will hopefully give consumers a little bit more disposable income, which these businesses and the region’s retailers will be hoping to see making their way into their tills, especially in the run up to Christmas.

“Qualifying businesses will welcome the support that has been announced around spiralling energy costs, while transport firms and other North East businesses with large vehicle fleets will gain a degree of much-needed relief from the extension of the freeze on fuel duty.

“The North East has never been short of entrepreneurial spirit, with the measures announced to underpin several different stages of the entrepreneurial journey likely to make a tangible impact in the region.

“The new Young Entrepreneurs’ Allowance and the targeted scale-up programme for female founders will grab a lot of attention here, as will the second phase of the Enterprise Management Incentive and the new Founder’s Allowance will be of great interest to those who are further along in their business careers or looking to exit for the companies they’ve built.

“On a similar theme, the reduction of Business Asset Disposal Relief may have an impact on the plans of business owners who were looking to sell in the near future.

“The additional funding being directed to the British Business Bank will also provide the region’s business owners with more capacity to access the investment capital they require to put their growth plans into action in what is already an active business finance market across the region.

“Spending promises are being very clearly linked to tax receipts, with the investment in HMRC’s compliance and tax collection capacity clearly designed to maximise the tax revenues that can be directed into the Treasury’s coffers and highlighting the importance of businesses and individuals ensuring their tax affairs are in proper order.”

Dr Arnab Basu, chief executive of Sedgefield-based technology company Kromek, said: “Continued investment in defence and national security is essential, particularly as the threats facing the UK become increasingly complex.

“Here in the North East, we have world-class expertise in developing technologies that can help protect the country, including systems capable of detecting biological threats. It's important that the Government continues to support British innovation, helping businesses bring new technologies to market while creating highly skilled jobs and supporting economic growth.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20096986.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Karl Pemberton of Active Financial Planners *(Image: Active)*

Karl Pemberton, managing director at Active Chartered Financial Planners said: “It’s hard not to be hypocritical. Before the Budget I was hoping there would be no big sledgehammers just to crack a nut and ultimately that’s what we got. But I do feel slightly deflated, it was boring and didn’t actually address anything we needed it to. There was lots of tinkering round the edges and things that sound good on paper, but the devil will be in the detail as to whether they deliver anything that will make a meaningful difference.”

Aman Chahal, CEO of Stockton-based flat roofing design specialist TaperedPlus, said: “There’s quite a bit to welcome around innovation and investing more in the regions, but the important thing is making sure it actually reaches businesses on the ground.

“We’re investing in AI and working with Teesside University to look at how technology can improve the way we work. That experience has highlighted just how valuable collaboration between business, universities and government-backed innovation programmes can be.

“I’m also pleased to see more recognition that economic growth must happen outside London and the South East. The North East has some fantastic universities, talented people, and ambitious businesses, but hasn’t always had the same access to investment.

“The £150m being made available through the British Business Bank for Northern scale-ups is encouraging, but the funding must be accessible.”

Kiran Fothergill, chair of Jobs Foundation North East, said: “Today’s Budget was a missed opportunity for the more than one million young people who are not in education, employment or training.

“We welcome the decision to raise the small business rates relief threshold for retail, hospitality and leisure from £12,000 to £18,000, which will help the high street and hospitality employers who give so many young people their first job. Slowing the rise in youth minimum wage rates also recognises the pressure on entry-level hiring.

“But these steps fall short of what is needed. Youth unemployment now stands at 15.6%, with 54,000 more young people out of work than a year ago, yet the Chancellor had little to say about getting them into jobs.

“Decisions on welfare and youth unemployment have once again been pushed back while the Government waits for the Timms review and Sir Alan Milburn’s review of young people and work. Every month of delay means more young people missing the chance to start their working lives, and the longer someone is out of work at the start of their career, the harder it becomes to get back in.

“Employers want to hire young people and have been clear about what would help. Extending employer National Insurance relief to all under-25s, a change the Work and Pensions Committee called for in July, would bring down the cost of taking on a young person straight away.

“We urge the Government to act now, and we stand ready to work with ministers and employers to get young people into work.”

Bob Borthwick, a director of Teesside-based recycling specialist Scott Bros, said: “I understand why the Government is providing further relief on red diesel, but there are plenty of businesses like ours no longer permitted to use it but are still facing rocketing fuel prices.

“We lost the right to use red diesel across our operations in 2022 and had to switch to fully taxed fuel, which added significantly to our costs. Diesel prices have risen sharply this year and, when you’re running trucks, vans and heavy plant, that soon adds up.

“You can only absorb so much of those increases, particularly when businesses are competing hard for work and can’t simply pass every extra cost on to customers.

“I also welcome anything that helps get more worthwhile infrastructure projects off the ground here on Teesside and across the North East. There’s a lot of talk about spreading investment around the country, and what businesses here want to see now is that turning into projects, jobs and opportunities on the ground.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21642875.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Baggaley, Head of Tax and Tax Partner at Clive Owen *(Image: Clive Owen)*

David Baggaley, Head of Tax and Tax Partner at Clive Owen LLP, added: "The Autumn Budget is a significant one, being the first under Andy Burnham’s leadership, but I would describe it as balanced rather than transformational.

“Despite speculation ahead of the announcement about substantial changes to tax rates, many of those anticipated increases have not come about. That is welcome news and should reassure businesses and individuals alike.

“There are positive measures for businesses and households, including support with energy costs and an increase in the minimum wage. However, the latter will inevitably place additional pressure on employers already managing rising operating costs.

“For businesses across the North East, particularly those in the hospitality and leisure sectors, the increase in business rates relief from £12,000 to £18,000 is encouraging and will provide welcome support.

“However, there are areas of disappointment in relation to further tax increases on property and savings income, which will impact savers. Furthermore, the government has announced a reduction in Business Asset Disposal Relief from £1 million to £500,000 for entrepreneurs. At a time when we should be encouraging entrepreneurship and business growth, this setback will affect some business owners looking to sell soon.

“On a more positive note, the announcement of Founders Relief is an encouraging step towards supporting people looking to establish new businesses, with the potential for future capital gains and income tax benefits and we look forward to understanding the details on how this will work.

“Ultimately, what businesses across our region are looking for is stability. Confidence is essential to investment, and investment is what will drive sustainable economic growth.

“While this Budget may not deliver sweeping changes, its relatively measured approach could provide the certainty businesses need to plan ahead, invest and grow. That, in itself, is a positive outcome for the North East economy.”
