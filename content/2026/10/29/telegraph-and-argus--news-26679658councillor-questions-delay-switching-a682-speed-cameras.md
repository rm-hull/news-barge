A COUNCILLOR has questioned why average speed cameras between Gisburn and the North Yorkshire border are still to be switched on - two years after they were installed.

The fixed cameras on the A682 were installed by Lancashire County Council in September, 2024.

They were part of a package of measures, also including solar-powered road studs, aimed at improving safety along the A682 from Barrowford to Long Preston and were paid for with £449,000 from the Government's Safer Roads Fund.

But, while the cameras between Barrowford and Gisburn are in operation, the cameras between Gisburn and the Lancashire/North Yorkshire border are yet to be switched on, due to what a meeting of Lancashire County Council heard was a 'snagging list'.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21624468.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Average speed cameras on the A682 - still not turned on *(Image: Google)*

Councillor David Whipp, who represents Pendle Rural, told last week's Environment, Economic Growth and Transport Scrutiny Committee that it would be 'very unfortunate' if someone was killed on the road before the cameras were turned on.

Cllr Whipp said: "We installed average speed cameras from Barrowford to Gisburn some time ago and they have been hugely successful.

"The number of crashes and fatalities, and a lot of these are motorcycle crashes, have reduced significantly."

The cameras have been installed in the next section through to Long Preston two years ago, but they don't appear to be operational, so why has £450,000 been invested in these average speed cameras but they are not turned on."

Peter Bell, highways regulation manager, told the meeting the intention is to turn the cameras on, but that the council was in the hands of Lancashire Police.

"We don't put stuff up just to leave it sat there doing nothing," he said.

Mr Bell, who said he did not have the exact details to hand, added: "There were issues with the picture quality from the supplier and the police were not satisfied with the video footage that they were collecting; there are various bits of signing, we are in the hands of the police in terms of what they find acceptable for enforcement as obviously, they provide the enforcement whereas we provide the investment for the cameras.

"They like to have everything 100 per cent, so all the relevant signs and foliage cut back so it is great visibility.

"There could just be a snagging list that they are working on."

Mr Bell told Cllr Whipp he would find the exact reason for the delay and get back to him.

Cllr Whipp added while he was grateful, "The more general point is, obviously, multi-year delays in actually bringing these safety schemes in; it would be very unfortunate if someone was killed in the meantime."

In October a spokesperson for Lancashire Police told the Craven Herald: "We would never reveal the operational status of any enforcement equipment. It is not in the public interest to do so; drivers should be adhering to the law regardless."
