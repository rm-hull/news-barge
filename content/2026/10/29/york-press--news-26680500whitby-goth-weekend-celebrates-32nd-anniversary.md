## Hundreds of goths expected in North Yorkshire town this weekend

A man attends the Whitby Goth Weekend in Whitby, Yorkshire, where Bram Stoker found some of his inspiration for 'Dracula' after staying in the town in 1890 in 2018
. *(Image: PA)*

HUNDREDS of Goths are expected to flock to a North Yorkshire town which celebrates its 32nd anniversary this weekend.

Whitby Goth Weekend started in 1994 with Jo Hampshire putting an advert in NME magazine asking if any goths fancied meeting in the town around the weekend of Halloween.

Now, the bi-annual weekend attracts participants from all over as a celebration of gothic culture.

Whitby is also famous as the town where Bram Stoker sourced ideas for his vampire book, Dracula.

The author visited the harbour town in 1890 - and viewed the ruined monasteries and graveyards as the perfect setting to feature his blood-sucking vampire.

In the novel, and taking the shape of a black dog, Stoker’s most infamous character ran up the 199 Whitby steps into the shadow’s of the Abbey’s ruins.

The Goth weekender is now thought to be one of the most famous gothic events in the world.

The event runs from from October 30 to November 1.
