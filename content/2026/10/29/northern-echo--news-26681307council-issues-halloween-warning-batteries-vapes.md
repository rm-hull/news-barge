A fire in a rubbish lorry has sparked fresh safety warnings about the dangers of putting batteries and disposable vapes in household bins.

The blaze broke out in a Durham County Council refuse vehicle in Shildon earlier this month and is believed to have been caused by lithium batteries or vapes igniting after being crushed during collection.

Durham County Council says this incident is a reminder of the risks, especially in the lead-up to Halloween.

A council spokesperson said: "Don't let a Halloween scare become a real-life danger.

"While Halloween is all about spooky surprises, fires in our collection vehicles are no trick.

"Batteries and vapes contain lithium and other materials that can ignite when crushed during collection, putting our crews and the public at serious risk."

Photographs shared by the council show the aftermath of the fire, which broke out during a routine collection round in Shildon.

Lithium-ion batteries are commonly found in disposable vapes, mobile phones, laptops, and rechargeable household devices.

When damaged or crushed, these batteries can overheat and catch fire, quickly spreading through loads of waste and endangering both workers and the public.

The council is urging residents not to place batteries or vapes in general waste bins.

Instead, they should be bagged and left alongside food waste caddies for separate collection.

Alternatively, they can be taken to a Household Waste Recycling Centre or a community Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) collection point.

The warning comes as many households prepare for Halloween.

The council hopes the reminder will help prevent similar incidents in the future.

The spokesperson said: "This Halloween, keep the scares on the doorstep, not in the back of a bin wagon."
