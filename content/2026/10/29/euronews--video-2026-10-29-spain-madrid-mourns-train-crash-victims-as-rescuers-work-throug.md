As night fell over the crash site near **Urda**, rescue teams continued working among the mangled carriages, using** heavy cranes to lift sections of wreckage** from the tracks. Footage shows a crane hoisting what appears to be part of a train's wheel assembly, while emergency workers search the damaged carriages under artificial lighting.

The disaster, which occurred when **one train struck another that had stopped**, is** Spain's second deadly rail collision this year** and one of Europe's deadliest railway accidents of the century.

In Madrid, dozens gathered outside City Hall in **Cibeles Square for a minute of silence** in memory of the victims. The tribute, organised by the Spanish Federation of Municipalities and Provinces, was attended by opposition leader**Alberto Núñez Feijóo**, Madrid regional president** Isabel Díaz Ayuso**, Mayor** José Luis Martínez-Almeida**and central government delegate** Pilar Trinidad**. But the gathering also became a protest against the government, with some participants holding placards reading**"Corruption kills"** and chanting for**Transport Minister Óscar Puente to resign**.

**At least 68 people have died** and**more than 100 have been injured** in a collision between two high-speed trains near**Toledo, central Spain, on 28 October**.** Fifteen of the injured remain in critical condition**.
