Cracked planters that sparked safety fears are finally being removed from a North East high street.

Work is finally underway to take down the defective concrete planters from Redcar High Street after cracks appeared in the structures.

![Redcar Town Centre. 31.3.2026. Photograph: Stuart Boulton/RCBC](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646206.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Controversial concrete planters in Redcar to be removed. *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Temporary barriers will go up this week as workers remove the plants and trees, many of which will be replanted in Locke Park or elsewhere in Redcar.

Once the greenery is cleared, a secure compound will be created to allow the planters themselves to be taken away.

Redcar councillor Alec Brown said: "The time has come to say goodbye to the marmite planters.

"Whilst chosen before my time, I have to defend previous Redcar Town Deal board members: they looked a lot better in the drawings. Being defective was the last straw, they had to go."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646205.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Concept image for what could replace the planters *(Image: Cllr Alec Brown)*

Installed earlier this year, the planters featured seating and etchings reflecting Redcar’s links to Samuel Plimsoll but quickly divided opinion.

They were criticised for their appearance before visible cracks emerged, prompting temporary measures to stabilise them amid safety fears.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646203.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Concept image for what could replace the planters *(Image: Cllr Alec Brown)*

The planters were commissioned after a public consultation by the Redcar Town Deal board, which oversees £20m of Government investment in the town.

The council allocated £77,000 to the planters but withheld payment from the contractor after defects emerged.

The council said the faults were not isolated, but resulted from a wider design and manufacturing defect affecting multiple planters.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646204.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Concept image for what could replace the planters *(Image: Cllr Alec Brown)*

The planters will be removed through the contract at no extra cost to taxpayers.

Cllr Brown said: "We appreciate everyone's patience while these works are carried out. Public safety remains our priority, and we're pleased that many of the plants and trees will be given a new home in Locke Park."

The council is now preparing a public consultation on the replacements, with different designs, colours, shapes and seating arrangements under consideration.

Cllr Brown previously said the replacements should be "smaller and more visually appealing."

Businesses will stay open as usual during the removal work.
