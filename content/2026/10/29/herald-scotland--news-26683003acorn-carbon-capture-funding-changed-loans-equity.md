Business leaders have accused UK ministers of leaving the Acorn carbon capture project in political limbo after they gave differing accounts of what the Budget means for its funding.

Russell Borthwick, chief executive of Aberdeen & Grampian Chamber of Commerce, described it as a "spectacular shambles".

However, carbon capture and storage projects in England and Wales had been prioritised.

That was despite the then UK Energy Secretary Ed Miliband promising the scheme £200 million last year.

The news prompted fury in the North East, with the local SNP MP describing it as "the gravest betrayal of Scotland's energy sector".

Scottish Secretary Douglas Alexander defended the decision telling journalists after the Budget that there were "significant challenges" around the project and ministers had a "responsibility to think carefully about the use of public money".

However, his colleague in the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero , Martin McCluskey, took to social media to say the grant funding allocated "remains in place" but that it would "be delivered through financial transactions - loans/equity stakes - to deliver greater value for money for taxpayers".

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19062519.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A carbon capture and storage plant in Norway (Leon Neal/PA)

And then on Thursday morning, Emma Reynolds, the Chief Secretary to the Treasury, told the BBC the government was "honouring the £200 million that was announced".

She added: "We're going to work with the Scottish Government and others to push forward with this project, and there hasn't been a cut to the £200 million. I can reassure you that that commitment stands."

Asked whether it would be paid as grant funding, Ms Reynolds said: "That is the grant funding, but there could well be also loans or stakes taken, because we will need, probably, additional funding to ensure the future of this project."

Put to her that the grant was therefore secure, with any further funding on top, she replied: "That's what I'm saying. We're not cutting the grant funding, and we will probably need to look at some sort of financial transactions to secure the rest of the project."

That, however, is not correct. The Herald understands that only some of the money has been paid as grants, with the remainder to come through government loans or equity investment.

Mr McCluskey sent a letter to Scottish Government Transport Minister Stephen Flynn, after the SNP MSP posted a video on X titled The Burnham Betrayal.

> What Stephen doesn’t say here. Funding is still on offer. It’s now loans and equity funding meaning that the taxpayer would get a return.

Mr McCluskey said: "Government funding for Acorn remains. Acorn is currently in receipt of grant funding to support project needs until the end of the year.

"The change announced yesterday concerns the mechanism for further financial support, where we are moving from capital grants to financial transactions - government loans or equity investment — that need to see the taxpayer receive a return, and like all such cases, will be subject to a business case."

Mr McCluskey added: "A change in the form of government investment should not be presented as the removal of UK Government support for Acorn. The project itself has confirmed that yesterday's announcement does not change its current development programme and that it will continue working with both governments on funding for its next phase."

He then turned on the Scottish Government's reaction to the Budget, saying: "I am concerned by commentary from the Scottish Government suggesting that Acorn has been scrapped or that there is no longer Government support available.

"That is not true and risks undermining confidence at precisely the point when we should be helping the project secure the investment and partnerships it needs. I urge you to avoid talking the project down and to make clear that there remains a route to further government support."

That plea sits awkwardly with the remarks made by his UK Government colleague the day before. Mr Alexander said: "Respectfully, there have been significant challenges in relation to Acorn - both in relation to the market-readiness of the technology and in relation to the commercial arrangements around the project.

"We have a responsibility to think carefully about the use of public money at the same time as ensuring that we continue to facilitate the transition of the North Sea ."

He added: "That is but one piece of the jigsaw. There are other steps that we are taking in terms of the North Sea transition, but we have a responsibility to look at each project on a case by case basis, and we've reached important judgments in relation to Acorn."

When Ms Reynolds was asked about his comments, she declined to say whether he was right. She said: "Well, obviously as a chief secretary, I need to be reassured that any project has value for money. That's something that we look at very carefully."

Asked whether the Treasury had identified any challenges before it allocated the money, she replied: "Well, we allocated that money, as I said, and we stand by that commitment."

First Minister John Swinney told MSPs at Holyrood on Thursday that he was concerned by the funding row. Mr Swinney said: "What the Acorn carbon capture project requires just now is certainty - certainty of direction, certainty of time, certainty on funding. The UK Government has added significant confusion to all of those elements and has undermined the certainty that was in place."

Mr Borthwick said: "This is a spectacular shambles. Ministers are contradicting one another over Acorn, leaving businesses and investors trying to decipher what the Government's own Budget actually means.

"At the heart of this confusion is a project critical to the future of North East Scotland's industrial transition, with thousands of jobs and billions of pounds of investment dependent on getting it right.

"Acorn, Scotland's only carbon capture cluster, cannot be left in political limbo. Ministers must urgently set out a clear funding plan and timetable for delivery, and explain why its funding terms are being changed while grant support for other clusters is protected."

![Martin McCluskey](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/13776024.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Martin McCluskey wrote to the Scottish Government urging them to stop talking down Acorn *(Image: Martin McCluskey)*

On Wednesday, an Acorn spokesperson said: "Today's Budget announcement does not change Acorn's current development programme, which continues with UK Government support through grant funding announced in the June 2025 Spending Review.

"We will continue to work closely with the UK and Scottish Governments to agree the next phase of the project, including securing long-term funding via loan or equity investment, as outlined in the Budget 2026 and subject to business case and approval."
