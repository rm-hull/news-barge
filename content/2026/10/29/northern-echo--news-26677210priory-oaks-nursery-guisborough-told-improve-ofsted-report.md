A North Yorkshire nursery must improve after inspectors found children were not always getting the support they needed.

Priory Oaks Nursery, based at Prior Pursglove College in Guisborough, is part of Tees Valley Collaborative Enterprises Limited.

The nursery was rated "needs attention" after its latest Ofsted inspection on Tuesday, September 29.

Safeguarding standards were met, but inspectors raised concerns about children's learning, particularly their communication and language skills.

While older children consistently took part in activities to build their vocabulary, younger children did not consistently receive the support needed to build strong educational foundations.

As the nursery's new curriculum had not yet been fully embedded, inspectors said the quality of teaching varied, with staff not always focusing on areas where individual children needed the most support.

Inspectors also found that babies and young children sometimes waited so long for lunch that they became restless and unsettled.

During the inspection, a lapse in safer eating procedures was identified when staff briefly left the table to collect children's water bottles, although leaders addressed the issue immediately and reminded staff of the procedures.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641148.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Priory Oaks Nursery is based at Prior Pursglove College in Guisborough *(Image: Google)*

Inspectors also highlighted a number of positives, including a passion by the children for literature and a connection to the staff.

While older children were developing some of the skills needed for starting school, babies and toddlers were also well supported to build their physical skills and independence, inspectors found.

Staff were described as effective role models who generally promoted positive behaviour, while parents told inspectors they were "very happy" with the support provided around children's behaviour and routines.

Overall, inspectors praised the nursery's relationships with families, saying leaders and staff "regularly go above and beyond to help where possible".

Children also ate healthy meals and snacks, played outdoors every day and sometimes even visited an allotment to make soup with vegetables they had grown.

While these changes to the nursery's environment, resources and curriculum were still in the early stages, Ofsted said parents had noticed improvements in the nursery community and their relationships with staff.

Nursery manager Charlotte Armstrong said the setting had undergone major changes and was already acting on Ofsted's recommendations.

She said: "We have undergone a rebrand, name change, written and implemented a new curriculum, and completed a whole nursery makeover, which has involved the children and families every step of the way.

"We have also made positive steps to reintroduce the nursery into the local community with different open events such as our summer fairs, stay and play sessions, and open evenings for working parents."

She added: "Although disappointed with the report outcome, the actions set by Ofsted are very achievable and we are already working very hard to meet these actions and improve our offer even more."

Despite the positives, the nursery failed to meet the compulsory Childcare Register requirements and was ordered to improve teaching, embed its curriculum, follow strategies to close learning gaps and train staff in communication and language development.

Ms Armstrong added: "The support we have received from parents has been incredible and has shown us, as a team, how valued our nursery is."
