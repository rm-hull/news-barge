Donald Trump has not ruled out getting involved in Israel’s efforts to form a coalition government after the election, saying he would “take a look” at the matter.

A US official said the president had not yet decided whether the US would intervene in efforts to assemble the next Israeli government, adding that reports that Washington preferred a specific outcome were “false and premature”.

Trump has previously given his backing to Benjamin Netanyahu and his Likud party, who earlier claimed that Tuesday’s elections were “stolen” by Arab parties after his right-wing bloc fell short of a governing majority.

The unsubstantiated allegations attack an Arab bloc that claimed a historic 19 seats in the 120-seat parliament, potentially positioning it as kingmaker in coalition talks.

But Balad party leader Sami Abu Shehadeh accused Israel’s main political leaders of “racism” for refusing to form a coalition with Arab-led parties.

“This is racism,” he told *Al Jazeera*. “We are a legitimate elected leadership of the national minority. The vast majority of our people decided that we are their leadership.”

## Netanyahu’s party repeats unsubstantiated claims Arabs ‘stole the election’

Benjamin Netanyahu’s Likud party has repeated unsubstantiated claims that Arab citizens of Israel “stole” Tuesday’s election, despite the official elections committee finding no evidence of widespread irregularities.

Analysts say Netanyahu is seeking to undermine the legitimacy of Arab voters’ ballots because Arab parties are expected to win a historically high number of seats, potentially enabling his Jewish Israeli rivals to form an alliance with them and remove him from power.

## Netanyahu vows to eliminate Iran threat

Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu has warned against forming a coalition that relies on Arab-led parties while vowing to eliminate what he described as the existential threat posed by Iran.

Addressing supporters at Likud party headquarters in Jerusalem on Wednesday, Netanyahu said his right-wing bloc had a “decisive majority” among Jewish voters and those who support the Jewish state, despite preliminary results showing the alliance falling short of the 61 seats needed for a parliamentary majority.

“It is simply forbidden to endanger Israel and form a government that rests on anti-Zionist Arab parties. Parties that oppose our very existence, that see Hamas as a charitable organisation, that call our soldiers war criminals,” he said.

His remarks came as Arab-led parties appeared poised to win a record 19 seats in the 120-member Knesset, potentially giving them a decisive role in efforts by opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to form a government and end Netanyahu’s hold on power.

Netanyahu also said Israel had a “historic, once-in-a-lifetime opportunity”, alongside US president Donald Trump, to eliminate the threat from Iran.

“Because as long as that fanatical regime exists, a regime that has inscribed on its banner, that has defined the very purpose of its existence as the destruction of ours, it will try again to annihilate us. And it is within our power, together with our wonderful friend president Trump, to remove this threat once and for all.”

## 90% of votes counted as Netanyahu’s bloc 8 seats short of majority

Israel’s election results are still yet to be confirmed, with nearly 90 per cent of ballots counted leaving Benjamin Netanyahu’s bloc eight seats short of a parliamentary majority.

The Central Election Committee said Netanyahu’s alliance was on course to secure 53 seats in the 120-member Knesset, compared with 48 for opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot’s bloc.

The figures mark a reversal from pre-election opinion polls and initial exit polls, which had put Eisenkot’s alliance ahead, *The Times of Israel* reported.

The remaining count includes an estimated 430,000 to 480,000 double-envelope ballots cast by soldiers, prisoners, diplomats abroad and other voters unable to vote at their designated polling stations.

Officials are due to begin counting these ballots early on Thursday, with the tally expected to be completed by Thursday evening.

Final official results are expected on 4 November.

## Balad leader accuses Israeli parties of ‘racism’ over exclusion of Arab bloc

Balad party leader Sami Abu Shehadeh has accused Israel’s main political leaders of “racism” for refusing to form a coalition with Arab-led parties despite their projected record 19 seats in parliament.

Balad is one of the Joint List’s three parties.

He said that Palestinian citizens of Israel are being treated as illegitimate political partners.

“This is racism,” he told Al Jazeera. “We are a legitimate elected leadership of the national minority. The vast majority of our people decided that we are their leadership.”

Daniel Shek, a former Israeli ambassador told the outlet: “Probably the most important phenomenon of this election is the rise and the revival of the Arab vote. The statement is clear: You cannot disregard such a large portion of the electorate in the calculations for Israeli politics.”

## Four scenarios that could play out after Israel’s election as Netanyahu tries to cling onto power

Israel appears headed for another impasse following Tuesday’s general election, with early counts leaving both prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot short of a governing majority.

With roughly 85 per cent of votes counted by Wednesday afternoon, neither the incumbent’s right-wing religious bloc nor the broad coalition aligned behind Mr Eisenkot commanded the 61 seats required to form an administration in the 120-seat Knesset.

Because key parties have ruled out working with one another on ideological or personal grounds, political analysts note that forming an administration will require at least one faction to reverse its campaign commitments. In the absence of an agreement, Israeli law mandates a return to the ballot box.

## Arab parties in Israel poised for strongest electoral performance in generations

Arab parties in Israel are poised for their strongest electoral performance in generations, potentially winning 19 seats, up from 10 in the outgoing Parliament.

Their support could prove decisive in helping opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot secure the 61 seats needed to unseat Benjamin Netanyahu, whose alliance also appears short of a majority.

“Netanyahu knows that without the Arab parties, there’s no majority to unseat him. That’s why he incites against us,” Yousef Jabareen, the leader of the Joint List alliance said in an interview.

“The opposition should reject that incitement.”

He added: “The ball is now in Eisenkot’s court. Whoever wants to remove Netanyahu and change his policies – we are ready to cooperate to topple his government.”

However, partnering with the Arab parties, particularly the more nationalist Joint List, risks alienating Jewish voters and splitting Eisenkot’s bloc, analysts have said.

Arab Israelis make up about 20 per cent of the population in Israel but have long been marginalised in national politics.

## Israel's high-turnout vote brushed aside in rest of the Middle East

In Gaza, displaced Palestinian Majed Jundia made no distinction between Netanyahu and Eisenkot, a former military chief.

"They want to eradicate the Palestinian people so they do what they want," said Jundia.

Netanyahu was heavily criticised over security failures leading up to the Hamas assault on Israel's deadliest day.

Portraying himself as the only leader who could protect the country, the consummate political survivor argued that his assassinations of the leaders of Hamas, Lebanon's Hezbollah and their ally Iran made Israel, and the world, safer.

Kamel Haddad, an 81-year-old Lebanese man who has witnessed Israel's conflicts with neighbouring states over decades, expected no changes.

"As for who wins? I couldn’t care less. Let them tear each other apart, what difference does it make to me?” he said. "They might get preoccupied with one another since there are issues between them now. Perhaps they would leave us be for a bit.”
