Two callous killers who beat their friend to death with an iron have each been jailed for life with a minimum of 15 years after being convicted of murder.

Neither Zbigniew Chlebek nor Beata Walczak made any reaction when they were jailed today at Bradford Crown Court for killing Jaroslav "Yanic" Daniszewski.

After time spent on remand is taken into account, Chlebek, 67, of Stead Street, Shipley, will serve 14 years and 168 days.

Walczak, 58, of Barkerend Road, Bradford, will serve 14 years and 169 days.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21641544.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Walczak (left) and Chlebek *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Mr Daniszewski, 43, who was a friend of Chlebek and the boyfriend of Walczak, was found dead in Chlebek’s blood-spattered one-bed flat on April 22 last year following an all-day drinking session.

He had suffered 24 separate wounds to his head and face as well as catastrophic brain injuries, which rendered him deeply unconscious and unable to defend himself.

Sentencing the pair, His Honour Judge Geoffrey Marson KC said: “Each of you took part in this brutal and merciless attack.

“Then, at least half an hour later whilst he lay on the ground unconscious, he was struck another severe blow to the head with the iron.

“This caused a further fatal injury to the brain causing almost instant death.

“You were finishing him off and making sure he was dead.”

He added: “We will never know what the motive or cause of this attack was. It is clear to me – and I am sure that from the ferocity of the attack and that final blow – that each of you had the intention to kill.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/19361944.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Newsquest)*

Judge Marson said scientific evidence showed that whilst a third man, Bolestaw Roj, was present at the drinking party he played no part in the violence and has since died.

Judge Marson told Chlebek and Walczak: “You were both principals in this killing.

“Roj used his phone to contact the emergency services. There were a number of calls in which each of you was heard calling for an ambulance but because of language difficulties the emergency services did not respond.

“Roj left shortly after. There was no scientific evidence to connect him to the attack.

“Your callous reaction after you must have known he was dead was to remain in the flat and you, Walczak, simply went to bed in the bedroom where he lay. You left early next morning, leaving him lying there.

“You, Chlebek, remained in the flat until the Tuesday with the body in plain view. Then a phone call to you prompted you to call the emergency services. By then, however, the police had already become involved.

“Although your recollection may have been affected by the amount of alcohol you had consumed, I am satisfied that, when the police interviewed [you], each of you could remember something of the attack.

“You chose to lie. Neither of you has shown any remorse for what you did.”

Chlebek, wearing a black jacket, and Walczak, in a grey hoodie, sat silently in the dock and were assisted by a Polish interpreter.

Judge Marson noted that there had been no premeditation in the commencement of the pair’s attack and took into account that neither had previous convictions.

He said Chlebek had worked until he suffered mental health difficulties, and that until she became addicted to alcohol Walczak had lived a decent, hard-working life.

However, he said aggravating and mitigating factors balanced each other out resulting in a minimum term of 15 years.

He added: “I see no reason to distinguish between you.”

After Chlebek and Walczak left the dock Judge Marson commended the officers who handled what he described as “a particularly difficult investigation as the police had nothing to go on.

“The whole thing had to be pieced together with great care.

“I have no hesitation in commending them all for their sterling hard work.”

Speaking after the jury delivered its verdicts on Wednesday, Senior Investigating Officer Detective Superintendent Matt Holdsworth from West Yorkshire Police’s Homicide and Major Enquiry Team said: “This investigation centred on a sustained assault in which Jaroslav was subjected to a prolonged assault with an iron and then left unconscious by those responsible.

“Rather than seeking help for a gravely injured man, the defendants left him to die.

“The evidence gathered by the investigation team demonstrated not only the severity of the attack but also the deliberate failure of the offenders to provide or summon assistance when it was clear the victim was in urgent need of medical attention.

“The conviction of those responsible is the result of meticulous investigative work by the detectives in partnership forensic scientists involved and reflects our determination to secure justice for victims of serious violence.

“Our sincere condolences remain with the Jaroslav’s loved ones at this time and hope that the verdicts today provide some comfort."
