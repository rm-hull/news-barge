A North East lecturer has joined a humanitarian mission to Ukraine, helping deliver a 4x4 packed with medical supplies to communities affected by the war.

Kate Snowdon, from Northumberland, travelled to the Ukrainian city of Lviv as a volunteer driver with the charity Drive 2 Save Lives (D2SL).

The charity, which sources vehicles for delivering urgent medical and humanitarian aid, operates along the front lines and in support of emergency services.

Ms Snowdon, who works at the University of Sunderland as a lecturer in paramedic science, said: "My attitude is, if you have the opportunity and the means to help people, why would you not?

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643226.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kate Snowdon *(Image: Supplied)*

"There's so much suffering in Ukraine and as the war continues, it falls out of the media and the public consciousness.

"So it's more important than ever to keep raising awareness and sending aid."

She was joined on the six-day journey by her father Tom and family friend Phil Billingham, who has made several trips to Ukraine through his involvement with D2SL.

The team raised nearly £3,000 to buy a 4x4 vehicle, which they then filled with 140kg of medical supplies before setting off for Lviv.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643223.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kate Snowdon *(Image: Supplied)*

The group covered their own travel and insurance costs, ensuring that every donation went directly towards the vehicle and aid.

Ms Snowdon, originally from Chesterfield, Derbyshire, said: "It’s been an amazing experience that’s difficult to put into words.

"It was a privilege to visit Lviv, if only for a short time.

"It’s a beautiful city, full of culture, heritage and welcoming people."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21643224.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kate Snowdon with her dad Tom, left, and family friend Phil Billingham, centre *(Image: Supplied)*

The University of Sunderland supported the mission by donating medical supplies, including personal protective equipment (PPE).

Mark Robinson, healthcare technician at the university, said: "I am proud to support initiatives like this, where surplus medical supplies can be given a second life helping those who need them most.

"Through the recycling and reuse networks we have developed at the University of Sunderland, we have been able to reduce waste, divert usable equipment from landfill and support incredible charities such as D2SL in delivering vital aid to Ukraine."

The team covered the remaining cost of the vehicle and necessary repairs themselves.

Although the vehicle has now been donated, the fundraising page remains open.

Any additional donations will go towards the purchase of more vehicles for future humanitarian missions.

Ms Snowdon said the trip was originally her father's idea, and that Mr Billingham has been an active supporter of D2SL for years.

D2SL is a volunteer-led charity focused on delivering essential aid to communities most affected by the conflict.

It sources vehicles for frontline delivery of medical and humanitarian supplies.

Ms Snowdon and her team returned to the UK using public transport after handing over the vehicle in Ukraine.

She said the experience reinforced the ongoing need for support in the region, especially as media attention shifts elsewhere.

The University of Sunderland has developed recycling and reuse networks that enable it to support international aid efforts while reducing waste and environmental impact.
