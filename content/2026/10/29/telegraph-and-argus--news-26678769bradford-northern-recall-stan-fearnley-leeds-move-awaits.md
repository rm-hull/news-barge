BRADFORD Northern were about to enter a golden era just after 1976, but the players who laid the foundations should not be forgotten.

And the Odsal faithful were breathing a sigh of relief after learning that loose forward Stan Fearnley was finally back fit for their Halloween clash against Oldham.

Fearnley had played more than 200 games for Bradford over two spells at that point, some while his father Albert was general manager, helping them win a Yorkshire Cup and a League Cup.

He also scored for them in a Challenge Cup final, only for Northern to lose that heavily to Featherstone in 1973.

Three years on, and Fearnley had fallen victim to the classic summer curse, a pre-season injury.

He dislocated his shoulder in a friendly against Halifax, and it forced him to miss the first two months of the season, but Brian Smith was the bearer of good news in the T&A on this day 50 years ago.

Fearnley's return paid immediate dividends, Bradford winning that match 20-10 at Oldham's old Watersheddings ground.

His time at Northern was almost up though, and by the turn of the year, he had completed a move to bitter rivals Leeds.

And that decision paid immediate dividends, as Fearnley got his hands on the Challenge Cup trophy that had eluded him four years earlier when Leeds beat Widnes in the 1977 Wembley final.

But Bob Haigh soon came the other way from Headingley, the loose forward named man of the match as Bradford won the 1978 Premiership final against Widnes.

And less than three years later, an outstanding Northern forward pack had inspired the club to back-to-back Championships.
