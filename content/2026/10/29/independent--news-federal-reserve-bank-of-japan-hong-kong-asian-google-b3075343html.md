Asian shares and U.S. futures were mixed Thursday, weighed down by some big technology stocks after Google parent Alphabet’s earnings were lower than expected.

As anticipated, the Federal Reserve held rates steady as it wrapped up its policy meeting Wednesday. Two Fed officials were in favor of a hike, and a rate hike in December is likely since inflation remains too high, analysts said.

Markets are watching for the outcome of a meeting of the European Central Bank later Thursday. The Bank of Japan is expected to stand pat when it wraps up its policymaking meeting on Friday.

Oil prices fell more than 1% even as U.S. officials warned that renewed military action against Iran could come after the Nov. 3 midterm elections.

In Asian trading, Japan’s Nikkei 225 edged up 0.2% to 64,073.17. OpenAI investor SoftBank Group's shares fell 0.5%.

In South Korea, the Kospi index lost 2.2% to 5,880.25.

Samsung Electronics, a key component of the Korean market, sank 5.4% despite announcing strong quarterly results as its latest shareholder return policy arrangements disappointed some investors. Memory chipmaker SK Hynix rose 1.6%. Its shares soared 10.9% the day before on the company’s better-than-expected quarterly results.

The Taiex in Taiwan, which has also benefited from the AI boom, declined 1.9%.

Hong Kong’s Hang Seng edged less than 0.1% higher, to 23,101.86. The Shanghai Composite index shed 0.9% to 3,647.12.

Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 gained 0.5% to 8,888.50.

The latest round of U.S. technology giants’ earnings have shaken markets riding on high expectations from investors due to the global craze for artificial intelligence.

Shares of Google parent company Alphabet Inc. fell about 10% in after-hours trading. The California-based technology giant posted quarterly revenues that disappointed investors, raising questions over whether it can generate enough sales to justify its huge investments in AI.

Shares in Microsoft, however, gained nearly 3% in after-hours trading as it logged better-than-expected earnings results.

On Wednesday, Wall Street’s benchmark S&P 500 added 0.3% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.2%. The technology-heavy Nasdaq composite climbed 0.3%.

In the bond market, the yield on the 10-year U.S. Treasury eased to below 5.00% after briefly jumping to around 5.04% Wednesday. The yield on the two-year Treasury also steadied to 4.68%, down from higher than 4.77% on Wednesday.

In other dealings early Thursday, Brent crude oil, the international standard, fell 1.2% to $110.32 per barrel. That's still way higher than the roughly $72 a barrel level it was at before the Iran war began in late February.

Benchmark U.S. crude fell 1.3% to $101.46 per barrel.

The U.S. dollar fell to 157.72 Japanese yen from 157.84 yen. The euro was trading at $1.1146, down from $1.1154.
