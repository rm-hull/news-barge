The Glasgow-born boss of Watches of Switzerland has revealed the luxury retailer examined a potential US stock market listing after growing its American business from nothing to more than £1 billion in less than eight years.

Brian Duffy said the group, which has been listed in London since 2019, had looked at the implications of a US listing as the country accounts for an increasing share of the business. But he also stressed that Watches of Switzerland remains happy on the London Stock Exchange as the company backed up its commitment to the UK with a major expansion of its Rolex boutique on Buchanan Street in Glasgow.

The showroom has reopened following an extensive refurbishment which has approximately doubled its footprint to more than 6,600 square feet across two floors.

“You'll see around the store here the quality and the environment," Mr Duffy said. "Nothing has been compromised and, of course, with Rolex, you couldn't compromise anyway.

“The use of materials, the wood, the marble: it's an expensive store, but we're very confident in what it will mean.

“This is a great market for luxury watches, Scotland generally, and west of Scotland in particular. We've been here for decades. So, yes, we're very confident that this will be a smart investment.”

The relaunch comes amid a dramatic shift in the geographical balance of Watches of Switzerland, which entered the US market in 2017.

“We're very proud, and to some degree surprised, at just how successful we've been in America," Mr Duffy said.

“I think we spotted the opportunity: retail there was under-invested, and nobody was quite doing what we were doing. We went in with our formula, got great support from the brands, and we've gone from nothing to over £1 billion in the US in less than eight years.”

In its latest full-year results, US revenue rose 24% at constant currency to £927 million, overtaking the UK, where revenue increased 5% to £901m.

The group operates in 22 US states but does not have a presence in California. Mr Duffy described the opportunity to become bigger and more geographically spread in the US as “obvious”.

“We have looked at a US listing: what would it mean, and what would the drawbacks be?” Mr Duffy said.

He noted the group remains relatively small by US standards, adding: “American valuations are higher, everybody knows that. So we're happy where we are now.”

Watches of Switzerland joined the London Stock Exchange in June 2019 after previously being controlled by US private equity group Apollo Global Management. Asked whether the company could return to private ownership, Mr Duffy refused to rule it out.

“You never say never on anything,” he said. “We're happy as we are, and we're doing well. I think public ownership is a good thing for this category.”

He said being publicly listed provided long-term certainty for the luxury watch brands with which the retailer works.

“It's not like being private-equity owned, as we were before, where you know there's going to be a change at some point, which is probably not ideal for retailing Swiss watches.

“We could be on the London Stock Exchange for the next 20 years-plus, or forever. So we have that certainty, and our brand partners have certainty about us.”

Despite the increasingly American complexion of the business, Mr Duffy remains confident about the UK.

“Our business grew 5% in the UK. We're doing a bit better than that now, but we've consistently grown in the UK,” he said. “Per capita, this is the best market in the world for luxury watches, the UK. And we're proud to be a part of it.”

The Glasgow expansion has taken the operation into the neighbouring unit formerly occupied by Peloton. The dedicated Rolex boutique opened in 2020, replacing an existing multi-brand Watches of Switzerland store.

Mr Duffy, who grew up in Glasgow, reflected on how the city's retail landscape has changed.

“We've always loved retailing, I think, and shopping in Glasgow. Buchanan Street is one of the most productive streets in the country. I think it might be second after Oxford Street. It's very, very productive.

“For me, it's a wee bit concentrated now. In the years I grew up, Argyle Street, Buchanan Street and Sauchiehall Street were the three streets you would shop in. I remember Buchanan Street before it was pedestrianised."

Despite the obvious challenges and dramatic shifts that have taken place in Glasgow's retail landscape, Mr Duffy remains positive about the market.

“It's always been a naturally social culture here,” he said. “We like to talk, we like to engage, and shopping is part of that as well.”
