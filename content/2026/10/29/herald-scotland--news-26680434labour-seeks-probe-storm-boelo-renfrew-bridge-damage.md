**Renfrewshire Labour councillors have called for a special council meeting to investigate both the failure to activate flood defences during Storm Boelo and the subsequent closure of the £117 million Renfrew Bridge after flooding damaged its operating systems.**

The demand comes after Renfrewshire Council confirmed the bridge will remain closed to road traffic for several months following extensive water damage to electrical systems housed in one of its operating plant rooms.

The £117m crossing, which links **Renfrew** with**Yoker** and**Clydebank**, opened in May 2025 as the first opening road bridge across the River Clyde. The weekend storm caused significant damage to the electronic equipment controlling its swing bridge mechanism.

Labour group leader Iain McMillan said residents and businesses already dealing with the aftermath of flooding in Renfrew would be concerned to learn that water ingress had also severely affected a key component of the major infrastructure project.

"Everyone understands that severe weather can put pressure on infrastructure," he said.

"However, residents and businesses already reeling from the damage caused when the River Clyde burst through on Saturday due to flood defence systems not being activated will be shocked to learn that a key operational component of a £117 million bridge has suffered such extensive damage from water ingress that the bridge must now close for months."

McMillan said there were "reasonable questions" about how a critical operational facility became vulnerable to flooding and whether the bridge's design included adequate protections.

Labour wants to know whether the plant room was located in an area known to be at flood risk, what resilience measures the project included, and whether the storm exceeded the conditions the bridge was designed to withstand.

"The obvious question is why a critical plant room appears to have been vulnerable to flooding in the first place," he said.

"Did this event exceed the conditions the bridge was designed to withstand, or has a significant design weakness been exposed? These are reasonable questions that taxpayers will expect to be answered."

Renfrewshire Council said it is assessing and repairing serious water damage to the bridge's electrical systems. Officials have said investigations are ongoing and that the flooding incident significantly damaged the electronic systems controlling the hydraulics on the Renfrew side of the bridge. The crossing must remain open to river traffic while repairs are carried out.

In its original statement announcing the closure, the council said the exceptional tidal surge saw water levels on the Clyde reach 6.7 metres at Renfrew, the highest recorded since digital records began in 1998 and more than twice the forecast level.

It also maintained that activating the North Renfrew Flood Prevention Scheme would not have prevented damage to the bridge because the operating plant room is located outside the area protected by the defences.

McMillan said the immediate priority should be restoring the crossing to service and helping affected residents and businesses recover, but argued there must also be "complete transparency" about what happened.

He added: "When public money has been invested in a major infrastructure project on the Clyde, the public has every right to expect resilience, accountability and openness.

"We're calling for a special council meeting because we owe it to local taxpayers to establish exactly what happened, what it will cost to put right and what steps will be taken to ensure it cannot happen again."

Labour said it is seeking clarification on flood risk assessments undertaken during the bridge's design, the level of protection built into the plant room, the estimated repair bill and whether warranties, insurance policies or contractor liabilities could cover some of the costs.

On Wednesday, Renfrewshire Council’s Head of Climate, Public Protection and Roads, Gerard Hannah, said work was underway to assess the full circumstances and restore the bridge's full function.

He said: “We’re very sorry for the inconvenience this will cause all those who use the bridge.

“We have two operating rooms, one on each side of the river, which manage the opening and closing of the bridge. While one side suffered minimal impact from the high-water levels, the plant on the Renfrew side suffered significant damage to the electrical systems required to operate the bridge.

"Urgent work is underway to identify the issues behind the damage and get the bridge back into action as soon as possible. We’ll keep the public updated with further details as the work progresses.”
