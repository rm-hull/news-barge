Two further members of a Bradford gang involved in a multi-million-pound drugs trade have been jailed.

Mohsin Bangash and Lukmaan Patel were sentenced on Wednesday at Bradford Crown Court.

Bangash, 28, of Fairburn Gardens, Eccleshill, Bradford, received 10-and-a-half years for conspiracy to supply heroin and cocaine.

Patel, 35, of Portland Towers, Leicester, was handed seven years for conspiracy to supply cocaine.

Together with other participants that dealt drugs worth millions have been locked up for more than 100 years.

Their sentences mean 13 men and one woman have together been jailed for drug and money laundering offences including ringleader Idris Khan, 35, who began a 16-year sentence.

The activities of the Bradford-based organised crime gang (OCG), which was responsible for the industrial-scale supply of Class A drugs and associated money laundering within Yorkshire, the Humber, and beyond, was uncovered via an investigation by West Yorkshire’s Serious and Organised Crime Unit.

Officers found that the gang supplied more than 135kg of Class A drugs with 55kg of cocaine and heroin recovered.

Idris Khan, from Ashgrove, Bradford, was identified as the ringleader who arranged the movement of drugs with trusted Bradford-based couriers Akif Hashmi, 23, and Ibrahim Sajad, 27, to supply the drugs.

Hashmi was arrested in August 2025 in possession of 20kg of cocaine, 23kg of heroin, and three bags of crack cocaine totalling a street value of £4.3 million.

His wife Kiran Mahmood, 24, was also present and subsequently arrested.

Ibras Khan, 38, was found to be a customer of the OCG.

He and his right-hand man Ataf Ali, 35, arranged and facilitated the movement of 31 kilos of heroin and cocaine over a two-month period.

In April 2025, Ibras Khan was arrested on suspicion of supplying Class A drugs and officers seized two mobile phones from him.

Evidence from the devices proved he was dealing directly with the gang and arranging the movement of large-scale amounts of drugs.

Data from the phones also proved that between August 2024 and April 2025, Ibras Khan oversaw the supply of more than 52 kilos of drugs and collected £364,992.

The gang appeared at Bradford Crown Court on Monday (October 12).

Members of the OCG and their associates were sentenced as follows:

* Ibras Khan, 38, of Naseby Rise, Queensbury, Bradford: 14 years and nine months for conspiracy to supply cocaine, possession with intent to supply heroin, and possession of criminal property.
* Ataf Ali, 35, of Grange Drive, Bradford: 14 years and nine months for conspiracy to supply cocaine.
* Idris Khan, 35, of Ashgrove, Bradford: 16 years for conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to supply heroin.
* Ibrahim Sajad, 27, of Cavendish Court, Bradford: seven years and six months for conspiracy to supply cocaine.
* Akif Hashmi, 23, of Moorland Lane, Tyersal, Bradford: 13 years and three months for conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to supply heroin.
* Kiran Mahmood, 24, formerly of Hazelwood Road, Bradford: three years and nine months for conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to supply heroin.
* Moshin Mian, 30, of Westgate, Huddersfield: 12 years and four months for conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to supply heroin.
* Noor Hamid Khan, 33, of Grimesthorpe Road, Sheffield: six years and three months for being concerned in the supply of heroin.
* Muhammed Chaudhary, 34, of Queens Walk, Ashford: four years and six months for conspiracy to supply cocaine and possession of cannabis.
* Mohammed Usman, 30, of Lindley Road, Bradford: five years and three months for conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to supply heroin.
* Mohammed Usman, 44, of Courtnay Close, Bradford: five years and three months for conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to supply heroin.
* Michael Littlewood, 38, of Sunnyhurst Avenue, Blackpool: 10 years and six months for conspiracy to supply cocaine, possession of criminal property, and possession with intent to supply cocaine.

Detective Inspector, Kevin Daly of the Force Precision team said: “This was an incredibly complex investigation, which involved the assistance of many officers to tackle a sophisticated conspiracy to supply Class A drugs on an industrial scale.

“The supply of illegal drugs fuels crime and serious violence in our communities and has devastating impacts.

"By investigating all levels of the organised crime group, we struck those who profit greatly from criminality, and I hope these sentences send a clear message and reassure our communities.”
