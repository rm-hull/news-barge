One of six men charged with the brutal murder of a 35-year-old father-of-two in Keighley has given evidence in his defence at a murder trial.

Suhail Choudry was assaulted by a group wielding metal poles and similar weapons shortly after 3am on February 8 this year on Flasby Street in Keighley.

He suffered 52 marks and injuries including multiple skull fractures as well as extensive brain injuries and died in Leeds General Infirmary three days later.

Six men – described as being involved in the fatal assault or being drivers or look-outs – deny murder and other offences.

![Police in Keighley conducting enquiries in relation to the murder investigation](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20533105.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police investigating the death of Suhail Choudry *(Image: Newsquest)*

They are Mohammed Aliyaan Khan, Faisal Khan, Hakeem Gulzar, Majid Hussain, Waseem Islam, and Rashid Hussain.

At Sheffield Crown Court yesterday (October 28) Rashid Hussain denied receiving or making phone calls in which anybody mentioned going to injure or kill anyone.

Asked if Mr Choudry’s name was mentioned to him, Hussain replied, “No. Not at all.”

Asked if there had been any mention of perpetrating violence against any individual, Hussain repeated: “Not at all.”

Asked if he had encouraged anyone to attack anyone during phone calls that were made, he said, “No, nothing.”

Questioned by his defence barrister Peter Moulson KC, Hussain admitted knowing Mr Choudry “from childhood” but that they had “parted ways”.

He denied knowing Mohammed Aliyaan Khan, Faisal Khan, or Hakeem Gulzar.

![People gathered to remember Suhail Choudry on Monday](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20550018.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A vigial held after the death as people gathered to remember Suhail Choudry *(Image: Newsquest)*

He admitted knowing Waseem Islam many years previously when they had attended a local youth club together.

And he said Majid Hussain was the brother of his childhood friend Basharat Hussain, with whom he was in touch multiple times a day.

Rashid Hussain said he would cover for Basharat, who worked at Majid’s takeaway, if Basharat was unable to fulfil his hours.

He said he was asked to do so on the night of February 7 but declined as it was his wife’s birthday and he had been out all day with her and his three children at Xscape near Castleford and later at a restaurant in Bradford.

He said Basharat was “struggling” as Majid was not at work having injured his shoulder at the gym.

![Police have released this image of Suhail Choudry](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20532265.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Suhail Choudry *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Despite numerous calls, Rashid Hussain said he was unable to work that night.

Phone data showed that Rashid Hussain made several calls during the early hours of February 8, which he told the jury were connected with a hobby selling cars that had turned into a side business.

He said he was in bed at the time.

He also said he tried calling Majid because he felt he had “fobbed him off” over not working at the takeaway.

During cross-examination by prosecution barrister John Harrison KC, Hussain said he learned about the attack on Suhail Choudry the next day from his wife, who was related to his family.

Questioned as to whether he asked her for further information, Hussain said: “She just informed me that there had been an attack. That’s about as far as it went.

“An attack had taken place in our area. It was none of my concern.”

He said the news also appeared in a public announcement group on Facebook.

Hussain described how he was driving on February 17 when he was stopped by armed police who smashed the windows of his car.

He said that was when he learned he was being arrested for murder.

The jury heard that he told police “I am complying” but later gave “no comment” answers in three interviews about the killing of Mr Choudry following legal advice to do so.

Describing his arrest he told the court: “I thought I was kidnapped. They had balaclavas on. They had their face covered. They were in plain clothes. They had guns.

![A forensic officer at a scene in Keighley on February 12, 2026](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20533205.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police at the scene of the death *(Image: Telegraph & Argus)*

“I was in a state of shock. What can you possibly say in a state of shock?

“I was dragged on the roadside with my hoodie over my face. I couldn’t even find my footing. And I was put up against a brick wall.”

Mr Harrison said: “At no time had you thought to say, ‘Hang on, you’ve got the wrong man. This is nothing to do with me?’”

Hussain said the murder charge was “massive” and that the ordeal of the arrest had left him “genuinely numb”.

He added: “It felt like a really bad dream – that this isn’t happening to me.

“My whole world had turned upside down in a matter of seconds. I’d never experienced anything like that before. It was life-changing.”

Mr Harrison suggested that Hussain had had the opportunity to give an explanation to the police but chose not to do so “because you were waiting to see what the police had found – waiting to see what could be proved against you before you decided to devise a version of events to fit that evidence?”

Hussain replied: “That’s incorrect.”

Mohammed Aliyaan Khan, 23, of Malsis Road, Keighley; Hakeem Gulzar, 28, of Plover Street, Keighley; Faisal Khan, 26, of Malsis Road, Keighley; Rashid Hussain, 41, of Redcliffe Street, Keighley; Majid Hussain, 42, of no fixed abode, Keighley; and Waseem Khan, 40, of East Avenue, Keighley all deny murder.

The trial continues.
