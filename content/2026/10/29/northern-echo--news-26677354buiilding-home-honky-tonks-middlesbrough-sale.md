The building home to a popular country nightspot and sports bar has been put up for sale.

Occupied by Texas-style bar Honky Tonk’s, the five-floor building at the corner of Albert Road and Wilson Street in Middlesbrough has hit the market with a £799,950 price tag.

The site, once known as the Lloyds Bank and then the Ayresome Park pub, has been described as a “prime commercial investment”.

It previously had venues in both Guisborough and Stockton but decided to close them when it acquired the Middlesbrough building in a “strategic decision”.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641230.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Honky Tonk's, on Albert Road in Middlesbrough *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

The sale describes how the Grade II listed building offers “significant scale, character and flexible potential for commercial, leisure, hospitality or mixed-use redevelopment”.

It boasts approximately 20,000 sq. ft. across five levels, including the Honky Tonk's bar, sports bar upstairs, and a second floor currently set out as a bar and dance floor.

The third floor is accommodation while the basement has extensive storage and cellar accommodation to support the upper floors.

The sale, described as an “exceptional opportunity”, is freehold, meaning the buyer would own the property, including the land it's built on, with no fixed time limit.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21641229.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Honky Tonk's, on Albert Road in Middlesbrough *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Over the years, the venue has operated under many different names both on the ground floor and upstairs, including nightclubs Mint and, before that, Haze.

It is not yet known what the sale would mean for Honky Tonk’s, which has been in the town centre building opposite Flares since September 2024.

The firm has been contacted for comment but did not respond in time for publication.

Honky Tonk’s, known for its live country music, line dancing, and cowboy hats, has grown in popularity in the last two years.

The venture was started by North East entrepreneurs Glenn Bartlett and Michael Kitson after Glenn visited a country music bar in Las Vegas.

Glenn previously said he had ambitious plans at the time to open as many as ten Honky Tonk’s and had his eyes on Darlington and Newcastle.
