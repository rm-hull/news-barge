Patrick Stewart, the classically-trained actor who gained worldwide fame in the 1980s as Captain Jean-Luc Picard on Star Trek: The Next Generation and later as Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men film series, has died. A message posted on Stewart's official X account said his passing came "following a brief and poignant journey with Alzheimer's Disease and Lewy Body Dementia."

Stewart joined the Royal Shakespeare Company in 1966, and took his first television role a year later on the long-running British series Coronation Street. He debuted on Broadway in 1971, and subsequently appeared in several feature films. He was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 2010, becoming Sir Patrick, which he described as "an unlooked-for honour."

Stewart also accrued a number of videogame credits over the years in various Star Trek and X-Men games but others as well: He narrated Lands of Lore: The Throne of Chaos, portrayed Khelben Arunsen in Forgotten Realms: Demon Stone, and maybe most famously gave his deep, sonorous voice to Uriel Septim VII in The Elder Scrolls 4: Oblivion.

![TES 4 Oblivion - Dialogue with Emperor Uriel Septim - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/2xDm6zpBKm0/maxresdefault.jpg)

William Shatner, who played Captain Kirk on the original Star Trek series, shared a heartfelt message of condolence on X, calling Stewart "a friend, a colleague, and a magnificent actor."

"He has been held in the warm embrace of family and friends, surrounded by live music, prayer, tears, laughter, and the kind of profound emotion that only he could inspire after a lifetime of devotion to us all," the announcement reads. "We will forever cherish the peaceful ease and reflection on his face, an enduring reminder of his invincible grace."

Sir Patrick Stewart was 86.
