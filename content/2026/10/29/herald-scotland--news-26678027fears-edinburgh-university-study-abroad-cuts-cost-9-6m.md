**Cuts to 60 per cent of Edinburgh University’s study abroad team could cost as much as £9.6 million in lost tuition, staff at the institution have warned.**

Last week, The Herald reported that 16 of 28 positions in the university’s Study and Work Away (SWAY) team are set to be cut.

Staff raised concerns about the impact of the restructure on the university’s ability to participate in the Erasmus+ exchange scheme, which the UK will participate in next year for the first time since 2020.

Now, The Herald can reveal that an academic-led financial analysis claims that reputational damage could cost the university millions of pounds.

However, a university spokesperson contested the figures, saying they were unfounded.

A letter signed by 43 academics, exchange coordinators and directors of internationalisation states: “The University of Edinburgh’s study and work away programmes have been highly successful, allowing 1,461 students to go abroad this past year, up from 1,399 last year and a projected 1,590 to go abroad this academic session."

Furthermore, the group estimates that 3,000 international students come to Edinburgh for their year or semester abroad.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19610386.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Thousands of students travel to and from Edinburgh each year for semesters abroad. *(Image: Newsquest)*

“The new, scaled down staffing arrangements will mean that the current variety of agreements cannot be sustained, which will directly impact the university’s attractiveness and result in a loss of student income, including from international, full-fee paying students,” the open letter adds.

“The opportunity cost of these students choosing another university with a more attractive exchange programme can be estimated conservatively at £9.6 million a year.”

According to the group, 126 exchange students were classed as overseas fee payers during the 2025/26 academic year, up from 103 three years ago.

International students pay more than £30,000 a year in tuition - about £20,000 more than the university receives per domestic student from the Scottish Government.

“Assuming that the additional cost of serving overseas students is negligible, the £20k surplus fees can be used to cover fixed costs or cross-subsidise other activities,” the analysis, which has been shared with senior managers, explains.

“There are currently up to 480 overseas fee-paying students (in years 1 to 4) who either have returned from exchange (4th years), are on exchange (3rd years), are applying for exchange (2nd years) or are planning to do so (1st years), and who may not have chosen Edinburgh if they had thought exchange opportunities were limited.

“If 480 international fee-paying students were missing, the university would lose 480 x £20k = £9.6m per year in surplus fee income.”

By comparison, the 16 redundant positions could save the university around £800,000, assuming a median staff cost of £50,000 is applied.

According to this arithmetic, losing 40 fee-paying international students (£20,000 x 40 = £800,000) would erase any savings made by the cuts.

Even if the number of enrolments falls by just a quarter, the university stands to lose £2.4m in tuition, a net negative of £1.6m after the £800,000 saving in staff costs.

A university spokesperson has pushed back against the figures, stating that the projected loss of income is "entirely unfounded and unequivocally rejected" by the institution's leadership.

A group of staff familiar with how the department works told The Herald that they fear “an Erasmus fiasco” if the cuts go ahead, leading to a “loss of reputation in Europe and with our partners”.

“The way in which these cuts are proceeding is extremely brutal - no rationale has been provided, no basis for them has been shared, and there has been no opening for dialogue,” the group said.

“It seems to all be proceeding shockingly fast and in secrecy.

“We are all in shock and disbelief.”

However, a university spokesperson has defended the restructure, insisting that study abroad opportunities would continue to be provided.

A University of Edinburgh spokesperson said: “There is no intention to withdraw from nor diminish our commitment to student exchange.

"A central team will continue to support student exchanges, which will remain an important component of the curriculum. The ambition is to develop a service that is sustainable and integrated into a wider institutional approach to international learning.

“Undergraduate students will continue to go abroad on exchange, and students will continue to come to Edinburgh through reciprocal exchange arrangements.

"Widening participation, inclusion and access will remain important considerations. The proposed model does not seek to reduce the number of outgoing exchange students nor fee-paying visiting undergraduates.”
