The **Poor Man's Rembrandt Project**, first launched in 2023, has returned by popular demand, bringing** tattoo artists**into the house where** Rembrandt**once lived and worked. Among those taking part is Associated Press correspondent** Mike Corder**, who had covered the original event without imagining he would one day get a tattoo himself. This time, he sat down with renowned Dutch tattoo artist** Henk Schiffmacher**, better known as** Hanky Panky**, to have Rembrandt's 1630 etching***Self-Portrait with Beret, Wide-Eyed***reproduced on his arm. After around half an hour, the young artist's wide-eyed expression was** permanently etched into Corder's skin**.

**Schiffmacher**, whose career spans** more than 45 years**, has tattooed celebrities including** Lady Gaga**and members of the** Red Hot Chili Peppers**. He embraces the nickname**"Poor Man's Rembrandt"**, seeing tattooing as an art form that accompanies its owner for life.

The museum's director, **Milou Halbesma**, says Rembrandt's self-portraits reveal his** remarkable honesty**, capturing every wrinkle and imperfection, including his own.

The project brings together two very different artistic traditions, transforming centuries-old etchings into **contemporary body art**. As Schiffmacher finished Corder's tattoo, he welcomed him into what he jokingly called the**"world of freaks"**. The journalist's verdict was simple: happy with the result, if a little sore. The project runs until** 7 November**.
