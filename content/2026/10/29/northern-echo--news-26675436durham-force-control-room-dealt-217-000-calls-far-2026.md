Durham Police has praised the often unseen staff who keep the force moving around the clock.

Since the start of the year, the force control room has handled more than 85,000 999 calls and 132,000 calls to the 101 non-emergency line.

The force said no two calls are the same: “Whether it be reports of anti-social behaviour to domestic abuse, to serious collisions, our call handlers are there when you need help.

“Working alongside dispatchers, they ensure the force’s on-the-ground officers get where they’re needed day and night.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21638671.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham Police's call handlers have been praised for their often unseen efforts in keeping the force moving around the clock

“They may not always be seen, but their impact is felt hugely across the force.”

Last year, the force recorded its lowest-ever waiting times after investing £1.5 million in new technology and extra staff.

National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPPC) figures showed Durham answered 101 non-emergency calls in an average of six seconds, matching other regional forces.

A new phone system introduced early last year cut the time between dialling 999 or 101 and reaching a call handler.

NPCC figures released last year showed 91.7 per cent of 999 calls were answered within ten seconds, continuing a steady rise since 2023/24.

During that period, 24 new call handlers completed training and mentoring as part of the drive to strengthen the control room team.

In March 2024, the force moved to Single Online Home, a national website where the public can submit queries, report crimes and complete applications online.

The move brought Durham in line with most UK police forces.

Speaking at the time, Chief Superintendent Chris Barker said: “By introducing innovative technology, we are making it quicker and easier for people to contact us – whether in an emergency or for non-urgent matters.

“Over the last few years, we’ve invested heavily in extra staff and new technology, and as a direct result, our call handling performance and waiting times have dramatically improved.

“Average waiting times on 101 used to be measured in minutes.

"They are now measured in seconds, and our 999 response rate matches that of other forces across the country.

“There’s still work to be done, and there will be busy times when we’re dealing with peak demand, but these figures prove we’re heading in the right direction, and we’re confident we will continue to improve.

“These improvements allow us to better serve the people of County Durham and Darlington, helping them feel supported and reassured that help is available when required.”

Last year, the force launched an automated victim-update system providing real-time updates by text or email.

After reporting a crime, victims can register on the MyDurham Portal to track their case, contact the officer in charge and stay informed throughout the investigation.

Victims receive a link by text or email to access the secure portal once their crime is logged.

The MyDurham Portal is currently available for selected crime types, including criminal damage, vehicle crime, burglary, violence against the person, harassment, anti-social behaviour and suspicious activity.
