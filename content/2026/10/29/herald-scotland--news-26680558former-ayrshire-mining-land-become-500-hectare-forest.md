Countryside once spoiled by open-cast mining is being turned green again after work began to plant a 500-hectare forest.

Brockloch Forest, in East Ayrshire, received approval in June 2026 and ground preparation work is now underway, with planting expected to begin in early November.

Landowner True North said the site west of New Cumnock, along with a second site named Greencroft in County Durham, represents the "forests of the future", which will boost biodiversity and help tackle climate change.

Planting at Brockloch is expected to be completed by March 2027. True North Real Asset Partners, the largest afforestation investor in the UK, bought the site in 2021.

Harry Humble, CEO of True North, said: "Our planting design for Brockloch showcases modern, best-practice forestry.

"It is rewarding to see work starting on site now, preparing the ground for a resilient woodland that will boost biodiversity, help tackle climate change and deliver a sustainable source of timber."

Brockloch, formerly known as Bank and Afton, lies within both the Central Scotland Green Network, a zone targeted for woodland creation, and the Strategic Woodland Creation Area in East Ayrshire Council's non-statutory planning guidance.

Formerly defined by open-cast mining, this part of East Ayrshire has since shifted to a mixture of commercial forestry, wind farms and pastoral farming on the lower slopes.

At Brockloch, about 100 hectares of the lower ground was used for mining. Some landforms created from leftover spoil and boulders remain as part of the landscape restoration.

The trees will be planted around them. This lower ground is relatively sheltered and fertile, making it well suited to more sensitive conifers.

The 493-hectare forestry creation scheme will comprise 303.7 hectares of Sitka spruce, accounting for 61.6% of the total area. The remaining species mix is as follows: Douglas fir, 15.9ha (3.2%); Norway spruce, 11.6ha (2.3%); noble fir, 7.6ha (1.5%); Pacific silver fir, 5.3ha (1.1%); Scots pine, 4.5ha (0.9%); native mixed broadleaves, 97.9ha (19.9%); and open ground, 46.3ha (9.3%).

With the highest ground unsuitable for planting, True North joined a consortium developing the Enoch Hill Wind Farm.

The wind farm was connected to the national grid this summer, and True North subsequently sold its share in the powered land to Electric Land Investors.

The 174.46 hectares of open hill ground were sold with a 32-year lease to RWE Renewables UK Offshore Wind Limited.

Mr Humble said: "After long and complex planning processes, particularly at Greencroft, we are excited to see practical groundwork taking place. These are forests for the future and I look forward to watching them take shape."
