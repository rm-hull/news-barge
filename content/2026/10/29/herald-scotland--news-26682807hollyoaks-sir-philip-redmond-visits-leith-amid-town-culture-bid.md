**Award-winning television producer Sir Philip Redmond visited Leith on Thursday as £3m in cultural funding hangs in the balance.**

Sir Philip, who is best known as the creator of Grange Hill, Brookside and Hollyoaks, chairs the judging panel for the UK-wide Town of Culture competition.

In July, Leith was announced as one of 15 finalists for the 2028 award.

Speaking to The Herald at the Shore, the veteran showrunner spoke of his excitement of learning about the town’s bid, which is due to be submitted in late November.

The winning bid will receive £3m in prize money to develop local art and culture programmes, while two finalists will receive £250,000 each.

![Leith Theatre requires extensive refurbishment before it can reopen as a permanent venue](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19057830.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Philip visited Leith Theatre, which requires extensive refurbishment before it can reopen as a permanent venue. *(Image: Paul Hollingwood)*

“The purpose of the trip, and I keep saying to everybody, this is an informal trip by me to visit all 15 towns,” Sir Philip told The Herald.

“What I really wanted to see was the engagement and the level of enthusiasm and energy.

“I think that’s come across loud and clear, particularly as they wore me out trying to take me around everywhere,” he quipped.

As part of the visit, Sir Philip met with bid organisers and community members at Leith Theatre.

A community-led trust received £4.5m in National Lottery funding last year in a bid to permanently reopen the historic venue, which fell into disrepair in recent decades.

“We’re constantly reminding people of why it's really valuable,” Leith Theatre Trust director Lynn Morrison told The Herald in August.

“[Leith] has an incredible polarization of wealth and poverty.

"And actually, this building just can be for all of those people. I think that's not that typical on a high street.”

“I think it is the type of thing that is quite common in all the places that bid for Town of Culture,” Sir Philip said of the campaign. “They want to celebrate their place.”

“Leith Theatre demonstrated that [well],” he added. “It is a fantastic resource in the way the community came together to fight to keep it.

“It is heartening, and that is what culture is all about. I can see a great future for that place.

“If I was a bit younger, I might relocate and try and do something with it,” Sir Philip joked.
