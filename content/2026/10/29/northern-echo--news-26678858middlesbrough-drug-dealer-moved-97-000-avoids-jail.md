A drug dealer who moved £100,000 through two bank accounts has narrowly avoided jail.

Vayeed Khan was carrying cash and drugs when police stopped him in Middlesbrough.

Police found three mobile phones, while checks showed £97,533.54 had been moved through Khan’s bank accounts, Teesside Crown Court heard.

Prosecutor Shaun Dryden said the Crown Prosecution Service had accepted Khan’s account of the offences.

He said police stopped Khan after spotting him driving his Audi A3 along Maddren Way in Middlesbrough.

“Three mobile phones were recovered from the vehicle,” he said. “When he was searched, six wraps of cocaine and £2,770 in cash were recovered from his trouser leg.”

Data from his phones revealed details of drug dealing and the two bank accounts used to move the cash, the court heard.

Khan racially abused a custody sergeant at the police station, the court heard.

Mr Dryden said the Crown Prosecution Service had accepted Khan’s account of the offences.

He said: “The defendant says he was a user of Class A drugs; he ran up a debt and was selling drugs to pay that debt off.”

“He does accept that there came a point where he was dealing without any pressure from others involved in the drugs trade.”

Khan, of Devonport Road, Middlesbrough, admitted supplying and possessing cocaine, possessing and transferring criminal property, and racially aggravated harassment dating from May 2024.

His lawyer, Stephen Constantine, said Khan began dealing to pay his debts but accepted he kept selling drugs after clearing them.

“At the heart of this case is someone who needs help,” he said.

“This is someone who is willing to engage with the authorities. He doesn’t want to be in this position again.”

Recorder Simon Kealey KC sentenced Khan to three years in prison, suspended for two years.

He said: “You have been sentenced on your basis of plea and the fact that your family have reported pressure and threats being made to you and them.”

Khan was also given a nine-month drug rehabilitation requirement.
