**Sir Peter Mathieson has warned that ‘urgent’ and ‘difficult’ decisions will be taken as the University of Edinburgh presses ahead with a package of cuts.**

The principal and vice-chancellor has published a communiqué outlining his thoughts on the university’s future and addressing “misconceptions about the changes ahead”.

Last month, The Herald revealed that 90 programmes in the university’s College of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences would be ‘taught out’, with no new enrolments from 2027.

The programme closures come amid plans to cut £140m from the university’s annual budget, which trade union officials have warned could lead to as many as 1,800 redundancies.

Sir Peter wrote: “Uncertainty and pessimism in the current circumstances are fully understandable. Misinformation and disinformation are not acceptable.

“As an institution devoted to the pursuit of knowledge and truth, we have a duty to challenge falsehoods and explain our intentions openly so that our students, staff and supporters – including those who may join us in the future – understand where we are going and why.”

![Sir Peter Mathieson appearing at a Education Committee hearing in June 2025.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21501913.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Peter Mathieson spoke about the cuts at a education committee hearing at Holyrood in June 2025. *(Image: Scottish Parliament)*

Sir Peter pointed to reports which circulated on social media last month suggesting that the university planned to cut Celtic and Scottish studies provision.

“The changes being made across our programmes are intended to give students greater flexibility and encourage them to explore subjects and disciplines they might not otherwise have considered,” he wrote.

“As a result, more students at Edinburgh will have the opportunity to study Scotland’s rich histories, literatures, cultures and languages – including Gaelic – as part of their degree pathway, not fewer.”

Sir Peter also responded to a report by The Herald which revealed that 16 of 28 jobs would be cut from the university's Study and Work Abroad Service (SWAY) as part of a corporate restructure.

The principal, who announced he will leave the university in early 2028, wrote: “The focus on internationalisation will remain a priority.

“The reforms to the organisation of incoming and outgoing international student experiences are designed to make things better and more inclusive, not to stop them from happening, so that students are offered improved and different ways to develop global perspectives, intercultural understanding and relevant skills.”

Sir Peter also responded to claims suggesting that the university, which has £3.1 billion in reserves, does not need to make budget cuts.

“When I am confronted with ‘but we are not in a deficit’, my answer is ‘true, but the only reason we are not is that we have been taking decisions for the last couple of years that have prevented a deficit’, he wrote.

As The Herald has previously reported, Sir Peter said that the university made £73m worth of savings during the financial year 2025-26, primarily due to voluntary severance and other cost-cutting measures.

“At the end of the year our financial surplus was £16 million,” he wrote.

“Do the maths: without the savings we made, the deficit would have been £57 million.”

“Urgent, difficult but strategically important decisions need to be taken now,” he went on to say.

“That is the role of responsible leaders: aiming to safeguard the future as well as taking care of the present.

“It is my fervent hope that those of us that genuinely care about the University of Edinburgh will work together to ensure its future.”

![University College Union (UCU) members and student supporters from universities across Scotland take part in a rally against cuts and job losses in universities outside the Scottish Parliament.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21529900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Staff and student rallied in Edinburgh earlier this month. *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

Staff have pushed back against this framing.

"Why are you going ahead with the most sweeping plan of staff cuts in Scottish higher education when you're in a way better financial place than basically any other educational institution?" Dr Marion Lieutaud, Edinburgh UCU co-vice president, told The Herald last month.

"What should the university be for?" her colleague Dr Courtney Stafford-Walter, also co-vice president, added.

"Is it a profit-making business? Or is it a charity that's focused on actual educational learning, teaching, all of these beautiful things that we're meant to do?”

Sir Peter also outlined his vision for the next decade of university growth.

He wrote: “Ten years from now the university will continue to be highly international in its composition and its outlook, attracting the brightest and best students and staff from Scotland, the rest of the UK and from all over the world.

“Edinburgh will continue to offer a wide choice of high-quality education and research programmes addressing societally important needs for skills and new knowledge, built on disciplinary and interdisciplinary strengths.

“Importantly, the university will be financially resilient,” Sir Peter added.

“Edinburgh will be an even better place to study or work than it is today because things will be done differently, embracing modern technologies but not being dictated to by them, supporting students and staff with more efficient and people-friendly methodologies.”
