The creator of some of Britain’s best-loved television programmes will visit Stockton as the town bids to become the UK’s next Town of Culture.

Sir Phil Redmond, the award-winning writer and producer behind Grange Hill, Brookside and Hollyoaks, will visit Stockton alongside Lady Redmond on Friday, October 30, as judges assess bids for the UK Town of Culture 2028 title.

Stockton is one of 15 shortlisted locations and will use the visit to showcase its communities, culture and civic pride.

Sir Phil, widely regarded as one of Britain's most influential television figures, is leading the expert panel charged with selecting the winning town.

He is expected to meet residents who helped shape Stockton’s bid and hear about the area’s culture, community spirit and ambitions.

The Liverpool-born writer revolutionised British television through programmes which tackled major social issues and gave a platform to voices often underrepresented in mainstream media.

His work helped launch the careers of numerous actors and presenters and earned him a knighthood for services to the creative industries.

Councillor Nigel Cooke, Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council’s cabinet member for environment, leisure and culture, said the visit was a key stage in the campaign.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645947.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stockton has launched a bid to become UK Town of Culture 2028 as part of a new government initiative aimed at boosting economic growth, civic pride, and access to the arts across the country *(Image: Stockton Council)*

He said: "The Stockton Community Collective can't wait to show Sir Phil and Lady Redmond around Stockton as part of our UK Town of Culture journey.

"We've been speaking to so many people to find out what they think Stockton Town has to offer and what should make up our bid.

"We know he's really keen to hear a wide range of local voices, to hear their stories. It's a great chance to share what makes our town such a unique and vibrant place."

The judging panel is considering how culture can transform communities, strengthen local identity and boost civic pride as part of the nationwide competition.

Residents, community groups, artists and cultural organisations have all played a role in developing Stockton's bid, which aims to highlight the town's heritage, diversity and creative talent.

Supporters say the title would bring investment, cultural activity and tourism while putting Stockton and its communities in the national spotlight.

The winner of the 2028 UK Town of Culture title is expected to be announced early next year.

The winning town will receive £3 million to deliver a cultural programme in 2028, while each of the two runners-up will receive £250,000.

Stockton's bid is organised around the themes of Connection, Creativity and Confidence, reflecting its aim to use culture to strengthen community life.

The Stockton Community Collective, formed by more than 40 cultural and business organisations alongside the council, is helping to shape the bid.
