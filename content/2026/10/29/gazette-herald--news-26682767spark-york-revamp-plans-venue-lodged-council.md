NEW plans to revamp a major York food and drinks venue have been lodged.

An application from Spark:York to replace its existing stairs, relocate its lift and refurbish its entrance has been submitted to City of York Council.

Plans stated the changes would make the street food venue safer and easier to use for everyone.

They added some parts of the latest plans had been scaled back since the submission of earlier ones which were withdrawn after talks with council officers.

Current plans for the Piccadilly venue follow those which were originally lodged with the council in June.

The venue, which offers space to budding food and drinks businesses, received permission to stay in its current home for another five years last October after first opening in 2018.

The latest plans stated a sound management system and limiter has been installed in line with conditions for its current planning permission.

People living in homes in Nelson's Yard which face on to the venue have previously complained about noise while its operators have said measures are in place to manage sound levels.

New plans include replacing its two existing scaffold staircases with steel structures and moving the venue's lift to the back of the site.

First floor decking is set to be extended in two places to provide space for extra unexposed outside seating.

The entrance forecourt is set to be resurfaced and fixed furniture removed from the foyer to create a space which could be used for community purposes such as art exhibitions.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/18682246.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Changes to the entrance of Spark:York are planned as part of the redevelopment

A lightwell is planned on the first floor to help illuminate the entrance hall below.

Timber cladding is set to be extended along the venue's southern boundary, with cosmetic repairs also planned along with upgrades to scaffolding railings.

Changes from plans submitted in June include removing a proposed entrance canopy in response to concerns about the impact on nearby listed buildings and the surrounding historic conservation area.

First floor decking extensions have also been limited and there will be no exposed tables close to neighbouring homes.

Plans stated the changes were necessary to make the venue fit for purpose until the end of its current lease in 2030.

The application stated: “Following the extension of the site’s consent, Spark has begun to implement a series of measures aimed at enhancing the overall quality, durability, and appearance of the space for the remainder of its operational period.

“Since opening, Spark has become a well-loved and established destination.

However, the site is now showing signs of cosmetic wear and tear, reflecting the temporary nature of a scheme originally designed for a three-year life span in this location.”
