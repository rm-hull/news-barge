A tiny hedgehog has been brought back to life after being dropped off at a rescue centre in a Greggs box.

The sweet animal has been given a second chance after two gym-goers stepped in to save its life.

Hope for Hedgehogs, in Ryton, Tyne and Wear, said the hedgehog was brought to its rescue centre yesterday (October 28) after being found weak and surrounded by crows.

Two students had just been to the gym when they spotted the animal in distress and contacted the rescue centre for advice.

The girls were told to place the hedgehog in a box and bring him in, but after being unable to find a suitable container, they went to a nearby Greggs, where staff gave them a limited-edition festive box.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645846.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rescue workers named the hedgehog Walter-Gregg, after the box he arrived in. *(Image: Hope for Hedgehogs)*

After arriving in a Greggs box, the hedgehog, who staff estimate to be between 5 and 6 weeks old, was then taken to Hope for Hedgehogs for assessment and treatment.

Rescue workers named the hedgehog Walter-Gregg, after the box he arrived in, and began assessing him.

He was found to be underweight and tested positive for intestinal fluke, an internal parasite which can affect hedgehogs' nocturnal cycle and cause health problems.

Staff believe this may have been the case for Walter-Gregg, or that he was searching for his mother.

They also said he would have almost certainly died if the girls had not brought him in.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21645845.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Anyone seeing a hedgehog out during the day should contact a wildlife rescue centre for advice, Hope for Hedgehogs said, and act immediately if the animal is being attacked by birds or covered in flies. *(Image: Hope for Hedgehogs)*

Hedgehogs are nocturnal animals, so being outside during daylight can be a sign that one is sick, has lost its mother, is injured or in difficulty.

Walter-Gregg weighed 194 grams on arrival, which is seriously underweight for a hoglet of his length, but after feeding and treatment, his weight has risen to 227 grams.

Hope for Hedgehogs said the rescued hedgehog was "spirited" and determined to shred his cage, with staff and the girls who brought Walter-Gregg in being hopeful that the hoglet will recover.

Further tests are due to take place this week, with treatment ongoing.

The rescue centre said people should not ignore hedgehogs seen during the day, particularly if they appear weak or unwell. Acting quickly is a key way that members of the public can save hedgehogs before its too late.

Anyone who sees a hedgehog out during the day should contact a wildlife rescue centre for advice, and act immediately if the animal is being attacked by birds or covered in flies, Hope for Hedgehogs said

Hope for Hedgehogs is an RSPCA approved charity based in Tyne and Wear which cares for sick, injured and orphaned hedgehogs in the North East.
