*Scotland’s universities face a funding crisis, but EIS argues compulsory redundancies threaten teaching quality, student support and the future of higher education*

There is much to be proud of in Scottish Higher Education. Scotland has 19 Higher Education institutions (HEIs), including 15 universities – both ancient and modern – offering a wide range of quality education and research to people of all backgrounds, right across the country.

Scotland ranks highly in the percentage of its population with a Higher Education qualification, with a long-term commitment to widening access, including the highly laudable policy of fee-free education for Scottish resident students, a hugely important commitment that has benefited people right across the country.

However, in recent years, we have started to see cracks appear in the veneer, and indeed in the under-layers, of our proud traditions in Higher Education. As government funding has been squeezed, institutions have relied more and more on attracting students from outside Scotland – specifically the fees that they pay – to bring more money into the sector.

But, even more recently, numbers of international students have waned as a result of the UK Government’s hostile immigration policy, with the result that the associated level of fee income has also declined. This is where government, both at UK and Scotland level, should have stepped in to increase to level of funding support across the sector, but has failed to do so.

Sadly, this has led to some University Courts and Principals seeking to slash costs by reducing course provision and cutting jobs, in a short-sighted approach which ignores that excellence in learning, teaching and research are the core purposes, and key selling points, of Scotland’s Higher Education sector.

Over the past year, the EIS has fought, and won, against a number of HEIs seeking to impose compulsory redundancies on staff. Currently, the EIS is in dispute with Glasgow Caledonian University over its plans to make 100 staff redundant.

Since GCU announced in March that it was seeking these redundancies, the EIS has, along with sister trade unions, engaged in good faith in two collective consultation processes, submitted detailed alternative proposals that would remove any need for compulsory redundancies, and engaged in ACAS arbitration to attempt to resolve the dispute.

Sadly, despite the best efforts of the recognised trade unions, GCU management has rejected all proposals in their entirety and pushed ahead with their plans to make staff compulsorily redundant.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21647268.jpg?type=mds-article-620)EIS remains fully prepared to enter meaningful talks with GCU leadership *(Image: EIS)*

The EIS believes that these redundancies will have a hugely detrimental impact on the range and quality of the provision offered at the university and will negatively impact the quality of education that students receive. Fewer staff at GCU means less support for students, including less time for teaching, marking, feedback and academic support.

Incredibly, all of this comes against the background of GCU already having one of the worst staff to student ratios in the entire country – and not just in Scotland, but across the whole of the UK. While seeking to cut 100 jobs, GCU has recently spent £4.5M+ on land purchases. It currently holds one of the highest levels of cash reserves of any modern Scottish university, at £90.7M as of July this year. The University also has an anticipated surplus of £5.6M for the year 2025/26 and total reserves upwards of £300M. This university is not broke.

Meanwhile, staff are already dealing with unmanageable workloads, partially due to the already very poor staff to student ratio at GCU, and are liable to burn out if further job cuts are made.

While this is extremely detrimental to staff, ultimately students will also suffer as a result of senior management’s choices.

In their determination to fight the cuts, EIS members at Glasgow Caledonian University have now taken 25 days of strike action, as they seek to force a change of mind from university management.

They have been joined by colleagues – both academic and support staff – in other trade unions at GCU, with all four recognised staff trade unions, for the first time in the history of GCU, now engaged in programmes of industrial action in defiance of the University’s cuts agenda.

Support from students at GCU has also been strong, with many visiting picket lines and lobbying the University Principal and Court to halt the planned programme of cuts. This show of support has been greatly appreciated by lecturing and support staff.

Commenting on the ongoing dispute and programme of strike action, EIS General Secretary Andrea Bradley said: “The persistent absence, stonewalling and inaction of GCU management throughout this dispute is deeply disappointing and deeply concerning. Senior leaders have failed to meaningfully engage with trade union representatives to discuss a resolution. By choosing to ignore the legitimate concerns of its staff, management is abandoning its responsibility to both employees and students.”

Ms Bradley continued: “We commend our members, and members of our sister trade unions at Glasgow Caledonian University for their unwavering courage, solidarity, and commitment during this programme of strike action in defence of quality learning and teaching at GCU by protecting jobs and professionalism.

Taking strike action is never an easy decision, but the remarkable unity shown across campus, and between the different recognised trade unions and the student body, demonstrates just how vital the fight against compulsory redundancies is for the future of GCU."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21647271.jpg?type=mds-article-620)EIS members at Glasgow Caledonian University have now taken 25 days of strike action, *(Image: EIS)*

Reiterating its commitment to finding a suitable resolution, the EIS remains fully prepared to enter meaningful talks with GCU leadership at any time to settle the dispute. The union urges management to return to the negotiating table with a genuine commitment to provide the requested no compulsory redundancy guarantee, thereby bringing an end to the disruption and restoring stability to the University community.

Glasgow Caledonian University’s motto is, “For the Common Weal”. That common good would best be served by halting the programme of cuts, and working with unions to deliver a brighter, fairer future for GCU, its students and staff.
