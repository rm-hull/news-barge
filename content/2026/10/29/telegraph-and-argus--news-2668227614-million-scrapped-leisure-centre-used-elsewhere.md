£14 million of funding allocated for a scrapped leisure centre CAN be used elsewhere in Bradford, despite the council’s Reform UK leader recently saying this was “categorically untrue.”

For weeks Bradford Council’s Reform leadership and Shipley MP Anna Dixon (Labour) have been in a back and forth spat over the funding for the Squire Lane scheme, which was scrapped in July.

The MP claimed the Government had said they would allow the council to use the funding allocated for other projects – including work to re-open Bingley Pool.

But Reform had dismissed this, and earlier this week put out a statement that Ms Dixon had been making “false claims.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21223752.jpg?type=mds-article-620)What the Squire Lane Leisure Centre would have looked like, had it not been scrapped

## Reform UK corrected

The Shipley MP has now produced a letter from Government saying the use of the funding “including any consideration of alternative projects” was up to Bradford Council.

On Wednesday morning, Reform welcomed the news, and said it would “look to utilise the funds in the future.”

## Why was the Squire Lane Leisure Centre scrapped?

The Squire Lane leisure centre had been in the planning stages for years, and in 2021, the council secured a £20m Levelling Up Fund grant for the scheme from the then-Conservative Government.

It was awarded to the Bradford West Constituency.

Planning for the centre, which would have included a pool and studio space, was approved earlier this year.

But shortly after Reform swept into power in May’s local elections, the decision was made to scrap the centre, with the Executive saying the financial burden of moving ahead with the £32.9 million scheme was too high.

£5.4 million had already been spent developing the scheme and preparing the site for demolition.

At the time it was claimed that the Government would “claw back” the remaining £14.6 million.

## Where does Bingley Pool come into this?

Since then there have been calls for the funding to be diverted to re-open Bingley Pool, which has been closed since 2020.

![Bingley Pool shut in 2020](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21383239.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bingley Pool

The plan to re-open that pool had also been scrapped due to cost concerns, and earlier this month Reform ruled out the pool re-opening as a council facility.

## Council leader and MP row

Ms Dixon had argued that the funding for Squire Lane could be diverted from Bradford West to the Shipley constituency, and used to re-open the pool.

Her claims lead to a statement from Reform earlier this week, saying: “Anna Dixon MP (Shipley) and some local councillors continue to falsely claim that monies which would otherwise have been spent on the cancelled Squire Lane project are now available to spend on Bingley Pool.

“Bradford Council has confirmed, however, that no advice has been received from the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government to that effect.”

Councillor Place said: “It is unfortunate that our critics are now seeking political gain, not the good of local people.

"Claims that Bradford Council has existing funding available to spend on Bingley Pool are categorically untrue.

"Instead of making these false claims, I invite Anna Dixon MP to join us in lobbying her Labour colleagues in Westminster, for that Levelling Up award to be allocated again.”

## MP 'told Reform in September'

But Ms Dixon produced a letter from the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government that confirmed the money could be spent elsewhere by Bradford Council.

It said: “Decisions regarding the future use of Bradford's allocation, including any consideration of alternative projects following the cancellation of the Squire Lane scheme, are matters for Bradford Council.”

Ms Dixon says she shared this letter with Reform in September.

## 'I am glad that he has now accepted that I was right'

The Bradford Reform group has now acknowledged the funding will remain available for schemes in Bradford.

Ms Dixon said: “While I welcome that Cllr Place has finally u-turned on his position and accepted that the remaining £14.6 million of funding can be reallocated, it should not have taken the Reform UK Leader more than three months to publicly acknowledge this.

“When the Squire Lane project was cancelled in July, I immediately recognised that this money could potentially be redirected to other projects.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21297106.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shipley MP Anna Dixon *(Image: Submitted)*

“It is deeply concerning that the Reform leadership on the council were willing to cancel a multi-million-pound project without a clear understanding of whether this funding could be retained for the benefit of Bradford.

“I know residents across the Bradford district will be furious that the council leader has wasted months, failing to make use of millions of secure government funding.

“Instead, Cllr Place chose to repeatedly and publicly accuse me of making false claims on this issue. I am glad that he has now accepted that I was right.

“What is most important now is that residents get a clear alternative plan for how this money will be spent, and I urge Cllr Place to publish it as soon as possible.”

## So how ill it be spent?

Cllr Place acknowledged that the funding was available for spending elsewhere in Bradford, but claimed the council was only made aware of this on Wednesday.

On Thursday morning, he said: “After first raising enquiries with senior council officers in early September, only yesterday afternoon did our Executive receive written confirmation from the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government that monies planned for the Squire Lane project will be retained by Bradford Council.

“We warmly welcome that these monies will not be clawed back by national Government and that they remain available as a capital grant.

“As we have only learnt of these available monies very recently, we are not yet able to commit their use for any particular project across our district but, of course, will look to utilise them in future, to the benefit of Bradford residents.”
