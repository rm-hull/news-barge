Children across the North East will face tougher protections from vaping and nicotine addiction from today (Thursday) as a major crackdown on products aimed at under-18s comes into force in the UK.

The government’s Tobacco and Vapes Act bans the sale of nicotine-free vapes and nicotine pouches to under-18s, while tightening rules on other nicotine products.

The new rules build on the existing ban on selling nicotine-containing vapes to children.

Dame Diana Johnson, Minister of State for Public Health and Patient Safety, said: "Our landmark Tobacco and Vapes Act was the greatest piece of public health legislation in a generation.

"Six months to the day since the Act became law, we're extending protections for young people and stopping businesses from cashing in on our children’s health."

Adults are now banned from buying or trying to buy vaping or nicotine products for under-18s.

Giveaways, deep discounts and vending machines selling or dispensing these products are now banned in shops and online.

Trading Standards will enforce the rules in England, backed by new fixed-penalty powers and up to £30 million a year until 2028/29.

The package includes up to £10 million a year for Trading Standards and 120 specialist apprentices to tackle underage and illicit vape and tobacco sales.

Professor Chris Whitty, Chief Medical Officer, said: "Addicting children to nicotine or encouraging them to vape could lead to the risk of serious health harms – it is completely unacceptable.

"These measures will help protect children from marketing of nicotine products and attempts to encourage them to vape."

Health officials say vaping is less harmful than smoking and can help adult smokers quit, but it is not risk-free and should not be used by children or non-smokers.

The measures are part of the phased rollout of the Tobacco and Vapes Act.

The legislation backs the government’s goal of a smoke-free UK and a stronger focus on prevention.

Public health ministers across the devolved nations have welcomed the changes.

Maree Todd, Scotland’s Minister for Public Health, said: "We want to protect future generations from nicotine addiction and we know that nicotine products, particularly pouches, are starting to gain popularity among young people.

"By banning the sale of these products to under-18s, the Scottish Government is taking a crucial step in protecting people’s health.

"This in turn is helping people across Scotland to live longer, healthier lives."

Nerys Evans, Deputy Minister for Public and Preventative Health in Wales, said: "We all want to see our children grow up happy and healthy, free from the risk of nicotine addiction.

"Vapes and nicotine products have been marketed towards young people, drawing far too many in.

"The new restrictions mean that all vaping and nicotine products cannot be sold to under 18s and the tactics used to tempt them will no longer be allowed.
