JD WETHERSPOON has said “poppies are not banned” after an incident where a customer wearing a poppy was allegedly turned away from a pub.

The pub chain, which runs three venues in the Bradford district, spoke out after reports that a poppy-wearing customer was turned away.

The incident happened during an anti-immigration demonstration outside The Star pub in Gosport, Hampshire, on Sunday, October 25.

Pub bosses said extra security was brought in for the demonstration, and staff refused entry to several visitors, including people wearing poppies.

JD Wetherspoon assured Bradford veterans and other customers wearing poppies that they would be welcome.

The Turls Green in Centenary Square is only stone’s throw away from the main Bradford War Memorial in Victoria Square, on Prince’s Way, which will soon hold Remembrance Day services.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21642468.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A Remembrance Sunday service at the Bradford war memorial

A Wetherspoon spokesman said: "In fact, Wetherspoons has not banned veterans wearing poppies.

"Wetherspoon is a long-standing supporter of the Royal British Legion Poppy Appeal and sells poppies in its pubs."

A video posted on social media shows one security staff member at The Star pub in Hampshire answering questions about people entering wearing a poppy.

In the video, he said: "You put it in your pocket and take it off mate, yes you can but don't put it on inside... you'll be asked to leave."

The incident comes after reports that Wetherspoon’s profits fell by more than a quarter as higher wages and property taxes drove up costs.

The company, which runs 792 pubs across the UK, said pre-tax profits fell 28 per cent to £58.6 million in the year to July 2026.

The Turls Green opened on December 5, 2004, as part of a £1.8 million development and was originally a Lloyds No.1 bar in the JD Wetherspoon chain.

In addition to The Turls Green, JD Wetherspoons operates two other pubs in the Bradford district – The Myrtle Grove on Main Street, Bingley, and The Livery Rooms, on North Street in Keighley.

Bradford’s war memorial has stood in the city centre for more than 104 years, after it was unveiled on July 1, 1922, the sixth anniversary of the first day of the Battle of the Somme.

A Remembrance service will be held in Centenary Square on Sunday, November 8.

A short Armistice Day service starts at 11am on Wednesday, November 11, at Bradford’s war memorial.
