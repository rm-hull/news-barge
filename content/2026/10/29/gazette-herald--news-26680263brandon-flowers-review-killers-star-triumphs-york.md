**REVIEW: Brandon Flowers, York Barbican, Wednesday, October 21, 2026**

THERE are frontmen, there are showmen, and then there is Brandon Flowers, a man seemingly incapable of walking onto a stage without making the occasion feel several sizes bigger than the building containing him.

Stepping back in time, and at the invitation of the legendary York music promoter Tim Hornsby, The Killers played Fibbers, Stonebow on May 3,2004 before the release of debut album Hot Fuss.

Over two decades later, The Killers’ singer arrived at The Barbican to promote his latest album Thrasher without the arena machinery of his day job.

Opening with The Red Ground, Flowers immediately established that this would not simply be a victory lap through familiar hits.

Johnny Cash style Does It Ever Cross Your Mind? followed before Crossfire, still one of his finest solo songs, provided the first reminder of just how naturally his widescreen romanticism survives outside The Killers.

Flowers told stories of how the newer Tiger’s Blood, Plans and Paradise were inspired by childhood memories in Utah of his father playing Cash, Waylon Jennings and Willie Nelson on vinyl.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644723.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Brandon Flowers at York Barbican - photo by Rob Loud

These sat alongside Still Want You and Angel, Flowers moving between generations of his catalogue with the assurance of someone who no longer needs to prove there is life beyond his band.

But Flowers has always been at his most compelling when the polish allows a little vulnerability through.

From the album Flamingo, Magdalena supplied that characteristic mixture of faith, yearning and desert-road Americana, while recent release Miss America demonstrated his enduring attraction to songs that sound like journeys in search of something just beyond reach. Then came the inevitable crossing of musical borders.

The Killers’ In The Car Outside sounded perfectly at home among the solo material, followed by Hard Enough, Lonely Town and the irresistible pulse of Can’t Deny My Love. Only The Young offered another reminder of what a strong collection Flowers assembled away from the Las Vegas mothership.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21644722.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Brandon Flowers at York Barbican - photo by Rob Loud

But no Brandon Flowers audience was ever likely to resist The Killers entirely. All These Things That I’ve Done provided the communal release such a song demands, its enormous chorus seemingly designed for rooms to sing rather than individuals. More intriguing was Runaway Horses, receiving its solo debut, before Read My Mind brought another of Flowers’ most treasured songs into the Barbican.

The closing An American Dream was a wonderful and appropriate destination, a special song which on its own captures exactly what Flowers is aiming to achieve with his latest release.

Dreams, cars, highways, small towns, escape, romance and disappointment are the thread which runs through his songs and tonight saw Flowers stepping outside the neon, embracing childhood influences and turn his songwriting into something new and rather special.

The venues may become smaller when Brandon Flowers goes solo. The songs, however, stubbornly refuse to.
