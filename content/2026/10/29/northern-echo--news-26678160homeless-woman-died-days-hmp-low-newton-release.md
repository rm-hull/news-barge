A County Durham prisoner died within days of being released with no home to go to.

Kate Allen, 36, was released from HMP Low Newton on November 27 last year, having served time for theft.

She'd been in hospital since November 18 until her release without any accommodation, and within days was back in hospital being treated for pneumonia and sepsis from. She died in hospital in Harrogate on December 8.

A Prisons and Probation Ombudsman (PPO) investigation found probation staff missed the chance to find Katie accommodation before her release, despite her hospital stay, poor health and a long history of substance use. Allen died from sepsis and cardiogenic shock caused by pneumonia and a pulmonary embolism.

A government spokesperson said they accepted the recommendations and are “pulling every possible lever to address prison leaver homelessness”.

Allen's Community Offender Manager (COM) said she had been unable to submit an application for housign as she could not obtain the consent needed to make a referral.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20575353.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HMP Low Newton *(Image: Stuart Boulton/The Northern Echo.)*

However, the PPO said there had been an opportunity for prison and probation staff to work together to complete the necessary arrangements while Katie remained under prison escort in hospital.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/16170727.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Harrogate Hospital *(Image: Google Streetview)*

It added that this could have included asking prison staff to speak to Katie to obtain her consent for the accommodation application.

The report said: “While it is evident that Ms Allen’s COM made efforts to support Ms Allen to source accommodation following her release, we consider that there was a missed opportunity to support Ms Allen with her accommodation needs prior to release.”

The PPO called on the Yorkshire probation director to make stronger arrangements to ensure accommodation referrals can be completed when prisoners are in hospital before their release and said prison and probation services need to communicate better.

The recommendation was accepted and an action plan said staff would be reminded to continue referring people to councils for accommodation assessments while they are in hospital before release.

Katie was treated for pneumonia and sepsis at York Hospital from December 1 to December 5, when she discharged herself.

There was no further information about her whereabouts between discharge and her death at Harrogate Hospital three days later, the report said.

A coroner accepted the cause of death provided by a hospital doctor.

An inquest concluded on October 14, 2026, that Katie died from naturally occurring disease against a background of chronic intravenous drug use.

The coroner found it was not possible to determine whether her death would have been prevented had she remained in hospital and continued treatment.

Allen was remanded to HMP Low Newton on August 15, 2025, charged with theft and sentenced to 22 weeks on September 16.

She was released on October 15 but recalled on November 14 for breaching her licence conditions.

She later reported thigh pain, shortness of breath and confusion to prison staff and after being assessed, was admitted to hospital on November 18.

The PPO said: “Ms Allen’s recent hospitalisation, poor physical health and long history of substance use meant that she was particularly vulnerable, and homelessness would likely have had a significant impact on her.”

A government spokesperson said: “Our thoughts remain with Kate Allen’s loved ones.

“We’ve fully accepted the Ombudsman’s recommendations, and we’re pulling every possible lever to address prison leaver homelessness. We’re working with charities and councils to halve it by 2029, backed by record investment in probation services.

“More broadly, the Prime Minister is making rough sleeping a national priority, with £874m invested to help people off the streets and into long-term support.”
