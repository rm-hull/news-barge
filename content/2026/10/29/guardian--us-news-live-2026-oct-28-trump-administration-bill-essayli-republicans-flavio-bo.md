Obama campaigns in Georgia as Trump stumps in Ohio in dueling midterm rallies

With midterm elections less than a week away, Donald Trump and Barack Obama were set to appear at rallies on Wednesday night as Republicans and Democrats make some of their final appeals to voters in key states.

Obama, who was recently in Michigan to support Abdul El-Sayed, is in Georgia, where he will campaign for Keisha Lance Bottoms, the former Atlanta mayor seeking the governor’s office, and the senator Jon Ossoff.

Meanwhile, Donald Trump, who has appeared at more than 20 rallies in October, was returning to the battleground state of Ohio to support Republican candidates, including Vivek Ramaswamy, who is running for governor, and Jon Husted for Senate.

JD Vance was in North Carolina stumping for Michael Whatley, the former RNC chair, who faces a tough race for Senate against Democrat and former governor Roy Cooper.

Obama mocks Trump's response to Cheesecake Factory jibe. 'They mobilized a whole of government response to that joke, not to inflation, but to that joke!'

We are still watching simultaneous rallies, for Republicans in Ohio, and for Democrats in Georgia, that have overlapped in interesting ways at times.

Minutes ago in Ohio, Donald Trump responded his indignation to his predecessor Barack Obama’s joke that “he’s got the White House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory.”

“I paid for it myself. I fixed it, and now it’s more beautiful and in better shape,” Trump said, defending the quality of his renovations to the White House. “President Obama,” he added, was miffed because he is someone “who doesn’t understand cleanliness, who doesn’t understand broken, cracked stone and sidewalk, who doesn’t understand that having asphalt going to your main entrance is not the right thing”.

A short time later in Georgia, Obama repeated his jibe and then reflected on the outraged response to his joke.

“The guy is building a ballroom that is bigger than the White House. He’s paved over the Rose Garden, put a helicopter pad on the South Lawn, and there is gold everywhere. So much gold,” Obama said. “This is a fact, I’m not making this up. He, the president of the United States, is spending time super-gluing gold medallions to the mantel in the Oval Office. I am not making this up. He is literally doing arts and crafts while we are fighting a war and trying to deal with out-of-control prices.”

“I heard the president was not happy when I joked over the weekend that he’s got the white House looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory,” the former president said. “First of all, let me get this on the record: I am the father of two daughters. Do you think, could have survived their childhood without going to the Cheesecake Factory?”

“Chucky Cheese and California Pizza Kitchen and the Cheesecake Factory – that was my jam for about a decade,” Obama said.

“The interesting thing about how the administration responded to the speech I gave the other day. They did not dispute what I said about them being directly responsible for rising prices. They did not argue with that,” Obama continued. “They did not deny tariffs were contributing to higher costs for families. They didn’t say anything about that. I accused this administration, based on the facts, that they were engaging in unprecedented levels of corruption. They didn’t say anything about that. The president wasn’t even mad about the Saddam Hussein comparison. That part didn’t bother him, but he was very upset about the Cheesecake Factory comments. He took great umbrage. He said, I didn’t understand the work of the artisans in the White House.”

“It’s all crazy, but this was the really crazy part,” Obama added. “They mobilized a whole of government response to that joke - not to inflation, but to that joke.”

“The White House even called the secretary of the treasury – this is the man who is in charge of the economy, the person who’s supposed to be figuring out how to make your life more affordable, the guy who’s supposed to be figuring out how to lower inflation and reduce interest rates. They told him he should hold a press conference in front of the Cheesecake Factory,” Obama said, to laughter from the crowd.

'Trump went on the Jeffrey Epstein plane!' heckler shouts at Trump rally in Ohio

Donald Trump is 40 minutes into his remarks in Ohio, where he was heckled over his ties to Jeffrey Epstein, the convicted child sex offender he socialized with for at least 17 years.

During a pause in the president’s remarks, a man shouted: “Trump went on the Jeffrey Epstein plane!” before being removed from the crowd.

“He mentioned Epstein. So I threw Jeffrey Epstein out of a club like 20 years ago, because I thought he was a whack job. That was a great thing I did, I must say,” Trump said. “I threw Jeffrey Epstein the hell out of Mar-a-Lago. And I was the one that was totally exonerated from him.”

A protester ( in sunglasses) yells at Donald Trump as he speaks during a midterm campaign rally at Shoemaker Center in Chillicothe, Ohio on 28 October. Photograph: Kent Nishimura/AFP/Getty Images

While Trump’s aides have repeatedly claimed that Trump banned Epstein from Mar-a-Lago “because he was a creep”, the president has explained the falling out with his old friend differently, saying that the reason was that Epstein “stole people that worked for me”.

Last year, when a reporter asked Trump if one of the people he was referring to was the late Virginia Giuffre, one of Epstein’s most prominent accusers who said in a lawsuit that she was hired away from the Mar-a-Lago spa by Epstein’s accomplice Ghislaine Maxwell in 2000, when she was 16.

Giuffre, who died last year, alleged in her complaint that she was first abused by Epstein and Maxwell together, and then “lent out to other powerful men”, including then Prince Andrew.

“I think she worked at the spa,” Trump replied then. “I think so. I think that was one of the people, yeah. He stole her. And by the way, she had no complaints about us, as you know. None whatsoever.”

The president’s claim that one of the employees Epstein “stole” from him, leading to their break, was the 16-year-old Giuffre complicated the timeline. Giuffre was hired away from Mar-a-Lago in 2000, but two years later, Trump spoke highly of Epstein to a reporter.

“I’ve known Jeff for 15 years. Terrific guy,” Trump told New York magazine in late 2002. “He’s a lot of fun to be with. It is even said that he likes beautiful women as much as I do, and many of them are on the younger side.”

Sarah Blaskey, a Miami Herald investigative reporter, also pointed out in her 2020 book on Mar-a-Lago that Epstein remained on the membership rolls of Mar-a-Lago until October 2007, more than a year after he was first arrested and charged with soliciting prostitution from a minor.

As Trump boasts about Iran war in Ohio, Ossoff mocks his claims in Georgia

At rallies occurring at the same time, Donald Trump just boasted in Ohio about what he calls the great success of his war in Iran, while Jon Ossoff, in Georgia, mocked Trump for that war and other failures.

Trump made no mention of the fact that the war with Iran he started in February is still not over, and repeated his lie that the war was necessary to prevent Iran from making a nuclear weapon.

“While the crook president and his crook family rake in billions from all over the world, you pay more for everything,” Ossoff told Georgians. “They lied us into war and spent most of the last year covering up for the world’s most famous pedophile.”

“Now remember, they also promised peace. They promised peace. Instead, today marks eight months of a needless war that the useless, draft-dodging commander-in-chief can’t figure out how to end or how to win,” Ossoff continued.

“Remember last year, last year they claimed Iran’s nuclear program had been obliterated? Do you remember that? And then this year, they told us it posed an imminent threat. Both times lies,” he added.

“On day four of the war, day four, the president declared victory,” he continued. “On day 11, Trump said, quote, ‘the war is very complete’. Day 13, quote, ‘we won’. Day 21, ‘getting very close’. Day 32, quote ‘leaving very soon’. On day 40, the president declared, quote, ‘total and complete victory’. Today is day 243. Twenty one Americans are dead and hundreds wounded. Iran still has its uranium and drones and missiles while diesel hits its all time high. They’ve drained our stockpiles to achieve nothing while the president builds himself a victory arch.”

Democratic congresswoman Haley Stevens' fiancé says he is voting for Republican Mike Rogers, not Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan Senate race

Jason Turkish, who is engaged to be married to Democratic congresswoman Haley Stevens, appeared at a campaign event for Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers in Michigan on Wednesday and told reporters that he was voting for the Republican, not Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, who narrowly defeated Stevens in the Democratic primary in August.

Turkish, a disabilities and civil rights lawyer, said at a Republican Jewish Coalition event for Rogers that El-Sayed’s refusal to say that Iran was a greater threat to the United States than Israel in the first general election debate was “100% disqualifying to be a United States senator”.

Turkish, described by Jewish Insider as “a prominent Jewish Democrat in Michigan”, said that El-Sayed’s rhetoric “makes folks unsafe”.

“When he says genocide over and over,” Turkish said of El-Sayed’s criticism of Israel’s destruction of Gaza, “he makes my community less safe.”

“We’ve never seen a candidate for US Senate act so recklessly toward the safety of Jews in the community as we have from Abdul,” Turkish told the Detroit News.

According to the Detroit News, Turkish has donated Democrats and Republicans, including $200,000 to Democratic candidates in the midterm elections and over $24,000 to support Maine Republican Susan Collins.

Donald Trump is scheduled to speak soon at a rally for Republican candidates in Chillicothe, Ohio, where the party’s nominee for governor, Vivek Ramaswamy, and the sitting senator Jon Husted, have already spoken.

Meanwhile in Georgia, Barack Obama is waiting to address a Democratic rally in Atlanta, in support of the party’s nominee for governor, Keisha Lance Bottoms and sitting senator Jon Ossoff.

Bottoms and Ossoff are scheduled to speak immediately before Obama. The Georgia rally is running about 30 minutes behind schedule, meaning that it is likely that Trump and Obama will be speaking at the same time.

As we watch the simultaneous rallies for in Ohio and Georgia, we are currently being treated to a musical split-screen.

Janelle Monáe is currently performing live in Atlanta, following a performance by the HBCU Clark Atlanta University marching band, while Trump supporters in Ohio are being treated to his rally playlist, which includes songs from a number of artists who sued to have him stop using their music. The crowd is currently listening to Sinéad O’Connor’s version of Nothing Compares 2 U, which her estate said in 2024 she would have been “disgusted, hurt, and insulted” to have used at Trump’s rallies.

During tonight’s rally in Georgia, Raphael Warnock, the state’s Democratic senator, described the upcoming election as a “moral moment in America”.

“There are demagogues at the highest levels of our government who are trying to divide us and distract us in order to dominate us, but we come here tonight to say the jig is up.”

Warnock criticized Republicans in Congress for supporting legislation that will take away healthcare from millions of Americans, and cause 1.2 million children to lose access to Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (Snap) benefits. He referenced a photo of Republicans praying before voting on Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill Act.

“Our nations leaders, led by the speaker of the House, Mike Johnson, gathered together, put their arms around each other and they knelt down to pray, donning the garments of virtue while literally taking food out of the mouths of hungry children,” Warnock said.

The US Federal Reserve opted to keep interest rates steady. Despite inflation remaining above the central bank’s target of 2%, its leaders chose to maintain interest rates at their current level.

Trump rages at Alaska Democrat whose campaign reportedly recruited man with same name as Republican opponent

In a social media post, Trump insisted that Peltola’s actions were illegal (it’s not clear that they were) and that she should be disqualified from the race.

“It has just been determined that the Democrats in Alaska illegally searched for, and recruited, a man named Dan Sullivan to run against our distinguished Republican Senator, Dan Sullivan, in order to defraud, confuse, and obfuscate the Election, thereby taking votes away from the REAL Senator, Dan Sullivan,” Trump wrote on his personal social media account.
