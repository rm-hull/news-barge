# Judge's 'charitable' decision as dealer who controlled crack and heroin line escapes jail sentence

## The decision was taken after taking into account delays in his sentencing.

A man who controlled a Leeds drug line selling crack cocaine and heroin has avoided prison due to a delay in his court case.

Kailam Longley, 25, of Haslewood Drive, Burmantofts, was said to be responsible for the line selling class A drugs. Recorder Baird said at Leeds Crown Court on Wednesday, October 28: "These matters go back to 2022. Had you been dealt with nearer the time, I'm absolutely sure you would be facing an immediate sentence.

"But the reality is we are now four years on and you have managed to address your alcohol and drugs problems and are keen to put your life on better footing.

"You were responsible for operating a drugs line in the east Leeds area supplying crack cocaine and heroin and I have no doubt your role was significant in supplying drugs to those on the street."

Mitigating, Stephen Walsh told the court a stand-down report had been prepared in the case. He said: "The real mitigation is that he pleaded guilty at a relatively early opportunity... Although four years have passed it is right the nature of that delay, in the main, was not due to his actions.

"What that has allowed him to do is demonstrate he is no longer involved in criminality. Since that time, he has not been arrested for any other offences.

"He is actively looking for employment and the Probation Service say that unpaid work could be an avenue to that as they can help him in that department."

Addressing Longley, the judge said: "I have got to take into account the delay and the progress you have been making and I think sending you to prison would have an adverse effect on you so I'm taking a charitable approach."

Longley was made the subject of a sentence of two years suspended for two years during which he has to carry out 240 hours of unpaid work.
