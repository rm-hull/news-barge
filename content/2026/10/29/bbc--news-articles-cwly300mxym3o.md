**Northern Ireland's First Minister Michelle O'Neill has been blocked by the DUP from attending an economic summit involving Prime Minister Andy Burnham.**

The Sinn Féin vice-president and Deputy First Minister Emma Little-Pengelly were both invited to attend the National Economic Council in Glasgow on Friday with other devolved leaders.

The DUP leader Gavin Robinson confirmed that his party colleague Little-Pengelly will not participate in joint engagements with O'Neill and permission will not be granted for the first minister to operate on a solo basis.

The move follows unionist anger over Sinn Féin's involvement in protests which prevented a contentious Orange Order march in Portadown from passing along the mainly nationalist Garvaghy Road.

![Emma Little-Pengelly with brown hair, dark blazer and white blouse standing beside Gavin Robinson who has grey hair, glasses, dark blazer, tie and white shirt](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2383/live/f2f62b00-d3b0-11f1-8d02-39b68eca6add.jpg)

Gavin Robinson, pictured with Emma Little-Pengelly in September, met party officers on Thursday morning

A meeting of the party's ruling officer committee was held earlier on Thursday.

DUP leader Gavin Robinson said the first minister's "arrogance will have consequences", but O'Neill accused the party of being "reckless and irresponsible".

"Her complaints today that she's frustrated from engaging with the British Prime Minister within the sovereign United Kingdom will so be it because her law breaking must have consequences," Robinson said.

O'Neill said the DUP leader's comments were "arrogant and supremacist" adding that the DUP "is consumed with their own internal leadership troubles and divisions".

She said she would be at the Assembly on Monday with her party colleagues to "keep showing up every day for people".

Earlier she described the move to block the meeting with the prime minister as "reckless and irresponsible" and meant people in Northern Ireland "will not be represented".

"This is a reactive stunt, driven by the DUP's own internal pressures and electoral problems," she said in a statement posted online.

The prime minister revived the National Economic Council over the summer to encourage greater collaboration between the nations and regions.

Earlier Little-Pengelly said the concerns arising from O'Neill's conduct and the "anger and hurt felt by many people across Northern Ireland, cannot simply be ignored or minimised".

She said the decision comes after stopping a separate planned engagement with the President of Ireland, Catherine Connolly.

"I have requested a separate meeting with the Prime Minister to ensure Northern Ireland's interests, including the urgent budgetary and funding challenges facing the Executive, continue to be represented directly," she added.

The Secretary of State Sir Chris Bryant said it was "deeply frustrating" that the Northern Ireland executive would not be represented at the National Economic Council.

"I understand feelings are running high but it must be in the interests of the people of Northern Ireland that everyone gets round the table."

The DUP ruling committee met to discuss what steps it would take against Sinn Féin over the Drumcree dispute

O'Neill and other senior Sinn Féin figures have been interviewed by police investigating last month's unnotified demonstrations in Portadown, with unionists accusing the party of acting unlawfully.

The Sinn Féin deputy leader has defended her involvement, saying it was the "right thing to do".

The Traditional Unionist Voice (TUV) leader Jim Allister said "the DUP's sham response today was beyond pathetic" adding "the DUP is interested only in clinging to office, not principle".

Alliance leader Naomi Long said refusing to attend or preventing others from attending the meeting with the prime minister was reckless and put party interests ahead of Northern Ireland's economic future and the needs of the public.

The Ulster Unionist Party leader Jon Burrows told BBC's Evening Extra programme the situation was a "political fudge" that was "all choreography and no consequences," adding that the move would "probably galvanise Sinn Féin further at the ballot box."

The Social Democratic and Labour Party's (SDLP) Matthew O'Toole said "plunging this place into more political chaos is not a sensible strategic move for unionism".

"Ultimately it's only going to make reasonable people here conclude that the largest unionist party simply doesn't want to make the status quo work," he added.

The DUP has been under pressure from unionist rivals and some within its own ranks over how to respond to the row over Drumcree.

Last week, DUP MP Carla Lockhart stepped down from the party's officer board over their leader Gavin Robinson not withdrawing Little-Pengelly to force O'Neill from their joint Stormont office.

On Thursday Lockhart said she will follow her "own course" if the party does not seek to remove Sinn Féin's Michelle O'Neill as Stormont's first minister.

Robinson said that while O'Neill and her party colleagues "should be punished, that does not include punishing the rest of Northern Ireland".

The Upper Bann MP, who last week quit the DUP's ruling officer board in opposition to party leader Gavin Robinson's approach, outlined her position in a letter to colleagues which she later posted on Facebook.

Other DUP representatives including assembly member Diane Forsythe have since backed Lockhart's calls for O'Neill to be "removed from office".

On Tuesday, Forsythe said that in the absence of such "meaningful action", official engagements through the joint Executive Office should be stopped immediately as a "bare minimum".

On Wednesday, a second DUP assembly member called for O'Neill to go.

Maurice Bradley said removing O'Neill "would not, in itself, require the Assembly to collapse".

Bradley added that the party "must stand together" and "support Carla Lockhart".

Other party members including former MP Lord McCrea and Mid Ulster councillor Clement Cuthbertson have also backed Lockhart.

Party leader Robinson said last week that he was "sorry" to see Lockhart quit the DUP's officer board but added that she remained "a key part of our team".

Speaking on the BBC's The View programme, the Robinson said the party had reached a democratic decision on how to deal with the Drumcree fallout.

He questioned whether it was necessary to "punish everyone in Northern Ireland when actually we should be punishing those who broke the law".

"We have considered all options and we keep all options on the table," he added.

Last week, a court heard Police Service of Northern Ireland intelligence information that dissidents may have mounted gun attacks on police had officers cleared a path for the parade.
