Stocks in London closed higher on Thursday, as investors weighed earnings, rate calls in Europe and the US and a hefty share price fall for Google owner Alphabet.

In London, the FTSE 100 Index ended up 27.57 points, 0.3%, at 10,722.07.

The FTSE 250 ended 34.90 points higher, 0.1%, at 24,674.16, and the AIM all-share advanced 2.53 points, 0.3%, at 763.45.

In European equity markets on Thursday, the CAC 40 in Paris ended up 1.1%, while the DAX 40 in Frankfurt climbed 1.3% after the European Central Bank’s decision to leave interest rates unchanged.

The central bank maintained its deposit facility rate at 2.50%, the main refinancing operations rate at 2.65% and the marginal lending facility rate at 2.90%, as it warned that rising energy prices could push inflation higher while weighing on economic growth.

“Energy prices have continued to rise, posing upside risks to the inflation outlook and downside risks to the growth outlook,” the ECB said.

Thursday’s decision came hours after ECB president Christine Lagarde announced that she would step down on February 7 2027, eight months before her term was due to expire in October.

Ms Lagarde, who has led the central bank since November 2019, informed the Governing Council and European Council president Antonio Costa of her decision, setting the stage for a leadership contest at a critical time for eurozone monetary policy.

JPMorgan analyst Greg Fuzesi observed: “The decision was expected by financial markets with the expectation that an interest rate hike will follow in December.”

He noted that Ms Lagarde said, however, that there was a debate about hiking at Thursday’s meeting and that the ECB “could” have hiked given the data.

“She also said that barring surprises, ‘the direction of travel is clear to all of us’. A hike in December therefore remains very likely and we continue to expect at least one further hike after that, most likely in March,” Mr Fuzesi said.

Against the dollar, the euro firmed to 1.1162 dollars on Thursday from 1.1148 dollars on Wednesday. Against the yen, the dollar was trading at 157.85 yen, down from 158.23 yen.

The pound was quoted at 1.3369 dollars, up from 1.3310 dollars. Against the euro, sterling climbed to 1.1977 euros from 1.1941 euros.

In New York, markets were lower.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 0.3% at the time of the closing bell in London on Thursday.

The S&P 500 was also 0.3% lower and the Nasdaq Composite slid 1.3%.

The US central bank also left interest rates on hold on Wednesday, despite accelerating inflation.

At the press conference following the announcement, Federal Reserve chairman Kevin Warsh reaffirmed his determination to ensure price stability as he looked to justify leaving interest rates unchanged despite the backdrop of accelerating inflation.

Mr Warsh told reporters it was the “right policy decision for this moment” and that the US central bank “will deliver price stability, no ifs, no buts”.

Eric Winograd, chief US economist at AllianceBernstein, said he was “struck” again by the gap between Mr Warsh’s “very strong rhetoric” about fighting inflation and the committee’s decision to delay action.

“My perception is that Chair Warsh prefers not to raise rates if he can avoid it. If the data force his hand he will do so, but the bar to a hike seems higher for him than the bar to a hold,” he added.

Morgan Stanley expects two more 25 basis point hikes in December and March, bringing the terminal range to 4.25%-4.5%.

“The Fed’s measured approach, tightening financial conditions, and Chair Warsh’s emphasis on productivity-led growth suggest less need for aggressive tightening,” Morgan Stanley analysts said.

The pressure on the US central bank was reflected in figures on Thursday which showed the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index rose 3.6% in September, accelerating from 3.4% in August and meeting the FXStreet cited forecast.

The core reading, which excludes food and energy, is the Federal Reserve’s preferred inflationary measure.

The core PCE price index picked up 3.1% on-year in September, quickening from 3.0% in August and also in line with the market forecast.

On Wall Street, Google owner Alphabet slumped 12% after poorly received earnings.

David Morrison, at Trade Nation, said Alphabet’s falls came as “investors clocked a sharp drop in free cash flow, along with a slowdown in Google’s search engine growth”.

The latter raised concerns that alternative AI large language models were “increasingly eating” into Google’s search engine dominance.

“Both earnings per share and revenues came in below expectations, something that hasn’t happened in close to four years,” Mr Morrison noted.

The yield on the US 10-year Treasury was quoted at 5.00%, trimmed from 5.03%.

The yield on the US 30-year Treasury was quoted at 5.33%, unchanged from Wednesday.

Also supporting equity markets was an easing in oil prices amid a renewed diplomatic push to bring the US and Iran back to the negotiating table.

US vice president JD Vance has contacted Iran’s security chief in a bid to revive peace talks that could head off a renewed American assault on the Islamic Republic, the Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday.

Brent was quoted at 113.75 dollars a barrel, down from 116.37 dollars at the same time on Wednesday.

In London, WPP rose 5.4% as it reiterated its annual guidance following a third-quarter performance in line with expectations, as the company continued implementing its Elevate28 strategic turnaround plan.

The London-based advertising and marketing services group said revenue for the three months to September 30 rose 0.5% to £3.27 billion from £3.26 billion a year earlier.

Revenue less pass-through costs fell 2.0% on a reported basis to £2.41 billion from £2.46 billion.

Shell gained 2.1%, despite the lower oil price, as it reported better-than-expected third-quarter results and increased the size of quarterly share buybacks.

Adjusted earnings rose to 10.81 billion dollars from 5.43 billion dollars a year ago, beating 10.33 billion dollars consensus compiled for Shell by Vara.

This was supported by higher refining margins, higher realised liquefied natural gas prices and higher integrated gas volumes, partly offset by lower crude and oil products trading and optimisation, higher well write-offs and lower realised liquids prices.

Analysts at RBC Capital Markets commented that Shell’s “underlying earnings and cash flow performance highlight a business that is extremely well suited to take advantage of the current disruptions in the energy complex, across oil, products and gas”.

Elsewhere, Haleon slumped 15% as it lowered full-year revenue guidance after a weaker-than-expected third quarter.

The Surrey-based consumer healthcare company now expects 2026 organic revenue growth to be around 2.5%, down from between 3% and 5% previously.

Haleon, which owns Aquafresh oral health brand, Centrum vitamins and Panadol painkillers, said sales rose 2.5% to £2.87 billion in the three months ended September 30.

Organic growth of 2.6% was below consensus which looked for an increase of 3.4%.

The FTSE 100 listing said performance in the quarter was held back by soft cold and flu consumption leading to lower orders in North America, and a raw material classification change for Centrum over-the-counter products in China which meant it had to “cease importation until further notice”.

Gold was quoted at 4,012.39 dollars an ounce, down from 4,039.18 dollars.

The biggest risers on the FTSE were WPP, up 20.7p at 404.9p, 3i Group, up 77p at 2,432p, London Stock Exchange Group, up 258p at 9,444p, ICG, up 47p at 1,851p and Melrose Industries, up 11.9p at 475p.

The biggest fallers on the FTSE 100 were Haleon, down 56.3p at 317.7p, Lloyds Banking Group, down 5.24p at 98.66p, St James’s Place, down 41p at 1,072.5p, Rio Tinto, down 187p at 6,206p and Diageo, down 36p at 1,701.5p.

Friday’s local corporate calendar has third-quarter results from lender NatWest, oil major BP and drugs firm AstraZeneca.

Friday’s global economic calendar has an interest rate decision in Japan overnight, inflation figures in France and Germany, GDP data in Canada and the Chicago PMI.
