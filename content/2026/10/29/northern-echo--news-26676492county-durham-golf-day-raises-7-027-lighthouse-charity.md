A charity golf day in County Durham has raised more than £7,000 to support construction workers across the UK and Ireland with their emotional, physical and financial wellbeing.

The annual golf day, now in its 12th year, was organised by the Lighthouse Charity’s North East committee at Ramside Hall Golf Club in Durham.

The event drew 22 construction-industry teams and raised £7,027 for the charity’s 24/7 helpline, online chat service and other support.

Sarah Sidey, chair of the Lighthouse Charity North East, said: "Following our 70th anniversary celebrations, the construction sector in the North East continues to support this vital charity.

"Every pound raised goes back into the construction industry to provide much needed physical, emotional or financial support to our workforces."

Teams from Tier 1 contractors, regional firms and suppliers played the Prince Bishops Course for the charity, which has supported construction workers and their families across the UK and Ireland since 1956.

Esh-Stantec won this year, with Tyne Tees Crushing & Screening Ltd second and Northern Counties Safety Group Ltd third.

The Lighthouse Charity offers confidential, round-the-clock help with wellbeing through its helpline, online chat and self-support app.

Founded in 1956, the charity has strong roots in the North East and takes its name from St Mary’s Lighthouse in Tyneside, a symbol of hope and guidance that reflects its mission.

Ms Sidey led the charity’s North East committee in organising the event, with support from Angela Carney of Carney Consultancy; Nick Redpath of Northern Counties Safety Group; Debbie Flynn of the Health and Safety Executive; and Rachel Howey of Daniel Owen.

Ms Sidey said: "We will be back at Ramside Hall Hotel, Golf & Spa on Friday, October 1 next year for what has become a key event in our sector."

The Lighthouse Charity is marking 70 years of supporting construction workers and helping workers and their families through crises.
