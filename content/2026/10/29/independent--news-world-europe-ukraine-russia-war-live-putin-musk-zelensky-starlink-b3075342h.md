## Oleshky truce holding for second day, Kyiv says

A US-brokered local ceasefire in southern Ukraine held for a second consecutive day on Wednesday, raising hopes that humanitarian aid could reach thousands of civilians trapped in Russian-occupied territory.

An estimated 2,000 people remain stranded in Oleshky, in the Kherson region, amid severe shortages of food and medical supplies. Reports suggest some residents have been reduced to eating weeds to survive.

Human rights ombudsmen from both Russia and Ukraine confirmed that the ceasefire, which took effect early on Tuesday, remained intact.

“30 hours of the ceasefire in Oleshky have passed without a single incident,” Ukraine’s human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets said.

## Moscow airports shut as Russia claims to have intercepted 166 drones overnight

Moscow faced an onslaught of drone attacks overnight, the mayor said, prompting authorities to shut down airports.

Russian air defence units intercepted nearly 60 drones bound for the capital over 90 minutes, Sergey Sobyanin said.

An additional 57 drones were intercepted in Moscow Oblast.

He later reported another 15 interceptions, with subsequent updates bringing the total number of drones reportedly downed to 166.

The total number of drones launched remains unclear, as Russian authorities have only reported those intercepted.No casualties or damage have been reported, and the intended targets remain unknown. Emergency services were deployed to the sites where drone debris fell, Sobyanin said.

Russia's aviation authority imposed temporary flight restrictions at Vnukovo, Domodedovo and Zhukovsky airports.

Restrictions were subsequently lifted at Zhukovsky, while those at the other two airports remained in place.

Two flights travelling from Istanbul to Moscow were forced to turn back because of restrictions at Vnukovo airport, Russian state news agency TASS reported.

## North Korea sending workers to Russian war factories in violation of UN sanctions, report finds

A Russian network of brokers backed by Pyongyang has been sending North Koreans to work in exploitative conditions in Russia, including at factories producing supplies used in Moscow’s war in Ukraine, according to a new investigation.

The Hague-based rights group Global Rights Compliance (GRC) said North Korea was pocketing much of the workers’ wages, while labourers were forced to work 12-hour shifts with just two days off a month under threats of punishment against them and their families.

The workers reportedly entered Russia on student visas to circumvent UN sanctions banning the employment of North Korean nationals overseas. Brokers received commissions of up to 150,000 roubles (£1,400) per worker.

Undercover investigators posing as prospective employers found North Koreans being recruited for production sites, including a factory manufacturing military rations.

"This investigation exposes a dangerous convergence between two states' military ambitions," said the group's legal adviser, Elise Lauriot Prevost.

These were "North Korea's use of overseas forced labour to finance ⁠its weapons programme, and Russia's growing reliance on labour to sustain its war economy," she added.
