TWO top York Labour politicians have added their support to yesterday’s budget.

Their backing adds to that of York Central MP Rachael Maskell, who said the budget offered "fresh hope for the future and real life changes that all add up to supporting working families."

Both York Outer MP Luke Charters and City of York Council leader Claire Douglas are backing a £100 payment to eligible households for help with fuel bills.

Mr Charters says this measure will help the 32,680 properties in his constituency whose council tax bands are in A to D.




Both the MP and Cllr Douglas highlight central government increasing the threshold for Small Business Rates Relief from £12,000 to £18,000 for eligible retail, hospitality and leisure properties.

Mr Charters says this will mean around 1,232 properties in York will benefit from a real-term freeze over the next two years.

The MP also praises the extension of the 5po fuel duty cut, with rates frozen until July 2027, which he says will benefit the 13 businesses employing 1,000 the Road Haulage Association estimates are in York Outer.

Cllr Douglas says the £2 maximum bus fare in 2027 will also help household finances.

Mr Charters says speaking to residents he accepts the pressures household bills are putting on finances, so is really pleased the budget contains "some practical help, particularly the £100 energy payment."

He also welcomes the rates relief for small business and the fuel duty freeze to help those who need their cars or work in the haulage sector.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/17278753.jpg?type=mds-article-620)City of York Council leader Cllr Claire Douglas. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

The MP said: "This is a Budget that delivers real support for families and businesses across York Outer, while investing in our region's future. I'm proud that this Labour Government is taking action to ease the pressure on household bills, back our high streets and put more money back into people's pockets."

Mr Charters also backed plans for regional mayors to have more control over funding, including a share of business rates from 2027 and income tax in 2028.

He added: "We've already seen what devolution can deliver locally, and I want to see us build on that.

"My focus will continue to be making sure York Outer gets its fair share, whether that's investment in transport, support for our high streets or more opportunities for young people."

Leader of City of York Council, Cllr Claire Douglas also added: "I’m pleased to see more detail provided on more devolved powers and funding which provides real incentives for increased economic growth locally. And following our extensive lobbying over the past two years, I’m glad to see overnight visitor levy powers confirmed. These powers will need to work for York and demonstrate a benefit to York residents."
