A knifeman burst into his victim's York home and stabbed him in the neck, the city’s crown court heard.

Matthew Nigel Watson, 37, was among a group of people including the victim’s ex-partner who went to the victim’s flat and were kicking on his door, said Nick Peacock, prosecuting.

When the resident opened the door, Watson “forced himself through the doorway, asked someone to watch his can of beer, grabbed the complainant by the neck and used the knife to stab him five times,” said Mr Peacock.

The victim suffered cuts to his neck, the back of his head, and his arm that needed 24 stitches in total to close. They were not life-threatening injuries.

Watson, of no fixed address, pleaded guilty to wounding.

The prosecution had originally charged him with wounding with intent to cause grievous bodily harm but accepted the plea to the lesser charge after his trial had to be postponed until September 2027.

“You are extremely lucky you didn’t cut this man’s jugular or any other vital organ, vein or artery,” the Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, told him.

Had the victim died, Watson would have been looking at spending at least 30 years behind bars, said the judge.

Watson was jailed for three years. The maximum sentence for his offence was five years.

Mr Peacock said the victim, Watson and the others present all knew each other.

The victim had been expecting his ex-partner to go to his home to collect some items on May 9 after the pair split up.

But instead she arrived in the early evening with Watson and a friend and one of the group shouted insults at the victim through the letter box.

The victim armed himself with a knife and was heard to say he was going to hit Watson, said Mr Peacock.

But before he could do so, a witness inside the address took the knife off him.

Then the victim opened the door.

After he was stabbed, he was taken to Leeds General Hospital for medical treatment.

Watson was arrested an hour later and claimed someone had come at him with a knife that he had then seen dropped to the floor.

He claimed he ran away and didn’t see who injured the victim.

Watson had 42 previous convictions including some for what the judge called “low-level” violence.

Nick Hammond, for Watson, said police found evidence that the knife used for the stabbing came from the flat and had not been taken there by the defendant.

He had been in custody since his arrest.
