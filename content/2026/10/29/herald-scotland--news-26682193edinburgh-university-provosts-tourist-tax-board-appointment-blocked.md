**The provost of the University of Edinburgh has been blocked from joining the capital's visitor levy advisory board.**

Labour, SNP and Green councillors voted to defer a decision on appointing Professor Kim Graham to the forum, which advises the council about 5 per cent nightly tourist tax.

Professor Graham faced calls to resign from staff and student groups after The Herald reported that 90 programmes would be ‘taught out’ in the College of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences.

The campaign to block the provost from sitting on the advisory board was led by Green councillor Lorenzo Martinico.

Commenting after the vote, Councillor Martinico said: “Edinburgh University’s senior management team has cut courses which focus on crafts and Scottish culture.

![OLYMPUS DIGITAL CAMERA](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21294945.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Lorenzo Martinico speaks at a rally with Zack Polanski in Portobello. *(Image: Scottish Greens)*

“To appoint their provost to the visitor levy forum when they are currently engaged in diminishing access to Scotland’s culture – the culture our tourist industry relies on – is unacceptable and I’m pleased a majority of the council saw it that way.”

Ahead of a full City of Edinburgh Council meeting on Thursday, councillors were urged to reject Professor Graham’s appointment because of the programme closures.

An amendment submitted by Councillor Martinico stated: “Regardless of the merits of the dispute, there are widespread concerns around the governance of the university around its financial management.

“Appointing any members of the current senior leadership team to any governance body would be seen as showing undue confidence in its members, and should therefore be avoided until the dispute has been resolved.”

A similar amendment by SNP group leader Councillor Lesley Macinnes stated:

“Council notes concerns raised with councillors by the Student Staff Association regarding the appointment, and therefore agrees to defer the appointment at this time to allow the chair of the Visitor Levy Advisory Forum to consider this feedback.”

The Herald understands that the council’s Labour administration accepted both amendments, while the Liberal Democrats and Conservatives voted to appoint Professor Graham to the board.

Prior to the vote, Professor Graham told The Herald that she was keen to give back to the city.

“I am pleased to be nominated and, if appointed, I look forward to playing an active role on the board helping support the development of positive projects for the region associated with the Visitor Levy,” she said.

“As the largest educational institution in Edinburgh, it matters a great deal to us to work together with our partner organisations, including the City of Edinburgh Council, to help enhance and benefit the city that founded us.”

![Edinburgh University](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20897649.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Edinburgh University. *(Image: Agency)*

In addition to her role at the university, Professor Graham is a fellow of the Royal Society of Edinburgh and serves on the Universities Scotland Funding Policy Group.

The university’s Student-Staff Solidarity Network and UCU trade union praised the decision by the council.

"Many students and staff reached out to councillors to object to this appointment, on the grounds that the senior leadership team at the University of Edinburgh has shown serious disregard towards its students and staff, has refused to adhere to minimal standards of transparency and democracy around its financial management, and has consistently ignored students and staff voices, including the no-confidence votes issued by the Senate, the Students’ Union and the UCU," the groups said.

"It is clear that if Kim Graham demonstrates a basic disregard for responsible governance at the University of Edinburgh, she is ill-suited for the post under consideration."

The groups added: "The SNP-Green motion which was carried by a comfortable majority does not deny the appointment altogether - nonetheless, the council’s refusal to appoint Kim Graham at this point sends a strong message that the University’s Senior Leadership Team should not be in anyone’s good books right now.

"It also shows that there is growing awareness, in the Edinburgh community, of the failures of governance and management that this university leadership has come to embody, and that students and staff have been suffering under."

A City of Edinburgh Council spokesperson said: "At today's Council meeting, it was agreed to defer the decision on the recommendation to appoint Professor Kim Graham to the Edinburgh Visitor Levy Advisory Forum pending further consideration by the Chair.”

Edinburgh University declined to comment.
