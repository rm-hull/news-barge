Scotland could stop publishing annual figures on homeless deaths despite the country being in the grip of a housing emergency.

National Records of Scotland (NRS) is proposing to release its standalone homeless deaths statistics only once every two years as part of a wider shake-up of official data publications.

The change would come more than two years after the Scottish Government declared a national housing emergency, with the latest figures showing 231 people died while homeless in 2024.

That figure represented an almost 48% increase since tracking began in 2017.

Glasgow’s homelessness deaths rose to 36 in 2024, with almost 300 total recorded in the city over five years.

Scottish Labour housing spokesman Mark Griffin hit out at the proposal to reduce the frequency of homeless deaths statistics.

He said: “We face so many public policy challenges in that regular and robust data is absolutely crucial.

Meanwhile, NRS is also proposing to halve the frequency of its standalone publication on deaths from Alzheimer’s and other forms of dementia, moving it from an annual to a biennial release.

Annual analysis of dementia as a leading cause of death would continue to be published elsewhere, however.

The changes are part of a wider review of Scotland’s official statistics, which NRS said was designed to ensure its statistical output remained sustainable and provided maximum public value.

Under the proposals, monthly mortality analysis would be reduced to quarterly releases, while the Households and Dwellings in Scotland publication would move from annual to every two years.

Avoidable mortality statistics would become a data-only release, while provisional Small Area Population Estimates would no longer be published.

Responsibility for producing Healthy Life Expectancy statistics would also transfer from NRS to Public Health Scotland after the 2023-25 reporting period, while statistics on people registered to vote would move to the Scottish Government.

NRS said its initial engagement had focused largely on organisations and stakeholders which use the statistics for policy development, but it is now seeking views from the wider public.

A six-week consultation on the proposed changes launches on Thursday, with feedback to be considered before final decisions are taken.

NRS said the review was intended to ensure Scotland continued to have a “sustainable, high-quality statistical system”, with resources focused on publications providing the greatest value to users and decision-makers.

A spokeswoman for the NHS said: "NRS has invited feedback on proposed changes to our statistical publication programme. People whose work is impacted by the suggested changes in frequency or format of publications are asked to send their views to us by 3 December."

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: “Every death of a person experiencing homelessness highlights its unacceptable cost.

“We are taking decisive action to shift our approach from crisis management to prevention, backed by our £50 million Homelessness Prevention Fund, the delivery of 1,500 Housing First tenancies and our £2 million support for Social Bite’s efforts to address homelessness.”
