![The headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph reads: 'Streeting: Stockpile food for cyber war.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/69d6/live/04946a90-ce5a-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting has made headlines in today's papers after launching a national campaign on public preparedness for disruption caused by natural disasters and cyber attacks. The Daily Telegraph reports on Britons being asked to make "Be ready" kits for emergencies, "including three days' worth of non-perishable food and bottled water, an FM radio and blankets".

![The headline on the front page of the Metro reads: 'Prepare for the worst.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/198e/live/95a55e50-ce59-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

Meanwhile, the Metro warns readers to "prepare for the worst" after websites around the world were taken offline after cyber-security flaws were revealed in a globally-used programming language, PHP. "In the UK, 32 government services were down", the paper reports, before asking the question "are you emergency ready?"

![The headline on the front page of the Independent reads: 'Prepare for food shortages in cyber attack, public told.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/990a/live/3592e3b0-ce5f-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

The Independent leads on Streeting's messaging for Britons to be prepared in case "'state-sponsored terrorism' disrupts everyday life". The government's campaign comes as global security flaw knocked dozens of government services offline, the paper says.

![The headline on the front page of the Guardian reads: 'Stock up on supplies to survive 72 hours in a crisis, Britons told.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/267c/live/1bf7a190-ce5d-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

Similarly, the Guardian leads on UK households being urged to stockpile enough food and water to "survive 72 hours in a crisis", the Guardian reports. The new guidance from the government follows concerns on "climate change, technological dependency and hostile state threats", the paper says.

![The headline on the front page of the Times reads: 'Be prepared with 3 days of supplies, Britons told.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/491a/live/f512e560-ce5e-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

Streeting "issues checklist for coping after catastrophe" and to "be prepared with 3 days of supplies", the Times reports. Elsewhere British singer Raye is pictured singing on stage as a "golden wonder" following her Mercury Prize win.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mail reads: 'Don't Panic!'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/5437/live/84c528f0-ce5d-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

"Don't Panic!" the Daily Mail instructs after ministers were "accused of lecturing families" to stockpile "in case of a national emergency". Elsewhere on the page, Catherine, Princess of Wales, is pictured with her son Prince George, who she is said to be missing while he is at Eton.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror reads: 'Indefensible.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/bade/live/e2ce4cc0-ce5c-11f1-9a39-31b474234113.jpg)

Former Health Secretary Matt Hancock has been heavily criticised after another Covid inquiry over "untested patients being discharged into care homes where tens of thousands of residents died", the Daily Mirror reports. "Bereaved families are calling for Hancock to be stripped of his Right Honourable title" following his handling of Covid, the paper says.

![The headline on the front page of the Financial Times reads: 'Barclays to axe 5,000 jobs this year in AI adoption and cost-cutting overhaul.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/480/cpsprodpb/32fe/live/b5558d10-ce59-11f1-9a39-31b474234113.jpg)

Barclays plans to cut 5,000 jobs as it introduces more artificial intelligence (AI) into its workforce, the Financial Times reports. Support roles are predicted to bear the brunt as "AI makes our people more productive", the bank said, according to the paper. It comes as chancellor John Healey prepares to deliver his Budget next week that is likely to tax banks.

![The headline on the front page of the i Paper reads: 'Career plans for 14-year-olds set to be created in schools to help avoid benefits.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/8214/live/20ce8560-ce5a-11f1-a64c-550be9e3c66b.jpg)

A landmark report proposing to create career plans for 14-year-olds and above to help them with their future employment, is the lead of the i Paper. The plan aims to tackle the high number of young people not in education, employment or training once they leave school, the paper explains.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Star reads: 'Ross: Traitors harder than any war zone.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/2661/live/9761d400-ce5c-11f1-9a39-31b474234113.jpg)

The Daily Star splashes on The Celebrity Traitors and how one contestant admitted their experience was "harder than any war zone".

![The headline on the front page of the Sun reads: 'Meghan Dad in child sex probe.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ee1c/live/befcaee0-ce5c-11f1-bc2e-018d645d8d21.jpg)

The Sun reports that Thomas Markle, the estranged father of the Duchess of Sussex, is being investigated by police, after a "huge stash" of child sex abuse images were allegedly found at his home in the Philippines. The paper reports that the discovery was made by the 82-year-old's son, Meghan's half-brother, Thomas Junior. It says he told detectives that "hundreds, if not thousands" of pictures and videos of young girls were "mixed up with innocent family mementos" in four large boxes. There is no response from Thomas Markle Senior.

A number of the papers lead on government warnings about preparing for national emergencies. The Guardian says there is "growing concern", external about the impact of climate change, technological dependency and hostile state threats, with the Daily Telegraph highlighting Britain's reliance, external on undersea cables and a "heavily globalised food supply". But the Daily Mail dismisses the warnings, external, accusing what it calls "Nanny Burnham" of "wasting a fortune on lecturing" the country. Its headline is: "Don't panic."

The Times says that the government is to make, external its revived Help to Buy scheme "more generous" by raising the earnings cap on who can qualify. It says first-time buyers outside London with a combined income of up to £110,000 will be able to access the scheme, up from £90,000 pounds. In the capital, the threshold is being raised to £140,00 pounds, from £110,000.

The Financial Times says that Nato will allow Ukraine, external to buy weapons, ammunition and equipment through its centralised procurement agency. The paper says this could potentially allow Kyiv to obtain supplies faster and more cheaply, and without having to rely on bilateral negotiations.

And reports that the search for the next James Bond is nearing its end are highlighted in the Times, external. It says that among the final contenders are the Slow Horses' star, Jack Lowden; Callum Turner from Masters of the Air; and Josh O'Connor, who played Prince Charles in The Crown. They - along with up to two others - are said to be gathering in London this weekend for screen tests. The paper says the next 007 is set to be recruited before the end of the year.
