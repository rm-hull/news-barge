Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, one of the state's most prominent Democrats, has been indicted by federal authorities over allegations that she led a wide-ranging criminal enterprise that procured and distributed substantial quantities of marijuana while laundering the financial proceeds.

The indictment names Lucas alongside six co-defendants, including several of her relatives, and alleges she exploited her political authority to insulate the illicit operation from public and legal scrutiny.

U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche, who announced the charges alongside FBI Director Kash Patel during a news conference on Thursday, claimed that Lucas "put her own financial interests before the people she was elected to serve."

Lucas surrendered to federal custody and was scheduled to complete an initial appearance in federal court on Thursday afternoon.

The counts of drug trafficking and money laundering follow federal searches conducted by the FBI in May, which targeted Lucas' hometown office alongside a cannabis store in which she holds an equity stake. At the time of those raids, Lucas—a prominent critic of Republican President Donald Trump’s administration—characterized the federal law enforcement actions as a direct effort to intimidate her.

![U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the charges against Lucas in a news conference with FBI Director Kash Patel on Thursday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/15/24/AP26295541475871.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"This is the circus we always expected it to be, and we will wait for further details to have a more specific response," said Brian Woolfolk, chief counsel for the L. Louise Lucas Legal Defense Fund.

A social media statement issued by the lawmaker did not explicitly address the merits of the federal charges, claiming instead that Thursday’s enforcement actions demonstrated the federal government applying its "full weight" against a great-grandmother.

## Sen. Lucas blasts indictment

“Today, the FBI, the Department of Justice, and the full weight of the federal government have been wielded against an 82-year-old great-grandmother,” she wrote.

“I grew up under Jim Crow. I’ve seen what happens when people in power try to silence those who stand up to them. I spent my life fighting back, and I’m not stopping now.”

She continued, “I’m not new to this. I’m true to this. I will not be intimidated. I will not be bullied. And I will not back down.

The bold 82-year-old politician has spent decades advancing through Virginia’s political landscape, holding a seat in the state Senate since 1992. Serving as president pro tempore, she presides over the legislative body in the lieutenant governor’s absence. More critically, she presides over the powerful Senate finance committee, through which all bills with fiscal consequences must pass, allowing her to halt and eliminate legislative proposals she opposes.

The indictment comes at a time when Trump’s Justice Department is navigating significant public scrutiny concerning inquiries and criminal prosecutions directed at critics of the president, which detractors have labeled politically motivated.

Blanche denied that political motives influenced the case against Lucas, emphasizing that federal agents began the investigation well ahead of the second Trump administration taking office in January 2025.

"We don’t do political paybacks," Blanche told reporters.

A person with knowledge of the May law enforcement action stated that the investigation was initially opened under Democratic President Joe Biden’s administration.

## The FBI alleges a ‘criminal marijuana enterprise’

Lucas, who has been a vocal critic of Trump's administration, characterized the search in May as an attempt to intimidate her. Court records in the case had been sealed for months.

Earlier this year, the Department of Justice sent at least two rounds of letters notifying journalists, politicians and others in Lucas' orbit that their communications with her had been intercepted as part of a court-approved wiretap that ran for two one-month time spans.

Many Democrats spoke up to defend Lucas when the search happened, expressing skepticism about the basis for it, given the Department of Justice's efforts to establish criminal conduct by perceived Trump opponents.

Lucas, 82, has spent decades ascending the ranks of Virginia politics and has served in the state Senate since 1992. She is the chamber's president pro tempore, overseeing it in the lieutenant governor's absence.

More significantly, she leads the powerful Senate finance committee, through which all legislation with any budget impact must flow. She has used the position to stop and ultimately kill legislation she opposes.

Lucas represents a state Senate district that includes her hometown of coastal Portsmouth. She operates with a sometimes-profane, unapologetic style that has drawn comparisons with Trump.

She has amassed a six-figure following on X and national attention, using the social media platform to advocate forcefully for her priorities and to bash her critics, including former GOP Gov. Glenn Youngkin. Lucas has also occasionally blasted members of her own party, including Gov. Abigail Spanberger, with whom she has had disagreements.

![FBI personnel enter a building in Portsmouth, Va., May 6, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/16/02/AP26294608097107-(1).jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A forceful advocate of legalizing marijuana, Lucas partnered in opening the shop, The Cannabis Outlet, in 2021, selling hemp and CBD products. Marijuana is legal to possess in Virginia, but recreational retail sales of the drug are not yet underway, though shops across the state do sell other cannabis-derived products.

Lucas has spoken publicly about her ownership stake in the cannabis shop as recently as June, though her most recent financial disclosure forms do not list the business by name. She also runs a network of companies that provide residential, transportation and other services to adults with intellectual disabilities.

Lucas was a leading voice in Democrats' ultimately failed attempt to redistrict the state's congressional map this year, a bid to hedge against Republican efforts in other states to redraw maps for their advantage in midterm elections.

## Lucas has been in politics for decades

Earlier this year, she helped lead Democrats' ultimately unsuccessful plan to gerrymander the blue-leaning state's congressional districts into a map intended to elect 10 Democrats and only one Republican. Along the way, she posted quips about four GOP members the intended map sought to oust, joking that they soon would be working at McDonald's.

She and other Democrats defended the maps as a reasonable response to Trump-backed efforts to boost the GOP's midterm advantage in other states. Lucas has notched several historic firsts in her career.

According to a biography from the state library, she became the Norfolk Naval Shipyard's first female shipfitter in 1971, a physically demanding job that involves working on metal assemblies for vessels. She launched her political career in 1984, when, the biography said, she became the first Black woman elected to the Portsmouth city council.
