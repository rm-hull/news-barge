Andy Burnham is set to become the first prime minister to appear on Channel 4’s Gogglebox when he takes part in a celebrity charity version of the show tomorrow night.

The Labour leader was pictured sitting on the sofa in Downing Street alongside his old friend Steve Rotheram, the mayor of Liverpool, for *Gogglebox for Stand Up To Cancer*.

A trailer for the episode, released on social media, shows a close-up of the famous No 10 black door while the two men discuss snacks for their marathon TV-watching session.

Mr Burnham can be heard saying he has beer, Skips, pork scratchings, bacon fries and Twiglets, adding: “Twiglets are probably the best, aren't they?”

![Steve Rotheram, the mayor of Liverpool, and Andy Burnham have been friends for many years](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/12/14/Steve-Rotheram-and-Andy-Burnham-copy.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Mr Rotherham seems to plump for Skips, as long as they are prawn cocktail flavour.

They are described on the voiceover as “good friends Andy and Steve”.

He is not the first Labour leader to appear on *Gogglebox for Stand Up To Cancer,* after Jeremy Corbyn featured on the Channel 4 programme in 2017.

Alastair Campbell, Tony Blair’s former spin doctor, and Boris Johnson’s father, Stanley, have also taken part over the years.

It is also not the first time the PM has been filmed discussing snacks. In August Anna Turley, a Home Office minister, was forced to defend Mr Burnham’s post ranking bar snacks to highlight a business rates cut for hospitality venues, saying his style was “reaching people”.

![Andy Burnham and chancellor John Healey raised eyebrows with a social media video rating bar snacks](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/28/15/2286653936...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The episode is set to air on Channel 4 on Friday 22 October at 9.05pm.

The show, which films people watching TV, has attracted millions of viewers and won numerous awards since it was first aired in 2013, while *Celebrity Gogglebox* followed in 2019.

Mr Burnham’s down-to-earth communication style has been seen by some as a breath of fresh air after Sir Keir Starmer’s time as PM.

But it has not been without controversy.

While the video of Mr Burnham and the chancellor John Healey discussing their favourite pub snacks was viewed more than five million times on X, formerly Twitter, it led No 10's social media experts to tell the PM to tone down his "blokeish" videos to appeal to a broader audience, according to reports in the *Times*.

His predecessor, Sir Keir, had faced criticism for being stiff in public and failing to connect with voters, as well as struggling to properly explain a unifying, positive story about what his government stood for.
