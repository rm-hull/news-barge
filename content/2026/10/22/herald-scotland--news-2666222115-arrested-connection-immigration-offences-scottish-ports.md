Police have arrested 15 people in connection with immigration offences following two weeks of action at Loch Ryan and Cairnryan Ports.

Operation Lockstream, a multi-agency operation coordinated by the Organised Immigration Crime Domestic Taskforce, aims to tackle organised immigration crime across the country.

The intelligence-led intensification focused on disrupting activity linked to organised immigration crime on the UK’s road, rail and port network.

During the two weeks at the ports, police said officers engaged with 340 people and arrested 15 people aged between 20 and 54 in connection with immigration offences.

One man aged 55 was arrested in connection with facilitating immigration and referred to partners, four people were subjected to a police warning for drug offences and 11 road policing offences were also detected.

A 36-year-old man was also arrested and charged in connection with drug offences after officers recovered drugs with an estimated value of £950 along with a quantity of cash and an offensive weapon. He was due to appear at Stranraer Sheriff Court on Monday, October 5.

Detective Superintendent Steven Bertram said: “We are committed to protecting vulnerable individuals and those at risk from organised immigration crime.

“Criminals will use the road, rail and ports to move people, assets and commodities and will look for every opportunity to exploit potential weaknesses. By carrying out action like this we are reducing criminal activity and making it harder for organised crime groups to operate.

“We work closely with partners to detect and stop this type of activity and staff working at ports and on the railways are trained to spot and report the signs of suspicious or criminal behaviour.

“Our road, rail services and ports are policed around the clock, and strong enforcement action will be taken against those involved in criminality.

“While action from ourselves is ongoing I would appeal to anyone who sees something that doesn’t look right, who is worried about someone, or sees something that looks suspicious, to contact us.”
