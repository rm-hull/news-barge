President Donald Trump delivered a speech marked by gaffes and missteps at a South Carolina rally Wednesday, raising new questions about his health.

Speaking at Clemson University, Trump confused the names of two prizes he has not won, stumbled over investment figures and appeared to struggle to recall the name of Lake Ontario.

The 80-year-old president has faced persistent questions about his health during his second term, fueled by episodes of apparent daytime drowsiness, bruising on his hands and swelling in his ankles. Trump and the White House have dismissed the scrutiny as politically motivated, insisting he remains in top shape.

“President Trump’s sharpness, unmatched energy, and historic accessibility stand in stark contrast to what we saw during the past four years when the legacy media intentionally covered up Joe Biden’s serious mental and physical decline from the American people,” White House spokesperson Davis Ingle told *The Independent*. “President Trump is working nonstop to solve problems and deliver on his promises, and he remains in excellent health.”

Polling suggests a substantial share of the public has concerns. In an April Economist/YouGov survey, 48 percent of Americans said they believed Trump was experiencing modest or significant cognitive decline.

![Speaking at Clemson University, Trump confused the names of two prizes he has not won, stumbled over investment figures and appeared to struggle to recall the name of Lake Ontario](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/12/00/GettyImages-2301386910-(1).jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

At Wednesday’s rally, where he appeared with Republican Senate candidate Darline Graham, Trump revisited his longstanding complaint that he had been denied the Nobel Peace Prize — before confusing it with the Pulitzer.

“I settled all these wars, and they gave the wonderful peace prize — right, does anybody know the name of that peace prize?” Trump said. “They gave the Pulitzer prize to a young lady, she’s 80 years old, a young lady who nobody ever heard of.”

This year’s Nobel Peace Prize went to Navanethem Pillay, the 85-year-old former United Nations human rights chief.

Trump also offered conflicting figures while touting investment commitments to the United States.

“We secured commitments for $22.2 trillion in new investments coming into the United States,” he said. “It’s the greatest, the largest amount of money ever invested in our country times five. We’ve never had so much money, 22.2 million.”

“Uh billion, sorry, billion with a B, not an M,” he said afterward. “If it was an M, we'd be in big trouble—with a B. $22 billion.”

Later, Trump appeared to struggle to name Lake Ontario, which he directed federal agencies to refer to as “Lake America” in August during the bitter trade war with Canada.

“Recently I changed the name of a lake…that fronts…you know the lake…,” he told the crowd at Clemson’s T. Ed Garrison Arena. “I did it very lightly and the damn thing got changed…that’s power right? When you do it sort of half-heartedly and it gets changed. It’s changed. It’s called the Lake of America.”

Trump also punctuated his remarks with several odd sounds, drawing attention from critics who described the performance as unusual.

“Wow Trump is just making huge gaffes during this speech,” a MeidasTouch editor Acyn Torabi commented. Juliet Jeske, the founder of Decoding Fox News, added: “Even his diehard fans didn't know how to react to Trump's random vocal sound effects.”

Trump has repeatedly defended his health, saying after a May physical that “everything” had “checked out PERFECTLY.” He has also frequently accused the media of misrepresenting his remarks. On Wednesday, he said reporters would falsely describe his asides as rambling.

Trump’s rally comes ahead of the midterm elections, which are less than two weeks away. Multiple polls indicate Republicans could face steep losses.
