Strong response from Macron as he seeks to differentiate between legitimate protests and ultra-left riots - snap analysis

Jakub Krupa

It’s a strong political response from Emmanuel Macron as he seeks to draw a clear line between legitimate demonstrations organised by recognised trade unions and the looser, often spontaneous gatherings called by the “ultra-left” that descend into rioting and street violence – and show the state’s determination to fight back against the latter.

The signal he wants to send is very clear – but in the age of social media, can top-down regulation actually work? And will authorities have enough resources on the ground to respond to every banned demonstration?

France will seek to ban demonstrations not organised by trade unions, Macron says

Macron declares the government will show “absolute” determination to stop the riots, with a “multi-pronged” approach in response to the events to restore order.

He says that legitimate demonstrations held by trade union groups will be “reviewed in accordance with our laws” and allowed to go ahead in a way ensuring they can go ahead safely, with potential changes to their routes and subject to additional conditions.

But he says any other protests, not organised by trade unions, will “wherever possible” be “prohibited entirely”.

On substance, he says that PM Lecornu will meet with student organisations next week to work on “legitimate requests and grievances”.

He ends by saying that he will hold further meetings with relevant authorities tomorrow.

French city of Lyon bans all protests on Saturday fearing repeat of riots, street violence

Officials have banned “all protests” in France’s third-biggest city of Lyon on Saturday, ahead of nationwide demonstrations that day following rallies last weekend that descended into widespread street violence, AFP reported.

A protester wrapped in a French national flag stands in front of Lyon's tourism office set on fire during a demonstration to protest against the "high cost of living". Photograph: Jeff Pachoud/AFP/Getty Images

The prefecture for the Rhone region said on Thursday in a statement to media that, “given the events of 17October, the prefect has decided to ban by decree all protests in the city of Lyon this Saturday, 24 October”.

Lyon, like Paris and other French cities, suffered clashes and looting when masked youths took to city centre streets on the sidelines of mass rallies protesting high inflation.

The mayor of Lyon, Grégory Doucet, has said that his city experienced hours of “chaos” during the riots, with between 150 and 200 shops looted and a tourist office torched. Police arrested some 200 people.

Southern Italy brought to standstill with food, fuel shortages, and business impact

Lorenzo Tondo

in Palermo

As lorry drivers’ protests bring much of southern Italy to a standstill amid food and fuel shortages, with businesses warning they are on the brink of collapse, Italian authorities have launched a police crackdown on road blockades.

The empty shelves in a supermarket due to a disruption in supplies following road blockades by Italy's hauliers in Quarto, near Naples. Photograph: Eliano Imperato/AFP/Getty Images

The interior ministry said on Wednesday that 1,339 protesters had been identified, 71 reported to judicial authorities and 242 fined over what it described as “illegal road blockades”. Police escorted hundreds of vehicles carrying goods, including 500 in Campania alone, while the interior minister, Matteo Piantedosi, warned of possible infiltration by criminal groups.

Industry associations in Campania and Puglia warned that businesses were on the brink of collapse, prompting the prime minister, Giorgia Meloni, to convene an emergency cabinet meeting.

Truck drivers take part in a road blockade to protest against the price of diesel in Campo Calabro near Reggio Calabria, in southern Italy. Photograph: Gianluca Chininea/AFP/Getty Images

“The truckers’ dispute requires an immediate response,” said industry associations representing Naples and other parts of Campania and Puglia in a joint statement. “Production has ground to a halt due to a lack of supplies. The strike must end; otherwise, the damage to consumers and businesses, which is already significant, will become unsustainable.”

The protests began last week, with lorry drivers blocking roads and slowing traffic across the country, particularly in the south, following strikes and sit-ins by farmers and fishers.

The disruption has left supermarket shelves empty in several regions. Stocks at some supermarket chains have reportedly fallen to 50% of normal levels.

Italy’s air traffic management agency, ENAV, said on Wednesday that Crotone and Reggio Calabria airports had run out of jet fuel because of supply disruptions.

According to estimates gathered by Confcommercio Sicilia, the five-day disruption to freight transport has caused between €40m and €50m in losses in Sicily alone, affecting supermarkets and the wider fresh food supply chain.

The protests threaten to deepen the crisis facing Meloni’s government, which is struggling to contain the unrest. Measures announced so far by Rome appear to have done little to appease lorry drivers, prompting the government to take the controversial step of attempting to quell the protests through a police crackdown.
