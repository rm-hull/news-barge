More than 300 pieces of royal jewellery, two-thirds of them never before exhibited, will go on display at Buckingham Palace next year in the first exhibition devoted to 500 years of British royal jewellery.

*Royal Jewellery: From Coronations to Keepsakes* will span adornments worn by monarchs from Henry VIII to Charles II, and Queen Victoria to Queen Elizabeth II.

The items on show date from the 16th century to the present day, and the display will explore the human stories behind the royal jewels of “love and loss, power and politics, family and faith”, its curators said.

The King's Gallery will host the famous Diamond Diadem, made for George IV's extravagant coronation and later immortalised in coins, banknotes and stamps bearing the image of Elizabeth II.

Queen Victoria's Small Diamond Crown will also be on view.

![Queen Elizabeth wears the Diamond Diadem in this image from 2019](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/14/01M5HESXCSYJDTS91AS8NDDMBG.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)
![Queen Victoria wearing the Small Diamond Crown](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19150450-dc97b0cb-e3a2-415e-9666-76750ca02cee.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It was so closely associated with the 19th-century monarch that it was placed on her coffin during her lying in state.

The cufflinks said to have been worn by Charles I on the scaffold for his execution in 1649, and handed to his loyal supporters, will be on public display for the first time.

At the heart of the exhibition will be a collection of jewellery associated with every queen and consort from Queen Charlotte to Elizabeth II.

It traces changing fashions from Georgian splendour and Victorian sentimentality to sleek Art Deco styles and bold mid-century designs.

![Cufflinks, dating from around 1640, said to have been worn by Charles I on the scaffold](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19150622-1308a9ce-0057-4505-a303-afafe806567e.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Caroline de Guitaut and Kathryn Jones, co-curators of the exhibition, said: "This unprecedented exhibition will bring together some of the most important and spectacular royal jewels in British history, many never exhibited publicly before.

“Each jewel carries with it a human story: of love and loss, power and politics, family and faith.

“Together, they have borne witness to the defining moments of royal lives and offer a deeply personal insight into the individuals and relationships that have shaped the monarchy across generations.”

Queen Charlotte's set of garnet jewels will be included for the first time, newly connected to George III's wife through recent research.

![Queen Alexandra’s articulated serpent bracelet, c.1870](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19151453-fa6603e5-1964-4568-afc1-6d4d3d0ecdae.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)
![A necklace from Queen Charlotte’s set of garnet jewels, dating from the late 18th century](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19151810-49caa948-981b-4a13-9823-d537a8bd304f.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Queen Alexandra's gold articulated serpent bracelet, set with diamonds and a black pearl, will also feature.

A Faberge aquamarine and diamond pendant given to the future Elizabeth II by her grandmother Queen Mary will be among the pieces.

So will the glittering Greville emerald and diamond necklace, part of Dame Margaret Greville's celebrated bequest to the Queen Mother, and worn by Elizabeth II.

Moving keepsakes associated with Queen Victoria and Prince Albert, capturing moments of love, loss and family life, will also be on show.

![The Greville emerald and diamond necklace](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19151227-4955b43f-e1ae-4259-b0e9-c2479233c2fe.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)
![Queen Elizabeth II wearing the Greville emerald and diamond necklace at a diplomatic reception in Buckingham Palace in 2019](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/c707c7431e534975f38ae4cd06266472Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyNTA1NDcz-2.48962370.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Victoria's first ring, a flowerhead set with five emeralds forming the petals around a single ruby, was worn by her in infancy.

The diamond and sapphire brooch Albert gave Victoria on the eve of their wedding is also included in the exhibition.

Visitors will see a bracelet set with pebbles collected by Victoria and Albert on their first journey alone together.

![Queen Victoria’s first ring, c.1820–5](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19153734-536691c3-203e-41a8-83b5-5c1a72ef914c.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)
![Queen Victoria’s wedding brooch, given to her by Prince Albert on the eve of their wedding](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19153927-62068d27-6bb8-45b7-9688-bf26b18403f6.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

A thistle brooch incorporates their eldest daughter Princess Victoria's first milk tooth, removed by Albert himself.

Victoria's mourning ring features Albert's photograph and their intertwined cyphers, made after he died at the age of 42 in 1861.

The priceless Crown Jewels will not feature in the exhibition and will remain on show at the Tower of London.

![A brooch, from 1847, set with the first milk tooth of Queen Victoria and Prince Albert’s eldest child, Princess Victoria](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19154118-b649f59d-4a8d-4105-88f1-170493707de6.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)
![Victoria’s mourning ring for Prince Albert, 1861](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/22/10/19154227-0fddb700-4fcd-480a-8988-471650c2ee6e.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

But visitors will see other glittering jewels created for coronations and major royal occasions.

Tickets will be available to Royal Collection Trust e-newsletter subscribers from 28 October and to the general public from 3 November.

The exhibition will open at The King's Gallery, Buckingham Palace, in London on 25 June 2027 and run for nine months.
