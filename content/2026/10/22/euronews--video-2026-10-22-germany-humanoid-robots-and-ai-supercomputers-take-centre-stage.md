At Nvidia’s GTC conference, visitors can explore a range of live demonstrations, from **robotic arms sorting objects and fruit** into boxes to**facial recognition systems** identifying people in real time. Other exhibits showcase**humanoid robots**,** interactive safety technology**and** Nvidia’s latest AI computing hardware**.

The company’s **Vera Rubin NVL72 supercomputer rack** is attracting attention alongside its RTX Spark devices, illustrating the growing range of applications for AI technology. Nvidia has also unveiled the**Vera Rubin DSK Pod**, a** modular, container-sized AI data centre**designed to** cut deployment times from months to days**.

**Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang** addressed about 3,000 developers, analysts and journalists, showcasing the company’s push to bring**AI beyond software** and into**factories, vehicles and robots**.

During his keynote, Huang demonstrated small robots and outlined the company’s ambitions for AI across European industries. **“There’s no question everybody will become an AI company, and we’re going to have a thriving AI economy here in Europe,”** he told the audience.

The event, which runs until 22 October, highlights how AI is moving beyond software into **robotics, industrial automation** and increasingly**powerful computing systems**. But as governments and businesses explore these opportunities, researchers are also emphasising the importance of developing technology that remains** safe, reliable and useful to people**.
