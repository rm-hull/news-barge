Guests are heaping praise on the new luxury spa at Gisborough Hall, with one visitor describing the experience as "perfect" after trying out the hotel's latest attraction.

The Sakura Spa, which opened on August 1, marks a major new chapter for the historic country house hotel in Guisborough, combining modern wellness facilities with the setting of one of the region's most impressive historic buildings.

Nestled within secluded woodland grounds, the spa has been designed as a tranquil retreat inspired by nature.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21548694.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: MICHAELLAZENBY)*

Visitors can enjoy six treatment rooms, relaxation lounges, a thermal garden, vitality pool, saunas, steam room and dedicated relaxation spaces surrounded by greenery.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21548725.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: MICHAELLAZENBY)*

The spa officially launched with an event attended by guests, local dignitaries and media representatives, who were given a first look around the facility and invited to experience treatments and sound baths.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21548692.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Trustee Ren Barons, Lord Gisborough, finance director Roger Barons, spa director Vicki Jackson and general manager Ruairidh Taylor, opening the spa on August 1. *(Image: SUPPLIED)*

The spa represents a significant investment by the owners of Gisborough Hall, which has a history stretching back centuries.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21548724.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: MICHAELLAZENBY)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21548721.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: MICHAELLAZENBY)*

The estate's origins lie in the aftermath of the Dissolution of the Monasteries, when Sir Thomas Chaloner acquired the manor of Gisborough and the site of the former Gisborough Priory during the 16th century.

A manor house was built beside the priory ruins, although that property was later demolished in the early 19th century.

The present Grade II-listed mansion was commissioned by Admiral Thomas Chaloner in 1856 after he inherited the estate.

Built in an imposing Jacobean style, the hall has remained one of the area's most recognisable landmarks ever since.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21548722.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: MICHAELLAZENBY)*

Following the Second World War, when the building was requisitioned by the army, the hall served as a nursing home before later becoming a restaurant and banqueting venue.

In 2002 it underwent major refurbishment and was transformed into the four-star hotel now known across the region for weddings, conferences and luxury breaks.

The addition of Sakura Spa is the latest investment by the Chaloner family.

Lord Gisborough said: "As a family, we remain deeply committed to the long-term future of Gisborough Hall.

"The creation of Sakura Spa is a significant investment that reflects our belief in the potential of our hotel.

"For generations, the Chaloner family has been deeply rooted in this cherished corner of the country, committed not only to preserving its natural beauty but to nurturing the community.

"The Sakura Spa represents more than just a new chapter for our hotel, it is a heartfelt investment in wellbeing, inspired by the surrounding woodland.

"It is a privilege to help shape something that will serve both our guests and our neighbours for generations to come."

Designed by international interior designer Nic Tamlin and developed alongside leading spa consultant Nicki Kurran, the woodland retreat aims to offer a nature-inspired wellness experience while complementing the historic surroundings of the Victorian mansion.
