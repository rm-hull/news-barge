Police have called on the public to help find two women in connection with the theft of more than £1,000 worth of goods in Reading and Calcot.

Thames Valley Police is looking into two incidents of shoplifting at the start of September. The first incident occurred at clothing store Next in Reading Gate Retail Park, at around 4.30pm on 3 September. The second took place at the Boots store at the Calcot Sainsbury’s retail park at around 1.45pm on 7 September.

During both incidents, two women entered the stores and selected a large number of items from display shelves before leaving without making any attempt to pay.

![Police are looking to speak to two women in connection with the incident](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/07/10/49/2026-10-06-cctv-appeal-shoplifting-reading-female-1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The stolen goods included a “significant quantity” of clothing and other items which the force said were worth more than £1,000 in total value.

The force put out CCTV photos of two women who they believe have “vital information” to assist their investigation. One woman dressed in blue and carried a pink bag while the other dressed in beige with sandals.

Investigating officer PC Richard Tarrant, of the West Berkshire Local Command Unit, asked that anyone who recognises the two women get in touch with the police as soon as possible.

![The force put out CCTV photos](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/07/10/35/2026-10-06-cctv-appeal-shoplifting-reading-female-2.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I am appealing to anyone who recognises the two women shown in these CCTV images to get in touch,” he said. “We believe they may have vital information that could assist our investigation into these theft offences.

“If you recognise either of these women, or have any information that may help, please contact us.”

The force asked that anyone with information contact Thames Valley Police by visiting their website. They can also call 101 and quote the reference numbers 43260466137 and 43260465972.

People can also provide information anonymously via Crimestoppers on 0800 555 111 or via their website.
