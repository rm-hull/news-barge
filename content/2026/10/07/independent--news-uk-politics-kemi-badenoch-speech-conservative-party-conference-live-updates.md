Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch vowed to make major changes to inheritance tax should her party be elected in her keynote conference speech.

Kemi Badenoch promised to scrap inheritance tax on family homes and raise the threshold to £1m for a couple. Her announcement on Wednesday was met with whoops and cheers from the audience, who gave a standing ovation.

She said: “The Labour Party think it is selfish to pass on what you have earned to your children; we don't.”

Ms Badenoch added: “The Conservatives are and always have been the party of home ownership. We understand why putting roots down and having a stake in society matters... passing on your home to your family is one of the most human instincts of all.”

It comes after growing pressure on the Tory leader to slash inheritance tax, dubbed the “death tax” by its critics, though she stopped short of abolishing it completely.

She also attacked Nigel Farage, his Reform UK party and the new prime minister Andy Burnham, warning there is a “battle for the soul of the nation” in politics.

## Tory leader stops short of abolishing inheritance tax completely

The Tories are not pledging to abolish inheritance tax, Kemi Badenoch has said.

She said: “We are not abolishing inheritance tax at this conference. I know you’re disappointed.

“We could do it. We could do it, but not if we want to keep our country safe with a ‘Britannia Shield’.

“Not if we also want to keep people safe with 50,000 new prison places.

“Not if we want to cut the deficit, threatening to bankrupt our country and burdening our children.

“Governing is about choices, tough choices, and the Conservatives will not announce a tax cut without telling you how we are going to pay for it.”

## Analysis: Inheritance tax announcement not what we were expecting

Kemi Badenoch had been flirting with the idea of abolishing inheritance tax all conference but has confirmed in her speech that she will not completely scrap it.

Nevertheless, the more moderate measures of exempting family homes and the first £1 million in cash received a huge cheer.

But essentially this announcement was a contorted attempt to show that she is fiscally responsible with her tax cuts, making much of the need to bring down the deficit, pay for 50,000 extra prison places and the Britannia shield drone defence.

It provides clear blue water with Labour but is still an odd way to deliver what should be a big moment.

## Leader calls for third runway at Heathrow

Kemi Badenoch has urged the prime minister to “stop faffing about” and build a third runway at Heathrow.

Ms Badenoch pledged to make growing the economy the central principle of the next Conservative government as she told the conference: “I’ve got a plan to get Britain growing again, the kind of growth that you can actually see and feel.”

## Badenoch next takes aim at Zack Polanski

Kemi Badenoch has criticised the Green Party membership’s decision to back a motion declaring that “Zionism is racism”, adding: “Shame on Zack Polanski.”

The Conservative Party leader said: “Back in the day, we were the ones who put out a poster saying – ‘Labour say he’s Black, we say he’s British’. We are still that same party.

“I don’t know whether you’ve noticed, but I am Black, I am British.

“I am proud of my country. I’m proud of our history, all that we have done and all that we will do in the world.

“Our best days are not behind us and we will not let this country go backwards.

“You know what else is taking us backwards? The disgraceful antisemitism we saw at the Green Party conference in Brighton last weekend.

“They are seeking to erase Jewish identity. Shame on the Green Party and shame on (the party’s leader) Zack Polanski.”

## Reflection on October 7 attacks

The Conservative Party “stands with the Jewish people”, its leader has said, as she reflected on the October 7 attacks in 2023.

On stage at her party’s conference in Birmingham, Mrs Badenoch said: “Exactly three years ago today, we saw the horrific October 7 attacks, the worst slaughter of Jews since the Holocaust.

“The Conservative Party stands with the Jewish people.

“We will never stop fighting antisemitism.”

## Badenoch compares Conservative Party to M&S

Kemi Badenoch also likened the Conservative Party to the retail brand Marks and Spencer, saying the brand had been criticised for being “a bit stuffy and stuck in the past – some were even questioning whether the brand would survive”.

The Tory leader continued: “Now, M&S is back in fashion – great looks at affordable prices. They have adapted their style for today’s market and people are flooding back, including me, as you’ve probably noticed this week.

“Just like M&S, we must learn from our mistakes, win back trust and make people feel good about our party again.”

Ms Badenoch said: “I want us to win in three years’ time.

“To be honest, I want us to win next week. We are ready for an election whenever it’s called because we are the only party that understands what has gone wrong in Britain and knows how to put it right.”
