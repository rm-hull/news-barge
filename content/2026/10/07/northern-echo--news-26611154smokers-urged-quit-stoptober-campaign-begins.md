Smokers across the North East are being urged to take part in this year’s Stoptober campaign and make a quit attempt.

Health officials from University Hospitals Tees and Hartlepool Council are encouraging smokers to use the national campaign as a starting point to become smoke-free.

Steve Gaffney, a stop smoking facilitator with the Hartlepool stop smoking service, said: "The Hartlepool service provides friendly, informal and non-judgemental stop smoking support through either regular face-to-face appointments at a local Hartlepool clinic or telephone consultations over a 12-week programme.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21543664.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Steve Gaffney, a stop smoking facilitator with the Hartlepool stop smoking service *(Image: Supplied)*

"We help people choose their quitting aids, give tailored practical advice, coping strategies, and guidance to help individuals achieve and maintain a smoke-free lifestyle."

Mr Gaffney said quitting is easier with support, noting that smokers are three times more likely to succeed with help from trained advisors.

He also highlighted the many benefits of giving up smoking.

He said: "Quitting smoking offers lots of physical, health and social benefits which include reduced risk of heart disease, stroke, cancers, lung diseases and better immune function.

"Becoming smoke-free you can expect improved sense of smell, breathing, increased energy and stamina levels, less stress, better mental wellbeing and of course more money in your pocket."

Councillor Christine Wiley, chair of the adults services and public health committee at Hartlepool Council, said: "Stopping smoking is one of the best things people can do for their health, and Stoptober provides a great opportunity to take that first step.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21543665.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Donna Mason, smoking cessation service *(Image: Supplied)*

"We know that quitting can be challenging, but no one has to do it alone.

"Through our partnership with University Hospitals Tees, residents can access free, expert support that is tailored to their individual needs."

The stop smoking team will be out in the community throughout October as part of the Stoptober campaign, offering advice and support from a health promotion bus.

The team will visit Hartlepool’s Waldon Street car park on Thursday, October 8 and Thursday, October 22, as well as Billingham Market on Monday, October 12 and Stockton town centre on Wednesday, October 28.

University Hospitals Tees has delivered a specialist stop smoking service on behalf of Hartlepool Borough Council for the past two years, supporting more than 500 quit attempts.

The service offers a 12-week programme that includes nicotine replacement therapy, e-cigarettes, face-to-face clinics, telephone consultations, home visits for housebound patients and workplace clinics.
