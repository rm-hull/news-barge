Leaked audio shows Ken Paxton blaming high gas prices and Iran war for uphill battle in midterms - report

Ken Paxton, aware of just how competitive his race for Senate is, acknowledged that high gas prices as a result of the ongoing Iran war appear to be hampering his campaign, according to an audio recording obtained by Axios.

At a fundraiser in Washington DC, Paxton can be heard responding to questions about the main issues affecting his competition. “It’s not tariffs,” he said. “It’s the war, it’s gas prices, it’s diesel.”

The embattled attorney general is currently trailing his opponent, Democratic state lawmaker James Talarico, in most polls leading up to the November election.

At the meeting, Paxton acknowledged that affordability is “by far our biggest problem”, according to the recording Axios obtained, and why his campaign is “having a hard time with numbers”.

A reminder that another recording from this event was leaked to the New York Times last week, where Paxton said that Donald Trump’s midterms GOP convention “dropped our numbers” and many of the campaign’s directives were coming from Washington.

Paxton noted that the ability to end the conflict with Iran, and ease prices is beyond his control, according to the audio from Axios. “So many factors in this federal race are uncontrollable,” the Republican nominee said. “I don’t make the decisions.”

This is Trump’s second visit to Texas in the past week. He’s set to speak at 7pm ET.

The review of voting records, public statements and campaign materials found candidates in 41 of 62 competitive congressional races seeking some distance from Trump ahead of the 3 November midterm elections.

The candidates have ⁠largely criticized or diverged from Trump on individual issues ⁠rather than repudiated his broader agenda. The findings ​suggest an effort to attract independent voters while avoiding a direct break with their party leader.

Policy differences were the most common point of divergence, appearing in 23 of the 62 races.

Most recently, the Trump administration announced plans to construct the largest steel plant in the US, a $15bn plant in Lee county, Iowa to be built by India-based Mesabi Metallics.

That project, in swing state Wisconsin, failed to produce the jobs Trump and Foxconn had promised.

Rubio to call for west to restore founding democratic principles and values

Secretary of state Marco Rubio is set to call for the nations of the west to return to their civilizational roots and restore the values that drove the rise of Europe and North America to global dominance for centuries, AP reports.

In Athens to discuss deepening economic, energy and security cooperation with Greece, Rubio will deliver a speech later today overlooking the ruins of the Acropolis, a widely recognizable symbol of the city’s ancient civilization associated with the creation of popular participatory government and the social contract.

Rubio intends to remind listeners of qualities he says were born in Greece and later defined the West, such as curiosity, courage, ambition and a restless spirit of exploration and discovery of the unknown.

“It has become fashionable today to believe that enlightened foreign policy means prioritizing the ‘global community’ over one’s own people. Here, too, we have much more to learn from the men of Ancient Greece. They knew that there was nothing selfish or wrong about loyalty to your nation, nor in fighting to defend its interests above all else,” Rubio plans to say in the speech, excerpts of which were distributed by the state department.

“Today, we find ourselves at a crossroads,” he will say, according to the excerpts. “The next decade will write the story of the next century. The course we take will determine whether the West maintains its place at the head of the world, or resigns itself to a future of atrophy, servitude, and decline. We will either choose to act now or lose the choice to act at all.”

Susan Collins distances herself from Trump in Maine debate

Robert Tait

Susan Collins, the Republican senator for Maine, pointedly distanced herself from Donald Trump in a televised debate with Democratic challenger Troy Jackson on Tuesday, as she sought to present an image of independence in a state that has voted against Trump three times.

Proclaiming herself as not the US president’s “favorite senator”, Collins made the highly unusual boast for a Republican that she had voted with Joe Biden, a Democrat, more than 90% of the time when he was president. She also emphasized her differences with her fellow Republicans, saying she voted against her own party more than any other senator.

Collins’ defensiveness came amid a determined onslaught from Jackson, 58, a veteran former state legislator who was getting his first chance to present himself to a broad audience since replacing Graham Platner as the Democratic candidate in a contest that could be vital to the party’s hopes of winning the Senate.

Platner, a former harbor master, was forced to withdraw from the race in July after facing sexual assault allegations, which he denied, leaving Democrats racing against the clock to replace a candidate who had built up momentum campaigning on a populist progressive platform.

Jackson painted Collins as a Trump loyalist, saying she voted with the president 96% of the time. He also doggedly focused on her 2018 vote for the conservative supreme court justice Brett Kavanaugh, tying it to the court’s subsequent overturning of Roe v Wade, which had for half a century guaranteed the national right to abortion.

“Tonight is about who you stand with when it matters,” Jackson said in a pithy opening statement. “Eight years ago today, Maine women went to DC and begged Senator Collins not to confirm Brett Kavanaugh, but she did it anyways.

“He helped overturn Roe v Wade, and today she says she has no regrets. Would you trust her to pick the next supreme court justice?”
