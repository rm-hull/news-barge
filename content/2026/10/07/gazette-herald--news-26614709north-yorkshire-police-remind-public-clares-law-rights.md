People in North Yorkshire are being reminded that they have the right to ask police about a current, former or potential partner's history of abusive behaviour.

North Yorkshire Police has issued a series of myth-busters to tackle misinformation surrounding the Domestic Violence Disclosure Scheme (DVDS), commonly known as Clare's Law.

The scheme allows people to ask police whether a partner or ex-partner has a history of violent or abusive offending.

This can include domestic abuse, coercive and controlling behaviour, emotional abuse, stalking, harassment or financial abuse.

Police stressed that there is no national register of domestic abuse perpetrators that members of the public can search.

Instead, each application is assessed individually, with officers carrying out checks on police systems to determine whether any information can legally and safely be disclosed.

The force also warned that the scheme is only intended for people who are in, have been in, or may enter into a relationship with someone. Requests cannot be made to obtain information about friends, employees, housemates or other acquaintances.

Officers said any disclosure made under the scheme is provided verbally and cannot be shared with friends or family. Recipients are required to sign an undertaking agreeing not to pass on the information.

If you have concerns for involved children but you do not fall into this category, please speak to Social Care for advice and guidance.

Police also highlighted that Clare's Law is not restricted to women or heterosexual relationships.

Anyone concerned about a current, former or potential partner can submit an application.

North Yorkshire Police said people can apply online, by calling 101, or by visiting their local police station.

Applicants will normally be contacted within 10 days of making a request and may be invited to attend an appointment to discuss their concerns.

The force also reminded residents that the scheme includes a "right to know" element, allowing police to proactively disclose information if they believe someone may be at risk from a partner or former partner.
