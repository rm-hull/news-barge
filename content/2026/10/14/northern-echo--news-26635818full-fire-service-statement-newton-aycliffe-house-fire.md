The cause of a fire which tore through a Newton Aycliffe home and spread into neighbouring properties is under investigation, fire crews have confirmed.

Emergency services were called to Greenlee Garth shortly after 1am on Wednesday, October 14, following reports of a house fire.

Two women inside the property managed to escape before firefighters arrived and suffered minor injuries.

Nearby properties were evacuated as firefighters worked to bring the fire under control, with crews remaining at the scene until 5.41am.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service has now issued a full statement following the blaze.

A spokesperson said:“Shortly before 1.10am today (Wednesday), we received multiple reports of a house fire in Greenlee Garth, Newton Aycliffe.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577997.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police and fire at the scene of the house fire in Newton Aycliffe

“On arrival, crews were faced with a well-developed fire involving the property and roof space, which had spread into neighbouring homes.

“Two occupants self-evacuated before crews arrived and firefighters evacuated nearby properties and worked to contain the fire, leaving the area at 5.41am.

“The cause of the fire is under investigation.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21578219.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scene of house fire in Newton Aycliffe

Neighbouring properties were also damaged in the fire, although police said the majority of residents have since been able to return to their homes.

A force spokesperson said:“An arson investigation is underway following a house fire in Newton Aycliffe overnight.

“Emergency services were called to an address in Greenlee Garth, just after 1am following reports the terraced house had been set on fire.

“Thankfully the two women inside managed to escape with minor injuries and were taken to hospital where they remain.

“No one else was injured but neighbouring houses were also damaged and had to be evacuated. The majority of residents have now been able to return to their homes.
