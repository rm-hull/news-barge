## Russia's history of targeting foreign journalists

Russia has arrested a number of foreign citizens in recent years on charges widely dismissed as baseless.

One of the most prominent cases of a foreign journalist arrested in Russia ‌was the detention of Wall Street Journal reporter Evan Gershkovich in ​2023.

He was found guilty of espionage by a Russian court, sentenced to 16 years and then freed in an East-West prisoner swap in August 2024.

Radio Free Europe editor Alsu Kurmasheva, a dual US-Russian citizen, was held for more than nine months before being convicted on charges of spreading “fake” news about the Russian army.

She was also freed along with Gershkovich during the prisoner swap.

## Russia hits energy facilities in Ukraine's Kyiv, mayor says

## Strikes on Ukraine kill four and wound 41

At least four people were killed and 41 injured in Russian strikes across Ukraine yesterday.

A woman was wounded in a Russian strike on a private home in Kyiv's Darnytskyi district, the Kyiv city military administration said.

A Russian drone hit a residential building in the village of Murovane, near the western city of Lviv, during a morning attack Tuesday, Lviv mayor Andrii Sadovyi said.

One person was killed in the Fastiv district of the Kyiv region, and at least two others were hurt in a Russian attack on the area, said Tymur Tkachenko, head of the regional military administration.

A Russian drone struck an educational institution in the center of Rivne in northwest Ukraine, wounding 14 people, the official said.

## Zelensky says Ukraine received no intelligence about missiles headed to Kyiv

Ukraine received no early warning from its partners about five Russian ballistic missiles that hit civilian infrastructure in the capital of Kyiv, president Volodymyr Zelensky said.

Zelensky did not specify which partners he was talking about. He characterised the barrage as "particularly vile" and emphasised that receiving intelligence from partners about such attacks "helps save lives" and is "one of the most critical issues".

"This is precisely what helps prevent people from being killed — when there is an early warning about ballistic missiles," he wrote in a post on X.

## Burnham sends fresh message to Ukraine ahead of Zelensky visit

Andy Burnham will welcome Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky to Downing Street on Wednesday in a demonstration of support following US president Donald Trump’s diesel arrangement with Russia.

The Prime Minister is expected to utilise the bilateral visit to announce accelerated military assistance for Kyiv, alongside promises of extra sanctions targeting Moscow.

As part of the increased aid, the UK will speed up deliveries of lightweight multirole missiles, which have proven effective at countering Russian jet-powered drones.

## Ukraine has ‘broken Russia’s ability to advance’, claims Kyiv’s former top general

Ukraine has “broken Russia’s ability to advance” by killing so many of its soldiers, Kyiv’s former top general has claimed.

General Oleksandr Syrskyi claimed Russia’s overall war losses were six times higher than Ukraine’s, adding that “if you count only dead, then it’s up to 11 times”.

Gen Syrskyi, who was talking to the BBC in one of his first interviews since he was removed in the summer, said the higher death toll for the Russian army partly accounts for why Russian advances have largely stalled across the eastern frontline. Technological advances in the Ukrainian military, and strategic attacks on Russian weak points were other factors, he said.
