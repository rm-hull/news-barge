# Historic Grade II-listed Wetherby police monument set for restoration after council gives approval

## Repairs can be carried out on a historic police monument in Wetherby after plans were agreed with the city council.

Timber and stonework will be restored at the remains of the Grade II-listed West Lodge Monument.

![Repairs to the Grade II-listed West Lodge Monument in Wetherby have been approved to address deteriorating stonework and help secure its long-term future](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/14/12/44/onecms_14fe5158-c040-4844-93da-1d330c62db47_534cf731-ea80-4ea9-98e7-d08f3093c6ec.jpeg?trim=0,94,0,28&crop=&width=990&quality=65)


Leeds City Council granted an application for listed building consent to carry out the works.

West Yorkshire Police sought permission to to preserve the Georgian monument on Boston Road.

A heritage report said: “The significance of this listed structure is that it represents an example of 18th-century Georgian country architecture and the surviving remains of the Wetherby Grange Estate.

“Recognising the significance of this structure, not only to the town of Wetherby but also to the wider Leeds area, West Yorkshire Police are committed to ensuring that all works are undertaken to a high professional standard.”

The report said the monument, which bears the coat of arms of the West Riding of Yorkshire, was built in the early 19th century in the grounds of Wetherby Police Station.

The limestone structure was partially demolished during the 20th century.

Work was needed to maintain the building after incorrect materials were used during previous repairs.

The report said the weathered condition of stonework in the site’s east elevation was of particular concern.

It said: “If left untreated, this deterioration has the potential to result in structural issues that may compromise the stability and long‑term preservation of the monument.”

A qualified stonemason would be hired to ensure the repairs matched the existing fabric of the site.

A council planning officer’s report said traditional materials and building methods should be used.

It said: “The works have been carefully designed to preserve the special architectural and historic interest of the listed structure, reduce ongoing deterioration, and secure the asset’s long-term conservation

“As such, the proposed works are considered to preserve the significance of the designated heritage asset and will result in no harm to its significance.”
