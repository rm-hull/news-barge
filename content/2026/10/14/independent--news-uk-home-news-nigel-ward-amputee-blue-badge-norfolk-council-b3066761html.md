A 71-year-old amputee has been asked by his local council to prove he still has difficulty walking before his blue badge can be renewed because he might have a “prosthesis that allows him to run Olympic marathons”, according to the disabled man.

Nigel Ward, who lives in King’s Lynn in Norfolk, first received a blue badge after losing his leg three years ago, which happened while he was on a cruise. His initial application went through without difficulty.

His attempt to renew the badge was a different matter, and Norfolk County Council responded by asking for “medical evidence” that he had mobility issues.

A council worker suggested during a conversation with Mr Ward that some amputees did not have difficulty walking or even running.

The council has insisted it is working within the guidance set by the Department for Transport (DfT). It reportedly told Mr Ward that a letter from his GP would not be considered sufficient evidence.

The DfT blue badge scheme allows disabled people with important mobility difficulties to park closer to their destination, either as a passenger or driver. Mr Ward said he was not currently in touch with any medical professionals about his amputation because he was otherwise healthy.

![Ward has been asked to prove his mobility issues](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/15/01M4X6NZNAG99Q2PC43SPK13XJ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I have no need to see anybody medically about the amputation: the stump is fine, and the prosthetic limb works perfectly,” Mr Ward said.

“So I don’t have any contact directly with medical professionals on my disability.”

“I said to him that my leg isn’t going to grow back,” Mr Ward, recalling his conversation with the council worker, said.

“And he said, ‘Well, see it from our perspective’. He said, ‘You might have one of those spring prosthetics that allows you to run Olympic marathons’.”

Mr Ward added that his mobility was severely impacted by the loss of his leg, and so his need for a blue badge to support his day-to-day independence was “critical”.

“I think they (the council) are alluding to the fact that there are certainly people, amputees, who wear prosthetics and who are able to cope and manage quite well, as if it was just an extension of the normal limb,” Mr Ward said. “Now, I’m not in that category. My walking with the prosthetic limb is very limited.

“I’ve never actually counted, but I doubt that I could walk 50 steps without needing to stop for a few minutes to take a breath. I have never been able to manage my balance, which means that I need walking aids as well as the prosthetic.

“Therefore getting closer to the destination using a parking bay is critical, really.”

A spokesperson for Norfolk County Council said: “We know how important blue badges are for helping people to remain independent.

“We have to work within the guidance set for us by the Department for Transport, which includes detailed guidance how eligibility should be assessed through evidence and assessments.

![Ward was first issued with a blue badge three years ago](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/06/00/22122646-11bb4643-9487-4bf9-9813-dfecffd63288.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“As detailed on our website applicants must adequately evidence the difficulties they experience whilst walking, this applies to those reapplying for an expiring blue badge as well as first time applications.

“There is no renewal process, and applicants are required to evidence their ongoing eligibility with each application.

“Evidence could include letters, reports or details of treatment from a medical professional.

“Anyone requiring help with a blue badge application or who is unable to use the online application process can call the council’s Customer Service Centre on 0344 800 8020, and we would be happy to provide support and advice.”
