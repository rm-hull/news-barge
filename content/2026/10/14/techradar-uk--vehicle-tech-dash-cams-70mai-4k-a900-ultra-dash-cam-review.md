### 70mai 4K A900 Ultra: two-minute review

While many of the best dash cams on the market come with plenty of features and functions, not all of them boast 4K shooting capability. The dual-channel 70mai 4K A900 Ultra captures 4K footage not only with its front-facing camera, but also with the rearward-facing unit that makes up this potent dash cam bundle.   
This camera combo benefits from Sony Starvis 2 IMX678 sensors in both units too, which combined with the 4K, HDR and 70mai’s own Night Owl Vision 2.0 processing, means the bundle is perfect for capturing footage night and day. The resolution is impressive, with the f/1.8 front lens capable of shooting 3840 x 2160P (4K) at 30fps, and the f/1.6 rearward camera able to shoot the same 4K quality at 25fps. The field of view for both units is a generous 130 degrees.  
The rest of the specification is similarly impressive, with the chunky main camera featuring a 3-inch rear-facing screen and four control buttons, which makes adjusting settings and previewing footage easy. There is a 5-system built-in GPS, and buffered emergency recording that captures up to 3 minutes and ten seconds before and 30 seconds after an incident.   
Bonus appeal is delivered with the likes of 70mai’s Smart Parking Surveillance 2.0 (subject to hardwiring the unit into a vehicle), a Driver Fatigue Alert, and 4G Cloud+ 2.0 compatibility that delivers a live view, remote playback and two-way voice communication that covers all bases on the security front.  
While capturing 4K footage soon amasses lots of chunky files, the 70mai 4K A900 Ultra features Wi-Fi 6, which means the transfer of footage and general file management is both quick and easy, with speeds of up to 45MBps. Another plus of the 70mai 4K A900 Ultra comes via compatibility with 70mai’s Battery Pack Mini, which gives assured backup power, and up to 30 days of parking surveillance without draining your vehicle’s battery.

![interior image of a car showing 70mai 4K A900 Ultra Dash Cam attached to windscreen](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/CLCGsST9NivQLtPWbPVtLG-1200-80.jpg)

Topping off an impressive set of features is hands-free voice control, while the unit itself features supercapacitor technology that offers more durable performance across the ranged of temperatures that occur inside vehicles over time.   
All of this is squeezed into a compact box-shaped design for the main camera unit, while the rearward-facing camera is a fairly typical tube-shaped design. Both units can be attached to screen glass using supplied adhesive pads, and everything needed for the job comes inside the box.   
The 70mai 4K A900 Ultra is therefore a one-stop solution, although it is worth noting that a hardwire kit, which unlocks the potential of around-the-clock surveillance monitoring, will need to be purchased separately. Otherwise, the units can be powered via a standard 12V in-car power socket, for which an adapter is supplied. The 70mai Battery Pack Mini is also sold as an optional extra.

### 70mai 4K A900 Ultra: Design

I’ve tested several dash cameras from the 70mai stable, and always found them to be well designed and very nicely made. The 70mai 4K A900 Ultra follows that theme, with a good-looking exterior for the main camera unit that sits upfront. Meanwhile, the tube-shaped rear-facing camera looks fairly conventional, but is also very well put together.  
The main camera unit feels great in the hand, weighty and chunky. It also features a built-in mounting bracket, which is non-detachable. The angle-adjustment bracket that joins the camera unit, and the sticky attachment that fits to the windshield felt quite stiff to adjust, but this makes sense when you consider how much vibration and bumping around the unit will be subjected to over time, and should mean that the position of the camera remains consistent over time.  
Up front, the lens protrudes from the front of the camera unit, but not so far as to be intrusive when fitting to the windshield. The side of the unit has a slot for microSD cards up to 512GB capacity, and the port for connecting the power and rear camera cable. I like the way 70mai does its connectors, with a push fit for the camera wire that feels almost locked in place once it has been pushed in. Again, having a durable design for components like this means there’s less chance for failure over time.

There’s a 3-inch screen on the back of the front dash camera unit, which is a good size for previewing footage and the like, as well as making the setup process nice and easy. It took me mere minutes to power-up the camera using the supplied 12V power plug and cable. From there, picking through the installation options and setting the language, time and date options was easily achieved via the four plastic buttons directly below the screen.

![70mai 4K A900 Ultra Dash Cam](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/8gt7hXpXTawUSVifuggReQ-1200-80.jpg)

Both cameras can be fixed to the glass using the supplied self-adhesive pads. In the case of the front unit this has to be applied to the bracket and then stuck to the screen. There are static sheets for anyone who wants to stick these to the glass first, which isa good option if you think you’re going to need to peel it off and move to another vehicle at some point. The rear camera has its sticky pad already fixed to the bracket.   
Getting this into position in a rear window can be tricky, depending on the vehicle, but the good news is that the lens barrel can be rotated into its optimal position, and the view it captures checked within the front-facing screen on the front. This is presented as a picture-in-picture view, so any tweaks can be made if the view doesn’t look quite right. 70mai does sell an optional hardwire kit for anyone wanting to access the entire suite of features and functions, including round-the-clock vehicle surveillance.

### 70mai 4K A900 Ultra: Performance

I found the 70mai 4K A900 Ultra dash cam very easy to use once I’d got it installed and ready to go. As mentioned, the initial setup procedure is very straightforward, and can largely be done without consulting the manual thanks to the simple step-by-step process, which includes formatting the memory card. I especially liked the way the chunky buttons on the back of the camera could be used for making adjustments. Combined with the 70mai app, which I’ve used before, this combination makes light work of everyday file management duties.  
Which brings us to the subject of the video. The 4K quality produced by this twin-channel camera combination is excellent, and I found the after-dark footage it captured was as good as footage shot in daylight. The 70mai engineers have clearly put some effort into squeezing the maximum detail from their HDR software, and the results are clear to see in the clips on this page.   
The cameras also coped admirably with variable light quality and weather conditions, as well as sudden changes in lighting such driving through a tunnel, as illustrated in the clips here. Importantly, there's good definition in details such as license plates and road signs.  
One of the more interesting features of this dual-channel dash cam is its use of low-voltage differential signaling, or LVDS. 70mai claims this means the 4K feed from the rear camera is relayed as a digital signal, with no re-encoding, which ultimately means there's no compression carried out, even though there is quite a long cable connecting the two camera units. 70mai also says the connection features 20ms ultra-low latency. In essence, that should mean that if you're using the feed from the rear camera when reversing, you'll see it in real time, without any lag. On a more practical note, the more efficient cabling seems thinner than usual too.

## Buy it if...

**You want top-quality video**  
There's no doubting the capability of the 70mai 4K A900 Ultra dash cam, thanks to its 4K shooting power. Better still, you get similarly potent video capture from the rear camera too.

**You value extra features**  
The 70mai 4K A900 Ultra dash cam comes with a host of added-value features, including excellent after-dark capability, and parking surveillance via the optional hardwire kit.

**You want more power**  
The 70mai 4K A900 Ultra dash cam can be boosted by adding 70mai's Battery Pack Mini as an option. This avoids any worries about battery drain, and makes the twin-camera setup even more versatile.

## Don't buy it if...

**Twin cameras are more than you need**  
Lots of us tend to think driving standards have been slipping, and so all-round protection is useful. But if you crave simplicity then a single-camera setup will suffice.

**You're looking for a budget option**  
The 70mai 4K A900 Ultra dash cam is best described as a mid-range model, and while it is competitively priced, there are cheaper bundles out there.

**Fitting a dash cam is a chore**  
The downside of the 70mai 4K A900 Ultra dash cam is that it requires twice as many cables to be plumbed in behind trim panels. A single-channel camera might therefore be a better bet.
