From traditional festive reds and golds to chocolate-inspired displays and a farm-themed Christmas experience, Mainsgill Farmshop has once again transformed into a festive wonderland.

The family-run destination off the A66 has unveiled six themed Christmas displays for 2026, filling its gift halls with decorations, lights, seasonal gifts and a selection of artificial trees.

The Christmas department has become an annual tradition for families from North Yorkshire, County Durham and beyond.

Rachel, daughter of owners Andrew and Maria Henshaw, said: "Our Christmas displays are now open and we've got six huge themes this year.

"The first one is a traditional theme called Christmas is Served, so that's all the traditional reds, golds and greens.

"It's Christmas how people imagine it. It then goes into our nostalgic vintage theme, which is how my mum remembers her childhood Christmas.

"It's very 80s and 90s, with lots of fun and colour. One of our customers said it felt like joy."

She added: "We've then got Jingle Bell Choc, which is browns, beiges and everything chocolate and cakes.

"We've then got Merrygill Farm, which is all about the farm and that has gone down a storm.

"It then goes into Wrapped With Wonder, which is an elf theme, before moving into Cosy Christmas Chalet, which is more modern home interior vibes with a skiing element to it."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577114.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She continued: "The food hall is an absolute abundance of everything from festive chocolates, biscuits and cheeses.

"We've got Christmas orders open now, and the brochures are all launched in store.

"It's looking pretty good."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577113.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577108.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Rachel said the public response has been positive.

She said: "They've been blown away and a lot of people are saying it's our best Christmas yet."

The displays are the latest chapter in a success story that began more than 30 years ago with a struggling 57-acre farm and a young couple determined to make it work.

Andrew and Maria Henshaw moved to Mainsgill Farm from Lancashire in 1995 shortly after getting married.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577106.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577103.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The farm had pigs, beef cattle and dairy replacements, and the couple hoped to build a future as independent farmers. However, the late 1990s were a difficult time for British agriculture. As debts rose and income fell, the Henshaws began selling their home-reared meat directly to customers.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577104.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577117.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

They converted a garage beside their house into a small farm shop and preparation room, then packed orders late into the night after working all day on the farm.

As demand grew, they won permission to build a new farm shop beside the A66 and opened it in June 2001, during that year's foot-and-mouth outbreak.

The business expanded over the following decades, adding larger food halls, a tearoom, butchery and gift departments. A two-storey extension in 2014 increased its retail space to about 20,000 square feet.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577096.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The family now farms more than 1,200 acres, while the site has become one of the region's best-known rural shopping destinations.

The festive displays arrive as Mainsgill's annual pumpkin patch welcomes visitors in October.

The attraction is open from 10am, with last entry at 3pm, from October 17 to 31.

Entry is free; visitors pay only for the pumpkins they pick, and seasonal photo opportunities are available too.
