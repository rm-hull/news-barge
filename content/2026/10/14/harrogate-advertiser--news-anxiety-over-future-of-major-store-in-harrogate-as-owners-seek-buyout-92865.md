# New fears over iconic business in Harrogate's Victoria Shopping Centre as parent company seeks buyers

## “A buyer may prefer a selected group of stores that it believes can trade profitably, rather than assume responsibility for the entire estate”

There is concern over what comes next for Harrogate's only discount store as the hunt for a buyer goes in at one of Britain's biggest high street retailers.

![A fixture at Victoria Shopping Centre for years, there is no information as yet on whether Harrogate’s  Poundland store will be involved in any rescue deal or not.](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/14/9/05/PoundlandSign.jpeg?trim=0,0,0,0&crop=&width=990&quality=65)


The national chain is currently in rescue talks as its American owners, US investment firm Gordon Brothers, seek a buyer at a time when high street businesses are facing a tough world to do business in.

With 600 stores in the UK and Ireland, and 12,000 employees in total, there are fears any buyout may end up being a a partial rescue, leaving the future of as many as half the stores up in the air.

A fixture at Victoria Shopping Centre for years, there is no information as yet on whether Harrogate’s Poundland store will be involved in any rescue deal or not.

One insolvency expert told the Harrogate Advertiser that Poundland’s situation raised a crucial question beyond the headlines about jobs: if a buyer saves fewer than half the chain, what happens to the stores left behind?

“A buyer may prefer a selected group of stores that it believes can trade profitably, rather than assume responsibility for the entire estate,” said Molly Monks of Parker Walsh, a UK-based insolvency, business recovery and corporate restructuring firm.

"A partial sale can preserve value where a business cannot realistically continue in its existing form.

"It may allow viable stores to keep trading rather than risk losing the entire operation.

“But the stores left outside the deal still need a plan. They could potentially attract another buyer, continue trading for a period while options are explored, or ultimately close if there is no viable way forward.”

Times have been tough in recent years even for this well-known retail giant, though the company is reporting a “strong performance” despite rising costs and growing competition.

In October 2025, Poundland launched a restructuring plan, reducing its network from 800 to around 600 stores.

Last summer, the retailer was sold for £1 last summer by previous owner Pepco to US investment firm Gordon Brothers.

The current owners have confirmed they are in talks with bidders including Irish property development group, Martin Property Group, and TG Jones owner Modella.

Poundland management, including managing director Barry Williams, confirmed they have aligned themselves with the Martin Property Group bid, suggesting it would be the preferred partner for any management buyout.

But Usdaw, the union representing shop workers, has warned that the retailer could fall into administration if that deal falls through, potentially putting thousands of jobs at risk.
