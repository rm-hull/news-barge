# Martin House Children's Hospice, Boston Spa: Sweet moment as seven-year-old Yorkshire girl interviews documentary filmmaker Louis Theroux

## The roles were reversed when documentary filmmaker and broadcaster Louis Theroux was interviewed by a seven-year-old girl at Martin House Children’s Hospice.

Louis Theroux, the documentary filmmaker, visited Martin House Children’s Hospice on Tuesday, October 6, 2026, where he was given a tour of its newly transformed facilities and met children and families supported by Martin House.

During the visit, he saw the new children’s wing and hydrotherapy pool, as well as learning more about the specialist palliative care and support Martin House provides to children and young people with life-threatening conditions and their families.

Tables were turned when Louis met seven-year-old Hooriya, who is supported by Martin House and had prepared some questions of her own for the experienced interviewer.

Hooriya interviewed Louis with questions about everything from what he was like as a child to his favourite chocolate (‘revels’), as well as asking what he thought of Martin House.

Louis said: “It was my first time visiting a children’s hospice and I was really impressed by the facilities.

“Everyone at Martin House has made me feel incredibly welcome.

“I really enjoyed meeting Hooriya and being interviewed by her. I’m normally the one asking the questions, so it was a bit of a role reversal - I’ve certainly never had an interview quite like it before.”

![Hooriya interviewed Louis Theroux. (Pic credit: Martin House Children's Hospice)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjM1MmU1MjcwLTcwNzUtNDIxOS1iOGI4LTBiYzQ5OWEyMTk0NzoxMzkzMmQ5Yy05NDVmLTRlYWQtYjhmOC01ZjBhNDFjMmNiNzA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The interview was filmed during Louis’ visit and has been shared on Martin House’s social media channels.

Chief executive of Martin House, Clair Holdsworth, said: “We were delighted to welcome Louis to Martin House and give him the opportunity to see first-hand the care and support we provide.

“What really stood out was the time he took with the children and families he met. They told us afterwards how genuinely interested he was in hearing their stories and how much they valued having the opportunity to talk openly about their experiences.

“For some, simply having that conversation meant a great deal, and meeting Louis will be a memory they treasure for years to come.

![Louis Theroux with Hooriya at Martin House. (Pic credit: Martin House Children's Hospice)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU2YTdjNDk3LWVmNmYtNDhkMS04MzUyLTg1OWJlNTRiYzAzYTpiYzdhOTg3OC0yNGE5LTQ5NmQtOTUwYy1iZTIwZjgzN2VmZWI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


“And of course, Hooriya did a fantastic job of turning the tables on one of the country’s best-known interviewers.

“We hope their conversation will give people a glimpse of the warmth, fun and personality that is such an important part of life at Martin House.”

Martin House recently completed a £21.9 million transformation of its hospice, creating new and improved facilities including a hydrotherapy pool, new children’s and young people’s bedrooms, sensory spaces and enhanced clinical facilities.

Every year, Martin House supports more than 550 children and young people with life-shortening conditions, and their families, across West, North and East Yorkshire.

Care is provided at the hospice in Boston Spa, in hospitals and in families’ own homes.

Providing this specialist care costs around £13 million every year, with the majority coming from voluntary donations and fundraising.

For more information you can visit the Martin House Children’s Hospice website.
