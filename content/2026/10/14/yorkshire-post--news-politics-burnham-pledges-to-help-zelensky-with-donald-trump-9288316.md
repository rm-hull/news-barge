# 'Repair bridges': Andy Burnham pledges to help Zelensky with Donald Trump

## Andy Burnham has said he will seek to restore Donald Trump’s support for Ukraine and “repair bridges” as Volodymyr Zelensky visited Downing Street.

The Ukrainian president received an unprecedented honour guard on his arrival at No 10, made up of troops from the Irish Guards, amid what the Prime Minister described as “dark and difficult days” for his country.

Mr Burnham told Mr Zelensky: “You have seen the solidarity and the support you have got from everybody here in Britain.

“We are behind you and that will not change.”

In return, Mr Zelensky said he was “thankful” for the support, and “very proud” that the British people had proved “one of the strongest partners in our history”.

Prior to the historic guard of honour, the Ukrainian President met King Charles in Buckingham Palace.

![Volodymyr Zelensky and Andy Burnham shake hands outside 10 Downing Street, flanked by soldiers in red dress uniform](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/14/17/cs-5daa37ad-134f-4b70-9952-6070aed8683a.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The visit came during a period of strained relations between Ukraine and the United States after Donald Trump eased sanctions on Russian diesel last week as high fuel prices in the US threatened to overshadow November’s mid-term elections.

The UK responded to news of the deal by pledging to “maintain pressure on Russia”.

Recent days have also seen the US president suggesting Ukraine needs a new leader and claiming Russian leader Vladimir Putin wants a peace deal more than the government in Kyiv.

Concerns have also been raised about intelligence sharing from the US, with Mr Zelensky saying Kyiv had received no warning about Russian missile strikes on Monday and Tuesday.

Speaking in the Commons ahead of his meeting with Mr Zelensky, the Prime Minister said he would seek to restore Donald Trump’s support for Ukraine.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham welcomes President of Ukraine Volodymyr Zelensky (Joran Pettitt/PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/14/17/v2-image-production-5aced2fd390824dbcbd1c854a6cdf528Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyMDgxNjgw-2.86183271.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


During Prime Minister’s Questions, Mr Burnham said he was being “careful” in his language after Mr Trump’s diesel deal with Russia, as he said he was “laying the ground” for a reconciliation.

“I called him on Saturday because I recognised it was another very dark and difficult moment for him as they contemplate a winter that will be severe for his people,” Mr Burnham told MPs.

“I recognise that he is carrying the heaviest of weights right now, and it is extremely difficult for him.

“What I have to do in my role is try and repair bridges so that the support comes back.

“Therefore I should, at this moment in time, be careful in terms of laying the ground to bring people back together.

“That is the role I intend to play. It’s the right role for me to play in this moment.”

He added: “I will listen to President Zelensky carefully, but he won’t be going away from Britain today empty-handed.”

![The King shakes hands with President Zelensky at Buckingham Palace (Aaron Chown/PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/10/14/16/v2-image-production-6455e742c6754923967839c229478458Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyMDc1ODc0-2.86182878.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


During his meeting with Mr Zelensky, he confirmed that the UK would accelerate the supply of crucial air defence missiles to Ukraine amid “heavy bombardment” from Russia.

The accelerated support will see faster deliveries of British lightweight multi-role missiles to Ukraine, which have proved effective against Russian jet-powered drones.

Mr Burnham also said he had asked his ministers to examine other options for supporting Ukraine, telling Mr Zelensky they were “looking at all possible avenues”.

And he insisted that the Ukrainian president was “prepared to sit down and discuss” the energy ceasefire proposed by Mr Trump, but added: “Sadly, what we have seen from Russia is further attacks on critical national infrastructure in Ukraine.”

He said: “In these moments, Britain will continue to provide you with whatever support we can.”

The UK will also urge its allies to increase the pressure on Russia, with Chancellor John Healey using IMF meetings in Bangkok to call for a ban on Russian liquified natural gas (LNG).

The meeting in Downing Street followed what Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband described as a “very difficult few days” in relations with Washington following Mr Trump’s decision to ease sanctions on Russian diesel ahead of US mid-term elections overshadowed by rising fuel prices.
