These days, web hosting providers are much more user-friendly and flexible than they once were. Most offer technical support and automated or AI features for ease of use, no matter your ability level. Plus, you’ll likely have the option to expand your plan to cover VPS hosting, which can be better for larger or growing businesses.

As a tech journalist, I’ve tried, tested and compared many different web hosting options. Right now, I’m looking at Hostinger and Bluehost. Both services will tick a lot of boxes for many users, but the one I’d recommend depends on what you’re looking for in a web hosting provider.

It’s worth spending some time learning about the main features available, so you can confidently find the right plan for your needs. It’s possible to sign up to each for just one month but it’s a smarter (and financially prudent) idea to tie yourself in for an extended period of time. Here’s what you need to know to make the best decision for your situation, whether you want a web host for personal or business needs.

## 1. Hostinger vs Bluehost: Pricing and plans

### Hostinger

**Pros:** Cheaper; shorter minimum subscription

**Cons:** Best prices require you to commit to longest period

### Bluehost

**Pros:** Faster SSD storage comes as standard

**Cons:** More expensive; basic plan only offers 10GB of storage

Understandably, Hostinger and Bluehost both gear their best prices towards longer-term subscriptions. It makes sense. After all, who’s launching a website for just one month? When it comes to Standard web hosting, Hostinger starts at the equivalent of £1.49 per month (working out at £71.52 for four years) with the more popular option being its Premium plan for £2.99 per month (£143.52 for four years) and offering 20GB of SSD storage, up to three websites, two mailboxes per website for free for a year, and a free domain for one year. If you want faster NVMe storage, the Unlimited plan for £3.99 per month (£191.52 for four years) is the better option.

All of Bluehost’s plans are either monthly, 12 months, 36 months, or 48 months. The most basic plan is the Starter package for £2.86 per month for 36 months, it only offers 10GB of storage but it’s faster NVMe-based storage. It also offers AI site creation tools, and a free domain for the first year. For £5.12 per month, you can increase storage space to 50GB with the Business plan. Meanwhile, the eCommerce Essentials plan increasing storage to 100GB while offering ecommerce tools, all for £5.27 per month.

Both are closely matched with their standard web hosting plans. Hostinger and Bluehost also have VPS hosting options, OpenClaw and n8n plans, and dedicated hosting if you need the ultimate solution for your business. It’s worth spending some time investigating which solution is best for you, as some people may prefer the flexibility of VPS hosting. It essentially gives you access to a server rather than just web hosting so you could use it for many more features than a website such as game server hosting or n8n automation.

## 2. Hostinger vs Bluehost: Features

### Hostinger

**Pros:** User-friendly control panel; effective AI website builder; SEO tools available

**Cons:** AI-built websites require some tailoring

### Bluehost

**Pros:** Adaptive; SEO tools available

**Cons:** VPS hosting sign-up required

Hostinger and Bluehost are equally feature-packed, offering functionality that works well for both beginners and more advanced users. I have very few complaints about either service thanks to that.

![For those designing a website offline, Hostinger is simple enough to upload files via the file manager or FTP](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/30/15/53/Hostinger-website-example.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Hostinger divides things up so it’s easy to set up your site. For those designing a website offline, it’s simple enough to upload files via the file manager or FTP. However, if you want Hostinger to create a simple site for you, that’s very easy to do too. There’s the WordPress-based website builder which can get you started nicely with key plugins to match your theme choice. There’s also Hostinger Horizons which is a full AI solution. Just enter a few prompts and Hostinger does all the hard work for you. Wait a minute or two and you get an attractive simple website to get you started. It’s a good idea to customise it then so it doesn’t look too generic but it’s ideal for a quick setup.

Bluehost has a similarly clear control panel with a WordPress onboarding process that easily gets you started. As with Hostinger, you can simply upload your website directly but if you want some help then WordPress will create a template via a few prompts and insights you provide. It only took me about a minute and I was soon provided with three sites to choose from. Again, like Hostinger, you’ll want to customise the site to make it more in keeping with your vision but it’s useful to play around with ideas in this way. Another nice touch is how WordPress via Bluehost tailors itself based on your experience with WordPress in the past.

With more general features, both hosts offer all you could need. Besides the plan details mentioned above, both offer free SSL, malware scanning, DDoS protection, and weekly backups. More regular backups can be added for a cost. Bluehost provides Yoast SEO for improved marketing and a free trial of its Pro Email service.

Hostinger offers a similar form of SEO tool and WooCommerce across the board. On Hostinger, you can deploy Node.js web apps on all plans from **Premiuim** upwards while Bluehost requires you to sign up for its VPS hosting rather than regular web hosting. That won’t be a dealbreaker for everyone but worth considering if it matters to you.

I found myself preferring Hostinger’s control panel compared to Bluehost’s – but that isn’t to say that Bluehost’s is tricky to figure out. It’s just that the Hostinger control panel is that touch more intuitive, giving it an advantage.

## 3. Hostinger vs Bluehost: Ease of use

### Hostinger

**Pros:** Intuitive custom hPanel

**Cons:** Could be more attractive visually

### Bluehost

**Pros:** Easy to get to grips with

**Cons:** There are more intuitive and attractive solutions out there

Both Hostinger and Bluehost take moments to sign up to. There’s hardly anything between them in terms of how simple they are to use. As with all web hosting plans, it’s worth checking exactly what you need. For some people, a VPS hosting plan may prove more useful than traditional web hosting but both companies tend to make whatever you choose pretty simple to get to grips with.

The little details like finding the right server location for you automatically adds up and it’s never confusing to know what to do next. Hostinger has its own custom hPanel which is very intuitive but Bluehost’s standard interface is still easy enough to figure out.

Refreshingly, setting up a server only took a couple of minutes with both hosts. It only took me under 20 minutes to create something simple yet effective with both sites. Pairing up a domain was very quick as well. Most importantly, Hostinger and Bluehost are both easy to understand even if you haven’t used web hosting services before.

## 4. Hostinger vs Bluehost: Support

### Hostinger

**Pros:** AI assistance available via Whatsapp; real support agents available

**Cons:** No phone support

### Bluehost

**Pros:** Real tech support agent available; phone support

**Cons:** AI assistance can be slow

In a continuing trend for many web hosting services, Hostinger and Bluehost both offer AI bots as your first port of call for any issue. Hostinger’s bot is called Kodee while Bluehost has one called Blu. Both are good for solving many simple queries but it’s helpful to know there are alternatives.

Hostinger has a highly extensive knowledge base with a combination of text and video tutorials. Again, that should assist with most issues. There’s no phone support, although you can use Kodee via WhatsApp if you prefer that over a browser. If you ask Kodee to talk to a human, you’ll be transferred to a real person to solve your problem.

Bluehost’s AI bot is certainly helpful but a little slow at times. Fortunately, you can chat to a real tech support person, and the knowledge base is valuable. Best of all, on the pricier plans, you also gain phone support.

## Should you get Hostinger or Bluehost?

I’d say that whichever service you choose out of Hostinger and Bluehost, you’re onto a good thing. I prefer Hostinger for its more beginner-friendly features but Bluehost is still very simple to get to grips with. It really comes down to how you plan on designing your site. If you’re ready to upload your previously-made website then either will suit you.

However, if you need the guidance, go with Hostinger. Its AI tools are near unparalleled and very good at supporting your ambitions and website dreams. Still, if you’re looking for something WordPress-based then Bluehost is worth checking out. Its AI tools do a good job of creating a WordPress website and I really appreciated the fact you’re provided with options for the design even before you get into customisation.

When it comes to the essentials, Hostinger and Bluehost have everything covered. They provide a speedy service, both for browsing and for implementing changes. Their control panels are clear and support options work well. Bear in mind for specific needs, you may need to spend more (or consider a VPS plan) but that’s the case with both web hosting companies here. Ultimately, it really does come down to personal taste as to whether you want or need the more advanced AI tools that Hostinger offers as standard.
