Marine Le Pen, the far-right leader in France, has vowed to engage in a “trial of strength” with the EU over its finances if she is elected as president next year.

Le Pen, the former leader of the far-right National Rally, is the frontrunner for France’s presidential election in April, as incumbent Emmanuel Macron steps down following two consecutive terms.

The 58-year-old has defined much of her political career against the European bloc, which is now working to push through a seven-year budget before the French presidential elections. Speaking to the Financial Times from her Paris office, Le Pen described the move as “deeply objectionable”.

“In fact, I think it reveals the contempt the Commission has for democratic processes in Europe,” she said, although accepted that her promise to cut France’s contribution to the EU budget would be difficult. “We know there will be a tough debate, even a trial of strength. But we have the will and the conviction that we will be able to do it.”

![Far-right party Rassemblement National (RN) candidate in France's 2027 presidential election Marine Le Pen](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/16/2299339867...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Brussels wants a deal by Christmas, but France and Germany are in dispute over the long-term budget amid proposals for deep cuts.

The EU Commission has proposed a budget for 2028-2034 that is worth nearly €2 trillion, well beyond the roughly €1.2 trillion budget during the 2021-2027 period.

Germany, Austria, Denmark, Finland, the Netherlands, and Sweden want to cut several hundred billion euros from the budget, but France has hit out at these calls, with Macron declaring that the bloc needs an “ambitious” budget.

Le Pen’s five-year, €140bn plan to cut the deficit if she is elected, would see France save €11bn by cutting its contributions to the EU budget in 2027, she said. Paris would ultimately limit its net contribution to the EU budget to €5 billion, the level it was at in the early 2000s, a move that would prompt anger in Brussels.

The negotiations come ahead of a busy election year, with French polling currently forecasting a run-off between French far-right leader Marine Le Pen and hard-left rival Jean-Luc Melenchon, an indication of the growing polarisation as Macron's era ends.

![French far-right party Rassemblement National (RN) and allied party UDR's members of parliament, with group president Marine Le Pen sitting in the middle](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/14/17/2298560131...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Le Pen, who in recent weeks has been polling around 33 to 37 per cent, promised pragmatism with the economy, admitting that her main constraint if elected would be the “gigantic, enormous debt... that could become uncontrollable”.

“Politics is in reality a question of conviction confronted by constraints,” she said. “I have no intention of losing my convictions.”

Fighting in her fourth presidential election - and projected to reach her third successive run-off - Le Pen believes next year could be significant for nationalist and anti-EU parties across Europe.

Next year will be an “extraordinary event” as elections also take place in Spain, Italy and Poland, Le Pen said, expressing hope that it could signal a radical new era for the continent.

Le Pen hit out at EU Commission president Ursula von der Leyen, and suggested she “no longer has the confidence of many governments in the EU” and therefore cannot remain in post until the end of her mandate in 2029.

Le Pen officially launched her presidential campaign in western France in July, just one day after an appeals court confirmed her conviction for embezzling EU funds, yet crucially enabled her to stand for office.

The Paris appeals court had upheld her March 2025 conviction for misusing European Parliament funds to pay party staff, but simultaneously shortened her ban on running for office, allowing her to run in the 2027 race.
