York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority has highlighted progress in supporting victims and survivors of domestic abuse over the past year, including expanded safe accommodation, specialist services and improved access to support in rural communities.

The authority says work has continued with local authorities, North Yorkshire Police, health services, criminal justice agencies and voluntary organisations to improve support for people affected by domestic abuse and other forms of violence against women and girls.

Mayor David Skaith and Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime Jo Coles have identified supporting victims and survivors and preventing violence and abuse as key priorities.

## Support and access to justice

Developments over the past 12 months include investment in specialist domestic abuse services and efforts to address barriers faced by people in rural areas, where isolation and distance can make accessing help more difficult.

Live Link facilities have also opened in Northallerton, helping vulnerable victims and witnesses give evidence in a way that can feel safer and more accessible.

October marks one year since the launch of the York and North Yorkshire online Service Directory, which brings together information about services supporting people affected by domestic abuse and other forms of violence against women and girls.

The authority says the directory is intended to make it easier for people to find appropriate support in one place.

## Deputy mayor calls for continued action

Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime Jo Coles said progress had been made, but further work was needed to ensure victims and survivors could access help.

> **Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, said:** Domestic abuse has no place in our communities. I am proud of the progress we have made over the last year, particularly in strengthening support for victims and survivors and improving access to services, but there is still much more to do.

She said Domestic Abuse Awareness Month was an opportunity to recognise progress and renew the commitment to ensuring victims were heard, supported and able to access help when needed.

> **Jo Coles added:** Together with our partners, we will continue to work towards a safer York and North Yorkshire where everyone can live free from abuse, fear and violence.

## Where to find support

Residents can find information about support services through the York and North Yorkshire Support Services Directory.

Although the directory is principally aimed at women and girls, the authority says many of the services listed also support people of all genders, including men and boys.

Anyone experiencing domestic abuse, or concerned about someone else, can use the directory to explore the support available in their area.
