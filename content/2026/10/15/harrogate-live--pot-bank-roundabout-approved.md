Pot Bank roundabout won permission on 13 October with 19 conditions, from bat checks to a rebuilt wall. Up to 960 west Harrogate homes still wait on the land.

The larger roundabout planned for the junction of Otley Road and Pot Bank, on the western edge of Harrogate, now has planning permission.

North Yorkshire Council granted it on Tuesday 13 October, the day its Strategic Planning Committee heard the application. The decision notice for reference 25/03390/RG3 was issued the same day. The committee’s minutes, which will record the vote, have not been published yet.

The scheme replaces the mini roundabout with a conventional three-arm roundabout, with two-lane approaches and new crossing points. The council is both the applicant and the planning authority. We set out the full plans, the 13 trees that will be felled and the objections in our preview of the decision.

## Why it matters for west Harrogate’s new homes

Two housing estates west of the town already have planning permission:

* **Land east of Lady Lane**, up to 480 homes
* **Bluecoats**, up to 480 homes

Both carry a condition that no homes can be built until a Pot Bank scheme is approved and the land for it has been handed to the council. The 13 October approval deals with the first half of that. The land transfer is still to come, and the council has published no date for it.

A third site, **Windmill Farm**, with 643 homes, has not been decided. Officers
want the same condition on it.

## What the permission requires

The notice carries 19 conditions. These are the ones people living and driving nearby will notice:

* **A construction plan first.** No work can start until a construction management plan is approved. It must cover lorry routes, working hours, deliveries, dust, noise, parking for site workers and a named contact for complaints.
* **Rush-hour limits.** The council’s lane rental scheme applies on Otley Road and Pot Bank from 7.30am to 9.30am and from 3.30pm to 7pm on weekdays. Contractors are charged for occupying the road in those hours.
* **Bat checks before felling.** Seven named trees must be inspected by a licensed bat ecologist and can come down only once it is confirmed safe. If a roosting bat is found, work stops until Natural England issues a licence.
* **Newts.** Measures from the great crested newt assessment apply for the whole build.
* **Replacement trees, guaranteed.** Full planting details must be approved. Failed stock must be replaced for 10 years after planting, and any tree that dies within five years of completion is replaced in the next planting season.
* **The wall comes back.** The roadside stone wall must be rebuilt with the salvaged stone wherever possible, and finished before the roundabout is first used.
* **No signs in front of the listed gates.** No highway signs can go up in front of the Grade II listed gates and piers to Moor Park unless the design is agreed first.
* **Lighting and wildlife.** Street lighting needs a plan showing it will not harm bats.
* **Habitat for 30 years.** A habitat management plan must keep the scheme’s biodiversity gains in place for 30 years after completion.

The council has five years to start, under the notice’s first condition. Its planning register gives a permission expiry date of 13 October 2029, which is three years. The notice is the legal document.

## What it means for you

* **If you drive Otley Road or Pot Bank:** nothing changes yet. The council has
  published no start date and no cost. Closures and lane restrictions will go
  on our Harrogate roadworks page when they are
  notified.
* **If you are waiting on the Lady Lane or Bluecoats homes:** the roundabout’s
  permission was one of two steps. Building cannot start until the land transfer
  is also done.
* **If you objected:** the permission is granted. Your route now is the
  conditions. The construction plan, tree planting and lighting details all have
  to be submitted and approved, and those submissions appear on the planning
  register under the same reference.

We will add the committee’s vote when the minutes are published. Other big applications are on our Harrogate planning news page.
