### Acer Swift Spin 14 AI review: Two Minute Review

The Acer Swift Spin 14 AI is an easy recommendation outside of its price tag. It’s light, slim, and pretty attractive in its one colorway. It also comes with a touchscreen and an incredibly smooth 360-degree hinge that makes putting it in tent or tablet mode very easy outside of swinging around a 14-inch piece of hardware. If you’re looking for one of the best laptops with 2-in-1 capabilities, I think this laptop fits right in.

It’s surprisingly powerful for a laptop that relies on integrated graphics (rather than a discrete GPU) – I was able to do some semi-serious gaming on it, though with some compromises – and it comes with a battery life that competes with the best.

Besides the lacklustre sound quality, which I expect on even the best Ultrabooks since they’re so thin, the main issue I have with the laptop is the price tag. Considering everything I’ve mentioned above, as well as the included AI apps, I don’t think the laptop is overpriced. But at about a grand and a half, it’s not quite the value proposition that I expect from Acer, either. Of course, knowing the company, that price will probably come down before long.

### Acer Swift Spin 14 AI review: Design

* **Attractive build**
* **USB-C port on either side**
* **Keyboard is good but not great**

I say that it’s only available in Cobalt Blue, as if that’s a bad thing. In fact, it’s a very attractive dark-ish metallic blue aluminum chassis that’s accented on the lid with silver etchings, giving it an almost space age feel. I like it.

I also like the fact that not only is the port selection pretty comprehensive but that it spreads the ports pretty evenly. Each side has a USB-C port so you don’t have to worry about your orientation to an outlet when you need to charge up – something that invariably happens when I’m reviewing a laptop that uses USB-C to charge and then puts them all on one side.

Along with the two USB-C ports are two regular USB-A ports, an HDMI 2.1 port, and a headphone jack. There’s also on the right side the stylus and its built-in carriage. I always seem to knock off styli that magnetically clip on the side of a laptop so having it tucked away is pretty nice.

![The Acer Swift Spin 14 AI not only has a USB-C port on the right side but storage for the included active stylus.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/HvQ7AY37BAwqoi8PN7gNTe-1200-80.jpg)

The screen may be just 1920x1200p but it looks pretty sharp nonetheless. Maybe it’s its 300 nits of brightness. The screen is also a touch display and feels responsive to the touch. I find the large touchpad to be just as responsive, not to mention is pleasant to use thanks to Acer using Corning Gorilla Glass.

The quality of the keyboard can make or break laptops like this – remember Apple’s butterfly keyboard? Thankfully, the feel of the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI’s is pretty good. There’s a decent amount of travel so I don’t get that cramped feel from ultraslim keyboards. While writing this very review, I never found the keyboard fatiguing.

I will say that the keycaps are flat, and I wish they had a little form fitting concave shape. The keyboard switches also are linear. Since this isn’t a mechanical keyboard, I would have liked a little softer touch. Still, overall, I think it’s good for a thin portable laptop like this.

![The keyboard and touchpad on the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI are pleasant to use.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7EGZNqW7FZZysmcLmSzCce-1200-80.jpg)

Lastly, the 360 hinge for its 2-in-1 capabilities is smooth, so switching to tablet or tent mode is as quick as it can be, maybe only hampered by the size of the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI, which might be on the smaller side for a laptop but is pretty big for a tablet.

![The Acer Swift Spin 14 AI easily folds into tablet mode and can be navigated with your fingers or a stylus.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/GvY5UDRMuaLBuA4csE3Eee-1200-80.jpg)

The Acer Swift Spin 14 AI is supposed to be a light and portable laptop, not a heavy hitter. Despite that, it has a good deal of power for everyday use. The Qualcomm Snapdragon X2 Elite X2E78100 processor may be the more entry level CPU from the company, but it has plenty of juice. The included 16GB of RAM helps too.

Browsing, streaming, and all the day-to-day work barely pushes this laptop. And if you do want to push the Swift Spin 14 AI, it can keep up to a degree. Since I don’t do 3D design or video editing, my way of pushing a computer is testing with gaming and I did that here.

I ran *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, Grand Theft Auto 4,* and*Little Kitty Big City* on this laptop and found that the laptop could mostly keep up. If you look at the benchmarks, you’ll see that a game like*Far Cry 6* runs at about 25 fps depending on settings. That’s not great, but it’s playable, and that was the case with*Clair Obscur* and*Grand Theft Auto 4*.

I was able to play them without getting so frustrated that I wanted to quit, but they definitely pushed the system to where the framerates were mediocre and there was some screen tearing. *Little Kitty Big City* had no issues.

Again, this is not a gaming laptop. The point, though, is that this laptop can handle more than one would expect of one with just integrated graphics and a slim form factor.

Where I think the slim form factor has a bigger impact is in the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI’s sound quality. Could you load up a playlist on Spotify and enjoy while browsing holiday shopping? Sure, but there’s not room for big speakers so there’s no low end leaving me listening to pretty thin audio. That said, it doesn’t sound harsh, and the laptop does support DTS:X and spatial audio to external devices.

![Acer Swift Spin 14 AI running the game Kathy Rain 2 unplugged.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/YS7nhJtfnoTBfdQDzKeyXe-1200-80.jpg)

AI is a central part of this laptop if the name didn’t give it away. The Qualcomm CPU comes with 80 TOPS NPU so it can do quite a bit locally (a lot of the laptops I see have around 50). Of course, Copilot is a press away and has hands-free capabilities. It’s not quite as conversational as Google Gemini, but it’s close enough.

There are a number of other Acer-specific apps such as LiveArt for making personalized stickers and Purified View that give even deeper fine-tuning to what you look like during a video call beyond the usual Windows 11 settings including making you look like you came out of an animated Richard Linklater movie.

Of course, you need to download the Acer Quick Panel, which I would have assumed should be preloaded on the laptop, to get access to Purified View. Acer Purified Voice, which can reduce background noise and boost your voice, also need to be downloaded. You can also download a smart phone app to use your phone’s camera called Acer Phone Webcam.

AcerSense is an app that does come preinstalled that functions as the proprietary utility software. But I appreciate it because, beyond changing power and fan settings, I can do wellness checks on the hard drive, RAM, and battery (or all of them at once).

* **Performance score:** 4.5 / 5

### Acer Swift Spin 14 AI review: Battery life

* **Very impressive battery life**
* **Charges quickly if not quite fast charging**

The battery life on laptops are getting pretty outrageous (in a good way). We typically see brands like Apple leading the way with models like the MacBook Air 13 (M5), which itself lasts 15 and a half hours. It used to be impressive when you could get eight, just enough to last a full work or school day.

With that in mind, the Acer Swift Spin 14 AI is just plain impressive. In our benchmark, it lasted 16 hours and almost 12 minutes. Take that, Apple. During use, I found the battery life to hold well, so that I could use it and really do some intensive tasks and not worry about it dying on me. Of course, if you plan on gaming or doing some demanding editing, expect to get significantly lower battery life. But still getting eight hours while pushing the laptop is pretty good.

I didn’t notice any fast charging-specific capabilities, but the laptop does charge up relatively quickly at a percent per minute pace. Consider that if you charge the laptop from empty to 20%, you should be able to get an additional four hours of use.

## Buy it if…

**You want a bunch of AI features**  
Yes, all Windows laptops these days come with Copilot. But, Acer has included a bunch of additional apps, some of which need to be downloaded (for free) after the fact that take advantage of the CPU’s NPU.

**You need a long battery life**  
The battery life on this laptop is about as good as it gets on a laptop, making it ideal for those that need a computer to last a whole work day and more without access to power.

**You want an attractive 2-in-1 laptop**  
This laptop looks good in its aluminum Cobalt Blue styling. Plus, its smooth hinge and touch screen makes 2-in-1 use easy.

## Don't buy it if…

**You’re on a budget**  
At a grand and a half, it’s not exactly cheap. If you’re on a budget, there are cheaper 2-in-1 options. You’ll just have to compromise on performance or AI features.

**You want a good sounding laptop**  
We really shouldn’t be expecting a lot out of a laptop’s sound quality (just get a good pair of earbuds or headphones), but if that’s important to you, there are (some) options out there that are better.
