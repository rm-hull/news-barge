A North East museum has won a national accessibility award for being one of the UK’s most welcoming family attractions.

Woodhorn Museum in Northumberland won the Best Accessible Museum prize at the 2026 Family Friendly Museum Awards, run by charity Kids in Museums.

The award recognises UK museums, galleries and heritage sites that go the extra mile for families.

Jo Raw, Northumberland venues manager for North East Museums, said: "We're absolutely delighted that Woodhorn Museum has been named Best Accessible Museum in the Family Friendly Museum Award 2026.

![People get award](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21579462.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The award *(Image: Supplied)*

"This recognition means so much because it reflects the experiences of the families who visit us and the dedication of our team, who work hard every day to ensure everyone feels welcome, supported and able to enjoy all that Woodhorn has to offer.

"We'd like to thank Kids in Museums, the family judges and everyone who has visited and supported us.

"Accessibility is at the heart of everything we do, and we're incredibly proud to receive this award."

The Family Friendly Museum Award was established in 2004 and is regarded as a leading honour in the heritage sector.

An expert panel reviews hundreds of public nominations each year, while families make undercover visits during the summer holidays to choose the winner.

Judges praised Woodhorn’s inclusive approach, which helps families of all backgrounds and abilities enjoy their visits with confidence.

They highlighted the museum’s proactive staff, clear signs and sensory-friendly facilities.

A family judge described the museum as a "wonderful place for kids of the North East to learn about their heritage in a fun and interactive way."

Visitors can enjoy hands-on activities, outdoor play and exhibitions inspired by Northumberland’s mining heritage.

Accessibility features include sensory spaces, quiet areas, Braille signs, audio descriptions and an accessible play area.

Alison Bowyer, executive director of Kids in Museums, said: "Congratulations to Woodhorn Museum for winning the Best Accessible Museum category.

"Our undercover judges were impressed with the accessibility of the museum from the moment they walked through the door, with a warm welcome from staff helping visitors feel relaxed.

"Accessibility has been built into the multi-sensory interactives, activities and facilities from the start and families really appreciated how that enabled them to have a stress-free, fun day out."

Councillor Wendy Pattison, Northumberland County Council’s cabinet member for arts and culture, said: "What a fantastic achievement for Woodhorn Museum, and all the staff who work there.

"Ensuring attractions are accessible to all in Northumberland is really important to us, and this award definitely shows the team at Woodhorn have gone above and beyond to ensure the area's rich heritage can be enjoyed by so many.

"Well done."
