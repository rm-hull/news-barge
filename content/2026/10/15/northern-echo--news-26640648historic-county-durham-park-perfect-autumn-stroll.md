A TikTok praising Hardwick Park as the "perfect afternoon walking spot" has put one of County Durham's best-loved attractions in the spotlight.

Just outside Sedgefield, the historic park has been drawing visitors for centuries.

Its picturesque lakeside scenery, woodland walks and fascinating architectural features are hard to resist.

Today, it remains one of the county's most popular spots for a weekend stroll.

At the heart of the park is a circular route that winds around the lake and Serpentine River, offering ever-changing views across the water, through mature woodland and towards the park's collection of restored follies and monuments.

The attraction is built around an 18th-century landscape designed for landowner John Burdon by architect James Paine.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21521204.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: This is Durham)*

Unlike the formal gardens that came before it, Hardwick was designed to appear natural, with lakes, woodland and ornamental structures carefully positioned to create a sense of discovery as visitors made their way around the estate.

More than 250 years later, many of those same features continue to catch the eye.

The Temple of Minerva, Gothic Ruin and Gothic Seat remain among the park's best-known landmarks, sitting alongside sweeping views across the lake and parkland.

Autumn is one of the best times to explore; leaves turn shades of gold and amber, the park's waters reflect the changing colours, while wildlife can often be spotted around the lakeshore and among the trees.

Hardwick is also popular with families, offering accessible routes, children's play areas and a visitor centre with a café overlooking the grounds.

Durham County Council describes it as a Visit England-accredited attraction and an 18th-century landscape park with free admission, excluding parking charges.

The grounds were extensively restored in the early 2000s after decades of decline, with a £4.1 million Heritage Lottery Fund-backed project helping to revive many of the historic features visitors can enjoy today.

So while a viral video may have introduced Hardwick Park to a new audience, generations of County Durham residents already know what makes it special.

For those looking for a leisurely weekend walk, few places in the county combine history, heritage and natural beauty quite so successfully.
