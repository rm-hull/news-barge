Last week, I asked whether Razer's "Perfect 180" feature on its new Basilisk V4 Pro gaming mouse counts as cheating. For the unaware, said feature allows a near-instant 180-degree spin to be mapped within the software to a single mouse button. In other words, one click, one insta-turn to face whatever (or whoever) is behind you.

316 of you responded to the poll (where were the rest, asleep?), and the results are... unsurprising. 79% of respondents said yes, while 21% said no, it's totally fine.

Or just "no." I'm adding a bit of flavour to that one. Looking through the comments, many seemed to echo the sentiments of TrexLaser, who says: "Anything that moves the mouse for you is cheating."

However, some took a more nuanced approach. Terito697174 says the real issue might be in the determination of cheaters, as overly snappy aiming is often the only way for another player to reasonably assess whether they're up against an opponent with an unfair advantage.

Salvador Pardiñas makes a good point: "I can sort of understand it as an accessibility option for people who can't normally do a 180 by themselves, but other than that it's just plain cheating."

![A screenshot of Razer Synapse showing its Perfect 180 gaming mouse feature](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Ujo3PEQou798FVRe7VgoMD-1200-80.jpg)

Certainly, those with differing abilities might find a 180 turn on a mouse button a very useful thing indeed. However, that doesn't seem to be the way Razer is currently pitching it, as the feature blurb on the Basilisk V4 Pro product page describes it as "built for low-sensitivity gaming," rather than as an accessibility option.

Commenter Fojacko brings up a real blast from the past: "Welcome to the 2000s, Razer. The Intellipoint software says hi."

I'd forgotten all about Microsoft's SideWinder gaming mice from back in the day, which, via the Intellipoint mouse software, could reassign a side button to perform 180-degree or full 360-degree turns. "Quick turn never took off back then for a reason," says Fojacko. Erm, I'm not quite sure why. Was it not as useful as we might think?

Anyway, the feelings of the majority have been made clear. I doubt Razer will rescind the feature regardless, but I will definitely be looking out for insta-turning players in multiplayer games from now on.

"I know what mouse you use," I'll think to myself. Also, "How on earth am I supposed to compete with that?"
