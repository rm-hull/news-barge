The 3,026-metre, four-lane **New Yalu River Bridge** links the Chinese border city of**Dandong** with**Sinuiju** in**North Korea**. Hundreds of people gathered in Dandong on Thursday to watch as** lorries**began** crossing in both directions**, some decorated with Chinese and North Korean flags and flowers.

Completed in **2014**, the bridge had stood** unused for 12 years**because connecting roads and customs facilities on the North Korean side were not finished. Its** official opening**marks a significant step in efforts to** revive trade**between the two countries after years of** strained relations**and pandemic-era** border restrictions**.

The crossing is expected to ease **freight transport** and reduce reliance on the ageing**Sino-Korean Friendship Bridge** nearby, which has carried much of the**cross-border traffic** since**1943**. Lim Eul-chul, a North Korea expert at Kyungnam University, estimated that the new crossing could** triple or quadruple** cargo traffic, particularly shipments of heavy machinery and construction materials.

**China** remains North Korea's**largest trading partner** and a**crucial economic ally**. Residents in Dandong hope the new link will bring more** business**and improve** living standards**across the border, although North Korea has yet to resume issuing** tourist visas** to Chinese visitors.
