East Durham College has been named an Approved Skills Delivery Partner for the 2026–27 academic year.

The recognition comes as part of a major new adult education scheme backed by £250 million in funding through the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority.

The North East Skills Programme aims to support adults in accessing education and training to help them find work, change careers, or gain new skills for future opportunities.

Jackie Lanagan, vice principal curriculum and partnerships at East Durham College, said: "We are delighted to be named as a recognised skills provider within the North East Strategic Authority, we can help ensure adults access high-quality training that meets the needs of employers and communities.

"This is about more than qualifications - it is about creating pathways into sustainable employment, supporting people to progress and retrain, and helping businesses access the skilled workforce they need to grow."

East Durham College’s new status builds on its track record in adult education and its focus on flexible, industry-driven courses designed in partnership with employers.

The college is currently redeveloping its Houghall Campus into a modern centre for land-based, environmental, and sustainability education, with the first intake of students expected in September 2027.

Scott Bullock, principal and CEO of East Durham College, said: "Skills are fundamental to the future prosperity of our region, and colleges have a vital role to play in making sure people have the opportunity to develop those skills at every stage of their lives.

"Being recognised as an Approved Skills Delivery Partner for 2026–2027 is an important milestone for East Durham College and reflects the strength of our adult education offer and our commitment to working in partnership to meet the needs of our communities and employers.

"We are proud to be contributing to the North East Skills Programme and to the wider ambition of creating a skilled workforce that can support the region’s future growth."

The college already offers a broad range of vocational and skills-based courses for adults, supporting career changes, personal development, and new business ventures.

Mr Bullock said: "Our strong track record of working closely with employers is central to the success of our education and training.

"We are bringing that same employer-led approach to our adult skills provision, ensuring the training we offer is closely aligned to real opportunities in the labour market."

Adults interested in retraining or gaining new skills can view the latest courses at edc.ac.uk.
