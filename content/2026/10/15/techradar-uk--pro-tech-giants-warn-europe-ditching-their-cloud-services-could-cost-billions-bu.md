* **CCIA report says European Commission's cost estimates are way off the mark**
* **Pro-European restrictions could add to operating costs, migration complexity and more**
* **Importantly, these figures come from a group that represents Amazon, Google and other US Big Tech**

Europe's efforts to tackle foreign tech vendor reliance could cost €115 billion over the next decade, a new report by the European Center for International Political Economy (ECIPE) claims.

The paper, commissioned by the Computer and Communications Industry Association (CCIA), specifically refers to the Cloud and AI Development Act (CADA).

However, the CCIA is a group that represents major American tech giants including Amazon, Google and Meta, who all have major commercial interested in Europe.

## Europe could incur serious costs by ditching US tech hyperscalers

The Commission had previously estimated an upper-end cost of €18 billion over ten years, but ECIPE's estimate of €115 billion is around 6.4x higher. The paper specifically argues that restrictions favoring European cloud providers could increase operating costs, force customers to migrate workloads and spur on substantial software redevelopment, all of which adding to the long-term costs of ditching US cloud.

The paper splits this cost into two sectors – a €33 billion cost for the public sector, which includes governments and other public agencies acting in the interest of citizens, and a further €80 billion in costs for the private sector, which covers critical national infrastructure like banking, healthcare, energy and transportation.

Depending on how intensely the EU goes in on tech sovereignty, the paper's €115 billion projection is a mid-level estimate. Higher-end scenarios top €233 billion.

The group is lobbying European policymakers to assess sovereignty based on actual risks, rather than just the nationality of tech vendors.
