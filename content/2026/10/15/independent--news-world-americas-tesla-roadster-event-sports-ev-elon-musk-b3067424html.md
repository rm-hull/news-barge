Tesla will show off its redesigned Roadster on Thursday, nearly nine years after the electric sports car was first previewed in next-generation form.

The event is scheduled to begin at 8:55 p.m. EDT (0055 GMT) at a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas.

A prototype of the $200,000 vehicle was revealed by Tesla in 2017, when the company said deliveries would start in 2020. Reservations for the car remain $50,000 each, and the price has not been updated.

Chief Executive Elon Musk has played down the Roadster's significance to Tesla's finances, describing it in 2023 as a "small potato situation" that would not "move the needle in a major way financially."

But the car still carries symbolic weight.

"Tesla does not currently have a halo car, or high-performance luxury vehicle designed to showcase an automaker's best engineering and technology. The Roadster will be Tesla's new halo car," Morningstar analyst Seth Goldstein said in a note last month.

Musk set expectations for the vehicle in 2017, saying a four-seat Roadster would travel 620 miles (1,000 km) on a single charge. In 2024, he posted on X that the car had been redesigned.

The new Roadster would accelerate from 0 to 60 miles per hour (100 km per hour) in less than a second. "And that is least interesting part," he wrote, adding it was a collaboration between Tesla and SpaceX, the rocket company Musk also leads.

Goldstein expects Tesla to sell around 2,000 Roadsters a year – a fraction of the 1.6 million vehicles Tesla sold last year.

The original Roadster, released in 2008 and on sale until 2012, was Tesla's first-ever EV and used a Lotus Elise chassis.

It helped change the world's view of what an electric vehicle could be.

The company has since been known for its Model Y and Model X SUVs and Model S and Model 3 sedans.

The reveal of the second-generation Roadster also comes amid a sharp shift in Tesla's focus to artificial intelligence-led self-driving tech and robotics, from manufacturing cars for human drivers.

This year, tesla stopped selling Model S and X vehicles to make space for production of its Optimus humanoid robots.

It also launched Cybercab – a two-seater robotaxi that comes without manual controls such as a steering wheel, pedals and mirrors.
