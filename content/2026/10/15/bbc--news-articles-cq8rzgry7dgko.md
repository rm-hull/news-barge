Everton player Thierno Barry says it took him three weeks to post a video that showed him breaking down in tears

**Head in hands, tears flowing.**

It's not a sight you often, if ever, see from a top-flight footballer.

Keeping calm under pressure comes with the job description, but there's a sense it's what's expected from fans and team-mates.

So when Everton striker Thierno Barry posted a video last month, external of him breaking down during a private training session, it sparked conversation around showing emotion in an industry where revealing any sort of vulnerability can be a risk.

BBC Newsbeat met the French footballer at the club's training ground to find out why he chose to show the raw moment - and how he's using it to drive forward.

"I was really scared about posting that.

"And I said: 'No, I just need to show myself'," says Barry of the video released on his YouTube channel last month.

"Last season I showed a version of me [that wasn't] me and [people] need to learn about me."

In the video, the 23-year-old is seen getting frustrated and upset about his performance.

He then leaves the private training session, where he begins to cry, as his team works to reassure him.

The next day, Barry is seen reflecting on how it felt positive to let go.

"Sometimes it feels good to just lose it," he says to the camera.

Barry reveals he sat on the video for three weeks before choosing to release it.

'I was scared to post' - Barry on viral crying clip

"It's the first time I cried in front of my friends, they always see me as a strong [person]," he says.

"I'm happy that I can show that moment. They know I care about the club and I care about playing good on the pitch."

A Premier League move from Villarreal for £27m at the age of 22 was always going to be a jump for the Lyon-born player - and fans expect a lot from new signings.

Everton ended up re-posting the clip from his vlog on their own feed, external, with the caption "emotional resilience".

Comments streamed in underneath urging the player to keep his head up.

He acknowledges the support he has received from fans and his club.

"The people around me helped a lot because, like I said, I'm really hard with myself and the people with me try to help me to be more positive."

And for Barry, any disapproval wouldn't reach the levels of his own inner-critic.

"I'm always harder with myself, and I just need to be sometimes more calm," he says.

"Like, you're a crying guy, and I just want to show that I'm strong to show that, because I'm not scared to show that.

"That's why I posted this video."

He also spoke about the early days of adjusting to a new club and home, something he describes as "really difficult".

"It's hard because it's the hardest league in the world. New league, a new language."

'It's difficult for my family' - Barry talks about impact of online criticism

Last season he made 41 appearances and scored just eight goals.

This season he's already scored six.

"People can say I miss a lot of chances, but I also create a lot of chances," he says in his defence.

But he also knows the cost of not capitalising on chances.

"Every fan counts on you to score goals, so imagine you didn't score, you know, for three months like me [last season] it's difficult."

And he won't fully relax.

After Barry scored against Hull last weekend, he posted on his Instagram that he was "still learning".

"This season is more easy to switch because I know I have time on the pitch," he says.

"And I think when I miss that chance, seven minutes later, I have another opportunity to score. That's a real pressure.

"When I come to the ball, in my head, it's like time stops."

Barry is also finding time to celebrate on the pitch, even rolling back the years by bringing back the dab and hip-swinging celebrations.

As for his second year with the club, the 6ft 5in player says he wants to keep focus.

Does he see a call up to the French team any time soon?

"I didn't think a lot about that because for me it's like really far, you know?

"I just do my job, if something happens, something happens."
