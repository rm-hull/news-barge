A North East artist whose striking creations have taken him from Taiwan to the United States has unveiled an exhibition inspired by something much closer to home – the woodland on his doorstep.

James Brunt, a land artist based in Kirkleatham, Redcar, began his latest project the day after moving to the village on December 2, 2025.

This new body of work, titled Home, will be on display at the Bothy Gallery in Kirkleatham Walled Garden from October 17, and is rooted in the surrounding landscape.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577860.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Land artist James Brunt unveils will showcase his woodland-inspired art at Kirkleatham Walled Garden *(Image: James Brunt)*

Mr Brunt said: "This is such an exciting opportunity for me, this is a body of work that nobody asked me to do, so the works are as close to my own creative practice as possible.

"This is what I do when left to my own devices, just me, the landscape around me and wildlife that I share the space with, deeply connected to the natural world."

His work, created in harmony with the seasons, explores the textures and materials of the woodland, often using fallen branches, leaves, and other natural elements to form temporary installations.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21577865.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Land artist James Brunt unveils will showcase his woodland-inspired art at Kirkleatham Walled Garden *(Image: James Brunt)*

Mr Brunt has previously completed commissions for ITV, HBO, and international events in Taiwan, the United States, and Spain.

He is also known for large-scale public pieces in Knowsley, Ashington, and Waddesdon Manor in Buckinghamshire.

Hazel Robinson, who works at Kirkleatham Walled Garden, said: "We are so proud to be able to host James’ exhibition ‘Home’ at the walled garden.

"Its a real celebration of work set in the heart of Kirkleatham.

"Its always a pleasure to come across one of his creations around the village and parkland, its going to be fascinating to see all of the images together."

Mr Brunt will be present in the gardens on the opening Saturday, creating new work and signing copies of his book Land Art, creating works in and with the landscape.

The exhibition will run until mid-December.
