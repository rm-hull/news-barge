Watch: Harry and Meghan at first joint engagement since UK return

**The Duchess of Sussex has made her first official public appearance since moving back to the UK,telling well-wishers "it's great to be back".**

Meghan arrived at a youth charity in Nottingham alongside her husband, the Duke of Sussex, on Thursday morning. The pair, who are also expected to visit a food bank later, hugged and posed for selfies with the public.

The duchess spoke to a number of people in the crowd, telling one "we got lucky" as they chatted about the weather.

She has shared glimpses of her life back in the UK on social media, but this is the first time she has attended any public events since the family returned from California.

![Meghan smiles broadly in the centre of the picture holding her flowers as Harry can be seen in the background out of focus but grinning](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/b032/live/3dfdeba0-c886-11f1-a2e8-f71844f8002e.jpg)

Meghan clutched some flowers and some friendship bracelets she was handed

During a bracelet-making workshop, Meghan disclosed her daughter Princess Lilibet had been enjoying a new hobby.

When one youngster told the duchess she liked doing gymnastics, Meghan replied: "Oh yeah. Our daughter is doing that too right now. She loves it."

Later, she gave the young girls some advice on building confidence.

"We have to remember to talk to ourselves like we talk to our best friends," she said. "It's really important to be just as kind to yourself."

A spokesperson for Harry and Meghan said Nottingham is "a special place" for them both, as it is where they made their first joint appearance after announcing their engagement in 2017.

The pair are attending these events not as working royals, but as public figures and supporters of the projects they're visiting.

![Meghan smiles at a young girl who is making a bracelet beside her as they sit at a table](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/1b2c/live/30b2b620-c885-11f1-a2e8-f71844f8002e.jpg)

The duchess joined in with a friendship bracelet making session in the centre

The Royal Household has made it clear they view them as private citizens.

Despite that, Thursday’s schedule has a distinctly royal feel to it.

The first stop on their visit to Nottingham is the Community Recording Studio, a charity which Prince Harry has been supporting since 2013.

Its CEO Trevor Rose told the BBC they only found out about the trip a few weeks ago, and could not believe it.

"It's exciting times. Completely random, phoned, mentioned a idea, said they'd like to come down," he said.

"I thought it was going to be something in the future, next thing I know it's two weeks later and we are here, so yeah it's a complete shock."

![Meghan and Harry are seen in chef's aprons preparing food on a table with an electric hob heating a frying pan.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/368c/live/42c8ae90-c890-11f1-b0a4-81e564f1cfaa.jpg)

The duke and duchess helped prepare food in the Himmah Hub on Thursday afternoon

Later in the day the pair visited Himmah Hub, a local charity tackling food poverty.

They watched cooking classes taking place and helped prepare food parcels, donning chef's aprons to help with the prep themselves.

During the visit, Meghan and Harry kissed, hugged and held hands with their Grenfell "ladies", who featured in a community cookbook championed by Meghan.

The women who cooked for the Grenfell families in the wake of the fatal tower block fire received regular private visits from the duchess, who later said that catching up with the community cooks was "really, really meaningful".

Their third engagement is at Epic Partners, a group working to support young people through youth work, sport and mentoring.

![The Duke of Sussex watches as the Duchess of Sussex arranges birthday cupcakes during a visit to Epic Partners in Nottingham.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2376/live/65ae1f40-c8b2-11f1-bdfb-e3788aeae9ec.jpg)

Meghan arranges birthday cupcakes during their visit to Epic Partners

The couple have a private security team with them in Nottingham and local police teams are understood to be aware of their visit.

Harry and Meghan's spokesperson told BBC News they were "really excited" to be back together in Nottingham, nine years since their last joint visit.

![Prince Harry puts his arm round Meghan's shoulder as they talk to fans.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2361/live/fdc82070-c8ab-11f1-9c75-6ff1271de385.jpg)

The Duke and Duchess beamed as they greeted the public in Nottingham on Thursday

"They are back here primarily because they support and believe in the organisations they are visiting.

"They enjoy coming back to revisit projects they are personally invested in, to meet the same people and understand what is happening and how things are changing."

In 2017, Harry and Meghan visited Nottingham's Lace Market before a visit to a World Aids Day charity fair.

Pictures from that trip showed the couple smiling as they met enthusiastic crowds.

The pair and their children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet, returned to the UK in August after living in the US for six years.

![The Duke sits relaxed in the hall alongside the Duchess who has her hands clasped together and is grinning in her seat](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/0b4e/live/eb3a37e0-c8ab-11f1-9c75-6ff1271de385.jpg)

The royal couple shared a laugh as they took their seats inside the centre

They have reportedly settled in the Cotswolds, and their children have moved schools after issues were raised by their security team about the school run.

It also emerged that the Sussexes are going to be given a review of their publicly funded security, following their return to live in the UK.

It is understood that the committee which oversees royal and VIP security, known as Ravec, is reconsidering Prince Harry and Meghan's level of police protection now that they and their two children are full-time residents rather than visitors.

The Sussexes will not be spending Christmas with the King at the traditional royal family gathering at Sandringham, Norfolk. BBC News understands that they are likely to spend it privately with Meghan's mother Doria Ragland.
