A remarkable archaeological operation on the River Wear has successfully brought a potentially centuries-old cauldron back to the surface.

These pictures capture the recovery at Offerton, near Sunderland, from divers entering the water in challenging conditions to the moment the vessel was finally lifted aboard.

Underwater archaeologist Gary Bankhead and his team had spent months planning the operation after the cauldron was first discovered last September.

High winds forced the recovery to be postponed by 24 hours.

Debris washed into the river by the wind and spring tide left divers working in visibility which was at times almost zero.

After locating the site, the team cleared silt and debris before carefully manoeuvring the three-legged cauldron into a recovery cradle.

The remarkably complete vessel retains its three legs and handle and is covered in heavy concretion after its long spell underwater.

Its precise age remains a mystery, with Iron Age, Roman and early medieval possibilities to be investigated.

The cauldron has now been taken to the University of York for conservation. which will be followed by further scientific research at the Department of Archaeology at Durham University.

Analysis could help reveal when it was made, how it was constructed, how it was used and even what it once contained.
