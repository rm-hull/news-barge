Climate change-linked droughts have affected four out of 10 UK adults in the past 12 months, according to a new survey.

The poll also revealed that over three-quarters of respondents felt affected by heatwaves, while approximately a quarter reported having experience of wildfires.

It comes after severe drought was declared in parts of England on Thursday, alongside calls for residents to "use water wisely".

Significant portions of England and Wales have been officially in drought since the summer due to long spells of hot, dry weather that brought punishing heatwaves and little or no rainfall.

Some 85 per cent of adults said they had been affected in at least one way by climate change in the past 12 months, according to a survey carried out by the Office for National Statistics (ONS).

The figure is up from 74 per cent in autumn 2025 and 66 per cent in autumn 2024.

The most commonly reported way in which people have been affected by climate change in the past year was heatwaves (78 per cent of respondents), followed by drought (40 per cent) and wildfires (26 per cent).

Other answers included floods (18 per cent), increased health issues (18 per cent) and reduced access to safe and affordable food (16 per cent).

The percentage of respondents saying they had been affected by heatwaves is highest among 16-29 year-olds, at 86 per cent, then decreases with successive age groups, falling to 81 per cent of 30-49 year-olds, 73 per cent of 50-69 year-olds and 71 per cent of people aged 70 and over.

The ONS surveyed 10,620 adults in Britain between July 1 and September 27.

Severe drought was declared in parts of Cornwall, Devon, Dorset, Somerset, East Sussex and West Sussex on Thursday.

Around 30 million people in England continue to have restrictions on their use of water, with the North East and North West being the only areas not in drought status.

Much of Wales remains in drought, the exception being areas in the south-west of the country, which are now classed as recovering from drought.

More than eight in 10 adults (86 per cent) took at least one action in the past month to cut back on the amount of water they use, the ONS survey suggests.

The most commonly reported actions were not leaving a tap running (65 per cent of respondents); waiting until the washing machine or dishwasher is full before switching them on (55 per cent); and using less tap water, including hosepipes (44 per cent).
