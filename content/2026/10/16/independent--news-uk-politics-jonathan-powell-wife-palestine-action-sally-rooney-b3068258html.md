Downing Street has distanced itself from remarks made by the wife of the Prime Minister’s national security adviser following accusations she supported the banned protest group Palestine Action.

Sarah Helm, who is married to Jonathan Powell, on Thursday wrote on X: "God help us authors who support Palestine and Palestine Action."

Under the ban introduced on 5 July 2025, membership or support of the direct action group is a criminal offence punishable by up to 14 years in prison.

The Campaign Against Antisemitism has called on the police to investigate Ms Helm, who is a journalist and author.

“This is gobsmacking,” a spokesperson said.

“Clearly, the police need to investigate and Sarah Helm must face the full force of the law, just like others who appear to express support for any proscribed terrorist organisation.

“But this raises extremely serious questions for Jonathan Powell and the Government.

“Spouses are entitled to independent views, but plainly Mr Powell, who leads on our country’s national security and has been intricately involved in our Middle East diplomacy, must publicly denounce this rhetoric if he is to continue in post.”

A Government spokesperson said: “These views are held by an individual who does not represent the Government in any capacity.”

Ms Helm was responding to a discussion about Normal People author Sally Rooney, who has said she can no longer sell her books in the UK because she supported the group.

![Sally Rooney books](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/15/17/Britain_Sally_Rooney_9848_.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Irish author previously said she would donate earnings from her books and BBC adaptations of novels to Palestine Action, which has a stated goal of “ending global participation in Israel’s genocidal and apartheid regime”.

As a result, Rooney’s publisher, Faber, has determined it cannot sell her novels in Britain.

Figures have shown a continued surge in terror arrests after the ban on supporting Palestine Action was introduced.

Police have made mass arrests at multiple demonstrations since, where protesters have typically held up placards, worn T-shirts, and donned badges declaring “I oppose genocide, I support Palestine Action”, leading to their arrests under anti-terrorism legislation.

There were 3,867 arrests for suspected terrorist activity in the year to June, of which 3,630 (94 per cent) were linked to supporting the group.

In the previous 12-month period, before the ban came into force, 234 terror arrests were made.

In August, the US government also announced sanctions against Palestine Action, designating it as a terror group.

The Metropolitan Police has been approached for comment.
