A centre for children with complex needs has officially opened.

The Social, Communication, Interaction and Learning (SCIL) Centre is the first of its kind in Bradford and will support children from across the district who have special educational needs and disabilities (SEND).

Based at Margaret McMillan Primary School, it was created by Bradford Council’s Specialist Teaching and Support Services (STaSS) team and offers assessment, intervention, and support for children at risk of permanent exclusion who may require more specialist intervention.

Councillor Sally Birch, executive member for children and families at Bradford Council, said: "Every child deserves the opportunity to thrive and achieve their potential.

"The SCIL Centre will provide specialist support for children with complex needs while helping schools access the expertise they need to support pupils more effectively.

"By working together, we can ensure children receive the right support at the right time and help prevent unnecessary exclusions from school.

"I have visited this provision myself to see first-hand the dedication and professionalism of the service and how it has already started to make a difference to the lives of these children and their families.

"It is helping children remain engaged in education and receive the support they need to succeed."

The centre also serves as a training hub where specialists can model best practices and share knowledge with education staff from schools across Bradford.

Since opening, the SCIL Centre has already helped reduce permanent exclusions and improve attendance and engagement among children with complex needs.

It also supports education professionals and SEND services in identifying appropriate long-term support, funding, and placements.

Developed with input from Bradford headteachers, the centre is based on a successful model already in use in Worcestershire.
