Pornography that depicts strangulation and incest will be banned from early November, the government has announced.

The content will be outlawed from November 6 after cross-party pressure about the negative impact on women and girls led ministers to announce the new laws earlier this year.

It means pornography which depicts strangulation or suffocation, sex between people who are related or pretending to be related, or when somebody is pretending to be a child under 16, will be illegal.

Justice minister Alex Davies-Jones said: “This government has a commitment to halve violence against women and girls in a decade. For many people, it is still easy to access harmful pornography online.

“From November 6, it will be illegal to possess or publish pornography showing strangulation or suffocation, sexual activity between certain relatives, or adults roleplaying as children under 16.

“This isn’t about criminalising harmless sexual preferences. But porn can shape what people think a healthy relationship looks like, especially young people.

“Content that normalises, or even glorifies, violence against women is dangerous and can blur the line with what is acceptable in real life.”

Conservative peer Baroness Gabby Bertin made the recommendations in her review into pornography regulation which reported in 2025.

She said the news came “not a moment too soon”, after previously saying strangulation in porn had become “totally normalised”.

Lady Bertin’s report found pornography which shows women being choked can have “devastating” real-world consequences.

![Conservative peer Baroness Gabby Bertin: ‘Not a moment too soon’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/06/28/18/Official_portrait_of_Baroness_Bertin_crop_2%2C_2025-copy.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It highlighted “disturbing” role-play, including incest and adults portraying children. Both will be banned.

A study by the Institute for Addressing Strangulation in 2024 found more than a third of 16 to 34-year-old adults had reported being strangled or choked at least once during consensual sex.

A survey by the same group last year found 43% of 16 to 17-year-olds, who are sexually active, said they had been subjected to it.

In her report, published in February 2025, the Conservative peer said there had been a “total absence of government scrutiny” of the pornography industry.

Lady Bertin previously said that strangulation had “gone into the bloodstream” of society and it should be “wiped out of the culture”.

Non-fatal strangulation is already an offence in its own right, but it is not illegal to show it online.

She said: “It is very good news these offences will come into force, and not a moment too soon.

“This change in law alongside better enforcement and stronger regulation will send the unequivocal signal not only to the companies that host and facilitate porn but also to wider society that extreme and violent content that encourages real-life harm is not acceptable.”

Ms Davies-Jones said: “Young people especially deserve better examples of what healthy relationships look like. Real intimacy is built on respect and trust, not abuse.

“If you’ve got this material, delete it. If you’re sharing it, stop.

“It’s not as harmless as you may think, and after 6 November it is criminal.”
