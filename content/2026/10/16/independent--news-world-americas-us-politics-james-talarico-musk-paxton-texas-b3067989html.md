Addressing supporters at a packed rally in Texas on Thursday, Democratic candidate James Talarico drew a biblical comparison between his U.S. Senate campaign and the story of David and Goliath. The candidate is confronting more than $158 million in outside spending backed by Republican leadership and Elon Musk, the world’s richest man.

Speaking under bright lights, Talarico observed that while David lacked Goliath’s physical strength, he possessed a slingshot.

"You are my slingshot," Talarico told the crowd, without explicitly naming Musk or the substantial funding directed against him. "We are going up against our own Goliaths."

![Should he win, Talarico would become the first Democrat to capture a statewide office in Texas in over 30 years](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/10/2299881859...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The metaphor aligns with Talarico's ongoing narrative, which frames American politics not as a struggle between right and left, but as a clash between the ultrawealthy and everyone else.

Should he win, Talarico would become the first Democrat to capture a statewide office in Texas in over 30 years. His campaign has already raised record sums in what has become one of the nation's most expensive and closely watched Senate contests.

The national perspective on Talarico's campaign has shifted in recent months. Initially, the state lawmaker was regarded as merely the latest Democratic contender expected to generate temporary optimism before losing on Election Day.

Now, he is viewed as a genuine contender against Republican candidate Ken Paxton. Talarico’s progress is partly attributed to voter hesitation regarding Paxton, who has faced persistent questions regarding corruption, including an impeachment by his own party in the Texas legislature, though he was later acquitted.

![Trump listens as Republican candidate for Senate Ken Paxton speaks at a campaign rally at the Freeman Coliseum in San Antonio, Texas, on October 7](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/08/20/33/GettyImages-2298501614.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While outside money supporting Paxton has saturated Texas broadcasts with advertisements, Talarico’s campaign has outpaced Paxton’s in direct fundraising from donors hoping the state will break from its solidly Republican tradition.

The contest in Texas will help determine control of the US Senate during President Donald Trump’s final two years in office, shaping whether the administration experiences clear legislative passage or faces persistent investigations and resistance.

Talarico, a seminarian and state representative, has carefully navigated a broad geographic path, building support across Democratic-leaning cities, border towns that shifted toward Republicans in the last election, and conservative rural areas.

He has distanced himself from former President Joe Biden’s administration and national Democratic leaders. Although progressive Sen. Bernie Sanders is campaigning in Texas this month, he will not share a stage with Talarico.

Talarico’s focus on affordability resonated strongly with the audience at the evening event, which attracted numerous younger voters. He argued that rising living costs stem from a system manipulated by billionaires and corrupt politicians.

![Talarico speaks during an event in Dallas, Thursday, Sept. 10](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/08/15/preview.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"They think that they can buy us, and that they can essentially be the kings of the country," said Kaitlyn Johnson, a 29-year-old bartender who attended the rally.

Johnson felt Talarico accurately expressed her long-standing frustration over mounting living expenses, noting that despite earning decent money, purchasing a home anytime soon remains out of reach.

Recognizing Talarico's capacity to connect with voters and Paxton's difficulties in a traditionally safe red state, Trump has made multiple visits to Texas to campaign for Paxton.

Paxton has received heavy financial support from Musk, who previously led Trump’s Department of Government Efficiency.

Filings released on Thursday showed Musk contributed $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund, which then transferred $158 million to Texas PAC, the main backer of Paxton’s effort. Musk also gave $10 million to Lone Star Liberty PAC, another committee supporting Paxton.

The rally in San Marcos, a small city south of Austin, followed a nine-day period earlier this month when Talarico was away from public view with the flu. His absence prompted unfounded online rumors from conservative influencers falsely claiming he was at a mental health center in Ohio, narratives that quickly disintegrated upon examination.
