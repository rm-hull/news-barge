A powerful synthetic opioid believed to be significantly stronger than fentanyl has been linked to its first confirmed overdose death in Los Angeles County, prompting an urgent warning from public health officials.

The drug, known as cychlorphine, can stop a person from breathing even in extremely small quantities and may be lurking undetected in illegal drugs, authorities warned Thursday.

Making the substance particularly dangerous, standard fentanyl testing strips cannot detect it, meaning people could unknowingly consume the opioid without realizing they have been exposed.

![An officer holds seized red Tapentadol tablets at the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) facility in Lagos, on February 19, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/15/2275524478...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Los Angeles County Department of Public Health confirmed that a fatal overdose in February marked the first verified detection of cychlorphine in the county's illicit drug supply.

The discovery has raised concerns about the potential spread of another highly potent opioid as communities across the United States continue to grapple with drug overdose deaths.

## Deadly drug can evade standard fentanyl tests

Cychlorphine, chemically known as N-propionitrile chlorphine, is a synthetic opioid capable of causing severe respiratory depression, a potentially fatal condition in which breathing becomes dangerously slow or stops altogether.

Health officials say the drug is substantially more potent than fentanyl, although they have not provided a precise comparison of its strength.

![Unlike fentanyl, cychlorphine cannot be identified using conventional fentanyl test strips](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/31/14/DEA_Unseized_Fentanyl__7895.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Unlike fentanyl, cychlorphine cannot be identified using conventional fentanyl test strips, creating an additional challenge for people attempting to determine whether illegal substances have been contaminated.

The drug can be mixed into other illicit substances without a person's knowledge, increasing the risk of an accidental overdose.

Combining cychlorphine with alcohol, prescription opioids, sedatives or other drugs can make an overdose even more dangerous.

The substance has been discovered in several forms, including powders, counterfeit prescription pills, liquids and rock-like crystals.

## Powerful opioid has already appeared in 19 states

Cychlorphine was first identified in the illicit US drug market in 2024 and has since been detected in 19 states, according to the Los Angeles County Department of Public Health.

Another death associated with the substance was recorded in Northern California in April.

![The Food and Drug Administration has never approved cychlorphine for medical treatment](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/15/2026-10-01T170420Z_478543324_RC2HUNAEAO4L_RTRMADP_3_HEALTH-USA-INFLUENZA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Although detections have been more common elsewhere in the country, Los Angeles health authorities say they are closely monitoring the local drug supply for evidence that cychlorphine is becoming more widespread.

In August, the Drug Enforcement Administration placed the substance in Schedule I, a category of controlled drugs with no currently accepted medical use and a high potential for abuse.

The Food and Drug Administration has never approved cychlorphine for medical treatment.

"The safest course of action is to avoid using illicit substances, given the possibility of the presence of cychlorphine," county health officials warned.

## Health officials warn multiple doses of overdose antidote may be needed

Despite the drug's extreme potency, health authorities stressed that naloxone, the medication used to reverse opioid overdoses, can also work against cychlorphine.

However, multiple doses may be necessary to restore breathing.

![A man looks at a box of Narcan given to him by members of Catholic Charaties in Baltimore, Maryland on June 10, 2025](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/15/2219315079...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Officials urged people who may encounter opioids to carry naloxone, commonly available as a nasal spray or injectable medication.

Anyone who suspects an overdose should immediately call 911, administer naloxone and follow emergency responders' instructions.

Naloxone is available through pharmacies, medical providers, public health centers and local Community Health Stations.

Because conventional fentanyl strips cannot detect cychlorphine, Los Angeles County residents can also access free drug checking specifically designed to identify the substance.

The service, available through **drugcheckingla.com**, can provide results in approximately 15 to 20 minutes.

People seeking treatment for substance use disorders can access resources through SUDHelpLA.org and RecoverLA.org or call the county's Substance Abuse Service Helpline at 800-854-7771.

Additional overdose prevention guidance is available through the Los Angeles County Department of Public Health and Poison Control at 800-222-1222.
