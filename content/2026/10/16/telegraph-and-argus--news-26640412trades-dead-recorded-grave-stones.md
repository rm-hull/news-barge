by Robin Longbottom

DURING the 18th and 19th century it was not uncommon for families to record the trade of the deceased on their grave stone.

One simply stated that he was a clockmaker, and another that he was a wool comb maker, whilst others displayed it pictorially.

Benjamin Muscroft, a gardener of Oakworth, had a spade, a garden fork and a rake displayed on his.

Whilst Samuel Tillotson of Silsden was a monumental mason and displayed his set square, dividers, hammer and chisels.
