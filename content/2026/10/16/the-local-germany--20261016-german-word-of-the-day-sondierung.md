Readers may have noticed headlines this week about parties in Berlin continuing talks on a possible state coalition government. The reports have focused on ongoing *Sondierungsgespräche* between the Left Party, the Social Democrats (SPD) and the Greens.

But what exactly is a *Sondierung*, and why does Germany seem to have so many of them?

**What does* Sondierung*mean?**

*Sondierung*(plural:* Sondierungen*) is a feminine noun –* die Sondierung*. In its broadest sense, it means a probing, sounding-out or exploratory investigation.

The word comes from the verb *sondieren*, meaning "to probe" or "to test the waters". German borrowed* sondieren*from the French verb* sonder*, which originally referred to measuring the depth of water with a sounding line.

The image is quite a useful one. Before jumping in, you first check how deep the water is.

In political reporting, the term *Sondierungsgespräche*(exploratory talks) refers to preliminary discussions between parties to determine whether they have enough common ground to justify entering formal coalition negotiations, known as* Koalitionsverhandlungen*.

Outside politics, the word also appears in technical fields. Engineers may carry out *Sondierungen* to assess ground conditions, doctors can*sondieren* a wound or cavity and construction projects often involve*Kampfmittelsondierungen* to search for unexploded wartime ordnance.

**Why do I need to know* Sondierung*?**

If you follow German news for more than about ten minutes after an election, you'll almost certainly hear someone use the word *Sondierung*.

Germany's proportional electoral system makes coalition governments the norm rather than the exception. Since parties rarely win outright majorities, potential governing partners first meet for *Sondierungsgespräche* to see whether cooperation is realistic.

Take the exploratory talks currently under way in Berlin between the state chapters of the Left Party, the SPD and the Greens. Despite the attention they are attracting, they aren’t yet formal coalition negotiations.

The parties are simply trying to establish whether enough areas of agreement exist to make the next step worthwhile.

The process has become such a fixture of German political life that the politically-minded may follow *Sondierungen* almost as closely as the election results themselves.
