We were all a little mystified by Epic's announcement last week that it had created a "vibe coded" launcher, mostly because the optics of the entire thing are… well, they're terrible. Just rocking out the gate with a statement that programming—the job you *do* for the product you*sell,* is hard, and that you've essentially half-arsed it instead? Baffling.

Joining in that air of confusion is former Valve writer Chet Faliszek, who worked on Half-Life 2, Team Fortress 2, the Left 4 Dead series, and Portal 2 before his departure in 2017. He spends about eight minutes ripping into the thing on his YouTube channel. And the whole thing about the yacht obsession, as quoted above?

That bit's true—here's Sweeney blaming the Steam Deck's price hike on the "component parts supply chain for megayachts." It's something of a fixation. As is, Faliszek points out, the immediate graphs comparing Epic's launch time to Steam's.

"Epic does have a problem—and it's a valid problem, right? Not only do you have to overcome Steam's first-mover advantage where they cemented their place in the marketplace, you have to think of what is their north star, and what is yours?" Faliszek talks about how Epic was originally a developer engine first—and they bolted on videogames to that and "made a mess".

"So what is Valve's north star in Steam?" asks Faliszek. "Gabe's talked about this a bunch, it's an MMO. It's that simple. Think about Steam, there's a whole bunch of places to express yourself inside of there, and you can do that by levelling up."

![I got a feeling you are going to love the new Epic Game Store - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/m5x0PSKano0/maxresdefault.jpg)

In contrast, Epic's store "always feels sterile to me—you can complain about the Steam comments all you want … but, what they also do is they give the player a place in that world. They give the player actual agency and import and power inside of that ecosystem." Yeah, that tracks. The Steam forum is basically a replication of the horrors (and hilarity) of Barrens Chat, just with a few less Chuck Norris jokes.

Appealing to vibe coders, then, is a bizarre move, since "they have nothing" for people who just want a platform where they can play videogames. "It's nothing about community … And this is your store that you're complaining that Valve is unfairly competing against!

"When you fumble your announcement this badly, and you fumble what this is this badly, and you're gonna blame it on Gabe, like this is Gabe's fault? That you decided to make a jab at him and talk about vibe-coding and say that 'making a store is hard!'

"It's just bizarre to me, man. And I know a bunch of people I used to know there that were really smart about how to communicate and talk about this kind of thing, [they're] gone. And I think they're gone 'cause of little Timmy [whispering]: 'Make sure you make a jab at Gabe. Where should we put it, first paragraph? No. Second. Second, we'll be subtle. We'll be subtle. No-one will catch it."

To be honest, given how tumultuous the topic of AI has been in video gaming, it does smack as strange that Sweeney has gone so heavily in on it. Sentiment's not the same everywhere in the world, mind, but a good portion of gamers and developers both are a little exhausted by having an otherwise interesting bit of technology crammed into every single nook that'll fit it.
