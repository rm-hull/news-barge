The Princess of Wales has commissioned a study examining how the natural world can support the social and emotional development of babies and young children.

The research will be carried out by her Royal Foundation Centre for Early Childhood.

The centre is working with Prince William's Duchy of Cornwall estate on the project.

William is taking a keen interest in the initiative.

On Thursday, the prince and princess made a private visit to the duchy's Nansledan site, a sustainable urban extension of Newquay.

![Princess Kate has commissioned a study on the benefits of the natural world in the earliest years of life](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/12/11/preview.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The couple visited the area to learn more about the potential of nature-based learning during the earliest years of life.

Kate said she hopes the study will enable society to “build supportive environments” that make the natural world more “accessible” to babies and young children.

The princess has long championed the benefits of experiencing nature, especially during her cancer journey.

She wrote in a recent essay: “By spending time in nature or being creative, we can nurture the very skills and emotions that cannot be digitised: awareness, empathy, humility, and above all, love.”

In a video message recorded in Cornwall to mark the announcement, Kate said: "New scientific research increasingly shows just how important nature is for our psychological and physiological wellbeing.

"Nature is such an extraordinary environment for us all and, in this tech-driven, digital age, spending quality time outdoors has never been more important.

![Kate is championing nature as a place to nurture social and emotional skills in youngsters](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/16/13/d20be163430053ac3a130de381bc35b6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkyMjM4MjQw-2.86128440.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"This project is about exploring how nature, creativity and relationships can best support the healthy development of babies and young children.

"We believe that taking a whole-child approach is important, and nature becomes a wonderful place to explore how we can successfully nurture the social and emotional skills that underpin lifelong health and wellbeing.

“My hope is that through this practical, nature-based research, we can build supportive environments that help make nature more accessible, benefiting babies, young children and their families across the UK.”

The Princess of Wales has said time outdoors has never been more important in this “digital age”

The new research will explore how nature can support youngsters during their earliest years.
