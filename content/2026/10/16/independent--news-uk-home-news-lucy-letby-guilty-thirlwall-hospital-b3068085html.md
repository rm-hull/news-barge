A doctor who raised suspicions about Lucy Letby has said he should have gone to the police earlier about the baby murderer.

Dr Stephen Brearey, speaking for the first time since the publication of the Thirlwall Inquiry report into events at the Countess of Chester Hospital, said he was naive to believe hospital managers would act on his concerns.

He was the lead neonatal consultant at the hospital when Letby murdered seven babies and attempted to murder others.

Dr Brearey told the inquiry his warnings about Letby were repeatedly ignored.

He also said there is a need to change the culture around raising concerns in the NHS.

Speaking to BBC Radio 4’s Today programme, he said: “I was naively thinking that raising concerns with senior managers, they had the same values that I did and, once they got it, they would be thinking the same way.”

The hospital carried out a series of internal reviews into a rise in baby deaths in 2015 and 2016, but did not call in Cheshire Police until May 2017.

Dr Brearey said he and other consultants supported each other in raising the alarm over Letby, but “in retrospect, clearly, if we’d known what was ahead of us, we would have gone to the police earlier”.

He said there was no psychological support for staff at the time or when Letby was arrested, adding: “It really was the team looking after the team, and colleagues looking after colleagues.

“It’s a bit akin to a soldier in a battle with PTSD (post-traumatic stress disorder) being taken out of the battle zone to recover, and yet the staff on the neonatal unit were still coming back to work and being retraumatised.

“If they had to stabilise a sick baby, you can understand the triggers that might elicit, and the personal costs to staff who are parents dropping the kids off to school, and not wanting to talk to other parents.”

![Stephen Brearey](https://static.independent.co.uk/2023/08/22/15/Stephen%20Brearey.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Dr Brearey said Lady Thirwall had recommended “the uncontroversial duty of every manager to put patients first”.

He added: “It feels a bit of a sad indictment on the organisation when that has to be written down into the contracts of senior managers.

“It’s one thing writing into a contract… another thing actually enacting it into the entire organisation, and that takes a cultural change and cultural changes take time, and I very much hope that the NHS and the Department of Health can make that turnaround effectively.

“But it doesn’t feel like something that’s going to happen overnight to me, certainly, no.”

The Thirlwall Inquiry was launched in 2023 after the former nurse’s multiple convictions for murder and attempted murder of infants.

Publishing her final 822-page report in September, Lady Justice Thirlwall said: “There was a complete failure to protect babies on the neonatal unit at the Countess of Chester Hospital.

“This was because no-one seems to have understood that safeguarding action is required when a member of staff is suspected of causing deliberate harm and does not require colleagues to be sure of guilt.

“My report describes dysfunctional management and governance, a gulf between hospital leadership and clinicians, and failure to understand the fundamentals of safeguarding.”

Letby has always maintained her innocence. Appeals against her convictions have ​been rejected by the courts.
