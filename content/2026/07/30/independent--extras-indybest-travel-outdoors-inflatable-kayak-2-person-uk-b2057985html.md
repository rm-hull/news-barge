With the UK weather switching between breezy sunshine and sudden heatwaves, there’s plenty of reason to make the most of the outdoors. Stand-up paddleboards have become a common sight on beaches, lakes and rivers, while camping mats and travel backpacks are making it easier than ever to escape for spontaneous trips. But if you want to explore beyond the shoreline, the best inflatable kayaks could be the next piece of kit to add to your outdoor gear collection.

Inflatable kayaks have come a long way, offering the freedom of a traditional kayak without the cost, bulk or storage challenges. Modern designs can be packed down small, set up quickly and are surprisingly durable, making them suitable for everything from leisurely lake trips to gentle coastal exploring.

Living by the sea gave me the perfect setting to put these kayaks through their paces. Alongside a fellow IndyBest tester, I’ve rounded up the best inflatable kayaks for every budget and ability, from beginner-friendly options to more advanced designs.

Wave’s the roamer takes the top spot as the best inflatable kayak thanks to its durability, stability and value for money.

Despite inflating on pebbles, the military-grade PVC did what it needed to and felt like a robust car tyre, rather than a novelty rubber ring.

![Wave the roamer, best inflatable kayaks, IndyBest]([https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/21/14/47/Wave-the-roamer-best-inflatable-kayaks-IndyBest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/21/14/47/Wave-the-roamer-best-inflatable-kayaks-IndyBest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w)

Crucially, I felt really secure when out on the water. Compared with sharing a paddleboard, there was no wobbling or close calls despite sizable waves. The paddles come fitted with drip rings, which are great if you’re trying to stay dry. The storage area at the nose of the boat is covered rather than airtight, so you will need a dry bag and a clip to secure it in case you capsize. But it is handy being able to store belongings there.

One slight gripe is that the instructions were limited, so much so that my partner and I initially set up the seats and footrests in the wrong direction. It is also a little unclear what the various clips and attachments are for. While it’s easy enough to muddle through the setup, we nearly headed out on the water with our fins sending us backwards.

Getting the right PSI (air pressure) can be a real test of willpower with any inflatable kayak, likely leaving your biceps burning. But thankfully, the included hand pump is good quality, so you don’t feel like you’re losing precious air between bursts.

Once inflated, placing the seats inside is simple thanks to the Velcro-lined base, carabiner clips and adjustable seat straps (so you can personalise the recline of your backrest).

With its 93cm width, the roamer isn’t a speed kayak, but it’s a joy to take out and balances affordability and durability brilliantly.

It took me 10 minutes to inflate the two-person challenger with the provided hand pump, and the seats are easy to attach with Velcro fixings to the floor, making them stable and comfortable when the boat is out on the water. Construction was good but the heavy-duty vinyl is probably better suited to lakes and rivers rather than the changeable conditions that are inevitable when heading out to sea.

A large front-facing cargo net provides good storage, and you could easily fit a couple of backpacking tents in there if you were feeling particularly adventurous, or just a day pack and some hikers if you weren’t straying too far. Easily carried to the water, the boat was fast but needed some concentrated paddling to track true in the line that we wanted, so a more experienced pair of hands on the paddle in the back seat might be required.

The Wave kayak above was my favourite overall once you took into consideration the quality of the materials, the items included and its performance on the water. By comparison, this Decathlon model was my favourite for its ease of use and comfort, given that it gave my partner and I oodles of room to stretch out when used as a two-person kayak (all three seats can be unstrapped and removed).

I loved how the seats and kayak base are fixed in position upon unzipping the carry case, meaning you simply need to inflate the three chambers and the seats (this latter task takes seconds), slide in the fins and you’re ready to hit the water. Even better, the triboard model has a softer shell than those of Wave and Aqua Spirit, meaning you don’t need to break a sweat trying to reach a sky high PSI of 15, but rather five for the base and three for the sides – easy.

![best inflatable kayaks indybest]([https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/07/10/16/best-inflatable-kayaks-indybest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/07/10/16/best-inflatable-kayaks-indybest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w)

It wasn’t lost on me how the pre-attached seats disguise the increased difficulty of removing them. Where many of the models I tested opted for simple carabiner attachments to clip on and off the seats, Decathlon has opted for tri-glide adjusters. This isn’t a deal breaker by any means, but when you’re hosing down the hull in a bid to remove sand from all the nooks and crannies, it admittedly adds a few extra minutes of labour.

My main bugbear with Decathlon’s kayak was the scant selection of accessories. The kayak is currently reduced to £399.99, but it’s still more expensive than competitor models. Granted, it can accommodate three people, but the omission of paddles, a pump and an ankle leash from the included items didn’t impress me. If you were to purchase these extras from Decathlon, you’ll be on track to spend an additional £141.95, which then puts the price difference between Wave and this model at a whopping £241.95, provided it’s still on offer.

Thankfully, the Decathlon triboard was a delight to take out on the water and performed more like a one or two person kayak despite being a bigger unit. Thanks to its enormous 410cm length, I was able to stretch out and sunbathe (keeping my wits about me, of course) on a solo paddle at sea and, when it was time to return to the shore, turning the hull left and right with a single paddle was no bother.

For parents with little ones hoping to get the most out of family and solo sea excursions, you couldn’t go far wrong here – if it’s within budget, that is.

Aqua Spirit’s barracuda can be used as a kayak and a stand-up paddleboard. As someone who lives in a small flat, this versatility was appreciated.

While it doesn’t offer quite as much security as the raised sides of a traditional kayak, once you’re in the seat, you won’t feel at risk of falling off.

![Aqua Spirit barracuda, best inflatable kayaks, IndyBest]([https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/21/14/54/Aqua-Spirit-barracuda-best-inflatable-kayaks-IndyBest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/21/14/54/Aqua-Spirit-barracuda-best-inflatable-kayaks-IndyBest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w)

In its paddleboard iteration (the seat clips off), it’s more unwieldy than most beginner boards I’ve tried. Admittedly, Aqua Spirit hasn’t positioned the barracuda as a beginner model; however, sitting at 30in wide (most pro boards are less than 25in), it skews more toward the novice end of the size spectrum. With bent knees, my core engaged and a bit of determination, I was able to find my balance relatively quickly, but I’d recommend taking the board out past the choppy white water before attempting to stand.

You can’t beat the peacefulness of a sunbathing session out at sea, but the task of getting your board past the crashing waves can be tricky – especially if there’s a current. This is the beauty of the barracuda’s two-in-one functionality. The included paddle can be set up with a single or double blade, with the latter getting you where you want to go in half the time. With my kayak seat in situ, I thoroughly enjoyed power paddling (double-bladed) out to the flat calm.

Setup is simple and so are the included instructions, which cover everything from recommended PSI to fin installation. It wasn’t clear which clips the seat fixings secure to, so this took a little trial and error, but the sheer number of accessories made up for it. A changing mat, waterproof phone case, GoPro mount and more are included, so you certainly get your money’s worth. Compared with a two or three-person kayak, the value per person is arguably less here, though, with the seat removed, my partner and I were able to tag-team the board… with a few falls along the way.

This three-person kayak’s broad profile made it feel ultra-stable, even in quite choppy sea conditions, making it a really good confidence-building boat for anyone who’s new to kayaking inflatables. There was minimal flooding or air loss, even after hours of paddling and the kayak was very comfortable offering up good back support from the seats if you’ve got a few hours of paddling ahead of you.

The heavy-duty liner used in construction meant that the boat was very durable and coped well with close encounters with unseen rocks and riverbanks. The kayak is easy to portage with front, side and rear handles and ingeniously packs away into itself so you can carry it as a backpack or a holdall.
