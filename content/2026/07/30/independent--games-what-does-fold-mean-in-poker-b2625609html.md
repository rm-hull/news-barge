And one such key term that is a vital part of each hand is the ‘fold’, or ‘folding’.

Folding is the term used for when a player decides to give up their hand and withdraw from the round, thereby forfeiting their chance of winning the pot – but saving their own remaining chips in the process.

Below, we have provided an in-depth guide, answering the question of what folding means in poker. We’ve also examined how to fold in poker, when to fold in poker and how to use the fold as part of a winning strategy.

**What does fold mean in poker?**

So, what is folding in poker exactly? And what does it mean to fold in poker?

In simple terms, a fold is when a player decides to give up their hand and withdraw from the current round.

When you fold, you forfeit any chance of winning the pot, but you also avoid having to commit more chips.

**How to fold in poker: when and why to fold**

Players fold by pushing their cards face-down toward the dealer and verbally declaring “fold.” They will place their cards into the pile of cards that is no longer in play – this is sometimes referred to as the ‘muck’. Folding is often done when a player believes their hand is too weak to compete against others, based on the community cards and opponents’ betting actions.

But why do players fold in poker?

Essentially, folding is a defensive move, as it protects players from losing chips. Although you have no chance of winning the pot once you fold, it is likely that you would never have won anyway if your hand is that weak.

**Common scenarios for folding in poker**

There are several key situations where folding is the best option during a poker game. It is vital to learn when to fold in poker, so we have discussed the main situations below.

- **Weak starting hands:** In games like Texas Hold’em, hands like 7-2 offsuit or 9-4 are considered weak and often not worth playing, so folding pre-flop is a smart choice in order to preserve your remaining chips. Be sure to memorise the relevant poker hands to fold before playing.
- **After a big bet:** If an opponent makes a large bet and the player has a marginal or weak hand, folding can prevent unnecessary losses.
- **When you’re beat:** When the community cards suggest your opponent has a much stronger hand (for example, if you hold top pair but the board shows a potential flush or straight), then folding is often the best option.
- **Bluffing gone wrong:** If you’ve been bluffing and your opponent calls or raises aggressively, folding can be a way to cut your losses and move on to the next hand.

**When not to fold**

Folding too often can be a mistake, and can lead to what’s known as ‘fold remorse’. It’s important to stay aggressive in some situations, particularly if:

- **You have a strong hand:** Players sometimes misread their own hand strength and fold when they actually have a decent chance of winning. Make sure you are prepared by knowing what constitutes a strong hand in certain situations.
- **Your opponent is bluffing:** If you suspect your opponent is bluffing, folding might not be the best option. Think carefully and avoid folding too quickly without considering the full situation.

While folding is a smart move in many cases, players should not fold simply to avoid risk all the time, as a certain level of risk-taking can be beneficial in poker.

**Fold vs check**

Folding is a similar process to checking, though it helps to understand the important distinction.

In short, folding means withdrawing from the hand entirely, while checking means passing the action to the next player without betting (this can only be done if nobody else at the table has placed a bet).

Essentially, think of it like this: folding ends your hand, whereas checking allows you to stay in the game without committing more chips.

**Folding as part of a winning strategy**

Though folding may seem like admitting defeat, it is an essential part of any solid poker strategy, as it helps to balance risk and your potential gain.

The ability to fold and save chips for future hands is a worthwhile tactic, and experienced players fold frequently to avoid risky situations and conserve chips for hands that offer better chances of winning.

**How to know when to fold**

There are a few specific tips that will help players to recognise when it’s the right time to fold – we have provided some detail below.

**Understand hand strength:** Get familiar with which starting hands are strong and which are weak. In Texas Hold’em, hands like Ace-King, Ace-Queen and pairs are worth playing, while weaker combinations often require a fold. 

In short, unless your cards include a pair or a suited ace, you should fold a hand with a card of six or less. If you have sevens, eights, or nines, unless you have a pair, a suited ace or consecutive suited cards, you would also consider the fold.

**Read the board:** Pay attention to the table cards and how they interact with your hand. If the board is unfavourable (for example, an opponent could have a straight or flush), then folding is often the safest move.

**Watch your opponents:** Observe betting patterns. If a player who is usually passive starts putting down serious chips, it might be time to fold.

**Folding in different poker variants**

Folding plays a role in different poker games – we’ve explained the differences in the three main games below.

**Texas Hold’em:** Folding in Texas Hold’em is fairly common due to the variety of hands that are dealt. Players often fold weak starting hands to avoid getting trapped and losing chips. 

**Omaha:** Since players have more cards to choose from, folding can be more complex in Omaha, but it’s still important to know when to let go of weak hands. 

**Seven-card stud:** Folding happens less frequently in seven-card stud, but knowing when to fold against strong visible cards is still critical. There are various situations when you would fold – for example, if two or more of your suit were to be out and you didn’t have at least a fully live king-high among your first three cards.
