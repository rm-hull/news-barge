Researchers from Texas A&M University at Galveston is studying the ecology and conservation needs of the smooth hammerhead shark (Texas A&M University at Galveston)

Scientists have recorded smooth hammerhead sharks in the Galapagos Islands for the first time in over two decades, including an aggregation of juveniles.

The discovery sheds new light on the ecology and movements of this elusive species, which was previously unrecorded in the archipelago for a long period.

Researchers confirmed the species' identity through morphological and genetic analysis, identifying the Galapagos waters as an important nursery area.

Ongoing research uses advanced tools like underwater video systems, genetic analysis, and dietary studies to track their movements and understand their behaviour.

The aim is to better understand their nursery areas, migration routes, and population connectivity to inform conservation efforts, as the species is threatened by overfishing.
