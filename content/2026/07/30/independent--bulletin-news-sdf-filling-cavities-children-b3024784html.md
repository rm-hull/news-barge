Scientists discover a way to fix children’s tooth decay without drilling

University of Michigan researchers say that SDF is safe for children as young as one year old (Getty/iStock)

Silver diamine fluoride (SDF), a dark liquid, offers a drill-free and rapid treatment for tooth decay, potentially transforming dental care.

University of Michigan researchers led a trial involving 830 children, showing that a 38 per cent concentration of SDF successfully halted decay in over half of affected baby teeth.

The researchers said that the treatment, which is painted onto the cavity using a sponge-tipped applicator, is effective and safe for children as young as one, and could act as a temporary solution for adults awaiting further dental procedures.

SDF has been used internationally for decades and off-label in the US since 2014, with the recent trial data now supporting its regulatory approval.

Although affordable, typically costing between US$20 and $75 per application, the primary disadvantage is that SDF permanently darkens the decayed portion of the tooth.
