The opening night performance of *Cleansed***at London’s Almeida Theatre saw Sarah Kane’s provocative play live up to its reputation – with one audience member fainting during a graphic scene.

First staged in 1998, *Cleansed* is one of five plays by the late playwright Kane, who died by suicide in 1999 at the age of 28. It is noteworthy for its visceral violence, prolonged nudity, and dark themes.

Previous productions of the play have drawn similar responses from some theatregoers, with the 2016 version at the National Theatre making headlines thanks to a spate of faintings and walkouts among the crowd.

During Wednesday’s (29 July) press night performance of the play, directed for the Almeida by Rebecca Frecknall, an audience member was seen receiving assistance from staff before being helped from the auditorium.

![Stuart Thompson in 'Cleansed' at the Almeida](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/07/30/9/36/Almeida-Theatre_Cleansed_Stuart-Thompson_Credit--Marc-Brenner-(1).jpeg)

In a statement, a spokesperson for the Almeida told *The Independent*: “One audience member required assistance during last night's performance of* Cleansed.* Our Front of House team were on hand to provide care and the audience member was quick to recover.

“We will continue to warn audiences of the content in the production on the Almeida website – __here__, on the booking page, in pre-visit emails, and on Front of House signage in the theatre.”

The content warning for *Cleansed* reads: “This production includes prolonged nudity, torture, mutilation, murder, sex, incest, violence including sexual violence, forced eating, suicide including hanging, drug abuse and drugging, gender reassignment surgery as well as homophobic and misogynistic language, needles and blood.”

*Cleansed* is ostensibly set in a university campus, and follows a sadistic ringleader (played here by Leo Bill) who tortures and abuses a disparate group of outsiders.

Speaking to *The Independent* earlier this month, Frecknall and Bill were asked about the play’s propensity for causing audience members to faint.

![Lizzie Connolly and Leo Bill in 'Cleansed'](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/07/24/13/45/Almeida-Theatre_Cleansed_Lizzie-Connolly-and-Leo-Bill_Credit--Marc-Brenner.jpeg)

“It’s certainly not the aim,” Frecknall said, “but I don’t think it was [director Katie Mitchell’s] aim when she did it at the National. People have physical reactions to all types of works; it’s just some get reported more than others.”

Bill said he had had “quite a lot” of friends and acquaintances tell him they were “scared” to see the play due to its content.

“It’s like, ‘Well, we could all go and watch a Noël Coward play,’” he said, “And that’s fine – but what world are we in, where we are afraid to be challenged? It all becomes about that, about, like, ‘oh, some hands get chopped off.’ Whereas actually, it’s so beautiful.

“And the beauty in [*Cleansed*] is so much more important than whether somebody faints or not.”

*Cleansed* is at the Almeida Theatre until 29 August.
