'Most likely' it was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile that crashed in Poland, defence minister says

Poland’s defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz confirms it’s “most likely” it was a Kh-101 air-launched cruise missile.

“That’s the most probable hypothesis,” he says.

He says Poland is in regular contact with Nato allies on the incident.

Maj Gen Ireneusz Nowak, the Inspector of the Polish Air Force, joins in to offer a bit more detailed briefing.

He says that Russia fired 20-22 Kh-101 missiles inside Ukraine last night as part of its strikes on the country, and two missiles were identified as posing potential threat to Poland’s territory.

An F-16 fighter jet was scrambled to follow one of the missiles and observed the moment of its crash in an uninhabited area.

'No reason to think Poland was the intended target for this missile,' PM Tusk says

Poland’s PM Tusk is now visiting the site of the incident, and confirms that two missiles violated Polish airspace during the night, but clarifies that the other one quickly crossed back into Ukraine.

He confirms that early indications suggest the missile that crashed in rural Poland was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, as reported earlier.

But he stresses “there areno reasons to think Poland was the intended target for this missile.”

He also says that the Polish air force was ready to shoot down the missile if it threatened an inhabited area.

He says the US is interested in joining the investigation into the incident.

Ukraine requests to join Polish investigation into missile crash, prosecutor says

Ukraine has requested to join the Polish investigation into the missile incident, Lublin prosecutor Grzegorz Trusiewicz told a media briefing.

“This request is currently being considered, and it is possible that they will come here and work with Polish authorities to identify the object that entered Polish airspace and determine what exactly we are dealing with.”

The crash site is being “mapped” and will be examined in detail, he says.

'Looks like it was a Russian missile,' Poland's PM Tusk confirms

Poland’s PM Tusk is speaking now, chairing an emergency meeting on the incident with senior representatives of the military and the emergency services.

He confirms “a missile” fell on the Polish territory, and says “it looks like” it was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, as reported by Ukraine (9:19).

“Let’s not jump to conclusions before the detailed investigation is complete; we’ll want to be 100 percent certain about what kind of projectile was fired.”

He calls the incident “serious,” but confirms there are no casualties or damage.

He says Poland was prepared to shoot down the rocket if it continued its flight into the Polish territory.

'Russia's aggression is a threat to the security of Europe as a whole,' EU's Costa says

European Council president António Costa stressed that “Russia’s aggression is a threat to the security of Europe as a whole,” as he condemned the overnight strikes on Ukraine and an airspace violation in Poland.

“Last night, Russia launched a massive combined strike against Ukraine.

During the attack, Polish airspace was also violated, underlining once again that Russia’s aggression is a threat to the security of Europe as a whole.

My thoughts are with the victims of these attacks. The European Union stands in full solidarity with the Ukrainian and Polish authorities working tirelessly to protect and defend their citizens.

We are stepping up our support to strengthen Ukraine’s air defence, including anti-missile and anti-drone capabilities. Europe will continue to stand united in the face of this aggression.”

A Russian cruise missile crossed into Poland, Ukraine's foreign minister says

Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said “a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile crossed into Poland as part of Russia’s massive strike against Ukraine, violating Nato airspace.”

Polish authorities have yet to formally identify the object that exploded in eastern Poland.

“This is yet another clear demonstration that strengthening Ukraine’s air defence now is urgent and serves as a guarantee for the protection of the entire Euro-Atlantic community,” Sybiha said.

Referencing recent bitter disputes between Poland and Ukraine over shared history, Sybiha added:

“The Russian missile incursion into Poland also serves as a reminder that there is nothing more urgent for our two countries than countering our common, ages-old enemy, which poses a direct threat to both of our nations.

All other disagreements must be put aside during this brutal war. Our top priority must be our shared security.”

Morning opening: At least eight killed in Russian attacks on Ukraine as Poland investigates airspace violation

Jakub Krupa

At least eight people were killed and dozens injured in another night of heavy Russian attacks on Ukraine, with neighbouring Poland also investigating a reported airspace violation and explosion on its territory.

Russia launched 74 missiles and 284 drones against targets in Ukraine, with casualties reported primarily in Dnipropetrovsk and Lviv regions.

“This Russian terror once again proves that protection against the Russian missile threat is the most important,” Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in response to the attacks, adding Ukraine faced a “critical shortage of air defence missiles from our partners”.

Local police said that officers investigating reports of a loud bang “discovered a crated and scattered debris from an unidentified object in a field located about 2 km from the nearest buildings.”

Separately, air raid sirens sounded in the eastern city of Lublin. The incident took place just hours after Zelenskyy met in Lublin with Poland’s prime minister Donald Tusk.
