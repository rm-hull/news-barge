# Robert Bush: Funeral director ‘ripped off’ vulnerable pensioners with fake funeral plans

## Disgraced funeral director Robert Bush “used lower prices” to lure customers before defrauding vulnerable pensioners of savings they could not afford to lose, a court heard.

Bush, 48, is to be sentenced for 67 offences spanning 12 years and affecting hundreds of victims at his business, Legacy Independent Funeral Directors in Hull.

Hull Crown Court has heard Bush defrauded 226 people by charging them for funeral plans he was not authorised to set up, and keeping the money, which amounted to more than half a million pounds over almost 12 years.

On the fourth day of his sentencing hearing on Thursday, Bush sat with his head lowered in the dock as victim personal statements were read by some of the people who had lost their money to his fraudulent funeral plans.

Anne Hillerby said Bush had charged her “around £5,000” for two funeral plans but offered to “knock off £100 for paying in cash”.

Her statement read: “We have since found out the plans are not in place and I have not received a refund due to paying in cash.

“Robert Bush has ripped off so many vulnerable people. I can’t believe he could do this to people.

“He has used lower prices to get the customers in then ripped them off.”

Several victims said they had other savings and the plans had been intended as a safety net to save their children from worrying about their funeral costs.

Jean Hunter said: “That money was a lot for me, and he knew this because he agreed for me to pay it as and when I could afford it.”

A statement from Alma Hunter said: “It is so unfair that I have lost such a substantial amount of money that I couldn’t afford to lose.

“Robert Bush preyed on innocent and vulnerable people, devastating lives and causing a huge amount of distress, not only to me but to my siblings that he also stole from in a despicable, and utterly shameful way.”

Carol Grantley said finding out his funeral plan was not valid gave her uncle “a real fear of dying”.

She said: “Patrick was not a wealthy man. He only took the plan out in the first place because he had come into some extra money from an insurance policy that had matured, and he thought he should cover the cost of his funeral with it before he spent it all.”

A court has heard he breached the trust placed in him by grieving families “on an almost industrial scale”, by keeping bodies for months after they should have been cremated, giving out the wrong ashes, taking money for funeral plans without making any of the arrangements, and stealing charity donations collected at services.

In April, Bush pleaded guilty to 30 counts of preventing a lawful and decent burial, and theft from the 12 charities.

He had already admitted 30 counts of fraud by false representation relating to bodies found at the site, and four “foetus allegations” of fraud, where he presented ashes to women falsely saying that they were “the remains of their unborn”.

Bush also pleaded guilty to a further charge of fraud, covering the ashes of 57 people between 2017 and 2024, and one of fraudulent trading relating to funeral plans between 2012 and 2024.
