Heatwave-related deaths in the UK have almost doubled this year compared to last summer, new figures from the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) reveal.

A report estimates 2,877 heat-associated deaths occurred during the heatwaves in May and June alone.

This total includes an estimated 753 deaths linked to the May heatwave (24-27 May) and a further 2,124 during the June heatwave (21-28 June).

These figures are nearly twice the number reported for the entire summer period of June to August last year.

The UKHSA report warned: "While these figures remain interim, they are already approaching the annual record reported by UKHSA in 2022."

Last month was England’s hottest June on record, the Met Office has confirmed, with temperatures hitting 37.7C in Lingwood, Norfolk, on the 26th. This surpassed the 35.6C set in 1957 and equalled the 1976 record.

This year has marked the first time that temperatures have reached 35C in May, June, and July in the same year as scorching temperatures continue.

The heatwaves this year forced several hospitals to declare critical incidents – meaning they cannot provide normal safe services due to pressures on patient flow, bed capacity, or staffing – due to vast demand on services.

London Ambulance Service recorded the highest number of life-threatening emergencies in its history in June, with paramedics saying the rise had been “driven by extreme heat”.

It said 24 June was its fifth busiest day ever, with 7,900 calls and ambulance crews responding to nearly 3,600 patients in a single day.

The news comes as almost half of England’s population is experiencing official drought conditions following a period of low rainfall and back-to-back heatwaves.

Up to 28 million people live in the affected areas, namely East Anglia, Hertfordshire and the whole of London, Thames Valley, Hampshire and the Isle of Wight, Devon and Cornwall, the West Midlands, and Wessex – covering Dorset, Somerset, Wiltshire and Gloucestershire.

The Environment Agency has described the conditions as a “flash drought”, with July set to be England’s driest month on record.
