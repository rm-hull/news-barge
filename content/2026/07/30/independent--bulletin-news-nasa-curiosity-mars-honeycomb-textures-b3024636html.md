NASA’s Curiosity rover recently discovered an unprecedented number of honeycomb-like polygonal patterns on the surface of Mars, specifically in the Martian valley known as 'Valle Grande'.

While similar polygonal shapes have been observed before, this is the first time such a high concentration has been found in one location, prompting scientists to investigate their formation.

The polygons, measuring between 1.3 and 3 inches in diameter, wrap around a nearby hill nicknamed 'Miraflores', and their exact origin remains a mystery to researchers.

Previous observations of similar features were linked to cracks in ancient mud, suggesting past Earth-like wet-dry cycles on Mars, which could have been crucial for the molecular evolution leading to life.

Scientists are analysing data to determine how these features formed, with possibilities including ancient mud cracks or water being squeezed out of sediment, adding to Curiosity's ongoing discoveries about Mars's past habitability.
