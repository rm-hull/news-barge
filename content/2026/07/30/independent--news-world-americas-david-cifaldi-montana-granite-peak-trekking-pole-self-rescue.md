A Montana nurse impaled by his own trekking pole high on the state's tallest mountain stunned rescuers by refusing a helicopter evacuation and hiking 10 miles back to safety with the steel shaft still sticking out of his body.

David Cifaldi, 32, was climbing Granite Peak with two friends after crossing the notorious Froze-to-Death Plateau when he slipped and his 44-inch steel-tipped trekking pole drove beneath his left arm and out through his back.

Remarkably, the pole missed his lungs and other vital organs.

![This photo provided by David Cifaldi shows Cifaldi while he was being treated Monday, July 20, 2026, at St. Vincent Regional Hospital in Billings](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/30/05/Impaled_Hiker_72588.jpg)

Instead of calling for an airlift, Cifaldi — a wound care nurse at a Billings hospital — examined himself and concluded the injury was survivable.

"I was like, 'We've got to get off this mountain,'" Cifaldi said. While his friends questioned the plan, he remained confident. "I'm pretty confident this is outside my chest wall. 'Tis but a flesh wound."

The trio used a satellite Garmin inReach device to notify authorities they intended to make their own way down before setting off on a grueling six-hour trek across boulders, snowfields and rugged backcountry.

By the time they reached the trailhead, Cifaldi had hiked roughly 10 miles and descended nearly 5,000 feet with the pole still lodged in his body.

"The only reason it was possible at all was his confidence," friend Jesse Ross said. "He's the medical person. Our job was just to support."

Stillwater County Search and Rescue monitored the group's progress but did not intervene after Cifaldi declined assistance.

![Mystic Lake is seen from the Phantom Creek Trail in the Beartooth Mountains on Monday, July 20](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/30/05/Impaled_Hiker_94765.jpg)

"There was no danger to anybody else," Search and Rescue commander Ty Williams said. "He didn't want our help and we're not going to force it on him."

After reaching their vehicle, the hikers drove to a nearby clinic before Cifaldi was transferred to St. Vincent Regional Hospital in Billings — the same hospital where he works.

Doctors removed the trekking pole, treated his injuries and released him. Within days, Cifaldi was back at work.

![Impaled Hiker](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/30/05/Impaled_Hiker_8_375.jpg)

Looking back, he said he would make the same decision again.

"I think 99 times out of a hundred I would make the same decision," he said. He acknowledged the potential cost of a helicopter flight factored into his choice but said having two experienced friends and a way to contact rescuers gave him confidence to hike out on his own.
