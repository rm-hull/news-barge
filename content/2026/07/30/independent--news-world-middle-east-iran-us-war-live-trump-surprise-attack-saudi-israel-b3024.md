The US military has resumed strikes against Iran after Donald Trump promised Tehran would “get a beating”.

## Iran says talks with Oman over management of Hormuz continues

Iran has said that talks with Oman over management of the Strait of Hormuz is continuing after Muscat put forward a Gulf-backed proposal on “voluntary fees” for vessels transiting the vital shipping route.

Sources told Reuters that Yemen’s Houthis are also considering a similar proposal for the Red Sea.

## Trump’s Iran war is becoming a political disaster, new poll shows

A new AP-NORC poll suggests Americans are growing increasingly skeptical of the war with Iran, with about two-thirds saying the conflict has not been worth fighting and support for President Donald Trump's handling of the war slipping.

The survey, conducted July 23-27, found just 28% of U.S. adults approve of Trump's handling of Iran, down from 34% last month. Support also dipped among Republicans, with 61% approving, compared with 71% in June, though the poll's margin of error means the decline is modest.

The findings come after nearly two weeks of intensified fighting in which U.S. forces struck Iranian military and commercial targets and Iran retaliated against American facilities in the Middle East. Eighteen U.S. service members have been killed since the conflict began on Feb. 28.

## Fire breaks out on tanker after trying to use 'unsafe route' in Hormuz, says IRGC

Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps have said on Thursday that two tankers had attempted to use what it called an unsafe route in the Strait of Hormuz but turned back after a fire broke out on one of the vessels, according to a statement shared by state media.

## Saudi Arabia GDP falls in second quarter as oil activity contracts amid Middle East war

Saudi Arabia's real gross domestic product fell 4.8% in the second quarter compared to the same period a year earlier, preliminary government estimates showed on Thursday, as war in the Middle East impacts the world's top oil exporter.

The contraction was largely the result of a 24.7% drop in oil activity, showed data from the General Authority for Statistics (GASTAT). Non-oil and government activity ticked up 0.9% and 0.6% respectively.

The US attacked Iran on Wednesday, intensifying a five-month-old war that has disrupted shipping and energy markets and affected countries in the region beyond the main fronts.

Most Gulf economies will contract more sharply this year than expected three months ago before rebounding in 2027, a Reuters poll found this month.

GASTAT said oil activity in the quarter contributed negatively to real GDP growth by 5.4 percentage points, while non-oil activity contributed positively by 0.4 percentage point.
