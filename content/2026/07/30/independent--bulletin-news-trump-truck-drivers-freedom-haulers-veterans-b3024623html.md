The Trump administration has launched a new campaign encouraging military veterans to pursue careers as truck drivers, aiming to address a national driver shortage.

This initiative follows a crackdown by the Transportation Department that resulted in the revocation of approximately 56,000 commercial driver's licenses from immigrant drivers due to issues with English language proficiency and illegally issued permits.

The push for stricter enforcement of driver qualifications intensified after a fatal August crash in Florida involving an immigrant truck driver, leading to audits across states.

Veterans can leverage programs that allow those who drove large military vehicles to bypass the commercial driver's license skills test, or attend CDL training during their final six months of service, with GI Bill benefits covering tuition and potentially housing.

The trucking industry has expressed strong support for recruiting veterans, highlighting their valuable skills and discipline, and noting that truck drivers currently earn an average annual salary of $70,000.
