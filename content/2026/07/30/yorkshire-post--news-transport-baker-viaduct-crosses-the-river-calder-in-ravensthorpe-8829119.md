# Baker Viaduct crosses the River Calder in Ravensthorpe

## The newly built Baker Viaduct in Ravensthorpe now spans the River Calder after the penultimate pair of beams were installed on Wednesday (July 29).

The 350m long, 2,400-tonne viaduct now spans two waterways after crossing the historic Calder & Ribble Navigation Canal last year.

This forms a pivotal part of TRU work at the programme’s Ravensthorpe site, which is one of the biggest civil engineering sites in the country.

The viaduct will be connected to a newly built grade-separated flyover to accommodate new rail alignments with four tracks, that allow direct services to overtake stopping services and reduce journey times across the route.

To facilitate the connection of the railway between the Viaduct and the Flyover, approximately 130,000m ³ of material was placed for earthwork structures like embankments and retaining walls in 2025 alone, with a similar volume expected to be placed throughout 2026.

In addition to the track realignment works across this stretch of railway, TRU are also building a brand-new Ravensthorpe station 200m west of its original location, to allow passengers in the future to access services to both Leeds and Wakefield.

Adam Bunce, Senior Project Manager for TRU, said: “Baker Viaduct now spanning the River Calder is a significant milestone for TRU. This is an impressive feat of engineering and we’re really proud of everyone across our teams who have made it possible.

“When completed, Baker Viaduct will unlock faster, more reliable services for customers in the North. Work is also progressing well on the new Ravensthorpe station which, when reopened in 2028, will have a footbridge with lifts for step-free access, a new forecourt area and drop-off facilities, and new, extended island platforms so longer trains with more seats can stop there.

“Overhead line equipment (OLE) will also be installed throughout the area to power greener, electrified services.”

The TRU delivery team comprises of a number of contractor companies, including BAM Nuttall and Severfield, who played a key role in the work on Baker Viaduct.

Gareth Day, Projects Associate Director for Severfield, said: “Today’s lift is the penultimate and thirty first installation lift of the Baker Viaduct and follows over sixty offline assembly lifts since we started on site last September.

Installing the final drop-in span is a very proud achievement for the Severfield Bridge team. 3D modelling of the structure started back in 2024, so the final lifts on site this week mark nearly two years of meticulous planning and engineering to successfully fabricate, deliver and install the 2,400t viaduct.”
