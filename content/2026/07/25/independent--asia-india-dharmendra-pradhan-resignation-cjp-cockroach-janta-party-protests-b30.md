India’s education minister Dharmendra Pradhan has resigned after weeks of youth-led protests demanding that he take responsibility for an examination paper leak that affected millions of students.

The move marked the first major concession from prime minister Narendra Modi’s government after a movement led by the Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) resulted in weeks of nationwide demonstrations, sit-ins, and hunger strikes demanding Mr Pradhan’s resignation.

The CJP sprung up as a satirical platform, providing a rallying point for millions of young Indians frustrated over high unemployment and exam paper leaks and seeking greater government accountability.

Over the past week, tens of thousands gathered in the capital of Delhi demanding the resignation of Mr Pradhan over leaks of the medical entrance test paper that affected some two million students in May and have been linked to several student suicides.

The demonstrations have become one of the most visible signs of public dissent against Mr Modi’s government in recent years, with thousands returning to the streets even after police used tear gas and batons this week to disperse marchers trying to reach parliament.

The resignation handed a major victory to the protesters nationwide who demanded that Mr Pradhan take responsibility for the leak of National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET), India's undergraduate medical school entrance exam.

“This is democracy. He has resigned,” said Abhijeet Dipke, the founder of CJP, who started it as a mocking response to a pointed comment from India’s top judge, Justice Surya Kant, who likened India's unemployed youth in a speech to "cockroaches".

“We have two more demands. We won't go like this. Dharmendra Pradhan has resigned. But the families of the children who committed suicide should receive compensation of one crore rupees (£77,700). They should get it. One crore rupees compensation to all those families. And second, these police goons who acted on the 20th (July). Action must be taken against the police officers for that. Remember, do not mess with cockroach.”

Mr Pradhan’s resignation came even as police fired tear gas to scatter young protesters in the Indian capital, hours before their leaders were set to hold another round of talks with ministers to push for the move.

"Considering the situation that has arisen at Jantar Mantar and across the country, so that anti-national forces do not take advantage of this situation ... I have sent my resignation letter to the Prime Minister," Mr Pradhan wrote in his post.

"I deeply respect the aspirations, feelings, and legitimate expectations of the country's youth."

Cheers erupted among the thousands of protesters who have been rallying in central Delhi and elsewhere as the news spread.

The government has shut down the exit of 18 metro stations closer to the protest site and suspended the internet in the area, in a bid to contain it.

Anger had boiled over since police on Monday injured many students, launching baton charges, and firing tear gas to deter crowds of protesters marching on parliament.

The protests, led largely by young people and triggered by discontent over leaks of question papers for high-stakes entrance exams, reflect anger over job scarcity, corruption and government accountability.
