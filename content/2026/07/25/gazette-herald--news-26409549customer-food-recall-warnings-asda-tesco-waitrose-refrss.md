A series of food safety alerts has triggered multiple product recalls across major supermarkets, including Asda, Tesco and Waitrose.

The Food Standards Agency (FSA) has issued "do not eat" notices for potentially unsafe items due to potential health risks.

Customers are advised not to consume the products and to follow recall guidance, which may include returning the items for a refund or disposing of them safely.


## Tesco, Asda and Waitrose product recalls you should know about

### Waitrose recall

A popular bakery item sold at Waitrose is being recalled due to fears it may contain hard plastic and metal pieces.

The supermarket chain is recalling its eight-pack of Chocolate Chip Brioche Rolls.

Customers are being warned that the item could be "unsafe to eat," and if you have bought the affected product, you should package it up and send it back to your local Waitrose shop for a refund.

The FSA has warned shoppers "do not eat it."

The recall only applies to one batch of Chocolate Chip Brioche Rolls.

To check a pack of the brioche rolls, look out for the batch code B26177 and the best before date of July 26, 2026.

### Tesco and Asda recall

A popular dairy item sold at Tesco and Asda is being recalled due to the risk that veterinary medicines, including antibiotics (penicillin), could be found in it.

Graham's Family Dairy is recalling the Graham's Family Dairy Semi-Skimmed Milk of various sizes.

Customers are being warned that the items could be "unsafe to consume" and if you have bought the affected products, you should return them to where you bought them for a refund.

The FSA has warned shoppers "do not consume" the milk.

The recall applies to the following products:

- Graham's Family Dairy Semi-Skimmed Milk - one pint
- Graham's Family Dairy Semi-Skimmed Milk - one litre
- Graham's Family Dairy Semi-Skimmed Milk - two litres

To check the milk, look out for the best before date of July 31, 2026.

Graham's Family Dairy said: "At Grahams Family Dairy we believe that the quality of our products and the safety of our consumers is paramount.

"This is a precautionary measure we have taken as there is a small risk a trace level of antibiotics may be present in the milk which could cause a reaction to consumers with an allergy to antibiotics."

## What is a product recall?

The FSA explains what a product recall is: "If there is a problem with a food product that means it should not be sold, then it might be 'withdrawn' (taken off the shelves) or 'recalled' (when customers are asked to return the product).

"The FSA issues Product Recall Information Notices to let consumers and local authorities know about problems associated with food.
