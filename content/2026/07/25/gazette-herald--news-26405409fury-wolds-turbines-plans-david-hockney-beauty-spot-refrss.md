Search the Public Notice PortalVillagers have criticised plans to build six giant wind turbines in an East Yorkshire beauty spot immortalised by David Hockney.

They said the plans, submitted days before the Hockney’s death last month aged 88, will “ruin” the rolling landscape in the Yorkshire Wolds which inspired the artist.

But the firm behind the plans said the turbines would "not result in significant adverse effect" on the area and would produce the equivalent power as consumed by around 24,000 average UK households annually.

The 492-foot-high (149m) turbines are being proposed for a site in Woldgate, near Burton Agnes, west of Bridlington.

  The site in Woldgate Woods in Yorkshire which was painted by David Hockney in a piece entitled The Arrival of Spring 

*(Image: SWNS)*

They will form part of the previously approved Three Oaks Renewable Energy Park, which includes a solar farm and battery storage.

Hockney created a series of paintings of nearby Woldgate Woods which he regularly passed on the way to Bridlington, where he had a studio.

One piece, titled Woldgate Woods, sold for £9.4 million in New York in 2016.

Hockney previously objected to wind farm proposals in the area, describing the turbines as “big ugly things”.

  The countryside around Woldgate near Bridlington in Yorkshire near to where six wind turbines are planned 

*(Image: SWNS)*

The fresh proposals submitted by developers Ridge Clean Energy last month have alarmed residents in the nearby villages.

Retired art teacher Nancy Millar, 75, who lives in a nearby village of Carnaby said she is a “huge champion of green energy and the fight against climate change”, but the turbines would have a negative impact on the area.

"The recent loss of David Hockney has made his depictions of the landscape under discussion even more irreplaceable,” she said.

"If the project goes ahead on this site it shows a huge lack of respect for national artistic heritage. Let’s face it, there are other fields that haven’t been painted by one of the most iconic artists in modern history.

"The battle against climate change doesn’t have to be a war on culture."

  Nancy Miller, a local artist who draws inspiration from the landscape and who works near where six wind turbines are planned 

*(Image: SWNS)*

According to East Riding of Yorkshire Council’s planning portal, the proposal is for a wind farm consisting of six turbines and one 300-foot-high (91.4m) meteorological mast inside a 5.4-hectare area.

It will also include the installation of underground cabling, transformer kiosks, construction of crane pads, access tracks and widening of existing vehicular access.

Ridge Clean Energy said the turbines would "not result in significant adverse effect" on the area, adding that they are "capable of being accommodated within the host landscape".

The company also said the turbines would be a "significant contribution" to the country's green energy requirements.

Ridge Clean Energy has offered a "community benefit fund" of around £126,000 a year, which is worth more than £5 million throughout the 40 years it will be operational.

But several Rudston residents have rallied together to create the group "Keep Wind Off Woldgate".

  Rob Booth, left, and Brendan McEvoy who are opposed to wind turbines planned to be built near Bridlington 

*(Image: SWNS)*

Rob Booth, 58, a Rudston resident and Keep Wind Off Woldgate member, said: "Residents are absolutely appalled that the developer has said these turbines can be 'accommodated within the host landscape' and would not result in significant adverse effects, when East Riding Council's own pre-application assessment concluded precisely the opposite.”

"We will continue to fight these plans and encourage everyone who values this unique destination to submit their objections,” he added.

Ridge Clean Energy said it would combine the turbines with the already approved solar energy farm which will "offset emissions" and "contribute to the national legislation of net zero emissions by 2050". East Riding County Council approved the 161-acre solar farm in Spring 2024.

Richard Barker, project manager of the proposed Three Oaks Renewable Energy Park Extension, said: "The proposed Three Oaks Renewable Energy Park Extension would make a meaningful contribution to the critical need for renewable energy in the UK, to limit the effects of volatile fossil fuel costs.

"The planning application for the proposed six wind turbines is accompanied by an Environmental Impact Assessment, which includes detailed reports on landscape and visual impact, noise, biodiversity, and cultural heritage.

"The noise report demonstrates that the wind turbines would meet the strict guidelines established by the UK Government.

  The countryside around Woldgate near Bridlington in Yorkshire near to where six wind turbines are planned 

*(Image: SWNS)*

"Whilst recognising there could be localised landscape and visual impacts, as is the nature of onshore wind in the countryside, it is important to consider these in the context of delivering much needed renewable energy.

"Alongside providing the equivalent annual electricity needs of approximately 24,000 East Riding homes, the 25.2MW wind extension would deliver at least £126,000 each year for the local community, totalling more than £5 million for the lifetime of the project."

If the plans are agreed, work can begin in early 2028 with construction time expected to take around eight months.

East Riding County Council said it cannot comment on an active planning application.
