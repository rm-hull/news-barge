There’s a cracking Saturday in prospect on the Knavesmire, with world-class music from Tom Grennan following an excellent seven-race card with an intriguing feature.

It is day two of Music Showcase Weekend, the final day of the Go Racing in Yorkshire Summer Festival, and the last day of racing at the track before the flagship meeting, the Sky Bet Ebor Festival, gets underway on Wednesday 19th August.

The £165,000 centrepiece of the card, the Group 2 Sky Bet York Stakes (3.35 pm), is a clash of the generations with the youngest, three-year-old Item a leading player and getting weight from his rivals.

Item (pictured) has already been seen to good effect in these parts, winning the Al Basti Equiworld Dubai Dante Stakes in May.

His only run since was in the Derby when he finished eighth, fading out of contention at Epsom, but has been given plenty of time to get back to his best.

Still, it was 2006 the last time this race went to a horse of the classic generation.

The others are headed by the Yorkshire-trained Royal Champion, from Karl Burke’s Middleham yard, who won the race a year ago but is now eight, so bidding to be the oldest ever victor.

Pride of Arras, who achieved a notable York double last year, winning both the Dante and Sky Bet Great Voltigeur Stakes, and Damysus, who steps down in grade and up in trip, and the field is completed by Dancing Gemini, who was placed in the French Guineas in 2024.

One bizarre fact is that since this race was first run in 2006, no jockey has won the race more than once.

Clifford Lee (Royal Champion) can be the first.

The card begins with something completely different and one of the most eagerly anticipated of the year as the jump jockeys, more used to three miles and 18 fences than speedballs, line up in their own version of the Nunthorpe Stakes, over five furlongs.

The Weighing Room is never more competitive, and northern-based Danny McMenamin brings a good track record to the race.

He was third two years ago and last year booted Air Force One home to a popular win.

He dons the same Bond Family colours this time with Managing Director, also trained by Geoff Oldroyd, but Johnny Burke, second last year, has a live chance of going one better this time on Lethal Nymph (1.30 pm), beaten in a photo last time at Pontefract.

It says much about the race that Jonjo O’Neill junior and top Irish rider Sean Flanagan are York-bound for rides, as is multiple champion jump jockey Brian Hughes.

The big sprint handicap (2.00 pm) has attracted a maximum field of 22.

The Sky Bet Dash is a race that could help decide who is Champion Trainer in the Go Racing in Yorkshire Festival, and Red Spells Danger, drawn in stall 11 for Tim Easterby and his stable jockey David Allan, was second here at the Charity meeting and could be poised for another good day.

As part of the festival, there’ll be jockeys demonstrating their alleged football skills in Paddock and Chips in the paddock before racing, and after the last race is run at 4.50 pm, Tom Grennan, one of the very best in the business, will take to the stage.

What’s not widely known is that Grennan at one time trained to be a professional footballer with Luton Town, near his Bedford home, and has been in the stars ever since he released his debut album, Lighting Matches, eight years ago.

Gates open at 11.15 am, and to get close to the stage, racegoers need to purchase admission to the County Stand or Grandstand and Paddock.
