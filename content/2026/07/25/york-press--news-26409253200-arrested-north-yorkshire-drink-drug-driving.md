Dozens of drink and drug drivers are already facing court charges after almost 200 people were arrested in York and North Yorkshire.

North Yorkshire Police officers arrested 97 people on suspicion of drink driving and 95 on suspicion of drug driving between June 11 and July 19.

Additionally, 31 motorists were arrested for failing to provide samples for analysis.

A seasonal increase in enforcement activity led to extra high-visibility patrols and roadside operations across the area.

Speaking at the start of the operation, Chief Inspector Tom Ibbetson warned that there was no excuse for getting behind the wheel impaired, and that robust action would be taken against anyone who chose to drive under the influence of drink or drugs.

About 10 per cent of the arrests followed a road traffic collision, highlighting the risks that impaired drivers pose to themselves and other road users.

More than a third of those arrested have already been charged and are facing a day in court, with most of the other cases awaiting forensic blood analysis results before further action is taken.

The force reminded the public that punishment for those found to be over the drink-drive limit, or driving while impaired by drugs can include a criminal record, a maximum penalty of six months in prison, an unlimited fine, and an automatic driving ban of at least one year.

The maximum penalty for causing death while driving dangerously under the influence of alcohol or drugs is life imprisonment.

A North Yorkshire Police spokesperson added: "If you believe someone is about to drive under the influence, dial 999 - we will always aim to respond immediately".
