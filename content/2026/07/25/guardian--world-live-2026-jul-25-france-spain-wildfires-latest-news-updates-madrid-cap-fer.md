French authorities evacuated thousands of people from suburbs near Bordeaux on Saturday as wildfires in areas around the city showed no signs of abating, the Reuters news agency reports.

Sophie Brocas, who heads the local administration of La Nouvelle-Aquitaine et de la Gironde, which covers the Bordeaux region, said on X that the areas of Le Haillan, Eysines and Merignac were being cleared out.

French Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu said that more than 141,000 people had so far been evacuated from the Gironde and Landes regions which lie close to Bordeaux, and where the wildfires first broke out this week.

“The fires which are hitting our country have reached a level that has never been seen before,” Lecornu wrote on X on Saturday. The French army has also been drafted in to help.

The French government has mobilised soldiers to help tackle the wildfire ravaging south-west France and distributed 1.5m face masks to protect people from smoke exposure.

Authorities in the Gironde region that includes Bordeaux ordered the evacuation for parts of three of the city’s western suburbs, including its airport, according to the Associated Press. Other towns and villages west of the city were also ordered to evacuate, adding to the tally of 141,000 people who have already been forced to flee in the face of two major fires in the south-western Gironde and Landes regions.

Sánchez warns Madrid wildfire situation remains 'complex' as he calls for 'state pact against climate emergency'

The Spanish prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, said the wildfire situation near Madrid remained “complex” as he visited affected areas.

“We don’t know how the wind will develop, even though temperatures have dropped,” he said, according to El País.

The Spanish newspaper reported the prime minister reiterated his call for a “national pact against the climate emergency”, as he asked the public to “accept and understand” that climate change is causing increasingly severe and intense forest fires that are even “exceeding scientific predictions”.

Spanish prime minister Pedro Sánchez visits the firefighters’ control centre in the village of Cenicientos, Madrid. Photograph: MARISCAL/EPA

A French foreign minister has urged people not to abandon travel plans despite severe wildfires affecting parts of the country’s south-west.

Éléonore Caroit, the minister for francophone, international partnerships and French nationals abroad, said France was experiencing an “exceptionally intense” fire season, but stressed that many regions remained unaffected and visitors should keep their plans while staying informed about local conditions.

Speaking on BBC Radio 4’s Today programme, Caroit described the fires as a “tragedy”, with nearly 50,000 hectares (123,600 acres) of land burned this year, surpassing the impact of previous severe fire seasons.

She added: “You have entire areas that are fine, you’re seeing similar phenomena in Spain and other countries. I’m in Paris and nothing is happening, and most other regions are OK, so do please come to France and do not cancel your travel plans.

“But wherever you are, please do be advised as to what might be happening.”

Swinney: Cairngorms wildfire 'a very serious situation'

Scotland’s first minister John Swinney said the Cairngorms national park wildfire is still a “very serious situation”, as the Scottish government reconvened an emergency meeting this morning to review the ongoing response.

The village of Nethy Bridge was evacuated as a precaution last night after Police Scotland declared a major incident following changes in wind conditions that intensified the pace of spread of the wildfire.

“This continues to be a very serious situation. Residents, businesses and people staying in the affected areas will understandably be very worried. My thoughts are with them and I thank them for their understanding,” Swinney said in a statement.

“With the fire changing to be in the direction of Nethy Bridge yesterday afternoon, a precautionary evacuation was put in place and a fire break was successfully established overnight to protect the village. Police officers visited all properties in Nethy Bridge and officers are in the village today to support residents.”

He added that changing winds had shifted the wildfire’s path, reducing the immediate threat to Nethy Bridge while firefighting teams focused on containing the blaze and creating new firebreaks.

“This demonstrates how these situations can change very quickly and dramatically,” he said.

My colleague Kim Willsher reports from France on how relentless heat and prolonged drought are transforming the country’s rivers and the ecosystems that depend on them.

While the fires raging across forests in France are a dramatic reminder of three successive heatwaves in as many months,the state of the country’s rivers provides another, no less worrying insight into the impact of extreme heat supercharged by the climate crisis.

The Loire, France’s longest river, is at a historical low, with the flow of some branches, including through the city of Orléans, reduced to a trickle between sandbanks. Two weeks ago, 16% of French rivers were reported to have dried up. A quarter of small streams were stagnant or had disappeared altogether, according to the national low water observatory. Scottish government holds emergency meeting as wildfire prompts evacuation of Cairngorms homes

In the UK, a major incident has been declared after a wildfire in the Cairngorms national park in Scotland forced the evacuation of hundreds of people.

Nethy Bridge, a village of about 600 people, was evacuated last night as a precaution and all access roads to the village have been closed, police said.

The Scottish government convened its emergency response committee – the Scottish government resilience room, known as SGORR – yesterday to the evacuation of Nethy Bridge, with the meeting chaired by first minister John Swinney.

“[Scottish Fire and Rescue Service] resources are focused on creating a fire break and additional police resources are being deployed to assist with the evacuation, including identifying vulnerable residents who may need extra support,” Swinney said in a statement.

The Scotsman newspaper reported the wildfire, which broke out in the Glenmore area of the Cairngorms national park more than a week ago, has prompted calls for military support after strong winds caused the blaze to spread despite a large-scale response involving more than 500 firefighters and other emergency personnel.

Supt Judy Hill of Police Scotland said: “The weather conditions experienced within the Cairngorms over the last 24 hours have added further complexities to this situation. With evidence suggesting the fire is spreading towards Nethy Bridge, the decision has been taken to declare a major incident and evacuate residents as a precaution.”

Official warns windy conditions could intensify Madrid wildfires

Authorities in Spain said weather conditions in the early hours of this morning offer “a real opportunity” for firefighters to control the blaze, but stronger winds later today could hamper efforts and intensify the fire.

Francisco Martín, the government delegate in Madrid, said the drop in temperature, easing winds and increase in humidity meant firefighters could tackle the wildfires near the Spanish capital “with some guarantee of success”.

But he warned “erratic gusts” forecast later today could make the situation “difficult”.

The Madrid regional government’s emergency management chief, Carlos Novillo, told reporters yesterday that the fire was “at its peak and … currently beyond the capacity of firefighters to contain”, AFP reported.

Spain’s government declared a national emergency on Thursday evening after wildfires threatened areas near the capital and in the neighbouring province of Ávila, leading to the evacuation or confinement of more than 63,000 people.

A national emergency, which allows central government to assume overall control of powers and responsibilities that are usually the domain of Spain’s self-governing regions, has only been declared once before, during the countrywide blackout of April 2025.

The regional government of Madrid requested assistance, describing the situation as one of “extreme gravity”. It said at least four active fires could advance “beyond current extinguishing capacity”. The fires remained uncontained on Friday.

Spanish authorities said on Friday afternoon there was “very little time left” before the blaze in Madrid, already made up of two merged fires, combined with the Ávila fire.

Madrid’s regional environment minister, Carlos Novillo, said the fire had reached “a critical moment” and was “beyond our extinguishing capability”. He added: “The priority at the moment is protecting people, helping those who have been evacuated, and making the most of windows of opportunity.”

Spain’s prime minister, Pedro Sánchez, chaired an emergency response meeting at the Moncloa palace on Friday morning as the state meteorological office warned that the danger of more fires remained very high.

He said the government had deployed “all available resources”, adding: “It will be by your side during the emergency and the recovery.”

The prime minister also thanked “all the emergency services that are fighting day and night”. Sánchez is due to visit the affected area on Saturday morning.

On Friday, France’s president, Emmanuel Macron, said he had activated the EU’s civil protection mechanism as the country, which has endured three almost back-to-back heatwaves since May, battled numerous wildfires.

“We will soon be able to count on reinforcements from two Croatian aircraft, two Portuguese planes, as well as two heavy helicopters from the Czech Republic and Slovakia,” Macron said.

Meanwhile, France’s interior minister, Laurent Nuñez, said that 32 wildfires were burning, with 50,000 hectares scorched already so far this year – nearly four times the total by the same date last year.
