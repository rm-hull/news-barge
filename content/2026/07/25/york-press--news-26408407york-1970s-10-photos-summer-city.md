## When York got a taste of Paris – 10 photos of summer in the city from the 1970s

July 8, 1970 - A touch of Paris in the centre of York. The scene outside the Theatre Royal. YEP PIC.

  *York in the 1970s - 10 photos of summer in the city from The Press archive*

JULY may be holiday season for many, but York's Press archives show it was also a month packed with memorable events, civic occasions and everyday scenes that now provide fascinating glimpses into the city's past.

One of the most intriguing images comes from July 8, 1970, when York briefly acquired "a touch of Paris". Magistrates had agreed to allow drinks to be served in the forecourt of the Theatre Royal for a three-week trial period.

The photograph shows people sitting around French-styled cafe tables and under Martini-branded sun umbrellas enjoying outdoor refreshments in front of the theatre, with York Minster looming large in the background.

  July 8, 1970 - A touch of Paris in the centre of York. The scene outside the Theatre Royal following the decision of York magistrates to allow drinks to be served for a trial three week period in the forecourt. YEP PIC.


Industry was never far from the headlines in 1970. Another Press photograph from July 6 shows engineers leaving the Joseph Rowntree Theatre after accepting a new wage offer from Rowntree Macintosh Ltd. The deal, worth an immediate £2 a week with a further £1 increase the following month, brought an end to negotiations involving 460 maintenance workers at one of York's most important employers.

  July 6,1970: A new wage offer of an immediate £2 a week, with a further £1 in August was accepted today by 460 maintenance men at the York factory of Rowntree Macintosh Ltd. Rowntree engineers leaving the Rowntree Theatre after their meeting


The River Ouse also features prominently in the archive. A delightful image from July 1970 shows "barges galore" crowding the river, a reminder of a time long gone when barges were commonplace on our waterways.

Sport was well represented too. A team photograph from July 1970 captures York City manager Tom Johnston photographing his squad. Familiar names of the era include Tommy Thompson, John Mackin, Albert Johanneson and Bobby Sibbald, preserving a snapshot of the Minstermen at the start of a new football campaign.

  York City July 1970 - Manager Tom Johnston taking pictures of his team..Back L-R Phil Burrows, Barry Swallow, Gordon Morritt, Mick Gadsby, Chris Topping, Ian Davidson..Middle L-R Archie Taylor, Paul Maloney, Paul Aimson, Kevin McMahon, Bobby Sibbald,


Moving into July 1971, the archives offer a glimpse inside the elegant Rose Room at the Royal Station Hotel, a venue associated with generations of celebrations, dinners and special occasions. Another photograph from the same month takes us onto the cricket field with the Nunthorpe Grammar School cricket team, capturing youthful sporting ambition during a long Yorkshire summer.

One of the decade's biggest royal occasion arrived on July 13, 1977, when crowds lined York's historic streets to welcome Queen Elizabeth II and the Duke of Edinburgh during their Silver Jubilee visit. A striking Press image shows the royal couple arriving at Micklegate Bar as thousands gathered to celebrate the milestone year.

  July 13, 1977 - The Queen and Duke of Edinburgh arrive outside Micklegate Bar on their Silver Jubilee visit to York


A year later, musical talent was making headlines at St George's Secondary Modern School. In July 1978, the school's senior brass band proudly displayed their medals after winning the York Schools' Brass Band Festival. The junior band also competed, highlighting the strength of music education in York schools at the time.

Community life and faith feature in another archive picture from July 1979, showing Archbishop of York Stuart Blanch talking with pupils from Archbishop of York's Junior School at the old St Andrew's Church. The occasion marked the 80th anniversary of the church's final service and the opening of its replacement.

Finally, a wonderful photograph from July 1979 brings together a collection of York Police Force uniforms through the ages, modelled in front of Clifford's Tower.
