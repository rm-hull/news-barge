An MP has called for firefighters in York to have and run their own rescue boat after an inquest heard delays potentially resulted in a student drowning in the city.

Emergency services were unable to save Evan d’Andilly, 19, a University of York student from Scunthorpe, who drowned after entering the River Ouse from Ouse Bridge on March 5, 2025.

During the inquest, it was revealed that North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service does not have its own rescue boat stationed in York – instead, the fire service relies on the volunteer-led York Rescue Boat for its river rescues in the city.

The closest fire service rescue boats to York are stationed in Selby and Malton.

  Evan d'Andilly who drowned in the River Ouse in York on March 5, 2025 

*(Image: Family)*

In Evan’s case, the fire service called for its rescue boat in Selby but it did not arrive until 14 minutes after the last sighting of the student.

Police called for the York Rescue Boat’s crew to come and save Evan, who had been in the freezing water for 10 minutes by this point, but it took another 10 minutes for the York Rescue Boat’s crew to arrive and launch the vessel – by which point Evan had dropped beneath the water.

According to North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service’s website, the fire service has a York rescue boat listed as one of its specialist appliances.

But it was revealed during the inquest that the fire service does not have a crew in York trained for river rescues – meaning no one is trained to use that rescue boat.

  Police search the River Ouse in York on March 5, 2025, for Evan d'Andilly 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Ben Illsley, North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service’s director of operational support and assurance, told the inquest there are “no plans” to move the fire service’s rescue boat from Selby to York in the next four years.

He also said that if the rescue boat was moved to York, it would take one to two years for the fire service’s staff to be trained to use it.

Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central, told The Press that York fire station’s river rescue capability “must be delivered, alongside the funding to train and support our firefighters to back this operation”.

She said firefighters and the Fire Brigades Union (FBU) have asked for the fire boat “to be returned to York fire station”.

“I understand the FBU have discussed a number of proposals with the service to reinstate the rescue boat, with the final one last year not accepted and therefore I’ve written to the mayor to ask for his intervention,” Ms Maskell said. “This must be resolved without delay.

“The training required for firefighters to run the rescue boat must happen now to reduce the possibility of another tragic death in our rivers."

  Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central 

*(Image: UK Parliament/PA Wire)*

Evan’s mother, Lorraine d’Andilly, told the inquest her son had “fought for another 10 minutes” to stay above the water after the York Rescue Boat was called.

“I will never understand how a safety protocol can be so flawed,” she said.

Speaking after the inquest, Evan’s father, Gordon d’Andilly, said there was “no doubt” that Evan could still be alive if other assets, such as a York-based fire rescue boat, were available to the emergency services.

Mr d’Andilly stressed that he had “nothing but praise and thanks” for the emergency services who worked to rescue his son but said: “In relation to the assets that are available [to the emergency services], my opinion differs. It’s become apparent that the resources that first of all the police thought were available, aren’t there.

“By that, I mean the [fire service’s] York rescue boat that was previously in York was decommissioned a number of years before.”

He questioned why the fire service did not have its own rescue boat stationed in York, saying: "If you’ve got an issue in your house with the plumbing and there’s a leak downstairs but you have the fittings upstairs, you’re going to move the fittings to where the actual issue is.

“York is an issue … That’s difficult to live with.”

  Gordon and Lorraine d'Andilly speak to the media after an inquest concludes into the death of their son Evan on Wednesday (July 22) at Northallerton Coroners' Court 

*(Image: Dylan Connell)*

Ms Maskell said it was “distressing to read details of the inquest and my thoughts are with the friends and family of Evan d’Andilly”. “Evan had his life ahead of him, and it is so tragic that his, as with others, have been lost in the Ouse because the infrastructure has been insufficient.

“I want to again thank the police and York Rescue Boat for all they do in the city to keep people safe, but the report is rightly critical on the time lost to contact York Rescue Boat thereby delaying access to the river, not least when every second counts.

"All agencies must be held to account, and immediate contact must be made to York Rescue Boat if someone enters the river and more measures need putting in place to back up the work that they do to improve river safety.

"Another river death in York only adds to the ongoing concern York residents have for river safety.”

## 'Vital' that water safety is 'aligned to risk' across county, says deputy mayor

Jo Coles, York and North Yorkshire’s deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime, said it is “vital” that “water safety provision is appropriately aligned to risk across the county” to keep people safe.

"Everyone wants to see the best possible water safety measures in place across in our area and I'll continue to seek to ensure that the fire service is managing risk effectively in my role holding the chief fire officer to account,” Ms Coles added.

"The death of Evan D'Andilly was a tragedy and my thoughts are with his family and friends as they come to terms with their terrible loss,” she said. “Across a county, like ours, that includes both rural areas and the city of York, I regularly seek assurances about the levels of water safety provision, most recently in June of this year.”

  Jo Coles, York and North Yorkshire’s deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime 

*(Image: Gavin Priestley)*

A North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service spokesperson said: “Our thoughts remain with Evan’s family and friends following this tragic loss.

“Keeping people safe around water is a shared responsibility and one we take extremely seriously. North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service provides water rescue capabilities across the county, including bankside rescue capability in York. This is supported by a memorandum of understanding with York Rescue Boat, which provides a dedicated response in the city, ensuring a coordinated approach to water rescue incidents.

“We welcome the continued commitment of all partners to improving water safety across York and North Yorkshire, ensuring that together, we provide responsive and sustainable water rescue capabilities for local communities.”

After the inquest, a North Yorkshire Police spokesperson said: “First and foremost, our thoughts remain with the family and friends of Evan d’Andilly.

“The tragic death of Evan is felt deeply by all our officers and staff who, under extremely difficult circumstances, tried to save him.

“The findings of the inquest will be carefully reviewed by all the emergency services and partner agencies involved in the response.”
