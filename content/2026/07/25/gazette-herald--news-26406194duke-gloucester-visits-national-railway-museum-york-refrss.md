A special royal visit has put the spotlight on a major museum transformation and a vision for the future of rail.

His Royal Highness The Duke of Gloucester visited the National Railway Museum in York on Wednesday, July 22, as part of a wider tour of the city, which also included stops at York Castle Museum and York Minster.

During his time at the railway museum, the Duke was given a first-hand look at the museum’s multi-million-pound transformation.

He was shown proposals for a new entrance building and a future-facing gallery.

Andrew McLean, assistant director and head curator at the National Railway Museum, said: "We were delighted to welcome His Royal Highness to the National Railway Museum and showcase both our historic collection and our exciting plans for the future.

"The Duke of Gloucester's longstanding interest in railway history and engineering made this a particularly meaningful visit.

"When viewing the Duke of Gloucester Cab, His Royal Highness shared his own memories of driving the real Duke of Gloucester locomotive back in the 1980s."

The Duke toured several key exhibits, including Stephenson’s Rocket, the Mallard, and a full-size replica cab of the Duke of Gloucester locomotive.

With his interest in rail, His Royal Highness was also keen to know more about the Queen Mary nameplate, named after his late Grandmother, and commented on his passion for the Duchess Class locomotives while viewing the Duchess of Hamilton.

His visit included a guided tour of the museum’s Our Transformation exhibition, which outlines plans for Central Hall, a major new entrance designed to provide level access across the site.

The Duke also heard about the Futures Gallery, a planned permanent space to explore innovation in rail and engage visitors in imagining the future of rail.

Lisa Moore, masterplan project director at the museum, said: "Alongside celebrating the remarkable story of the railways, our transformation will help create a more accessible, welcoming and inspiring museum for millions of visitors in the years ahead, and it was a privilege to share our ambitions with His Royal Highness.

"With his background in architecture, the design for Central Hall, based on a working railway roundhouse, coupled with an ambition to welcome even more visitors in the future, were of particular interest to the Duke."

The museum’s transformation is part of the York Central partnership, one of the UK’s largest urban regeneration projects, positioning the National Railway Museum at its cultural heart.

Central Hall, designed by award-winning architects Feilden Fowles, will serve as a unifying entrance, featuring a new gallery, café, shop, and toilets.

For more information, visit: www.nationalrailwaymuseum.org.uk
