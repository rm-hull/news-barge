# New lottery funding to build legacy from Bradford City of Culture

## A global spotlight was to shine on Bradford's heritage gems for its incredible year as City of Culture.

Now, celebrating the depth of the district's diversity, £400,000 is agreed to ensure a legacy carries on.

Among new projects to secure Lottery funding are the hidden threads of Bradford's South Asian Muslim Women, and the textile stories founded here.

Then a community grants programme, sharing cultural traditions, and with bids available of up to £10,000 before a heritage festival next summer.

![Better Communities exhibition](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjMwYmM1MTRlLWE5NmQtNDBhNC1iNTc1LTQwZTY5YmUxNmJiZjo2NzgzMWY1YS01MmQzLTQ4ZDMtODUxNS0xYzUwZjQ2ZmU4YzU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Helen Featherstone is director for the North at The National Lottery Heritage Fund. She said: "Bradford boasts some of the most amazing heritage assets and stories that are pivotal in creating the district's unique sense of place, identity, and helping people connect to their past. We are excited to see what is next for Bradford.”

Some £4.95m in Lottery funding was initially awarded for a year-long celebration in 2025, with over three million people participating.

Now the new funding from The National Lottery Heritage Fund sits alongside sums previously awarded to Bradford Culture Company.

Projects are already underway from new artist residency programmes to a documentary on the district's rural spaces and a book exploring the district's African Caribbean communities from the 1980s.

![The Leap](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmU5NWUxNWM0LWNkYzktNDdkYS05YjZlLTE3MzliY2RjZmY4NzphYjRmZTYwOS1kNTQyLTRjNzUtYjI0Zi0zM2IwMDM1N2JjZmM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Now £160,000 has been awarded to Better Communities Bradford (BCB) for Hidden Threads: Revealing the Heritage of South Asian Muslim Women.

Many South Asian Muslim families settled in Bradford from the 1960s, yet much of the women’s textile heritage is undocumented.

Now the project will preserve and share these traditions, from embroidery techniques to the stories of migration and family lore, passed down through generations.

Abbas Najib, chief executive of BCB, said it will help preserve not only beautiful textile traditions, but also people's memories, stories and experiences: "We are proud to help ensure that this heritage is not lost and can be appreciated by future generations.”

A community-led heritage project from The Leap has also been awarded £249,974 to deliver a grants programme, with groups invited to bid for sums between £500 and £10,000.

This aims to share traditions and skills of Bradford's diverse communities dating from the 1940s, before a heritage festival next summer.

Executive director Zulfiqar Ahmed said sums will help people take an active role in preserving their own heritage: "Through the community-led heritage programme, The Leap will help ensure that many of the traditions, skills and histories that define our district are documented, valued and safeguarded for future generations.”
