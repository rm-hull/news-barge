Fire crews tackled a series of deliberate fires, bonfires and emergencies across North Yorkshire on Friday evening and into the early hours.

Firefighters from Knaresborough, Harrogate and Ripon were called to a grass embankment fire on the A1 between junctions 48 and 49 at 6.12pm. Two hose reel jets were used to extinguish the blaze, with a thermal imaging camera deployed to check for hotspots.

Minutes later, at 6.19pm, crews from Kirkbymoorside attended a deliberate fire in woodland on Peaslands Lane, Thornton Le Dale. The fire was put out using knapsack sprayers and the incident was left with police.

In Selby, firefighters were called to a bonfire at a residential property on Charles Street at 8.28pm. The fire was extinguished and advice given to the homeowner.

Two further deliberate fires were reported shortly after 9pm. At 9.13pm, crews from Acomb attended a blaze in a wooded area at Leeman Park, believed to have been started by youths. Minutes later, at 9.16pm, firefighters responded to reports of a disposable barbecue on dry grass at Thornton Moor Close, using water from a nearby pond to put it out. In both cases, advice was given and police were informed where appropriate.

At 9.29pm, crews were called to a large bonfire in Sand Hutton, initially thought to be out of control, but later confirmed as a controlled burn. Similar incidents in Pickering (9.59pm) and on Cleveland Way, Scarborough (10.21pm), were also found to be supervised burns, with crews offering safety advice.

In Scarborough, firefighters also carried out a lift rescue at St Nicholas Cliff at 9.44pm, freeing multiple people who were unharmed. Later, at 10.26pm, crews attended a property on Queen's Parade after reports of water coming through a ceiling affecting electrics, advising the occupier to contact their landlord.

Elsewhere, a small garden fire in Ripon at 10.55pm was extinguished, while in New Earswick, York, a bonfire reported at 11.06pm was deemed safe and supervised.

In Harrogate, firefighters responded to basement flooding on Royal Parade at 12.21am caused by a burst water main, isolating the supply and assisting occupiers while Yorkshire Water attended.

Crews were also called to assist ambulance services in two early morning incidents. At 5.26am in Scalby, Scarborough, firefighters forced entry to a property to reach a casualty inside. At 5.59am in Richmond, crews helped a person who had fallen at home, gaining access using ladders and lifting them to safety.

Throughout the night, crews from Knaresborough, Scarborough and York also attended several automatic fire alarm activations, all of which were found to be false alarms.
