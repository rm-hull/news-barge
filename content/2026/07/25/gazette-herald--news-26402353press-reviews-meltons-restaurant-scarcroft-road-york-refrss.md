*Press reporter Abby Backhouse visited one of York’s most renowned fine dining restaurants Melton’s to try out its summer menu.*

Located in Scarcroft Road, a short walk from the city centre, the family-run restaurant has been a fixture of York’s food scene since 1990.

Diners can expect modern British dishes made using seasonal, locally sourced ingredients.

Melton's had been on my radar for some time, with many insisting it was long overdue being awarded a Michelin star.

  


The restaurant is owned by Michael and Lucy Hjort.

Michael trained with the prominent Roux Restaurants and moved to his native York in the 90s.

Calvin Miller, who has worked for Michael for over 15 years, and has been Head Chef for the last nine, proudly developing Melton’s dishes over that time and earning a coveted Third AA Rosette this year.

In 2001 Michael opened Melton’s Too; now known as ‘Walmgate Ale House’ and he heads the kitchen at The Chopping Block – the Anglo-French restaurant above the Ale House.

I booked ahead for a 6pm reservation for two and was guided up a flight of stairs to the first-floor dining area.

  Melton's has been a fixture of York's food scene for more than 30 years 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

The restaurant is unlike other fine-dining restaurants I’ve been to.

I was surprised at how unpretentious it was.

The atmosphere was relaxed and inviting and I was relieved to see some diners wearing more casual dress.

The meal began with a look through the fixed-price and extensive wine menu – an impressive catalogue of labels from around the world.

  The scallop option cost an additional £8 but was worth every penny 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

I was pleased to see a range of price options.

We selected for a 2021 bottle of Prunus Branco, a Portuguese dry-white wine with fruity tones, at £29.50.

Taking our first sip, we were soon presented with three amuse-bouches.

My favourite was the beef tartare with black cow cheddar custard, gherkin and fermented lettuce – a tiny parcel, packed with flavour and texture.

We were also served a selection of delicious warm homemade breads, paired with pepper butter – beautiful and velvety, with a touch of salt that made for the tastiest spread I have ever eaten.

  The North Sea cod, complete with artichokes, broad beans, hazelnuts and smoked pike roe 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

I believe everyone should experience fine dining. In the fast-paced world we live in, it offers a rare chance to slow down and savour the unique experience.

All previous anxiety about formalities melted away as we chatted about our own dishes, which arrived in quick succession.

For our starters, we chose Orkney Scallops and a garden pea and Iberian ham tartlet.

The scallop option cost an additional £8 but was worth every penny.

  The Nidderdale lamb was exquisite and melted in the mouth 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

The tartlet oozed flavour and was elegantly presented, with a crisp pastry casing and rich confit yolk which cut through the saltiness of the ham perfectly.

Moving on to our main course, we opted for the North Sea cod, complete with artichokes, broad beans, hazelnuts and smoked pike roe, and Yorkshire lamb with turnip, little gem lettuce, alliums and madeira wine.

The lamb was exquisite and melted in the mouth.

  I swapped my rich chocolate dessert for a raspberry souffle and tonka bean ice cream 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

As we reached our desserts, I realised just how comfortably full I was.

This came as another pleasant surprise, as previous fine dining experiences had often left me feeling hungry by the end of the meal.

I did regret my choice of dessert though.

I had chosen the Huila chocolate with caramelised white chocolate, cherry and woodruff.

  The 70 per cent Huila chocolate with caramelised white chocolate, cherry and woodruff 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

I found this a little rich, so swapped with my partner’s dessert – a raspberry souffle and tonka bean ice cream.

We rounded off the delicious meal with two tiny complimentary chocolate tarts and the last of our wine.

The price? Excluding a 10 per cent tip, our bill came to £208.50.

Despite Melton’s being an expensive choice, I believe it is worth it – a great destination for those major milestones, and one where you can relax over beautiful wine, paired with stunning food.
