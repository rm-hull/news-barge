**A couple have praised staff at three restaurants that stepped in to save the day when they were let down by wedding caterers at the last minute.**

Kate and Matt Dean from Skelmersdale, Lancashire, were preparing to celebrate their wedding with family and friends at a venue in Ormskirk when the caterers failed to turn up and they were unable to get in touch with them.

With guests starting to arrive, Kate said they decided to ask two local restaurants if they could help.

Kate said she first approached restaurant staff at Piri Piri Grill, saying: "Hi! I don't suppose you can cook for 100 people?" and "he just went, 'oh, when?'... and I went, 'now'".

The couple, who have four children aged two to 12 between them, were married at Ormskirk Register Office on 13 July.

They decided to organise a special celebration for family and friends at Lost and Found Ormskirk on Sunday and were looking forward to the food arriving.

They arrived at the venue two hours ahead of time to get it ready but when time was ticking on - with no sign of the caterers - their concern grew.

They tried to call and message the business but there was no response and despite initially feeling upset - including crying in the bathroom at one point - Kate quickly hatched a back-up plan.

"I'm quite a laid back person anyway, so I was in fire-fighting mode, like we have got all these people coming and no food, 'what are we going to do?'," she said.

Kate said when guests started arriving she thought about local venues she liked.

"I just turned to Matt's family and said, 'if I can sort this, is everybody OK?'"

The 32-year-old nail technician said they had been planning their wedding since September, after postman Matt, 36, proposed in 2022.

She said the couple approached the teams at Piri Piri Grill and also at Scoozi Pizza and Pasta in Ormskirk and were hugely relieved when they agreed to help out.

Despite both restaurants being busy with the World Cup final on that day, they insisted on closing to the public and devoting themselves to helping out the newlyweds, she added.

Kate said it was great to see "towers of pizza boxes" which were "taller than Matt and he is 5ft 9ins (1.8m)".

Meanwhile, staff at the Lost and Found venue in Ormskirk were "more than accommodating" and "so helpful" in working to ensure the change of plan ran smoothly, she said.

Kate said the restaurant staff "were so amazing" and came back later on to check that everything was fine.

She said that despite having "ended up crying in the bathroom" at one point, they could see the funny side to what happened as they looked back on the photos.

"It ended up being quite funny - it was kind of like the wedding story."

Their children enjoyed "going around like little waiters with like four pizza boxes" with one saying "you ordered a pizza", she said.

Kate said she and Matt were grateful to family and friends for their support, including her best friend Jade Foley, who stepped in to pay a deposit for the hastily arranged new catering.

She said she turned her phone off later on her wedding day to try to switch off but, when she turned it back on the following day, she had missed messages and calls from the catering business owner.

They had apologised for what happened, refunded their money and explained that the no-show was due to an issue on the day beyond their control.

Kate said that, as a fellow business owner, she did not wish to name the firm as she understood that such things could happen and did not want them criticised on social media.

Liam Goulding, manager of Lost and Found Ormskirk, said Kate and Matt were "fantastic" and they were "more than happy to help out on such a special day".

He said he was proud of his team, including fellow manager Abi Rose and employee Abbie Lemaire for all they did that day.

Scoozi Pizza and Pasta declined to comment. The BBC has approached Piri Piri Grill.
