**'Business rates cuts are a start - but don't go far enough'**

THE Government’s announcement of a 20 per cent reduction in business rates for pubs, clubs and grassroots live music venues will land differently across the country – but in York, a city whose visitor economy depends heavily on its pubs, inns and historic hospitality venues, it’s a change that genuinely matters.

The cut is expected to save the typical venue around £1,100 next year.

York’s hospitality businesses know how tough the last few years have been. Rate increases, energy costs, staffing pressures, and the sheer volatility of tourism patterns have left operators firefighting.

This announcement doesn’t fix that – but it does finally feel like Westminster has remembered who keeps Britain’s visitor economy alive.

This matters for York because our pubs aren’t just places to grab a drink – they’re part of the city’s cultural identity.

But there’s a catch. The relief excludes restaurants and hotels, and that’s already raising eyebrows across York’s tourism sector.

York’s visitor economy is unusually interconnected. A typical tourist might stay in a hotel near Bootham, eat in a restaurant on Fossgate, enjoy drinks at the Fat Badger, and finish the night in a pub on Stonegate.

If one part of that chain weakens, the whole experience suffers. Pubs benefit from thriving hotels. Hotels benefit from vibrant bars. Restaurants rely on footfall generated by all of the above.

Targeted support is welcome – but selective support risks creating fragmentation in a sector that only works when everyone is healthy. Industry voices have already said the move “doesn’t go far enough,” and they’re right.

Hospitality needs structural reform: a simpler, fairer business rates system, VAT reform, and a long-term strategy for tourism-dependent cities like York.

  *Simon J Cotton,*

  *managing director,*

  *Fat Badger Group*

  **Burnham is 'straight talking PM who gets things done'**

SO, since Andy Burnham became Prime Minister on Monday, he’s announced the scrapping of VAT on electricity bills, the return of the £2 bus fare cap, a further 20 per cent off business rates for local venues.

Thus not just hitting the ground running but also reducing the cost of living for everyone.

We all know there’s a lot more to do, but let’s hope people now finally understand why we needed a change of leadership at the top of the Labour Party because under Andy Burnham we have an honest and straight talking Prime Minister who gets things done.

  *Jessica Goetia Brooking,*

  *Saxley Court,*

  *Havant, Hampshire*
