A children’s hospice is set to hold its first dedicated conference on children’s palliative care.

Martin House Children’s Hospice in Boston Spa will host the event on Tuesday, September 15, at its newly redeveloped hospice.

Open to professionals from hospitals, GP practices, community services, ambulance services and other care settings, the conference will share specialist knowledge with professionals supporting children with life-shortening conditions.

Charlotte Walker, deputy director of clinical services at Martin House, said: "Children’s palliative care is about helping babies, children and young people live as fully and comfortably as possible, while supporting their whole family."

The conference will help professionals better understand children’s palliative care, challenge common misconceptions about children’s hospices, and provide practical advice they can use in their own roles.

The day will feature workshops, a panel discussion, and a tour of the hospice’s new facilities, including a hydrotherapy pool and sensory spaces.

Dr Michelle Hills, chief medical officer at Martin House, will lead a session with clinical nurse specialists exploring real-life cases.

Jen Knell, practice educator at Martin House, said: "Many people associate children’s hospices only with end-of-life care, but Martin House supports children and families in many different ways and often over a number of years."

Tickets cost £20 and are available through Eventbrite.
