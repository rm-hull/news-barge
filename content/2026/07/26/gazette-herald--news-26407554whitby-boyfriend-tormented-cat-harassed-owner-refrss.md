A boyfriend tormented and injured his partner’s cat before subjecting his girlfriend to months of harassment, York Magistrates' Court heard.

The woman came home on November 25 to find her beloved pet’s face covered in blood, bleeding from nose and eye, and having suffered what a vet said was a “significant trauma”, said Alison Whiteley, prosecuting.

CCTV had captured Oliver Brown, 27, suddenly picking the cat up in “not a very nice way” and taking it upstairs. It had then recorded the sound of a bang.

On January 1, Brown was again caught on CCTV attacking the cat. This time he was kicking it and hitting with a slipper and chasing it around the house, said Ms Whiteley.

The cat was crying and meowing and when it sought refuge in its litter tray, Brown started dismantling the tray to get at it.

“Watching an innocent and defenceless animal suffering in that way is something I will never forget,” the woman said in a personal statement.

District judge Mark Daley told Brown: “Cruelty to an animal is not just cruelty to the animal, it is also cruelty to (the cat’s owner) because she cares for the animal."

Brown, of Sandstone Grove, Whitby, pleaded guilty to two charges of causing unnecessary suffering to a cat, harassment between February 13 and June 3 and failure to attend his sentencing hearing.

When he was arrested on warrant, the court heard he had got his dates mixed up.

He was given a 29-week prison sentence suspended for 12 months on condition he does 20 days’ rehabilitation activities and must wear a GPS tag informing the authorities of his whereabouts at all times for six months.

He was banned from having any involvement with animals for five years and made subject to a five-year restraining order banning him from contacting his now former girlfriend, going near her home or place or work and from contacting her employers.

He must pay a £187 statutory surcharge, £85 prosecution costs and the cat’s £83.46 vet bill.

Ms Whiteley said the woman, fearing that Brown would go on to harm a human being, ended the relationship but Brown harassed her for four months, turning up at her place of work where she hid in a bathroom while her colleagues dealt with him, waiting outside for her to leave and go to her car, setting up fake social media profiles in her name which said insulting things about her and making false allegations about her via email to her boss.

He continued to harass her despite being arrested and put on bail with conditions aimed at protecting her from him.

The woman is now preparing to move house because she fears for her safety at the hands of Brown, said Ms Whiteley.

In a personal statement she said every time she thought the harassment was over, Brown had found another way to continue it.

She no longer feels safe and the quality of her life has “changed dramatically,” she said.

“I will continue to live with the consequences of his actions every day,” she said.

Brown represented himself and said he had never done anything like his offences before.
