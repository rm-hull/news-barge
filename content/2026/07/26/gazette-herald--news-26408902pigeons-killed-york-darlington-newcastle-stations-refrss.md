MORE than 600 birds have been shot at York railway station, according to new data obtained through a freedom of information request.

According to new data - which was sourced directly from York-based train provider LNER (London North East Railway) under the Freedom of Information Act, 806 birds were culled in their stations since the beginning to last year.

Of the 11 stations managed by LNER, three – York, Newcastle, and Darlington – saw pest control experts visit in an effort to reduce the bird population – something that the train provider says is a last resort option.

In York, 608 birds were shot on 11 dates between January 2025 and March 2026 with 152 of the birds ‘culled’ on a single day - January 26, 2025.

In Darlington, 36 pigeons were killed on one specific day – September 17, 2025 – and Newcastle saw a total 162 birds shot on five occasions between June 2025 and February 2026.

  An LNER spokesperson said**:** “The safety of our customers and colleagues is our number one priority. Pigeons can pose a safety risk within stations, with significant amounts of bird droppings creating slip risks in areas used by the public, and the birds also presenting a risk to public health and railway infrastructure. 

"Specialist teams use a number of humane deterrent methods to respond to pigeons at stations where numbers become unmanageable. These methods are exhausted before any further ways of managing populations are implemented to protect customer safety and the rail network.”
