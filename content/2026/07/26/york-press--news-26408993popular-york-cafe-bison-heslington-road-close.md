## 'A sad loss' – Popular York coffee spot loved by locals is set to close

Nicholas West at Bison cafe on Heslington Road which will close on August 6. Photo - York Press

A YORK cafe with an outdoor seating dubbed a "locals' favourite" is set to close in a matter of weeks.

Bison in Heslington Road will shut its doors on August 6.

Owner Nicholas West said it was with a mix of "relief and sadness" that he was selling the business.

He took over the ownership of the popular coffee spot three years ago; it first opened in 2012 as a cafe-cum-vintage shop.

Nicholas told The Press the new owner was likely to change the cafe's name from Bison and give the business a rebrand.

  Nicholas West at Bison cafe on Heslington Road which will close on August 6. Photo - York Press


And he revealed another reason for shutting up shop – he is returning to his previous career as a teacher.

He admitted trading had been tough, revealing he did not pay himself a wage and, like many small businesses, was going from day to day hoping to just break even.

"What you earn in one day goes to pay for the next," he said.

Customers of the cafe took to social media to share their sadness at the news.

Posting on Instagram Tim Farnhill wrote: "Very fond memories of the best coffee in York. A sad loss."

Another fan wrote: "This makes me sad, Bison was such a great place when I lived across the road."

While another posted: "My favourite spot to hang with my housemate and enjoy a coffee and a cigarette when I lived on Willis Street."

  Bison cafe on Heslington Road which will close on August 6. Photo - York Press


On Bison's Instagram page, Nicholas has been backing top chef Tom Kerridge's campaign to halve VAT in the hospitality sector to 10 per cent.

And while he welcomed news from Andy Burnham that some hospitality business were to receive a business rates cut, he wanted the new Prime Minister to do more.

"The government has got to support more independent businesses on the high street.

"There needs to be a rebalance and a level playing field between majors like Starbucks and Costa and places like Bison.

"Three cafes a day are going under in Britain."

He would like to see bigger companies paying more tax.

Small cafes like Bison have had to swallow a huge rise in costs in recent years, from fuel to food. Without the bulk- buying powers of major brands, it's a struggle, he said.

"Starbucks and Costa can buy coffee for £10-£15 a kilo, but I'm paying £35-£40. And yet I have to be competitive and so charge the same price or less than the majors."

  Nicholas West at Bison cafe on Heslington Road which will close on August 6. Photo - York Press


As an indication of the impact of food-price inflation, Nicholas said: "Bagels are 70 per cent of my food offering here: when I first opened, a pack of six cost 59p. Now they cost 99p.

"Add that increase to every ingredient and if I was to reflect that in my prices I would drive the customers away."

Before opening Bison, Nicholas worked part time as a teacher and also ran the popular YO31 Wood Fired Pizza, selling pizzas from a vintage horse box, often stationed in the Coppergate Centre courtyard.

He said besides returning to teaching he also planned to revive a private catering business.

Reflecting on his time at Bison he said: "It was a passion of mine to run a coffee shop."
