# Andy Burnham to allow mayors to keep portion of income tax to spend in region

## The new Prime Minister has made devolution a major focus, and believes that handing down powers to local and regional governments will help restore faith in politics and drive economic growth.

Andy Burnham is set to announce that mayors will be able to retain a portion of income tax and spend it on their regions, which could boost major projects like West Yorkshire mass transit.

The new Prime Minister has made devolution a major focus, and believes that handing down powers to local and regional governments will help restore faith in politics and drive economic growth.

Mr Burnham will make the announcement on Friday, according to The Times, alongside handing mayors significant new spending powers to boost investment and infrastructure.

He is expected to mirror the plans unveiled by former chancellor Rachel Reeves in her Mais lecture earlier this year.

The Leeds West and Pudsey MP announced that mayors would be able to collect taxes and spend them on their own regions, but, besides the overnight visitor levy, not set them themselves.

This was designed to end the so-called “begging bowl” culture, where mayors have to go to individual departments to ask for money for individual projects.

The mayor has staked her reputation on the success of the £2.5bn project, which would connect Leeds and Bradford as well as Elland Road and the White Rose Centre.

The project has hit a few bumps, with the proposed completion date being pushed back to the late 2030s after Whitehall told the West Yorkshire Combined Authority to change its process.

The Department for Transport is also insisting that Ms Brabin puts together a business case for a bus network as well as trams, before it approves the most cost effective proposal.

Sir Keir Starmer’s administration made it easier for mayors to approve mass transit projects, however Mr Burnham is expected to go further.

A government source told The Times: “For too long, governments have acted as if the answer to every problem sits somewhere in a Whitehall filing cabinet.

“Power should sit as close as possible to the people it affects, because the people best placed to solve a problem are usually the people living with it every single day.”

Ms Rayner explained: “In Greater Manchester, Andy saw that where he got powers and real devolution he was able to drive that economic change that we needed.

“That’s around transport, it’s around housing, it’s around education, it’s around employment.

“When you can have those levers close to where it’s happening to people.”

Ms Rayner is expected to work from the site regularly, alongside Mr Burnham’s de facto deputy, Sheffield Heeley MP and First Secretary of State Louise Haigh.

The UK is one of the fiscally major economies in the world, according to the Centre for Cities.

Anthony Breach, director of policy and research at the think tank, said: “Income tax sharing is excellent news for the national economy. Cities have been underpowered for decades, in large part because even if they grow their local economy they don't get to retain any of the revenue.

“The UK is an extreme outlier in this regard - there is no similarly large economy that is as centralised.

“With this move the Prime Minister will hand mayors a powerful financial incentive that rewards building homes, investing in infrastructure and supporting businesses.

“It is crucial for helping cities work as engines of growth and opportunity.”
