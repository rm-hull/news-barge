‘Rebel teacher’ to contest Victorian education minister’s seat in state election

A group of self-described “rebel teachers” in Victoria are running a campaign to target eight sitting Labor MPs, including the minister for education, Ben Carroll, in November’s state election.

The candidates are members of the “Socialists in Schools” group, in opposition to the department of education. Victorian Socialists have announced they are aiming to contest every lower house seat in Victoria.

Ben Carroll. Photograph: Luis Ascui/AAP

Brendan Laws is going up against Carroll in his formerly safe seat of Niddrie, calling for the ending of public funding to high-fee private schools, the abolition of Naplan and full funding for uniforms, excursions, materials and lunches.

Laws said the Victorian school system had been underfunded for years.

There’s a consensus that public education deserves better. On the issue of education, the ALP is not just out of touch – they’re on the other side of the picket line, with the Liberals and One Nation.

Economic segregation is increasing, as working-class students are more and isolated while high-fee private schools get more and more funding each year. This must end immediately.

Fuel excise relief extension unlikely as war threatens price hike

The US-Israeli war with Iran will probably drive petrol prices above $ 2 a litre in the coming weeks, but the government has no plans to extend its fuel excise relief when it ends in a week.

AAP reports:

Fuel excise relief for motorists is unlikely to be extended despite the war in the Middle East flaring up.

The Labor government halved the fuel excise for three months from April in response to rising prices as a result of the US-Israeli war with Iran, saving drivers 32 cents a litre on fuel.

The measure was extended for a further month in July, but at a reduced rate of 16 cents per litre.

The extension is due to lapse on 2 August.

Vessels in the strait of Hormuz seen from the beach at Bandar Abbas, Iran. Photograph: Majid-Asgaripour/Reuters

Fresh tensions between the US and Iran have caused Brent crude oil prices to rise to more than $US100 a barrel, but Anthony Albanese has indicated continued excise relief is not on the cards.

“That’s not in our planning at this point in time,” the prime minister told ABC’s Insiders program on Sunday.

“(Fuel) usage has come down and is indeed lower than it was a year ago as well, but importantly as well, we have kept supply going.”

Albanese indicated some fuel-related measures were in the pipeline.

“We’ll have some more announcements about fuel in the coming days and weeks as well,” he said.

“We’ll continue to examine things that were in that process in the budget.”

Drivers have been warned petrol prices could reach more than $2 a litre as the excise discount is wound back.

Average prices for unleaded petrol have climbed to more than $1.80 per litre in all state capitals in recent days, up from about $1.50 at the end of June.

The jump in oil prices comes as Iran-aligned Houthi militants fired on Saudi oil installations on the coast of the Red Sea and Tehran attempted to shut down shipments through the strait of Hormuz again.

US strikes have recommenced in response to Iranian attacks on vessels travelling through the strait, a key oil transit route.

Albanese said a continuation of the conflict would lead to economic pressure.

“The war is having an impact here in Australia and the longer it goes, the bigger the impact will be,” he said.

“This is a war on the other side of the world.

“We are not protagonists in it, but we are impacted by it, and we’ve consistently called for a de-escalation.”

‘Awkward’ selection drama won’t derail Aussie sprinters

Rohan Browning has put the Commonwealth Games track selection drama in the rear vision mirror as he seeks to spearhead Australia’s sprint charge in Glasgow.

The 28-year-old former national champion was picked alongside the current title holder, Lachie Kennedy, and Kiwi defector Eddie Nketia in a three-pronged 100m assault.

Rohan Browning. Photograph: Joel Carrett/AAP

It begins on Monday at Scotstoun Stadium, with the men’s 100m heats among the first events on the Games athletics program before nine finals are contested in the evening session. Australia’s 100m selection call sparked a challenge from Josh Azzopardi, whose appeal, heard by the National Sports Tribunal, was ultimately unsuccessful.

Azzopardi said he felt “absolutely robbed” by the decision, but hoped it wouldn’t dent the mood in an Australian camp eyeing unprecedented track success at the Games. It appears there are no hard feelings from Browning, who is adamant the saga hasn’t dampened his relationship with Azzopardi.

The pair are both part of Australia’s five-man 4x100m relay squad in Glasgow, and worked together at training camps in Italy and Spain in the lead-up to the Games. “The nature of going through an appeal process can make this feel a little personal, but ultimately it’s not,” Browning told AAP.

Labor under pressure to allow telehealth for euthanasia but PM opposed

Labor will come under pressure to implement changes to its national policy platform and allow Australians to access voluntary assisted dying through telehealth services, with the Greens and independent MP Kate Chaney to introduce legislation.

This week’s Labor national conference in Adelaide changed the party’s platform and committed to overturn a Howard-era law banning people from having medical appointments regarding euthanasia via telehealth services.

New language added to the platform suggests Labor MPs would have a conscience vote on the issue, but the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, said on Sunday the government might not progress laws through parliament.

Rate watchers eye inflation data and RBA chief’s speech

Economists are awaiting key quarterly inflation data but expect the Reserve Bank to hold interest rates despite renewed fighting in the Middle East sending oil prices up again. The Australian Bureau of Statistics will publish the consumer price index’s second quarter on Wednesday, which shows household inflation.

The Reserve Bank governor, Michele Bullock, will also speak in Sydney on Tuesday, with her speech to be closely watched for indicators on how the central bank intends to approach inflation risks.

Michele Bullock. Photograph: Dean Lewins/AAP

Taylor Nugent, a NAB senior economist, says the bank does not believe another rate rise is likely in 2026 despite oil prices rising again due to tensions between the US and Iran.

Our position is there will be enough confidence in growth slowing in the labour market so that rates don’t need to be tightened further.

That’s despite risks such as elevated inflation, renewed cost pressures and new oil price levels. The Reserve Bank’s board will next meet in more than two weeks, with the Commonwealth Bank noting the “backdrop shifting rapidly”.

The housing market has deteriorated, household spending is slowing from the elevated pace seen earlier this year and businesses’ capacity to pass on higher costs is likely weaker following three interest rate hikes.

The Reserve Bank was expected to keep rates on hold for the remainder of 2026, the CBA said.

Investigation into police shooting after bystander allegedly hit with stray bullet in Queensland

An internal police investigation has been launched after a bystander was allegedly shot during a tense standoff between officers and an armed offender, AAP reports.

The woman was one of two people allegedlyshot by police in Cairns, in north Queensland, on Saturday night. Ethical standards are investigating the incident after a woman was allegedly hit by a stray bullet when officers opened fire on an armed offender.

Police had been called to Benjamina Street in the southern Cairns suburb of Mount Sheridan, about 5.10pm on Saturday after reports of a man armed with knives and an attempted armed robbery. They found the 28-year-old man at a roundabout and shot him.

The state’s police minister, Dan Purdie, was reluctant to comment in detail on the shooting as it was under investigation, but said he had seen footage. He told reporters on Sunday:

They are highly trained. They do have equipment, and I’m sure some of those operational matters have probably all been answered by the police this morning. I acknowledge the situations, the dangerous situations, police have put themselves in.

It’s the second high-profile Queensland police shooting in three days. It follows the shooting of a police officer and the death of a man after a domestic dispute at Yengarie, near Maryborough, on the state’s Fraser Coast, on Friday evening.

The ethical standards command is investigating the incident, with oversight from the Crime and Corruption Commission, and it will be reported to the coroner.
