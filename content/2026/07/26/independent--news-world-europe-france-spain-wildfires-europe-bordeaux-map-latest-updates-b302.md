A man has died in a wildfire in Manises, near the Spanish city of Valencia, the country's interior ministry has said.

The man was found in a car in a ravine hundreds of kilometres away from the severe wildfires spreading near to the Spanish capital Madrid.

It comes as wildfires in Europe have displaced about 280,000 people this week, including some 70,000 people evacuated in central Spain.

Residents in western France have described the scenes as “totally apocalyptic” after fires in the village of Saumos destroyed more than 100 homes.

Spanish officials said that winds in Spain are to pick up with gusts of up to 60 kph (38 mph) affecting the inferno, complicating the emergency mission to contain the crisis.

On Saturday authorities ordered the evacuation of large parts of Bordeaux’s suburbs, including the areas of Le Haillan, Eysines and Merignac.

“The fires which are hitting our country have reached a level that has never been seen before,” French prime minister Sebastien Lecornu wrote on X on Saturday.

Wildfires are also affecting parts of Scotland with a major incident declared in Cairngorms National Park .

## Wildfire leads to evacuations in some suburbs of the French city of Bordeaux

Authorities in France evacuated some suburbs of Bordeaux on Saturday as a massive wildfire shifted toward the city famous for its wines.

The prefect of the Gironde region that includes Bordeaux ordered the evacuation in the early hours of Saturday, covering parts of three of the city’s western suburbs, including its airport.

## Restaurant owner says Bordeaux is 'full of smoke and ash'

A British restaurant owner in Bordeaux said the city is “full of smoke and ash” and residents are wearing masks if they go outside.

Lance Attwood, 58, who owns Sweeney Todd’s in Bordeaux city centre, told the Press Association: “It’s not really conducive to coming out.

“The town is full of smoke and ash, so there’s a big drop in business, people are just walking around town in masks.

“Our biggest concern is people’s safety and the safety of the emergency services.”

Mr Attwood, who has lived in Bordeaux for 25 years, said it was “too soon” for friends who had been evacuated to want to talk to the media about their experiences, with their focus instead on recovering sentimental belongings and finding alternative living arrangements.

He encouraged people to donate dry food with a long shelf-life to local charities to help those affected.

## Pictured: Cairngorms wildfire causes billowing smoke

A wildfire broke out in the Cairngorms National Park last week.

A fire near the village of Glenmore, led to residents and campsites being evacuated.

It covered an area of about six square kilometres of heather and small trees at its height.

More than 100 firefighters have been mobilised to the incident alongside specialist wildfire appliances, all-terrain vehicles and partner agencies.

Homes around Nethy Bridge were also issued a precautionary evacuation order on Friday night as the wildfire intensified.

## Residents told to return home after wildfire in Cairngorms National Park

Residents of Nethy Bridge have been told they can return to their homes, following a large fire in the Cairngorms National Park.

Superintendent Judy Hill, of Police Scotland, said: “Thanks to the efforts of fire crews, gamekeepers, contractors, partners and many others over the past 24 hours, there is no longer the immediate threat which prompted the evacuation of properties in Nethy Bridge.

“We are grateful to the community for their support and understanding in what has been a period of concern and disruption.”

First Minister John Swinney welcomed the news, saying: “Efforts to tackle the wildfire in the Cairngorms are in their tenth day, and changeable weather conditions have presented a range of challenges for those on the ground. I praise their resilience and tireless efforts to bring it under control.

“With residents in Nethy Bridge now able to return to their homes, Police Scotland have lifted the Major Incident declaration.

“I want to stress however, that the Scottish Government, the emergency services and all relevant authorities will continue to treat this wildfire, as has been the case since its onset, with the utmost seriousness and necessary resources until the last flames are out.”

## Germany sends military aircraft to help with wildfires in France

Germany is set to send military aircraft to help France respond to the wildfires, Chancellor Friedrich Merz has said.

A400 military aircraft, helicopters and rescue teams are on their way to France.

## Is it safe to travel to Spain and France? Updated advice as wildfires rage

Travel advice has been updated for Spain and France as wildfires rage through both countries.

The UK government is advising people in Spain to avoid travelling through affected areas of Madrid and Avila, while in France, people are being advised to follow the advice of local authorities and check with their accommodation or travel provider before travelling.

## UK travel advice for those affected by fires in Spain

The government website provides the following advice for those affected by wildfires in Madrid and Avila in Spain:

“Spanish authorities have declared a national emergency because of wildfires in parts of Madrid and Ávila. There are also reports of wildfires in other inland areas of Spain.

“If you are in an area affected by a wildfire:

- follow instructions from local authorities and emergency services at all times
- avoid travelling to or through affected areas
- check updates from the emergency services
- call 112 if you need emergency help.”
