Anthony Joshua flirted with disaster but came through a chaotic, albeit short, fight with Kristian Prenga in Saudi Arabia to finally set up a long-awaited clash with Tyson Fury.

Heavyweight star Joshua – making his comeback after surviving a fatal car crash in Nigeria, which claimed the lives of two of his teammates – was expected to brush aside Albanian unknown Prenga with ease at Jeddah Superdome but instead found himself on the canvas within 30 seconds as his unfancied, but heavy-hitting foe, floored the Brit with a devastating uppercut. Another scuffle shortly before the end of the first round then saw “AJ” hit the deck for a second time.

However, he somehow recovered in the time between the first two rounds, came out stronger in the second and promptly dropped Prenga through the ropes with a monstrous punch that saw the Albanian unable to get up, to secure a 27th KO of his career.

After the fight Joshua called out Fury, who one day earlier won his bizarre, non-televised warm-up against Mariusz Wach in Thailand via seventh-round TKO, but “the Gypsy King” wasn’t ringside in Saudi Arabia, meaning the build-up for their long-overdue fight later this year – which came so close to being derailed here – will have to wait.

## Bunce: Fury vs Joshua talk too early after warning signs

Independent columnist and boxing expert Steve Bunce believes there were warning signs from Joshua’s performance.

On BBC Radio 5’s coverage, Bunce stated: "As much as I want to say it's on, as much as I want to say we can move on to Tyson Fury, I think we need to look at that first round and look at how easily AJ was hurt. We can't just skip past that.

"Let's just be a little bit realistic here. If there was any fighter inside the top 100 in the ring with Joshua tonight, we would be reading and running over the end of his career."

## Fury vs Joshua - location a stumbling block?

We’ve been waiting for years for Anthony Joshua and Tyson Fury to share a ring, and it looks like we’re finally there. A deal has been agreed, both fighters have won their warm-up bouts - but there is still no concrete noise on the venue.

Surely an all-British heavyweight clash for the ages has to taken place at Wembley Stadium, or at least in the UK?

Eddie Hearn suggested prior to Joshua’s win over Prenga that the venue could still be a stumbling block, citing pressure for an American-friendly ring-walk time in other interviews.

## How emotional Anthony Joshua survived the chaos to set up Tyson Fury showdown

Anthony Joshua survived a huge scare on a night of carnage on his boxing comeback, rising from the canvas twice before knocking out unknown Albanian Kristian Prenga to pave the way for a Tyson Fury showdown.

Joshua was shockingly sent crashing to the canvas by a thunderous right uppercut within the first 20 seconds in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, as Prenga threatened to ruin the prospect of a long-awaited heavyweight showdown with Fury later this year.

The former two-time world heavyweight champion also took a knee later in the first round in his first fight since being involved in a car crash in Nigeria that killed two of his close friends, Sina Ghami and Latif ‘Latz’ Ayodele, in December, just days after his win over YouTuber-turned-boxer Jake Paul.

However, Joshua showed all his heart to dig deep and hit back in the second round with a barrage of heavy blows that sent Prenga tumbling through the ropes and the Albanian’s inability to beat the count ensured a 27th knockout victory of the Brit’s career.

## Emotional AJ subdued when thinking about the past seven months

Seven months ago Anthony Joshua was involved in a car crash that took the lives of his two close friends Sina Ghami and Latif “Latz” Ayodele.

Asked how difficult the last seven months has been and if felt the fighting spirit of his friends on **DAZN**, Joshua musters in response: “It’s been tough. They’re my brothers, but I don't want to talk about it.”

## 'He's got a bright future' - Joshua's praise for Prenga

He’s come through unscathed, but Anthony Joshua looked lost in that first round. He credits opponent Kristian Prenga. AJ tells **DAZN**: “I also want to give a massive thank you to Kristian Prenga and his team for putting on a good fight. It means a lot to them, and it means a lot to me.

“I think Eddie Hearn wants to sign him now. He’s going to do well, he’s got a bright future. I’m a serious fighter, I know what I’m doing. I know my way around the ring.

“Maybe with a little bit more experience he will be up there with the rest of us. But he’s a serious fighter, strong, durable, and he come and gave me a good test.”

## Hearn: Fury vs Joshua is signed and 'done'

Anthony Joshua has navigated what should be the final warm-up fight before he faces Tyson Fury. Matchroom Boxing promoter Eddie Hearn believes the fight is all wrapped up - but believes no one should look past Joshua tonight.

He tells **DAZN**: “We've signed a contract, everything's done. But to be honest with you, tonight is all about the strength that he showed to even be here tonight. 

And the strength that he had to show in that fight, you know? I mean, no one can imagine what he's been through this last seven months.”
