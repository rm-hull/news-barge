!["Future looking droughtful for baking Britain," reads the headline on the front page of the Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/c37b/live/7494f7a0-8b8f-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

Several papers lead with declarations of drought across the UK, the third in five years. "Future looking droughtful for baking Britain", says the Metro, which reports that dry weather has impacted "more than half of the country", with millions more warned this is a "new normal". It also cites forecasts that it could be the "driest July on record", with England having had "just 7% of its expected rainfall" for the month.

!["Take a shorter shower to save water in drought, public urged," reads the headline on the front page of the i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/8b82/live/8e95eb50-8b8f-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

The i Paper leads with official advice to "take a shorter shower to save water in drought" under a new Environmental Agency plea to "save every drop". The agency warns that households are "using water faster than nature can replenish it", as experts caution "another year of dry weather may trigger formal water rationing measures".

!["Scorched earth Britain," reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/956/cpsprodpb/0b4e/live/70de7f50-8b8f-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

Parched brown fields cover the front page of the Independent under the headline "Scorched earth Britain", with the paper noting that Wisely in Surrey has seen "43 days with no rain".

!["How's that plan going, PM? Dinghy flotilla in channel" reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/38e7/live/9fdd2640-8b93-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

The Sun leads with reports of a "dinghy flotilla in Channel", writing that the boats carried "up to 1,000 migrants headed for Britain". Next to a photo of Prime Minister Andy Burnham, the paper asks: "How's that plan going, PM?" It adds that French authorities "admit waving migrants through", despite receiving "more than £1billion from UK taxpayers to stop them".

!["Still say your boats plan is working, Andy?" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/5008/live/92dd3520-8b93-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

"Still say your boats plan is working, Andy?" continues the Daily Mail, after Burnham says the government's plan to stop small boats is "working".

!["Dying tell PM: We don't have time for these delays," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/5b03/live/54814c20-8b8f-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

"Campaigners devastated after Burnham says law on assisted deaths must wait," is the lead for the Daily Express. Those impacted by the hold up to assisted dying legislation say: "We don't have time for these delays." Elsewhere, "Zendaya wows with her Spider-Man" is written next to a photo of the actress on the red carpet for the new Marvel movie.

!["Get tax rebate from your mayor," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/91fb/live/4edb94b0-8b94-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

"Get tax rebate from your mayor," leads the Times, which reports that "local leaders may hand revenue back to residents under PM's radical devolution plans". It quotes Burnham previously describing the UK as "one of the most overcentralised countries in the world", as data shows that local authorities "receive just 5 per cent of the tax revenue raised in their areas, while 95 per cent goes directly to the Treasury".

!["Calls grow for England to boycott World Cup," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2048/cpsprodpb/344c/live/97729bc0-8b93-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

Following Fifa boss Gianni Infantino's "£15bn sell-off" plans, the Daily Telegraph reports "calls grow for England to boycott World Cup". The paper refers to pressure on the FA after "England's governing body joined the Prime Minister and three other federations in criticising the plan". The proposal would involve selling World Cup stakes "to private investors, including a member of Donald Trump's family".

!["Fed holds rates despite fears of price pressures from renewed Iran conflict," reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2048/cpsprodpb/3d81/live/5aacbc10-8b8f-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

"Fed holds rates despite fears of price pressures from renewed Iran conflict" writes the Financial Times. The move "defies markets", although it says the "central bank hinted that rate rises could come as soon as September as a rapid rise in energy prices threatens to morph into a broader bout of inflation".

!["PM Andy cheers up Fiona," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/5c86/live/4f5ec0b0-8b8f-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

"PM Andy cheers up Fiona" writes the Daily Star, featuring a new photo of the retired broadcaster Fiona Phillips, who has Alzheimer's, shared by her husband Martin Frizzell. The paper quotes Frizzell saying the prime minister's social care speech "cheered up our morning".

!["Inside funeral horror home: Where are our babies?" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/69a8/live/4bd48880-8b8f-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

The Daily Mirror leads with pleas from four "grieving mums", who ask: "Where are our babies?". The paper writes that it comes after the remains of their children were treated with "callous disregard" at the hands of funeral home director Robert Bush, who admitted to 67 offences, including failing to bury and cremate people, and giving out wrong ashes.

!["Cottrell transferred $2m to mother days before her Reform donations," reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/787b/live/1efe6910-8b9a-11f1-b2ab-0dd01740f9f6.jpg)

"Burnham puts Trident at heart of UK renewal" says the Guardian, writing that the new prime minister "intends to emphasise that defence spending on projects such as the £31bn Dreadnought-class vessels built in the Cumbrian town is a case study of taxpayer investment revitalising communities around the UK".
