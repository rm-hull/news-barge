Some love stories are built on fairy tales – Johnny Cash and June Carter’s wasn’t one of them. It was messy, complicated, heartbreaking and hopeful in equal measure, and as *The Ballad of Johnny and June* takes to the Grand Opera House stage this week, it embraces every note of it.

From the opening bars, the production immediately showcases the exceptional musicianship that remains at its heart, thanks to musical director Connagh Tonkinson and company. As the washboard, autoharp and unmistakable country sound emerge, we’re transported straight into the world of Cash and Carter. Before a word is spoken, the music has already done its job of setting the scene.

And as our renowned duo make their entrance to warm applause, Christopher Ryan Grant and Christina Bianco are instantly recognisable in these iconic roles. Whilst the sound balance between the live band and vocals wasn’t always as polished as it could have been, it somehow added a raw authenticity, making it feel less like a refined tribute concert and more like stepping into a moment in history.

The cast remain present on stage, seamlessly stepping into the many figures who shaped Cash and Carter’s lives. The multi-roling is slick and effective, ensuring every significant character has their place without ever slowing the pace. Years pass, relationships evolve and careers soar, yet the transitions remain effortless.

Whilst Des McAnuff and Robert Cary’s book flows beautifully, and the musicianship soars, so too does the emotion. Johnny and June may be remembered foremost for the songs they created, but this production never lets us forget the often turbulent journey behind them.

Ryan O’Donnell takes on the role of John Carter Cash, narrating the story through the eyes of the couple’s son. There’s an immediate warmth to his performance as he breaks the fourth wall to share memories, observations and heartfelt anecdotes, quickly building a genuine rapport with the audience. It feels less like we’re watching a narrator and more like we’re being trusted with family stories. His storytelling also allows the production a little creative licence, weaving numbers such as the iconic ‘A Boy Named Sue’ into the narrative despite not being chronologically accurate. It doesn’t matter; it works, and it delivers one of the evening’s standout moments.

Whilst Johnny Cash naturally commands centre stage with classics including ‘Ring of Fire’, ‘I Walk the Line’ and ‘Folsom Prison Blues’, musical excellence isn’t limited to one performer. Special mention must go to The Carter Family, whose rich harmonies beautifully capture the unmistakable sound that helped shape country music.

Grant delivers a convincing Johnny Cash, capturing both the familiar mannerisms and the darker struggles that shadowed his success. His portrayal of Cash’s dependence on pills is handled with sensitivity, culminating in a haunting rendition of ‘Hurt’ that leaves a shared silence hanging across the auditorium. Alongside him, Christina Bianco’s June Carter is every bit the firecracker history remembers, quick-witted, fiercely optimistic and overflowing with charisma. Together, the pair share a believable chemistry that anchors the entire production.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/219_TBOJJ_PAMELA-RAITH-PHOTOGRAPHY.webp)

The staging itself remains refreshingly simple. There are no elaborate scene changes or grand theatrical flourishes because they simply aren’t needed. The focus stays firmly where it belongs, on the performers, the music and the story. Props are utilised effortlessly between scenes, supporting rather than distracting from the narrative.

One of the production’s greatest strengths is how naturally the songs are woven into the storytelling. Rather than feeling like a greatest hits concert, every musical number serves a purpose, seamlessly stitched into the fabric of the narrative to move both the story and the characters forward.

At its heart, this is a love story – but not one wrapped up in neat, perfect endings. Instead, it reminds us that sometimes the happiest chapters come somewhere in the middle. As the production gently tells us, ‘You love with everything you have, because it all passes in the blink of an eye.’ If there’s one message audiences leave with, it’s surely that.

The evening concludes with deserved applause and a welcome reprise of ‘Ring of Fire’, finally inviting the audience to join in after two hours of happily resisting the temptation.

*The Ballad of Johnny and June* lets the music lead the story. Honest, heartfelt and full of authenticity, it never shies away from the imperfections that made Johnny and June’s relationship so compelling. 

Fans of the legendary couple will leave satisfied by a production that celebrates not just the songs, but the people behind them.
