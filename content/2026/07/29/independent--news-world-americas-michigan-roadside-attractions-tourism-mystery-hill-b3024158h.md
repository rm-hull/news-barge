Nathan Wakefield, a Michigan native who grew up visiting the vibrant roadside attractions of the Irish Hills, has purchased Mystery Hill, a beloved landmark known for its gravity-defying house. His acquisition aims to preserve a piece of "southern Michigan’s vacation land" that has largely faded into history.

For decades, the region along US 12 was a bustling tourist destination, featuring attractions where children could explore prehistoric forests or witness Western-themed scenes. However, most of these stops in Jackson and Lenawee counties have since disappeared, casualties of shifting tourism trends and a significant decline in traffic after I-94 rerouted motorists away from what was once a primary route between Detroit and Chicago.

![Nathan Wakefield, the new owner of Mystery Hill, swallows a sword during a pop-up carnival at the roadside attraction Onsted, Mich., on Saturday, July 18, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/29/20/19/AP26210662549390.jpg)

Wakefield, who grew up about 20 miles south of the Irish Hills, hopes to encourage "generational nostalgia" with his purchase. He expressed his desire to prevent Mystery Hill from suffering the same fate as other attractions that have fallen into disrepair or closed their doors. "I’m just elated to be a part of this ongoing legacy," Wakefield told Bridge Michigan.

Mystery Hill stands as one of the few remaining roadside attractions in the Irish Hills, a region that continues to draw visitors for its natural beauty and the Michigan International Speedway.

Other once-popular spots, like Frontier City and Stagecoach Stop, which featured banks, blacksmiths, and saloons reminiscent of Western films, closed in 1974 and 2007, respectively. The dinosaur-themed Prehistoric Forest also met its demise in 2002, with a subsequent restoration attempt failing, and the property now houses the Irish Hills Print Co.

Michelle McLemore, a historian for Cambridge Township in the Irish Hills, notes that viable roadside attractions today offer "full-body experiences like putt-putt golf or go-kart racing" – activities people "can’t do virtually."

Mystery Hill fits this description, she explained: "At Mystery Hill, you can … go inside, explore the building to see and feel the craziness with the gravity." McLemore attributes Mystery Hill's longevity partly to its lower overhead, requiring fewer employees and food vendors compared to larger attractions.

Wakefield’s journey to ownership began last year when he revisited Mystery Hill and was struck by its remarkably preserved condition. "I left thinking, ‘Wow, it’d be nice to own something like this,’" he recalled. "I’m surprised it’s in such good condition. I’m glad somebody swooped in and kind of kept the legacy going."

That "somebody" was Dirk Dole of California, who purchased Mystery Hill in 2022, adding an oddities museum and a gemstone mining experience. Wakefield described Dole’s business model: "He’ll buy these roadside attractions, fix them up, and flip them, and then sell them to locals that are really passionate and can run them."

For Wakefield, a professional sideshow artist for 15 years, performing stunts like lying on a bed of nails and eating fire, the acquisition felt like a natural fit. "Between my business acumen as well as my show business background, it just felt like a total perfect match," he said.

He plans to continue the famous gravitational house tours that have made Mystery Hill a destination for generations, while also transforming the property into a broader family entertainment hub, potentially incorporating some of his unique performance experiences.

Wakefield has observed the powerful draw of nostalgia firsthand: "So many people show up with their kids or their grandkids, and they say, ‘Man, I remember coming here when I was a kid, and so I just had to bring my kids or my grandkids to check it out.’"

This emphasis on family experiences and nostalgia aligns with the messaging from Pure Michigan, the state’s tourism arm. Kelly Wolgamott, Vice President, stated, "As new generations of travelers are looking to both create new memories and revisit familiar stops when planning their vacations, attractions like Mystery Hill in Irish Hills add to the unique yet nostalgic experiences that are waiting to be discovered throughout Michigan’s two peninsulas."
