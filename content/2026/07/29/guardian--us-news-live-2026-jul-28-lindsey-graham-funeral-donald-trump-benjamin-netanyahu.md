Ossoff cites 'massive new tariff authorities' for Trump to explain vote against Russia sanctions bill

Senator Jon Ossoff, the Georgia Democrat, was one of 12 senators to vote against advancing the measure to sanction Russia and Iran on Tuesday.

A spokesperson for Ossoff explained why: he opposes the expansive new tariff authority the Senate bill would give to Donald Trump.

“Senator Ossoff continues to support Ukraine’s valiant self-defense against intolerable Russian brutality and aggression,” the spokesperson told NBC News. “He does not support the provisions of this bill that delegate massive new tariff authorities to a president who has demonstrated he will wield tariffs recklessly and destructively.”

The other 11 senators to vote against advancing the bill were 10 who caucus with Democrats, including Peter Welch, who plans an amendment to limit the tariff authority, and one Republican, Rand Paul.

Democratic senator wants to strike broad tariff authority from Russia sanctions bill

Immediately after the US Senate overwhelmingly voted to advance legislation sanctioning Russia and Iran, Peter Welch, a Vermont Democrat, explained why he had changed his vote to oppose the revised version of the Senate bill. The latest version of the bill, Welch said, gives far too much power to Donald Trump to impose tariffs on other countries.

“From the first day of Putin’s savage attack on Ukraine, no one has been a more stalwart defender of Ukraine, of Ukrainian victory, than our departed colleague, senator Lindsey Graham,” Welch said. “He and Senator Blumenthal had a compelling insight: you hurt Russia by going after the buyers of Russian oil. And the way to go after the buyers was by imposing a tariff on the major buyers of Russian oil, making it too costly for those buyers to continue purchasing it. It would starve the Russian beast of the revenue fueling its cruel aggression.”

“The language in that original bill by senator Blumenthal and by senator Graham was specific and limited,” Welch said. “It was a limited and specific delegation of congressional tariff authority that could be used for the limited and sole purpose of cutting off oil revenue to Russia, all for the purpose of assisting US efforts to help defend Ukrainian freedom and repel Russian aggression.”

The revised Graham-Blumenthal bill, Welch said, does two things: “No 1, it imposes tariffs at the discretion of the executive of up to 100% on the top five purchasers of Russian oil and gas. No 2, it allows the executive to impose 100% tariffs on the top five countries ‘facilitating’ the evasion of sanctions on Russian oil.”

“There is a fatal absence of specificity as to what countries could be considered facilitators or what conduct or the extent of that conduct that could constitute being a facilitator,” Welch said. “This language is very general. In effect, this would be a blanket delegation of congressional tariff authority to the executive, all in the name of targeting a facilitator that the executive could target in tariff any country for any reason.”

The senator then said that he would be proposing an amendment to the current bill before it comes to a final vote.

“I will offer an amendment to strike the overly vague delegation of tariff authorities to also target ‘facilitators’ of sanctions evasions,” Welch said. “As currently written, the facilitator provision enables the executive with full congressional authorization, to impose any tariffs on any country for any reason, so long as the executive labels the tariff country a facilitator.”

Zelenskyy had previously urged senators to support the bill before the vote, despite Democratic reservations that the legislation, as written, would give Donald Trump expansive new powers to waive sanctions on Russians and impose tariffs on “countries that purchase Russian-origin crude oil or natural gas or facilitate sanctions evasion”.

Aaron Fritschner, an aide to Democratic congressman Don Beyer of Virginia, argued on social media that the legislation in the Senate bill gives the president too much discretion to waive sanctions and impose tariffs.

“The bill has a huge new tariff authority with no expiration, delay, or oversight mechanism,” Fritschner wrote before the Senate vote.“The optional sanctions take up most of the text, but the tariffs are the key policy ingredient and more importantly they are the reason why Republicans and especially the president want this bill to pass.”

“There is no limitation, the president can decide which countries this bill applies and hit them with a 100% tariff for five years,” he adds. “Historically, tariff provisions required either long investigations in advance, consultation with Congress, or imposed short, limited durations. This one does not.”

“Trump has abused every single major tariff authority!” Fritschner notes. “If you give Trump a tariff authority he WILL abuse it. That’s what he does, it’s all he does.”

According to Fritschner, Democrats all want to help Ukraine: is significant even if Trump twists it later. The good news is the House passed a much better bill – the Ukraine Support Act.”

“The Ukraine Support Act has proactive support for Ukraine, in addition to sanctions on Russia that are written better, and no new tariffs power,” he adds. “If they pass this bill, Senate Dems will be handing Trump new, massive, unchecked tariff powers in the hope that he will use them against Russia, to help Ukraine, and won’t abuse it in ways that raise Americans’ prices or enrich his own family. Does that sound like a good bet to you?”

Senator Ron Wyden says Jay Clayton will be Trump's 'election conspiracy wingman' as intelligence director

Donald Trump is pushing the longtime vaccine opponent and current health secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr to be more aggressive on cutting vaccines in the US, according to reporting from the Wall Street Journal.

Trump has a long record of anti-vaccine statements, but the White House recently put a hold on vaccine changes after polling indicated they were a losing issue in the midterm elections.

Kennedy, the secretary of the US Department of Health and Human Services, recently slashed the routine childhood immunization schedule in an unprecedented move.

Trump reportedly asked Kennedy at his northern Virginia golf course in May if the health secretary had the “yips”, according to the Journal.

Trump wasn’t talking about a sudden inability to make putts. Kennedy wasn’t making enough cuts to the US vaccination schedule, Trump reportedly said.

The US president hopes to make “alleviating autism” a component of his legacy, the Journal reported – and he has taken issue with Kennedy’s less aggressive changes to vaccination recommendations in recent months.

“Donald Trump has been anti-vaccine at least since 2015,” said Paul Offit, director of the Vaccine Education Center and an infectious diseases physician at Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia. At the Republican primary debate that year, Trump said that autism had gotten “totally out of control” and vaccines should be in “smaller doses over a longer period of time”.

Trump regularly posted on social media about debunked links between vaccination and autism as early as 2014, and his posts on Truth Social have recently advocated for breaking up shots into individual components and delaying some childhood vaccines for more than a decade.

Trump administration plans to end Medicare subsidy that holds down drug costs for seniors - report

The Trump administration plans to stop paying subsidies to health insurance companies that helped to lower premiums for Medicare drug plans, the Wall Street Journal reports.

The change will stop the estimated $3.6bn in subsidies that went to insurance companies this year, making the cost of prescription drug plans, known as Part D, more expensive for seniors starting next year, according to administration officials.

About 25 million Americans have Part D plans, but the change will not take effect until after the upcoming midterm elections.
