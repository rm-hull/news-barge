The US military has announced that it intercepted Iran’s latest “surprise attack” following US President Donald Trump’s meeting with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu Tuesday.

“At 5:45 p.m. ET today, Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps forces launched multiple ballistic missiles from Iran in an attempted surprise attack on U.S. forces based in the Middle East. All Iranian missiles were successfully intercepted”, US Central Command wrote on X.

CENTCOM later said the Saudi Arabian Armed Forces aided the US military in a separate military effort Tuesday after “Iran-aligned” terrorists launched more than 30 drone attacks in the last 72 hours.

The US military accused Iran’s paramilitary of directing terrorists to attack American forces and Saudi energy infrastructure.

“U.S. and Saudi fighter aircraft struck multiple terrorist logistics and weapons sites across eastern Iraq in a strong response” to the Iran-directed attacks, CENTCOM said.

Earlier, Netanyahu went to the White House for a closed-door talk with Trump during the prime minister’s first visit since the US and Israel launched a joint war against Iran.

Netanyahu said his meeting with Trump was “one of the best” they’ve had and the White House called it “positive and productive”.

## How a vague clause in Trump’s peace deal led to anarchy in the Strait of Hormuz

The future of the global economy once again rests on an 100-mile strip of water sandwiched between the Persian Gulf and the Sea of Oman.

Shipping through the vital waterway has halved following tit-for-tat strikes between the US and Iran this week, shaking the foundations of a sketchy 14-point interim peace deal between the warring nations.

It started when Iran attacked three commercial vessels passing through the Omani corridor of the Strait of Hormuz in overnight attacks on Monday.

## Recap: What officials said after Trump meetings with Netanyahu and Zelensky

US President Donald Trump welcomed Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to the White House for separate meetings on Tuesday. Here’s how the conversations went:

- White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt called both meetings “positive and productive”.
- Netanyahu said his conversation with Trump included an “understanding of the common goal of ensuring that Iran does not have nuclear weapons”.
- Zelensky said he and Trump “discussed licenses for Patriot interceptor production and several other ideas that could help” in its war with Russia.

## Nearly 7 in 10 Americans say Trump has failed to explain Iran war goals, poll finds

US public support for the war in Iran has fallen to its lowest level since the conflict began five months ago, with only one in three Americans backing the military campaign, according to a new Reuters/Ipsos poll.

The survey found growing skepticism over US President Donald Trump's handling of the conflict, with 69 per cent of respondents — including four in 10 Republicans — saying he has not clearly explained the goals of military involvement in Iran.

Trump has offered varying reasons for the war, including preventing Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, weakening its missile program and encouraging regime change.

## Netanyahu says he's 'going to New York' despite Mamdani's calls for his arrest

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has told Fox News he's “going to New York,” despite the city’s mayor, Zohran Mamdani, calling for the federal government to arrest him.

Mamdani has accused Netanyahu of being a “war criminal” for Israel’s actions against Palestinians in the Gaza war. Netanyahu has maintained his innocence.

Netanyahu told network host Sean Hannity that he will go to the United Nations headquarters in New York City in September.

## Netanyahu says Iranian officials 'differ on the assessment of how tough we are'

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has said Iranian officials “differ on the assessment of how tough” he and US President Donald Trump are.

“Those who think that the president is very tough and they should, they say, ‘Let's not confront this guy.’ And those who think, ‘We can play America,’ they tend to be more demanding [in negotiations]”, Netanyahu told Fox News Sean Hannity.

## Saudi Arabia says it 'does not seek escalation' following strikes with US against Iran-aligned militias

Saudi Arabia’s Defence Ministry has said it “does not seek escalation” following strikes with the US against what it called Iran-aligned “terrorist” militias.

The Saudi Arabian Armed Forces, in coordination with US Central Command, conducted “precise and targeted strikes” against those militias in Iraq after air defense systems “intercepted and destroyed several drones that attempted to target petroleum facilities in the Eastern Province and Riyadh regions”, the ministry wrote on X.

“The Kingdom emphasizes that it does not seek escalation but will respond to any aggression it faces”.

## Netanyahu says meeting with Trump was ‘one of the best’ they’ve had

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has told Fox News that his meeting with US President Donald Trump at the White House Tuesday was “one of the best” they’ve had.

“I always hate to disappoint our would-be critics who are trying to find cracks in our alliance, and what they find like today is a wall of granite”, Netanyahu told network host Sean Hannity.

The prime minister said he and Trump “have a common commitment”.

“We don't want to see this fanatic regime in Tehran have nuclear weapons to threaten every American, and to threaten the peace of the world, and to threaten the existence of Israel”, Netanyahu said. “We have a common goal, and it's going to be achieved either through diplomatic means or other means”.

## Lindsey Graham warned that Trump was letting the Iran war ‘slip away’ just before he died

US Senator Lindsey Graham candidly warned that Donald Trump was letting the war in Iran “slip away,” just weeks before his sudden death.

The South Carolina Republican, who died July 11, had been a vocal champion of the US-Israeli military campaign against Iran, which just hit its five-month-mark with no end in sight.

As part of filmmaker Alex Holder’s upcoming documentary, *Lindsey’s Game*, Graham divulged some of his honest thoughts about how the US president’s war had progressed during a June sit-down interview.

“Iran, he’s letting this thing slip away. We had them on the ropes, and his indecision is coming back to hurt us”, Graham said, according to a clip aired on CNN. “I’ve got to go talk to him, you know?”
