## Finalists in prestigious awards in York announced

Joanne Nicholson is up for an Against All Odds award *(Image: Supplied)*

FINALISTS of a prestigious competition celebrating unsung heroes in and around York have been announced.

The wait is over for nominees in The Press Community Pride Awards 2026.

It came after hundreds of readers nominated a person whose exemplary work deserves to be applauded.

Three nominees have been selected for each of the 11 awards, which include the following: Young Person of the Year, Against All Odds, Charity Fundraiser of the Year, Education Hero of the Year, Volunteer of the Year, Health Service Hero, Sports Person/Team of the Year, Carer of the Year, School of the Year, Best Community Project – and York Community Pride Outstanding Contribution Award.

  Chair of the York Parkinson's Support Branch, Tim Bryan, has been selected for a Volunteer of the Year award 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Among the finalists is Joanne Nicholson, who is up for an Against All Odds awards.

Joanne transformed her grief for her daughter Emily, who passed away from cancer, into purpose by co-founding EmAble with her son Matt.

The pair created EmAble – a fashion brand - after witnessing Emily’s struggle to find comfortable and dignified clothing during her treatment.

The brand is designed to provide comfort, dignity and easy medical access for people undergoing treatments like chemotherapy, dialysis or radiotherapy.

Tim Bryan has been selected as a finalist for a Volunteer of the Year Award.

Chair of the York Parkinson’s Support Branch, Tim dedicates his time to supporting those living with the condition while being a full-time carer for his wife Lynette.

His nominee said: “Tim is always ready to offer advice, encouragement and practical help, including supporting committee members with modern technology.

“His kindness and patience ensure that everyone feels supported.”

  After overcoming cancer during the pandemic, often facing treatment alone, Sarah turned her experience into a force for good by raising more than £35,000 for cancer charities and the Breast Unit at York Hospital 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Up for a Charity Fundraiser of the Year award is Sarah Dargue.

After overcoming cancer during the pandemic, often facing treatment alone, Sarah turned her experience into a force for good by raising more than £35,000 for cancer charities and the Breast Unit at York Hospital.

Through initiatives including her “Run Like a Girl” team, charity runs and a Robbie Williams-themed Pink Tie Ball, she has brought people together to support others facing cancer.

Joanne, Tim and Sarah will join 30 finalists at the ceremony held at York Racecourse on Thursday (September 10).

Sponsored by Aviva, the event this year will be hosted by ITV Calendar’s Duncan Wood, with a special appearance from Britain’s Got Talent star Finley Barrett-Carter, who will sing to guests on arrival.

  Finley Barrett-Carter will be performing to guests of the York Community Pride Awards on arrival 

*(Image: Fremantle / Britain's Got Talent)*

Speaking about the ceremony, Aviva’s community impact manager (York), Holly Hennell, said: “We’re proud to be sponsoring the York Community Pride Awards once again.

"York is home to so many inspiring people and initiatives and these awards are a chance to celebrate those who go above and beyond for their communities.

"As a long‑standing employer in York, our colleagues are incredibly proud of the city we live and work in and supporting the fantastic work happening across York is something we’re deeply committed to.”
