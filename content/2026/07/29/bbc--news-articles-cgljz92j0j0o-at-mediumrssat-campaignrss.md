A golfing green surrounded by dried grass in Dorset, just one of the areas declared to be in drought on Wednesday

**Drought has been declared across more than half of England as hot, dry weather takes its toll on the country's water supplies.**

The announcement means there have been three droughts in the past five years - 2022, 2025 and now 2026.

Photos from across the country have captured some of the dry conditions - including receding rivers, parched parks and sportspeople trying to play on.

![Boats are seen stranded on the exposed riverbed of the River Thames as water levels drop amid hot and dry weather conditions in London, United Kingdom, on July 24, 2026.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1920/cpsprodpb/d346/live/01c28df0-8b53-11f1-b8ee-9b3c26ad07bb.jpg)

There is no single definition of drought, but it usually refers to a period of prolonged unusually dry weather that can have impacts on people, nature and the environment.

A lack of rainfall has meant rivers have lowered, including the River Thames, exposing the riverbed in London.

![A general view of the River Thames, where water levels have dropped due to prevailing hot and dry weather conditions in London, United Kingdom, on July 24, 2026.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1920/cpsprodpb/8ca1/live/f02e1b60-8b64-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.jpg)

Some areas of the country have now entered their fourth heatwave of the year.

Beauty spots and parks have been left yellow and parched, including London's Greenwich Park, Primrose Hill, Hackney Marshes, Peckham Common and Leyton Flats.

While droughts happen naturally, scientists say that climate change is raising the risks, with higher summer temperatures drying out soils more quickly. This can lead to more severe and longer-lasting droughts.
