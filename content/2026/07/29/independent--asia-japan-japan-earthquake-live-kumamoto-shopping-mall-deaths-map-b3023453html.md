The death toll from Tuesday's earthquake in southern Japan has risen to 13, as the country's leader said rescuers face a “race against time” to reach more people trapped after the 6.8-magnitude tremor.

Prime minister Sanae Takaichi said emergency workers in Kumamoto Prefecture “will marshal all available resources on the ground to save and rescue as many people as possible”.

The earthquake left a trail of destruction, with collapsed buildings, buckled roads and rescue teams combing through piles of rubble.

One of the worst-hit sites was an Aeon shopping mall in Kumamoto, where two women in their 20s were killed and rescuers pulled eight people from the rubble after part of the building collapsed in an explosion.

Hospitals near the epicentre were overwhelmed after power outages disrupted operations, with one describing itself as "like a field hospital" and another suspending new admissions after treating 86 injured patients.

The earthquake has knocked out electricity to nearly 50,000 homes, and residents have been warned to prepare for strong aftershocks over the coming week, alongside heatwave conditions and an ongoing risk of landslides.

## Japan records over 170 aftershocks after powerful quake

Over 170 aftershocks had been reported in Kumamoto by Wednesday morning following yesterday's powerful 6.8-magnitude earthquake, according to NHK.

The tremors ranged in magnitude from 1 to 6, the agency said, referring to Japan's own seven-point seismic intensity scale.

Authorities have warned residents in the areas that felt the biggest tremors to be on alert for further strong quakes for about a week, as well as for the risk of landslides.

## Tremor of Japan earthquake felt in parts of South Korea

The powerful earthquake that struck Japan was also felt in parts of South Korea on Tuesday, authorities said.

Tremors were felt in the southern port city of Busan as well as Incheon, west of Seoul, and the central city of Daejeon.

Authorities said more than 170 phone calls reporting the tremor were received in Busan and some people had evacuated. Many people on the upper floors of buildings reported feeling the shaking and that parked vehicles moved slightly.

A total of 760 reports had been received across the country, the interior ministry and fire authorities said, adding that no damage had been reported.

## Aerial view shows destruction at Nippon factory

Aerial footage from the Nippon Paper Industries factory shows the scale of destruction after the earthquake caused a towering chimney to snap and collapse onto the site.

The collapse trapped workers beneath the rubble, with rescue teams continuing to search for seven missing people.

Four others are seriously injured, a local government official said.

The factory has suspended operations while the company verifies employee safety and assesses damage.

## 'Like a field hospital': Quake-hit facilities struggle in Kumamoto

Some hospitals have reported being overwhelmed in the aftermath of the disaster, as power outages exacerbated the strain on emergency services.

One hospital in Uki City, near the epicentre, said a power outage caused by the earthquake left it unable to function.

"It's become like a field hospital," the head of the hospital's administrative department told NHK.

Another hospital in the city said it had suspended admissions because it could no longer handle additional emergency cases after treating 86 people injured in the quake, including three with serious injuries.

## Kumamoto quake likely caused by fault left unbroken after 2016 earthquakes, expert says

Seismologists believe the powerful earthquake that struck Kumamoto on Tuesday may have been caused by a section of an active fault zone left unruptured after the devastating twin earthquakes that hit the same region in 2016.

The earthquake, which was measured at 7.1 magnitude by the Japanese disaster management authority and 6.8 by the US Geological Survey, hit Kumamoto Prefecture on Tuesday.

It occurred close to the epicentres of the magnitude 6.5 and 7.3 earthquakes a decade ago, which killed more than 270 people.

Experts told Kyodo that the 2016 disaster may have increased strain along the fault, triggering the latest rupture, and warn that strong seismic activity could continue.

Shinji Toda, a professor of seismology at Tohoku University, said: “It is believed that the 2016 earthquake caused a significant strain and stress, resulting in movements along part of the Hinagu fault zone".

## Many remain missing at workplaces

As the full extent of the damage from the earthquake continues to emerge, many people remain unaccounted for in companies and factories.

Around 20 to 30 workers remain missing at the damaged Aeon shopping mall as emergency crews continue searching areas where calls for help were received.

Rescuers pulled eight people from the rubble of the partially collapsed shopping mall early today.

Seven people are also missing after a chimney collapsed at a Nippon Paper Industries factory, while four others are seriously injured, a local government official said.

## At least 13 people dead in earthquake as search continues for missing

Japan’s prime minister Sanae Takaichi said 13 people have died after a powerful earthquake and subsequent tremors rocked the Kyushu region.

Kyodo News said a hospital in the city of Yatsushiro took in about 40 people with injuries and about 50 others were taken to a hospital in Kumamoto city.

"There are people who are still waiting to be rescued, and it's a race against time," Takaichi said. "We will go all out to find and rescue as many people as we can."

Takaichi offered her condolences to the victims and sympathy to their relatives, while urging those affected by the quake to take care of themselves under the hot weather.
