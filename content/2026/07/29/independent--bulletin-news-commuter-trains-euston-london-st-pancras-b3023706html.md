The UK’s busiest commuter trains running at 170% capacity

Commuter trains in England and Wales are experiencing significant overcrowding, with one in four London passengers forced to stand during peak hours.

New data from the Department for Transport reveals that some services, particularly those through Euston and St Pancras International, often operate at over 170 per cent capacity.

The most overcrowded service identified was a Thameslink morning rush-hour train from Bedford to Three Bridges, reaching 176 per cent capacity.

Despite these pinch points, the DfT states that 98.9 per cent of passengers typically travel on non-overcrowded services, with average capacity across all services being 29 per cent.

The DfT is working to improve capacity through measures such as adding carriages, boosting train capacity, introducing timetable changes, and plans for Great British Railways to create a more joined-up service.
