## Top chef Tommy Banks adds to publicans' calls that Andy Burnham must do more

Tommy Banks says the support needs to be extended to the rest of the hospitality sector. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

LEADING publicans have welcomed the Prime Minister’s announcement of a 20 per cent cut in business rates for pubs and clubs, but say Andy Burnham needs to do more.

And North Yorkshire celebrity chef, Tommy Banks, says the government also needs to act faster to save businesses, and extend its support to the rest of hospitality.

On Thursday, the Prime Minister announced the rate cut for pubs, clubs and live music venues, which the government says will save a typical pub £1,100 a year.

Mr Burnham said this was the “first step” and pubs “need to know that the cavalry is coming.”

The move follows a 15 per cent cut in business rates, announced in January.

Paul Crossman, of the Campaign for Pubs says the £1,000 or saving helps and “is not to be sniffed at.”

  Paul Crossman of York, national chairman of the Campaign for Pubs. 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

But he told the Press: “It’s not going to save any pubs, that’s for sure.”

“It’s really good to have a gesture in the right direction. This is a gesture but it needs to be followed up by something more substantial.”

Paul says in 2022, when in opposition, Labour used to talk about abolishing business rates. Pubs need a system that is ‘small business friendly’ and looks at the contribution businesses make to the community.

He added: “We need Andy Burnham to walk the walk he’s talking about. This is a big job that needs big measures.”

Top North Yorkshire chef Tommy Banks told the Press: “It’s incredibly frustrating to hear the Government present this as meaningful support when, in reality, the net effect is negligible and it does nothing to reverse the damage caused by previous decisions.

“It’s also difficult to understand why this support has been targeted at only certain parts of hospitality. Why should cafes, restaurants and hotels be left out? Hospitality is one interconnected industry, and businesses across the sector are facing the same pressures.”

On social media, Tommy Banks said Andy Burnham doesn’t need to make a ‘first step’ as Prime Minister, but rather a ‘giant leap.’

  Mr Banks explained*:* This business rates change works out at around £3 to £5 a day for many businesses. That’s less than the price of a pint. It isn’t going to save businesses. It isn’t going to save jobs.”

The chef said Andy Burnham was recently saying he supported a cut in VAT for hospitality, which was “the kind of meaningful change the industry needs right now.”

He added: “We are losing four hospitality businesses every single day. This is not the time for politicians to congratulate themselves over token gestures. We need action that genuinely changes the outlook for businesses, protects jobs and gives our industry a chance to thrive again.”

  Simon Cotton (right) of the Fat Badger Group welcomes the new PM acting quickly, but says he needs to do more. 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Simon Cotton, managing director of the Fat Badger Group called the business rates cut “a meaningful nudge in the right direction.”

Mr Cotton continued: “York’s hospitality businesses know better than most how tough the last few years have been. Rate increases, energy costs, staffing pressures, and the sheer volatility of tourism patterns have left operators firefighting week to week.

“Thursday’s announcement doesn’t fix that — but it does finally feel like Westminster has remembered who keeps Britain’s visitor economy alive.”

However, support was needed for all of hospitality.

He explained: “Targeted support is welcome — but selective support risks creating fragmentation in a sector that only works when everyone is healthy. Industry voices have already said the move “doesn’t go far enough,” and they’re right.

  Simon Wade of Boroughbridge says that by boosting the hospitality sector, with it creating more jobs, and driving economic growth, cuts in business rates will pay for themselves. 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

“Hospitality needs structural reform: A simpler, fairer business rates system. VAT reform for hospitality. Long‑term strategy for tourism‑dependent cities like York.

Simon Wade, who operates a chain of four pubs in and around Boroughbridge, welcomed the support for pubs, calling it ‘great’ the new PM had acted so ‘so quickly’ but said the rates cut won’t help the rest of hospitality or affect employment.

Mr Wade said: “The only way the industry as a whole can thrive again, is to reduce the VAT.”

  John Pybus of the Blue Bell welcomes the business rates move, but say more rates reform is needed. 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

This, would be funded by more businesses opening, operating and employing, rather than higher taxes leading to fewer jobs, businesses and tax revenues as is currently happening.

He added: “The politics of envy deployed by Labour are increasingly hostile to business, growth and wealth creation.

“I, as a business owner, feel this more acutely than most, as I carry the risk, create the jobs, absorb the pressure yet are taxed more, regulated more, blamed more and appreciated less - by a political class that seems to believe private enterprise exists chiefly to bankroll the expansion of a bloated state!”

John Pybus of the Blue Bell in Fossgate said: “It's wonderful to have a Prime Minister focusing on pubs so early, but the devil is in the detail. We are still living with the recent business rates valuation increases, so we hope for full reform of the system."
