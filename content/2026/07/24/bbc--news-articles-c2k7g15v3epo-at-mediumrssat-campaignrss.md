A change in the wind direction caused a flare-up on Thursday night

**Police Scotland has declared a major incident as emergency services continue to tackle a wildfire which has been burning in the Cairngorms National Park for 10 days.**

The force said it followed "a significant change in weather conditions" which has increased the risk of fire spreading towards the village of Nethy Bridge.

All Nethy Bridge residents have been evacuated to allow emergency services to continue their response.

Smoke from the wildfire can now be seen 59 miles away in Aberdeen following a change in wind direction.

Supt Judy Hill said: "The weather conditions experienced within the Cairngorms over the last 24 hours have added further complexities to this situation.

"With evidence suggesting the fire is spreading towards Nethy Bridge, the decision has been taken to declare a major incident and evacuate residents as a precaution.

"Additional police resources are being deployed to assist in contacting those who need to leave their properties and to maintain cordons around restricted areas while our fire service colleagues continue to tackle the wildfire."

The risk of the fire spreading has increased because of a change in wind direction

A Police Scotland statement said Nethy Bridge residents were being directed to a dedicated respite centre at the Macdonald Aviemore Hotel and access roads into Nethy Bridge had been closed.

More than 100 firefighters have been tackling the blaze which first broke out near Ryvoan Bothy, south of Nethy Bridge, last Wednesday.

Eleven fire engines, wildlife units and a command support unit are still at the scene.

Firefighters said miles-long firebreaks had helped to contain the latest break-outs on Thursday night while drones were directing crews to hot spots.

![several police officers and vans near woods](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1440/cpsprodpb/3e6f/live/f9a92040-879a-11f1-9d55-0b6742729f5d.png)

Police officers on the scene are directing evacuated Nethy Bridge residents to an Aviemore hotel

The SFRS said the fire remained "dynamic and unpredictable".

A spokesman said: "Changing wind strength and direction can cause flare-ups, reignition and the spread of smoke, with conditions continuing to evolve.

"Firefighters remain deployed to tackle the incident across a vast and complex landscape, supported by ongoing helicopter water-bombing operations.

"Crews, specialist wildfire resources and partner agencies continue to monitor conditions closely and work to prevent further spread."

![A map of the Scottish Highlands, showing an area of red in Cairngorms National Park labelled "active fires in the last 24 hours".](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1600/cpsprodpb/e19e/live/8c2b5710-8775-11f1-a7ab-8b30adf0800a.png)

The SFRS had previously said residents and businesses in the east area of Aviemore, as well as across parts of Aberdeenshire - including Inverurie, Banchory and Kintore and surrounding areas - and as far as Aberdeen city, may see or smell smoke on Friday and into the weekend.

The wildfire in the Cairngorms has brought a haze to Aberdeen

Before the police major incident was declared, First Minister John Swinney told BBC Scotland News he was keeping the fire "under very close review" and was speaking to the fire service as things develop.

He said some of the measures put in place such as firebreaks were under "significant pressure" as a result of changing weather conditions.

"We will be undertaking a review of this incident because it's an illustration... that the climate is creating different circumstances for us to manage and we've got to be confident we've got the resources in place," he added.
