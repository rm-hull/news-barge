Scientists have issued a warning about the spread of a deadly tickborne illness after finding the first tick infected with it in the state of New York.

The team found a lone star tick carrying Bourbon virus, an illness that can lead to fever, fatigue, vomiting, rash and body aches.

Lone star ticks are common throughout the Northeast, but cases of Bourbon virus are rare.

Cases are often confused for other illnesses the tick can spread, such as the red meat allergy-causing alpha-gal syndrome, experts at Stony Brook Medicine explained in a statement.

“There are a lot of lone star ticks in New York and in the Northeast, we have dense populations, and when someone is infected with Bourbon virus symptoms they are similar to other tick-born infections,” Dr. Luis Marcos, director of the Tick-borne Disease Clinic at Stony Brook Medicine, explained. “For these reasons, the Bourbon virus is likely more prevalent than we think in our region and other cases are likely not being diagnosed.”

![Lone Star Tick (Amblyomma americanum) on human skin. The tick can carry Bourbon virus, which was recently detected in New York](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/11/13/20/59/LONE-STAR.jpeg)

With tick populations spreading northward due to warmer and more hospitable conditions, the researchers say more case of Bourbon virus could be coming.

There are no vaccines or medicine to prevent or treat the illness, which was discovered in 2014 and named for the Kansas county where it was discovered.

It’s also hard to diagnose because there is currently test to confirm the viral infection and many doctors aren’t familiar with it.

The researchers determined this case was Bourbon virus after a patient “had shown some very severe symptoms and was hospitalized,” Marcos, who is also a professor at Stony Brook University, said. The patient was initially diagnosed with Lyme disease, but typical treatments for Lyme made no improvements.

Bourbon virus has killed three times: once this year, once in 2017 and once in the patient where the virus was first found.

The deaths were in Missouri and Kansas, but the virus has also been detected in Oklahoma.

![Bourbon virus is a RNA virus that was discovered in Bourbon County, Kansas, in 2014](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/24/19/13/bourbonvirusisolate16-9.jpeg)

It’s unknown where else the virus could be spreading right now, according to the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

That’s why the researchers say their finding signals the call for more awareness and expanded treatment efforts.

That includes more surveillance and developing clinical testing.

“Without identifying the cause of an infection, the chances of developing accurate diagnostic tests or effective treatments are minimal. That is why it is so important to first understand the true impact of the virus in high-risk areas,” Marcos said.
