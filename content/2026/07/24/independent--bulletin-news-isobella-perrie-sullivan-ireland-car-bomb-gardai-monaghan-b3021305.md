Student charged in connection with bomb intercepted near Irish border

Isobella Perrie Sullivan leaving court after being arrested and charged (Damien Eagers/PA Wire)

Isobella Perrie Sullivan, a 25-year-old law student, has been charged after Irish police intercepted a suspected dissident republican bomb in a car she was driving near the Irish border.

The intelligence-led operation by Gardaí on Wednesday afternoon led to the seizure of a "highly sophisticated" device containing suspected military-grade explosives, including Semtex and a detonator.

Ms Sullivan appeared before Trim District Court, where she was granted bail with strict conditions, including a 15,000-euro surety, passport surrender, and a daily sign-in at Naas Garda Station.

Garda Commissioner Justin Kelly described the seizure as "extremely significant," attributing the threat to a "small minority of dissident republicans" who did not adhere to the Good Friday Agreement.

Political figures from both sides of the border, including Ireland's Minister for Justice and the PSNI Assistant Chief Constable, praised the cross-border cooperation that led to the successful interception, preventing potential loss of life.
