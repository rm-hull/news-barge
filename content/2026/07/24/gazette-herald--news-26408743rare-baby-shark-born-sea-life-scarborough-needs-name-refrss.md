A critically endangered baby shark born at a North Yorkshire aquarium is making his public debut this weekend – and visitors are being invited to help choose his name.

The male Short Tail Nurse Shark was born at Sea Life Scarborough last year but is making his public debut for Shark Week.

The healthy male pup was born at the Scarborough aquarium on October 28 to proud parents Persephone, who has lived at the aquarium for the past 20 years, and Hades.

  Short Tail Nurse Shark born at SEA LIFE Scarborough. Photo supplied


The sharks are playing an important role in Sea Life's conservation programme.

To mark Shark Week – running from July 26 to August 1 – visitors and shark fans are being encouraged to vote for their favourite name via Sea Life Scarborough's Facebook and Instagram pages, with the winning name to be revealed after the public vote.

Todd German, curator at Sea Life Scarborough, said: "Welcoming a healthy Short Tail Nurse Shark is a fantastic achievement for our team and an important milestone for the conservation of this incredible species.

  Short Tail Nurse Shark born at SEA LIFE Scarborough. Photo supplied


"Every successful birth helps strengthen the population of the species we are so determined to protect; we can't wait to see which name the public chooses. He's already captured the hearts of our team, and we're sure visitors will fall in love with him too.”

The baby shark is small enough to fit in the palm of your hand; the Short Tail Nurse Shark is one of the smallest nurse shark species.

"Unlike most sharks, they are docile and will happily snooze on the ocean floor, with the Sea Life team affectionately describing them as the lazy dogs of the ocean," said the centre.

The youngster is reported to be thriving under the care of Sea Life Scarborough's animal care team, who carefully weigh and measure him every week to monitor his growth.

  Short Tail Nurse Shark born at SEA LIFE Scarborough. Photo supplied


"The species grows slowly, meaning he'll remain in his nursery habitat alongside other captive-bred marine animals for some time yet," said a spokesperson.

The centre said that although he was "'still finding his fins", the young shark already had a favourite meal: krill and prawns, with prawns proving an irresistible treat at feeding time.

## 
  **Short Tail Nurse Shark - facts**

While he may only be little, the new addition represents something much bigger.

• Populations of the species have estimated to have declined by around 80 per cent over the past three decades.

  Short Tail Nurse Shark born at SEA LIFE Scarborough. Photo supplied


• These sharks call just a small corner of the Indian Ocean home, meaning any pressure on their habitat can have outsized consequences for their population.

• Coastal reefs put them on the front line of fishing activity, making them highly vulnerable to accidental overfishing.

Voting for the shark's name will take place on Sea Life Scarborough's Facebook and Instagram pages during Shark Week.
