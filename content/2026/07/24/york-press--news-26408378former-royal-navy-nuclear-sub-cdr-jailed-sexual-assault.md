A former nuclear submarine commander who sexually assaulted a “drunk and asleep” subordinate in officers’ quarters has been jailed.

Former Lieutenant Commander John Cursiter, 46, was convicted of sexual assault, false imprisonment and possession of cocaine after a court martial in Catterick in May.

On Friday (July 24) he was sentenced to 33 months’ imprisonment for the “planned and predatory assault”.

The court martial heard that Cursiter invited the victim back to the officers’ mess bar after a night of heavy drinking in Plymouth.

He plied the man with alcohol and cocaine and took a picture of him asleep in the bar, before steering him to his bedroom, where he sexually assaulted him.

When the victim woke up “in a confused state” on Cursiter’s bed with his trousers removed, he tried to leave the cabin, but Cursiter took hold of his hand and stopped him leaving.

In a statement read during the hearing, the victim spoke of the “emasculating feeling of being taken advantage of” and described the assault as an “ambush”.

He said: “Before therapy all I could think about was what I could have done differently to change the outcome of that night … There is nothing I could have done differently to stop the actions of another person from happening.

“It was never something I thought would happen to me … This was an ambush.”

The man said the incident had “affected my life forever” and said he had been left afraid to go out in public “due to a lack of trust”.

David Scutt, defending Cursiter, said both he and the victim were “extremely drunk”, and that Cursiter had taken steps to address his issue with alcohol.

He said the defendant had been affected by the deaths of his father and half-brother, and had experienced two traumatic events during his 22-year Royal Navy career – one in which a submarine he was in hit a protruding rock on the seabed, and one involving a collision with another ship off Gibraltar during a training event.

Mr Scutt said: “The defendant had a long and successful career, and that is over.

“He has lost his rank, he has lost his reputation, he is in many ways a broken man.”

“He is going to find gaining employment in civilian life a real challenge.”

Judge Advocate Edward Legard said the court martial board was satisfied that Cursiter, of Balloch, Dunbartonshire, had used alcohol and drugs to render the victim “incapable” so he could assault him.

The defendant was dismissed with disgrace, after being previously administratively discharged following a positive test for cocaine.

The judge said: “Your behaviour was inexcusable and represents a breach of the values and standards of the Armed Forces … Your disgraceful conduct fell well below the standards to be expected of an officer of your rank.”
