A YORK pub thought to be one of the oldest continuously-licensed premises in the city is shortlisted for an award.

Readers of The York Press have put Ye Olde Starre Inne in Stonegate into their top 10 pubs in York for 2026.

Located on Stonegate, the Grade II-listed building dates back to the 16th century and had become an inn called The Starre by 1644.

During the English Civil War, the inn served as a hospital for wounded soldiers after the Battle of Marsden Moor.

Owners Greene King highlight the pub food menu plating up classics as well as seasonal fare.

The brewer and pub company said Ye Olde Starre Inn has a well-stocked bar and patrons can "seize the summer sunshine" in one of its three beer gardens - with views of York Minster included.

  Ye Olde Starre Inne was first built in 1644 

*(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

The pub’s distinctive sign, which spans across Stonegate, has become a familiar feature of the street.

In 1733, landlord Thomas Bulman struck a deal with neighbouring shop owners to hang the sign between their buildings, a tradition that has continued ever since.

Visitors today experience a warm welcome and a slice of history with every pint.

Ye Olde Starre Inne holds a Tripadvisor rating of 4.0 out of 5 from 1,646 reviews, with many guests praising its character and friendly service.

The pub’s atmosphere is described as historic and cosy and the menu has received generally positive feedback.

Service is frequently described as friendly and welcoming, with staff members often singled out for praise.

One Tripadvisor reviewer said: "This pub was an extremely welcoming venue.

"Anyone coming here would be lucky to experience such a wonderful, historic and inviting venue in this truly one-of-a-kind city."

And one reviewer from Canada wrote: "There are a lot of cozy, dark corners in this place - which is exactly what we wanted.

"Most of the dining rooms are appointed with antique furniture and books and other nick-naks and it really can feel like you're stepping back in time.

"There's a really good selection of ales, lagers and cider on tap and the food menu serves a good range of pub grub and more hearty meals."

  Ye Olde Starre Inne was first built in 1644 

*(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Recent plans submitted by owner Greene King propose updating the historic sign with the company’s green and copper branding.

Greene King’s application stated the works would respect the building’s historic features while improving the experience for patrons.
