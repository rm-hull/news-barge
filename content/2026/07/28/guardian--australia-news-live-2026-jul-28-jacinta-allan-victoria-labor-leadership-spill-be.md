Microsoft sought to exclude Bing and LinkedIn from paying for news

Documents released by the treasury department under freedom of information law reveal that Microsoft had argued last year to exclude its Bing search service and LinkedIn platform from being included in the Albanese government’s news bargaining initiative.

It followed an extensive consultation process with tech companies and media.

According to the documents published yesterday, in March 2025, Microsoft argued against including its search platform Bing on the grounds that it is just 4.7% of the Australian search market and “applying the incentive to small search engines like Bing will harm their ability to compete with Google as the dominant search engine”.

For LinkedIn, although the company has gone to great lengths to woo journalists to post on the platform – including training 507 journalists in Australia and offering them free yearly LinkedIn subscriptions – the company argued because most people in Australia access LinkedIn on desktop computers, it shows that “members primarily access it for professional purposes rather than casual browsing or social interaction”.

The company said that its “significantly smaller active userbase” compared to sites like Facebook, Instagram and TikTok, and “low time spent on the LinkedIn mobile app would suggest that LinkedIn has a minimal use in news”.

Microsoft described news consumption on LinkedIn as an “ancillary activity”.

Nine newspapers reported in April that LinkedIn was expected to be excluded from the news bargaining initiative scheme when it goes ahead.

Kellie Sloane backs in shadow cabinet member named during Icac hearing

Returning to Kellie Sloane: the NSW opposition leader has called for “procedural fairness” to be observed after a member of her frontbench – the shadow finance minister, Eleni Petinos – was named during Icac corruption hearings.

The inquiry heard a phone call featuring fugitive property developer and prohibited donor Jean Nassif, in October 2021, in which he allegedly claimed he had paid for the removal of the building commissioner, David Chandler.

He also allegedly claimed that “the fair trading minister is gonna to be one of my girls”.

The inquiry heard that two months after the call, the then Liberal premier Dominic Perrottet appointed Petinos as state minister of fair trading in a cabinet reshuffle.

In July 2022, Chandler handed in his resignation after an investigation into his conduct that had been requested by Petinos, which later cleared him of any wrongdoing.

Sloane, who says she checked in with Petinos yesterday, says:

The allegations were made, and [counsel assisting] herself said that it is yet to be determined whether this was serendipitous, whether it was just wishful thinking by Nassif, and these were opening statements.

It’s going to be impossible for me to make a running commentary of the matters of Icac and there is procedural fairness that should be accorded to all.

Perrottet and Petinos – who is currently on maternity leave – are not accused of any wrongdoing.

The inquiry continues today, with Michael O’Hara – a hotelier and Tudehope’s former business partner – finishing his evidence, followed by former Liberal staffer Dylan Whitelaw.

NSW Liberal leader says Icac hearing ‘intensely uncomfortable listening’

The NSW Liberal leader, Kellie Sloane, described the first day of watchdog hearings into historic allegations of corruption in her party as “uncomfortable listening” and backed in members of her shadow cabinet named during hearings.

Yesterday, the Independent Commission Against Corruption (Icac) began the first of eight weeks of hearings as part of Operation Rosny. In her opening statement, counsel assisting, PeggyDwyer SC, said evidence would show how a group of young Liberal power brokers known as the “Reformers” allegedly solicited illegal donations to support branch stacking efforts to move the party to the conservative right.

Sloane, who was not an MP at the time of the allegations, has told 2GB the hearing was “intensely uncomfortable listening”.

Yeah, look, the whole thing was intensely uncomfortable listening. I wish Icac the very best in rooting out any corruption. At the moment, these are allegations and opening statements. We’ve got 8 weeks. So we just have to listen to this. But, you know … my job is not to be distracted by this.

Two current members of Sloane’s shadow cabinet were mentioned yesterday, including the shadow attorney general and Liberal leader in the upper house, Damien Tudehope.

The inquiry heard that multiple people named in allegations had connections with Tudehope, including some who had worked as staffers in his office. His son, James Tudehope, is not named in allegations but has declined a request to give evidence having moved to New Zealand.

Sloane tells 2GB she stands by her comments that Tudehope, who is not accused of any wrongdoing and has stood aside from shadow cabinet during the inquiry after being called as a witness, is “a man of integrity”.

Hannah Ferguson throws down the gauntlet to Albanese to run in seat of Grayndler

Krishani Dhanji

Cheek media’s co-founder Hannah Ferguson has declared she’ll challenge the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, in his seat of Grayndler at the next election, due by May 2028.

The popular commentator and podcaster made the announcement via social media, and said the inner west deserves better representation.

Ferguson had told the National Press Club last year she would run for the Senate, but says she reconsidered:

Towards the end of 2025, I realised that the more conversations I was having the more the question came up: why not Grayndler?

Ferguson says when she was born in Marrickville in 1998, Albanese had already represented the area for two years. She grew up in different parts of the country – from south-west Sydney to Orange but now calls the area home once again.

She says she intends to step away from Cheek Media at “some stage next year” to campaign full-time and ensure the company remains independent.

This is not about headlines or clickbait, its about hearing directly from people in my community who feel underrepresented. The political system is broken. The major parties are answering to vested interests, not the communities they’ve been elected to serve … I am not here to play politics, that is just another reason Australians are fed up with the political party machinery.

KPMG sacks former executive who allegedly stashed client info in locker

KPMG has told a parliamentary inquiry that its former chief operating officer kept confidential client information in her locker, after repeatedly denying the allegation.

The firm has admitted to two breaches of client confidentiality but denied a third by Eileen Hoggett. The new KPMG chief executive, John Sams, wrote in a Friday letter to a parliamentary inquiry that Hoggett had denied keeping Lendlease audit information in a locker in the KPMG Sydney office. KPMG had found that she had, he said. Sams wrote:

The substantiation of this allegation reflects the importance of ensuring concerns are thoroughly and objectively investigated … I regret the way aspects of this matter were handled in the past. It is unacceptable that it has taken this long for the truth to come out, and that it has come to light in this manner.

Sams has had Hoggett sacked from the firm, a KPMG spokesperson said.

The regulator, the Australian Securities and Investments Commission, has told the inquiry it is formally investigating Hoggett and former colleague Paul Rogers over their behaviour in the Lendlease audit. Neither of the former KPMG partners has commented publicly on the allegations.

Origin Energy hack hits 900,000 current and former customers

Origin Energy has found about 900,000 current and former customers’ records have been accessed in a data breach.

Australia’s largest energy retailer said today it was contacting and supporting customers whose information has been accessed. Origin’s chief executive, Frank Calabria, said the company was still reviewing the incident, revealed last week. Calabria said:

To our customers, I am sorry. We don’t take for granted the trust customers place in Origin and our safeguarding of their information … We are working with cybersecurity and forensic specialists to ensure the incident is contained, and we’ve taken a number of steps to secure our systems.

Calabria warned customers to watch out for suspicious activity and a heightened risk of scams. He said Australian government agencies had been notified, adding:

Importantly, this is a criminal matter that is subject to an ongoing investigation by the relevant authorities, and given this, we are constrained by the level of information we can provide about the incident at this time.

The company last week dismissed reports it had reached a deal with its hacker to avoid data leaks. After The Australian published claims from a person claiming to be behind the hack on Friday, an Origin spokesperson said:

Origin notes there is considerable media speculation in relation to the data security incident we are actively managing. Our investigation is ongoing, and we currently have no further updates.
