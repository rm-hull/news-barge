# Historic Knaresborough inn built in 17th century is to reopen under new management

## A historic Knaresborough pub which has stood beside the Low Bridge for hundreds of years is to reopen after a change of ownership.

Following a short closure, Mother Shipton Inn is set to reopen under new management in a matter of weeks.

One of the few Knaresborough pubs situated in a Grade II listed building, Mother Shipton Inn dates to the 17th century when it was originally built as a gatehouse.

In the 18th century it became an inn with a name which pays tribute to the legendary Yorkshire prophetess Ursula Sontheil, otherwise known as Mother Shipton.

The Harrogate & Ripon branch of CAMRA describes how to this day "there are meat hooks studding the ceiling and a poor box is still affixed to the fireplace."

Despite its rich heritage, this cosy and much-loved riverside pub located at the bottom of the Nidd gorge at the Calcutt end of Low Bridge has not had the most straightforward of times since the end of Covid.

Last September, Mother Shipton Inn closed briefly, then reopened under new management.

This month has seen it close temporarily again for a refurbishment and another change of ownership

New General Manager Matthew Chapman, who previously managed The Stansfield Arms in Apperley Bridge, says he plans to welcome customers back before the end of August.

Mother Shipton Inn is recruiting for both full-time and part-time positions across front and back of house.

Regulars will be fervently hoping the good times return to this popular and historic hostelry.
