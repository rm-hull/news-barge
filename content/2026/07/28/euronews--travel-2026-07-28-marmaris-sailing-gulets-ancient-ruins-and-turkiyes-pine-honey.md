## Most visitors come to Marmaris for the sea, but it's the pine forest above it that shapes much of what makes this stretch of Türkiye's coast distinct.

The journey begins at sea, aboard a traditional gullet, a wooden boat still built by hand along this coast, much as it has been for generations. Sailing the famous Blue Voyage, travellers pass the ancient hilltop ruins of Amos before dropping anchor in Kumlubük Bay, where the water is calm and clear enough for snorkelling straight off the boat.

But Marmaris's identity doesn't end at the shoreline. Inland, Marmaris National Park's 30,000 hectares of Mediterranean pine forest lead to Nimara Cave, used as shelter by humans since the Palaeolithic age.

Those same forests sustain a local tradition few visitors expect: pine honey, made not from nectar but from honeydew collected on the trees, darker and less sweet than most. At the Marmaris Honey House, that centuries-old craft is on full display, fitting, since Türkiye produces around 92% of the world's pine honey, much of it from this very region.
