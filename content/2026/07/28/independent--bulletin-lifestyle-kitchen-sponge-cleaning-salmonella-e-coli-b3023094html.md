New research from Germany indicates that kitchen sponges can harbour dangerous food-poisoning bacteria, such as Salmonella, which can survive for at least two weeks and easily transfer to kitchen surfaces.

The porous structure of sponges, combined with retained moisture and food traces, creates an ideal environment for bacteria and mould to multiply, making disinfection with bleach less effective on older sponges.

Contaminated sponges pose a significant infection risk by spreading germs directly onto worktops and other items during washing up, even after the sponge appears dry.

Beyond health concerns, most kitchen sponges are made from non-biodegradable plastics, are difficult to recycle, and shed microplastics, raising environmental concerns.

To reduce risks, it is advised to use paper towels for raw food spills, disinfect sponges periodically, replace them weekly, or consider switching to faster-drying alternatives like dishwashing brushes.
