Let me set the scene for you: you've planned the outfits (three versions, obviously), found the best drag night in the city, and then, just as you're about to hit “confirm” on your hotel booking, you freeze. Because sometimes, the hotel that looks great online turns out to be about as welcoming as a raised eyebrow. In the breakfast room it feels, somehow, as though everyone thought you'd sat at the wrong table.

I’m thrilled to report that Liverpool’s hotel scene doesn’t make you feel like that at all.

This city has long been home to bold voices, unapologetic nights out and genuine pride. From the bars around Stanley Street to Liverpool's thriving drag and performance scene, it is a place that builds a stage for queer life. But even in a city this welcoming, choosing the right place to stay matters.

To make my selection, I’ve looked beyond thread counts and rooftop bars, to pick the hotels that have an inclusive attitude that runs through the whole stay – and put you within easy reach of queer culture. These are my eight favourites, whether you want spa luxury, design-led cool, a good value stay or a hotel that puts you right in the middle of the Pride weekend action.

## The best LGBTQ+ friendly hotels in Liverpool

**At a glance**

- **Best LGBTQ+ friendly hotel overall:** The Municipal Hotel & Spa
- **Best boutique hotel:** Hope Street Hotel
- **Best value hotel:** The Resident Liverpool hotel
- **Best hotel for late nights:** Titanic Hotel Liverpool
- **Best hotel for proximity to Pride celebrations:** Innside by Melia Liverpool hotel

**1. Hope Street Hotel**

![Hope Street Hotel has a standout spa](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/07/15/03/Hope-Street-Hotel.jpeg)

Hope Street Hotel sits in Liverpool's Georgian Quarter and is welcoming from the moment you arrive. The 149-room Victorian building feels genuinely warm thanks to polished wood finishes, natural textures and soft lighting.

The spa is a destination in its own right, offering a Himalayan salt sauna, thermal pools and treatments that actually do what they promise.

The location puts you within easy reach of theatres, galleries, and good queer-friendly restaurants without being too noisy. Staff are attentive without being performative. For LGBTQ+ travellers, that quiet assumption that you're just another guest to look after brilliantly is worth more than any rainbow flag in the window.

**Address:** 40 Hope Street, Liverpool L1 9DA, UK

**Price:** From £99

The location is ideal if you're in the city for an event. Liverpool Pride, a concert at the M&S Bank Arena, a big night out on the waterfront – the walk back is short, well-lit, and rather beautiful.

The Municipal in Liverpool is doing luxury on its own terms. ## FAQs

### When is the best time of year to visit Liverpool outside of Pride?

This depends on what you plan to do during your stay. Visiting from late May to early July offers you the greatest chance of good weather outside of the busy summer holiday period. January to March is the cheapest and least crowded time to visit, but you may have to contend with rain and colder tempertaures. June brings Africa Oyé festival, the UK's largest celebration of African music and culture, while August is International Beatleweek, attracting fans of the Fab Four from around the world.

### When in Liverpool Pride?

Liverpool’s Pride kicked off with a concert at the M&S Bank Arena on Friday 24 July, with a stellar line up including Sugababes, Beth Ditto and Bimini. The following day, the Pride parade took place in the city centre. For more information, visit Liverpool Pride’s website.

### Where is the best LGBTQ+ nightlife in Liverpool?

The city’s Pride Quarter, around Stanley Street, has an array of LGBTQ+ bars and nightclubs with drag acts and karaoke. It’s also where you’ll find Britain’s first rainbow taxi rank.

Heaven, OMG, GBar and Masquerade are among some of the most popular LGBTQ+ nightlife venues.
