Social media influencers Andrew and Tristan Tate were arrested in Miami on 18 July, facing new charges from Britain's Crown Prosecution Service for rape, assault, and facilitating trafficking for sexual exploitation involving four alleged victims between 2010 and 2017.

The brothers, who hold dual U.S. and British citizenship, are currently being held in a federal jail in Miami as a U.S. judge considers Britain's request to extradite them to face these and previous charges of rape and human trafficking.

Their lawyers said Monday that the Tates, who deny all wrongdoing, will file a legal motion by 5 August seeking their release on bail, with a hearing scheduled for 13 August to determine their detention status.

Prosecutors from the Miami U.S. Attorney's Office argue that the brothers should remain detained due to flight risk and potential danger to the community, despite their lawyers asserting they are not a flight risk, citing their compliance with court hearings in Romania.

The extradition process could take months, with Britain having until 16 September to submit its full request, after which a U.S. judge will assess the evidence before the U.S. Secretary of State makes the final decision on their extradition.
