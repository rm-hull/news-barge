# The fatal 1952 plane crash that changed air travel forever

- The Pan American Clipper Endeavor crashed off San Juan, Puerto Rico, on 11 April 1952, with all 69 people initially surviving the impact, but 52 subsequently drowned due to a lack of emergency preparation.
- The US Civil Aeronautics Board's investigation found that passengers were not briefed on emergency exits or life vest use, and safety equipment was not readily accessible.
- These findings led to significant changes in aviation regulations, mandating airlines to provide accessible life vests and rafts, and to give verbal briefings and demonstrations for overwater flights.
- Later incidents, such as the 1996 Ethiopian Airlines crash and the 2019 Aeroflot fire, highlighted ongoing issues with passenger compliance, language barriers, and the obstruction caused by retrieving hand luggage during evacuations.
- The aviation industry continues to strive for solutions to improve passenger recall and compliance during emergencies, including the use of more entertaining safety videos, acknowledging the gap between knowing the drill and following it under pressure.
