Experts say new SA bird flu cases suggest disease has begun to spread in local birds

Lisa Cox

South Australia has seven new suspected cases of deadly H5 bird flu and experts warn the disease has likely begun to spread in local bird populations.

All seven birds are greater crested terns, with one found at Cape Jaffa, one at Port MacDonnell and five around Southend Jetty, near Beachport on the Limestone Coast.

Kate Millar, chief executive of BirdLife Australia, said:

If we were in a bushfire scenario, those sub-antarctic birds that arrived with the disease initially were like the embers. Now having it in crested terns, that’s the smoke.

This is now taking hold and is evidence of spread.

Governments have been saying it hasn’t spread, this is evidence of spread.

South Australia’s primary industries minister, Clare Scriven, told media on Monday the development was concerning. Samples have been sent to the CSIRO for verification the birds had the deadly H5N1 strain. That analysis will also help determine whether the disease has begun to spread between local birds, the government said.

Family take legal action after Aboriginal death in custody

The family of a young Aboriginal man who died in custody has launched legal action in the NT supreme court after the officers involved were not prosecuted, AAP reports.

Kumanjayi White, 24, died in Alice Springs in May 2025 after two plain-clothes police officers held him down in a supermarket, sparking national outrage and rallies calling for an independent investigation.

His family were told in May, on the eve of the first anniversary of White’s death, that no charges would be laid, marking “an incredibly traumatic experience”.

White’s grandfather Ned Jampijinpa Hargraves said there has been a lack of transparency from authorities since his grandson’s life was “violently cut short”. He said:

We said from day one that we did not trust this process.

Nobody will tell us who actually made the decision [that no charges would be laid].

We deserve to know all the information about his death and about the process including who decided that no one should be charged and why.

‘Entire Jewish religion’ revolves around Israel, royal commission hears

The president of the Australian Jewish Association (AJA) Robert Gregory has told the royal commission into antisemitism and social cohesion that the “entire Jewish religion” revolves around Israel. The AJA is a self-described centre-right advocacy group. This block of hearings is examining the security arrangements for the Jewish community. Gregory said:

It is inherent in every part of Judaism from the Jewish festivals … to Jewish weddings, where we break a glass to remember the destruction of Jerusalem, to even the burial of Jewish people who are buried with sand from Jerusalem … Judaism doesn’t exist without Zionism.

The commissioner, Virginia Bell, asked Gregory about other groups who didn’t subscribe to the idea of Zionism being inherent to their Judaism, including Hasidic Jews. She said Israel was “significant” in Judaism but there were “a variety of views about the expression of that significance”.

Gregory said that was a “small sect” in Judaism and a “minuscule” part of the Jewish community in Australia:

Some other people who these days call themselves anti-Zionists actually have more of a difference, but they’re obviously not keeping Judaism as it’s been practised for thousands of years.

The royal commission has heard from members of the Jewish community with opposing views, including the Jewish Council of Australia, who separate Jewish identity from the state of Israel.

Thus far, Mr Ellis has declined to give oral evidence to this public inquiry, during which he would be subject to cross-examination. He cites family responsibilities and different time zone.

That lawfully intercepted information also indicated that Mr Nasif had an association with not one but two of Dominic Perrottet’s brothers, and that he was seeking to leverage their connections with Dominic Perrottet to assist in having the building commissioner David Chandler removed from office.
