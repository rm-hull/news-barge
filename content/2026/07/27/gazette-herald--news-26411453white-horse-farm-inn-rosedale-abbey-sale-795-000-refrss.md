A historic and highly-rated inn at the heart of a North Yorkshire moors tourist hotpot is back on the market.

White Horse Farm Inn at Rosedale Abbey is being marketed by York-based Barry Crux and Co for £795,000.

The inn features a bar, restaurant and function room, with 17 en-suite hotel rooms.

The property agents say the venue, which is said to have operated as a pub and hotel since 1703, is in a “superb location in the North York Moors National Park.”

It also offers “stunning views over the surrounding countryside.”

They also describe the inn as a “profitable business with huge scope to grow trade.”

  The dining area of the White Horse Farm Inn 

*(Image: Barry Crux & Co)*

  The agents said: “A substantial and imposing stone built detached property extending to two storeys under pitched roofs and single storey extensions to the rear. There are some single storey outbuildings in the grounds, currently not in use. To the front is ample parking and a beer garden with stunning views over the surrounding countryside.

  

  “Internally, the White Horse Farm is fitted as a traditional country inn with ground floor public areas and rooms to the upper floors and in a couple of self-contained annexes attached to the main building. There are also extensive grounds which include outside seating, beer terrace, parking, unused outbuildings and grounds.”

The property is highly-rated online, receiving 4.4 stars out of five, based on 376 Google reviews.

  The bar area of the White Horse Farm Inn 

*(Image: Barry Crux & Co)*

Tripadvisor awards a similar 4.3 stars out of five, ranking it tenth out of 37 B&Bs and Inns in Pickering.

The latest Tripadvisor review gave White Horse Farm five stars, saying it offered a “delightful stay on the North York Moors National Park.”

It added: “When booking, the inn looked like a pleasant stay. But, it was unexpectedly more. The staff were friendly and helpful; the room simple but great, clean and spacious; the bathroom also clean and spacious.

“The breakfast very generous and tasty. We had dinner in the pub one night and it did not disappoint: great portions, tasty, and not a long wait; plus the selection of beers/ales/cider was great. Ample parking. And we felt safe and secure inside and outside. When traveling in this area, this is definitely a place to stay.”

The venue was previously on the market in 2016 for £695,000, when it was acquired by Phil Haxby, Nigel Haxby and Peter Scholey, whose other business interests include Doncaster-based Wilfreda Luxury Coaches Limited. The sale price was undisclosed.
