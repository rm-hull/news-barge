Andy Burnham will welcome Volodymyr Zelensky on Monday as his first international visitor since becoming prime minister and pledge his “unwavering” support to Ukraine.

The PM will host the Ukrainian president on a visit to a naval base where Mr Burnham will announce that Britain will hand over the intellectual property behind its new “Stone Cloak” electronic warfare system. It is designed to block Russian detection of any drones it is fitted to.

Mr Burnham said in a statement: “Britain stands with Ukraine, shoulder to shoulder, and our support remains unwavering.

“Russia should be in no doubt of our resolve and we will not back down until we achieve long-lasting and just peace for Ukraine.”

Mr Zelensky said he wanted to have a “productive bilateral meeting where we will speak about all sorts of things” when he meets Mr Burnham.

In a social media post, the Ukrainian leader also said he wanted to share expertise with Britain from his country’s four-year war with Russia, which included building a “big factory” for drones in the UK.

## Burnham and Zelensky to visit naval base

President Volodymyr Zelensky will join Andy Burnham to visit a naval base where the new prime minister will announce that Britain will hand over the intellectual property behind its new “Stone Cloak” electronic warfare system, enabling Ukraine to produce the technology at scale.

The tablet-sized device is designed to block Russian detection of any drones it is fitted to.

The Ministry of Defence has already given thousands of the jammers to Ukrainian forces to bolster their drone operations.

## Kremlin spokesperson's wife launches legal challenge to overturn EU sanctions

Tatiana Navka, the wife of Russian president Vladimir Putin's spokesman Dmitry Peskov, has launched a legal challenge aimed at overturning European Union sanctions imposed on her over the war in Ukraine, according to details published on an EU website. Navka, a former Olympic ice-skating champion, was put on a sanctions list by the EU in June 2022 that targeted people over Moscow's actions in Ukraine. Peskov's daughter Elizaveta and son Nikolai were included on the same list.

Brussels said at the time that the sanctions, which included a travel ban to the EU and financial restrictions, were imposed "in respect of actions undermining or threatening the territorial integrity, sovereignty and independence of Ukraine."

Details published on the EU's EUR-Lex website showed that Navka, 51 — who was born in Soviet Ukraine but is a Russian citizen — is now seeking to overturn the sanctions on her via the European Court of Justice.

The legal filing said she was arguing that the reason for the restrictions on her had not been properly set out, that errors of law and factual assessment had been made, and that the EU had failed to prove a sufficient link between her and the stated aim of its sanctions.

Navka is seeking damages from the Council of the European Union for material damage and for damage to her reputation and other non-material damage totalling just over €2 million (£1.71 million), the same document showed.

Another official EU database shows that Yekaterina Ignatova, wife of Sergei Chemezov, head of the state-owned Rostec defence and industrial corporation, is seeking to overturn EU sanctions on her via the same legal route.

## Ukraine strikes Russian export terminal and oil facilities

Ukraine struck a Russian export terminal in the Rostov region, as well as oil facilities in the Yaroslavl and Udmurtia regions overnight, President Volodymyr Zelensky said.

"We are carrying out our plan of long-range sanctions and reducing Russia's ability to finance the war," Zelenskiy said on X, referring to Ukrainian strikes deep inside Russia.

## Russia claims strike on military cargo ships in Ukrainian port

Russian forces have struck two vessels carrying military cargo in the southern Ukrainian port city of Mykolaiv, the Russian Defence Ministry said on Monday.

Russia and Ukraine have stepped up military activity in and around the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov in recent weeks, with each side attacking dozens of ships, including oil tankers and cargo vessels.

## Russia cancels annual Navy Day parade for second consecutive year

Russia cancelled its annual Navy Day parade in St Petersburg for the second year in a row, citing security concerns.

*The Kyiv Independent* reported that Moscow cancelled the annual event as Ukraine’s long-range drone attacks continue to threaten major Russian cities.

Instead of the parade, president Vladimir Putin addressed sailors at the navy headquarters.

The cancellation reportedly came amid reports of widespread internet outages in St Petersburg.

Since 2017, the Navy Day celebrations have featured large displays of Russian naval power, including a review of warships from the Baltic, Black Sea, Northern and Pacific fleets in St. Petersburg.

Navy Day is a national holiday in Russia and is celebrated on the last Sunday of July.

## Civilian cargo ship sinks a week after Russian attack killed 10

A civilian cargo ship carrying grain sank in the Black Sea near Odesa on 26 July, a week after it was hit by Russian cruise missiles, Ukrainian authorities said.

The vessel had been sailing through Ukraine’s maritime corridor when it was struck.

Ukraine’s Sea Ports Authority (USPA) said: “The Golden Leo, which was struck near Odesa on July 19, has sunk. As a result of the Russian attack, the ship sustained significant damage to its hull and superstructure, which led to the loss of its seaworthiness and subsequent sinking.”

The authority said no crew were on board when the ship sank because survivors had already been evacuated after the attack.

In the 19 July attack, ten people were killed in the Russian attack, according to *The Kyiv Independent*. 

## Russian attacks kill one and damage vessels in Ukraine’s Mykolaiv region, official says

Russian drone attacks on Ukraine’s southern Mykolaiv region killed one person and damaged three civilian vessels, acting regional governor Heorhii Reshetilov said on Telegram.

The strikes hit port infrastructure, and damaged ships that had been stranded in Ukrainian ports since Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022.

Ukraine’s seaports authority said others were also injured but did not give a number.

Russia’s defence ministry said it had targeted two vessels carrying military cargo in the port of Mykolaiv.

## Ukrainian drones kill two in Russia’s Rostov-on-Don, governor says

Ukrainian drones launched a large overnight attack on Russia’s Rostov region, killing two people in the city of Rostov-on-Don and injuring eight others, regional governor Yuri Slyusar said.

Around 50 drones were reportedly shot down by Russian air defences, but strikes damaged apartment buildings, warehouses, a homeless shelter and an industrial facility in the port city of Taganrog, Reuters reported.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine had targeted a Russian export terminal in the Rostov region, as well as oil facilities in the Yaroslavl and Udmurtia regions.
