# Andy Burnham responds to speculation he could call snap general election

- New prime minister Andy Burnham has definitively ruled out calling an early general election, despite recent speculation in Westminster that he might seek a fresh mandate for his leadership.
- In his first major broadcast interview since entering Downing Street, Mr Burnham stated that he would not force voters to the polls nationally before 2029.
- He affirmed his commitment to Labour’s 2024 election manifesto, which includes pledges not to raise headline taxes on working people, and to adhere to Rachel Reeves’ fiscal rules.
- Mr Burnham defended his position by arguing that the public voted for the manifesto in the last election, and he personally 'faced the public' by winning the Makerfield by-election and securing parliamentary support.
- The Prime Minister dismissed the idea that an early election is necessary, noting that the British parliamentary system allows the party with a majority to form a government, citing precedents like Liz Truss, Rishi Sunak, and Gordon Brown.
