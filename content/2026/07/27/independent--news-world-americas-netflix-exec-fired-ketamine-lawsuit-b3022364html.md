A former visual effects executive for Netflix has reportedly filed a lawsuit against the streaming giant, alleging he was terminated from his $1.1 million-a-year position after disclosing his use of medically prescribed ketamine during a trust exercise at an executive retreat.

Kevin Baillie, who served as vice president and head of creative at Eyeline Studios, is seeking compensatory damages, lost wages and punitive damages in a court filing detailing the circumstances around his dismissal in April 2026.

According to legal documents seen by the *New York Post*, the controversy stems from a January 2026 “Vulnerability-Trust exercise” held at Sendero Ranch, a Netflix-owned property in Northern California. During the exercise, Baillie disclosed that he had undergone supervised ketamine treatments at a Santa Barbara clinic in late 2022 to treat clinical depression following the death of his mother. 

The lawsuit states that Netflix then opened an inquiry into the matter. By mid-March, a company investigator questioned Baillie about the medical treatments, framing the inquiry in a way that implied potential recreational drug use, according to the court filings.

Baillie claims a Netflix attorney later confirmed the ketamine disclosure influenced his termination, which he alleges also cost him up to a year of severance pay.

![Former Netflix visual effects executive Kevin Baillie has filed a lawsuit against the streaming giant, claiming he was terminated from his position after disclosing he used medically prescribed ketamine](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/27/15/03/GettyImages-1080548164.jpeg)

Beyond the medical therapy, the lawsuit describes a broader workplace culture in which corporate leaders encouraged drinking and informal behavior. Baillie alleges that Eyeline Studios CEO Jeff Shapiro maintained a personal bar in his office — where he served alcohol after meetings with top executives, including Netflix CEO Ted Sarandos — and purchased alcohol for employees during company trips.

The court papers also outline an incident at the January retreat where Baillie drank a pint of Guinness while standing on his head after colleagues requested a demonstration.

The filing notes that during a prior performance evaluation, management had advised Baillie to slightly reduce his use of profanity without eliminating it entirely.

Baillie, whose career includes visual effects work on major franchises such as *Harry Potter* and *Pirates of the Caribbean*, has reportedly requested a jury trial for the suit, which also seeks compensation for emotional distress. 

*The Independent* has contacted Netflix for comment.
