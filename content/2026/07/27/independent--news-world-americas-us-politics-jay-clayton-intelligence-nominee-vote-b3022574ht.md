The U.S. Senate is poised to begin voting as early as Monday on the nomination of Manhattan U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton, President Donald Trump’s choice for the nation’s top intelligence post, with his approval anticipated within days despite a contentious confirmation hearing.

If confirmed as the director of national intelligence, Clayton would step into a role left vacant in June by Tulsi Gabbard, whose tenure was marked by clashes with congressional Democrats who accused her of advancing Trump’s political agenda and promoting debunked election claims.

The Senate is scheduled to hold a procedural vote Monday evening to advance Clayton’s nomination for the position, which oversees the country's 18 intelligence agencies.

The DNI role was established after the September 11, 2001, attacks to coordinate the nation's extensive intelligence apparatus. Many senators are keen for Clayton to assume the post, expressing dissatisfaction with the interim director, Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte, a Trump loyalist lacking national security experience who has been cutting staff at the agency.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/27/19/11/GettyImages-2285603445.jpg)

Should Clayton secure a majority in the 100-member Senate on the procedural vote, a final confirmation vote is expected later in the week. Trump’s Republicans, holding a 53-47 majority and rarely diverging from the White House, are expected to provide the necessary votes for confirmation, even if Democrats object.

Clayton’s confirmation process has been fraught with controversy. The Senate Intelligence Committee recommended him to the full Senate in a narrow 9-8 vote on Tuesday, with only Republican members voting in favor.

This came after Clayton repeatedly declined to directly acknowledge that Trump lost the 2020 presidential election during his confirmation hearing. Democrats also questioned him about his decision to issue subpoenas ordering *New York Times* journalists to testify before a federal grand jury after they reported on security concerns involving Trump’s new Qatari-donated Air Force One plane. A prosecutor later stated in court that the Trump administration would withdraw these subpoenas following a judge's scrutiny of the investigation.

Oregon Senator Ron Wyden, a senior Democrat on the Intelligence Committee, issued a statement saying, "From start to finish, Mr. Clayton has proven himself incapable of telling the truth or demonstrating an iota of independence from Donald Trump. For those reasons, I voted 'no' on his nomination."

However, Republican Arkansas Senator Tom Cotton, who chairs the committee, described Clayton as a "highly qualified nominee with a deep experience combating a wide range of national security threats."

The controversy surrounding Clayton’s confirmation began even before his July 15 hearing. In mid-June, Trump abruptly cast doubt on the nomination by ordering the postponement of the hearing in an effort to pressure Congress into passing an overhaul of U.S. voting rules.

Senators, including Cotton, initially expected the hearing to proceed as scheduled but later confirmed Clayton would not appear after Trump instructed him to stay away.
