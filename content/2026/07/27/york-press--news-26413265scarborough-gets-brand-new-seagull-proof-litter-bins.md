## Council launches gull crackdown with new bird-proof bins in North Yorkshire

The presence of gulls in coastal town is controversial - with issues including fouling, noise and scavenging *(Image: North Yorkshire Council)*

Gull-proof bins have been rolled out in a North Yorkshire coastal town.

The bins, which are fully covered with a strong metal flap, stop gulls from being able to reach the rubbish in Scarborough.

They are part of new measures in the long-term strategy across Scarborough, Whitby and Filey – set to be discussed by North Yorkshire Council’s executive on Tuesday (August 4).

  


Cllr Richard Foster said the growing number of gulls in coastal towns “creates challenges”, and he has urged the public to stop feeding them.

  The bins, which are fully covered with a strong metal flap, stop gulls from being able to reach the rubbish in Scarborough 

*(Image: North Yorkshire Council)*

He said: “They are opportunists and any encouragement can lead to scavenging and taking food.

“This proposed strategy sets out a clear, balanced approach that recognises both the importance of protecting these species and the need to minimise their impact on residents, businesses and visitors.

“Through targeted actions such as reducing food waste, improving awareness and using effective deterrents, we can support a long-term future to live alongside our gull populations and ensure our coastal towns remain clean, safe and welcoming places for everyone.”

As well as the roll-out of more gull-proof litter bins, the council will provide residents with gull-proof sacks and carry out seasonal jet-washing of the streets.

A total of £119,500 is due to be allocated in the first year.

Gull species, including herring gulls and kittiwakes, are protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act of 1981.

Their presence in towns is controversial, however, with issues including mess, noise and scavenging.

The council said it its strategy is to improve its understanding of gull behaviour, reduce their access to food and promote targeted cleaning hotspots and shared responsibility with landowners.

It will also install “safe” deterrents and carry out population surveys.

It will work with local businesses, national conservation data, and Yorkshire Coast Urban Gull Partnership, Natural England, Environment Agency, the Royal Society for the Protection of Birds, and Yorkshire Water.
