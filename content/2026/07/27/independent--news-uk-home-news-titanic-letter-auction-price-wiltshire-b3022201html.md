A letter written by a passenger on the *Titanic* just days before he died has sold for a record sum at auction.

Henry Price Hodges boarded the doomed ship at Southampton as a second-class passenger, paying £13 for his ticket.

He wrote the letter to his friend, Hector Young of the Newtown Conservative Association in Southampton, on 10 April 1912.

Mr Hodges died on 15 April, along with about 1,500 others, when the ship struck an iceberg and sank en route to New York City.

The letter went under the hammer at Henry Aldridge and Son of Devizes, Wiltshire, on Sunday.

Billed as a rare snapshot of life on board, it had been predicted to sell for up to £35,000 – but went for £60,000.

![An artist’s impression of the sinking of the Titanic on 15 April 2012](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/04/14/15/58/GettyImages-96518519.jpeg)

“The letter itself was written by a second class passenger so very rare indeed,” auctioneer Andrew Aldridge said.

“But it is a record price for an onboard letter written by a second class passenger.”

He added: “This letter gives us a historically priceless snapshot into what life was like on board the most famous ship ever to take to the seas, for a second class passenger.

“He describes the ship and fellow passengers and how they passed the time. Yet a little over four days later the *Titanic* was lying at the bottom of the north Atlantic.”

Mr Hodges was travelling to Boston to visit relatives and the letter was postmarked Queenstown.

The letter describes the fine weather and the good atmosphere on the ship.

![A rare letter written by Titanic passenger, Henry Price Hodges](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/21/07/01KY1MF5T2KXG1FQZRRPMTBSCR.jpg)

Mr Hodges wrote: “Dear Hector,

“Very many thanks for the telegram and for the good wishes. It was a kind and thoughtful act and much appreciated, and you will please convey my thanks to the members of our Association.

“Wasn’t it good of the President to come and see me off? I almost believe I could have persuaded him to come along. We have had a fine time up to now.

“You do not see anything of the movement of the ship, but the weather is very fine.

“On the top deck there are about 200 boys (from 20 upwards) marching round & singing, others are playing dominoes & cards in the saloons, some reading, some writing, everything is quite different to what you would expect to see at sea.

“Tell your Dad I am very disappointed he did not come, but I know he could not help it, and it was more than expected by your brother coming instead. (All for this but not him) I am going to bed early, for I feel as tired as a dead dog.

“Good bye Hector

“Best wishes

“From Yours faithfully

“H.P. Hodges.”

Mr Hodges’s body was later recovered and buried in Fairview Cemetery in Halifax, Canada.

Also auctioned was an “extremely rare” US one dollar bill recovered from the body of third class passenger Ernest Tomlin, which fetched £37,000.

The note was originally sewn into his waistcoat.

Mr Aldridge said it was another record price, and almost quadrupled its estimate.
