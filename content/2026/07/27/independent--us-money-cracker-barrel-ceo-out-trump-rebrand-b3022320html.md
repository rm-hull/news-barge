Cracker Barrel said Monday that CEO Julie Felss Masino will step down after a turbulent year marked by a controversial rebranding effort that drew conservative backlash and weighed on the company’s financial performance.

The popular restaurant chain announced that David Deno, the former CEO of Bloomin' Brands — the parent company of Outback Steakhouse — will succeed Felss Masino.

“Cracker Barrel is a truly iconic American brand, defined by its unique combination of warm country hospitality, timeless appeal, and deep connection with guests across generations,” Deno said in a news release. “I am honored to lead the Cracker Barrel team and look forward to unlocking the full potential of this remarkable brand.”

The leadership change comes on the heels of a $700 million rebranding effort last year that gave some restaurants a more modern look. But the defining flashpoint arrived on August 19, when the company unveiled a new logo. The redesign replaced an image of a man known as ‘Old Timer’ leaning on a barrel with a pared down logo depicting the company’s name against a gold background.

The update sparked a wave of backlash from prominent MAGA figures, who accused the company of going “woke” and abandoning a nostalgic symbol in favor of a more sterile logo.

![The CEO of Cracker Barrel has stepped down amid turmoil over the company's 'woke' rebrand](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/27/13/21/GettyImages-2232416742.jpeg)

Rep. Byron Donalds, a Florida Republican, wrote on X, “Their logo was iconic and their unique restaurants were a fixture of American culture. No one asked for this woke rebrand. It’s time to Make Cracker Barrel Great Again.”

Even President Donald Trump weighed in, writing on Truth Social on August 25: “Cracker Barrel should go back to the old logo, admit a mistake based on customer response (the ultimate Poll), and manage the company better than ever before.” The White House, echoing the president’s remarks, wrote on X: ‘Go woke, go broke.’

The controversy drove a sharp drop in the company’s stock, erasing $94 million in market value in one day.

On August 26, the company, headquartered in Tennessee, announced it would be reverting back to the old logo.

“We thank our guests for sharing your voices and love for Cracker Barrel,” the company said in a statement. “We said we would listen, and we have. Our new logo is going away and our ‘Old Timer’ will remain.’

Trump praised the decision, writing on Truth Social: "All of your fans very much appreciate it,” and shares of the restaurant operator rose after the reversal was announced.

The company appears to have weathered the storm successfully. Last month, it reported third-quarter profit of $42.8 million, up from $12.6 million a year earlier, according to *The Wall Street Journal*.

Last week, Cracker Barrel announced it was closing all locations of its sister brand, Maple Street Biscuit Company.
