Trump claims deal reached for 'complete disarmament of Hamas' in Gaza

Donald Trump has claimed his Board of Peace has reached an “historic agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas and all other armed groups in Gaza”.

The US president just posted on his Truth Social platform:

This agreement is a critical step towards Gaza finally being governed by a new Palestinian government that will work closely with the Board of Peace to help the Palestinian people. At the same time, Israel will have the security it deserves, with Gaza no longer used as a base for terror attacks.

Trump, who chairs the international group including more than 20 countries, said the agreement “will be carried out in carefully structured phases”.

As disarmament is completed, Israeli forces will withdraw, and the International Stabilization Force will work with a new Palestinian police force to take responsibility for Gaza being safe for its residents and its neighbors.

Trump also asserted in his post:

The threat that emerged from Gaza on October 7 will NOT be allowed to rebuild!

Under this agreement, Gaza will finally be in the hands of a new Palestinian government that serves its PEOPLE.

Donald Trump at the White House on Thursday. Photograph: Jim Lo Scalzo/EPA

Hamas and Israel have not yet responded to Trump’s announcement, which he called a “major milestone” in the implementation of his 20-point peace plan for ending the war in Gaza.

Trump thanked mediators Egypt, Qatar and Turkey as well as his own team for their efforts towards the agreement.

The US president announced the Board of Peace earlier this year to oversee Gaza’s reconstruction and governance after a ceasefire agreement between Israel and Hamas in October 2025.

The new agreement on Hamas disarming comes after the Palestinian militant group earlier this month announced the dissolution of its body that governed Gaza for two decades, clearing the way for a US-backed interim administration to implement civilian rule in the territory.

The move marked a significant political shift by Hamas, which has run Gaza since its forces seized control from rival Palestinian movement Fatah in 2007 after winning legislative elections the previous year.

While announcing that it was ready to hand over security as part of a transition, Hamas made no promise to disarm unilaterally, as Israel and the US had demanded.

Julian Borger and Seham Tanteshreported then that the interim administration to which Hamas has offered to transfer governance, known as the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG), had been blocked from entering Gaza by Israel since its creation in January as part of a US-brokered ceasefire, adding further doubt to the timing of any future handover.

Palestinians salvage their belongings from the rubble of a building and tents destroyed in an Israeli airstrike in Gaza City, northern Gaza, on Thursday. Photograph: Jehad Alshrafi/AP

Analysts said the Hamas announcement was largely a symbolic gambit aimed at reviving a stalled peace process that has blocked reconstruction and humanitarian relief for Gaza’s surviving 2.1 million population.

They also said the move was designed to counter Israeli-led proposals to limit relief, reconstruction and NCAG governance to a tiny proportion of Gaza’s population in purpose-built villages in the roughly 60% of Gaza under direct Israeli army control.

Australia’s foreign minister has welcomed the White House’s announcement that a peace plan for Gaza has been agreed, saying the Albanese government wants to see the reconstruction of the Palestinian territory.

But Penny Wong also said Australia wanted to know more detail about the US announcement.

Australia “wants to see the implementation of the Gaza peace plan, which has been endorsed by the United Nations security council”, she said in a statement.

We have been clear that Hamas must disarm and play no role in the state of Palestine and we welcome any progress towards this outcome.

Foreign minister Penny Wong says Australia continues to ‘push for the free flow of aid’ into Gaza. Photograph: Mick Tsikas/AAP

Wong went on to say there was still “a long road to recovery in Gaza, securing long-term peace for Israelis and Palestinians, and building a successful Palestinian state that delivers for the Palestinian people”.

Australia continues to work with partners to respond to the devastating humanitarian situation in Gaza and to push for the free flow of aid. Together with our partners, Australia will continue to do what we can to contribute to a just and enduring two-state solution.

Hamas says if Israel does not implement deal, 'neither will we'

A member of Hamas’s negotiating team has said the issue of the group’s weapons is tied to Israel’s withdrawal from Gaza as well as the deployment of a national committee and reconstruction work in the territory.

Ghazi Hamad, a senior official, said:

The agreement contains a clear provision stating that Israel must commit to and implement its obligations. If Israel does not implement the agreement, neither will we.

Agence France-Presse also quoted Hamad as saying:

Israel will not intervene in the issue of disarmament … The National Committee for Administration of Gaza shall manage and execute the cataloguing and storage of weapons in accordance with the agreed timetable.

Hamad said the Gaza agreement was reached after “lengthy and difficult” negotiations that began about six months ago and that Hamas was making “concessions for the sake of our people in the Gaza Strip, to save them from killing and displacement”.

Hamas fighters in southern Gaza last year. Photograph: Abdel Kareem Hana/AP

“The agreement addresses ending the war, the entry of the national committee into the Gaza Strip, the Israeli withdrawal from Gaza, reconstruction and the deployment of international forces,” he told AFP.

“This is an integrated framework whose components must remain interconnected,” Hamad said, adding that other armed militias must also be dismantled.

He said Hamas would now discuss the implementation details with mediators and agree on a timetable for carrying out the deal.

This process will take about 14 days, or perhaps longer. We must agree on every detail: how the agreement will be implemented, when it will be implemented and the mechanisms for carrying it out.

Board of Peace says it is now focused on implementing Gaza agreement

The Donald Trump-led Board of Peace has issued a statement hailing the Gaza agreement as an “historic breakthrough” and saying the board’s “focus now turns to implementation”.

For the first time, Hamas officially has committed to an actionable plan for relinquishing all its weapons, which will be followed by Israeli withdrawal from Gaza. It holds the promise of delivering significant benefits to the people of Gaza, who have waited for too long for a better future, and security to the people of Israel.

The post asserted Hamas had agreed to a “detailed roadmap” for implementing the next phases of the Gaza ceasefire in line with Trump’s “vision” for Gaza’s recovery.

Our focus now turns to implementation. The Board of Peace has worked closely with the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG). It will soon begin a phased transition toward full authority, backed by the International Stabilization Force (ISF), and will accelerate implementation of the rule of law, security, and humanitarian improvement in Gaza.

Turning back to the war on Iran, mediator Pakistan has insisted that negotiations between Tehran and Washington are continuing, even as the US carried out “heavy” strikes on Iran in retaliation for fresh attacks targeting American forces in Jordan.

“Negotiations between parties are ongoing, particularly on strait of Hormuz and to de-escalate,” Pakistani foreign ministry spokesperson Tahir Andrabi told reporters.

Pakistan had helped to broker a deal between the warring sides laying the groundwork for negotiations on a permanent resolution, and Andrabi – cited by AFP – said it was doing its “utmost to bring all parties back” to technical talks under the agreement.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said on Thursday that two oil tankers attempting to transit the Hormuz strait with US support turned back after one of them caught fire.

Israeli official says disarmament plan does not meet Israel’s demands for Gaza demilitarisation – report

An Israeli official has said the Trump-chaired Board of Peace’s 15-point disarmament proposal does not meet Israel’s demands for Gaza’s complete demilitarisation, the Times of Israel is reporting.

So it is “unclear how the agreement would be able to proceed”, it says

Reports of a breakthrough started emerging before Donald Trump’s announcement but an Israeli official appeared to temper the optimism, saying the proposal “does not adequately address” Israeli demands for the “complete demilitarisation, [which is] a precondition for any process”, the report said.

Benjamin Netanyahu’s government raised these objections recently to Board of Peace envoy Tony Blair, the Israeli official said in a statement, without specifying its issues with the disarmament proposal, according to the report.

The official added:

There will be no IDF [Israel Defence Forces] withdrawal from the Yellow Line in the Gaza Strip before Hamas is disarmed and the Strip is fully demilitarised.

A yellow block demarcating the ‘Yellow Line’ that separates Gaza’s Israeli-held and Palestinian zones since the October 2025 truce, seen in Khan Younis, southern Gaza. Photograph: Abdel Kareem Hana/AP

The proposal would see Hamas gradually hand over its weapons over an eight-month period to the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, the Board of Peace-overseen panel of Palestinian technocrats that is tasked with replacing the militant group in governing Gaza, the Times report quoted an Arab diplomat familiar with the negotiations as saying.

Hamas recognised that accepting the disarmament proposal would “put the ball in Israel’s court”, the diplomat said.

Hamas says deal includes Israel's 'gradual withdrawal' from Gaza – report

Senior Hamas officials are saying the agreement reached with Israel includes provisions addressing the group’s weapons and the gradual withdrawal of Israeli forces from Gaza, Agence France-Presse is reporting.

The news agency quoted a Hamas official as saying that “an agreement has been reached regarding the issue of weapons ... Furthermore, an agreement has been reached on a gradual withdrawal” of Israeli forces from Gaza.

The official also said the Islamist movement now expected the mediators and the Board of Peace to ensure that Israel complied with the terms of the deal.

We are yet to get official word from Israel in response to Donald Trump’s disarmament deal announcement.

Hamas officials confirm deal to end conflict with Israel - report

Senior Hamas officials have reportedly confirmed they have reached an agreement to end the conflict with Israel, following Donald Trump’s announcement the group was prepared to disarm.

AFP quoted two officials from the Palestinian Islamist movement, speaking on condition of anonymity, as saying it had come to the agreement.

The first weapons to be transferred under the deal will be from police, followed by the decommissioning of heavy weapons, a Board of Peace official is reportedly saying.

All military activity in Gaza would need to stop under the implementation of the plan, Reuters is also quoting the official as saying.

As mentioned, we are yet to hear an official response from Israel to Trump’s announcement.

There has been no immediate statement from Hamas on the Trump’s agreement announcement – but it came after sources familiar with the ongoing talks with mediators in Cairo told Agence France-Presse that the militant Palestinian group was progressing towards a deal involving the decommissioning of its weapons.

The Israeli embassy in Washington and Hamas did not immediately respond to requests for comment about Trump’s post, Reuters is reporting.

The drone strike, similar to others that have hit Egypt’s Gulf and Arab allies throughout the US-Israeli war against Iran, has not been claimed by any warring party.
