Rob Key has refused to rule out the possibility of Ben Stokes being tempted out of international retirement after backing new Test coach Stephen Fleming to be the man to turn England’s fortunes around.

England’s managing director Key admitted he "wouldn’t be surprised" if the all-rounder made a headline-grabbing return to the fold.

Key’s own position has faced considerable scrutiny following a turbulent summer that saw Stokes step away from international duty and Brendon McCullum removed as red-ball coach. Now, Key is attempting to rebuild, identifying former New Zealand captain Fleming and returning captain Joe Root as the crucial partnership to restore stability and success to a side that has clearly lost its way.

Speaking at Lord’s to unveil his latest vision for English cricket, Key did not rule out a dramatic comeback for the Durham all-rounder.

When asked if Fleming had raised the idea of Stokes’ return, Key responded: "We haven’t got round to that at the moment, but it would be a great story."

Pressed further by Sky Sports on whether Stokes himself had given any indication, a smiling Key added: "No, he hasn’t but I wouldn’t be surprised. Anything’s possible with Ben." He firmly dismissed any notion of irreparable damage, stating: "No (bridges have been burned)… not at all. Not as far as I’m concerned."

Fleming, 53, brings a reputation as a highly respected tactician and man manager from his extensive tenure as New Zealand captain, during which he forged a strong bond with McCullum.

This existing relationship is seen as a significant asset for the England and Wales Cricket Board, particularly as they navigate the complexities of a split coaching regime, an approach that has previously led to tensions between figures like Andy Flower and Ashley Giles, and Matthew Mott and McCullum.

However, Fleming’s immediate absence from the forthcoming series against Pakistan has caused some consternation. Having recently concluded a long stint in charge of Chennai Super Kings, he will take a break before arriving in the country in September, leaving him with seven Tests and no home series before the Ashes, but Key remained resolute on this point.

"My job is to get the best person. It wasn’t a stipulation for any of the candidates that you had to be there for Pakistan. Had that been the case, that would have limited the field drastically,” he explained.

He acknowledged Fleming’s prior commitments, adding: "In life, you have a lot of things that you have to do, that you can’t do, that have been planned a long way in advance, that are really important for him as well."

The appointment of Fleming marks the fourth overseas coach among England’s last six Test appointments, following McCullum, Trevor Bayliss, and Andy Flower, raising questions about the domestic coaching pipeline.

Key conceded that progress on this front has been slow but indicated that new opportunities for homegrown candidates to join the setup could be imminent, with Glamorgan’s Richard Dawson emerging as a leading contender.

![Joe Root has been reappointed England Test captain](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/31/13/ba67caf50b10fcc80f5bdf60ad4f2fc6Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1NTg1MzI2-2.84983393.jpg)

"It’s not happened quick enough, but that’s a massive part of what we’re going to try and do going forward," he stated.

Key emphasised the dual responsibility of appointing the best available talent while also nurturing domestic coaches.

"One of the first things Stephen spoke about was that. We have a duty as well to bring coaches through, to upskill and to give them experiences. It’s a problem we’re all trying to solve, it’s just not a quick fix.

"In Stephen and Joe, I think we’ve got the two best people to take what is a side with a huge amount of potential. I’m as optimistic as ever that we’ve got the best people to take English cricket forward."
