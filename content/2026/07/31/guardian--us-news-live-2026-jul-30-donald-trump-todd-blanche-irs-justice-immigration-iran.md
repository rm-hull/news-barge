Two federal appeals courts reject ICE policy to detain undocumented residents with no criminal records

Two federal appeals courts ruled on Thursday that the Trump administration cannot deny immigrants detained inside the United States a chance for release on bond, rejecting a new policy to detain even undocumented immigrants with no criminal records who have resided in the US for years.

In a pair of 2-1 decisions, majorities of the San Francisco-based 9th US circuit court of appeals and the Chicago-based 7th US circuit court of appeals agreed with four previous appeals court rulings across the country that a 1996 law does not mandate the detention of every undocumented immigrant who initially entered the country unlawfully, no matter how long ago.

The majority decision in the 9th circuit court was written by Daniel Bress, a judge appointed by Donald Trump in 2019.

Two appeals courts, the Fifth circuit in New Orleans and Eighth circuit in St Louis have sided with the Trump administration.

The split between the circuit courts increases the likelihood that the US supreme court will eventually take up the issue, as the administration requested last month.
