Anyone who’s pressed snooze one too many times will know the lifeline that the best dry shampoo can provide. Of course, there’s always the option of excess hairspray and a slick-back bun, but this can leave you contending with all-day neck pain and the risk of traction alopecia as a result of the tension and follicle stress?

Not only is a dry shampoo the quicker solution, but – according to hair stylist Joe Kelly (who’s tended to the locks of Iris Law and Maya Jama) – “Dry shampoo isn’t just for absorbing oil.” On the contrary, he told me, “I mostly use it as a texture or finishing spray – I love the fluffy, lightweight effect it gives, and how it can instantly make hair feel fuller, softer and more lived-in without making it stiff.”

These days, dry shampoos are a dime a dozen, whether you’re looking for a cheap as chips supermarket option or a luxury splurge. As such, the challenge ahead was clear: whittle down the hundreds of sprays, powders and aerosols on the market to an easily-digestible list of the very best dry shampoos.

Armed with more than 15 formulas, myself and Daisy Lester – the brunette to my blonde – set about testing the industry’s most hyped dry shampoos over the course of eight weeks. These were the ones that earned our IndyBest seal of approval.

K18’s dry shampoo almost appears too good to be true, but it really is the best dry shampoo we’ve tested. Simply spray on your roots and work it in with your fingertips – the results are instantly clean-looking hair that lasts all-day. I find it works even better as a pre-bed treatment to wake up with faux freshly washed hair.

Just like Daisy, I was impressed with K18’s airwash dry shampoo. Whether you’re suffering from day two oily roots or flat hair, the magic of airwash will come to your rescue.

The brand’s trademarked ‘odorbind’ technology claims to eliminate odours for up to three days, while translucent microbeads work to absorb grease like a sponge. There’s even hyaluronic acid in the mix to stave off the dryness that’s part and parcel of so many dry shampoos.

The result is a light-as-air formula that’s easily distributed across the scalp with a quick brush. I’ve found it takes as few as three spritzes to do the job, and that includes when my hair needs a serious intervention. Granted, it’s expensive, but my last bottle saw me through more than six months of oily roots, and it does the job of a texture spray, too.

Batiste’s brunette dry shampoo is a famously budget option if you need a quick refresh between washes. It’s cheap, easy to find and initially does the job well, absorbing oil and making hair look cleaner for a couple of hours. The brunette tint is useful and the range of colour options gives good versatility for different hair shades, which helps it blend in better than standard white dry shampoos.

However, the formula doesn’t feel particularly pleasant in the hair. Instead of leaving it lightweight and soft, it can make hair feel stiff, sticky and slightly coated after use. I also noticed some white residue, especially around dry-prone areas of the scalp, which defeats the purpose of a tinted dry shampoo somewhat. While it works well enough as a short-term fix on a budget, the finish and texture could definitely be improved for anyone wanting a softer, more natural feel overall.

Batiste’s dry shampoos are cheap as chips and, on the whole, are a failsafe for greasy roots and hair that’s begun to lay flat against your scalp. That said, I’ve always found it easy to turn my strands prematurely grey with an overzealous spritz – so the colour range (available in four shades) definitely caught my eye. I tested the version for blonde hair (£3.75, Amazon.co.uk)

It’s easy to be put off by the mustard yellow hue of the formula, but it’s this tint that allows you to apply carelessly without risking white patches. It also brings oodles of volume to heavy, build up-laden hair. You don’t need a huge amount (four to five spritzes was ample on my medium-length locks) and it really does drink up excess oils in a jiffy.

After use, the sweeping quiff I was able to give my front pieces was seriously impressive. Admittedly, you’ll notice this formula in your hair, and touching it through the day will leave your fingers feeling chalky. However for less than £5, that’s a trade-off I’m happy to take.

The shower-fresh scent is irresistible, too, and I’m guilty of using it like hair perfume on freshly cleansed strands.

For those unfamiliar, Typebea haircare is the brainchild of Rita Ora and Anna Lahey, founder of Vida Glow. The entire range is designed to support hair growth, so it’s no surprise the dry shampoo goes beyond basic oil absorption. Specifically, you’ll find exfoliating lactic acid, 3 per cent baicapil (a plant-based growth complex) and peptides, all of which are geared towards creating a healthier scalp environment.

In practice, it left my hair feeling genuinely clean without chalkiness or residual grease. It didn’t add much lift at the roots, but was excellent at tackling clumping (my personal between-wash nemesis) and stopped my hair from lying flat against my scalp.

![Typebea G5 3-in-1 dry shampoo for hair growth, best dry shampoos, IndyBest]([https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/02/15/50/Typebea-G5-3-in-1-dry-shampoo-for-hair-growth-best-dry-shampoos-IndyBest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/02/15/50/Typebea-G5-3-in-1-dry-shampoo-for-hair-growth-best-dry-shampoos-IndyBest.png?quality=75&width=608&auto=webp) 608w)

I’d say it’s best on day-two hair, rather than when you’re dealing with heavier oil build-up. At a push I could make it work on day three, but would need a second round of spritzing later in the day. That said, even with a generous application, there was no white cast in sight on my blonde hair.

After several uses, I can’t claim centimetre-level growth, but the added scalp focus certainly didn’t compromise its cleansing credentials. If anything, those ingredients made it feel like a slightly more considered take on a wash-day stopgap – and I’m more than happy to pocket the potential long-term perks.
