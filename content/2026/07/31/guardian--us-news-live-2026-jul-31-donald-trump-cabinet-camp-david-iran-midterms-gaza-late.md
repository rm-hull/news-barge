Trump attacks Republican senator and defends $1.8bn slush fund amid Blanche nomination standoff – live

Trump says John Cornyn, who is questioning Todd Blanche’s attorney general nomination, didn’t have a problem with IRS slush fund until he didn’t endorse the senator in Texas primary

Trump attacks Cornyn and defends $1.8bn slush fund amid Blanche nomination standoff

Donald Trump went after US senator John Cornyn this morning, saying Cornyn didn’t have a problem with the IRS slush fund until after Trump endorsed his opponent, Ken Paxton, in the Republican primary in Texas, which Paxton won.

Trump also defended the $1.8bn slush fund to pay out people who claim they were targets of political weaponization which also grants Trump and his businesses immunity from past tax investigations. The fund, and Trump’s refusal to officially end it, have stalled the nomination for attorney general Todd Blanche, with Cornyn and Thom Tillis holding out.

Trump’s defense of the slush fund is curious, as his administration has claimed the fund is not moving forward but not committed in writing to end it. Cornyn and Tillis have sought written confirmation from the justice department that the fund will not proceed.

In a Truth Social post, Trump said the fund “will not benefit me, but rather the great American Patriots who were hunted down like dogs and whose lives were unfairly and illegally destroyed by the Crooked Joe Biden Administration”. He went on to defend the people who could benefit from the fund, noting that he “gave up any payments for myself, which would have been substantial”.

“I will always feel that these victims of government abuse should be paid back for what they were forced to endure, their lives have been ruined!” Trump wrote. “Millions of people agree with me, as was proven in my landslide Presidential win, where this was a major subject of debate, and so did John Cornyn, until he lost his Election to an opponent that I Endorsed.”

In the last few days before Michigan’s 4 August primary, US representative Haley Stevens accused her opponent, Abdul El-Sayed, of blaming “all of (his) problems on Jewish Americans”.

Stevens quoted-tweeted a video of El-Sayed talking about massive outside funding from the groups affiliated with American Israel Public Affairs Committee (Aipac), where El-Sayed said Aipac should “come for me” and called Stevens “the least capable candidate in America”.

Stevens wrote that when she and El-Sayed were both on the ballot in 2018, in different races, she won her race and El-Sayed lost in a primary to Gretchen Whitmer, the state’s governor.

“Everyone in America understands you want to blame all of your problems on Jewish Americans,” Stevens wrote.

She said El-Sayed would lose to Mike Rogers, the likely Republican candidate in November, talking like he did in the video. El-Sayed will “be a celebrity whining and writing books to get even richer. You’re part of the complainer class, looking for their next book or podcast deal,” she wrote.

“Bro, I just want to work for Michigan,” she concluded.

A new survey of voters in 18 congressional toss-up districts by the Working Families Party showed voters, and especially independents,want candidates who challenge the political system.

The survey found 80% of voters agreed that elected officials care more about wealthy donors and powerful interests than people like them. When given a choice between a generic candidate who is an experienced elected official who focuses on “practical reforms” who gets funding from established groups and party donors, or a “political outsider who says the system is broken” who calls for changes to the status quo, the outsider wins by 14 points in the poll, including among independents and moderates.

“The survey does not show that every progressive policy or organizational cue is equally effective,” a polling memo from the Working Families Party says. “Instead, it points to a broader candidate posture: challenge concentrated power, demonstrate independence from party leadership, and connect change to material improvements in people’s lives.”

The Working Families Party, a progressive minor political party that’s growing in recent years, concludes that advocating for changing the political system instead of a conventional move toward the political center in a competitive district is a more winnable strategy.

“The strongest candidate in this environment is therefore not simply the most progressive or the most moderate,” the memo says. “It is the candidate who can credibly demonstrate that they are independent of entrenched power, willing to challenge their own party, and focused on delivering meaningful improvements for working people.”

President Donald Trump will convene a cabinet meeting later today at his Camp David retreat as he grapples with how to resolve his war against Iran.

He will also be keen to find a way to bring down gasoline prices as the Republicans head into the November midterm elections.

This will be only his third trip to Camp David during his second term, with Trump largely having stayed away from the mountaintop presidential retreat in western Maryland.

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt told reporters at a briefing last week that this would be the 13th Cabinet meeting of Trump’s second term and that it would be “a lot of fun and something different for the cabinet to experience together.”

A sign for Camp David, the presidential retreat, is pictured near Thurmont, Md., Aug. 18, 2023. Photograph: Andrew Harnik/AP

The agenda for the meeting will likely focus heavily on foreign policy, Reuters reported. Trump is engaged in tit-for-tat strikes against Iranian targets with no end in sight to a conflict that the president initially projected would last just a few weeks but is now five months old.

The Iran war has sent gasoline prices spiraling and taken a toll on his popularity across the US. This week, a Reuters-Ipsos poll found just one in three Americans support the war, the lowest in the poll since the conflict’s early days.

In another foreign policy development, Trump on Thursday night announced that his so-called “Board of Peace” reached an agreement for the complete disarmament of Hamas militants and other armed groups in Gaza. Israel has yet to respond officially to the plan.
