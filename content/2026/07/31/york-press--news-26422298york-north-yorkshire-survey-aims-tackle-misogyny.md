Young people are being asked to help shape a safer future through a new survey on gender violence, sexism, and misogyny.

Launched by the York and North Yorkshire Youth Commission, the survey aims to gather the experiences of 10 to 25-year-olds and help shape future responses from local authorities and partner organisations.

Jo Coles, Deputy Mayor for Policing, Fire and Crime, said: "The work of our Youth Commission shines a light on the reality too many young people face, from everyday sexism and misogyny to harassment and online abuse.

"They ensure the voices of young people across our region are heard and properly integrated into the work of North Yorkshire Police and North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service."

The survey, which can be viewed at [https://forms.gle/2zw7a8rzjjSiuQvT9,](https://forms.gle/2zw7a8rzjjSiuQvT9,) extends to those who are not in formal education or connected to youth organisations.

A previous study by the commission revealed that many young people feel unsafe and disrespected by behaviours such as catcalling, harassment, and unwanted attention in public spaces.

Emily Blavins, project manager at Leaders Unlocked, said: "This youth-led survey will help ensure even more young voices are heard, particularly those who may not regularly engage with schools, youth organisations or other services."

The survey is open until August 31, 2026.
