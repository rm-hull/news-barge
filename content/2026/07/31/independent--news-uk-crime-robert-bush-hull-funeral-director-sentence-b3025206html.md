A funeral director who hoarded bodies for months and gave bereaved families the wrong ashes has been jailed for 20 years.

For 12 years, Robert Bush breached the trust of his customers at Legacy Independent Funeral Directors “on an almost industrial scale” and “in a myriad of ways”, from mixing up ashes to fleecing vulnerable pensioners with fake funeral plans.

On Friday, he was told by a judge that the 20-year prison term could not “put right the harm the defendant has done”.

Mr Justice Hilliard said even people who had used Bush’s services but were not directly involved with the case could not be sure they had been given their loved one’s ashes.

He said: “Unless a body was one of those actually recovered by the police in 2024, and was properly identified, no-one who had a cremation at Legacy at any time can actually know for certain whether they received the right ashes for their loved one, whether or not their case features on the indictment.”

![Robert Bush has admitted multiple charges of fraud in relation to his business, Legacy Funeral Directors, in Hull (Annabel Lee-Ellis/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/31/13/c59297bfff6a7db98885436d35467744Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1NTY2Nzcy-2.85426967.jpg)

He went on to say Bush was “so pre-occupied with his own enrichment that he behaved in a shocking and disgraceful way towards the deceased entrusted to his care, and his behaviour towards their living relatives was characterised by dishonesty, deception and heartlessness”.

Bush was motivated by “financial greed” and had a “heartless side to him too”, while Legacy’s Hessle Road premises were “dreadful”.

The judge said: “If he could re-use a coffin someone had already paid for, he would do so.

“If it was cheaper to keep someone in non-refrigerated conditions, he would do that too.”

The judge said what the dead lacked in dignity at the hands of Robert Bush “is more than made up for by the dignity with which the relatives have conducted themselves on their behalf in these proceedings”.

![Police outside the Anlaby Road branch of Legacy Independent Funeral Directors in Hull](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/31/13/01KYVN223G6GHWAEVCPE95MSF8.jpg)

Over four days, Bush, 48, looked emotionless in the dock as statements from more than 200 victims were read in court, with many who chose to read them in person breaking down in tears.

One woman who was given the wrong ashes for her husband told the defendant he had fooled people in Hull with his mild manner, when in fact he was a “cruel conman who used people’s grief to fund his lifestyle”.

The court heard Bush’s offending came to light in March 2024 when he was on holiday with his daughter in Arizona and asked two directors of another funeral firm to help with a body recovery while he was away.

A casual worker at Legacy told the two men “out of the blue” that bodies had been at the site “for years”.

Police were called and searched the site, uncovering a “shocking” scene where decomposing bodies had been left on the racks of a cold storage area, with the remains of a foetus contained in a paper bag on the floor.

The court heard the rest of the site was in a “lamentable state”, with ashes kept in mouldy boxes and even strewn across the floor, photographs left out of coffins and jewellery not returned to families.

![Mr Justice Hilliard sentencing funeral director Robert Bush, 48, to twenty years in prison for 67 offences, including 30 counts of preventing a lawful burial, at Hull Crown Court, Kingston-upon-Hull. Picture date: Friday July 31, 2026.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/31/13/01KYW1M7CK0QNRNKTNK94S3DRQ.jpg)

The investigation found that 31 bodies, including that of unborn baby Sunny Beverley-Conlin, should have been cremated months earlier, and most of their families had already been given ashes.

Relatives of people whose bodies were discovered described their disgust at learning what had happened, with one daughter saying the image reminded her of Auschwitz.

Officers were able to identify another 57 families who had either been given the wrong ashes or no ashes, but the court heard there was also a large quantity of unlabelled ashes at the site, and no-one who used Legacy during that time could ever know for sure if they had the right ones.

Several people recounted how they had jewellery made out of the ashes, only to find out they had been carrying around “a total stranger”.

The details behind Bush’s widespread practice of giving out the wrong ashes are still largely unknown, and he refused to answer questions about it when asked by police.

Detective Superintendent Al Curtis said there was evidence that he was mixing up ashes and using a communal store to give out to families when they asked for their loved ones back.

The court heard of several cases when Bush gave a client the wrong ashes, but collected the right ones from the crematorium as little as two days later.

![People demonstrate outside Hull Crown Court ahead of the sentencing of funeral director Robert Bush (Annabel Lee-Ellis/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/27/18/4f179bd665572eae762773a0ac5787e2Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1MjU5Mjc4-2.85427028.jpg)

In his police interviews he said: “I’ve never given ashes back that weren’t ashes,” but refused to go into any further detail.

He also said that all the ashes were human, although an urn given to Lucy Ness – one of four women given the wrong ashes for their unborn babies by Bush – was found to contain what are thought to be animal remains.

In one police interview, Bush said he had developed a cash flow problem because “people did not always pay” and had debts of around £90,000.

He said his plan had been to generate more business and clear the backlog within two to three months, but had never managed to “catch up”.

Despite his debts, the court heard that Bush was “profligate” in his personal spending, and over seven years, while he was defrauding customers he spent £42,000 on holidays and £21,000 on motorcycle racing.

The court heard that many of his customers were on limited budgets, with one woman describing how she had paid for her mother’s funeral partly through an online fundraising page.

Bush often appeared to be helpful towards people looking for more affordable options, offering discounts and telling them it would be cheaper to use his on-site chapel.

Brooke Atkinson, who had been estranged from her father before he died, said Bush “could sense they hadn’t arranged a funeral” and accused him of “picking the most vulnerable of society”.

The court also heard from pensioners who said they had been “conned” out of what little savings they had after being sold fake funeral plans by Bush.

Over almost 12 years he sold plans to 226 people but kept the money, pocketing more than half a million pounds in the process.

Bush also stole money from 12 charities by not passing on donations collected at his funeral services.

He pleaded guilty to 30 counts of preventing a lawful and decent burial and 30 counts of fraud by false representation relating to bodies found at the site, four “foetus allegations” of fraud, where he presented ashes to women falsely saying that they were “the remains of their unborn”, a further charge of fraud covering the ashes of 57 people between 2017 and 2024, fraudulent trading relating to funeral plans between 2012 and 2024 and theft from 12 charities.

## ‘The hardest part is not knowing why’: Son of woman found in funeral home

A son whose mother’s body was left in Robert Bush’s funeral parlour for six months said the “hardest part is not knowing why he did it”.

Ron Marsh contacted Legacy Independent Funeral Directors when his mother Joyce Moulton died in September 2023, aged 98.

He said Bush was professional and the service he organised was “excellent”, but the family never received Mrs Moulton’s ashes – and attempts to contact Legacy went unanswered.

![Undated handout photo issued by Humberside Police of victim Joyce Moulton, whose body was recovered from Hessle Road](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/31/13/01KN6Y2AYR3RVJ6ARYD5N6W1SM.jpg)

In March 2024, he was visited at his Surrey home by police family liaison officers who told him his mother’s body had been found during a raid at Legacy’s premises in Hull.

Mr Marsh, 79, said: “I was gobsmacked. It was just not anything I ever thought happened, would happen, and certainly not happen to my mum.

“I couldn’t comprehend something like that happening, so it was a struggle for me to understand what had happened, why it had happened, and why he had allowed it to happen.”

He went on to say: “It was very traumatic, very upsetting. It’s probably not a strong enough word, but I can’t think of one.

“But I just would like to know what the hell he thought he was doing, because the complete disregard for the dignity of 30 people, and their families, I can’t understand how someone could do it.

“And, unfortunately, I don’t think we’re going to get the chance to ask him, because he’s pleaded guilty, so he doesn’t have to take the stand.

“It will be interesting to see what the mitigating circumstances are that his defence puts up, but as far as I’m concerned, he is a very despicable man with absolutely no morals or human compassion.”

Mr Marsh said his mother was “a grafter” who worked while raising him and his brother as a single parent, before marrying her second husband.

She returned to her home city of Hull to be near her siblings after being widowed in 1990.

Mrs Moulton had dementia and died in hospital in September 2023, two weeks after falling at home.

Mr Marsh contacted Bush after seeing business cards for Legacy at the morgue, and being told by a morgue attendant that “they’re very good”.

“He was professional, courteous, a businessman,” he said.

“We had the service. It was excellent. You could not have wished for a better service.

“When we paid the bill, he said: ‘Your mother will be cremated within the next two to three days.’ I thought that’s it, done deal.”

When the family had not received Mrs Moulton’s ashes by December, Mr Marsh attempted to contact Bush, but his phone calls and emails went unreturned.

After finding out that Bush had never cremated Mrs Moulton’s body, her family have had a second funeral paid for by Hull City Council, and her ashes have been scattered in one of their memorial gardens.

Mr Marsh said: “At the time, it looked like he was a genuine, bona fide, funeral director – three funeral homes, a successful person, and the service itself was very professional.

“But it has left me wondering, how many times has this happened somewhere else, maybe not to the extent of 30 odd bodies, but have other funeral people not carried out their duties, as they should do?

“Because there is no legislation with regard to funeral directors, and therefore, there’s no checking to see that once a funeral service has been had, if it’s not at the crematorium or at the church, that body has actually been either cremated or buried.

“So it still gets to me and I think it will for the rest of my life.

“But it’s the not knowing why he did it that is really more upsetting than anything else. And I don’t think we’ll ever know.”

Mr Marsh paid tribute to his mother as “pugnacious, but generous to a fault”.

He said: “When she went back up to Hull, and in the bungalow, there were a load of what she termed old people, albeit she was in her 80s, but there were old people around her who needed help, and she used to do the shopping for them.

“She always wanted to help people, and she was quite fearless.”

## Prime Minister backs calls for regulation of funeral home sector

The sentencing comes as Andy Burnham backed the regulation of funeral homes and mortuaries.

The Prime Minister offered his sympathies to the families of victims of Robert Bush.

A separate independent inquiry into the crimes of David Fuller, who abused at least 100 dead women and girls in hospital mortuaries in south-east England, recommended regulation of the funeral sector to prevent similar crimes being committed again.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/07/31/13/6c4eed17ef3834b8ffeadead46e90922Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg1NTgwNjEz-2.85456054.jpg)

Speaking on a visit to Sheffield, Mr Burnham said: “I do back those calls, and there were recommendations in the Fuller Report that will be implemented because we have seen appalling situations like this before.

“I feel for the families and I know it’ll be a difficult day for them with these matters in court again. I was truly horrified when I heard what had happened. Appalling for any family to go through that. This needs a proper response in terms of the regulation needed, to make sure no family goes through that again.

“People, I think, would assume that the highest standards apply to this area and we’ve got to make sure that they do apply and that there can’t be any repeat of what we’ve seen.”
