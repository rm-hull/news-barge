LinkedIn is reportedly testing a new 'seems like AI slop' button, allowing users to flag posts suspected of being generated by artificial intelligence.

This initiative follows a 404 Media report indicating that a significant percentage of LinkedIn's long-form (41%) and short-form (30%) posts are likely AI-generated.

Hari Srinivasan, LinkedIn's chief product officer, confirmed the company's commitment to tackling 'AI slop' to preserve authentic human interaction and perspectives on the platform.

The professional networking site is also replacing its 'enhance with AI' post-drafting option with a proofreading tool that avoids significant AI-driven content rewriting.

Other major platforms, including Meta (which removed its Muse Image tool) and Reddit, have also recently implemented measures to address or restrict AI-generated content following user concerns.
