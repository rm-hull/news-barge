Whether you’re a practicing yogi or a Pilates devotee, the best yoga leggings have become a wardrobe staple - taking us from the gym to brunch and even, if you have a casual dresscode, to the office.

Unsurprisingly, their boom has led to a surge in options. From £20 high street leggings to £130 moisture-wicking flares, almost every brand has released a version, making choosing the right ones even trickier.

As a former track athlete and daily yogi who’s encountered everything from chafing to slippage, see-through fabrics and annoying camel toe, I’ve spent the last month separating the best from the rest. I know just how personal yoga pants can be, with the right balance of style and comfort being key to a confident practice.

Saskia Bolscher, senior instructor at MoreYoga, agrees. “Yoga pants need to fit so well that you hardly notice you're wearing them, and shouldn’t need to be adjusted mid practice,” she says. “You should be able to move in all directions with no worries about them being see-through, especially in moves like downward dog.”

Fortunately, I’ve got Bolscher’s criteria covered. From household names like Lululemon and H&M to viral challengers such as TALA and Oner Active, there’s a pair here for everyone - whether you prefer flares or leggings, a snug fit or relaxed style.

I have *lived* in the ONER Active softmotion flared bottoms since they arrived on my doorstep. If you’re looking for a silky-soft, barely-there feeling, I couldn’t recommend them more as the best yoga pants I’ve tested.

They slip on easily, run true to size and come in three different lengths. For me, at 5ft 9in, the long length perfectly grazes the floor rather than my ankles, and importantly, they didn’t shrink even after multiple washes - a frustration I’ve often found with other flared leggings. I’d say for anyone 6ft and under, they’d be comfortably long enough.

For me, Oner finds the sweet spot between figure hugging and restriction. They’re snug without cutting you in half, which allows for free movement across all planes of motion.

They’re really hard to fault, though if you’re looking for complete versatility to suit both high and low intensity exercise, you might prefer a legging rather than a flare.

Switching to a natural fibre legging takes some getting used to, as the sensation is heavier, and less stretchy, than nylon. Made from bamboo, cotton and elastane, Bam’s enduro leggings do offer a little stretch but still definitely run looser than less natural materials.

It’s why I’d challenge the brand’s claims that the Enduro leggings are sculpting. I found the waistband to be a little too roomy, causing it to slip and ruche. If I was to repurchase, I’d definitely size down to get the more figure-hugging fit.

What I can’t critique, however, is the thickness of the material, which washes well, holds its colour, doesn’t thin and has absolutely no risk of going see-through mid squat. They’re also highly breathable, meaning they work well for hotter practices.

All in all, if you’re looking for a non-toxic composition, with top tier eco credentials, this is the pair for you. If a flattering shape is more what’s on your mind, I’d look elsewhere.
