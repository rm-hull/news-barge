Gianni Infantino is under mounting pressure from football federations as well as from within Fifa after Uefa’s boycott of the World Cup left his plans to privatise its tournaments in tatters.

Infantino’s long-term adviser Carlos Cordeiro, a key figure within Fifa, has quit in protest of Infantino’s proposals, while the Asian Football Confederation has become the third federation to reject them publicly, releasing a strong statement on Friday backing the stance of Uefa and Concacaf, while claiming that confidence in Fifa’s governance has been “undermined”.

It comes after Fifa hit back at Uefa, insisting that “nobody is selling football” and confirming that it will continue its consultation period ahead of a vote on 19 September. But after another federation joined Uefa’s side, Infantino looks unlikely to get the votes he needs and is instead facing growing opposition to his leadership.

On Thursday, Uefa announced that it would boycott the World Cup and all Fifa competitions if Infantino’s controversial plans to sell off stakes in its major tournaments to external investors are voted through.

“Uefa and its 55 member associations stand as one,” a statement said following an emergency Uefa meeting. “We unanimously and unequivocally reject Fifa’s proposal to transfer ownership interests in the World Cup and other Fifa competitions to private investors.”

**Oceania**

The Oceania Football Confederation (OFC) has 11 member associations, including nations such as the Cook Islands and Tahiti, that are among the smallest of Fifa’s 211 members. It does not include Australia, which is part of the AFC.

In a statement on Thursday, the OFC said Fifa’s proposals would be discussed by the by the OFC Executive Committee in August. It didn’t criticise Fifa’s plans or support them,

It added: “OFC remains committed to engaging constructively with its Member Associations, Fifa, fellow Confederations and other football stakeholders throughout the consultation process and in support of the continued development of football across Oceania and globally.”

**Asia (AFC)**

The Asian Football Confederation, which has 47 members, released a statement on Friday morning confirming that it “stands in solidarity with Uefa and Concacaf” in expressing “serious concerns” over Fifa’s proposal.

“The fact that the situation has reached the point where the real possibility of a World Cup boycott has entered public discourse should concern everyone who cares about the future of our game,” the AFC said.

“The AFC believes that the proposed [Football Forward Enterprise] cannot realistically achieve the necessary broad consensus and unity required to move forward,” it added.

Even stronger criticism came from the AFC president Salman bin Ibrahim al-Khalifa on Thursday. He said Fifa’s approach was “unacceptable” and “undermine the very foundations of continental football”.

The AFC also called for Fifa to “undertake an urgent review of its governance and decision-making framework”.

Or The Confederation of North, Central America and Caribbean Association Football, has 41 members. In a statement on Thursday, following their own emergency meting, Concacaf “rejected the proposal” and expressed “deep concerns about the lack of due process”.

Concacaf stopped short of joining Uefa’s boycott, however, but it is understood that the vast majority of its members have lost confidence in Infantino’s presidency. “The discussion reinforced the need for greater transparency and proper governance,” Concacaf said.

## 'A defining question for Fifa's future'

Cordeiro concludes his statement to Sky News by calling the proposal for external investment the ‘defining question for Fifa’s future’ and took aim at Fifa for prioritising commerical returns over strengthening the game.

"This proposal has become a defining question for FIFA's future. After careful consideration, I can no longer continue in my role as Senior Advisor to the FIFA President. I have therefore resigned with immediate effect,” he said.

"FIFA's responsibility is not to maximise commercial returns at any cost. It is to protect and strengthen football for future generations. When those principles come into conflict, football must come first.

"It has been the privilege of a lifetime to serve FIFA for more than five years and to work alongside dedicated, principled people who care deeply about the game. I hope they, too, will speak up, because decisions of this magnitude should be made in the interests of football, not those who stand to profit from it."

## 'Not a responsible way to determine the future of the game'

Cordeiro’s statement goes further in accusing Fifa of being irresponsible in putting this proposal to member associations at such short notice with many questions remaining unanswered.

His statement continues: "Such economic success was not accidental. It was delivered by experienced football professionals who shattered FIFA's commercial records. Yet despite those achievements, FIFA is now being asked to believe that outside investors are needed to unlock greater value. I do not accept that proposition. The people who built this success have already demonstrated that FIFA possesses the expertise to continue growing both the game and its commercial future.

"Most troubling of all is the absence of answers to fundamental questions. Why this deal? Why now? What oversight exists? Who benefits? Was there a competitive process? What governance will be in place? What will investors ultimately gain, and at what cost to football?

"These questions remain largely unanswered, and yet Member Associations are being asked to make a decision of enormous consequence in barely 50 days or risk being left behind. That is not a responsible way to determine the future of the world's game.”

## Cordeiro condemns World Cup plans

Cordeiro took the attack to Fifa’s plans to sell off stakes in the World Cup and told Sky News that this plan ‘makes little sense’ given Fifa’s secure and stable financial footing.

"Football has been central to my life, and after more than 35 years in banking, I understand both the value of this asset and the consequences of giving part of it away. That is why this proposal should be rejected,” Coreirdo said in a statement.

"FIFA already has access to extraordinary financial resources. The organisation sits on billions of dollars in reserves and no debt. The FIFA President himself has highlighted the $15 billion in revenue generated between 2022 and 2026.

“If Member Associations believe additional investment is needed to develop the game, FIFA already has the financial capacity to provide that support from its existing resources. Against that backdrop, selling a permanent stake in football's most valuable asset to raise $4.2bn makes little sense. It is mortgaging football's future without any compelling justification.”

## Who is Carlos Cordeiro?

Carlos Cordeiro is a senior advisor to Fifa president Gianni Infantino and has resigned his post in protest to the plans to sell-off stakes of the World Cup to private investors.

Infantino appointed Cordeiro in 2021 and Fifa explained at the time that his appointment was partly because "Cordeiro built a long career in business and global investment banking in Europe"
