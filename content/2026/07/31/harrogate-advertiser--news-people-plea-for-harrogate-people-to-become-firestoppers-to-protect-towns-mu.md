# Plea for Harrogate people to 'become firestoppers' to protect town's much-loved woodlands

## A highly-respected Harrogate conservation charity is urging people to report any suspicious activity in the Pinewoods to counter the increasing risk of wildfires at this precious site.

Pinewoods Conservation Group, which has brought people together to care for Harrogate’s famous woodland and its wildlife since 2002, is encouraging members to become fire stoppers.

With half of England declared to be in drought and firefighters in Derbyshire and Suffolk tackling major wildfires, Pinewoods Conservation Group is determined to do its bit to protect the town's cherished woodland and green spaces located above Valley Gardens.

The charity’s plea follows what the charity says is a rise in fires being started in the Pinewoods.

In their latest newsletter, Pinewoods Conservation Group says: “Regrettably, there is a rise in fires being started in the Pinewoods.

"This poses a significant danger, particularly given the current dry conditions, and has led to frequent visits from North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue.

"We urge visitors and members to report any suspicious activity to FireStoppers on 0800 1695558.

"If you need to report an incident requiring an emergency response, please dial 999.”

This weekend will also see the group turning its focus on hands-on maintenance and conservation work in the Pinewoods.

The Pinewoods Woodland Working Group session will takes place on Sunday, August 2 from 9.30am to 12.30pm.

The aim is to clear invasive species, maintain paths and tackle other practical woodland tasks.

Members are advised to bring sturdy boots and gloves and weather-appropriate clothing.

Whether you’re a regular or joining for the first time, you will be made very welcome.

Meet at the layby on Harlow Moor Road outside Harrogate Spring Water.

Please let PCG know in advance if you plan to attend via [email protected], so it can organise tasks and bring enough tools for everyone.

Pinewoods Conservation Group works alongside North Yorkshire Council to manage the Pinewoods, which is officially designated as an Asset of Community Value.

It hosts volunteer sessions on the first Sunday of each month, promotes local wildlife education and runs guided walks for visitors.
