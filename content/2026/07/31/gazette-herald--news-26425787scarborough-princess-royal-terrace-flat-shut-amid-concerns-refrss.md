A COURT order has been issued to a flat in Scarborough which restricts who can visit the premises whilst allowing the resident to remain there.

The address, located within a property on Princess Royal Terrace, was the subject of a court hearing at York Magistrates’ Court on Thursday, July 30 after concerns were raised by Scarborough’s Community Safety Hub.

The court heard evidence of ongoing criminal behaviour and anti-social conduct at the address, including fears from nearby residents who felt unsafe and did not want to leave their homes, a police spokesperson said.

  


Numerous reports had been made to police and partner agencies since the start of the year.

North Yorkshire Police said the partial order granted by magistrates "allows the resident to remain living at the address, but restricts other people other than those nominated by the court to attend the address.

"In this instance, professional support services, the landlord and anyone deemed to have a reasonable and lawful reason to attend the premises, such as utility service providers, gas, electricity and water, and emergency services."

Acting sergeant Stacey Begin of Scarborough’s Neighbourhood Policing Team said: "The behaviour shown by the resident and their associates have caused a detrimental impact on the residents in the immediate area and those living close by.

"The court has recognised the use of the premises has led to disorder within our community.

"We want our residents to feel safe in their own homes and we strive to make this happen."

The order will remain in place for three months, expiring on October 30.

Anyone who breaches the order could face arrest and up to 51 weeks in prison, a fine, or both, police said.
