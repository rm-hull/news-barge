**'Born and bred in York – will my son still have to pay tourist tax?'**

IT is not only visiting tourists who stay in hotels and guest houses.

People come to the city for various reasons – on business for example, as do visiting relatives and friends who need accommodating when visiting family, often the elderly who are in single accommodation or in care homes.

They are certainly are not ‘tourists’ – will they be expected to pay such inflated bills at the end of their stay?

Our son and grandchildren are prime examples.

They live in France and, to save us having to squeeze air beds on the floors in our old age, kindly stay in a small hotel in town.

Most certainly they are not tourists, having been born and bred here. Will they be expected to pay the tourist tax?

Another point in question – will VAT be added to this extra burden?

*Mary Morton,*

*Hob Moor Drive,*

*Holgate,*

*York*

**We can't afford to fight global warning just now**

COME on Andy Burnham, it's time to stop talking and start taking action regarding the pending energy crisis.

Following the Houthi rebels in Yemen taking over the entire Red Sea coast plus the Iran control over the Strait of Hormuz and with the oil pipeline across Saudi Arabia is closed our reliance on imported petroleum and gases is in a perilous state.

With the reliance on unpredictable wind and solar energy, it's time to stop your dithering and get your finger out.

We need oil and gas and we have it in the North Sea so we should be use it.

We have coal aplenty – start planning to dig it up.

We have decommissioned power plants – reopen them or build new ones.

The fight against global warming is an honourable fight, but can we afford it at this present moment in time? Power cuts will decimate the poor the sick and the elderly, stop dithering Andy and act before it is too late.

*D M Deamer,*

**Funicular fun**

A FUNICULAR thing happened to me when I opened The Press, (September 12), to page 14 and the title, ‘Scenic way to get to beach and sea’, showing the Saltburn Cliff Lift, a Victorian gem. It’s garnished with a dramatic photo by Mr Sturdy of the Press Camera Club, for which many thanks.

I think I’ve only ridden on the Saltburn funicular once.

As a boy I was far more familiar with its Scarborough equivalents, of which there were five at the peak of their service, but only one operational now, I think, due to various issues raised in a recent technical survey of the Scarborough Spa lift. Shame.

They were a pleasure to travel in, especially for lads of a certain ilk, and not simply for getting down there from up here or vice versa…several times.

I never experienced the Whitby cliff lift but I bet it was fun(icular).

Closed at present, I hope this ‘transport of delight’ isn’t decommissioned and eventually operates again. They were very popular in the 1940s, as I recall, but maybe demand has declined in recent years, trade being rather up and down. What’s that about ‘Use it or lose it’?

*Derek Reed,*

*Middlethorpe Drive,*

*York*
