A York musician turned businessman has unveiled a new hand-built guitar amplifier model.

Colson of York, a company founded by musician Drew Colson, has released Clear Evening Sky, a one-off analogue guitar amplifier built in the village of Bilton-in-Ainsty, east of Wetherby.

Drew said he wanted the amplifier, for sale for £995, to “blur the line between high-end musical equipment and bespoke furniture”.

The new amplifier model is the debut release in a new four-part collection called the Veils of York.

![]()Clear Evening Sky, a new guitar amplifier by Colson of York *(Image: Supplied)*

It comes after Colson of York launched its first series of amplifiers, The Three Sisters of Ainsty, drawing on Drew’s experiences of gigging in York.

The fully analogue 25-watt amplifiers have no valves or digital modelling, meaning the signal comes purely from the instrument using traditional components rather than it being converted into data then processed by software to create the sound.

Drew said Clear Evening Sky combines English sweet chestnut and Yorkshire sycamore, with its controls fitted into the solid-wood face instead of a metal chassis.

The amplifier is complete with wooden control knobs and the Colson brandmark burned into the wood.

![]()The Three Sisters of Ainsty, Maria, Charlotte and Emily, the first completed series from Colson of York *(Image: Supplied)*

Musically, Drew said the amplifier acts as a “pure, responsive canvas for effects pedals”.

“A lot of modern amplifiers can fight your pedals and guitar,” he said. “Digital amps and modellers convert the analogue signal into binary computer code, which can leave some players feeling disconnected from their instrument.

“I wanted to build an ecosystem where the guitar, the wood and the circuitry talk to each other in real time. Clear Evening Sky is designed to feel alive under your fingertips, offering an immediate, physical presence in the room.

“The foundations of this technology have been around since the 1960s, and it’s still used today.

“There’s a reason for that. It works, it sounds completely natural, and it can live forever.

“Analogue amplifier design is robust, understandable and repairable. If a component wears out years from now, the amplifier doesn’t have to become obsolete. It can be serviced, repaired and kept in use.

"That matters to me. I want these amps to be played for decades, looked after, and perhaps one day handed on.”

![]()One of the new amplifiers by Colson of York *(Image: Supplied)*

Drew said Clear Evening Sky is the first and “cleanest-sounding” offering in the new Veils of York family.

He said three companion models are set to follow later in the year, and will add varying degrees of gain and overdrive to the sound.
