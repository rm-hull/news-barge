Networks halt TV pool coverage of Trump following CNN ban

After President Trump’s snap decision to ban CNN from the White House, the four other networks that comprise the White House’s TV pool decided they would cease their pooled coverage of the president, Politico and other outlets are reporting.

Alongside CNN, those four networks – NBC, ABC, CBS, and Fox News – have provided rotating video coverage of the president both in the White House and around the country, broadly sharing the footage they gather. They are a near-permanent fixture of the presidency.

But they won’t be for the time being, it seems.

In an email to TV pool subscribers obtained by several outlets, the Washington bureau chief for Fox News said that the decision to ax pool coverage “follows the White House’s position preventing CNN from fulfilling its assigned pool duties.”

The five networks that comprise the standard pool rotation published a statement Monday afternoon: “The public has a vital interest in receiving accurate, independent information about its government. No administration should restrict a news organization because it objects to its reporting.”

Jared Golden, an outgoing Democratic congressman from Maine not running for reelection this year, published an op-ed Monday endorsing a Republican in a closely-contested House race several states away.

Golden came out in support of Mike Lawler, the GOP incumbent in New York’s 17th congressional district, located in the Hudson Valley just north of New York City.

In the pages of Lawler’s local newspaper, Golden extolled his Republican colleague for his bipartisanship, citing their work together on several pieces of aisle-crossing legislation. One of them, particularly crucial for Golden, is a bill to shield Maine’s lobstermen from what he describes as burdensome regulation aimed at saving a critically endangered whale.

Lawler is fending off a challenge from Democrat Cait Conley, a former Army officer and national security official in the Biden administration. (Golden’s op-ed makes no mention of her.)

A cryptocurrency industry super PAC is planning a sizable ad blitz to try to tip the scales against the Democratic candidate in a key midterm Senate race, according to a new report by The New York Times.

The group, Fairshake, told the Times that it’s launching $30 million in advertising money at the Ohio Senate race in a bid to help sink Sherrod Brown, the Democratic nominee for the seat. Brown, a critic of crypto, was once the ranking Democrat of the Senate Banking Committee, which handles crypto legislation, before losing his seat in 2024 – when the industry previously wielded its money against him.

(Brown has, since then, taken a somewhat softer stance on crypto, the Times reported.)

The spending comes after Senate Democrats took down the Clarity Act, a piece of legislation that the crypto industry was desperate to see passed. If successful, it would’ve carved into law many aspects of the Trump administration’s crypto-friendly regulatory environment. (The Biden administration, by contrast, had cracked down on the industry.)

Democrats’ objections to that legislation stemmed, in large part, from Trump himself. They wanted to see stricter provisions barring lawmakers from leveraging their offices to get rich off crypto – like Trump has done in his second term to the tune of $1.4 billion.

Trump set to begin busy week of diplomacy, with a Mamdani meeting along the way

President Trump is set to depart for New York later today ahead of the United Nations General Assembly, kicking off a busy week of international diplomacy.

Trump is expected to meet with several key figures on the sidelines of the annual gathering of world leaders. Among them will be Delcy Rodriguez, the interim president of Venezuela, who took charge after the US raided her country and arrested her predecessor, Nicolás Maduro, in January. Since then, Rodriguez has been pliant in response to the Trump administration’s interests, helping broker an oil deal that would see a large share of Venezuelan oil revenues diverted into US coffers.

Trump is also planning to hold discussions with the leaders of countries around the Persian Gulf, including Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, who have increasingly been drawn into Trump’s war on Iran.

And Trump signaled over the weekend that he’s open to meeting with the leadership of Iran itself, as both countries continue to trade strikes.

That’s all on Tuesday. On Wednesday, Trump is planning to host a visit from Chinese leader Xi Jinping, during which the two are expected to discuss trade, AI, and the war in Iran.

Trump will also meet today with New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, the democratic socialist whose insurgent campaign to run Trump’s home city rattled his MAGA allies, but with whom he has maintained a strangely cordial relationship. Afterwards, he’s scheduled to talk with French President Emmanuel Macron.

Trump responds to CNN, Politico, MS Now lawsuit over stripped White House access

Hours after the three news outlets he summarily barred from the White House announced their intentions to file a lawsuit, Trump took to social media to defend himself.

“The White House is not instituting an assault on the Free Press, something which I cherish,” Trump wrote on Truth Social, in response to the pending challenge from CNN, MS NOW, and Politico, the three outlets he banned.

Trump continued: “It is instituting an assault on the FAKE NEWS, something that has grown like Cancer in our beloved United States of America. It is corrupt, purposeful, pervasive, fully coordinated, and totally out of control. It is a threat to our National Security, and must be stopped, NOW!”

Trump banned the three news organizations from the White House on Friday via a surprise social media post. He cited no specific grievance against the outlets’ coverage when announcing the decision, saying only that they were “fake news” and that similar sanctions may follow for other outlets down the line.

ICE agent shoots and wounds Venezuelan man at traffic stop in Austin, Texas

A US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officer shot and wounded a Venezuelan man on Sunday afternoon in Texas’s capital city, Austin, prompting demonstrations at the scene against the Trump administration’s hardline anti-immigration campaign.

The man, identified by relatives and his lawyer as 28-year-old Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, was taken to a hospital in Austin in a serious but stable condition, officials said, before being taken into custody.

He was shot once in the torso on the city’s north side, Austin’s police chief, Lisa Davis, said at a news conference with the mayor and other city officials.

The Department of Homeland Security, which oversees ICE, said it was leading an investigation into the officer involved in the shooting, with the help of the FBI.

Austin police officers were not involved in the operation or the shooting, which occurred after a traffic stop, Davis told reporters.

The city’s police arrived on the scene after the shooting and could be seen on bystander video asking the federal officers what had happened and then rushing back to their squad car to get surgical gloves and a medical kit and administering first aid to the man.

The federal officers could be heard saying they had called an ambulance, but they did not appear to attempt to give first aid.
