MORE than one in 10 working-age adults in the Bradford district are claiming Personal Independence Payment (PIP), latest figures have revealed.

Analysis by the Press Association of Department for Work and Pensions data shows 38,970 (11.25 per cent) people have receive the benefit, which is designed to help those with long-term physical or mental health conditions or disabilities with extra living costs.

The figures come as a major review of PIP continues amid concerns about whether the current system is adequately supporting those who rely on it.

## Almost 40,000 people claiming PIP across Bradford

The data, as of July 31, 2026, shows:

* Bradford South has 9,090 working-age PIP claimants, equivalent to 13.0 per cent of adults aged 16 to 64.
* Bradford West has 10,092 claimants, representing 12.2 per cent.
* Bradford East has 9,317 claimants, representing 11.8 per cent.
* Shipley has 4,802 claimants, representing 8.2 per cent.
* Keighley & Ilkley have 5,669 claimants, representing 9.4 per cent.

Nationally, the proportion of working-age adults claiming PIP varies significantly, ranging from around one in 30 adults in some constituencies to more than one in six in others.

## MP points to review findings

The Timms Review was launched by the Government to examine how disability benefits can better support people while ensuring the system remains sustainable.

Responding to the figures, MP for Bradford South Judith Cummins (Labour) said they highlighted wider challenges facing communities across the country.

However, she focused on concerns raised by the Government's ongoing review of the PIP system.

![]()Bradford South MP Judith Cummins *(Image: Newsquest)*

Ms Cummins said: "These figures are a powerful reminder of the challenges faced across the country.

"The government's Timms Review of PiP has rightly highlighted that the current disability benefits system is not working as well as it should for many people.

"The Timms Review was launched to ensure PIP is fair and fit for the future, helping disabled people achieve better health, greater independence, and improved employment opportunities."

## **'The PIP process must be reformed'**

Meanwhile, Bradford West MP Naz Shah (Labour) said PIP plays a vital role in supporting disabled people and those with long-term health conditions to live independently and remain in employment.

She said: "When Personal Independence Payment (PIP) was introduced in 2013, its purpose was to help disabled people and those with long-term health conditions live independently.

"Many people rely on PIP to manage their daily lives, and for many it is what allows them to stay in work.

![]()Naz Shah MP *(Image: T&A)*

"The PIP process must be reformed so that it captures the needs of disabled people and those with long-term health conditions holistically, in a way that reflects their individual circumstances.

"Reform should also ensure that PIP supports people who want to work, so that no one is afraid to take up or stay in a job for fear of losing the support they need.

"I will await the full recommendations of the Timms Review before setting out my position further."

## Calls for a system 'fit for the future'

Ms Cummins said reforms should focus on improving outcomes for claimants rather than simply providing financial support.

She added: "Ultimately, everyone in Bradford deserves the chance to live a healthy, productive, and fulfilling life.

"That is the standard we should be striving for, and these figures show why there is still work to do."
