Germany’s AfD party has been consistently pro-Russia and pro-Kremlin, with the party always voting against sanctions on Russia and further aid to Ukraine. Several lawmakers have also made visits to Moscow.

Unsurprisingly then, the Kremlin has weighed into the election results with spokesman Dmitry Peskov this morning claiming AfD’s win was down to Berlin no longer buying cheap Russian gas.

Before the Ukraine war and sanctions, Germany had sourced more than one-third of its crude oil and half of its gas from Russia.

AfD has called for the Nord Stream pipelines to be repaired to restore cheap Russian oil and gas imports. Reuters reported on the weekend that AfD party leadership were preparing talks with the Kremlin to restart such supplies – even though AfD are not in office anywhere in Germany yet.

Tens of thousands of German car workers protest industry decline

Workers light flares and wave flags of the industrial union IG Metall as they protest against job cuts at a VW plant in Hanover Photograph: Ronny Hartmann/AFP/Getty Images

Staying in Germany, tens of thousands of automotive workers at Volkswagen, BMW, Mercedes-Benz and other car brands are protesting on factory floors across the country today for government and management help to save their jobs.

Volkswagen has announced plans to cut 100,000 jobs in the next three years and BMW and Mercedes are also slashing their workforce.

The problem is a near-existential one: the rise of Chinese EV carmakers with their cheaper, and in some cases more advanced models, has thrown the German car industry into crisis. The Germans, who have been far slower to transition to electric vehicles, simply aren’t selling as many cars anymore and need to retool to keep up.

The fears are reflective of wider concerns over Europe’s industrial base being eroded by Chinese competition. Union leaders – pointing to state support for Chinese car makers- are calling for government support, like energy subsidies and financial help, or putting up trade protections.

The Uzbekistan-born multibillionaire, who made his fortune in metals, mining and telecoms, was sanctioned by the EU in March 2022. He was described in the EU sanctions listing as “a pro-Kremlin oligarch with particularly close ties to Russian president Vladimir Putin”.

France stunned EU partners by joining Slovakia earlier this month in petitioning for his removal from the list, which covers nearly 3,000 Russian individuals and entities that are accused of offering support to Vladimir Putin’s brutal war on Ukraine.

The French government wants his release in order to facilitate the release of French nationals in Azerbaijan. France’s envoy has told his EU counterparts the government is seeking the delisting on national security grounds, while details of the prisoner release deal were first reported by the FT.

Good morning and welcome to our Europe live blog, where we’ll start in Germany after a bruising night for Friedrich Merz’s party.

The German chancellor is staring down pressure over his leadership and authority after the CDU suffered heavy losses in two regional elections. For the first time in 77 years, it’s failed to enter a state parliament, in the former East German state of Mecklenburg- Western Pomerania.

This was the home state of former CDU chancellor Angela Merkel; the party there last night pulled just 4.9% of the vote, losing more than half of its support.

Many of its voters have flocked to Alternative für Deutschland (AfD). The far-right, anti-migrant party gained 38% of the vote, doubling its share to now become the largest party in the state. This result comes just a fortnight after AfD romped to a majority in Saxony-Anhalt.

Merz called it a “disaster” last night in a public address, but vowed to stay the course and press on with his government’s reforms.
