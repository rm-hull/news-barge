A £21 million STEM Centre of Excellence planned for Darlington has been hailed as a landmark for the region, as figures reveal 173,000 unfilled STEM vacancies across the UK.

The Central Park facility will train people in science, technology, engineering and mathematics to meet demand for workers in artificial intelligence, robotics, green energy and advanced manufacturing.

The project has secured £16 million from the Mission Growth Fund and a further £5 million through Pride in Place funding, in a partnership involving Darlington College, MP Lola McEvoy, Darlington Borough Council and government departments.

David Gartland, Principal and CEO of Darlington College, said: “Securing £21 million for the STEM Centre of Excellence is a landmark moment for Darlington and the wider Tees Valley. This investment recognises the ambition of our region and the vital role education plays in driving economic prosperity.

“As industries evolve and technology continues to shape the future, it is essential that we provide local people with access to the very best training and opportunities. The Darlington STEM Centre of Excellence will help develop the highly skilled workforce our employers need while ensuring talent can build successful careers right here in the North East.”

David spoke to BUSINESSiQ before joining business leaders at a key event to discuss major North East projects and their impact on the region.

Guests and panellists at the September 25 breakfast at Darlington College will discuss how regeneration and local investment can build stronger communities in the latest BUSINESSiQ Impact event.

With a new Government in place, the spotlight on Andy Burnham’s regions is brighter than ever, with his pledge to deliver power ‘into every postcode', so a keynote conversation with Ellie Fotheringham, Deputy Director for the Strategy, Planning and Budget Group of HM Treasury, will help us understand how our own regional powerhouse is working in partnership with Government to build for the future.

The other panellists are Rose Rouse, CEO of Darlington Borough Council; Rachel Anderson, Assistant Director of Policy at the North East Chamber of Commerce; Mark Stamper, Head of Inclusive Growth and Partnerships, Durham County Council; Huw Lewis, Assistant Director of Communications at Kim McGuinness’s North East Mayoral Strategic Authority; David Hodgson, UK Bus Territory Manager at Darlington engines giant Cummins, and Sophie Walton, Director of Strategic Partnerships at CPI.

Demand for STEM skills is rising, with around 173,000 unfilled vacancies across the UK costing the economy an estimated £1.5 billion a year; in the Tees Valley, the technical sector accounts for 14 per cent of businesses and 7 per cent of jobs.

The new three-storey centre will include an auditorium, a 350-square-metre community workspace, science and data laboratories, IT suites, seminar rooms, breakout areas and social spaces for students and workers to learn, retrain and improve their skills.

The centre will support learners from school age through higher education and lifelong learning, with more than 600 students expected to take full-time STEM courses, apprenticeships and undergraduate or postgraduate programmes.

Employer partnerships will give students access to real-world projects, placements, internships and mentoring while keeping training aligned with current and future job needs.

The centre will sit in Darlington’s expanding education quarter near Darlington College, Teesside University, the Science Park, local businesses and transport links including the railway station.

Beyond its educational function, the project is intended to support wider economic development by creating a sustainable talent pipeline, helping businesses access skilled workers and encouraging local talent to remain and build careers within the region.

Darlington is one of only two areas in the country to receive this level of investment, underlining growing national confidence in the area’s educational strengths and economic potential.

With construction plans progressing, the STEM Centre of Excellence is expected to play a central role in shaping the future workforce of the Tees Valley, ensuring residents are equipped with the skills needed to take advantage of emerging opportunities in high-growth industries.

For Darlington College, the development represents a significant step forward in its mission to support learners, employers and communities, while reinforcing Darlington’s position as a centre for innovation, skills excellence and economic growth in the North East.

A planning application for the centre was formally submitted on September 3, 2026, with a decision expected before the end of the year.
