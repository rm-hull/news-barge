# RAF Fylingdales recognised for six decades of service to North Yorkshire town

## An RAF base which began operations in the 1960s in North Yorkshire will receive the ‘highest honour’ from a local council at a special meeting this week.

RAF Fylingdales will be given the Honorary Freedom of the Town of Whitby as a mark of the town’s “gratitude for the distinguished service of the base and, in recognition of its longstanding association with Whitby and its contribution to the defence of the United Kingdom”.

RAF Fylingdales, which is one of the most recognisable structures in the North York Moors, provides a continuous ballistic missile early warning service to the UK and US Governments.

The base has maintained a longstanding and close association with Whitby and the surrounding area since becoming operational in 1963.

![Alison Hume MP With Commanding Officer Toby Steward At RAF Fylingdales In The North York Moors](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmRhNTExYTJiLTkwNDItNDNmZi1hZWMxLTI0ZTU4OWViOWVmZTo1ZmJlNTU0MC04YmNjLTRlYTctYmY2YS0yYTg0ODJkNWE2NzY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It is now an integral part of the UK’s Space Command and is described by the Royal Air Force as the United Kingdom’s primary military space sensor.

An extraordinary meeting of Whitby Town Council on Tuesday, September 22 will see councillors vote on the conferral of the Honorary Freedom of the Town.

Holders of the title have the “right, privilege, honour, and distinction of marching through the town on ceremonial occasions with colours flying, bands playing, drums beating, and bayonets fixed”.

Town councillors recently granted the same title to the Corps of Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (REME), and a special Freedom Parade was held to mark the occasion this summer.

On a visit to the base last month, Alison Hume, the MP for Scarborough and Whitby, said: “What struck me most was that RAF Fylingdales is not simply a piece of critical national infrastructure. It is a community of highly skilled, dedicated people whose expertise is vital to our defence every single day.

“I was moved to hear how strongly Wing Commander Toby Steward and his team view RAF Fylingdales as part of our local community.

“That commitment is clearly reflected in the work of the Family Hub in Whitby, and in the close relationships the station has built across the constituency.”

For almost 30 years, the RAF Station was particularly recognisable for its three large radomes, commonly known as the ‘golf balls’, which became a prominent landmark on the North York Moors.

These were replaced by the present Solid-State Phased Array Radar, whose distinctive pyramid structure became operational in 1992.

The radar provides 360-degree coverage and is capable of detecting and tracking objects thousands of miles into space.

A Whitby Town Council report notes: “Generations of personnel serving at the Station and their families have lived, worked, and participated in the life of the local area, while RAF Fylingdales has maintained a visible presence within Whitby through its participation in civic, ceremonial, remembrance, and community events.

“The relationship represents an enduring connection between the town, the Royal Air Force, and the personnel who have served at RAF Fylingdales throughout its history.

“As an important Royal Air Force station situated on the North York Moors close to the town, it has formed part of the wider Whitby community for more than six decades.”

RAF Fylingdales has maintained continuous operations for more than 60 years.

Its primary operational role remains the provision of continuous ballistic missile early warning for the United Kingdom and its allies, providing vital warning and assessment of potential missile threats.

The town council report adds: “The relationship between RAF Fylingdales and Whitby extends well beyond the geographical proximity of the Station to the town.

“Since RAF Fylingdales became operational in 1963, successive generations of Service personnel, civilian employees, and their families have become part of the wider Whitby community, creating an association which has endured for more than six decades.

“Although the operational Station is situated on the North York Moors, Whitby has long served as an important home and community for those associated with RAF Fylingdales, with service families having lived within and around the town.

“This physical presence has helped ensure that the relationship between the Station and Whitby is not simply ceremonial but is reflected in the everyday life of the community.”

Arrangements will be made in consultation with the Station for a suitable civic and ceremonial event to take place during 2027 to mark the conferment formally, if councillors vote to grant the honour at Tuesday’s meeting.
