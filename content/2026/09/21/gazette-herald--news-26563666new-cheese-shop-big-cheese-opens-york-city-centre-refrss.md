GRAHAM NOAKES really likes cheese.

Add some decent pickles, a really good chutney, and a pile of crisp crackers and, as far as he is concerned, casual snacking doesn't get much better.

Which is one very good reason for opening up an independent shop in the heart of York devoted largely to cheese.

The Big Cheese is at the Micklegate end of Skeldergate in a premises which has been used as a takeaway, coffee shop and hairdressers over the years.

![]()Graham Noakes outside his new deli The Big Cheese in Skeldergate, York. Photo by Maxine Gordon

Compact but inviting, the shop begins tempting customers before they are properly through the door, with a refrigerated barrel filled with tubs of ice-cream.

Resist that and there's plenty more temptation inside.

Cheese lovers will find themselves in seventh heaven as they browse the selection which is heavy on local produce but with international flavours too.

*WATCH: Take a tour inside The Big Cheese*

"The idea behind it all is to try to support local suppliers," begins Graham who was once the landlord at The Knavesmire pub in York.

"We also have a range of pickles and chutneys from Yorkshire."

Proud Yorkshire foodies can certainly assemble quite a feast from God's Own Country.

You could, for example, put together a cheeseboard that Wallace and Gromit would be proud of: a Shepherd's Purse Yorkshire Blue from Thirsk, an unpasteurised Yorkshire Dales End premium cheddar, and, of course, a lovely hunk of Wensleydale.

![]()Just some of the cheese selection in The Big Cheese in Skeldergate, York. Photo by Maxine Gordon

Some cheeses can be cut to size – perfect if you just want enough for crackers, or to follow a recipe. Others come as waxed truckles, small barrels and wedges. The selection of flavoured cheeses, including ginger, smoked apple and garlic, is enough to keep you popping back for more.

Venture further afield and you can take your pick from an Alpine Tomme de Savoie or an Italian Provolone.

Can't decide between a blue and a brie? Graham reckons Burts Blue could be the answer. "It's a soft blue, a cross between a Stilton and a brie and is great as a spread," he enthuses.

If you are having cheese, then you'll be in need of a chutney – or some jam (we are in Yorkshire after all).

![]()Inside The Big Cheese in Skeldergate, York. Photo by Maxine Gordon

Luckily, a fine choice is on hand from Nidderdale brand Bessie's; Graham stocks a decent selection of their chutneys and jams as well as a range from Rosebud preserves of Masham.

Pre-packed premium charcuterie is also for sale, from Mount Grace Charcuterie based in the Hambleton Hills just outside York.

Slices of Gloucester Old Spot Chorizo vie with Garlic, Mixed Peppercorn and Red Wine Salami, and Air-Dried Coppa for the attention of your taste buds.

![]()Cosy seating in The Big Cheese in Skeldergate, York. Photo by Maxine Gordon

Graham has a favourite: "They are great for a cheeseboard if you are looking for something a bit different that looks and tastes great. A favourite is the Pork, Lemon and Pistachio Salami which looks lovely and has a nice crunch to it because of the nuts."

There's also a range of spices, while coffee, tea and cold drinks are coming soon.

Later this autumn, Graham plans to introduce cheese and charcuterie boards for customers to enjoy at the quaint window-bay seating he built himself.

![]()Products for sale in The Big Cheese in Skeldergate, York. Photo by Maxine Gordon

And that might be where The Big Cheese comes into its own.

Skeldergate doesn't have the footfall of some of York's busiest shopping streets, but Graham rather likes it that way.

"It's a bit off the beaten track, but nice and calm," he says.

And, ultimately, he has created the sort of place he would want to discover himself.

"It's the sort of place that I like to go!"
