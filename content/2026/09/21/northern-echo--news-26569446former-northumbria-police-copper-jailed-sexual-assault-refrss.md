A former police officer has been handed a suspended prison sentence.

Daniel Hedley, who served with Northumbria Police and was studying law at Durham University, was convicted of sexually assaulting a woman in January 2023.

The incident was investigated by Durham Constabulary and Northumbria Police’s Professional Standards Department, under the direction of the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC), after two women reported being inappropriately touched by Mr Hedley.

He was arrested in March 2023, and the case was referred to the IOPC, which directed the investigation and authorised a referral to the Crown Prosecution Service.

Prosecutor James Yearsley said Hedley would attend university and social events in his uniform, including his police utility belt, and would "boast of his exploits in the police" to other students, who were "significantly younger" than him.

Hedley would brag about "powers he had" and how to get away with "violence" inflicted on people he was arresting, while his fellow students regarded him as "odd" and were "disturbed by his conduct as a serving officer", Yearsley added.

Emily Barry, director of engagement at the IOPC, said: "Former PC Hedley abused his position, inappropriately touching a woman who said she felt intimidated by his role as a police officer.

"His behaviour was inexcusable and a complete betrayal of his oath to act with integrity and prevent crime.

"Thanks to the bravery of those who came forward, and the swift actions of Northumbria Police and Durham Constabulary once they were aware of the concerns about his behaviour, he has now been held accountable.

"This sends a clear message that reports like these will be taken seriously and officers who abuse their position will be dealt with robustly."

Following a trial at Teesside Crown Court in July 2024, Mr Hedley was found guilty of one count of sexual assault for touching a woman’s bottom.

He was found not guilty of three other sexual assault charges.

Today (Monday, September 21), he was sentenced to 32 weeks in prison, suspended for 18 months.

The court also ordered him to complete a rehabilitation programme, carry out 120 hours of unpaid work, and comply with a three-month curfew.

He was given a 10-year restraining order and placed on the sex offenders register for 10 years.

The IOPC concluded that Mr Hedley had a case to answer for gross misconduct.

The court heard he had since been dismissed from the force for gross misconduct.
