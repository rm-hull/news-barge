AN INVESTIGATION has been launched after a bird of prey needed to be euthanised after being severely injured in North Yorkshire.

North Yorkshire Police says it is appealing for information after a juvenile marsh harrier was found injured in Westerdale, North Yorkshire on August 28.

The bird - a specially protected species under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 - was discovered unable to fly and is believed to have been lying injured for several days before being found.

A spokesperson for the force said: "Following examination, it was established that the bird had been shot and is believed to have been lying injured for several days prior to being found.

"Unfortunately, the injuries were so severe that there was no prospect of recovery, and on welfare grounds the bird had to be humanely euthanised."

They added that under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, it is an offence to intentionally kill, injure, or disturb marsh harriers, or to damage or destroy their nests or eggs.

North Yorkshire Police is appealing for information and are urging anyone who witnessed suspicious activity in the area, or who may have information that could assist its investigation, to come forward.

Anyone with information is asked to contact North Yorkshire Police on 101 or by emailing [kimberley.laws@northyorkshire.police.uk](mailto:kimberley.laws@northyorkshire.police.uk), quoting incident number 12260171320.
