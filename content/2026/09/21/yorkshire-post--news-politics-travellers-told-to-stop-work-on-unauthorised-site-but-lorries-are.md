# Travellers told to stop work on unauthorised site but ‘lorries are still coming’

## Travellers building an unauthorised site in the North Yorkshire countryside have ignored a High Court injunction served by council officers at the weekend, it is claimed.

Planning officers from North Yorkshire Council served the interim injunction on occupants of the site near Hillam, west of Selby, on Saturday (September 19).

The order prevents any further development or expansion of the site off Betteras Hill Road unless authorised by planning permission.

It also prohibits additional caravans or mobile homes being brought onto the land.

![A drone image of the traveller site near Hillam. Approval given to use.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjdmNTg3NGQ4LTVmNGEtNDgyYy05ZmM0LTUyNDY0YWU1MmFkNjpjZTBjY2U5Mi1mMTgxLTQ3MGItYjhhOC04YWFiYjYzZTdkMDA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


No further hardstanding can be laid, while further building and engineering works, including roads, paths and sewerage, water or electricity infrastructure, are also prohibited.

However, Councillor Tim Grogan, whose Monk Fryston and South Milford division includes the site, said the travellers had ignored the injunction, just as he claims they previously ignored a temporary stop notice issued by the council.

The Reform councillor said: “The officers turned up on Saturday and were told where to go, apparently. They’ve then completely ignored the notice and the lorries are still coming.

“The local residents are furious. They’re holding a meeting to discuss what they can do.”

Work began on the land at around 4.45pm on Friday, September 11.

Over that weekend, HGVs delivered tonnes of hardcore and building materials, while heavy earth-moving machinery was brought onto the land.

At least 14 caravans have since been brought onto the site.

North Yorkshire Council served a temporary stop notice on September 14 in an attempt to prevent further unauthorised development.

The authority then went to the High Court to obtain the interim injunction imposing wider restrictions on activity at the site.

North Yorkshire Council’s corporate director for community development, Nic Harne, said the injunction had been served immediately by planning enforcement officers with support from North Yorkshire Police.

He added: “The injunction prohibits any further caravans being brought onto the land, the construction of additional structures, the installation of services or drainage, and the importation of materials or laying of hardstanding.

“We recognise the concern caused within the local community and have kept elected members and the parish council informed throughout and we will continue to provide updates as the case progresses.”

Mr Harne said the council would continue to monitor the site for compliance with both the injunction and the temporary stop notice.

The injunction does not require caravans or other items which were already on the land when the order was made to be removed.

It instead prevents further occupation, development and materials being brought onto the site unless authorised by planning permission.

The interim order remains in force until October 21, when a further hearing will take place at the Leeds District Registry of the High Court to decide whether it should continue.

Mr Harne added: “We will continue to take all appropriate action available to us to protect the interests of residents, ensure compliance with planning law and pursue matters through the courts where necessary.”
