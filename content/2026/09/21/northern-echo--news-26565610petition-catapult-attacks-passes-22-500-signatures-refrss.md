Wildlife rescuers have warned of a rising concern over birds being shot across the North East, as a campaign for tougher laws on catapult attacks attracted more than 22,500 signatures.

Darlington Bird Rescue ssay injured birds keep arriving, with the organisation receiving almost one shot bird a day over the summer.

Some were newly injured birds, while others were already in the rescue’s care and were shot after being released for the day.

![]()One of the wild birds treated by Darlington Bird Rescue after being found with pellet injuries. *(Image: DARLINGTON BIRD RESCUE)*

Darlington Bird Rescue told The Northern Echo that reports of birds being shot usually rise during school holidays, leading it to believe children and teenagers are behind many incidents.

The organisation said reported shootings and admissions after shootings “are always doubled, if not tripled, during school holidays”.

Last March, the BBC reported a TikTok trend in which teenagers deliberately hit pigeons with catapults to share the footage online.

The videos carried captions such as ‘catapult vs wood pigeon’.

![]()A bird being cared for by Darlington Bird Rescue after suffering injuries believed to have been caused by a pellet attack *(Image: DARLINGTON BIRD RESCUE)*

Darlington Bird Rescue has reported shootings against pigeons, corvids, seagulls and kestrels.

The birds included newly injured casualties and others already in the rescue’s care who were shot after being released for the day.

Just last week, the RSPCA launched an investigation after witnesses reported youths using a slingshot to kill two pigeons and injure a third at Darlington's North Park Recreation Ground.

And in recent days, police in Shildon seized a catapult after reports it had been used to target wildlife and cause criminal damage, amid an increase in youth-related anti-social behaviour.

![]()A pellet recovered following an incident involving an injured wild bird *(Image: DARLINGTON BIRD RESCUE)*

RSPCA figures show that 40 per cent of its wild-bird cruelty reports in 2025, the latest available figures, came during the summer months.

The charity said 2025 brought its “highest number of summer cruelty reports involving wild birds in five years”.

It blamed the rise on a “range of factors”, including catapult misuse and exposure to harmful online content.

The RSPCA received 38 reports of wild birds being attacked in the North East in 2025.

North Yorkshire had the seventh-highest number of reports nationally, with 18 recorded during the year.

Elsewhere in the North East, 12 incidents were reported in County Durham and eight in Tyne and Wear.

No incidents were reported in Northumberland.

An RSPCA spokesperson said: “We are extremely concerned by any incident in which animals are deliberately targeted with weapons, such as catapults and airguns. It is completely unacceptable to shoot or otherwise harm an animal for ‘fun’ or target practice.

“While most people will be appalled by any animal being attacked illegally with weapons, sadly there are people out there who are deliberately targeting them with not just catapults, but air guns and crossbows too. These weapons can cause horrific pain and suffering, but unfortunately, catapults are not currently classified as offensive weapons.”

Across the country, the RSPCA received 876 wild-bird cruelty reports in 2025 and 474 so far in 2026.

A petition published on the UK Parliament website on August 20 called to “make catapult attacks against animals a standalone criminal offence”.

The petition calls for a “clear and consistent” law and says catapults should never be used as weapons against animals.

There is no specific offence covering catapult attacks, but the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 makes it an offence to intentionally injure or kill a wild bird.

The petition closed with 22,501 signatures.

Because it passed the 10,000-signature threshold, the petition will receive a Government response.
