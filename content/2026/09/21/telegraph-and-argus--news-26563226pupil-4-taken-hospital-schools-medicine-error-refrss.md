A FOUR-year-old pupil was taken to hospital after being given double his prescribed dose of medication at school - and from a different child's medicine bottle.

Isaac-Hunter Broadley, a pupil at Bradford Academy, was prescribed 250mg of amoxicillin for a chest infection, according to his mother, Jessica Troy.

However, staff accidentally administered Isaac with a 500mg dose of amoxicillin from a bottle belonging to a different pupil.

Ms Troy said Isaac, who is autistic and non-verbal, was unable to tell staff they had selected the wrong bottle.

### 'I would like to offer my sincere and unreserved apology'

Bradford Academy, which has over 1,600 pupils from ages 2-19, offered Ms Troy a written apology.

In a letter to Ms Troy, seen by the T&A, Vice Principal Steph Griffiths said: "This was a serious medication error which resulted in Isaac becoming unwell and requiring hospital assessment.

"On behalf of the school, I would like to offer my sincere and unreserved apology for what occurred.

"Parents place significant trust in schools to safeguard their children, and on this occasion the standards expected were not met.”

![]()Bradford Academy staff administered Isaac-Hunter Broadley with the wrong medication *(Image: Google Maps / Jessica Troy)*

### Isaac 'violently sick' after taking wrong medication

Ms Troy received a phone call from the school on Monday, September 7.

She said: “It was about 2pm when the phone rang.

“It was a teacher who said that Isaac had been given the wrong medication, and that he was being violently sick.

“You never think that something like this is going to happen to your child.”

![]()Isaac Hunter-Broadley *(Image: Jessica Troy)*

Ms Troy said the school advised her to contact her local GP.

The GP then referred Isaac to accident and emergency.

Ms Troy said: “The doctor told me I had to rush him to hospital.

“At the hospital, they said he was lucky to have vomited, otherwise he might have had to have his stomach pumped.”

### Medication belonged to another pupil

Ms Troy said the bottle given to Isaac was labelled with another child’s address.

She said: “I was given the 500mg bottle to take to A&E, and it had another boy’s address on it.

“500mg is far too high for a four-year-old.

“Isaac’s medicine was in a bag with his name on it.

“I don’t understand how they got mixed up.”

### 'I don't trust what might happen to him there'

Ms Troy has kept Isaac away from school since the incident took place.

She said she took him back for one day, but removed him after discovering that the staff member she suspected to be involved was present.

Ms Troy said: “My son can’t go back into school.

“I don’t trust what might happen to him there.

“I did try to take him in one day, but.... I took him straight back home.”

### School launched an investigation

Bradford Academy launched an investigation into the incident.

A school representative said: “A thorough investigation was undertaken to establish an accurate account of events, determine whether school procedures were followed, identify any contributing factors and inform recommendations to reduce the risk of a recurrence.”

The investigation found Isaac’s medication had been administered correctly on the previous Thursday and Friday.

It also found that the staff member responsible for giving the medicine had recently completed renewed training.

A school representative said: “The member of staff administering medication had a valid First Aid qualification and had completed First Aid training on 2 September 2026.”

### Bradford Academy - full statement

Bradford Academy said the investigation remains ongoing and that immediate action had been taken.

The school declined to comment on further details or individuals.

A spokesperson for Bradford Academy said: “The school has received a complaint which has been investigated and immediate action taken.

"However, the complaint is still ongoing and so we will not comment further.

"The school will not be issuing any comment on any of the details or individuals involved to protect confidentiality.”

Bradford East MP Imran Hussain MP said: “I have contacted the school after my constituent raised concerns with me about this incident.

“It raises important questions around safeguarding and the procedures in place for administering medication to pupils, including whether appropriate procedures have been reviewed and safeguards are in place to prevent a similar incident happening again.

“I have asked the school to address these concerns and provide the reassurance that my constituent understandably requires.”
