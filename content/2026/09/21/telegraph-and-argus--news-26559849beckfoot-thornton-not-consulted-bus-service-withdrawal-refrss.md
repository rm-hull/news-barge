A school has been caught up in a bus route row after a service was withdrawn without consultation.

Beckfoot Thornton was not informed of the decision to cancel the BT13 service, which served pupils travelling through Clayton.

### **“A journey of up to 90 minutes each way”**

The BT12 now covers the former route, but parents say the change has led to significantly longer journeys, with some students now spending more than an hour getting to school.

One parent said: “The removal of the BT13 service has turned what was once a reasonable school journey into a journey of up to 90 minutes each way.

“Children are arriving at school tired, anxious and already drained before lessons have even started. Parents pay for this service and expect it to be reliable and suitable for pupils.

The parent added: “We simply want children's welfare and education to be prioritised and for a sensible solution to be found.”

Richard Hanson, headteacher at Beckfoot Thornton, said the school had not been given a say in the decision and has no control over public transport decisions.

### 'School is not currently involved in the planning or operation'

Mr Hanson said: "The school was not consulted regarding the withdrawal of the B13 service and does not have responsibility for decisions relating to bus routes, timetables or vehicle allocation, which are managed by external transport providers and the relevant transport authorities.

"We understand that lengthy and unreliable journeys can be frustrating for students and their families and, where pupils arrive late due to transport issues beyond their control, this is taken into account by the school.

"Our primary focus is ensuring that students are able to access their education and are supported when circumstances outside their control affect their journey to school.

"While the school is not currently involved in the planning or operation of these services, we are happy to pass on any concerns raised by parents to the relevant transport providers and authorities for their consideration."

Roadworks in the area are said to be causing further delays and contributing to late arrivals.

### 'Services combined to utilise resources efficently'

A spokesperson for the West Yorkshire Combined Authority said: "We took the decision to combine school services in the area due to low passenger numbers – ensuring we utilise our resources efficiently and deliver the best possible value for taxpayers.

"All pupils who previously used the BT13 service still have access to school bus travel and journey times remain well within Government guidelines."

The authority said the BT13 was operated in conjunction with the P78, which was withdrawn at the end of the last school year, and that there was spare capacity on the BT11 and BT12.

Adding that consultations are only undertaken when a service is removed without an alternative being available.
