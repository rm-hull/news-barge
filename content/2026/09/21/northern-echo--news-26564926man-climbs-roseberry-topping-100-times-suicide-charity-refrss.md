A Hartlepool man who lost a friend to suicide has raised more than £3,000 by climbing Roseberry Topping 100 times in 66 hours.

By the time he completed his final ascent, Justin Bramall had raised more than £3,100 for The Headlight Project - a Tees Valley charity dedicated to preventing suicide and supporting people bereaved by it.

Justin began the challenge at 7am on Friday, September 11, setting out to climb the iconic North Yorkshire landmark 100 times as part of a "Double Everesting" attempt.

![]()Justin Bramall was joined by friends and family throughout the 3-day challenge. *(Image: Justin Bramall)*

After 66 hours and two minutes, he finished his final climb having covered 93.1 miles and gained 62,123ft of elevation - more than twice Mount Everest’s height.

During the challenge, the climber also burned over 23,000 calories and recorded 301,798 steps.

But for Justin, the challenge was always about far more than numbers.

![]()Justin Bramall was joined by friends and family throughout the 3-day challenge. *(Image: Justin Bramall)*

The cause is personal for Justin, who lost a college friend to suicide and was later inspired by The Headlight Project’s work after a visit by the charity to his workplace.

The charity provides specialist therapeutic support for adults and children bereaved by suicide, as well as preventative education and training across the Tees Valley.

Justin said: "I'm incredibly proud to have completed it, but the challenge itself was only part of it.

"I wanted to use something I knew was going to be extremely difficult to raise awareness and money for The Headlight Project and the work they do.

"I chose to suffer for a few days, and I knew there would be an end to it. For people living with the aftermath of suicide, the grief and consequences can last for years.

"That was a price I was willing to pay if it could help."

![]()Justin Bramall was joined by friends and family throughout the 3-day challenge. *(Image: Justin Bramall)*

The latest Office for National Statistics figures revealed that the North East recorded England's highest suicide rate in 2024, with 15.1 deaths per 100,000 people.

After almost three days on the hill and just one hour and 40 minutes’ sleep, Justin said the challenge showed him the power of other people’s kindness.

More than 50 relatives, friends, colleagues and ultra-runners joined him during the challenge.

Some climbed with him, while others brought food, encouragement and company day and night.

One of those supporters was fellow runner Nick Kendall, who accompanied Justin for 47 of the 100 climbs.

![]()Justin Bramall was joined by friends and family throughout the 3-day challenge. *(Image: Justin Bramall)*

Justin said: "There were so many people who gave up their own time to come and support me, and I'm incredibly grateful.

"Nick staying for 47 reps was an achievement in itself, but some of the people I'd never even met before turning up in the middle of the night to climb with me was incredible.

"The ultra-running community, friends, family and colleagues all played a massive part in getting me through those 100 climbs."

For Justin, raising awareness matters as much as raising money.

He said: "I'm very proud to be surrounded by so many generous people.

"The money is obviously hugely important, but raising awareness of The Headlight Project is just as important to me.

"If someone sees what I've done and discovers the charity exists, or learns something that might help them support someone else, then that's another positive to come from the challenge."

Donations can still be made through Justin's JustGiving page: Roseberry Topping 100 Challenge.
