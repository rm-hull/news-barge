A series of early morning raids targeting suspected members of organised crime groups have been carried out in the wake of a fatal crash resulting in the death of two police officers.

More than 100 officers joined the operation this morning to arrest people linked to organised crime groups in Middlesbrough and Redcar.

PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38, died following a serious collision on the A66 in South Bank in the early hours of Saturday, August 22.

The crash sparked grief and anger in the community after Jakub Matusiak, Makai Saddington, Theo Rae, Michael Robert Cahill and Cole Robert Worthy also died.

![]()Cleveland Police officers conduct a raid on a property in South Bank, as part of Operation Artemis, to tackle drug dealers in the troubled South Bank and Grangetown areas of the force area. There has been increased police activity since the A66 crash which killed two officers and five young men on August 22 *(Image: PA WIRE)*

Superintendent Daryll Tomlinson, district commander for Redcar and Cleveland, said the force was determined to tackle growing drug dealing in the area.

Superintendent Daryll Tomlinson said: “We are tackling criminals and targeting anti-social behaviour in our hotspot areas.

“Officers from across the region will be joining us to take the fight back to the criminals in this area.

“We executed drugs warrants, arrested people wanted on recall, and sending a clear message out to the community.”

The force was joined by officers from the National Crime Agency and the North East Regional Organised Crime Unit for the day-long Operation Artemis raids.

A 22-year-old woman was arrested at a property on Lancaster Road, Middlesbrough, on suspicion of links to an organised crime group.

Three men and a woman were arrested at an address on Birchington Avenue, Grangetown, on suspicion of supplying a Class A drug.

As well as targeting addresses believed to be connected to OCGs, the officers were tackling off-road bike problems and carrying out public reassurance.

Assistant Chief Constable Mark Hall urged the local community to continue coming forward with information to enable them to crackdown on OCGs ruining their lives.

He said: “We want to continuously work with the community to understand their concerns and take action.

“For those who are involved in criminality, we will find out and we will take relevant action to disrupt you.

"A number of warrants have been executed. There have been a number of arrests and number of items seized.

"The support from the community has been phenomenal which has been reassuring that we have the vast majority of the public on our side.

"A lot of the activity taking place today is a result of community intelligence.

"We couldn't do this without the support of the local community. We want to build trust and confidence with the community."
