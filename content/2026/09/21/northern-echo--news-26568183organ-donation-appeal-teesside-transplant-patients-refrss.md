Two people waiting for life-saving transplants are urging people to become organ donors after seeing how one decision can transform a life.

After life-changing kidney transplants helped them regain their independence, Sophie Warren, from Loftus, and Will Postgate, from Guisborough, are once again waiting for donors.

Hairdresser Sophie Warren, 27, was diagnosed with lupus nephritis in 2018, an autoimmune disease that led to kidney failure.

After spending three years on dialysis, she received a kidney transplant from her mother in 2021.

![]()Sophie Warren with specialist nurse Vicki Ness. *(Image: University Hospitals Tees)*

But her body rejected the organ earlier this year, sending Sophie back on dialysis as she waits for another transplant.

Sophie said: "It just takes one person to change a life.

"I am currently waiting to be reactivated on the transplant list, but I have been receiving treatment for four months now after two attempts at preventative treatment failed."

Sophie suffers with symptoms such as exhaustion, joint pain and sickness, but continues to work two days a week as a hairdresser while also caring for her one-year-old son.

She said: "It is hard at the minute as I get treatment three mornings a week at the James Cook University Hospital and even though my treatment takes three hours, it is a five-hour round trip to and from the hospital."

![]()Sophie Warren with her one-year-old son Theodore. *(Image: University Hospitals Tees)*

Will Postgate, 26, has Alport syndrome, a genetic condition that causes kidney failure and hearing loss.

In 2022, he received a kidney transplant from a donor called Susan, who happened to be one of his mother's colleagues.

The transplant worked for around three years before, like Sophie’s, it eventually failed.

Will said: "I personally spend about 20 hours a week at the hospital receiving treatment so I can live as close to a normal life as possible.

"I cannot enjoy simple things like going on holiday, as I cannot spend more than two days away from dialysis. I have to watch what I eat and how much I drink.

"I walk around with a plastic tube hanging out of my chest, so I cannot bathe or shower properly.

"I had to quit my job at the start of this year because the physical demands were too much for my body. I had to give up my house and move back in with my parents because I could no longer work.

"It has been very tough these past 12 months."

![]()Will Postgate with specialist nurse Vicki Ness. *(Image: University Hospitals Tees)*

Sophie and Will joined forces to share their stories and encourage more people to register as organ donors.

Sophie said: "Signing up as an organ donor is an amazing gift.

"I'm a registered organ donor myself and, although I may not be able to donate my kidneys, I hope that one day I can help give someone else a better quality of life, just as my mum did for me."

Will added: "Although it is a personal decision, I would encourage people to be open-minded because you're not just ticking a box on the register; you're potentially giving someone years of their life back."

In August, NHS Blood and Transplant reported that 1,132 patients in the North East and Yorkshire remained on the transplant waiting list, with 63 people in the region dying while waiting last year.

Vicki Ness, a specialist nurse at James Cook University Hospital, has cared for both Sophie and Will.

She said: "I am incredibly proud of Sophie and Will for sharing their journeys.
