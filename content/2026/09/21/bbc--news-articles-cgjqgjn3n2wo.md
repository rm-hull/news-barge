**When Mia Jones left her home in Anglesey to go to university in London, she couldn't cook "whatsoever" - but quickly realised this had to change.**

"I couldn't live off pasta for the rest of my life," she says - and all the ready meals and pot noodles were starting to add up.

"I'd be spending £45 a week on food," says the 22-year-old. "That wasn't viable."

So she started buying fresh ingredients. "Then I realised, 'Oh, I need to learn how to cook now'."

She started following social media videos for tips and recipes, swapping out foods she didn't like.

![Mia is smiling at the camera. She has long hair and is wearing a green graduation robe with green accents. She is standing in front of a river. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/b43a/live/e67f3ae0-b341-11f1-91a3-393282eb9f17.jpg)

Mia says learning how to cook "made me realise I could function on my own"

While there were some early disasters it was not long before Mia was proud to say she could cook.

"It made me realise I could function on my own and live on my own", she says.

"[First year] me would not be able to believe it all".

What's more, Mia cut her weekly shop by £20 - and found she had more energy.

"I was feeling a lot more awake in my lectures, which was very useful," she says.

After finishing her masters degree she now shares her recipes online, hoping to help other students in the same position she was in.

Mia and other once culinary-challenged students have shared the signature dishes they learned that were cheap, easy - and most importantly tasty.

All of the recipes can be made by stocking up a starter store cupboard for under £13. Then each meal uses additional ingredients for under £2.50 - which make several portions perfect for the next day or popping in the freezer.

We asked retail tracking platform Assosia to find the rough cost of standard sized packs of ingredients using the average pre-promotion price across Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda & Morrisons on 11 September 2026.

Like Mia, Jayda Otuo-Mensah "didn't really cook" before going to university.

The physiotherapy student wanted to know how to make healthy food while spending around 40 hours a week on placement in hospitals.

"It's a lot easier when you're cooking because you know exactly what's going in," she says.

"I can have a chicken burger and it's really healthy because of the way I cooked it."

![Jayda is standing facing the camera. She is wearing a green dress and is smiling at the camera. She is standing on a street at night](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2560/cpsprodpb/6aa0/live/d4191b40-b5d6-11f1-8c16-65d8f060d73f.jpg)

Jayda says she learned to cook by following TikTok videos

After "a lot of trial and error" following TikTok videos, the 20-year-old Coventry University student felt confident enough to film herself cooking.

Now she buys staples, and while she tries to stick to a weekly meal plan she mostly cooks what she is feeling.

## Remember to bring the store cupboard essentials

Chef and cookbook author Claire Thompson remembers her daughter Grace leaving home to go to university, and wondering: "Can she really cook?"

She sent Grace off with three crates of supplies, which her daughter described as being "stocked for the apocalypse just in case anything went awry".

![Claire is standing in a white kitchen, leaning on a chopping board. She is smiling but looking away from the camera. She is wearing a green jumper with a blue apron on top. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/92a3/live/f772b820-b032-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

Chef and cookbook author Claire Thompson says there is an essential store cupboard of items everyone needs to make quick and easy meals

But for Claire there are certain essentials everyone should consider stocking up on when leaving home.

"I think spices are a really good thing to start getting your head around," she told the BBC World Service's The Food Chain podcast.

"They deliver lots and lots of flavour for not very much money, and they last well in your store cupboard," she says.

"Adding a jar of chickpeas to a tomato sauce and adding that [to] your pasta, you're boosting really good levels of protein and bringing nutrients to a really pretty cheap dish really."
