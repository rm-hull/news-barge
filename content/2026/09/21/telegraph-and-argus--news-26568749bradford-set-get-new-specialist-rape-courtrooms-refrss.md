Victims of rape and serious sexual offences in Bradford could benefit from a new specialist courtroom under a major government overhaul of the justice system aimed at reducing delays and improving support for survivors.

The government has announced that every Crown Court in England and Wales will receive at least one courtroom dedicated to hearing rape and serious sexual offence cases over the next two years, meaning Bradford Crown Court is set to be included in the rollout.

The reforms will see rape and serious sexual offence trials prioritised in the courts system, with cases given fixed hearing dates rather than being placed on "floating lists" that can be postponed or moved at short notice.

## **Dedicated facilities for victims**

The specialist courtrooms will be designed with victims in mind and are expected to include private waiting rooms and separate entrances, helping survivors avoid contact with defendants and their supporters.

They will also be equipped with facilities allowing evidence to be pre-recorded and for victims to observe sentencing hearings remotely.

Ministers say the changes are intended to encourage more victims to come forward by making the court process less traumatic and ensuring cases are dealt with more quickly.

## **Tackling lengthy delays**

The announcement comes amid ongoing concerns about delays in the criminal justice system.

According to government figures, rape cases that reached the Crown Court in 2025 took an average of 424 days to conclude, with officials warning that lengthy waits can contribute to victims withdrawing from proceedings before a trial takes place.

Under the reforms, rape and serious sexual offence cases will be prioritised as standard under a new judicial listing framework.

The government said this would end the uncertainty created by floating trial dates and provide greater confidence for victims throughout the court process.

## **New support measures announced**

Alongside the courtroom changes, all court staff will receive trauma-informed training and a pilot scheme will introduce a single point of contact for victims navigating the court system.

A new Rape Justice Taskforce is also being created to bring together criminal justice leaders to tackle postponed trials and address issues that lead some victims to disengage from the process.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the reforms were designed to "rebalance the scales of justice and put victims first", while Lord Chancellor Alex Norris said victims had too often been treated as "cases to process and not as people to support".

## **Wider plans to tackle violence against women and girls**

The specialist courts form part of the government's wider pledge to halve violence against women and girls within the next decade.

Measures already announced include dedicated rape and sexual offence investigation teams in every police force by the end of 2027, expanded legal support for victims and investment in services supporting survivors of sexual violence.

Ciara Bergman, chief executive of Rape Crisis England & Wales, welcomed the package, describing it as a significant step towards ensuring courts are "fit for purpose" and better meet the needs of victims and survivors.

The government said it would continue pursuing reforms through the Courts and Tribunals Bill, which is expected to return to Parliament next month.
