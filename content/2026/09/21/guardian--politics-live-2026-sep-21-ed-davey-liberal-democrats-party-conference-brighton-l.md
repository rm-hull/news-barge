Farage names Helen Jenner as Reform UK's Welsh leader, saying resignation of predecessor after arrest just 'bump in road'

Ben Quinn

Ben Quinn is a Guardian political correspondent.

Nigel Farage has unveiled Helen Jenner as Reform UK’s new leader in Wales after her predecessor stood down last week following his arrest.

Jenner, who was previously Reform’s deputy leader in Wales, joined Farage on the steps of the Senedd, where he described the loss of the party’s Welsh leader last week as a “bump in the road”.

Dan Thomas announced last week that he was standing down after he was arrested and bailed on suspicion of assault and controlling or coercive behaviour.

The former Tory councillor had only been announced as Reform’s Welsh leader by Nigel Farage in February.

Jenner is a Welsh speaker from Neath, near Port Talbot in south Wales. She represents Bangor Conwy Môn in the Senedd.

“I have known Helen for many, many year. She is a determined campaigner and I think she is going to do a very, very good job,” said Farage.

Referring to the departure of Thomas, he added:

We will carry on as if nothing has happened

We could have done without this bump in the road. But it has happened, We are making the best of it.

Thomas issued a statement last Tuesday in the wake of intense speculation about his future, saying that he had been reported to the police on the previous weekend and was released without charge.

“I stress that I have done nothing wrong, but I do not want the coming headlines to be a distraction from the fantastic work our Senedd team is doing in holding this government to account,” he added.

Gwent police has said that a 45-year-old man, from the Aberbargoed area, was arrested on 13 September, on suspicion of assault and engaging in controlling/coercive behaviour. “He has been bailed pending further inquiries,” the police said.

How Lib Dems say they would fix 'broken water system' and abolish Ofwat

The Lib Dems have uncovered what they claim is evidence of a “revolving door” between Ofwat and the water industry it is in charge of regulating. They have used freedom of information requests to find out that, over the past five years, 29 former Ofwat employees went on to take jobs either directly with some of the main water companies (10) or trade bodies or consultancies supporting the water sector (17).

Commenting on the figures, Tim Farron, the Lib Dem environment spokesperson, said:

We must smash the revolving door between the two water regulators and the industry they are meant to control. The recycling of regulator employees into the sector is an open secret, and shows just why responsibility is persistently and systematically evaded …

We utterly condemn the failure to fix our broken water system or protect our rivers from polluters. Labour must stop acting like campaigners and use their position in government to act now and prove they’re serious about cleaning up our waterways.

Farron released the figures ahead of a debate tomorrow at the Lib Dem conference on what they claim is a comprehensive plan to reverse the failures of water privatisation. Summing up the plan, the Lib Dems say they would:

Abolish Ofwat: Replace the current regulator urgently with a new, powerful Clean Water Authority with statutory powers to end the sewage scandal, ban unjustified bonuses, and hold water executives legally accountable.

Mutualisation: Convert failing water companies into Mutually Owned Public Benefit Corporations that are professionally managed, jointly owned by customers, and reinvest all surpluses directly into the water system rather than paying out dividends.

Strengthen Special Administration designation (SAR): Create an additional environmental trigger for the Special Administration of water companies to transfer operations to a mutual benefit model in cases of chronic or egregious environmental failure.

End the Sewage Cover-Up: Require water companies to monitor and publish live volume data for sewage spills, not just the duration, ensuring pollution is accurately measured and transparently reported.

Tackle Water Poverty: Introduce a nationwide single social tariff for water bills to guarantee vulnerable households are protected from skyrocketing costs, as over 2.5m households are currently in debt to their water company.

Ban “Forever Chemicals”: Pursue a four nations agreement to restrict PFAS to prevent the manufacture and release of ‘forever chemicals’, alongside a remediation fund requiring polluters to pay for the cleanup.

The Liberal Democrats exist to build and safeguard a fair, free and open society, in which we seek to balance the fundamental values of liberty, equality and community, and in which no one shall be enslaved by poverty, ignorance or conformity.

Most of this is quite standard for a modern, progressive party – until you reach “conformity” at the end, which gives this a distinctive, liberal flavour.

In an unusual move, the party is using the quote as part of the backdrop to the conference stage. Normally parties go for just a bland, or blandish slogan. (According to Ben Riley-Smithin the Sunday Times yesterday, Labour’s conference slogan this year will be: “Hope Again.” This is not entirely bland, because it contains an implied dig at Keir Starmer.)

Helen Jenner, the new Reform UK leader in Wales, has told the BBC that she thinks there are a “few things” Reform needs to change, including giving members more of a say on policy.

She also rejected claims that there were problems with unity in the Reform UK group in the Senedd, after it was claimed last week that James Evans had enough support to be elected leader if he had wanted the post.

Jenner said:

I think that it was a really difficult time for us.

They say a week’s a long time in politics – to be honest, 24 hours was a long time in politics. But I think we are really united in terms of what we’re trying to achieve here.

Reform UK’s difficulties in Wales were highlighted by polling last week by YouGov for ITV Cymru Wales showing that, in terms of Westminster voting intention, support for Reform UK in Wales has fallen by seven points since May.

In the first YouGov / Barn Cymru (ITV Wales) Westminster voting intention poll since Andy Burnham took over as prime minister, Labour has seen a significant improvement in their vote share, accompanied by a decline for Reform UK.

Labour now take 18% of the vote, a 10pt increase from our last poll in May, just ahead of the 2026 Senedd elections. This means they are now neck and neck with Reform UK, who have experienced a seven point decline, to 19%.

Both trail Plaid Cymru by some margin, however, with their 27% vote share broadly similar to the 30% they achieved a few months ago.

Lib Dems say maternity services in crisis as they release figures showing serious childbirth injuries at new high

Ed Davey has claimed this morning that NHS maternity services are in crisis. He did so as the Liberal Democrats released figures which they said showed “the risk of life-altering childbirth injuries has reached a record high in 2026”.

Explaining the figures in a news release, the Lib Dems said:

NHS data analysed by the Liberal Democrats has revealed that 3 in every 100 women undergoing a vaginal birth now suffer severe 3rd or 4th-degree tears. These are agonising injuries so profound that patients must be rushed to an operating theatre for surgical repair. The rate of these severe tears has surged by 29% since 2021.

The crisis extends to life-threatening postpartum haemorrhages, with 2026 recording peak rates of severe bleeds involving a blood loss of more than 1.5 litres. The most recent quarter (April-June) had the highest level ever recorded – at 32.7 women per thousand. More than 8,000 women have been recorded as suffering these severe bleeds since the start of the year, more than double the number recorded over the equivalent period in 2021.

Recently the government announced that it is appointing a maternity and neonatal commissioner. The Lib Dems say this was a victory for their MP Helen Morgan, who had been campaigning for one.

Commenting on the figures released by the party today, Davey said:

These figures are a devastating indictment of our NHS maternity services. Thousands of women are being left alone during labour, terrified and exposed to life-altering trauma – because severe staffing shortages and missing consultants have pushed wards to breaking point.

The government cannot turn a blind eye to this national safety scandal. Every mother deserves safe, compassionate care when bringing a child into the world. The health secretary must act now – with the staffing, training and resources maternity wards need. This crisis must end by the close of this parliament.

In response, a spokesperson for the Department of Health and Social Care said:

Every woman deserves safe maternity care and the new health secretary [Yvette Cooper] has made this issue a personal priority.

Through the national maternity and neonatal taskforce that she is chairing, this government will deliver the reforms needed to ensure the very highest standards of safety and care for every mother and baby.

Davey has raised maternity services at the last two PMQs and at the most recent one, responding to Andy Burnham’s answer, he said: “Britain should be the safest country in the world to have a baby. We will keep raising this issue until it is the safest.”

'Wrong question' - how Lisa Smart told John Curtice she thought he was too negative about Lib Dems' electoral prospects

Peter Walker

Peter Walker is a senior Guardian political correspondent at the Lib Dem conference in Brighton.

It is a brave person who tells John Curtice, the eminent political scientist and guru of British polling, that he doesn’t really understand modern politics, but that’s what Lisa Smart of the Liberal Democrats did at the party’s conference in Brighton.

It was the job of Smart, the Hazel Grove MP and Lib Dem Cabinet Office spokesperson, to respond to Curtice after he gave a presentation at a fringe event on Sunday evening to set out what the latest polling says about the party.

Curtice does a version at each party conference these days, and they essentially all say much the same thing in various ways: you’re probably doomed.

The Lib Dem variant pointed out that the party’s anonymity, one of the main talking points of the conference, was seeing them shed votes to Labour, the Conservatives and Greens, and that they were miles from the national presence they enjoyed in 2010.

Responding, Smart’s message was blunt: Curtice was thinking of the world as it was in terms of two-party politics, and it is now one of five or even six. As such, things have changed:

How will the party get back to the 24% it won in 2010? I might gently suggest, Prof Curtice, that with winning 57 seats on 24% in 2010 and winning a record-breaking 72 seats on 12.2% of the vote in 2024, I know which one I prefer. I think John is asking the wrong question. We are not in two-party politics any more.

This is a distillation of the plan under Ed Davey – there is no point piling up votes across the country, and in a squeezed political world, a party like the Lib Dems needs to pick its targets. As Smart put it: “We are unashamedly executing a strategy that’s interested in winning elections.”

In response, Curtice argued that while this worked well in 2024, when the Lib Dems took 60-plus seats from the Conservatives, they had “already maxed out the strategy”, and were at risk of stalling. They were also, he warned, “potentially very, very exposed to a recovery in Conservative fortunes”.

Badenoch accused of U-turn after she pledges to bring back tourism VAT refund scheme she scrapped when minister

Kemi Badenoch has pledged to bring back tax-free shopping for tourists by reversing a post-Brexit rule change, the Press Association reports. PA says:

The decision to scrap the VAT refund scheme for international visitors in 2021 left the UK “less competitive” than many other European destinations as the country left the EU, the Tories said.

Business chiefs including the bosses of Fortnum and Mason, Paul Smith and the operator of Claridge’s hotel hailed the policy change as “pro-growth” and “pro-jobs”.

Visitor numbers to the UK have returned to near pre-Covid levels, but real-terms spending remains 8% lower than before the pandemic, according to estimates from tourism agency VisitBritain based on data from 2024.

The Tories said returning the VAT refund would be partly offset by a proposed “crackdown” on so-called “health tourism”, including more consistent charging of patients ineligible for free treatment.

Tax-free shopping could also be extended to cover EU tourists subject to evidence, with the rollout complete by the end of the next Parliament, the party said.

The Tories have pulled off a spectacular U-turn. Kemi Badenoch used to say tax-free shopping was a costly giveaway and now she’s trying to sell it as an economic miracle.

Davey says split on right in UK politics presents 'historic opportunity' for Lib Dems

Ed Davey has said the split on the right in UK politics gives the Liberal Democrats a “historic opportunity” to overtake the Conservatives.

In his interview on the Today programme this morning, Davey said:

I’m excited by the prospect of more Liberal Democrat MPs and building on the success we’ve had in recent years.

I think there’s a moral responsibility to stop the far right. I really take that very seriously. It wears heavily on my shoulders. We have to win to stop Mr Farage and stop Kemi Badenoch giving him the keys [to Downing Street].

But we also have a huge opportunity, a historic opportunity, because the right is split. The right is fragmented for the first time in parliamentary history.

I don’t think this is recognised enough. With them being split for the first time in 200 years of parliamentary election, that Liberal voice can not only be heard louder, but can come through and win many, many more seats.

Davey was referring to the way the Conservative party and Reform UK are essentially competing for the same voters and that without a pact – which both parties have rejected – the split could allow progressive parties to win in right-leaning seats at the next election.

Davey even claimed his party could beat the Tories at the next election. He said that developments in the Conservative party under Kemi Badenoch meant that liberal conservatives and pro-European conservatives were increasingly attracted to the Lib Dems and he went on:

And that’s why I think there’s a real chance we could end up with more MPs than the Conservatives after next election.

But when the presenter, Justin Webb, asked Davey if that meant, in his conference speech tomorrow, he would be telling his members to “go back to your constituencies and prepare for government”, Davey declined to say that he would copy the David Steel soundbite that became a much-mocked emblem of Liberal hubris. With Trump deeply unpopular in the UK, it is easy to see why Davey criticises him harshly. She says:

Downing Street insiders expect Burnham to be a bit more forceful [than Keir Starmer was] when he feels there’s a need to stand up for British interests. But there is no suggestion he will follow the more combative approach of Mark Carney, the Canadian prime minister.

Instead, one source said he would probably take a “third way” between Starmer and Carney, pushing back more than his predecessor publicly and privately, but avoiding open diplomatic warfare. A second source said the prime minister had three ambitions for Tuesday’s talks: bond with Trump, make the case for Ukraine, and avoid any drama.

Ed Davey rejects claims party is 'sleepwalking into irrelevance' under his leadership

Good morning. Ed Davey became Lib Dem leader in 2020, which means he is technically the longest-serving leader of a national party in the UK. (“Technically” because, while Nigel Farage only became leader of Reform UK in his current incumbency in 2024, he had led it before when it was called the Brexit party and, if you include Ukip, he has been leader of the anti-migrant, anti-EU right in British politics for more than 20 years.) After six years in charge, it is inevitable that some Lib Dems are starting to complain and, with the Lib Dem conference taking place in Brighton, Davey has been giving interviews this morning where he has had to respond to complaints from his critics.

The most vocal has been Julian Brazil, the leader of Devon county council, who told the BBC yesterday that the Lib Dems were “sleepwalking into irrelevance” with Davey at the helm.

Not surprisingly, Davey does not agree.

Asked about Brazil’s comments, the Lib Dem leader told BBC Breakfast:

We’ve grown really fast in recent years under my leadership. Record number of MPs. We’re running more councils than the Conservatives – councils up and down the country.

[I’m] really proud of that record and it means when you have a bigger party, you can’t please everyone all the time.

And Davey was a bit more blunt when asked the same question on Sky News.

I would say to the Devon councillor, we’ve now got six Liberal Democrat MPs in Devon. That’s not happened since the 1920s.

We have a huge number of councillors across Devon and are leading the way in Devon, and I could go around the rest of the country.

I happen to know Brazil. We’ve rarely seen eye to eye. So, all I would say is, we’re going to build on the success we’ve had.
