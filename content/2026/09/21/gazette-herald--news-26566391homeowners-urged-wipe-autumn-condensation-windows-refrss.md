Homeowners are being warned to wipe condensation off their windows this autumn.

Neglecting to do so could lead to mould and rot, experts at Lifestyle Blinds have warned.

Condensation forms when warm, moist indoor air meets cold window glass, causing moisture to collect on the surface.

If ignored, it can either evaporate back into the room or run down the frame, increasing the risk of mould and, in the case of wooden windows, rot.

Heath Showman from Lifestyle Blinds said: "Autumn is a particular problem time for condensation.

"As outdoor temperatures drop, warm, humid indoor air meets glass that’s much colder than it was during the summer.

"The moisture drops out of the air onto the glass, causing condensation.

"We also have windows shut for longer, meaning ventilation drops, the nights are longer and cooler and the weather wetter.

"There are lots of gadgets on the market to help keep windows condensation free, and whilst they do work, there are much cheaper ways to do it.

"Simply wiping it down is certainly the most cost effective – just make sure it’s part of your daily routine to keep on top of it throughout the coming months."

The advice is simple and low-cost.

Mr Showman recommends using a microfibre cloth or squeegee to wipe away condensation.

He also advises wringing the cloth out over a sink or outside the room, rather than near the window.

It’s also important to clean not just the glass but also the sills and frame edges, where moisture can collect and cause the most harm.

Once the area is dry, opening the window briefly can help clear the damp air.

Following this routine each morning can help prevent long-term issues.

Additional tips to reduce indoor moisture include covering pans while cooking, using extractor fans, and drying clothes outside or in well-ventilated spaces.

Keeping bathroom doors shut and fans running during baths or showers can also help.

To improve airflow, Lifestyle Blinds suggests opening windows daily for a short period, keeping window vents open, and leaving space around furniture to allow air to circulate.

Consistently following these steps can help protect homes from avoidable damage throughout the colder season.
