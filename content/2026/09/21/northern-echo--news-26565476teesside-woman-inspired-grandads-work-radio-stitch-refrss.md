A Teesside woman has completed a 50km challenge inspired by her grandad, who has spent more than 40 years bringing music, companionship and comfort to hospital patients.

For four decades, Elliot Kennedy has been a familiar voice on Radio Stitch, the volunteer-run station supporting patients in hospitals across Teesside and North Yorkshire.

Radio Stitch first went on air on 26 February 1976 and now reaches patients at James Cook University Hospital, University Hospital of Hartlepool and the Friarage Hospital in Northallerton.

At 73, Elliot continues to volunteer three days a week, helping new presenters learn to use the station’s equipment.

![]()Elliot Kennedy and Holly Cairnes. *(Image: NHS University Hospitals Tees)*

Now, his granddaughter has taken on an epic challenge to celebrate the station's 50th anniversary and support its work.

Hollie Cairnes, 30, completed a gruelling 50km run to mark the anniversary of Radio Stitch, which broadcasts from the University Hospital of North Tees and reaches patients, visitors and staff across several hospitals in the region.

The Middlesbrough care worker said years of watching her grandad volunteer inspired her to take on the challenge.

She said: "He’s volunteered at the station for more than 40 years and I can still remember him taking me and my sister through the hospital and into the studio.

"Seeing how much it means to him, and the pride he takes in being part of the team, has always stayed with me.

"Hospital stays can often be difficult and patients can feel lonely and uncertain. Radio Stitch helps bring entertainment, companionship and a little positivity to people when they need it most."

![]()Colin Overton, Andy Tingle and Elliot Kennedy of Radio Stitch. *(Image: Supplied)*

For half a century, Radio Stitch volunteers have dedicated their time to supporting patients during some of the most difficult periods of their lives.

From playing favourite songs and reading out requests to sharing local news and familiar voices from home, the station provides comfort, companionship and a welcome distraction for people facing illness, treatment and time away from home.

Elliot said: "When Hollie told me she was going to run 50km to raise money for Radio Stitch, I knew she'd do it. Determined doesn't begin to describe her.

"I'm incredibly proud of what she's achieved.

"Running more than a marathon is amazing, but doing it to support a charity that means so much to me makes it even more special.

"Every penny she has raised will help us continue bringing music, entertainment and a bit of cheer to patients during their time in hospital."

Hollie’s challenge raised £212 for the station, but she says supporting Radio Stitch was about more than fundraising.

"This incredible radio station has played a huge part in my grandad's life," she said.

"I'd love to support it so it can continue making a difference to others for years to come."

Hollie is already training for her next challenge, as she prepares for an even tougher 100km run.

As Radio Stitch celebrates 50 years on air, Hollie and Elliot hope its familiar voices will continue bringing comfort and connection to patients for years to come.
