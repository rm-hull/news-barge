A small farm shop and café a few minutes from the County Durham border has one of the more unusual locations of any rural food stop.

The Nook Farm Shop & Café sits beside Epiacum Roman Fort at Kirkhaugh, around two miles north of Alston on the A689 towards Brampton, and only a few minutes away from the County Durham border.

Visitors can stop for homemade food and locally produced goods before walking straight from the car park onto trails exploring the ancient Roman site.

The Nook opened in autumn 2019 and combines a café, farm shop and information centre.

Its owners say the business was established with an emphasis on supporting the local community, promoting local produce and helping visitors discover Epiacum.

![]()The Nook Farm Shop & Café *(Image: TRIPADVISOR)*

The shop sells fresh and frozen produce, while beef and lamb bred on the farm are both sold in the shop and used on the café menu.

Other goods are sourced locally, with the café specialising in homemade food.

The combination appears to have proved popular with customers.

The Nook currently has a rating of 4.8 out of five from Tripadvisor reviews, with visitors regularly mentioning its food, setting and friendly service.

One visitor from Leicestershire, writing in June 2026, described it as an "absolute gem", praising the quality of the ingredients, generous portions and cleanliness of the café.

Another visitor that month said The Nook was "a must when in Alston" after stopping for lunch, highlighting the surrounding views and service.

Cheese scones also receive repeated mentions.

A returning customer who stayed overnight in a campervan praised the cheese scones and tray bakes, while another reviewer who visited in 2025 singled out the local cheese, artisan bread and rarebit served with sourdough toast.

Epiacum, also known as Whitley Castle, is a Scheduled Ancient Monument with evidence of thousands of years of occupation.

The Roman fort stands on the Maiden Way, an ancient Roman road that provided an important link through the Pennines towards Hadrian's Wall.

Its shape is particularly distinctive.

Rather than following the more familiar rectangular layout of many Roman forts, Epiacum has an unusual lozenge-shaped plan dictated by the steep terrain.

Its surviving earthworks include substantial defensive banks and ditches which remain clearly visible in the landscape.

Visitors to The Nook can pick up information about the site and access walking trails directly from the farm shop's car park.

The wider landscape contains even more history, with evidence of centuries of occupation across this remote part of the North Pennines.

The working upland farmland sits amid the North Pennines National Landscape, making the café popular with walkers, cyclists, motorcyclists and people simply travelling through the area.

For anyone travelling between Alston and Brampton, The Nook offers more than the usual roadside coffee stop.

It is somewhere visitors can eat food made using local produce, browse the small farm shop and then walk out of the car park into a landscape shaped by the Romans almost 2,000 years ago.

Anyone planning a special journey should check the latest opening hours directly with the business before travelling.
