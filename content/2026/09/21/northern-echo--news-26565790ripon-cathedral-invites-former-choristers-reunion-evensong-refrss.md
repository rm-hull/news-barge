Former choir members from across the country are being invited back to a North Yorkshire cathedral for a special reunion celebrating a rich musical tradition.

Ripon Cathedral has invited former choir members to an Alumni Reunion Evensong in October, marking 30 years of its Girls' Choir.

Former choristers from across the decades are being encouraged to return, whether to sing in the service or join the congregation, with archival photographs and memorabilia also going on display.

Dinner and refreshments will follow the service for alumni and their guests.

Dr Ronny Krippner, Director of Music at Ripon Cathedral, said: "This promises to be a memorable occasion, not only for returning alumni but for everyone joining us for this beautiful service of choral evensong, sung by a choir of more than 100 voices.

"For alumni it is also a wonderful opportunity to reconnect with former choir friends."

Visit the Ripon Cathedral website for more information.

Ripon Cathedral Girls' Choir was formed in 1996 and gave its first performance at a Sunday morning Eucharist.

Ripon Cathedral's choral history dates back to AD 680, when founder St Wilfrid introduced cantors from Canterbury Cathedral.

The Cathedral has built on that musical tradition over the centuries and has recently been broadcast on BBC One and BBC Radio 3.

In July 2025, Ripon Cathedral Choir sang four evensongs and a Sunday Sung Eucharist during a four-day residency at Westminster Abbey.

The Cathedral's organ, which still includes pipes from 1690, is a significant part of its musical appeal.

A wooden hand was famously added to the organ case in 1695, which moves in time with the music and can still be used to conduct the choir today.

The Alumni Reunion Evensong will take place on Saturday, October 10 at 5.30pm and is open to the public.
