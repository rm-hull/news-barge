## Sam Smith's brewery markets three more North Yorkshire pubs in £6m sale

The Cross Keys at Stillingfleet *(Image: Fleurets)*

At least three more Sam Smith’s pubs in North Yorkshire have been put on the market by the Tadcaster-based brewery as part of a £6 million sale.

Malton man Tom Taverner has updated his list of pubs that the brewery is disposing of.

This now extends to 21 Sam Smith’s brewery owned pubs, an increase of more than double the number reported on the market a week or so ago.

Tom said: “From what I've picked up there's 21 Sam's pubs currently up for sale, £6m would get you the lot! I've also added a few already sold. More details on Fleurets and BTG Eddison websites.”

The new listings include the former Cross Keys Inn at Stillingfleet.

Property agents Fleurets are marketing the property for £475,000.

It says the venue consists of a pub with land for 6-8 houses.

The pub itself includes Bar and Restaurant Areas, Catering Kitchen and 3 Bed Accommodation.

It is on a 0.83ha site with car park and garden.

The sales details speak of the property, which has been closed as a pub for more than seven years, being sold Freehold.

![]()Inside the Cross Keys *(Image: Fleurets)*

There is no clause saying it cannot be sold for pub use, as is the case for many other pubs Sam Smith’s brewery has put on the market.

Among them, as the Press recently reported, was the Wellington Inn at Knaresborough, which is on the market for £325,000 and is being sold for non-pub use only.

It is also being marketed by Fleurets and has been closed since 2022.

Other North Yorkshire Sam Smith’s pubs which have just gone on the market include the Abbey Inn at Guisborough for £195,000, which closed last year.

It is being sold for non-pub use only and is described as suitable for residential conversion.

The Arlington Hotel in Loftus is also on the market, but again for non-pub use only.

Fleurets are marketing the property for £175,000.

The agents describe the hotel as a “Substantial detached property.”

The agents add the venue is a “3 storey building with private yard to the rear” in an “Edge of village location” that is also “suitable for Residential Conversion subject to planning.”

Tom Taverner says these venues going to market leaves Sam Smith’s brewery with an estate of 288 pubs with 169 open.

He added: “With the high level of change going on I'm aiming to publish an updated list early October.”

Earlier this month, The Press approached Sam Smith’s Company Secretary Christian Horton about the brewery selling pubs and its efforts to fill its empty pubs.

Mr Horton replied he would prefer not to comment on commercial matters like pub sales, but he said “the brewery is continuing to look for new pub managers to reopen any closed pubs.”
