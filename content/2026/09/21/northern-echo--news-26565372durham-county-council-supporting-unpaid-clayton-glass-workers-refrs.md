Durham County Council has pledged to support workers at a County Durham glass factory after staff learnt they would not be paid and the firm was going into administration at the end of last week.

Around 160 Clayton Glass employees at Harelaw Industrial Estate in Stanley were told on Friday, September 18, that the company intended to appoint administrators.

The council said it was shocked by the news, and directed staff to available support and called for talks with the company’s bosses.

The council said it had not been told about problems at the company before staff learnt they would lose their jobs.

![]()The Clayton Glass factory *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT/NORTHERN ECHO)*

A statement from the council reads: "We are concerned to hear about the current situation at Clayton Glass.

"Our thoughts are very much with the company’s employees and their families at this difficult time.

"Unfortunately, we were not notified about any challenges Clayton Glass is facing in advance of today.

"However, we would welcome discussions with the company’s directors to see how we may be able to assist them and their employees.

"If any Clayton Glass employees are facing immediate financial hardship, they may be eligible for support through our Crisis and Resilience Fund."

![]()The Clayton Glass sign *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The post added: "Information about eligibility and how to apply is available at www.durham.gov.uk/crf. Alternatively, call 03000 267 900.

"Further information and advice can be found at www.adviceincountydurham.org.uk."

An email seen by The Northern Echo told staff they would not be paid because the company had no available funds.

The message stated: "Regrettably, due to our current lack of liquid funds and restrictions placed upon us during this process, we cannot process or issue due and upcoming payments.

"You will not receive your regular salary or wages on the upcoming payday.

"We know this news is devastating. We are deeply sorry for the immense stress and hardship this causes you and your families."

![]()The Clayton Glass factory *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The email said administrators would take control of the business once appointed and update staff about unpaid wages and other entitlements.

Clayton Glass has been approached for comment.

A worker who asked not to be named said staff arrived at the factory on Friday, September 18, expecting answers but were left frustrated and uncertain about their future.

He said: "We got told by email this morning that we basically weren't getting paid and that administrators were coming in.

"Payday's today. We've just never got paid."

The worker said the mood inside the factory was "horrible".

He added: "Everybody's frustrated. They feel hard done to.

"People have families, they've got bills to pay, and some people work Friday to Friday. Not everybody's got savings.

"It's going to be a hard time for them."

Annfield Plain and Tanfield councillor Darren Grimes said workers had been treated in an “absolutely disgusting” way.

"To come in, be told you're not going to get paid on payday itself, and you have to go away and wonder how you're going to actually feed your family, that's how serious it is," he said.

"I think the way in which they've gone about it is truly anti-human and really quite sinister.

"You turn up expecting to put in your shift only to be told it's going into administration and you're not going to get paid.

"I can't get over the heartless nature of the way in which it's been conducted."

North Durham MP Luke Akehurst said the situation had been “badly mismanaged”.

He said: "I am disgusted on behalf of the hard-working staff at Clayton Glass that their employers have put them in this position.

"While I am very appreciative of the pressures businesses are under, for example due to energy costs, there is no excuse for springing this kind of announcement on staff.

"This has been badly mismanaged, and I will support any constituent that reaches out to me."
