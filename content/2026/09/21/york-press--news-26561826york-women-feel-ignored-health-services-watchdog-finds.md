There is “a lot more that needs to happen” to give York women confidence that their health concerns will be taken seriously, the manager of a watchdog service has said.

Healthwatch York found that women in the city feel ignored, disbelieved and dismissed by healthcare professionals.

Its report said many women described years of symptoms being attributed to anxiety, depression or other mental health problems before eventually receiving a physical diagnosis.

One woman who endured a 21-year wait to be diagnosed with endometriosis – a condition affecting around one in 10 women – said she was “only taken seriously when I broke down in a GP’s office”.

“I saw GPs approximately twice a year with issues and they told me it was in my head, I should get on with it, it was just a part of being a woman and I was over-reacting,” the woman said.

Healthwatch York’s report found that of the 78 people who responded to the survey, more than half (55 per cent) said healthcare professionals did not listen to them, two fifths (39.5 per cent) said that they were not believed, and one third of respondents (33 per cent) said they were first told they had a mental health issue.

Almost a third (31 per cent) of respondents said they were told their symptoms were “normal for women or they experienced misogyny”, Healthwatch said.

One woman quoted in the report said: “Ableism, misogyny, gaslighting and ignorance are common experiences for me.

“I have to spend so much time rehearsing what to say in my appointments, bringing my own evidence, including professional guidelines, to appointments.

“Afterwards I am often overwhelmed by the experience and rarely feel a sense of relief that I have got the help I need.”

Meanwhile, nearly half (47 per cent) of the respondents said they had come across at least one sympathetic and understanding healthcare professional.

“We also heard from others who talked about good healthcare and reflected on their experience as a positive one,” the Healthwatch report said.

![]()Siân Balsom, manager of Healthwatch York *(Image: Supplied)*

Siân Balsom, Healthwatch York’s manager, said it welcomed and celebrated “York health professionals who are making a difference every day to women's lives in our city”.

“However, from what we have heard there is a lot more that needs to happen to give women confidence that they, and their health concerns, will be taken seriously.”

She noted that new strategies have been implemented to improve female healthcare in the city but said: “The proof of the new strategies and initiatives will be in women's experiences.

“We look forward to hearing more about a health service that consistently listens, respects and cares for women and women's health issues.”
