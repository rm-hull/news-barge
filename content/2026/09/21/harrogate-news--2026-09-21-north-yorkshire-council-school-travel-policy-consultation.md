North Yorkshire Council has launched a comprehensive six-week public consultation on proposed changes to its controversial **Home to School Travel Policy**, following a post-implementation review of the system. The service, which costs more than**£52 million annually** to transport approximately 11,000 eligible pupils across England’s largest county, underwent radical changes in September 2024 when free travel eligibility was restricted strictly to a child’s nearest suitable school rather than their traditional catchment school.

The newly unveiled review contains 24 total recommendations aimed at addressing operational challenges, though the council executive has confirmed that eligibility will continue to be based on the nearest school model rather than returning to catchment-based boundaries. While senior local authority leaders argue that the current system is necessary to keep the massive transport network financially sustainable, local campaigners and educational advocates argue the rules continue to inflict severe stress and administrative burdens on rural North Yorkshire families.

## The Proposed Changes and Council Justifications (The Pros)

Senior council officials and supporters of the current policy structure highlight several areas where the recommended revisions aim to refine and improve the practical delivery of the multi-million-pound service:

* **Discretion for Shared Drop-Off Points:** The consultation is explicitly seeking public feedback on introducing discretionary transport measures where two distinct schools share a single, unified drop-off point. This rule revision would directly benefit families navigating transport allocations between Ripon Grammar School and Outwood Academy Ripon, as well as Richmond School and St Francis Xavier School.
* **Removing Unsuitable and Hazardous Routes:** The review identifies five specific, remote rural pathways that are frequently hit by severe weather or impractical driving conditions, recommending they be permanently removed from the digital mapping assessment system. These high-altitude passes include Fleet Moss Road, Keld to Kirkby Stephen, Buttertubs Pass, The Stang, and Kidstone Pass.
* **Enhanced Transparency and Appeals:** The council has pledged to strengthen the independent appeals process, improve the accuracy of regional routing data, and provide much greater clarity to parents regarding exactly how home-to-school walking distances are digitally measured.

## The Impact on Families and System Disruption (The Cons)

Despite the council’s focus on operational tweaks, widespread criticism persists from opposition councillors, community advocates, and affected parents who argue the “nearest school” policy remains fundamentally flawed:

* **Severe Emotional and Educational Strain:** Critics maintain that treating school transport as a purely financial commodity ignores the human cost, introducing massive anxiety and disruption into children’s lives by effectively forcing them away from their local community or catchment schools.
* **Intrusive and Aggressive Appeal Barriers:** Parents attempting to appeal transport rejections under the 2024 guidelines report facing adversarial Stage 2 appeal panels, where single parents and lower-income families are routinely forced to disclose deeply personal financial details and sensitive domestic circumstances to justify support.
* **The Rural Penalty:** By continuing to reject a return to catchment-based transport eligibility, families living near boundary lines face situations where their nearest mathematical school sits outside North Yorkshire entirely, effectively penalising families living in isolated, deeply rural communities.

## Key Deadlines and Transition Timelines

The public consultation survey is officially open and will run until the final deadline on **Wednesday, 4 November 2026**. Following the collection of public data, the seven core recommendations will be debated and voted on during a full North Yorkshire Council meeting in November.

Local authority education teams have stressed that any approved policy changes will not take effect before September 2027 at the earliest, with the primary impact hitting children starting primary school or transferring to secondary school in September 2028. Parents currently completing their secondary school applications ahead of the upcoming **31 October 2026 deadline** are reminded that the existing, un-amended 2024 policy remains fully in place for the September 2027 intake, and school preferences should be submitted based on current transport maps.
