The bulldozers are posed to move into the Kirkgate Centre signalling the end of an era for the shopping complex - but this TV advert shows a time when things were much more upbeat.

The advertising campaign was commissioned in the late-1980s when the Kirkgate Centre was the city's pre-eminent shopping destination.

Kirkgate's Christmas decorations were so impressive that they were a reason enough for families with young children to visit the city centre.

Many readers will have fond memories of riding the escalators at Christmas.

T&A features writer Emma Clayton wrote earlier this year: "The Kirkgate Shopping Centre is still remembered by many as ‘the Arndale’, and anyone who grew up in Bradford will remember its fountains, escalators, the wooden hippo slide and Santa’s grotto.

"It has been a backdrop to many people’s lives - people went on first dates there, got engaged there, had their first Saturday jobs there, and spent happy times wandering round the shops and taking a breather in cafes there."

Its history stretches back more than 150 years, long before the concrete complex that many remember today.

The site was originally home to Bradford’s covered Kirkgate Market, built in 1872 and designed by architects Lockwood & Mawson, who also created St George’s Hall and the Wool Exchange.

The market became a central part of city life for generations, but changing attitudes to urban planning in the 1970s led to its demolition in 1973.

In its place, a modern shopping centre was built, opening on November 22, 1973.

The new Kirkgate Shopping Centre offered 350,000 square feet of retail space, an indoor market, and a multi-storey car park.

Designed by John Brunton & Partners in the Brutalist style, it became one of Bradford’s busiest retail destinations and was widely known as the Kirkgate Arndale Centre.

However, the rise of retail parks and online shopping led to a gradual decline.

By the 2020s, many of its units were empty, though loyal traders and customers continued to visit.

Bradford Council later approved plans to include the site in the City Village regeneration scheme.

The centre closed for the final time in June 2026, marking the end of an era that began with the Victorian market and continued through the decades.

Its demolition will bring to a close a significant chapter in Bradford’s commercial history.

The site will now be redeveloped to make way for new housing, green spaces, and a reimagined city centre as part of the wider regeneration project.

The transformation marks both an ending and a new beginning for this historic part of Bradford.
