Passengers at Newcastle Airport are still facing delays after an air traffic control fault disrupted flights across northern England and Scotland.

Flights were disrupted on Monday morning (September 21) after National Air Traffic Services (Nats) reported a "technical issue" at its Prestwick control centre in Scotland, which is responsible for managing air traffic across a large area of UK airspace.

A spokesperson for Newcastle Airport said the issue had caused delays to flights operating from the airport.

They said: “We are experiencing some delays due to a technical issue at NATS’ Prestwick centre in Scotland.

“We are monitoring the situation closely and are in regular contact with NATS as they work to resolve the issue as quickly as possible.

“Passengers should check with their airline for the latest flight information.”

Nats later confirmed the problem has since been fixed, but warned some disruption could continue as services return to normal.

A statement said: “We are working with airports and airlines to safely lift air traffic regulations as quickly as we can to minimise any further disruption.

"Passengers should still check the status of their flight with their airline. We apologise for the disruption.”

On X, Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander said the issue was “deeply frustrating” but confirmed engineers had resolved the problem.

She said: "Engineers have fixed the problem and systems are resuming.

"I know this will be deeply frustrating for passengers after the previous issue. There may be some delays as things reset. Please check with your airline for updates."

The disruption prompted criticism from airlines, with easyJet questioning the resilience of the UK's air traffic control systems following another technical failure.

The airline said: "A Nats air traffic control system failure in its Prestwick centre is causing delays to flights with some unable to operate.

"This, once again, calls into question the resilience of Nats’ systems and demonstrates the need for firm actions to prevent these repeated failures.
