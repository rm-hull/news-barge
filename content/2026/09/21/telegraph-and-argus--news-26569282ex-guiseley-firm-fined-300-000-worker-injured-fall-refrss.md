A former Guiseley housebuilder has been fined £300,000 after a worker suffered life-changing spinal injuries in a fall.

Joseph Tuck, 24, was working as a temporary labourer for a scaffolding company when he fell one storey on to concrete in January 2024.

He was working on a site where Hillbeck Homes (Sowerby Bridge) Ltd was building four new homes. The company was formerly based at Bretton Place, Otley Road, Guiseley.

It was Mr Tuck’s second week in the job when he stepped backwards and fell through an opening intended for a staircase that had not been installed.

The stairwell had not been securely covered after loose scaffold boards were removed. Its open edges were also not protected.

There was no alternative way to access the sub-floor.

West Yorkshire Fire & Rescue and the HART team had to rescue Mr Tuck, who had fallen unconscious.

He suffered serious spinal injuries and was unable to work for months.

He has not returned to a physical job since the incident.

A Health and Safety Executive investigation found that Hillbeck Homes had failed to put adequate measures in place to prevent falls from height.

The company was found guilty of three charges at Leeds Magistrates’ Court and fined £300,000.

It was also ordered to pay £9,284.05 in costs on September 16.

The HSE said the company failed to properly plan and supervise work at height and to take suitable and sufficient steps to prevent a fall.

HSE guidance says employers must plan work properly, complete task-specific risk assessments and use physical safeguards.

These can include scaffolding, guardrails and coverings around openings in floors, roofs and other work platforms.

HSE Inspector Louise Redgrove said: “Falls from height remain one of the leading causes of death in the construction industry, accounting for around a quarter of all fatal injuries to workers each year.

“This was a wholly avoidable incident caused by basic failures in planning and supervision.

“The stairwell opening was not adequately protected, and simple measures such as securing the scaffold boards or installing edge protection would have prevented Mr Tuck from falling and sustaining serious spinal injuries.

“This fine should serve as a clear reminder to the construction industry that both the courts and HSE take breaches of health and safety law extremely seriously.

“HSE will not hesitate to take enforcement action where employers fail to take the necessary steps to protect workers from harm.”

The prosecution was brought by HSE enforcement lawyer Matthew Reynolds and paralegal officer Henrietta Ruthven.
