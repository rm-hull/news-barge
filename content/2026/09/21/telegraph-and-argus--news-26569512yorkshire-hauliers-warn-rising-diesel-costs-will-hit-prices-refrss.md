Haulage firms across Yorkshire are warning that soaring diesel prices are driving up costs throughout the supply chain, with one operator reporting fuel bills have increased by as much as £40,000 a month and industry leaders are calling for Government intervention.

Transport businesses say they are being forced to review fuel surcharges, absorb rising operating costs and pass some increases on to customers as diesel prices continue to climb.

The warning comes as the RAC says diesel is approaching record-high prices, while businesses throughout the logistics sector brace for further increases.

## **Hauliers feeling the pressure**

Neil Chappelow, managing director of Leeds-based Farsley Transport Ltd, said rising fuel costs are having a major impact on the business.

The company operates nearly 70 vehicles and has seen its monthly fuel bill rise dramatically in recent months.

Mr Chappelow said: "We're probably up £30,000 to £40,000 a month in fuel...we've just got notifications that it's going to be another five-and-a-half to seven pence a litre increase over the weekend."

He said an average journey to London could now cost an additional £160 to £170 in fuel alone compared with previous rates.

While haulage companies can introduce fuel surcharges, he warned there is a limit to how much can be passed on without affecting customers.

"We can only absorb so much cost," he said. "The customer then passes it on to the person who's buying the goods."

## **'A domino effect' on prices**

A spokesperson for Bradford-based Nigel Rice Transport said the impact of higher diesel costs spreads through the entire supply chain.

They explained that fuel surcharges are reviewed regularly and communicated transparently to customers, but ultimately increased transport costs feed into wider inflation.

The spokesperson said: "When the fuel increases, obviously we need to pass this charge onto the customer.

"The customer then pays for it, but in the long run the consumer of that product pays for it because inflation increases. It's just a domino effect."

They added that some haulage firms operate on fixed pallet rates that include fuel costs, meaning sharp increases can quickly erode profitability.

## **Businesses face difficult choices**

Jamie Wood, general manager of KLG Europe, said recent fuel price rises are affecting the business every day.

The company runs its own fleet and has seen transport costs increase significantly.

Mr Wood said some firms are attempting to absorb part of the additional cost to retain customers, while others have little choice but to pass charges on.

He said: "At the end of the day clients are fighting to survive too."

Mr Wood added that support from Government should be considered, suggesting reductions in fuel duty or a rebate scheme for freight operators.

## **Diesel theft fears increase**

Rising diesel prices are creating additional challenges beyond day-to-day operating costs.

Mr Chappelow said the cost of filling an articulated lorry can now reach between £1,200 and £1,400, increasing concerns about fuel theft.

As a result, Farsley Transport has invested in additional security measures and closely monitors vehicles and fuel usage.

"We're like a honey pot," he said.

"The more fuel increases, the more threat there is of diesel theft."

## **Calls for Government action**

Industry figures have urged ministers to provide support as costs continue to rise.

Mr Chappelow said reducing fuel duty would provide immediate relief for haulage operators struggling with spiralling expenses.

"The Government need to do something very fast," he said. "They need to do something with the duty."

Mr Wood echoed those concerns, arguing that fuel taxation remains a major burden on the logistics sector and that additional support should be considered for an industry that underpins much of the UK's economy.

## **Why are fuel prices rising?**

According to the AA, global oil prices have surged due to geopolitical tensions involving Iran and the United States, pushing wholesale petrol and diesel costs to their highest levels since 2022.

The organisation has also criticised a lack of competition between fuel retailers, claiming "price matching" allows forecourt prices to remain higher than they might otherwise be.

Meanwhile, RAC head of policy Simon Williams warned diesel prices are on course to reach a new all-time high.

He said the average price of diesel had risen by nearly 12p per litre this month, while petrol has increased by more than 10p.

For Yorkshire's haulage firms, however, the message is simple: if diesel prices continue to rise, the financial impact will not stop with the transport sector.

As firms battle rising costs, industry leaders warn the effects are likely to be felt throughout the wider economy.
