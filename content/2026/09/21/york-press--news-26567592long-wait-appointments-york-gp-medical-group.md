## Patients 'waiting more than a month' for routine appointments in York

Fulford Surgery *(Image: Sarah Cooper)*

Patients at York’s largest medical group are being told routine GP appointments may take more than a month.

Priory Medical Group operates eight surgeries in the region, including practices in Fulford, Heworth and Acomb.

Patients requesting an appointment receive an automated text saying their request is “under review”.

They are then told that a routine appointment will be offered only after a minimum wait of 35 days.

Some patients say they have been informed that appointments could be five or six weeks away.

The group has a 'poor' rating on the GP Ratings website, where patients have raised concerns about securing appointments.

One review states: “Priory Medical Group must be one of the worst care providers in the UK”. Another says: “I strongly advise against [registering your] family here”.

Priory Medical Group has a 2.5 out of five rating on Google.

![]()Long waits for a GP appointment are being reported in York. *(Image: Pexels)*

A reviewer, in August 2026, wrote: “Been waiting over 24 hours for a response after [being] promised a call first thing in morning just nothing but lies and stress [being] caused from you lot”.

The response stated: “Thank you for your feedback. We are sorry to hear that you were unhappy with your experience. If you would like to discuss this further, please contact the practice directly through the following link: Priory Medical - General Enquiries form."

Joan Henderson, whose name has been changed, is an 87-year-old Fishergate resident with a chronic pain condition.

She is registered at the Fulford Surgery and says she has seen a GP in person only twice in four years, despite repeated calls and visits.

A broken bone in her back was diagnosed four years ago after she was referred to hospital in Hull.

The condition affects her daily life and she takes pain medication.

Ms Henderson said: “I just feel so disappointed.

“It’s four years ago, when I couldn’t move properly. I saw a [Priory Medical Group] GP then and was referred to a hospital in Hull, where I was told I had broken a bone in my back: too much bending over, weeding and planting, which really upset me because I love doing all that.

"You know, it was a bit of a bit of a blow actually. And I've been struggling ever since. Our GPs at the Priory Medical, they're just, I don't know what happens, but they don't see anybody.”

![]()Fulford Surgery *(Image: Sarah Cooper)*

She also said automated messages had caused confusion.

In May 2026, she received a text saying she had chronic kidney disease, despite not requesting help for the condition and having no tests.

When she attended the surgery, Ms Henderson said a receptionist told her: “Don’t worry, most old people get that.”

She later wrote to the practice and received a phone call. Ms Henderson said: “I go down there trying to make an appointment but I can’t see a doctor.

"It's extraordinary. You've asked for an appointment and [the receptionist says] well, we'll have a word with them. And then about a week or two weeks later, they ring me up. Whatever people say, it isn't the same as actually face-to-face.”

NHS patient survey data shows 57 per cent of patients nationally find it easy to telephone their practice. Priory Medical Group scores 31 per cent.

A Priory Medical Group spokesperson said: “A GP triages all routine and urgent cases on a daily basis - this includes determining if a patient would benefit from telephone or face-to-face appointment initially depending on clinical features.

"Patient preference is always taken into account, if they request a face-to-face appointment, this is always noted on the form completed by reception/patient central teams and the request granted.

"Any telephone appointments can also be converted to face-to-face following discussion between patient and clinician.”

Ms Henderson said: “I’d just love to talk to [a GP], only for 10 minutes, quarter of an hour, but just love to talk to them about various things, you know. There's a real inability [at Priory] to see a GP face-to-face.”
