There has been a "lack of meaningful engagement" between the mayor and York's hospitality sector over plans to introduce a visitor levy, an industry group has claimed.

Hospitality Association York (HAY) claimed it has attempted to raise its concerns with David Skaith about the levy, dubbed a "tourist tax", but the mayor's office "has yet to share the findings of the survey he commissioned with HAY members".

A spokesperson for Mr Skaith said they did not recognise HAY's claims and the mayor "remains committed to meaningful engagement with the hospitality and tourism sector".

Mr Skaith has said the levy could be a "game changer" for York and North Yorkshire by raising up to £26-million-a-year which would be reinvested locally.

But HAY said its members are concerned about the fairness of the levy, arguing that York's latest tourism figures show the city had 8.88 million visitors in 2025 with 7.01 million day visitors and 1.87 million overnight visitors.

"This means around four in five visitors would not pay an overnight levy," a HAY spokesperson said.

"There is also a concern around the term 'visitor levy', which suggests a charge aimed at people visiting from outside the area, or even from outside the country.

"The levy would apply to anyone staying in paid accommodation, regardless of where they live, including local people staying overnight in York.

"Overseas visitors made up just 14 per cent of York's total visitors in 2024, meaning the vast majority came from within the UK."

HAY also said it is feared that introducing a visitor levy could make "overnight stays less attractive" in York, resulting in fewer people deciding to visit the city.

However, a York St John University report found no evidence that people would avoid visiting the county if a tourist tax was introduced.

The report instead pointed to visitor growth remaining steady in places that have already implemented a levy on overnight stays, such as Edinburgh, Manchester, Paris and Barcelona.

Meanwhile, HAY questioned "the need to raise additional revenue" in York through the visitor levy.

HAY’s spokesperson argued that York businesses "already contribute significantly through the York BID, funding additional services and improvements to the city centre alongside the council's own responsibilities".

"With ongoing concerns around the cost and delivery of major council projects, there must be confidence that existing public money is being managed effectively before asking businesses and visitors to contribute more," they said.

## Concern over where money raised from tourist levy would go, group says

HAY's spokesperson said there is also a "question over where the money raised would go", after Mr Skaith told The Press that any money raised from the tourist levy in York would be put into a wider pot for the county.

"We want to see clear and transparent evidence that the areas generating the revenue would receive a fair and proportionate benefit," they said.

![]()David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire *(Image: Supplied)*

The spokesperson added: "Devolution was meant to make things easier and bring decision-making closer to local communities, yet we now appear to have another layer of government and administration making things more complicated.

"Having spoken to fellow hoteliers and hospitality businesses across North Yorkshire, we know these concerns are not unique to York.

"There is widespread apprehension about the potential impact of another charge on an industry already under considerable pressure.

"We remain willing to engage constructively, but consultation must be meaningful and give the hospitality sector a genuine opportunity to influence what happens next.

"If a levy is to be introduced, there must be robust evidence of its economic impact, a clear plan for the use of any revenue and proper engagement with the businesses expected to administer the scheme."

Mr Skaith's spokesperson said: "We don't recognise these claims. The mayor remains committed to meaningful engagement with the hospitality and tourism sector, including the many representative groups across the county.

"Following the publication of the government's response to the public consultation, the mayor will continue to meet with representative bodies as we move towards setting out an Indicative Investment Plan by the end of the year so that the sector, and crucially our residents, know the benefits that this extra investment will bring to York and North Yorkshire."
