Police stormed homes arresting 17 people as part of an organised crime crackdown sparked by the deaths of two of their colleagues.

More than 100 officers took part in Operation Artemis, targeting suspects linked to organised crime groups in Middlesbrough and Redcar.

It follows a horrific crash on the A66 last month which killed seven males including much-loved Cleveland Police officers PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38.

The officers suffered fatal injuries when a Volkswagen Passat which had been chased by police collided with their marked vehicle seconds after the pursuit was terminated on August 22.

During Monday's day-long operation officers from the National Crime Agency (NCA) and North East Regional Organised Crime Unit (NEROCU) Unit joined Teesside officers in the crackdown.

![]()Cleveland Police officers conduct a raid on a property in South Bank, as part of Operation Artemis, to tackle drug dealers in the troubled South Bank and Grangetown areas

Pictures show uniformed officers leading suspects away in handcuffs before they were placed in the back of police vans.

Cleveland Police has confirmed 17 people were arrested on suspicion of a variety of offences including drug dealing and theft of a motor vehicle.

Three men and a woman were arrested at a Birchington Avenue address in Grangetown on suspicion of supplying a class A drug.

The three men, aged 37, 43 and 60, and the 58-year-old woman remain in police custody.

Officers recovered suspected class A drugs and cash, while a dog believed to be a banned breed was also seized from the address.

![]()A 22-year-old woman arrested on suspicion of being a member of an OCG

Elsewhere, a 22-year-old woman was arrested at a Lancaster Road, Middlesbrough home, accused of having links to an organised crime group - another woman was arrested nearby on Ullswater Close.

At an address on Moorbeck Way in Ormesby, officers recovered a knife, baton, knuckle-duster and suspected class C drugs.

The operation also addressed off-road bike problems and included public reassurance activity.

Seizures included an illegal e-bike, an electric scooter, an electric assisted pedal cycle or “revvi” and a heavy construction tool.

It follows weeks of unrest across Teesside following the officers' deaths.

Cleveland Police **arrested 17 people** in the days after the collision.

One man, on Monday (September 21), appeared at Teesside Crown Court, where he pleaded not guilty to possessing cocaine with intent to supply, and possessing criminal property on August 22.

Cameron George, 26, of Micklow Close, Redcar, was remanded in custody by Judge Francis Laird, the Recorder of Middlesbrough, until his trial on February 4, next year.

Cleveland Police Chief Constable Victoria Fuller previously described the fallen officers as “brave, committed, professional people who put the public before themselves”.
