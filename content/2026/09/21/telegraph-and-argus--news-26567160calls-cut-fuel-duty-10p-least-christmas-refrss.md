The Liberal Democrats have called on the UK Government to cut fuel duty by 10p per litre until at least Christmas to ease cost of living pressures.

Sir Ed Davey, party leader, has urged Prime Minister Andy Burnham to cut fuel duty until Christmas, arguing that higher Treasury revenues from oil, gas and energy could fund the move.

Sir Ed said: "The Government’s getting a huge amount of money into the exchequer because of higher oil and gas prices, higher energy profits levy, higher gas tax revenue, higher VAT.

"And rather than keeping that in when people are struggling with the cost of living, with the cost of fitting up their car with petrol and diesel, we think they should give it back.

"And if they did that by cutting fuel duty by 10p, that’d be a 12p reduction at the pumps.

"That would really help people who are struggling with fuel costs."

The Liberal Democrats are also calling for the planned 3p fuel duty rise in January to be scrapped, alongside other measures to reduce travel costs.

They want to see bus fares capped at £1, rail fares cut by 10 per cent and the cost of driving electric vehicles reduced by cutting VAT on public charging and reviewing the network costs that push up charging prices.

The proposals come amid growing pressure on Chancellor John Healey to announce measures in the upcoming Budget to address rising living costs.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said Mr Healey will approach the economy with the "highest degree of prudence" while also aiming to protect living standards.

Sir Ed has linked the rise in the cost of getting around to "Donald Trump’s idiotic war with Iran,".

The Liberal Democrats are using their four-day conference in Brighton to finalise their general election platform, seeking to regain momentum after winning a record number of MPs in the last election but struggling to match the attention generated by Reform UK and the Green Party.

At a rally on the first night of the conference, Sir Ed criticised Nigel Farage and Elon Musk, vowing to oppose what he called "nasty populism."

Asked if he believed he had the charisma to challenge Green leader Zack Polanski and Mr Farage, Sir Ed said: "100 per cent.

"Now I do my politics differently from them.

"It’s very easy to be exciting if you say things that are a bit crazy.

"You know, Donald Trump’s very exciting, and he’s damaging America and damaging the world.

"Nigel Farage is very exciting, but he wants to bring that Trump damage to our country, and we’re going to stop him."

He argued the best way to challenge populism is with "real, clear policies" that will deliver results.

Sir Ed said: "And you know I think I’m known for having a bit of fun too, because I think you need to get a bit of joy back to politics.

"But it’s a serious thing too."

During the conference, Sir Ed joined campaigners for a beach clean on Brighton seafront and handed out donuts to carers on Brighton Pier.
