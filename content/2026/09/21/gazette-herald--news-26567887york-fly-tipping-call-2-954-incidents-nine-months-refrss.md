ASBESTOS left dumped in a York village road for months before being removed has been among fly-tipping incidents which have prompted calls for action.

The waste, which was found dumped in Wheldrake in May and was removed this month, was among the instances of fly-tipping locally raised by councillors who were discussing the issue.

Cllr Anne Hook, Liberal Democrat ward councillor for Rural West York, said inaction over the removal of the potentially hazardous waste was not good enough.

City of York Council's Labour environment spokesperson Cllr Jenny Kent said fly-tipping was deplorable but the rise in it was a national and not just a local issue.

It comes as councillors backed a call to do more to tackle fly-tipping and littering on Thursday, September 17.

The motion, from Westfield ward's Liberal Democrat councillor Andrew Waller, which passed with Labour amendments, stated money from a proposed visitor levy could be used to fund the efforts.

It noted efforts from the council's Neighbourhood Caretakers team which launched in September last year and extra cash being given for efforts to keep walking, wheeling and cycling routes clear.

Council data showed 2,807 incidents of fly-tipping had been recorded in the first nine months of 2025/6, compared to 2,954 during the whole of the previous year.

Figures also showed only around half, 50.8 per cent, of all street cleaning incidents were resolved within the council's three-day target response time.

Liberal Democrat Cllr Hook said farmers and other land owners had been left having to deal with waste dumped in country roads because of how long it took to remove it.

The Rural West York ward councillor said: “It took until a few days ago for asbestos dumped in Wheldrake to be cleared, a whole summer of inaction simply isn't good enough.

“Residents shouldn't be left with potentially hazardous waste sitting in their communities for weeks while they wait for the council to act.”

Westfield ward's Cllr Waller said initiatives such as community skips or tidy-up vans could help tackle the problem.

![]()Asbestos was discovered dumped by the roadside in Wheldrake in May *(Image: Richard Atkinson)*

The Liberal Democrat said: “Many residents are trying to do their bit but many others have opted out because they feel unsupported.

“With council tax having risen so much people are looking for visible returns.

“I don't want residents to feel that they live in a part of York that's been left behind.”

Cllr Dave Merrett, the council's Labour housing spokesperson, said community skips had previously been used.

But he added issues including waste being left by full skips or at collection points when skips were not there had emerged.

Labour environment executive member Cllr Kent said York was doing better with fly-tipping compared to other similar nearby authorities, where around 6,500 incidents were recorded in 2024/5.

The executive member said: “Fly-tipping is a real concern and we all deplore the effect it has on our communities, including in rural communities and on our city's streets.

“This isn't just a local issue, fly-tipping rose by 9 per cent nationally in 2024/5, we don't exist in a bubble.

“We need to address the rise in antisocial behaviour where a small but growing minority of people are happy to drop rubbish yards from a bin.”
