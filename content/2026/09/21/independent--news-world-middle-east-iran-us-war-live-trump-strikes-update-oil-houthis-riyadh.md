Donald Trump has threatened to “blow the entire nation up” in another bombastic rant against the Iranian regime, amid concerns the US is planning to scale up its war in the region.

Speaking to Fox’s Trey Yingst, the US president said the “question” was “if and when” it would be required to “blow the entire nation up” if Tehran failed to agree to his demands.

“They better behave,” Yingst said the president told him of Iran’s government, adding that the choices included “wiping them out, letting Iran rot economically, or making a deal”.

It comes after Tehran warned the US and Middle East regions they will be targeted by “painful strikes” if they make “any mistake” against Iran.

It comes as the US State Department issued a security alert warning Americans in the Middle East that hostilities between Saudi Arabia and the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen had the “potential to escalate rapidly”.

At least 11 US embassies in the region issued a warning that travel disruptions are possible.

## Trump administration prepares sanctions against International Criminal Court, report says

The Trump administration has prepared sanctions against the entire International Criminal Court and plans to announce them soon, Reuters reported, quoting two sources familiar with the matter.

Both Reuters and the Wall Street Journal reported the decision could be finalised as soon as this week, during the UN General Assembly meeting in New York.

Washington has already sanctioned a number of ICC judges and prosecutors. But entity-wide sanctions would prohibit American citizens and companies from providing funds, goods or services to the court without a licence from the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control, limiting operations including hiring investigators and paying dozens of American employees.

Secretary of state Marco Rubio announced a campaign in July to isolate the court, calling on other countries to withdraw from it. Mr Trump's opposition to the court dates back to his first term and resurfaced after the ICC issued an arrest warrant for Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu in November 2024.

France will summon Iran's ambassador and take ⁠appropriate measures in response after Iranian authorities sealed a French language centre affiliated with its embassy in Tehran, the French foreign ministry has said.

"This new attack against France’s cultural presence in Iran, following the assault on two staff members of the French embassy last July, is unjustifiable and unacceptable," ⁠spokesperson Pascal Confavreux said in a ​statement yesterday.

France has previously dismissed such allegations, saying there had been a pattern of intimidation in recent months as Paris sought to increase its support for, and contacts with, Iran's civil society following a crackdown on protests at the start of this year.

## Iranian state actors targeting UK dissidents, activists and journalists with spyware

British intelligence has issued a warning after finding Iranian spies have targeted dissidents with spyware that can enable tracking of their movements.

Dissidents, activists and journalists were targeted by state actors that impersonated their contacts on messaging apps to trick them into downloading spyware called Chosen Brick, the National Cyber Security Centre, part of GCHQ, warned.

The malware gave agents access to the person’s contacts, emails and social media messages as well as a device’s microphone and content on the screen.

## Arab and Muslim nations condemn Houthi attack on Riyadh

Egypt, Jordan and Kuwait said the Houthi attack on Riyadh was a threat to regional security and stability and a violation of international law.

The nations in separate statements said their governments stand with Saudi Arabia in support of its sovereignty and protection of its civilians.

The Muslim World League also condemned the attack on civilians and civilian infrastructure as a violation of “all religious values as well as international and humanitarian laws and norms.”

It was the first time the Saudi capital has been targeted since the escalation in fighting with the Iran-backed rebels opened a new front in the Iran war.

Saudi Arabia said the attack was intercepted.

Some residents in Riyadh heard an explosion, and later some saw a plume of smoke near the airport.

There were no reports of casualties or damage.

## US Central Command estimates Iran war cost at $43.6 billion

US Central Command has said the cost of the war with Iran reached about $43.6 billion as of 3 September, according to a new assessment submitted to members of Congress and reported by Associated Press.

The estimate represents the latest assessment of the costs of the conflict.

According to the report, the cost of weapons and munitions used by US forces increased by more than $6 billion in just over a month, reaching about $28.1 billion, compared with $21.7 billion in a previous estimate.

The reported costs include the operation of warships, aircraft and military bases, as well as equipment losses and medical support for US forces.

It does not include damage to US bases in the Middle East from Iranian ballistic missile and drone attacks.
