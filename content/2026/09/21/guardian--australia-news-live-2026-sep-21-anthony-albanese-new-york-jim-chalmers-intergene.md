Australia news live: ACT offers $500,000 reward for unsolved murders including possible Ivan Milat victim; Crisafulli says plans to reduce visas are ‘nuts’

ACT offers $500,000 reward to help solve murder cases including possible Ivan Milat victim

The ACT government will offer a reward of $500,000 for information that helps conclude a series of unsolved murder or missing person cases, including the death of Keren Rowland, a 20-year-old nurse killed in 1971 whose family believe she was the first victim of serial killer Ivan Milat.

The government said the rewards will apply to five new cases in addition to three others that have had rewards attached to them since 2012. Officials said while some of the cases relate to long-term missing persons, they hope the rewards will encourage a witness or someone with information to come forward.

The other cases include those of Elizabeth Herfort, who was last seen in 1980; Megan Louise Mulquiney, last seen in 1984; Troy Hugh Forsyth, who was fatally struck by a van in 1987; and David Abuoi, who was last seen in Canberra in 2012.

The ACT minister for police, Marisa Paterson, said in a statement:

The families of these five people have never given up hope of getting answers regarding what happened to their loved ones. Half a million dollars is a strong incentive for someone to tell police what they know.

If you know anything about Keren, Troy, David, Elizabeth or Megan’s matters, we are urging you to come forward.

Asic begins proceedings against former CEO of company behind Supercheap Auto, Rebel and Macpac

The Australian Securities and Investments Commission (Asic) has begun civil penalty proceedings against the former CEO and managing director of the Super Retail Group, alleging he breached his duties and provided misleading information to the board and the market.

Asic alleges Anthony Heraghty failed to disclose conflicts of interest that arose from an alleged undisclosed relationship with a senior executive at the company. Those failures, Asic says, included continuing to supervise the person and making decisions affecting their employment, including remuneration, rewards and a redundancy package.

Super Retal Group is an ASX-listed public company and the parent company of a group that has retail interest including Supercheap Auto, BCF, Rebel and Macpac. Heraghty was the CEO from March 2019 to September 2025.

Sarah Court, the chair of Asic, said in a statement:

The allegations in this matter raise important issues about governance, transparency and trust in the information provided to boards and the market.

This case is not about private relationships, but whether a director properly disclosed and managed conflicts of interest and met their duties to the company and shareholders.

Asic is seeking declarations of contravention, pecuniary penalties and disqualification orders. Heraghty has not yet filed a defence, according to The Sydney Morning Herald.

Chalmers says AI will bring ‘the biggest economic transformation of our lifetime’

Achol Arok

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, said artificial intelligence will play “a pivotal role” in driving Australia’s long-term productivity growth.

The AI revolution is the most consequential new emphasis since the 2023 report and the biggest economic transformation of our lifetime. The productivity, investment and labour market impacts of AI will be dramatic.

The IGR shows from 2030 onwards each extra 0.1% of productivity growth from AI could improve the bottom line by 0.7 percentage points and reduce gross debt by 0.3 percentage points within a decade.

Chalmers said Australia needs to be focused and deliberate in adopting AI to realise its benefits.

We are well positioned with Australians adopting AI at a higher rate than almost every G20 country, but the productivity boost from AI depends on translating that into better ways of working.

Treasurer Jim Chalmers. Photograph: Hilary Wardaugh/AAP

More from the treasurer’s response to intergenerational report

Jim Chalmers outlined the five major challenges identified in the intergenerational report:

The impact of the “AI revolution” on the economy

A “fragmenting world” shaped by growing geopolitical tensions

The transition to clean energy

A growing and ageing population, accelerated by falling fertility rates

Changes to the industrial sector, driven by AI and the energy transition

Chalmers said the challenges needed to be addressed through a holistic approach:

There’s no separating these five accelerating shifts from each other or from their consequences for our budget or our economy.

A smaller workforce means a proportionately smaller income tax base at a time when pressures on social services and tools of statecraft, including defence, will be growing, not easing, along with the fiscal risks of climate change and more frequent disasters.

Chalmers said it was “remarkable” that the budget was strengthening despite those pressures, with government spending over the next 40 years expected to increase by significantly less than previously forecast.

A jumping castle that became airborne due to a freak “dust devil”, killing six primary schoolchildren, was tethered at only four of eight anchorage points, an inquest has been told.

The children died during end-of-year celebrations at Hillcrest primary school in Devonport on 16 December 2021.

A long-awaited inquest began in Tasmania on Monday after years of delays due to criminal proceedings against the castle’s operator, Rosemary Gamble. Gamble operated Taz-Zorb, which set up inflatable equipment at the school. She was found not guilty of criminal workplace health and safety breaches in 2025.

The events of the day and the tethering of the castle and other devices would be placed under “extensive examination”, counsel assisting the coroner Maree Norton said on Monday.

Gamble was supervising on the day and had a policy of not operating the castle if winds were tipped to exceed 25km/h. The Bureau of Meteorology forecast for 16 December was light winds, reaching 15 to 20km/h by the evening, Norton said.

Several witnesses, including teachers and nearby residents, said the morning was largely windless before a “mini-tornado” hit around 10am.

In 2025, the magistrates court found Gamble could have taken further steps in relation to the castle’s anchorage, but even if she had, it would have made no difference to the outcome. The coroner can make different findings but cannot make rulings of criminal guilt.

Chalmers says intergenerational report warns of ‘very serious risks and pressures’

Achol Arok

The treasurer, Jim Chalmers, has delivered the Treasury’s latest intergenerational report, examining the potential impact of government policies over the next 40 years.

Speaking in Canberra, Chalmers said the report highlighted the challenges facing Australians in a “more fragmented and uncertain world”.

The world is becoming more dangerous, more unpredictable, more unequal, and more divided. And these aren’t just individual threads, they’re part of a bigger fraying of that intergenerational promise of better times.

Accelerating change is putting more pressure on people. It’s eroding trust in the institutions of democracy. It exacerbates the strain felt by younger Australians and younger generations in particular.

Chalmers said the report identified that Australia faces serious challenges but said the country is well prepared to manage them.

This intergenerational report shows that we face very serious risks and pressures, but that we are better placed and better prepared for them … this report also shows that we have a better plan to maximise our opportunities and minimise our risks, to grow our economy and lift living standards, embrace new technologies and share the benefits.

‘That’s nuts’: Queensland premier slams plans to slash visa numbers

Andrew Messenger

Queensland’s premier says bipartisan plans to reduce the number of foreign visa holders allowed into the country is “nuts” and could risk spiralling food prices and damage to regional communities.

Labor, the Liberals and One Nation have all recently released plans to cap or slash migration. Last week the home affairs minister, Tony Burke, released a plan to slash net overseas migration from 292,000 this year to just 225,000 in 2027-28.

“I think that’s nuts,” David Crisafulli said on Monday, referring to the idea of reducing visa numbers year on year. He said:

Without it [migration], you go into a country town during a fruit picking season, for example, and without those short-term visas, you won’t get a beer at a pub, and a farmer won’t be able to pick his or her produce, and those towns grind to a halt.

But if you don’t love those little towns as much as I do, see it through a self-interest point of view. The things you put in your trolley are going to be a hell of a lot more expensive if there’s not a sensible discussion around making sure that people can go into regional areas and do the kind of work that they have done for a long period of time.

Crisafulli said the country needed a discussion as much about welcoming new Australians who wanted to build the country of the future as about how best to accommodate them.

Four Australian sea lions test positive for H5 bird flu in South Australia

Four more endangered Australian sea lions have tested positive for the deadly H5N1 bird flu at an important breeding site in South Australia.

The animals, including one pup, were among several found dead on the Pages islands near Kangaroo Island, state authorities said on Monday.

SA recorded the first H5 death of a sea lion eight days ago, with fears about how deadly virus will impact populations of the endangered pinniped.

Experts estimate that about 12,000 Australian sea lions remain, most of which are found in SA. The Pages islands are one of three sites in the state with large breeding colonies, according to Parks SA.

Authorities identified three positive sea lions on North Page Island and one on South Page Island via aerial wildlife surveillance operations. Several dead pups were also found, but tested negative for H5 bird flu.

Invasive Species Council chief executive Jack Gough said:

We haven’t seen the mass deaths in pinnipeds that the experience in South America and Antarctica suggests may be coming but multiple deaths in a single location could indicate that devastation is just around the corner.

We’re now about to see millions of migratory birds arriving in Australia as part of spring migration as well as breeding events around the country which could be a new phase in terms of the level of devastation.

WA government calls off shark catch-and-kill directive

The WA government has called off the search for a shark that killed a Perth man on Friday, after a minister had ordered a catch-and-kill directive.

WA police said a 63-year-old man was bitten by a shark while swimming at Sorrento beach. The swimmer’s body has not been found.

In a statement, the Department of Primary Industries said it had concluded water and land based patrols around Sorrento beach.

There was no evidence of white shark activity in the area yesterday and a significantly diminished chance of catching the individual shark involved in the fatal incident on Friday.

Beaches in the City of Joondalup from Marmion to Iluka were set to reopen at 10am local time.

On Saturday the WA fisheries minister, Jackie Jarvis, revealed she had greenlit the order. She said in a social media post:

Given the shark had been positively identified, remained in the vicinity following a fatal attack and was considered to pose an ongoing threat, I authorised fisheries officers to destroy the shark if captured.

Australian households are expected to shrink but more people can expect to live well into their 90s, according to the latest update to forecasts of the country’s long-term future.

Treasury’s latest intergenerational report, to be published in full on Monday, will project the impact of government policies 40 years into the future.

An extract released on Sunday showed life expectancy at birth was forecast to reach almost 90 for women by 2065-66, up from about 86 years at present.

The report will also reveal that artificial intelligence (AI) will become the “defining influence” on the economy over the next four decades, as the treasurer, Jim Chalmers, warned it would come with “very substantial risks”.

Australian diplomats to meet Palestinian counterparts in coming months

Tom McIlroy

Foreign minister Penny Wong has spoken with her Palestinian counterpart, Varsen Aghabekian. The pair agreed that senior officials from both governments should meet in coming months to strengthen bilateral relations and discuss key priorities.

The pair spoke on Friday night, with Aghabekian briefing Wong on progress on the Palestinian Authority’s reform commitments.

“Australia is committed to a two-state solution,” Wong wrote on social media on Monday.

I spoke with Palestinian Foreign Minister, Dr Varsen Aghabekian, about the PA’s reform agenda and our joint efforts to strengthen Palestine’s institutional foundations.

We also discussed the devastating situation in Gaza. “I reiterated that Hamas must play no role in the State of Palestine.

The pair agreed that Israel must halt its illegal settlement activity and hold perpetrators of settler violence to account.

Wong also told Aghabekian that Australia continues to press all parties to implement the Gaza peace plan in full and facilitate unimpeded humanitarian access to Gaza.
