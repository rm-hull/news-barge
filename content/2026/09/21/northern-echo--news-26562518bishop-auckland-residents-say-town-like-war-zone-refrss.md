*“You can’t have a pretty bus station if the place it leads to looks like it’s been a war zone.”*

That is the stark message of fed-up Bishop Auckland residents who say break-ins, vandalism, nuisance bikes and litter louts are plaguing their town.

And despite millions of pounds being poured into regenerating the area residents have described feeling unsafe in a "deteriorated" town with many fearful of even reporting crime.

Smashed windows, shuttered shops and empty units have laid bare their concerns with frustrated residents at breaking point.

![]()Graffiti and discarded waste pictured in a side street in Bishop Auckland town centre. *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

One of those calling for change is Emily Blackmore, 23, who has founded the Bishop Auckland Community Action Group (BACAG) with more than 130 members.

“Over the past decade, we have watched the decline of our high street. Bishop Auckland was once viewed as a historic landmark that attracted visitors from across the North East," she said.

"Today, many residents feel that our town centre has been allowed to deteriorate into a place where crime, vandalism and urban decay have become increasingly visible.

“We have raised serious concerns about antisocial behaviour and urban decay within the town centre itself. It is not unusual to see illegal e-bikes and motorbikes travelling at high speeds around Newgate Street, creating a danger to pedestrians. There have also been multiple incidents involving shops being damaged or broken into over recent months.”

Barriers were erected outside the shop and the footpath was partially closed off to pedestrians.

And just last week the the Grade II listed St Peter's Church in Bishop Auckland caught fire sparking concerns from residents.

But despite the issues, Bishop Auckland has had millions of pounds pumped into the town and just this year made a bid to become the UK’s first Town of Culture in 2028.

However, Ms Blackmore says the bricks and mortar only tell half the story, claiming litter, danger drivers, theft and antisocial behaviour need tackling as a priority.

She said: "The recent rise in crime has sparked outrage within our community. Rampant vandalism, antisocial behaviour and the lack of support available to our town are reaching a crisis point, and people are tired of feeling like their concerns are being ignored.

"You can't have a pretty bus stop if the place that it leads to looks like it has been in a war. "

The group is campaigning for the council to install CCTV along Newgate Street and improve security measures, not only to protect residents but also to support the businesses that are keeping the town centre alive.

At a Bishop Auckland PACT meeting on Thursday (September 16), residents pressed police over antisocial behaviour, reporting and the visibility of officers on the street.

One BACAG member told the group she was fearful what could happen if she reported incidents, even citing that her children had begged her not to.

![]()A shattered shop window in Bishop Auckland town centre. *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

However, Durham Police said reports from the public are vital in identifying patterns and deciding where resources are needed and encouraged witnesses to come forward.

Neighbourhood Inspector Mark Edwards said officers regularly carry out high-visibility patrols and work with residents and businesses in the town centre.

He urged people not to assume somebody else had already reported an incident, adding: “Make the call and help us tackle the issues where you live.”

![]() *(Image: Supplied)*

Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen has also addressed the concerns, saying recent incidents have had a “real impact on community confidence.”

She has commissioned a review of CCTV provision across the force area – one of BACAG’s biggest concerns – while stressing that reports from residents help police identify patterns and target resources.

Making Bishop Auckland safer is only one part of the group’s ambitions though, with action on empty and deteriorating buildings, litter and the long-term future of the high street also demanded.

Durham County Council said in a bid to tackle antisocial behaviour neighbourhood wardens regularly patrol the town, its Clean and Green team tackles environmental problems and partner agencies work together to address bad behaviour.

Economic development manager Graham Wood said the council was working to ensure Bishop Auckland remained a “safe and attractive place to live and visit.”
