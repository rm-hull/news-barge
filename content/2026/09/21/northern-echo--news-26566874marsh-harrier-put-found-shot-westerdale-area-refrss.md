A protected bird of prey found seriously injured on a North Yorkshire moor was put down after experts discovered it had been shot.

North Yorkshire Police confirmed a juvenile marsh harrier was humanely euthanised after the young bird of prey was found in the Westerdale area on August 28, 2026, suffering from injuries that left it unable to fly.

Following an examination, it was established the marsh harrier had been shot and is believed to have been lying injured for several days before it was discovered.

Police said the bird's injuries were so severe there was no prospect of recovery and, on welfare grounds, it had to be put down.

The marsh harrier is a specially protected bird of prey under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981.

A spokesperson for North Yorkshire Police said: "It is an offence to intentionally kill, injure or disturb these birds, or to damage or destroy their nests or eggs."

Police are now appealing for information in connection with the incident.

A spokesperson added: "Anyone who may have witnessed suspicious activity in the area, or who has information that could assist the investigation, is asked to contact North Yorkshire Police on 101.

"If you have concerns regarding the persecution of birds of prey or any other wildlife crime, please report the matter to the police. Information from the public can play a vital role in helping to protect wildlife and bring offenders to justice."

Anyone with information can also email [kimberley.laws@northyorkshire.police.uk](mailto:kimberley.laws@northyorkshire.police.uk), quoting incident number 12260171320.

The RSPCA recorded 18 reports of wild-bird cruelty in North Yorkshire in 2025, the county's seventh-highest total nationally.
