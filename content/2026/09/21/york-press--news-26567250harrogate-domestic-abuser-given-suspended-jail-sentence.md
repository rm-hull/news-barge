A man treated his partner in a “medieval way” that led to her losing weight and becoming mentally ill, York Crown Court heard.

Victoria Ball, prosecuting, said Andrew Stenson, 31, strangled the woman, leaving marks on her neck at the end of a series of incidents in which he repeatedly assaulted her.

The woman tried to calm Stenson down when he was violent towards her but eventually reported him to police and left him.

They had been in a relationship for three years.

She told the court: “Throughout the relationship, my mental and physical health deteriorated. I was frequently ill, lost weight, and became uncharacteristically anxious over small things. I felt isolated, and ashamed that I could not protect myself.”

Her health had improved after leaving the relationship.

After hearing details of the incidents, Judge Simon Hickey told Stenson: “You seem to have treated that lady as a shackle. I thought that kind of behaviour went out in the Middle Ages.”

He also told Stenson the woman had shown great forgiveness towards him and wanted him to “recognise what he is like and not to behave like that to a future young woman.”

He decided not to send Stenson immediately to jail after the woman told him she thought Stenson needed counselling and training in how to conduct relationships properly.

Instead he suspended the 21-month prison sentence for 18 months on condition Stenson does 15 days’ rehabilitative activities, complete a probation rehabilitation programme of 26 sessions and take mental health treatment.

He also banned Stenson from contacting the woman, going near her place of work and making social posts about her for 15 years.

Stenson, of Ribbledale Road, Killinghall, pleaded guilty to one charge of strangulation and three of assault on the day he was due to stand trial.

For Stenson, Rachel Webster said he had no previous convictions and had held down jobs that required responsibility.

He had also served his country for 13 years until he was discharged on medical grounds.

He had been diagnosed with post-traumatic stress disorder, depression and anxiety.

“He accepts he needs help with his mental health and rehabilitation,” said Ms Webster.

Det Con Kirsty Greenbank, of North Yorkshire Police, said: “The victim suffered nightmares, weight loss and anxiety, all as a result of Stenson's treatment of her. Throughout the investigation he had ample opportunity to admit what he had done - but instead he lied again and again.

“Thankfully, the victim displayed extraordinary courage to pursue justice, and ensure he could not treat anyone else in the same way.

“I hope this case shows to any other victims of domestic violence: you are not alone, and the police are here to help. Talk to us, and we will work tirelessly to support and protect you.”
