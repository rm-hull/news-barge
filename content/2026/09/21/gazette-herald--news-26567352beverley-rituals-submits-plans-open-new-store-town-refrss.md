A LUXURY home and cosmetics brand could be set to open a new branch in the centre of an East Yorkshire town.

Rituals Cosmetics UK Limited has submitted planning applications to East Riding of Yorkshire Council for redecoration and advertising displays in Toll Gavel in Beverley, the site of a TG Jones and former WHSmith bookshop.

The cosmetics brand has stores in Harrogate and Leeds.




A heritage, design, and access statement submitted with the application outlines the proposed works to the mid-terrace retail unit, which is situated within the pedestrianised shopping area of the town and close to Saturday Market.

The area has recently seen applications submitted to open a new bank branch and a food and drinks destination.

The Rituals planning application details proposed alterations to the existing shopfront, including redecoration and new fascia and blade sign.

All signage would be non-illuminated and feature the company's brand logo.

The plans note that the shopfront is not listed but is adjacent to the Grade II listed buildings at numbers 10 and 12 Toll Gavel.

The proposed changes aim to have a positive impact on the surrounding conservation area and be in keeping with the character of the adjacent buildings, the plans said.

Internally, the ground floor sales area will be fitted out and alterations made to the ancillary and staff accommodation at the rear.

Rituals-branded door handles will be added, and the existing shopfront, entrance doors, and roller shutter will be redecorated.

Rituals Cosmetics, a Dutch luxury wellbeing and beauty brand, was founded in Amsterdam in 2000 by entrepreneur Raymond Cloosterman.

The company aims include transforming everyday routines into meaningful moments inspired by ancient traditions and Eastern philosophies.

Its range includes body care products, skincare, perfumes, home fragrances, candles, and gift sets.

Rituals operates in 33 countries, with more than 1,400 stores worldwide and thousands of additional shop-in-shop locations.

High street retailer TG Jones is closing stores across the UK as part of a major restructuring programme aimed at securing the chain’s long-term future.

The business, which took over WHSmith’s former high street estate in 2025, adopted a turnaround plan allowing up to 150 stores to close from a network of around 450 branches.

The TG Jones branding to replace WHSmith signage at the Beverley store was approved by East Riding of Yorkshire Council in July last year.
