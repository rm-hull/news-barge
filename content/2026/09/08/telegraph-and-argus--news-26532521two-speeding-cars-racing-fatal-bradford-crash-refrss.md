A jury has heard how two speeding cars were allegedly racing each other moments before an almost head-on collision on a Bradford road that left one young driver with fatal injuries.

Husayn Ali was pronounced dead around three hours after his Skoda Fabia crossed into the opposite carriageway of Bolton Road and crashed into a Volkswagen Golf shortly before 9pm on March 7, 2024.

Two friends who were passengers in his car were both seriously injured.

Bradford Crown Court heard that 21-year-old Mr Ali, from Shipley, was racing his friend, Mohammed Awais Iqbal, who was at the wheel of a Seat Leon immediately behind him.

Both cars were travelling at around twice the road’s 30mph speed limit.

Iqbal, now 24, of Beechwood Drive, Wibsey, denies causing death by dangerous driving, dangerous driving, and two counts of causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

Prosecutor Gerald Hendron opened the case against Iqbal at the start of his trial on Tuesday (September 8).

He told the jury: “It is the prosecution’s case that the defendant and the deceased were racing or driving competitively with each other.

“The defendant was driving to keep up with the deceased, and the deceased was driving quickly shortly before the collision.

“The prosecution say that the defendant was driving far too close to the deceased and would, by his presence, likely to be pressuring the deceased to accelerate or at least maintain his speed as opposed to braking.”

Mr Hendron added that the hazard lights on Iqbal’s Seat Leon had been modified to flash out of sequence when they were activated.

In particular, he said, the car’s indicator lights and dipped front headlights flashed on alternative sides of the car as opposed to dipped headlights on together and indicator lights flashing together.

CCTV footage screened in court appeared to show Iqbal’s car “blinking” its headlights intermittently as it drove down Bolton Road towards Bradford.

Mr Hendron said: “The prosecution say that was an indication for the car ahead [Mr Ali’s Skoda Fabia] to go.”

The jury heard how Mr Ali’s Skoda and Iqbal’s Seat both accelerated.

However, the Skoda failed to negotiate a left-hand bend and deviated into the oncoming lane where it collided with the VW Golf driven by a man observing the 30mph limit.

The “almost head-on” collision was captured on CCTV.

Said Mr Hendron: “The impact between the two cars was profound, causing the deceased’s car to lift off the ground and turn [in the air] before coming to a stop.”

Iqbal, travelling behind, narrowly avoided the two colliding cars and pulled up.

He and his passengers ran over to their friends’ car.

Husayn Ali, who had not been wearing a seatbelt, was unresponsive and slumped over in the driver’s seat.

His front seat passenger managed to crawl out of the car [onto the ground].

The rear seat passenger had to be helped out.

The driver of the Golf blacked out and had to be cut out of his car by emergency services.

“Miraculously,” said Mr Hendron, “he did not suffer serious injuries.”

Husayn Ali was treated with CPR at the roadside and on the way to Leeds General Infirmary by paramedics, but he could not be revived at hospital and was pronounced dead around three hours later at 12.05am on March 8.

Mr Ali’s front seat passenger suffered a broken femur and a fracture to his chest.

The rear seat passenger suffered a broken sternum and a broken right elbow as well as cuts and bruises.

The jury heard that Iqbal and Mr Ali had met up following a 22nd birthday meal for one of their friends.

The crash occurred around three to four minutes after they, with a third car containing other friends, had set off for Leeds Road to get dessert.

An expert determined that Mr Ali’s car was travelling at 65mph before impact, with Iqbal following at 60mph – more than twice the road’s 30mph limit.

An accident investigation examiner established that the maximum speed that the bend could safely be travelled at by a vehicle was 55mph if travelling in the middle of the lane of Bolton Road.

Mr Hendron told the court: “It is the prosecution’s case that the speed of not just the deceased’s vehicle but the defendant’s vehicle travelling down Bolton Road were contributory factors in the accident.”

In an interview with police Iqbal denied that he was involved in racing or reckless behaviour or encouraging anyone else to do it prior to the accident.

In a pre-prepared statement, he said he had seen Mr Ali’s car “swerving from side to side” and had put on his hazard lights to warn him how badly he was driving.

He said he was not doing it to encourage Mr Ali and denied that the incident was anything to do with him.

The trial continues.
