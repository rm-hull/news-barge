The design economy in Yorkshire and the Humber has grown by more than half since 2019.

New research from the Design Council shows the sector contributed £7.7 billion in gross value added (GVA) in 2023, accounting for 4.7 per cent of the region’s total economic output.

The Design Economy 2026 report, published every three years, highlights Yorkshire and the Humber’s 51 per cent growth in design GVA between 2019 and 2023, outpacing the UK average of 40 per cent.

Mat Hunter, chief executive of the Design Council, said: "These figures should change the way we think about design.

"It is not a niche creative activity sitting at the edge of the economy; it is a £136.7 billion capability embedded in the way organisations effectively innovate, tackle complex problems and grow."

In South Yorkshire, digital design rose by 26 per cent, while architecture and the built environment saw a 50 per cent increase between 2017 and 2024.

Craft employment in West Yorkshire grew by 175 per cent, and York and North Yorkshire recorded a 56 per cent rise in multidisciplinary design roles.

The Design Council argues that stronger design capability can help regions turn investment into higher-value jobs and long-term economic growth.

Levy, a leading hospitality business, has applied design thinking to its Biohub, a regenerative farming project in Yorkshire, to embed sustainability across its supply chain.

Rak Kalidas, chief creative officer at Levy, said: "Design is the invisible thread that runs through every facet of our business."

However, not all areas saw growth, with Hull and East Yorkshire experiencing a decline in design-intensive employment between 2017 and 2024.

The report also raised concerns about the national skills pipeline, pointing to a 68 per cent drop in Design and Technology GCSE entries over the decade to 2024.

Workforce diversity remains limited, with women making up 25 per cent of designers.
