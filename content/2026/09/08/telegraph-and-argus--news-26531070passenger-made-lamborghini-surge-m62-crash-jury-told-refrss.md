A Bradford woman has told jurors that her friend may have caused the Lamborghini she was driving to accelerate by tampering with its controls shortly before a deadly crash on the M62.

Humaira Batool is accused of causing the death of Mohammed Shaan Hussain, 20, through dangerous driving. She denies the charge at Leeds Crown Court.

Mr Hussain had hired the Lamborghini Urus involved in the collision, which struck a barrier near Ripponden and burst into flames in the early hours of December 21, 2022.

Batool, then 22, said Mr Hussain had been handling controls inside the vehicle in the period before the crash. She claimed this made the supercar "boost" in speed while she was driving.

Batool, of Smiddles Lane, Bankfoot, drove onto the M606 and subsequently the M62. During the journey, she said Mr Hussain told her: "Watch this, watch this".

Batool claimed he then "reached over" towards the controls. While she was unable to identify what he had touched, she said the Lamborghini began to "just surge".

She told the court she had been "not watching" the speedometer, as her attention was on the road ahead, but said she could "feel the speed increasing".

According to Batool, Mr Hussain "pressed the buttons multiple times".

The vehicle’s speed fluctuated during the journey and regularly exceeded 100mph, jurors heard. Batool denied deliberately driving at such speeds and said she had not done "anything different when the car surged".

A Lamborghini representative has previously told the jury that no feature on the Urus could produce that kind of acceleration, apart from a driver using their "right foot".

The vehicle came to rest shortly after passing the landmark "house in the middle of the motorway" at more than 120mph.

Batool escaped from the burning Lamborghini but suffered significant injuries, including burns to around 10% of her body and a shattered tibia.

## Prosecution challenge

Prosecutor Christopher Dunn has previously alleged that Batool drove at "grossly excessive speeds of up to 143mph [230 km/h], in wet conditions on the M62 motorway".

Questioning her on Monday, he accused her of lying to "avoid the consequences of [her] guilty actions".

Mr Dunn alleged Batool had been keen to see and drive the Lamborghini and was "putting it through its paces" on the night of the crash. He said: "The only thing that was making you go faster that night was your right foot".

The trial continues.
