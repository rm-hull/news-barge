Ukraine can buy Patriot air defence system parts from EU's support loan, von der Leyen says

The European Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen, has said that Ukraine will be able to buy crucial parts for Patriot air defense systems through the next €3.2bn (£2.7bn; $3.7bn) disbursement from the EU’s support loan.

“Together with Nato secretary general Rutte, I welcome the agreement by Member States on the derogation for Ukraine to purchase crucial products for Patriot air defence systems,” she said in a statement.

Ukraine heavily relies on advanced air defence systems, mainly US Patriot interceptors, to protect its skies from Russian ballistic missile attacks. But this year, supplies of Patriot missiles have run out, leaving Ukraine vulnerable to strikes.

After a brutal summer of deadly Russian missile and drone attacks, Ukraine last week requested an exemption from Buy European procurement rules to acquire US-made Patriot interceptors using the EU’s €90bn support loan.

Ukraine has been asking western allies for air defence equipment amid fears Russia will dramatically intensify its missile attacks this winter. My colleagues Jamie Grierson and Dan Sabbaghhave written a story about the UK committing to £100m to boost Ukraine’s air defences ahead of the anticipated attacks. We already mentioned the pledge in this post, but their story contains more detail. Here is an extract:

The air defence interceptors plus large-calibre artillery rounds and other drones will be supplied to Ukraine through a contribution to a Nato scheme to buy American weapons.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said he wants to obtain 300 Patriot missiles, costing about $3m (£2.6m) each, to help Kyiv protect its electricity, heating and water systems through the normally hard winter.

The UK did not say how many Patriots it would be able to buy, but the number is likely to be only a fraction of the number sought, though other western allies are also examining how they can help Kyiv defend itself.

40-year-old Russian man questioned over Romanian sabotage allegations

A 40-year-old Russian man suspected of being involved in the operation is being questioned by Romanian prosecutors on Tuesday, a statement from Romania’s Directorate for Investigating Organized Crime and Terrorism said.

Authorities are seeking court approval to detain him for 30 days pending inquiries into his encrypted communications, which reportedly included photos of several Romanian and multinational military bases.

Romanian intelligence service says it prevented Russian sabotage operation targeting defence, critical infrastructure

Romanian intelligence service said it “prevented the commission of a sabotage on national territory,” involving a Russian citizen in Romania and a number of defence and critical infrastructure targets.

In a statement, the SRI service said that the Russian man, who was being monitored as part of an extensive investigation, attempted to take photo and video documentation of “some communications centres and national military bases used by Nato allies,” as well as other defence institutions and critical infrastructure.

“The data obtained indicate a similar action pattern to that used on the territory of European states by networks of saboteurs coordinated by entities from the Russian Federation, including in the cases thwarted in Romania in 2024 and 2025: the Russian citizen was instructed by an intermediary to make photo-video materials with targets of strategic military interest, including a Ukrainian Antonov cargo aircraft during their presence on the national territory.”

It added that “the specific actions taken by the SRI, as well as the measures taken with the Romanian authorities and external partners, led to the prevention of an act of sabotage on national territory.”

The service did not offer more detail on whether it involved any arrests.

German police detain suspect after sabotage attacks on power grid

Elsewhere, we are getting a line from Germany that German police have detained a man suspected of carrying out a series of sabotage attacks on the country’s electricity grid, as reported by local media and Reuters.

A general view after a sabotage attempt which had been made to short-circuit a power grid installation in Dormagen, in the western state of North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. Photograph: Petra Wischgoll/Reuters

The suspect, identified as Daniel V. by German media, was detained in Weisweiler about 50 kilometres (30 miles) west of Cologne, where he allegedly carried out one of his attempted attacks, Reuters said.

German police have been searching for the 48-year-old since they obtained letters claiming responsibility for a series of sabotage attacks on the power grid, in which the suspect cited opposition to fossil fuels as a motive.

German news agency DPA said that the suspect’s “motive was reportedly climate-related, with the letters criticising electricity production from fossil fuels.”

EU backs Hungary's decision to expel 10 Russian diplomats

Separately, the commission was also asked about Hungary’s decision to expel 10 Russian diplomats this morning.

The commission’s foreign affairs spokesperson Christian Wigand said:

“We take note of Hungary’s legitimate decision.

This is an expected reaction [given] Russia’s persistent campaign in using hybrid attacks against the EU and its member states.

The European Union expresses its full solidarity with Hungary and reaffirms its support for the diplomatic and consular activities of all member states conducted in accordance with international law.

The European Union will continue to closely coordinate with Hungary and other member states, and remains committed to protecting the interests and the rights of EU citizens.”

Bosnia withdraws diplomatic staff from Serbia in protest at Mladić's 'hero' funeral

It’s worth noting that Bosnia and Herzegovina withdrew all staff from its Belgrade embassy yesterday, and transferred consular services to its embassies in Zagreb and Podgorica in protest against Mladić’s ‘hero’ funeral in Belgrade.

The ministry saidthe move intended to “reduce communication with Serbia to the lowest possible level,” while intensifying its dialogue about the situation with the EU, US, and the UK.

It’s also probably safe to say that the Bosnian foreign ministry was not impressed with the commission’s bland holding position yesterday.

Responding to questions from reporters yesterday, the EU’s lead spokesperson Paula Pinho insisted that “there is no place for glorification of war criminals,” but did not really engage with repeated questions about the apparent state and military honours involved.

In a late afternoon post directed at Pinho, the ministry said: “are you even aware of what you are saying?”

It continued:

“Five ministers from the Serbian Government were seated in the front row at the funeral, a convicted war criminal was given military honours, and his body was flown in on a Serbian Government aircraft.

What are you afraid of? Why are you unwilling to call this what it is?

Your position on the funeral of a convicted war criminal is a disgrace to the entire European Union!”

Difficult not to see a link between their furious reaction and this morning’s hasty statement from von der Leyen and Pinho’s notably different tone at today’s midday briefing.

She declined to say if von der Leyen would visit Belgrade on her annual trip to western Balkans, stressing the programme is still being worked on.
