A Bradford man who once rescued two women from a burning car in an act of great "courage" died years later after suffering from a 'flesh-eating disease', an inquest heard.

Angus Burrell, 54, died at Bradford Royal Infirmary on March 18, 2026, after developing necrotising fasciitis, a disease in which toxins from bacteria destroy tissue, in his lower right leg.

In a resumed inquest on Monday, assistant coroner Angela Brocklehurst concluded that his medical cause of death was necrotising fasciitis, multi-drug toxicity and intravenous drug use.

She concluded it was a "drug-related death".

Mr Burrell was born in Bradford on August 22, 1971. He was the youngest of four children and was unemployed when he died.

A statement from his sister, Fiona Hatfield, and his long-term former partner, Patricia Jackson, said he lost both parents when he was young.

He then moved to Dorset to live with his sister, but later returned to Bradford after struggling to settle.

The statement said the “trauma of losing both of his parents at a young age contributed to Angus taking drugs”.

His sisters visited him occasionally and said their “happy little brother was lost to drugs”.

However, Ms Hatfield and Ms Jackson described Mr Burrell as a “very caring, kind and loving man”. They said he loved his dogs and stray cats.

The inquest also heard about an incident in which Mr Burrell “courageously” rescued two women from a burning car.

Ms Brocklehurst noted that his actions had been reported in the Telegraph & Argus in June 2005.

Ms Brocklehurst said: “It was a brave act to do.”

The family also told the inquest that Mr Burrell, who lived in Allerton at the time, had been pressured to buy £15 worth of cannabis. The incident resulted in a conditional discharge from court.

His health had “deteriorated in recent years” and “we tried to get Angus off drugs but we were unsuccessful.”

A post-mortem examination found high levels of morphine and cocaine in Mr Burrell’s blood.

Dr Bennett said the drugs were the “most likely cause of his death”, which led to sepsis.

He reported that Mr Burrell had a severe soft-tissue infection caused by intravenous drug use. He also said: “This was not a death from natural causes”.

He identified three factors in the death: necrotising fasciitis, multi-drug toxicity and intravenous drug use.

A toxicology report by Dr Martin found that the morphine concentration was at a level that could “cause a fatality”. It also showed recent cocaine use, along with pregabalin and ketamine.

A GP report from Affinity Care in Shipley, where Mr Burrell had been registered since 2022, described a “long history of substance misuse”.

The report said he had first been diagnosed with drug dependency in 1993 and had used heroin, crack cocaine and cannabis.

In January 2021, he suffered a drug overdose and was diagnosed with Hepatitis C and deep vein thrombosis in his lower leg.

On February 22, 2026, an ambulance was called after Mr Burrell “could not walk because he couldn’t feel his legs”.

He was assessed at Bradford Royal Infirmary, where his “recreational drug use” was discovered.

On March 17, 2026, Yorkshire Ambulance Service was called to his flat on Brunswick Road, Bradford, after a report of lower limb pain.

The Yorkshire Ambulance Service arrived at the scene at 12.34am on March 18.

A paramedic said that the address was “uninhabitable” and “multiple obstacles obstructed” the route to Mr Burrell’s room.

Paramedics found him with swollen legs and several open wounds. He was also suffering from shortness of breath.

Mr Burrell was taken to hospital, where Dr Zakir Shariff, a consultant plastic surgeon, found swelling and discolouration on his lower right leg.

Dr Shariff said: “He was very unwell and needed treatment for severe sepsis.”

He added: “He had multiple ulcerated lesions and areas of purple tissue.”

Dr Shariff explained: “Necrotising fasciitis is commonly referred to as flesh-eating disease and is a severe bacterial infection that destroys skin. It is a life-threatening condition.”

Emergency surgery was scheduled for 6.25am to remove “necrotic and mottled tissue and dirty fluid”.

Mr Burrell later developed septic shock and multiple organ failure and was placed on life support.

He was then given end-of-life treatment and died at 9.09pm on March 18.

In her summing up, the Ms Brockehurst said Mr Burrell had sought help for his drug addiction but had also had a long-standing pattern of drug misuse.

She praised his “heroic rescue” and said he was loved by his family.

Ms Brocklehurst said: “Sadly Mr Burrell died from septic shock and multi-organ failure.”

“I conclude that this was a drug-related death.”

Addressing his family, she said: “Please accept my sympathies and condolences for your loss. I am sorry.”

She added: “Angus was loved and he had the courage to walk to burning car and rescue someone.”

The coroner said: “As so often is the case, drug use overrules everything and it comes to a stage where it is impossible to stop.”
