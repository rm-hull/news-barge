One of the district's high streets has been described as “revived” by business owners who say community spirit is driving a return to independence.

In Bingley, traders have praised a growing sense of pride and ownership among residents and visitors alike, crediting the town’s renewed energy to its supportive atmosphere and commitment to shopping locally.

### 'It's seen quite a revival'

Richard Hall, manager of Brick Banks Café, said: "A few years ago the high street was really quiet.

"Now it’s seen quite a revival.

"You can tell the local community is really involved, everyone seems really committed to getting people back into Bingley."

The café, which opened in June 2025, is run by Autism Bricks UK and offers a Lego-themed experience with sets available to build on-site.

It has drawn visitors from as far as New Zealand, as well as regulars from Skipton and Leeds.

Mr Hall said: "Lego appeals to everyone.

"We get a real community feel here, it’s a way for groups of all ages to sit down and connect."

The café is designed to be autism-friendly, with ear defenders, adjustable lighting and low music.

It also offers gluten-free and vegan menu options.

### 'Shopping here is cheaper than therapy'

Elsewhere on the high street, Hedgehog Organics has been trading since April 2022 and promotes environmental sustainability while sourcing products from nearby towns including Shipley, Wibsey and Silsden.

Owners Ben Oubridge and Maria Bija said: "We do what we can do to make people happier.

"Shopping here is cheaper than therapy, people like to have a chat."

Ms Bija said customers are often supportive, even when the shop is busy.

She said: "When we have a queue you expect people to complain, instead they say – oh it’s great to see you busy."

### 'A good group of people'

Mulligans of Bingley, a popular bar in the town, has also expanded recently.

Manager Abbie Turton said: "It’s been going really well, we have expanded, now we have a function room and a beer garden.

"We do food as well."

Ms Turton said the bar attracts a mix of ages and is known for its friendly atmosphere.

She said: "It’s a good group of people,

"We get more of young crowd on Saturday and then Sunday draws an older lot in."

  Louise Ainworth in Olive Lounge Travel 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

### 'Put things back into the community'

Louise Ainworth from Olive Lounge Travel, which opened in November 2024, also praised the town’s character.

She said: "Definitely, my job is a happy job which helps.

"Honestly everyone is so lovely here."

Ms Ainworth believes Bingley’s success comes from its self-sufficiency and focus on community.

She said: "They love their independence, people like to put things back into the community."

   

*(Image: Kenyon Opticians)*

Long-standing businesses share the same sentiment.

Liz Richmond, who has worked at Kenyon Opticians for a decade, said: "I love Bingley, I only live up the road.

"We get a lot of locals, but also people from out of Bingley."

She said: "We’re different to Specsavers because we’re an independent, so people who want that will come to us."
