# Britain is going to have to be selective with migrants - Yorkshire Post Letters

## From: John Riseley, Harcourt Drive, Harrogate.

Andy Burnham embraces the notion of “safe routes” to take away “the selling point of the gangs”. Is he saying that safe entry would be offered to all those who would otherwise come in small boats? Or is the point that inviting some people would free us emotionally to be less accommodating towards those who arrive uninvited?

The latter would represent a paradigm shift in our moral and legal framework regarding asylum. Traditionally, we accept for eventual settlement those who manage to reach here and who face a level of risk somewhat above what we regard as normal. The multitudes who couldn’t get here we ignored. This distinction has long been dubious, now it is untenable.

Softening that distinction, recognising it as a false dichotomy, would vastly increase the scope of our potential responsibility. But this would make it clear, to all except a lunatic fringe, that admission for settlement cannot be a right derived from facing a particular level of danger or persecution.

Instead, an annual quota would need to be set, notwithstanding that some people would be accepted and many rejected when we can hardly distinguish the gravity of their various plights. Selection would be discretionary rather than judicial. From what we know of abuse in other countries, I would expect most of those accepted to be women.

The size of the quota should be decided democratically. I would suggest a referendum or survey in which we each nominate a number between 0 and 9,999,999,999. We would then take the median value. That is to say the quota would be such that half the electorate wish it to be higher and the other half want it lower.

While we were about it, we could use the same method to set a cap on arrivals for employment and another on those coming for family reunion.

Those arriving in small boats would be outside of this quota and actively circumventing it. Rather than continuing the ineffectual struggle to stop them or deport them, we should confine them to basic, single sex, detention camps, without any assessment for asylum, until they agree to leave for their own or a third country. Any milder alternative must permanently deny them access to more than subsistence amounts of income.
