Months after Darlington Railway Station's multimillion-pound redevelopment opened, red barriers still block pavements, weeds and litter continue to build up, and temporary traffic lights are causing daily queues.

New pictures show little has changed at the station’s Neasham Road entrance, two months after officials promised to deal with the barriers and litter “as soon as possible”.

The eastern entrance opened in May as part of the £160m redevelopment, which added platforms, a station building, a 650-space car park and better links with the Victorian station.

However, residents say the approach does not match the modern new building and still looks more like “a building site”.

The Northern Echo first reported the concerns two months ago, when Tees Valley Combined Authority said it had raised them with its contractor.


Pictures taken this week show red barriers still blocking part of the Neasham Road pavement, while temporary lights restrict traffic to one lane and cause queues.

Other images show weeds around the fenced-off area and rubbish and debris beside the road and footpath.

Former Darlington MP Peter Gibson described the situation as a “disgrace”.

He said: “Darlington has had millions of pounds invested in upgrades to our train station and yet months on the surrounding area of the development is a disgrace.

“Our current MP and our council leadership seem oblivious to the problem and have made no effort to keep the community informed. It is just not good enough. Darlington deserves better.”

A resident, who did not want to be named, said: “The whole area just looks like a mess. There is a backlog of traffic every day due to the traffic lights, and you would expect the paths to be clear by now since the opening.

“There are weeds and rubbish piling up and it makes the area look neglected. You would not think all this money had been invested into the area the way it looks.”

TVCA said the highway works needed to complete the redevelopment are expected to start on the eastern side of the station in late September or early October, subject to Darlington Borough Council approval.

The work will cover Neasham Road between the redevelopment site and the mini roundabout linking Yarm Road and Parkgate.

The work will include resurfacing, drainage improvements, new pavements, pedestrian crossings, public-realm works and bus shelters.

Further work is planned for Victoria Road and Park Lane, on the station’s western side, with both schemes due to finish in late spring 2027.

A TVCA spokesperson warned: “The works on both sides of the station will cause disruption and longer journeys on routes into town and to the station. People should plan ahead and allow extra time.

“Subject to Darlington Borough Council approval, work on the Eastern Gateway is expected to start in late September or early October, followed by the West Gateway later in the autumn. Both sides are expected to be complete in late spring 2027.”

Darlington Borough Council leader Steve Harker said the authority was committed to completing the work, but the revised Eastern Gateway drainage plans still failed to meet the standards for technical approval.

He said technical approval was granted on April 17, but TVCA asked for changes to the drainage design four days later.

The council received revised proposals in early June, followed by another submission on August 21.

Mr Harker said: “We reject any suggestion that we are seeking to delay the project or move the goalposts.

“Our requirements have been consistent throughout and are focused entirely on ensuring that the scheme meets the technical and safety standards expected of works on the public highway.

“We fully support the completion of the station project and remain ready to work with TVCA to secure approval at the earliest opportunity.”

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said disruption was unavoidable but necessary to finish the project.

He said: “Whatever solution is going to be put in place is going to be a headache for local residents.
