A heritage railway has raised more than £400,000 for urgent bridge repairs that will help keep its historic trains running.

The North Yorkshire Moors Railway (NYMR) launched its Bridge 42 Appeal earlier this year to fund critical work on the bridge between Grosmont Level Crossing and the tunnel, aiming to raise £800,000 in total.

So far, more than half of that target has been reached, with money coming in from supporters around the world through donations and fundraising events.

Laura Strangeway, CEO at NYMR, said: "We are enormously grateful for the generous support we have received so far, including the grant awarded by the North York Moors National Park.

"But there is still more to do.

"Every donation takes us another step towards our goal and helps secure the future of the NYMR for generations to come."

The appeal has been boosted by a £50,000 grant from the North York Moors National Park’s S106 Landscape and Ecology Fund, as well as individual campaigns such as sponsored walks.

Donors giving £25 or more receive a limited-edition pin badge featuring artwork by Leeds-based artist David Cockayne.

Further fundraising will see staff and volunteers take on a 42-metre bungee jump from Larpool Viaduct in Whitby on September 20.

Ms Strangeway added: "The repair of Bridge 42 is a complex project for us and one that is incredibly important to complete so that the NYMR can continue to be the heritage railway that we all love."

Temporary propping has been installed at the bridge to allow trains to continue operating safely during the 2026 season.

Full structural restoration is planned for winter 2026/27, subject to securing the remaining funds.

The NYMR is a major heritage attraction, welcoming hundreds of thousands of visitors each year and supporting education, volunteering, and the regional economy.

To donate to the Bridge 42 appeal, visit: www.nymr.co.uk/bridge42
