A convicted paedophile from West Yorkshire who received a 10-year prison sentence in 2012 for child sex offences has been jailed again.

James S Hartley is now beginning a six-year sentence for historic sexual abuse involving a vulnerable child victim dating back to the 2000s.

A judge said his offending “formed a part of a wider and deeply concerning pattern of sexual misconduct extending over many years.”

Hartley, 63, formerly of Shaw Lodge, Halifax, and who appeared before Bradford Crown Court via video link from HMP Leeds, shook his head as the latest sentence was handed down.

Prosecutor Richard Walters said Hartley was convicted by a jury after a trial of multiple counts of sexual activity with a child. All involved the same victim.

Mr Walters said the victim, following the abuse carried out by Hartley, had been diagnosed with PTSD and anxiety, suffered from panic attacks and nightmares, struggled to go out, and could not have a normal life.

The court heard that Hartley had previous convictions for indecent assault in 2001, receiving a nine-month sentence of imprisonment, and for indecent assault and inciting a child to commit an act of gross indecency in 2012, for which he got ten years.

His Honour Judge Ahmed Nadim sentenced Hartley to six years’ imprisonment for each of the offences, all to run concurrent to one another.

He said: “Sexual offending against children frequently causes profound and enduring harm.

“In this case the victim’s personal statement demonstrates the reality of that harm.

“More than 20 years after these offences the complainant continues to suffer the consequences of your actions.”

He added: “He continues to struggle with the emotional effects of the abuse that you inflicted upon him.”

In mitigation, barrister Ian Howard said there had been long gaps in Hartley’s offending, with no reoffending since 2015.

He said Hartley had worked on being rehabilitated and had acted in a way “to ensure that this sort of thing does not happen again.”

Judge Nadim said Hartley was already subject to notification as a sex offender, and that that would continue for life.

He amended the term of an existing Sexual Harm Prevention Order, extending it to run for a further 20 years.

Detective Constable Carly Hemingway, of Calderdale Child Vulnerability Exploitation Team, said: “The complainant in this case has shown significant bravery in coming forward and supporting the investigation and prosecution, which has resulted in Hartley being jailed.

“The complainant has suffered significant trauma which has impacted on his life, and I hope this sentence has provided some comfort and helped towards closure.

“We encourage anyone who has experienced sexual abuse, whether recent or non-recent, to come forward and report it.

“As this case and many others demonstrates, the passage of time is not a barrier to investigating these offences.

“Victims will be supported, listened to, and treated with dignity throughout the process.”
