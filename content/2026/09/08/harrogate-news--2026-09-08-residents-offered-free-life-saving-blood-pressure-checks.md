**Potentially life-saving health checks** are being offered for free to communities across**North Yorkshire** after tens of thousands of people in the county are estimated to be undiagnosed with high blood pressure.

It is estimated that more than **50,000 adults** in North Yorkshire are living with high blood pressure without knowing it, based on data from 2024.

A blood pressure check can be the first step to preventing heart attacks and strokes. High blood pressure is a major cause of these diseases, but it usually has no symptoms until it is too late.

As part of Blood Pressure UK’s **Know Your Numbers Week**, which is taking place this week (from Monday, September 7 to Sunday, September 13),** North Yorkshire Council** is working with a range of local services to raise awareness and make blood pressure checks more accessible for residents.

## Free Monitoring at Local Libraries

Libraries across the county will loan a blood pressure monitor, which are available all year round to any of their members for free. If you are not a member, it is free and easy to sign up and then ask a member of staff for a blood pressure monitor.


**Cllr Michael Harrison, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for health and adult services, said:**

High blood pressure is known as the ‘silent killer’ – no symptoms, just risk. It can lead to heart attacks and strokes.

It can also cause kidney disease, dementia and other illnesses.

However, these tragedies can be prevented through awareness of high blood pressure, blood pressure checks and management.

I would encourage as many people as possible to take advantage of these free and life-saving convenient blood pressure checks. It only takes a few minutes and could make a significant difference to your health and wellbeing.



Library locations and opening times can be found at www.northyorks.gov.uk/local-libraries.

## Alison’s Story: The Importance of Checking

Meanwhile, the importance of regular blood pressure checks is highlighted by the experience of **Alison Marshall** from Selby.

During her first pregnancy in 2020, she developed a serious complication linked to high blood pressure at 36 weeks and required an emergency caesarean section after being admitted to hospital. Following the birth of her daughter, Mollie, Mrs Marshall’s blood pressure returned to normal and she focused on improving her health through weight loss and regular exercise.

However, despite feeling fit and having no symptoms, a routine GP appointment revealed that her blood pressure had become persistently high again, leading to ongoing treatment and monitoring.

As the community projects lead for the **Sherburn and Villages Community Trust**, she was keen to support the introduction of community blood pressure checks. When volunteers began offering checks at the Trust’s Cuppa Connect sessions, she decided to take part herself and the check revealed that her blood pressure was extremely high.

Prompted by the result, she contacted her GP and underwent further monitoring, which showed her medication was no longer working effectively. Following changes to her treatment and several months of follow-up care, her blood pressure is now well managed.

Alison Marshall said: “High blood pressure often has no obvious symptoms and can affect anyone, regardless of age or lifestyle. Inspired by the support and information I have received through local health initiatives, I have adopted healthier habits, including increasing my daily activity, reducing salt intake and switching to decaffeinated drinks. I am now a passionate advocate for community blood pressure checks and encourage others to take advantage of the free, accessible services available through Sherburn and Villages Community Trust, including checks offered at Cuppa Connect sessions and during Know Your Numbers Week events.”

## Where to Get Tested

- **Community Pharmacies:** Residents can access free blood pressure checks at locations including community pharmacies. More information is available at the NHS Pharmacy Finder.
- **Whitby Community Hospital:** Self-service blood pressure checks can be taken at the health kiosk in the urgent treatment centre reception area, seven days a week between 8 am and 8 pm.
- **Leisure Hubs:** To support Know Your Numbers Week, additional blood pressure checks are available at all of North Yorkshire Council’s**Active North Yorkshire** leisure and wellbeing hubs from September 7 to 13. (The only exception is the Selby Health and Wellbeing Hub, where a separate programme is being delivered through the Selby Healthy Hearts Community Champions project).

Centre locations and opening times can be found at www.northyorks.gov.uk/active-north-yorkshire/venues.

More details about Know Your Numbers Week can be found at Blood Pressure UK.
