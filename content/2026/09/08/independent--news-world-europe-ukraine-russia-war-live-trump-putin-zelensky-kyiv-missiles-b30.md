Russia has reportedly held secret war games in which their soldiers defeated Nato and seized a key Swedish island, according to a report.

Moscow’s general staff carried out several war simulations involving senior officers and as many as 1,000 participants over the past decades, according to Russian investigative outlet the *Dossier Centre*. 

The scenarios, which used software to model opposing forces, terrain and weapons, included the targeting of the Swedish island of Gotland and a fictional war with the US and Japan

Ukraine increasingly became a focus of the war games from 2021, ahead of the full-scale invasion in 2022.

Meanwhile, at least three people have been killed and 10 injured in a major Russian air atack on Kyiv, in which Volodymyr Zelensky said Moscow tried to “cause as much harm to life as possible” with “ballistic missiles, cruise and anti-ship missiles, and jet-powered ‘shaheds’”.

He added: “There is significant damage to residential buildings and other civilian infrastructure in the capital.”

It came just a day after US peace envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner held peace talks with Volodymyr Zelensky and left the Ukrainian capital.

## Editorial: Putin has no interest in peace – and Trump is falling into his trap

Steve Witkoff is the real estate guy with zero diplomatic experience who Donald Trump appointed in 2024 as his personal “peace emissary”. Jared Kushner is the president’s son-in-law, but did at least play some role in one of President Trump’s few foreign policy achievements – the Abraham Accords process that promised to deliver some stability to the Middle East during the first Trump term. As a team, they have enjoyed little success in securing the “settlement” to the war in Ukraine that Mr Trump so desires.

There is little reason to suppose that their latest exercise in shuttle diplomacy will succeed where their previous efforts failed. Indeed, the prospects for an end to the war, let alone one that will prove just and lasting, are as dismal as at any time since Vladimir Putin launched his ill-fated “special military operation” more than four and a half years ago – its duration now longer than the Soviet Union’s four-year “great patriotic war” against Adolf Hitler. Just as in that epoch-defining conflict, it is the civilian population of Ukraine that will face some of the harshest consequences as winter approaches.

Before the Witkoff-Kushner double act set out for Moscow and Kyiv, Mr Trump expressed his usual confidence in the pair – he could hardly do otherwise – and declared that “we have an idea for peace”. This idea remains opaque. Yet, for a change, this may be more than the usual self-flattering bluster. After a period when, easily bored, Mr Trump lost interest in the region (the situation in Iran didn’t help), this unexpected flexing of US power comes as a surprise.

On Sunday, the Alternative for Germany won 43.8 per cent of the vote in Saxony-Anhalt, the strongest result any far-right party has achieved in a German state election since the Second World War. It took 39 of the 83 seats, three short of an outright majority.

In Berlin, the result is being called an earthquake. In Kyiv, it is being read as a countdown.

None of this is a secret. It is a manifesto.

## Zelensky says US negotiators brought 'decent ideas' on ending war

Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelensky has said that US peace negotiators have presented several "decent ideas" to be discussed during talks on how to end the war.

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner visited both Russia and Ukraine over the weekend.

"As for the peace package that Kushner spoke of, and the new ideas – I can tell you that there were really some very decent, worthwhile ideas," Mr Zelensky told reporters in a WhatsApp media chat.

He added that the next trilateral meeting could be hosted by Turkey, the UAE, Switzerland, or other countries. He expected grain shipments and energy infrastructure to be discussed there, among other issues.

He said he hopes to meet Donald Trump later this month and plans to discuss a winter air defence package.

Russia has intensified its air attacks on Ukraine, with Kyiv facing a critical shortage of air defence supplies.

## Analysis | Why the AfD’s German triumph is a win for Putin – and leaves Ukraine exposed

The success of Germany’s far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) in state elections could herald a disaster for Ukraine, a European victory for Vladimir Putin, and a stampede of greed over democracy orchestrated by one man.

Kirill Dmitriev is to Donald Trump’s envoys, Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, what Putin is to the 47th president – their favourite and much favoured Russian.

What Putin gives Trump – the thrill of proximity to unlimited power – Dmitriev offers to America: unlimited potential wealth. He is the head of the Russian Direct Investment Fund, Moscow’s sovereign wealth government slush pot, and he has close links to the AfD.

There was unbridled delight in Moscow with the success of the AfD over the weekend.

Dmitriev called the result a win that “humiliates the arrogance, narrow-mindedness, warmongering, ineptitude and hypocrisy” of German chancellor Friedrich Merz’s governing coalition.

The AfD, seen by German intelligence as an extremist organisation, has enjoyed (among other things) Russian support for its social media campaigns to win the elections in Saxony-Anhalt, according to German and other European intelligence sources.

## Poland activates aircraft and suspends flights amid Russian missile attacks on Ukraine

Poland has launched preventive operations against Russian missile attacks on Ukraine, the Polish army has said.

The NATO and European Union member temporarily suspended flights at Lublin airport because of the military operations.

It has suffered repeated drone and plane incursions by Russia over the past year.
