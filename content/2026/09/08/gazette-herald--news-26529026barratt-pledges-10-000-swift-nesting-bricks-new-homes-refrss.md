A homebuilder in East Yorkshire is taking action to support wildlife by embedding nature into its developments.

Barratt and David Wilson Homes, part of Barratt Redrow, has pledged to install 10,000 swift nesting bricks in new homes by the end of 2027.

The company has already purchased more than 9,000 of the bricks since 2016, surpassing its previous target of 7,000 by 2025.

Daniel Smith, managing director at Barratt and David Wilson Homes Yorkshire East, said: "Swifts are an iconic part of the British summer, but their numbers have fallen dramatically in recent decades.

"By incorporating swift bricks into our homes, we can play a practical role in supporting their recovery while creating places where both people and nature can thrive."

Swifts have declined by two-thirds since 1995 and are now on the UK’s red list of conservation concern.

The decline is partly due to modern building methods, which limit nesting opportunities.

Barratt Redrow has worked with the RSPB and Manthorpe to design and manufacture swift bricks that integrate into building walls, offering safe nesting sites for swifts and other cavity-nesting birds.

The company has also collaborated with the RSPB to monitor the success of the bricks, finding that they can boost urban biodiversity.
