AN historic church is opening its doors for Heritage Open Days, offering a chance to explore centuries of spiritual history.

Bradford Unitarian Church on Russell Street, which dates back to the English Dissenting tradition of 1662, will be open to visitors on Friday, September 11 and 18, and Saturday, September 12 and 19, from 11am to 3.30pm, and on Sunday, September 13 and 20, from 12.30pm to 3.30pm.

Jo James, minister at Bradford Unitarians, said: "Bradford Unitarian Church on Russell Street is inviting the public to step onto a sacred path this Heritage Open Days, when it opens its doors."

The current building, constructed in 1969, features stained glass windows from the original Chapel Lane Chapel.

The congregation’s first non-conformist meeting house was built in 1719, reflecting Bradford’s tradition of religious dissent and intellectual radicalism during the industrial revolution.

Visitors are invited to walk the church’s labyrinth, a peaceful, meditative practice open to people of all faiths or none.

Ms James said: "The modern church also incorporates a labyrinth, an ancient walking practice to reflect and calm the mind.

"Anyone of any faith (or none) can walk a labyrinth, unlike a maze there are no dead ends or tricks, just a gentle contemplative practice."

Visitors can also browse the church’s archives, join guided talks with Reverend James, or simply enjoy refreshments and conversation.

Bradford Unitarian Church describes itself as a spiritual home for free thinkers.

The Heritage Open Days event is free and open to all.

A labyrinth walk, archive viewing, and guided talks will be available during opening hours.

More information about the church and its history can be found at bradfordunitarians.co.uk.
