A national project is aiming to set a new standard for cutting emissions by switching off street lights.

Led by East Riding of Yorkshire Council, the scheme has been recognised with 10 industry awards and aims to reduce carbon output and energy use through smarter street lighting.

Funded by a £3.3 million grant from the Department for Transport, the council ran a three-year pilot exploring more sustainable ways to light main roads, particularly as modern vehicle headlights reduce the need for some street lights.

Councillor Paul West, cabinet member for transport and environment, said: "We are incredibly proud of this scheme and the small team running it, because it shows the East Riding is leading the way in what could soon become a nationwide change."

The pilot involved turning off street lights on sections of the A164 and A1079, instead installing reflective road studs, brighter white lines, improved signage, and lighting focused on pedestrian areas.

Results showed a 10 per cent drop in driver speed and a 33 per cent reduction in night-time collisions, with no negative impact on driver behaviour.

The project is expected to cut 160 tonnes of carbon emissions and save £1.2 million over the next decade.
