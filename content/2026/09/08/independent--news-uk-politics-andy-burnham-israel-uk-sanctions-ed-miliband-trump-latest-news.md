The US ambassador to Israel has condemned the UK sanctions on Israeli settlements in the West Bank, describing them as “an irrational action” and “discrimination against the Jewish people”.

Andy Burnham faces a row with both Donald Trump and Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyhau, as No10 takes a tougher stance on the issue of Palestine.

Foreign secretary Ed Miliband is expected to set out a “reset” of the UK’s relations with Israel in a Commons statement on Tuesday.

Republican Mike Huckabee said he had “not personally” spoken to Mr Trump, but said: “I feel like that it’s a discrimination against the Israeli government, it’s discrimination against the Jewish people and I don’t understand the rationale of it.”

Ministers have suggested goods coming out of the illegal settlements in the West Bank, a Palestinian territory, could be subject to trade restrictions as part of the changes.

Mr Burnham discussed the Middle East with Mr Trump in a phone call on Monday ahead of the intervention, which risks drawing criticism from Washington, Israel’s closest ally.

*The Times* reported that the Trump administration would make a statement attacking the intervention.

Tensions in the West Bank have flared in recent months, with Israeli settlers involved in violent disputes with Palestinians.

## Sanctions could impact Israeli elections, former foreign secretary warns

Trade sanctions could “create a backlash” ahead of Israel’s upcoming elections, a former foreign secretary has warned.

Sir Malcolm Rifkind told Times Radio: "I think there is a perfectly sound argument and principle for trying to apply sanctions, not to Israel generally, but to the illegal settlements by Israeli settlers on the West Bank. But I do have reservations.

"First of all, it's an obvious, not just a matter of foreign policy, Burnham is doing this because of internal Labour Party problems."

"There is a general election coming up in Israel in October," Sir Malcolm said, "and all the polls say that Netanyahu is going to lose."

"The problem when you have foreign governments involving themselves in your domestic politics in this way, it could create a backlash."

## Israeli finance minister calls for expulsion of British ambassador

Israeli’s finance minister Bezalel Smotrich has criticised the sanctions plan and called for the British ambassador to be expelled.

“We will not be a doormat for an antisemitic government in a country occupied by radical Islam that is trying to escape economic and social collapse by attacking Jews and the state of Israel,” he wrote on social media.

“The British Mandate in Israel is over”.

## Minister slaps down US ambassador warnings that Israeli sanctions will do “great damage” to Britain

A government minister has dismissed warnings from the US ambassador to Israel that sanctions on illegal settlements will do “great damage” to British business interests.

Mike Huckabee told BBC Radio 4 that he didn’t believe the government was “really aware” of the impacts of the trade ban expected to be announced today.

“For example, 25 per cent of pharmaceuticals that are used in the UK are made in Israel. Are they willing to give those up? It looks like they are,” he said.

“Are they willing also to do great damage to any Brits who are doing business in a number of states in the US.”

![Ed Miliband's announcement is likely to sour relations with Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu even further](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/08/09/2026-09-06T145243Z_13245311_RC2IULAPS3LL_RTRMADP_3_ISRAEL-PALESTINIANS-SETTLEMENTS.JPG)

But work and pensions secretary Pat McFadden said he was sure Ed Miliband had “taken all this into account” when drawing up the proposal.

“This is not going to be a boycott of business with Israel,” he said. “This is an important economic relationship. It's an important security relationship that will continue.”

He added: “There is a difference between that ongoing relationship and the approval of specific policies by this Israeli government at this time, and it's the continued expansion of the settlements that have given rise to the statement.”

## Tom Tugendhat says plans could put UK's national security at risk

Shadow foreign secretary Tom Tugendhat previously said the plans could put Britain’s national security at risk and make it harder to fight terrorism.

He told the Telegraph: “It’s worth remembering that Israel is one of our most important intelligence partners, and has been for many, many years.

“I can tell you, as security minister, a lot of incidents did not happen because of co-operation with intelligence sources in Israel, who helped us to protect British people from terror attacks.”

## US ambassador to Israel calls sanctions an 'irrational action'

The US ambassador to Israel said UK sanctions on Israeli settlements in the West Bank are “an irrational action” and “discrimination against the Jewish people”.

Mike Huckabee told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme that he had “not personally” spoken to President Donald Trump about the policy this week.

The Republican politician said: “This is an irrational action. That’s what I’m saying. I don’t understand what it’s supposed to achieve.

“If the UK is really interested in dealing with issues they feel like are wrong, where are the sanctions against North Korea, China, Russia? Where are the sanctions against many other nations in the world?

“I feel like that it’s a discrimination against the Israeli government, it’s discrimination against the Jewish people and I don’t understand the rationale of it.”

He added: “I don’t know if they have thought through, it’s like throwing a stone and not having an idea of where it’s going to land.

“And I realise, look, the UK is a sovereign country, they can do what they want, but so is the US.”

## Anti-terror tsar voices concern over potential impact on British Jews

Asked about the intervention, Jonathan Hall KC, the independent reviewer of terrorism legislation, told BBC Newsnight: “From a terrorism perspective, I mean, obviously one’s got to think about international cooperation.”

He added: “That is a really big deal, particularly with Iran, thinking about intelligence. On the other hand, I suppose it’s also true to say that some settler violence does amount to terrorism.”

Mr Hall also expressed concern about the potential impact on Britain’s Jewish communities, adding: “I think there is a risk that if you stir the pot by making Israel a pariah state, then that will bring that flame up again, which we saw.

“Remember, it was only a year ago that there was the attack at Heaton Park. And I’ve been very worried about de-legitimising anyone who’s Israeli, certainly, and Jews, many of whom support Israel, and painting a bit of a target on the back.

“Now, I’m sure the government will be very careful, I hope they will be, in the way in which they describe this, but that is a concern.”

## Burnham to change tactic on Israel after Starmer lost key voters

Labour lost the support of many of its left-wing backers under Sir Keir Starmer over his initial response to Israel’s military action in Gaza following Hamas’s October 7 attack on Israel in 2023.

Prime minister Andy Burnham, who was among senior party figures to have called for a ceasefire by late October that year, previously suggested he would change the Government’s approach to the conflict soon after coming to office in July.

He has voiced particular concerns about the E1 settlement, where Israel has given final approval for a controversial project that would effectively separate the occupied West Bank from East Jerusalem.

## Sanctions carry 'real risk of increased chances of attacks on British jews', Labour MP warns

The honorary vice-chairman of Labour Friends of Israel said he warned Ed Miliband that sanctions carry “a real risk of increased chances of attacks on the British Jewish community”.

Labour MP Peter Prinsley told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: “My fear is that opponents of Israel won’t distinguish between Israeli government policy, Zionistic political philosophy, and British Jews.

“As far as they’re concerned, the whole thing’s the same, and I think it carries a real risk of increased chances of attacks on the British Jewish community.

“Which is exactly what I said to Ed Miliband when I was asked to see him two or three nights ago.

“I think the timing is terrible. I think we need to wait for the result of the Israeli election, and then we need to have another think.”

Mr Prinsley added: “We must do whatever we can to preserve the two-state solution, and I think the behaviour of the presently-elected Israeli government is really outrageous in this respect. I have a horrible feeling that what we’re doing will not only embolden the present government of Netanyahu, but has the capacity to influence the election itself, which is on a knife edge.”

## Trade ban is not a 'boycott of all Israeli goods', minister says

New sanctions on Israeli West Bank settlements are “specifically targeted” and not intended to be a “boycott of all Israeli goods”, work and pensions secretary Pat McFadden said.

Asked how it would be possible to tell whether goods come from settlements in the West Bank or the rest of Israel and to address concerns this could amount to an overall boycott by the back door, he told Sky News: “The foreign secretary will set out details of how this will operate when he stands up in Parliament this afternoon.

“But I think it’s also important to say that Israel is an important trading partner for the UK. This isn’t intended to be a boycott of all Israeli goods.”

He added: “It’s measures specifically targeted at the expansion of these settlements, which are illegal under international law, in the West Bank.”
