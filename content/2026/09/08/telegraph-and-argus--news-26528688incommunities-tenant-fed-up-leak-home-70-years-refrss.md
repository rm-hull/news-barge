A WOMAN who has lived in her Bradford district home for 70 years says she is "fed up" - claiming a leak in her house forced her to live upstairs.

Ann Speight, of Ryshworth Avenue in Bingley, claims a leak at her home on August 8 left her living room floor all wet underneath.

She now says the "house is a tip."

## What has Incommunities said?

Incommunities says a plumber "attended straight away" when it was first reported on August 8.

It adds that the leak to the stop tap was repaired "several weeks ago".

Incommunities says it can make a claim through its insurer for any damage caused, but it needs more information from Ms Speight in writing first.

*(Image: T&A)*

## 'I want the floor done'

Mrs Speight told the T&A: "Nobody has rung me.

"I'm fed up, absolutely sick of ringing [Incommunities].

"My home is not my home anymore.

"I want the floor done."

  The stop valve in the living room 

*(Image: T&A)*

The tenant's main gripe is with the flooring.

She says the wooden flooring is beginning to buckle with the amount of water sitting underneath it.

Mrs Speight adds that it needs pulling up as soon as possible.

  Mrs Speight's home 

*(Image: T&A)*

## What the housing provider had to say

An Incommunities spokesperson said: “We understand how upsetting this situation has been for our customer.

“When the leak was reported on August 8, a plumber attended straight away.

"The leak to the stop tap was also repaired several weeks ago.

“We have advised our customer that we can make a claim through our insurer for any damage caused but to progress this, we do need some information from her in writing.

"Our team will continue to support her through the next steps.”

  The stop valve in the living room 

*(Image: T&A)*

## Who are Incommunities?

Incommunities is a not-for-profit housing association and registered social-housing provider based in Bradford.

It manages more than 22,000 homes across Yorkshire—mainly in the Bradford district—and lets and maintains social and affordable housing.

It was established following the transfer of council housing from Bradford Council in 2003.

Alongside being a landlord, it builds new homes, carries out repairs and improvement work, and funds or supports community initiatives; it operates independently of Bradford Council.
