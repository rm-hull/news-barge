The Princess of Wales may now be one of the most recognisable figures in the world, but her family story begins far from royal residences and state banquets.

Instead, generations of Catherine’s maternal ancestors lived and worked in the coalfield communities in the North East, carving out lives in the shadow of the pithead and helping to power Britain’s industrial rise.

Kate’s family tree traces her mother’s side to the mining villages of Hetton-Le-Hole and nearby Low Moorsley, where the Harrison family worked underground for more than a century.

According to genealogical records, the family’s mining connection began with James Harrison, who entered the County Durham coal industry in 1819.

Two years later, he moved to Low Moorsley after news emerged of a new pit opening in the area.

He and his wife Jane settled in a miner’s cottage and became part of a community built around the rapidly expanding coalfield.

   

*(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

Coal mining would become the family’s way of life for generations.

The Harrisons remained in the North East coalfield for around 120 years, with fathers, sons and grandsons following one another into the pits that fuelled Britain’s factories, railways and homes.

The Princess’s maternal great-grandfather, Thomas Harrison, was born into that mining tradition.

He was the son of a miner from the Hetton-Le-Hole area - which was previously part of County Durham before becoming part of Sunderland in the 1970s - and grew up in colliery housing before choosing a different path.

Seeking safer and cleaner employment, he left County Durham and moved south to London in the early 20th century, where he worked as a carpenter.

That move would ultimately set the family on a dramatically different course.

Thomas’s daughter Dorothy Harrison went on to marry Ronald Goldsmith.

Their daughter, Carole Goldsmith, later founded the successful Party Pieces business and became Carole Middleton after marrying Michael Middleton.

Carole and Michael’s eldest child is Catherine, now Princess of Wales and future Queen Consort.

The story represents a remarkable journey through British social history, from the coal seams of east Durham to the heart of the Royal Family.

Local historians have previously described Kate’s ancestry as evidence of the social mobility that transformed Britain during the 20th century.
