A detective was sacked after lying to a senior officer and pressuring a junior colleague to alter police records to cover his tracks.

DS Christopher Hogg was dismissed without notice following a Northumbria Police misconduct hearing which found attempts to cover up his actions amounted to gross misconduct.

The hearing found Hogg altered a police document about a person C to say: "no consent was given and therefore no search was carried out."

The original entry said consent would have been given if a search was needed, but none took place because "it was not necessary and there was insufficient time".

Hogg changed the document without speaking to either officer involved or checking the relevant intelligence submission.

He told a former junior constable he changed the entry because it was "shoddy work" and he wanted to "avoid scrutiny".

The panel found Hogg instructed the junior officer, 'either expressly or implicitly', to change intelligence records to match the altered document.

It said: "There is no other reasonable explanation for the former police constable to change the records.

The panel said Hogg knew he had amended the entry before meeting the detective inspector, but lied when he blamed the discrepancy on a mix-up or misinterpretation.

The panel found a third allegation proven, saying the junior constable changed the records back at Hogg's request.

The report said: "The panel finds that the former police constable changed the NPICCS records back to the original text because DS Hogg asked him to do so and that his behaviour was dishonest."

The panel concluded Hogg had breached the standards of professional behaviour relating to honesty and integrity and discreditable conduct.

It said his actions were likely to undermine public confidence in policing.

Hogg was an experienced intelligence officer and detective sergeant trusted with a position of responsibility. The panel added: "He should have known better."

It said Hogg realised that morning he had done wrong but "made no attempt to admit and rectify his own errors", instead trying to "cover his own back by placing pressure on a junior officer."

The panel found the former constable was left upset and uncomfortable in a difficult and stressful position.

It added: "An officer who is dishonest in order to cover his own back and who places pressure on a junior officer to avoid detection seriously undermines the trust that the public should be able to place in police officers."

The panel also considered his good service record and character references.

The report said the behaviour was "out of character for DS Hogg" and that he had "a history of long service as a good officer."

But the panel, chaired by assistant chief constable Alastair Simpson, said Hogg's personal circumstances did not excuse his decision-making.
