A new food van has been named in honour of a long-serving catering manager.

Bedale High School has unveiled Café De Roche as part of its new outdoor food court, celebrating 20 years of service by catering manager Annette De Roche.

The van forms part of a student-inspired upgrade to the school’s lunchtime facilities.

Chris Stokes, headteacher at Bedale High School, said: "Annette’s contribution to Bedale High School over the past 20 years has been remarkable.

"I have personally never experienced a higher-quality catering service than the one Annette provides.

"She cares deeply about our students, takes enormous pride in her work and consistently goes above and beyond for the school."

The outdoor food court and Café De Roche were created in response to student feedback calling for improved outdoor spaces and a wider range of lunchtime options.

The latest addition reflects the school’s commitment to acting on student voice and creating a more sociable dining environment.

Mr Stokes said: "Naming the van Café De Roche felt like the perfect way to recognise her exceptional service.

"It means that Annette’s name will now sit proudly at the heart of a facility designed to serve the next generation of Bedale students."

The new food court is part of a wider investment programme, with around £750,000 spent on upgrades to the school site since September 2024.

The improvements were funded directly from the school’s main budget.

Mr Stokes said: "Our students deserve facilities that reflect the ambition we have for them.

"The outdoor food court is not simply about adding a food van; it is about responding to what students have told us, improving their daily experience and continuing to create a school environment of which the whole community can be proud."

The investment comes amid rising achievement across the school.

In summer 2026, Bedale High School recorded its best-ever GCSE results for the second year running, with Attainment 8 scores rising from 42.0 in 2024 to 47.8 in 2026.

The proportion of students achieving grade 5 or above in both English and maths increased from 30.7 per cent to 52.4 per cent during the same period.
