BRADFORD Council’s Executive has voted to re-open a tip, and will use £400,000 earmarked for cultural activities to do so.

But the a top finance officer will have to sign off on the decision after questions over whether the six-strong Reform UK Executive had the power to scrap funding that had been voted for by the council just a few months ago.

The Sugden End tip, which is on the outskirts of Keighley in Cross Roads, was shut in 2024 as part of a wave of budget cuts by the then Labour-run Bradford Council.

## What happened at the meeting?

At a meeting of the now-Reform-led Executive on Tuesday morning, Council Leader Stephen Place said reopening Sugden End had been a pledge of the party since before they were in power.

While other parties supported the decision to re-open the tip, there was a heated debate in the council chamber over how it would be funded.

A report before the Executive says £400,000 that was set aside for cultural events to “drive increased footfall in district centres” would be re-purposed to fund the re-opening and day to day operations of the tip.

The report said the re-opening of the tip would cost £95,000, and after that it would cost £315,000 a year to run.

At the meeting Councillor Ian Walker, Portfolio Holder for Neighbourhoods and Communities, said: “I imagine this will be welcomed by everyone.”

Dave Shepherd, director for place, told the meeting that Sugden End, along with Golden Butts and Ford Hill tips, had been shut due to the council facing intense financial pressures.

He said the closures had “successfully achieved budget savings” required for the council to maintain a functional waste service department.

## Support for the reopening

Councillor Falak Ahmed (Cons, Bingley West) said she “strongly welcomed” the plans, and asked council bosses: “Can you give a commitment that once it is re-opened it will remain open in the long term?”

Cllr Place said: “It has always been part of our plan to re-open Sugden End, and we’re committed to keeping it open.”

Councillor Andrew Judson, Deputy Leader, said: “There has always been an agreement that Labour made a big mistake closing the tip.”

He denied that using £400,000 set aside for cultural events was reducing money for culture.

## What Labour have said

Councillor Imran Khan, Leader of the Labour group on the council, said: “It is not a reduction? Of course it is.

“I completely support re-opening Sugden End if possible, we are in a completely different position to where we were in 2024 – financially things have changed.”

He said the Labour Government had vastly increased central funding for Bradford, and this meant the district was in a much better position than when the decision to close the tip was made.

Cllr Khan said the decision to close the tip was made on officer advice, and asked whether this advice had changed.

## Cultural funding pulled

Referring to the cultural funding, he said: “The council voted in the budget to put that money into our communities to help them run events themselves.

"Will pulling this money have an impact? Of course it will. You’re taking it from these communities.”

He questioned whether the Executive could vote to use this funding for other means, considering that it had been approved at a full council meeting earlier this year.

Referring to Cllr Place’s comments shortly after he came into power, Cllr Khan said: “You said Bradford was open for business.

"That you’d work with all groups to drive footfall, then you do the exact opposite of that.

“This investment in events would have brought people together and supported jobs. You’ve now scrapped all that despite what you promised when you took control.

“How can any business trust you again – that you won’t pull funding at the 11th hour after it has already been agreed.”

Cllr Place said: “We’re doing the best we can given the basket case we inherited.”

Due to concerns over whether the Executive had the authority to scrap funding voted for in the budget, a caveat was added to the final decision – if the re-opening was approved, it would need to be signed off by the Council’s Section 151 Officer.

Members voted to approve the re-opening.

If it goes ahead, it is expected to re-open before Christmas.
