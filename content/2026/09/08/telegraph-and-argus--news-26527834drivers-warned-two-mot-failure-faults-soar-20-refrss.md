STEERING and road wheel faults are becoming more common reasons for MOT failures.

New analysis by Rev Monkey UK using five years of DVSA data has revealed a 24.6 per cent rise in steering failures and a 23.3 per cent increase in road wheel defects.

These issues now make up 3.5 per cent and a growing share of all recorded faults, respectively.

Steering faults are not only more frequent but also more severe, with serious/dangerous defects rising by 20.1 per cent.

Road wheel faults have seen the fastest increase in danger, with dangerous/major failures up by 28.6 per cent.

Tyres remain the most hazardous MOT fail, making up just 12.9 per cent of all defects but accounting for 64 per cent of dangerous or major failures.

This represents a fivefold "danger multiplier," the highest of any category.

Emissions and leaks have also risen by 11.3 per cent, pointing to an ageing vehicle fleet or postponed maintenance.

Not all the news is negative, though.

Six categories of faults have declined, including seat belt issues, which are down by 10.5 per cent, and visibility problems such as lights and wipers, which have fallen by 7.8 per cent.
