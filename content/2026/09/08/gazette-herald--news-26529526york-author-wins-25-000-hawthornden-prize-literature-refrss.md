A writer from York has won one of Britain’s oldest literary awards.

Caleb Klaces was awarded the Hawthornden Prize for Literature for his second novel, Mr Outside.

The novel - based on the life of Mr Klaces’ own father - tells the story of a man helping his reclusive father move into a care home during a time of restricted movement, blending text and image to explore intimacy, memory and reality.

Mr Klaces said: "This prize is the best surprise of my life so far.

"I am grateful to the judges and to the Hawthornden Foundation for their generous support of Mr Outside.

"Few things make writing easier, and this is one of them."

It has been described as a "tender meditation on endings, the limits of understanding, and the act of letting go."

He will receive the £25,000 prize at a private ceremony in October.

The Hawthornden Prize is awarded annually to a British, Irish or British-based author for a work of "imaginative literature" published in the previous calendar year.

Past winners include Colm Tóibín, Hilary Mantel, and Ali Smith.

Emily Berry, judge and previous winner, praised the novel as "tender but unsentimental," calling it a meditation on "absence and intimacy, gratitude and regret".

Jess Chandler, editor and founder of Prototype, said: "Caleb is one of the most distinctive, committed, imaginative and uncompromising writers I have ever been lucky enough to read, let alone publish."

Mr Outside was also shortlisted for the RSL Encore Award 2026.
