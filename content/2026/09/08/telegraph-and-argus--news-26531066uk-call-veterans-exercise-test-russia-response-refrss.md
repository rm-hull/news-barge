Military veterans are set to be called up as Britain strengths its preparations to face the growing threat from Russia.

The exercise is due to take place next year as the UK tests its ability to respond to the increasing threat from Russia.

Former members of the Army, Royal Navy, and RAF will be drawn from the UK’s 95,000-strong strategic reserve to assess whether they can be contacted, mobilised, and equipped in the event of a crisis.

Armed Forces Minister Louise Sandher-Jones said the mobilisation would take place "on a scale we haven't seen for a long time," though the number of veterans involved has not yet been confirmed.

The exercise will put to the test a system that senior defence figures have warned is not currently up to the task of tracking and recalling ex-personnel.

Lord Robertson of Port Ellen, who led last year’s strategic defence review, previously said officials did not "know where most" reserve members were and called for a renewed effort to identify and prepare those who are "available and fit and willing."

Air Chief Marshal Sir Richard Knighton, Chief of the Defence Staff, has also warned that the UK lacks an "effective system of tracking and engaging" recent leavers from the Armed Forces.

Ms Sandher-Jones outlined the plans before the House of Lords’ national resilience committee on Monday.

She said: "The strategic reserve is a real commitment for those that are in it and they will be exercised as part of our exercise programme next year where we will go through measures to call out, use, equip and all the rest of the procedures we'd have to go through to use the strategic reserve properly."

She described the reserve as "a lot less used in my lifetime" but emphasised its growing importance in the current security environment.

The strategic reserve consists of former military personnel who remain liable for recall after leaving service and is distinct from the UK’s nearly 32,000 active reservists, who serve part-time on a voluntary basis.

Under existing legislation, all former regular and reserve officers can be recalled at any time, though the Ministry of Defence is only required to maintain contact with former personnel for six years after they leave full-time service.

The Government is also moving to expand its ability to mobilise veterans through the new Armed Forces Bill.

The maximum call-up age will rise from 55 to 65, and the threshold for mobilisation will be lowered from "national danger, great emergency or attack in the UK" to "war-like preparations".

These changes are expected to come into effect next spring, as the UK steps up preparations for a possible large-scale conflict.

Defence planners have increasingly warned that the UK could face direct confrontation with Russia before the end of the decade.

Tensions continue to rise between Moscow and NATO, with Russia threatening strikes against British drone factories supporting Ukraine.

The UK’s regular Army currently stands at around 71,000 fully trained personnel, its smallest size in more than 200 years.

However, the Ministry of Defence announced last week that recruitment outpaced departures across the full-time forces for the first time since 2021.

The Army grew by 170 personnel, the RAF by 840, and the Royal Navy and Royal Marines by 440.
