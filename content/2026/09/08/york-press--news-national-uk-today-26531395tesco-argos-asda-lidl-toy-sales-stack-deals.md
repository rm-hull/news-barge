The 2026 autumn toy sales are now getting underway, with major retailers including Lidl, Asda and Argos already launching their events and other supermarkets expected to follow in the coming weeks.

Last year's sales offered discounts of up to 50 per cent on big-name toys, including Peppa Pig, Hot Wheels and Barbie.

But parents looking to spread the cost of Christmas have been urged not to stop at the sale price.


Savings Expert Clair Hughes, known to her followers as ThriftyClair, from VoucherCodes.co.uk, has revealed her tips for squeezing even more value from the supermarket toy sales.

Clair said: “Whether you’re a parent like me or just a self-confessed savvy shopper looking to save this Christmas, the Autumn supermarket toy sales are your golden opportunity to spread the cost of the festive season and take the financial pressure off the final months of the year.

“But why should the savings stop at the sale price? You could be missing out on extra discounts or ways to stretch your Christmas budget further, without even knowing it.

“Out with the old, to fund the new: Before buying a single new toy, do a tabletop or children’s market sale. Not only does this clear out playroom clutter but it also gives you extra cash in your back pocket to spend when store discounts go live."


## What is discount stacking?

Clair says: “Master discount stacking: Don't settle for just the sale price. Over the year you can save up your loyalty card points, such as Nectar or Clubcard points, to splurge on the sales for even deeper savings.

“By shopping through sites like VoucherCodes, who reward customers with free gift cards just for making their everyday purchases, you can save up these gift cards to spend in-store or online during the toy sales.

"For example, you can currently nab a £10 gift card when you shop at Very through VoucherCodes.co.uk, which can be redeemed at all major UK supermarkets.”

She added: “If you add cashback on top of this, you could save even more. Banks, such as Monzo and Chase, offer supermarket cashback deals when you pay through them - you often need to activate the offers first to make sure you qualify. Once confirmed, the cashback drops straight into your bank account.

“Don't get caught at the checkout: Supermarket sales at Tesco and Morrisons are specifically gated behind loyalty cards. Make sure to sign up in advance and scan your Tesco Clubcard or Morrisons More Card at the till to cash in on the deals. Miss this step and you’ll be charged full price.

“Look beyond the obvious: There's always huge hype around the Aldi wooden toy event, with parents flocking to stores to grab their discounted toys, but you don't need to endure early morning crowds. Stores like Smyths, Sports Direct, and Lidl all offer fantastic wooden toy ranges at equally competitive prices - without the aisle chaos.

“Always check for in-store vs. online terms: Sainsbury’s and Morrisons toy sales are strictly in-store only, whereas Asda and Argos are available to shop online. If buying online, always opt for Click & Collect to prevent delivery fees from nibbling away at your savings.”

### Bag the Christmas bargains available now

It might seem strange to be thinking of Christmas so early, but that is exactly what some savvy consumers are doing.

Consumer expert, Helen Dewdney, AKA The Complaining Cow and author of 456 Ways to Save Money on Christmas Throughout the Year, is advising consumers there are always offers available, it’s never too early to start and that you can take advantage of special offers now.

"Many stores have offers on now, the end of the Summer and not near to Christmas means that stores are putting on a number of discounts and offers to move stock," she says.

Some of her examples include keeping an eye on the supermarkets. Tesco, for instance, usually have toy sales throughout the year.

"Smyths always have clearance offers but currently have toys up to 65% off and they won’t face the stock shortage issues that stores may have nearer Christmas."
