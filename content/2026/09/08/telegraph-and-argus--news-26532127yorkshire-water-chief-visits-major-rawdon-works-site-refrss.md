Yorkshire Water’s chief executive has visited Rawdon to see the impact of major waterworks.

Nicola Shaw was joined by Leeds North West MP Katie White at the site off Green Lane, where teams are replacing and refurbishing a major section of the local water network.

The area has long struggled with leaks and water discolouration, recording around 10 bursts each year.

Ms White said: "These works are important because people in Rawdon have put up with far too many leaks and bursts over the years, and replacing this section of the network should make a real difference."

While the improvements are welcome, the MP said many residents and businesses have raised concerns about disruption, communication, and the state of roads and pavements following the works.

She said: "But a project of this scale inevitably has an impact on people living and working nearby, which is why I wanted Nicola here in Rawdon to see the works herself and hear directly about some of the issues residents have experienced.

"We had a constructive conversation about getting the final stages completed, keeping people properly informed and making sure roads, pavements and green spaces are put right afterwards."

Yorkshire Water has said the renewed mains should not need replacing again for generations.

The company confirmed that “snagging” will take place after the main works are finished, with roads, pavements and other areas affected by construction expected to be restored to at least their condition before construction.

Ms White is encouraging residents to report any outstanding issues so they can be addressed.

Leeds Road is due to be cleared following the current works, with Harrogate Road expected to be cleared shortly afterwards.

Further works are then due around Green Lane and Yeadon Roundabout, with the main scheme expected to be completed in October.

During the visit, Ms White also raised other concerns from across her constituency.

These included repeated leaks in Rawdon earlier this year that created a "river of ice," and ongoing concerns about works around Arthrington Lane in Pool.

She also highlighted the need for clearer communication about road closures and better coordination with councillors, who are often the first point of contact for residents.

Ms White said she will continue to work with Yorkshire Water, councillors, and residents until the project is complete.

She said: "If there are outstanding problems I want people to continue getting in touch with me."
