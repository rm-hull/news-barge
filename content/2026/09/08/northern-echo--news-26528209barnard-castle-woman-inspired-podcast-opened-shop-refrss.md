A Barnard Castle woman who was inspired by a Dragon's Den star's podcast has opened her own shop after she decided she had 'nothing to lose'.

Tracey Bowes, 59, has launched Bowes Occasions in the town’s Market Place, selling cards, gifts, drinks and traditional pick and mix sweets.

The former newsagent's manager had always dreamed of running her own business but said listening to the Mind Your Business podcast with Sara Davies and Helen Goddard gave her the push she needed.

  Tracey Bowes, 59, has opened Bowes Occasions in Barnard Castle 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Tracey, originally from Consett and now living in Cockfield, said: "I have always wanted to do it and have my own business and after listening to the podcast I just felt really inspired to just go for it.

"I have worked 30 years as a manager of a newsagents/off license and worked with confectionaries and just before this I actually worked for the Decorating Centre Online in Durham.

"I worked with crafts and cards there and before and always enjoyed it. I find it sort of therapeutic and relaxing in a way."

  Inside the brand new Bowes Occasions in Barnard Castle 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

In July, Tracey spotted a closing-down sign at a nearby shop while heading to a jeweller after breaking her bracelet.

Rather than walk past, she decided she had nothing to lose.

  Inside the brand new Bowes Occasions in Barnard Castle 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

She said: "It has been a few months in the works now. It was sort of third time lucky, in July I had snapped my bracelet and went to go to the jewellers and nearby there was this closing down sign due to retirement.

"So I just went in thinking I had nothing to lose and spoke to the owner then landlord and because they had no other interest I got the keys pretty quick."

Now Tracey hopes Bowes Occasions can become a one-stop shop for people looking for cards, gifts and little treats in Barnard Castle.

Cards start from 59p, while the shop also stocks gifts for weddings, baby showers and special birthdays alongside grave ornaments and memorial items.

The opening weekend was quieter than Tracey had hoped because of the Wolsingham Show, but she is hoping business will now pick up.

The shop is open Monday to Saturday, 9am to 5pm.
