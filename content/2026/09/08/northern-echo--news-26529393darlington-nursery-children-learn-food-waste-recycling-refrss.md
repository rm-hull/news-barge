Nursery-age children in Darlington are learning to recycle food waste with the help of a friendly kitchen caddy named Colin.

Every nursery in the town has received a free educational pack featuring Colin, along with an outdoor food waste bin and information for parents and educators about the council’s new food waste scheme, which launched in early June.

The packs include hands-on activities that teach children where food comes from, how to prepare it safely, and how to dispose of scraps and peelings through the recycling system.

Libby McCollom, cabinet member for local services, said: "This is a great way to teach everyone why reducing food waste and recycling is so important.

"Nurseries have welcomed Colin the caddy and have used the activities to reinforce sustainability and healthy lifestyle messages in everyday play.

"It is great that childminders can now borrow a Colin the caddy activity pack from the library to help encourage the children in their care to think about the environment and healthy eating."

Many nurseries have chosen to add the kitchen caddy and outdoor bin to their role-play areas so that children routinely use the equipment as part of their play.

The council says feedback from both adults and children has been positive.
