Drivers are being warned they could face £1,000 fines for a common parking mistake.

The warning comes as a result of a little-known rule in the Highway Code, which could catch out motorists parking outside their own homes, especially as the clocks change and evenings get darker.

Rule 248 of the Highway Code states that drivers must not park on a road at night facing against the direction of the traffic flow unless in a recognised parking space.

The rule exists because a car’s rear reflectors are designed to alert oncoming drivers in the dark. If a vehicle is parked facing the wrong way, the front headlights and indicators do not provide the same level of visibility, creating a hazard on poorly lit roads.

The RAC said: "The Highway Code isn't the law, but many of its rules are in fact legal requirements under the Roads Traffic Act, which you can be prosecuted for contravening.

"You may be fined, given penalty points on your licence, disqualified from driving, or even sent to prison."

Fines for breaking this rule can reach up to £1,000.

Other parking guidelines in the Highway Code include not parking too close to a vehicle displaying a Blue Badge, stopping as close as possible to the roadside, and ensuring the engine, headlights and fog lights are switched off.

Drivers are also advised to check for other road users before opening their doors and to ensure passengers exit on the side next to the kerb.

The RAC also warns drivers to use parking lights on roads with a speed limit higher than 30mph.

A spokesperson said: "Parking lights must be used on a road or layby on a highway with a speed limit higher than 30mph.

"Check your vehicle’s handbook to find out how to operate them: often, they are engaged by clicking the indicator stalk either left or right as you leave the car, but some models have dedicated parking light buttons."

Mo Rafique, a Vehicle Security Expert from Motor Guards UK, said: "Drivers often think they're safe as long as they're not on a double yellow line.

"But this rule is about safety, not convenience.

"Many drivers are shocked when they receive a fine for this.

"They've likely been doing it for years without an issue, but as traffic enforcement becomes smarter, these lesser-known rules are being more strictly applied.

"The key to avoiding a fine is to take a few extra seconds to turn your car around before you park."
