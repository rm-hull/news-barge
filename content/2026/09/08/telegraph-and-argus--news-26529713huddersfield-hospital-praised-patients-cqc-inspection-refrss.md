PATIENTS have described it as the “best hospital they’ve been to”.

Following an inspection by the Care Quality Commission (CQC), Cygnet Hospital Wyke has been rated 'good' across all areas, including safe, effective, caring, responsive and well-led.

Located on Blankey Grange, Huddersfield Road, the 31-bed hospital provides acute, psychiatric intensive care unit (PICU), and rehabilitation services for men.

Matthew Ellis, hospital manager at Cygnet Hospital Wyke, said: "This Good rating across all five areas is a positive reflection of the work taking place across Cygnet Hospital Wyke every day.

"The CQC’s findings recognise Cygnet Hospital Wyke’s focus on recovery, increasing independence, involving patients and carers in decisions, and preparing people for life beyond hospital."

The inspection took place on April 29 and 30, 2026, with inspectors noting consistently positive feedback from both patients and carers.

The CQC report stated: "Patients described the service as good, with some patients telling us it was the 'best hospital they’ve been to'."

Patients said staff were available to talk to when they needed support, listened to them, and were caring and respectful.

One relative described staff as "very reassuring," adding, "they are treating him like a human being."

The report also praised the hospital’s recovery-focused, multidisciplinary approach, which incorporates input from a range of professionals.

The occupational therapy team was singled out for helping patients build independence and prepare for discharge.

Inspectors observed "warm interactions" between staff and patients and highlighted the service’s commitment to staff wellbeing.

The CQC said: "The service cared about and promoted the wellbeing of their staff, and supported and enabled staff to always deliver person-centred care."

"Leadership was also praised by the CQC, with inspectors finding that leaders “lead effectively” with “integrity, openness and honesty.”

Mr Ellis admitted the feedback from patients and carers was especially significant.

He said: "It is particularly pleasing to see the feedback from patients and carers reflected so clearly in the report, as their experience of the service is central to what we are trying to achieve.

"The comments about supporting people to become more independent and prepare for their future are especially important.

"Our role is not simply to support someone during their admission, but to help them develop the confidence, skills and strategies they need for the next stage of their life.

"We will use this report as a foundation to continue building on the progress we have made and achieving positive outcomes for the people we support."
