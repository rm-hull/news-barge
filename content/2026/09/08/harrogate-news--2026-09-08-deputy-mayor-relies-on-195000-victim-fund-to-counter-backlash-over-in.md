**York and North Yorkshire Deputy Mayor Jo Coles** has issued a strategic public statement on the Government’s incoming emergency prisoner early release scheme, pointing to a newly secured**£195,000 grant** as local authorities brace for a national policy change they cannot block.

The statement functions as a regional management strategy as prisons prepare to lower automatic release thresholds to **one-third (33%)** and**one-half (50%)** of standard determinate sentence lengths. While the Deputy Mayor has publicly aligned herself with worried residents by stating she “shares those concerns,” independent legal criteria confirm that despite her pledges of “robust safeguards” and GPS tagging, high-risk offender categories remain legally eligible for early release into the community.

## The Technical Rules: Who Gains Freedom Early?

The fast-tracked release mechanics, legally restructured under the **Sentencing Act 2026**, alter automatic release points for adults serving a Standard Determinate Sentence (SDS):

- **The One-Third Rule:** Inmates who previously served 40% or 50% of their sentence behind bars will now be automatically discharged into the community after serving just**33%** of their time.
- **The Halfway Rule:** A separate tier of prisoners, previously required to serve two-thirds of their sentence in prison, will walk free at the**50% mark** .

## The Legal Realities: Strict Exclusions vs. Open Loopholes

Following national safeguarding pushback, the government enacted amendments to add specific exclusions to the fast-tracked release program. However, an analysis of the statutory criteria reveals that many categories of offenders still qualify for early release based on their specific sentence lengths.

### Strict Exclusions (Inmates barred from early release):

- **Manslaughter and Unlawful Killing:** Excluded following high-profile public campaigns.
- **Rape and Serious Child Sexual Offences:** Entirely barred from any early release eligibility.
- **Dangerous Driving:** Convictions causing death by dangerous driving or death by drunk driving.
- **Indeterminate Sentences:** Life sentences and Imprisonment for Public Protection (IPP) remain unaffected.

### The Omissions (Inmates who still qualify for early release):

Official data confirms that individuals convicted of several serious offences can still legally walk free at the one-third mark, depending on their sentence threshold. These include individuals jailed for **coercive control, stalking, domestic burglary, non-fatal strangulation, and common assault**.

Independent support networks, including the **Independent Domestic Abuse Services (IDAS)**, have previously warned that regional probation networks are already operating at maximum capacity, raising operational questions over how effectively early-released individuals can be tracked on the ground.

## The Political Positioning and Oversight Claims

The Deputy Mayor’s statement explicitly notes that she met with private infrastructure contractor Serco to review tracking processes, and is “seeking assurances” from North Yorkshire Police—positioning her office as a tough local overseer despite having no legislative power to halt the national mandate.


**Jo Coles said:**

Many people will be feeling worried and concerned about the Government’s proposals around the early release of prisoners. I understand and share those concerns. At a time when the criminal justice system continues to face significant pressures, it is vital that the safeguards being introduced as part of the early-release scheme, including GPS tagging and monitoring arrangements, are robust and effective.



In her capacity as Chair of the **North Yorkshire Local Criminal Justice Partnership**, Coles plans to bring together partner agencies from across the regional justice system to coordinate public protection and direct the new £195,000 allocation to the services where it will have the highest impact.
