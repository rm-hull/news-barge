A PLANNING appeal for a large illuminated sign at a Starbucks drive-thru has been dismissed by a Government planning inspector.

The appeal related to proposals by EG on the Move for a five-metre-high illuminated totem sign at 2 Wheatley Lane, Ben Rhydding, Ilkley, on the corner of the A65 Leeds Road and Wheatley Lane. Bradford Council had previously refused advertisement consent, prompting the appeal.

In a decision issued on August 28, planning inspector N McGurk dismissed the appeal, concluding that the sign would have a harmful impact on the visual character of the area.

The proposed structure would have featured an illuminated Starbucks logo at the top, a directional sign beneath it and a further illuminated panel displaying electric vehicle charging prices. The inspector noted that the sign would occupy a prominent position within the site and would be visible from homes on Wheatley Lane.

Although the neighbouring petrol station already has a larger illuminated totem sign, the inspector said this was an isolated feature and not representative of the wider area. During a site visit, he found that most commercial signage in the locality was "modest, understated and muted in scale and illumination".

The decision states that the proposed sign would appear "disproportionate and discordant" and that its height, illumination and prominent position would make it an "incongruous feature" that would draw attention from a variety of locations, including nearby residential properties.

Mr McGurk also concluded that the additional signage would contribute to visual clutter and be detrimental to visual amenity, contrary to the National Planning Policy Framework.

The appellant argued that the sign would provide operational and economic benefits, including wayfinding information and EV charging prices. However, the inspector found these benefits did not outweigh the harm identified. He also noted comments relating to Policy INDP19 of the Ilkley Neighbourhood Development Plan, which does not support internally illuminated signs.

Dismissing the appeal, the inspector concluded that the proposed advertisement would harm the character of the surrounding area and should not be granted consent.
