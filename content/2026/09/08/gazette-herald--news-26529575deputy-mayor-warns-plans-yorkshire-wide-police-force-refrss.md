A Yorkshire-wide police force could put rural communities at risk, a senior official has warned.

Jo Coles, deputy mayor for policing, fire, and crime in York and North Yorkshire, raised the concerns in response to the Government’s White Paper on police reform.

Writing in the Yorkshire Post, Ms Coles said the plans could dilute accountability and reduce public confidence, particularly in less populated areas.

She said: "Place should be at the heart of police reform, balancing the need for visible and effective neighbourhood policing with the changing nature of crime.

"The suggestion that we could have one Yorkshire-wide police force is a real risk to our county."

She warned that a single force could weaken the connection between police and the communities they serve, especially given ongoing efforts to make other public services more accountable to mayors.

Ms Coles also questioned the financial impact of a merger, raising concerns about cost, value for money, and how council tax would be managed.

She said that centralising resources and decision-making could undermine efforts to improve neighbourhood policing and reduce serious violence.

North Yorkshire remains one of the safest areas in the country, with crime down 2.8 per cent year-on-year—equivalent to 1,291 fewer victims.
