PLANS for a new car business on the site of a former Bradford church have been slammed as “unacceptable and unworkable.”

An application to create a car sales site and car repair workshop in the car park area of 18 Pawson Street have been refused by Bradford Council.

Highways officers pointed out that any visitors to the site would have to drive through two buildings to exit, and that the development would lead to increased parking in an area “where parking problems already exist.”

The former St Mary’s Church building has been converted into a furniture store, with the surrounding land used as parking for this business.

But in recent years there have been attempts to turn the car park into a separate business.

  The former church on Pawson Street 

*(Image: T&A)*

A car sales business was set up in the car park without planning permission, and a retrospective application to keep the business was refused by Bradford Council in 2022.

Officers said: “The proposal would lead to intensification in use of the site and the loss of parking and servicing areas for the existing uses within the site.”

An enforcement notice demanding the site no longer be used for car sales was issued by the Council in 2021, and remains active.

Earlier this year a new application for the site was submitted to Bradford Council by S Kiyani.

  An aerial view of the site 

*(Image: T&A)*

As well as seeking permission for car sales use, the application would also involve the “construction of car repair/service garage and carport for vehicle storage.”

These two structures would be built in the corners of the car park furthest away from the Pawson Street entrance.

But the application has now been refused by Bradford Council after Highways Officers raised serious concerns.

These included that anyone accessing and leaving the site will have to drive through the two new buildings.

They also questioned how the site could support all the car parking that was required.

The 15 parking spaces allocated for the shop in the converted church would be reduced to four – without any allocated spaces for unloading or loading.

They said: “The proposed circulation for vehicles through the site is extremely poor.

“The suggestion that vehicles would drive through two buildings to exit the site is totally unacceptable and unworkable.

“The proposal submitted is overdevelopment of the site leading to intensification in use of the site whilst failing to provide adequate and suitable level of parking, access, and servicing arrangements for the existing and proposed uses within the site.

“This would result in an increase in on-street parking and servicing taking place from the highway on a road where parking problems already exist likely to lead to conditions prejudicial to pedestrian and highway safety.”
