A STUDENT who once ruled out university is now heading to higher education with ambitions of becoming a children’s nurse.

Millie Downey, from Shipley College, had originally planned to find work in a nursery after completing her T Level in Education and Childcare, but is now preparing to study at the University of Huddersfield thanks to the support and encouragement she received during her course.

Ms Downey said: "When I left school, I wanted to be a teaching assistant in a nursery.

"I didn't want to go to university.

"I just wanted to come and do a childcare course and then get a job in a nursery.

"Once I started at Shipley, the tutors and learning coaches made me think differently.

"I started looking further into university and realised there were courses that I really wanted to do.

"They helped me understand that university isn't just about the debt and that getting an extra qualification could help me go much further in life."

She achieved a Distinction, surpassing her predicted grade, and credited her success to the college’s supportive environment and focus on practical experience.

Ms Downey completed 750 hours of industry placement at Cavendish Primary School, where she gained hands-on experience in a professional setting.

She said: "The placement gave me experience of what working professionally is actually like.

"I was planning activities as if I was the class teacher, supporting children with additional needs and learning how you have to cater for different children's needs.

"It taught me professional behaviours, how to work with other people and how to communicate with children, families and other professionals.

"It definitely changed my mindset.

"It made me think more about working with vulnerable children and inspired me to look at children's nursing as the direction I want to take."

Her journey was not without its challenges.

She struggled with part of the first year of her course and had to resit, but with extra support, she turned things around.

Ms Downey said: "I was really shocked because I achieved higher than my predicted grade.

"It was quite a challenging course, but I persevered through all the challenges.

"The tutors and previous Year 2 students helped us get ready for the exams and helped us through our placement observations."

She also praised the college’s close-knit environment.

Ms Downey said: "Because it's smaller, you get that extra support and all the tutors want to help you get the better grade."
