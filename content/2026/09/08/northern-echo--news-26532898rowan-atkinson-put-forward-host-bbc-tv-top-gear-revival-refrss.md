County Durham-born Rowan Atkinson is being tipped to host one of the BBC's best-known shows.

The Mr Bean and Blackadder star is among the names linked with Top Gear after the BBC confirmed the motoring show is returning.

Atkinson, 71, was born in Consett, County Durham, and grew up there before becoming one of Britain’s best-known comedy actors.

Away from Mr Bean, Blackadder and Johnny English, Atkinson is also a serious motoring enthusiast.

  Rowan Atkinson during filming for the Graham Norton Show


His collection has included a 1964 Ford Falcon Sprint, a 1939 BMW 328 Roadster and a Honda NSX.

He also owned a McLaren F1, crashed it twice and sold the supercar in 2015.

Atkinson’s television experience and passion for cars have put him among the contenders for the new-look Top Gear.

The London Evening Standard named the County Durham star as a possible addition to the line-up.

It reported: "Rowan Atkinson is another car enthusiast and owns a multimillion-pound collection, making him another possible contender for Top Gear."

Atkinson has appeared on the programme before.


He appeared as the Star in a Reasonably Priced Car in series 17, completing the Top Gear test track in 1 minute 42.2 seconds.

The time put him top of the celebrity leaderboard, showing his interest in cars goes beyond collecting them.

Atkinson also competes in historic motor racing and regularly attends the Goodwood Revival.

His name has emerged after the BBC confirmed that Top Gear will return following a lengthy absence.

  Rowan Atkinson has been put forward as a potential host of BBC's Top gear revival


The programme has been off screens since Andrew "Freddie" Flintoff suffered serious injuries in a crash while filming at Dunsfold Aerodrome in Surrey in December 2022.

Speaking at the Edinburgh TV Festival, BBC director of entertainment Ed Havard said: "There has been a lot of speculation about this, and we can confirm that Top Gear is coming back.

"I'm not going to talk about talent today."

He was specifically asked about comedian Jimmy Carr, who has also been linked with the presenting job.

Mr Havard said: "Jimmy would be a great host, but I won't be discussing that today.

"We will have more information on Top Gear shortly."

The BBC has not confirmed whether Atkinson, Carr or any of the other names linked with the programme are in talks to present the new series.
