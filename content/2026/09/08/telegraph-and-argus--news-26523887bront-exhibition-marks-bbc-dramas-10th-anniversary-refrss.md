The Brontë Parsonage Museum has launched an exhibition marking the 10th anniversary of the BBC drama 'To Walk Invisible', which told the story of the Brontë sisters.

Located at the Parsonage in Haworth the exhibition will run until December 31, 2026. It explores the making of the 2016 BBC drama, featuring costumes, props, set designs, and production notes.

Mia Ferullo, digital engagement officer at the Brontë Parsonage Museum, said: "Over the past ten years To Walk Invisible has become a film cherished by many Brontë fans.

"It offers an authentic, well-researched interpretation of the sisters and their lives.

"We wanted to celebrate this anniversary by showcasing the creative team's process including a behind the scenes look at the screenplay, set design and costume design."

The drama starred Finn Atkins as Charlotte Brontë, Charlie Murphy as Anne, Chloe Pirrie as Emily.

Four original costumes designed by Tom Pye are on display in the museum’s period rooms, including a dress for each sister placed around the dining room table where they famously wrote their novels.

An additional costume for Emily is exhibited in the kitchen.

Ms Ferullo said: "In the dining room, we have Emily's lightning print dress, worn in the film by Chloe Pirrie.

"There is a story that while shopping in Bradford, Emily had selected a material with a lightning print on it, sadly the original dress no longer exists.

"The costume designer Tom Pye liked this anecdote and decided to recreate it for the film."

She believes the costumes help visitors imagine the sisters as they once were.

Ms Ferullo said: "I think displaying the costumes can help visitors visualise the family within the space, you can imagine the sisters walking around the dining room table discussing their writings.

"The cast were all similar heights to the original Brontë family, so you also get an idea of the height difference between them."

Other artefacts from the drama on display include bonnets, Branwell’s top hat, and his nightshirt.

Visitors can also see a recreation of the "Gun Group Portrait" of the siblings, concept art by set designer Grant Montgomery, and a prototype model of the Parsonage set.

Ms Ferullo talked about director Sally Wainwrights aim to present the Brontë family in a way they hadn't been portrayed previously.

"She wanted to portray them as a real family, challenging some of the myths around them."

She praised the production team’s dedication to authenticity, noting that Mr Montgomery measured every flagstone in the original Parsonage to ensure the replica set was as accurate as possible.

It also sits alongside the museum’s ongoing exploration of Brontë screen adaptations.

Props from Emerald Fennell’s forthcoming version of Wuthering Heights are already on display, and the museum has previously hosted exhibitions on cinematic interpretations of the Brontë family’s work from around the world.
