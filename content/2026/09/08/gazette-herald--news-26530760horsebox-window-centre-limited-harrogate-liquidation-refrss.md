A North Yorkshire business specialising in horsebox windows has gone into liquidation owing more than £30,000.

Horsebox Window Centre Limited, which was incorporated in 2000, entered a creditors' voluntary liquidation on August 20.

Andrew James Nichols of Driffield-based Redman Nichols Butler was appointed as the liquidator.

The company, registered at 7 Princes Square, Harrogate, reported assets of £12,478.

  


A liquidators' report, known as a statement of affairs, also detailed the various debts.

The now-closed business, which had a manufacturing facility in Pudsey, had preferential creditors of an employee owed £492 and pension contributions of £1,077.

This left a surplus of £10,910 as regards preferential creditors - those who are paid first.

The report says HMRC as a secondary preferential creditor - those who are paid next - was owed £5,392.

This leaves a surplus of £4,518.

However, the statement of affairs also reports non-preferential claims - those who are paid last - of £34,914.

One employee was owed £20,336, and trade creditors were owed £8,761. HMRC was owed a further £4,749 and connected creditors were owed £1,068.

This gave a deficiency of £30,396. With the business having issued and called up capital of £153, this have a total deficiency of £30,549.

The report also noted Horsebox Window Centre Limited was placed into liquidation by its creditors.
