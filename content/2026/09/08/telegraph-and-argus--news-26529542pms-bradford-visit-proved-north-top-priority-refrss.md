*This week's MP column is written by Bradford West MP Naz Shah*

It was a huge honour to welcome the Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, to Bradford last month, so early into his premiership.

A visit from a Prime Minister renowned for his love of the North should not shock us, but it does reemphasise a shift in the dial towards a premiership where the North is a top priority.

The fact that Bradford West was at the top of the agenda proves his pledge to devolve politics away from Westminster and empower the North of England.

For areas like Bradford West, neglected through decades of Tory austerity and left competing for recognition in recent years, this is a welcome change.

This sea change will allow Bradford to showcase its real value and contribution, not just to the region but to the wider United Kingdom.

This visit followed the opening of Heron House in Manchester as a No.10 for the North.

But this is not just the politics of recognition, it is the politics of decentralisation: moving power away from a few in Westminster and Whitehall to those in our communities.

I am optimistic that Prime Minister Burnham will keep his promise to decentralise politics from London.

As someone who has previously been a Member of Parliament, a government minister and then the Mayor of Manchester, one of the fastest growing regional economies in the country, he knows what levers are needed to get investment and growth into every postcode.

   

*(Image: T&A)*

This is exactly what my constituents in Bradford West and the wider West Yorkshire community need and want.

During the visit, I accompanied Prime Minister Burnham to visit Midland Road, where thousands of tonnes of household and commercial waste have been dumped.

He walked the site with Environment Agency officials, witnessing the atrocious waste crimes taking place.

He later confirmed that West Yorkshire and Greater Manchester would pioneer region-led approaches to waste crime by increasing fines and penalties for fly-tipping - waste offences will rise up to £5,000, as well as up to nine points on your driving license - to ensure that clean-up costs do not continue to rise, and that local residents do not have to live in an unlivable environment anymore.

With the National Crime Agency and Environment Agency officers coordinating tighter surveillance at the waste site, the penalties put in place will ensure that those committing these crimes are held accountable.

This also means that instead of investing money into clean-up costs, it can be invested back into Bradford’s infrastructure, industries and local communities: something that I have been advocating for since I first became an MP.

Following Prime Minister Burnham’s visit to Bradford, the Midland Road waste site has now been named as one of just three illegal waste sites in the country being prioritised under the government’s new waste strategy.

This brings practical benefits for all of the 2,900 families in Bradford affected by the site.

I express my support for this initiative to the Prime Minister, as the children at the nursery next door can finally play outside without holding their breaths, and that local residents can take pride in their neighbourhoods without the sight of illegal dumps.

With the powers announced at Midland Road and the resources to use them, the sense of urgency is there, and I will ensure that the Prime Minister continues to advocate for a cleaner Bradford.

I used this opportunity to highlight the anti-social use of fireworks in Bradford, to which the Prime Minister responded with hopes to regulate them.

The Prime Minister also met with campaigners who have pushed for stricter regulations on fireworks in Bradford.

Subsequently, during his first Prime Minister’s Questions, he said that communities should not accept the anti-social use of fireworks as the norm.

For campaigners in my local community and local families, hearing about this issue being raised in Parliament brought a sense of relief, after years of writing to previous Prime Ministers and getting nowhere.

Prime Minister Burnham’s willingness to listen to those being directly affected by the issues he raises in Parliament proves to me that he is willing to put action behind his words, and proudly represent the people from the north of England.

The Prime Minister discussed the growing concern of wildfires, committing to supplying the fire and rescue services with adequate resources to face all extreme weather types as a national response, including in the North of England.

We went on to discuss the difficulties that farmers have faced in particular during the recent heatwave.

I met with the Environment Secretary Angela Eagle in Bradford, where she discussed her plans to provide extra flexibility to farmers, including more funding for reservoirs and more grazing areas for cattle.

I strongly believe that in the Prime Minister’s first month in his premiership, the people of Bradford can already be reassured that their issues are being dealt with with the backing of national agencies.

I have faith that the Prime Minister will see his promises through, by allowing local Mayors to take the initiative in their own areas, alongside the partnerships between national and regional agencies and councils.

My biggest priority is to restore trust in Bradford, tackle anti-social behaviour, and help local industries to thrive, and the Prime Minister intends to see that through with his government’s promises.
