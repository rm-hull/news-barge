Family and friends of a Bishop Auckland woman described as “one in a million” will run the Great North Run in her memory.

Bishop Auckland woman Shannon Hauxwell died aged 32 on New Year’s Eve, nearly a year after being diagnosed with aggressive mouth cancer.

Shannon’s best friend, Donna Goundry, 44, will run the half marathon this weekend for St Cuthbert’s Hospice, which cared for her in her final days, with Shannon’s brother, Paul.

Donna’s eight-year-old son, Joel, will run the Junior Great North Run on Saturday in memory of his godmother.

Shannon spent time at St Cuthbert’s Hospice, whose care inspired Donna and the family to raise money for the charity.

  Donna Goundry, 44, who was Shannon’s best friend, will run the half marathon for St Cuthbert’s Hospice this weekend 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Donna, 44, said: “Shannon spent time there when she was poorly and honestly, they were just absolutely amazing with her.

“They rely on donations to keep the charity going, so that was one of the reasons why we wanted to raise funds for them.

“Joel wanted to do the mini race on the Saturday as well. He was there throughout her journey when she was poorly. He is only eight, but that is what he decided he wanted to do for his godmother.”

Donna and Shannon became close after meeting through mutual friends and going on holiday together.

Their friendship lasted for years, and Shannon became Donna’s maid of honour at her wedding two years ago.

Shannon supported Donna through life’s biggest moments and was a much-loved godmother to Joel.

  Shannon and her godson Joel 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

She said: “After that holiday, we were just inseparable. Two years ago, she was my maid of honour at my wedding as well.

“For all she was younger than me, she looked after me. When Joel was born, she was just my lifeline.

“She looked after everybody. She was so selfless.

“She was honestly the most caring, thoughtful person, with the darkest sense of humour. The one-liners she used to come out with; she was so quick-witted.

“I know people say this when somebody passes away, but she genuinely was a one-off. She was the nicest person you could ever wish to meet, and she is such a big miss for all of us.”

Shannon was a supervisor at The Manor House care home in Barnard Castle, where her family said she devoted herself to caring for others.

  Left to right: Brother Paul and Shannon 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Since her death, family and friends have held fundraisers and found comfort in Bishop Auckland’s support.

Donna said: “It has been incredibly hard, but every time there is a fundraiser or an event, masses of people turn out to honour her memory and raise money for charities.

“Everything that we do in Shannon’s honour turns to gold because everybody thought so much of her.

“For all this year has been difficult, we have put our energies into things like that to keep us going. It has really helped us, although we all miss her terribly.”

Donna said the family had focused their energy on preparing for the Great North Run.

  Mum Jean (Left) and Shannon (Right) 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Donna has raised more than her £1,500 target, while Joel has beaten his £500 goal.

Donna said: “We are trying to do as much as we can for them because they were just so fantastic with Shannon. They were fantastic with us as a family.”

Donna and Joel have trained together around Bishop Auckland, while Paul has taken up running during their preparations.
