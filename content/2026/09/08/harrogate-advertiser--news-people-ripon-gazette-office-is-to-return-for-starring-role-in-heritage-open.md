# Ripon Gazette office is to return for starring role in Heritage Open Days but only briefly

## Ripon is gearing up for the annual Heritage Open Days this weekend with a rare chance for the public to step inside some of the city’s best-kept secrets.

England’s largest festival of history and culture will see Ripon throw open the doors of the city’s historic buildings, hidden corners and community spaces free of charge over four days from September 11-20 and explore places that are usually off-limits or easily overlooked.

Supported by Ripon Civic Society and Ripon BID, this year’s programme promises to be bigger and better than ever with two new venues joining in the fun – The Portly Pig bar and the Chapel and Hospital of St Anne.

Two of the, perhaps, most unusual events taking place in this year’s Ripon Heritage Open Days involve the former offices of the Ripon Gazette and a lost mural from a legendary Ripon butchers shop.

On Friday, September 11 at 10am the launch event will take place for a full-sized replica of the tiled mural that once was once part of Dewhurst Butchers at 6 Queen Street.

Ripon Civic Society has managed to track down butcher Peter Hartley from an article in the Ripon Gazette from 1983 at the time that the tiles were removed.

Meanwhile on Sunday and Wednesday afternoons, September 13 and 16, the Portly Pig at 5b Kirkgate will host a recreation upstairs of a news desk from the days when the Ripon Gazette had its office there.

Former Ripon Gazette reporter Peter Mudge will be on hand to encourage visitors to have a go at writing a short article and/or headline.

Another big draw for Ripon Heritage Days regulars will be the return of The Gazebo to the programme.

This charming and rarely-seen structure is only ever opened to the public during Heritage Open Days, making it a genuine once-a-year opportunity.

Anyone who has walked past and wondered what lies behind its walls now has their chance – but only for this brief window, so it’s well worth planning ahead.
