An arsonist who threatened to kill a man stood "proud" watching as flames tore through his home.

Emma Largan set a duvet alight, fled and then alerted neighbours to the fire at the North Yorkshire block of flats.

Teesside Crown Court heard how she caused thousands of pounds worth of damage to the social housing property.

Elisha Marsay said the trouble began on January 24, when the 45-year-old threatened to kill a neighbour during a lengthy call to police.

“She made repeated threats, refused to put the knife down and said she needed to be stopped from committing murder,” she said.

“Police attended and were greeted by the defendant who was continuing to make threats from her window.”

Largan eventually dropped the knife from the window before being arrested and charged, Miss Marsay said.

Miss Marsay said Largan had been drinking before making a series of threats and smashing her way into the flat.

“Outside the property, while flames were coming out of the window, the defendant remained calm,” she said. “She told people not to grass on her, was denying that she lit the fire but was also acting proud of herself.”

Largan, of Woodbine Close, Great Ayton, pleaded guilty to making threats to kill, burglary and arson while reckless as to whether life was endangered.

Annelise Haugstad, defending, said Largan had been in custody since her arrest on April 8 and was addressing her mental health problems and dependence on drink and drugs.

Judge Jonathan Carroll sentenced Largan to two years in prison, suspended for three years.

He said: “You went to your neighbour’s address in the early hours of the morning, you got into the address and you set fire to it.

“It is right to say, that after setting fire to the premises, you made the effort to alert other people to give them the opportunity to get out.”

Largan was also ordered to complete 25 rehabilitation activity days and undergo a 12-month drug rehabilitation programme.
