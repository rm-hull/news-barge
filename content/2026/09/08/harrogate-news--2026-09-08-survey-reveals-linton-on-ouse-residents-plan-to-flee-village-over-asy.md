**A major survey of Linton-on-Ouse residents** has revealed overwhelming opposition to Home Office proposals to house 1,200 male asylum seekers at the former**RAF Linton-on-Ouse** site, with a significant number of families stating they plan to move away if the scheme proceeds.

The data, compiled from 404 local responses, highlights deep community anxieties regarding safeguarding, a 150% population explosion in the small village of 800 residents, and an immediate negative impact on the local rental market.

## Key Findings Show Widespread Alarm

The community-led anonymous poll, which was strictly promoted within local resident groups to filter out external responses, yielded stark statistics on how the proposal is affecting the village fabric:

- **96% of respondents** stated they are deeply concerned about integrating a large, transient population of adult males directly into the heart of the village.
- **93% believe the proposal** will place an unsustainable strain on already-stretched local services, including healthcare, education, transport, and critical utility networks.
- **92% expressed severe concern** about public safety and crime, identifying an urgent requirement for additional policing resources that emergency services are currently neither funded nor prepared to provide.
- **Nearly half of all village renters** are preparing to leave, with 32% stating they are “very likely” to stop renting in the area and a further 15.9% actively seeking to terminate their leases immediately.

## Safeguarding Fears for Local Children

The survey detailed specific panic among local parents regarding the physical layout of the proposed open-door facility. The uncontained base sits less than 500 metres from the local primary school and under 200 metres from the community play park.

Among families with children, 35% are very concerned and 22% stated they will actively move their families out of the village if the asylum seekers arrive. For households with teenage children, 38% intend to significantly restrict their children’s daily freedoms, while 18% plan to uproot entirely.


**Olga Matthias, spokesperson for the Linton Action Group, said:**

People are deeply concerned for their children’s safety walking to school or waiting at the bus stop which is just metres from this open centre. The village has a high proportion of ex-military rented houses which have just been refurbished and rented and now this decision will just kill off the village. This survey shows residents aren’t merely worried, many will be forced to uproot their families, or withdraw their children from school. This plan is killing our village. It is an insane plan.

## Veteran Community Voices Direct Warnings

The Action Group has compiled a 25-page document filled with heartbreaking testimony from the survey’s optional comment box. One prominent anonymous account came from a local military veteran who recently moved their vulnerable, elderly in-laws onto Main Street.


**The resident said:**

My main concerns are for the safety and security of my family. In particular, my 13 year old son, whose life will completely change as a result. Also, I am concerned for the security and safety of my elderly and vulnerable in-laws who have recently moved to Main Street.

Their garden is open and allows for easy access. Finally, I am a veteran of 28 years military service and my son is a serving soldier. We have both been deployed to where many of the potential incumbents come from which could make my family an easy target for anyone looking for any type of retribution.



## Demands for Residential Housing Alternatives

Campaigners highlight that the Home Office is bypassing the exact local consultation mechanisms that Prime Minister Keir Starmer explicitly demanded when he publicly objected to using the exact same RAF Linton-on-Ouse site back in 2022. Furthermore, utility providers like Yorkshire Water have previously established that the local sewage, drainage, and electrical networks are running at maximum capacity and cannot support the site without multi-million-pound upgrades.

The Linton-on-Ouse Action Group is demanding that the Home Office immediately revoke the scheme to allow North Yorkshire Council to proceed with alternative plans to purchase the site for strategic local residential housebuilding.
