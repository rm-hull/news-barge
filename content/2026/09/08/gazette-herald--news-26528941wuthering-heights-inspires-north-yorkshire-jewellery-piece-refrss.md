A jeweller is capturing the spirit of Wuthering Heights in a new piece inspired by the Yorkshire landscape and the writing of Emily Brontë.

Joanne Gowan, who moved to North Yorkshire two years ago, is developing a piece based on the well-known “Cathy at the window” moment from Brontë’s classic novel, a design she hopes will become a future heirloom.

Ms Gowan will showcase the work-in-progress at an upcoming exhibition at Ampleforth Abbey, where visitors will have a chance to see the ongoing development of the piece.

She said: "I had the idea to produce a Wuthering Heights piece about two years ago, when I moved to North Yorkshire, celebrating the first connection I had with the area from reading the Bronte novels from the age of 10.

"This Emily Brontë piece sort of 'ate into' my subconscious on so many levels: spiritual ideas, eternal love, passion, the wild of the landscape and the contrast of good and evil, all wrapped into one."

She said the creative process has taken her from Yorkshire to Germany, adding: "Initially I took some images from illustrations of Cathy in early book editions; contacted my gem dealer in York ( R.M Weare ) who sourced a highly talented gemstone carver in Ida Oberstein, Germany.

"Then the rock crystal carving took over six months to develop, with lots of input back and forth during the cutting process."

The piece is designed to function as both a brooch and a pendant, but it is also intended to be displayed as a work of art.

It features a "window frame" of carved soapstone inspired by the box bed window at Ponden Hall near Haworth, believed to have influenced Brontë’s famous scene.

Ms Gowan said: "Cathy in rock crystal will appear to be coming through the leaded glass.

"The ash tree leaves knock against the window, also representing the moment when Nelly speaks to Heathcliff on the morning of Cathy's death and he leans against the ash tree.

"In the foreground on the windowsill is a sprig of yew with its toxic red berries, which was often planted in graveyards and represents the bridge between mortal death, eternal endurance, and the supernatural spirit.

"Alongside is a bunch of yellow crocus, the earliest spring flowers brought to Cathy's bedside by Edgar Linton, representing renewal and hope.

"Which ties to the end of the novel when the children of Cathy and of Heathcliff reach an eventual mutual connection."

The piece will be made from rock crystal, soapstone, silver, 18ct yellow and red gold, black rhodium plating, platinum, rubies and yellow sapphires.

Ms Gowan said: "It feels like the perfect time for me to create an iconic art piece of Wuthering Heights."
