A man with a history of violence towards two women defied a court ban by staying at the home of his latest victim, York magistrates heard.

As well as locking him up for 23 weeks, York magistrates made him subject to a two-year restraining order banning him from contacting the woman or going to her home.

But when police called on her on Sunday, September 6, Blaney answered the door and on his phone were a series of text conversations the woman and he had had some days earlier, said Georgina Fishwick, prosecuting.

They arrested him for breaching the restraining order and sent him in custody to York Magistrates Court.

The court was not shown the messages, but through his solicitor Kevin Blount, Blaney claimed they showed the woman wanted him to be in her home.

“She said she would get the order removed,” said the solicitor. “She is happy with him being at her home, and he thought it was ok for him to be there. She has initiated the circumstances of the breach.”

Blaney, 40, of no fixed address and formerly of Manchester, pleaded guilty to two breaches of the restraining order committed in August and on September 6 and possession of cannabis for his own use. The drug was found on him when he was arrested, the court heard.

Magistrates told him their colleagues in December had warned him about the consequences of contacting the woman or going to her home.

They ordered him to do 40 hours’ unpaid work under a 12-month community order and told him to pay £85 prosecution costs and a £114 statutory surcharge, totalling £199.

He had been on a suspended prison sentence for violence towards a second woman when he had assaulted the first woman and committed criminal damage to her phone in Whitby in December.

Mr Blount said after Blaney was released from prison, he was homeless and the woman had contacted him saying: “Come round I’ll help you out.”

It was not a case of the two of them resuming their relationship. They were just friends and only wanted to be friends, he said.

Magistrates reminded Blaney that the restraining order remains in force until Christmas Day in 2027.

Mr Blount said the onus was on the woman to get the order removed if she wanted further contact.
