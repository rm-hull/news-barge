Residents are demanding the removal of an "illegal" Quran burial site which suddenly appeared in an allotment on the outskirts of a West Yorkshire market town.

A mysterious marble structure - which is inscribed with Arabic writing - was first spotted by locals on Wood Nook Lane, in leafy Sowerby Bridge, Calderdale, earlier this year.

Rumours first suggested that the nine feet-by nine-feet memorial surrounded by nine pillars contained a body.

However residents now believe it is the centre piece of an "illegal" burial site for old Qurans - the text sacred to Muslims - called 'Rest Gardens'.

It is thought that thousands of Qurans have been buried in at least four large holes nearby in the last year.

*(Image: SWNS)*

TikTok videos posted on the landowner's account, now deleted, appear to show men using diggers to bury "thousands of Qurans" in a ritual known as Dafn.

In Islam burial is the preferred method to dispose of worn out Qurans which still remain sacred.

A petition created by Wood Nook Lane Residents Association to have it removed has been signed by over 1,200 locals.

It sits on barren private land which is earmarked as an allotment site, according to Calderdale Council.

Residents claim the Islamic holy books have been buried without the correct permission.

The Environment Agency declined to say if the landowners had permission to bury the Qurans.

But they added they are "working with local organisations to gather further information" and "will take action" if "wrongdoing" is found.

Some have even feared an illegal burial may have taken place before the "memorial" structure was built, a worry which councillors say they are "aware of".

Residents of Sowerby Bridge have called on council officials to exercise their planning powers to have the plot fully investigated.

On the petition, the resident group said: "This petition is not directed against any religion, faith, or community. We respect the right of all people to practice their beliefs lawfully.

"Our concern is that significant changes to land use and development should comply with the same planning and environmental standards that apply to everyone."

Other locals have branded it a "disgrace" and an "eyesore".

One local woman - who didn't want to be named - said: "[It is a] complete eyesore to local residents."

Another local said: "The place looks dreadful and it should be held to the same accountability as all other sites, regardless of what religion you are."

Another woman said: "Support this investigation. This is completely wrong."

One other said: "Yet another resident flagrantly abusing the rules."

Drone images show the massive memorial with nine standing columns, all supporting either a Union Jack or an English flag.

In the middle appears to be a raised plinth - also inscribed with Arabic writing - with a purple ceremonial cloth lying on top.

On the opposite side of the land, around 20 metres away, sits a large banner which reads: "The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings of Allaah be upon him) said: Gather us and pray for you."

Videos from the landowners' TikTok page showed alleged "quran burials" with the caption The Shaheed Quran Team.

Others show the landowners going door to door to collect Qurans in areas across Yorkshire.

The Shaheed Quran Team are a self-described group who collect damaged or used Qurans which they then "bury", according to a GoFundMe page.

Locals have claimed the landowners do not have the correct permission to bury the holy books on the land.

Calderdale Council served an enforcement notice to the landowners on July 17, 2026 saying there had been a "breach of planning control" in relation the structure.

Officials said that the shrine was visible form outside the site.

They added this makes it "out of keeping with the character of the allotment site and the surrounding landscape" and that "collectively they create a discordant form of development that fails to respect the character, appearance and local distinctiveness of the area".

But it has since been revealed that the landowners, Rest Gardens Limited, have appealed the enforcement notice to the Planning Inspectorate, according to it's appeals page.

Mohammed Khorshid is named as the director, according to Companies House.

This means the matter will now be considered through the formal planning appeal process and no action can be taken until it is complete.

Labour Councillor of Sowerby Bridge, Adam Wilkinson, has said that the Inspectorate have yet to "formally open" the appeal.

Cllr Wilkinson said: "I feel the frustration from residents who feel the planning rules are not being adhered too.

"I live locally, nobody wants an eyesore or an alleged illegal waste site nearby.

"I am aware of worries from residents about the Qurans being buried and the worry that an illegal burial may have taken place.

"I've urge those who are concerned about the latter to speak to the police.

"I've worked hard with our MP and cross party to ensure the site is being investigated by the relevant authorities.

"It's clear people are currently finding it too easy to avoid enforcement action - I welcome the government's moves to get a firmer grip of problem waste sites."

The Forestry Commission says they are "aware" of trees being knocked down and "officers have inspected the site" adding the case is "being assessed".

Reform Cllr Mike Payne, also of the Sowerby Bridge ward, has reported these worries to the Environment Agency.

A Calderdale Council spokesperson said they are "aware of the structure".

They added: “As the Council is actively involved in a planning enforcement process at present, including the serving of a planning enforcement notice, we are unable to comment further at this time.”

The Environment Agency added they are unable to share further details due to "possible legal implications".

They also said when more is found out about the site, a "lead organisation will be appointed".

The site owners have also been approached for comment on multiple occasions.

In a statement from the EA they said: “No community should have to live with unacceptable disturbance"

"We are working with local organisations to gather further information.

“If we find any evidence of wrongdoing, we will take action."

West Yorkshire Police have been approached for comment.

A Planning Inspector appointed by the Planning Inspectorate will now consider the company’s appeal and people can comment until October 5 – comments can be made by logging on to [https://appeal-planning-decision.service.gov.uk/comment-planning-appeal/appeals](https://appeal-planning-decision.service.gov.uk/comment-planning-appeal/appeals) and entering the reference number 6014342.

Coun Wilkinson, who has previously said it is “entirely reasonable for residents to raise concerns and for the relevant authorities to establish exactly what has taken place” is urging people to have their say.

In a message to constituents he said: “As previously mentioned, an appeal has been lodged against a council enforcement notice concerning the allotment site at the junction of Wood Nook Lane and Willow Terrace.

“The enforcement notice relates to the raised plinth, marble slab structure and around ten columns built without planning permission.

“The council’s view is that these features are out of keeping with the allotment setting and surrounding area, and required their removal.

“The occupier, Rest Gardens Limited, has now appealed this decision on several grounds, and the case will go to the Planning Inspectorate for a decision based on written representations.

“Want to have your say? Comments can be submitted online, quoting appeal reference 6014342. The deadline is October 5.”
