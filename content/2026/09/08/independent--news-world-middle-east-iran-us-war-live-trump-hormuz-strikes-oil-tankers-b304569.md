Iran has said it will unveil new sanctions for ships trying to pass through the Strait of Hormuz following a weekend of renewed exchanges with the United States, casting fresh doubt on progress towards an end to the six month conflict.

Mohsen Rezaei, the secretary of Iran’s supreme national security council, told state TV on Sunday that Tehran will announce a new ‘exclusion zone’ outside of the waterway in the coming days.

He said any ship seen entering the new restricted zone would be added to a sanctions list, reiterating that the waterway would only fully open again once the Americans “stop the sabotage, threats and attacks on Iran”.

Iran and the United States traded fire again over the weekend, forcing oil prices back up to $93 per barrel and driving Hormuz traffic down to its lowest since May.

The US claimed strikes on three Iranian oil tankers, including one off the coast of Kharg Island, after Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched ballistic missiles at two American naval vessels.

## Oil rises as Mideast conflict heightens supply worries

Oil prices extended gains today as risks of a prolonged conflict in the Middle East grew after Iran threatened to retaliate against any new U.S. attacks on its assets, heightening worries over supply disruption.

Brent crude futures climbed 34 cents, or 0.35 per cent, to $97.34 a barrel. US West Texas Intermediate crude was at $92.63 a barrel, up $1.15, or 1.26 per cent.

## Iran raises gas price again for its heaviest users

Iran introduced a higher price for its heaviest gasoline users, state media reported, the second such increase since December.

Gas price hikes can be sensitive, as seen in 2019 when an increase led to nationwide protests and a deadly crackdown.

The government did not specifically mention the war with the US as the reason for this latest increase but cited the "current situation."

Under the new system, people buying more than their quota of 110 liters of gasoline a month now must pay 100,000 rials (about 7 cents) per liter, or twice as much as they have been paying since December.

## Israeli airstrikes on southern Lebanon kill at least 12

Israel carried out airstrikes on the southern Lebanese village of Kfar Rumman, killing at least 12 people, including a paramedic, four women and two children, the health ministry said.

The strikes came during a new round of escalation between Israel and the Iran-backed Hezbollah. Israel's military said it struck several Hezbollah militants it accused of transferring weapons to Kfar Rumman.

The military also said it was reviewing the claim that "several uninvolved civilians were harmed" in overnight strikes across southern Lebanon, which it said were in response to Hezbollah militants launching explosive drones against soldiers in the area of the Ali Taher ridge.

## Iran threatens US with maritime exclusion zone

Iran has threatened the US with "economic warfare" and said it had fired an advanced missile at US warships, underscoring the risks of further escalation in the war only days after both sides traded blows again.

Mohsen Rezaei, the secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, reissued dual economic and military threats on X.

"In recent days, Washington has received a clear warning from Iran’s new missiles. Economic warfare will be met by a maritime exclusion zone across the Persian Gulf to the blockade perimeter. The operational posture toward US warships and bases has been fundamentally recalibrated," Rezaei said.

Rezaei was likely referring to the Qassem Basir ballistic missile that Iranian media reported was fired at US warships near the Strait of Hormuz. The US military said its warships evaded any missile attacks.

## Houthis say Saudi Arabia struck Yemen prison, killing child and 6 others

Houthi rebels in Yemen accused Saudi Arabia of striking a prison in the country's north, killing seven, including a child, as tensions escalate between the Iran-backed group and Saudi-backed Yemeni forces.

The strike on the Central Corrective Facility in the strategic city of Hazm in Jawf province also wounded at least seven people, including a woman, said Anees al-Asbahi, a spokesman for the Houthi-run health ministry.

The prison's warden told the Houthi al-Masirah news channel 35 prisoners and a woman who was visiting her husband were trapped under the rubble.

## Strait of Hormuz shipping drops, Trump claims the waterway is 'open'

Commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz dropped by 28 per cent last week, falling to 77 vessels as security concerns continue to disrupt Persian Gulf trade routes, according to data from analytics firm Kpler.

Beyond declining overall traffic, Kpler observed a distinct shift in navigation patterns. Between September 4 and September 6, 21 out of 28 transiting ships bypassed international corridors to use Iran’s unilateral routing scheme.

The data contradicts claims from US president Donald Trump, who has repeatedly asserted that the waterway is "open" and under total US control following mine-clearing operations earlier last week.

Maritime monitors note that while international shipping lanes are technically navigable, traffic remains severely depressed compared to pre-war levels, with commercial operators increasingly forced onto Iranian-managed routes to avoid security risks.

## Iraqi diplomat urges trade and tourism boost with Iran’s Ilam province

Iraq’s consul general in Kermanshah, Saadoun al-Saedi, has called for expanded commercial and tourism links with Iran’s Ilam province, citing strong geographical and cultural ties.

Speaking with Ilam Governor Ahmad Karami on Monday, al-Saedi emphasised that shared border heritage provides a strong framework to deepen economic, trade, and security cooperation.

The diplomatic push comes despite mounting US financial pressure on Iraqi banks aimed at curbing cross-border capital flows to Tehran.

Al-Saedi noted that efforts are underway to expand cross-border trade, urging regional officials from both nations to leverage proximity to drive commercial growth and regional stability.

## Houthis threaten retaliation against Saudi Arabia following air strikes

Continued Saudi military strikes against the country "will not go unanswered," Yemeni Armed Forces general Yahya Saree has said,

Speaking on Monday, Saree accused Saudi Arabia of ramping up its military campaign alongside an ongoing naval and air blockade.

## Tehran accuses IAEA of bias over calls for strategic nuclear data during war

State-linked Iranian outlet *Nournews* has launched a fierce attack on International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) chief Rafael Grossi, accusing the agency of taking sides by demanding sensitive nuclear data while refusing to condemn US and Israeli military strikes on Iranian facilities.

Grossi told a Board of Governors meeting in Vienna on Monday that the IAEA’s inability to verify safeguards obligations stems from a severe lack of information and access.

However, *Nournews* has argued that the loss of access was caused directly by enemy bombardment. 

Tehran maintains that demanding peacetime transparency without providing security guarantees or acknowledging the physical destruction of safeguarded infrastructure reveals systemic bias within the UN watchdog.

## Republicans in battleground states are ditching Trump from their campaign websites: reports

A number of Republican candidates facing tight races in November’s midterm elections have dropped mentions of President Donald Trump from their campaign websites, according to reports.

The president’s approval rating stands at just 33 percent in the latest *Financial Times*/Focaldata survey, with respondents again expressing concern over the state of the economy and the impact of Trump’s ongoing war with Iran on domestic gas prices.
