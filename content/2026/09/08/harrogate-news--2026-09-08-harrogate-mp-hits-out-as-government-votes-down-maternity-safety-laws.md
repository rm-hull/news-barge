**HARROGATE**— The Liberal Democrats have fiercely criticised the Government after ministers voted down a series of crucial amendments to the Health Bill designed to legally transform safety standards across British maternity services.The proposed legislative changes would have forced a legally binding duty on ministers to ensure every single NHS maternity unit in the country achieves a “Good” or “Outstanding” safety rating from the Care Quality Commission (CQC). The amendments also sought to mandate one-to-one midwifery care alongside 24/7 consultant presence on every active labour ward.

The rejection of the law comes amid skyrocketing national anxieties over the state of NHS maternity provisions. Recent independent inspectorate reports have exposed widespread systemic failings, crippling staffing shortages, and dangerous, inconsistent care standards across regional trusts.

## “Act Fast”: Local Realities for Yorkshire Mothers

Reacting to the parliamentary vote, **Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough**, warned that local families are facing harrowing risks due to failing regional infrastructure.


**Highlighting the immediate proximity of substandard care for his constituents, Mr Gordon said:**

If you’re pregnant in Yorkshire, there’s a very real prospect of having to attend an ‘inadequate’ maternity unit at Leeds Teaching Hospital NHS Trust if you come up against complications.

I’ve listened to constituents who’ve been affected by poor care at some of their most vulnerable moments and we need to act fast. We welcome the ongoing review into services, including at Leeds, as well as the creation of a Maternity and Neonatal Commissioner, which we’ve been calling for. But the government needs to keep going until this is the safest place in the world to have a baby.



## National Maternity Crisis Deemed “Unacceptable”

The political pushback was echoed on the frontbenches by **Liberal Democrat Health Spokesperson Helen Morgan MP**, who delivered a scathing assessment of the baseline safety across the country’s hospitals.


**Morgan said:**

65% of maternity units are unsafe.

More women are suffering severe bleeds. More women are suffering major injuries. More women are dying. How long will we continue to accept the unacceptable for mothers and babies?

By rejecting these amendments, the Government has turned its back on a vital opportunity to raise standards and rebuild trust. Safe maternity units should be the bare minimum, not an ambition ministers can simply brush aside.

The Liberal Democrats will continue to fight for the 1-1 midwifery, 24/7 consultant cover and the repairs to crumbling wards that we need to make Britain the safest place to have a baby. We won’t let this go.


## The Defeated Blueprint for Reform

The rejected clause, officially designated as **Amendment NC6**, put forward a strict operational recovery scheme. Under the blueprint, the Secretary of State would have been required to establish a funded framework within six months to overhaul struggling NHS trusts.

The statutory mandate specifically called for:

- **24/7 Consultant Presence:** Guaranteed obstetrician cover on every single labour ward;
- **One-to-One Midwifery:** Dedicated, individual clinical attention for every laboring mother;
- **Structural Leadership:** A designated Director of Midwifery embedded within every maternity service;
- **Ringfenced Funding:** Secure development cash streams protected from general hospital deficits;
- **Neonatal Staffing:** A dedicated national workforce plan specifically targeting newborn care.

The law would have additionally forced ministers to present an annual progress report to both Houses of Parliament evaluating the scheme’s real-world effectiveness.

## A Partial Concession at the Dispatch Box

Despite voting down the strict legal safety mandates, sustained pressure from opposition benches forced a significant late-night concession from frontbench ministers.

At the dispatch box, the Government committed to establishing a “statutory Maternity and Neonatal Commissioner” — a key role intended to provide independent oversight and champion patient safety across the healthcare system. However, opposition MPs warn that without binding targets on staffing and CQC ratings, a commissioner alone will face an uphill battle to turn the tide on a systemic crisis.
