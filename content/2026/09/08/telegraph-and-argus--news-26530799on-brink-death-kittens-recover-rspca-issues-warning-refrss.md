EIGHT critically ill kittens who were "on the brink of death" have made an incredible transformation in just two weeks.

The 14-week-old kittens—Bunny, Marshall, Nimbus, Xander, Woody, Ace, and Oslo—were brought to the RSPCA Halifax, Huddersfield and Bradford branch at the end of July after being found in a life-threatening condition.

They were covered in fleas, suffering from severe cat flu, and infected with Giardia—a parasite causing digestive issues, bloating, and diarrhoea.

Some were so poorly they had collapsed, while others had such sore eyes they could barely open them.

Fay Gibbons, digital manager at the branch, said: "When these kittens were brought into us, the state they were in was truly shocking—they were on the brink of death and we honestly weren’t sure if they’d make it.

"We see a lot of animals in truly horrific states, but it doesn’t get any less heartbreaking."

With intensive treatment and round-the-clock care in the branch’s isolation unit, the kittens made a remarkable recovery within just two weeks.

Ms Gibbons said: "It goes to show what love and care can do for an animal—their transformation has been amazing in such a short time, and we’re all so proud of how far they’ve come.

"Some recovered more quickly than others and some still need additional care, but none of them are suffering or in pain anymore and it’s been amazing to see them blossom."

  Nimbus has also made a full recovery (right picture) 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

Treatment for the litter cost the branch £1,800.

Despite the challenges, most of the kittens have now found new homes.

Nimbus, Xander, Oslo, and Neo have already been rehomed, while Bunny and Woody are set to leave soon.

Marshall and Ace, now nearly six months old, are still waiting for their forever homes.

The RSPCA is currently experiencing a "cat crisis," with more cats in care than dogs and limited space to house the growing number of felines in need.

The charity has reported a 67 per cent rise in multi-cat rescues in the past year, with 1,752 incidents involving 10 or more cats in a single property recorded in 2025.

This is a 67 per cent increase from 2024, and a 186 per cent rise compared to 2021.

The Halifax, Huddersfield and Bradford branch is currently undergoing its "Really Big ReFURb" project, which includes the development of a new cattery to increase capacity.

Ms Gibbons said: "Our new cattery will give us the space and facilities we need to continue saying ‘yes’ to cats and kittens who have nowhere else to turn."
