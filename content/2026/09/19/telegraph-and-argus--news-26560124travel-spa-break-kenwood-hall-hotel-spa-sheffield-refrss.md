THEY had me at ‘hot stones’. Invited to a spa package break at Kenwood Hall, I browsed the treatment options and decided that heated volcanic rocks would be just the thing to de-stress with.

I’d never had a hot stone massage before, and I was intrigued. How hot were these rocks? How would it feel to have them placed along my back and rubbed into my skin..?

Just one mile from Sheffield city centre, Kenwood Hall Hotel and Spa, stands in 12 acres of beautiful parkland.

It is well located for city breaks in Sheffield and the Peak District, and day trip attractions such as Chatsworth House.

The hotel is an impressive building, particularly the oldest part, built in 1845 as the home of Sheffield cutlery manufacturer George Wostenholm. After his death in 1876, the property passed to his widow, whose second husband added a significant extension in 1882.

In 1923 the estate was acquired by Kenwood Ltd and opened as a hotel. An additional bedroom wing was added. In the 1970s the Hotel St George was built in the grounds, and the two hotels were later combined to create Kenwood Hall Hotel. Today it’s a Mercure hotel, owned and operated by the Vine Hotel Group.

![]()The elegant hotel was originally a private Victorian house

The decor blends timeless elegance with contemporary chic. The snooker table artwork in the foyer is a nod to Sheffield’s heritage as the home of professional snooker.

After checking in, my friend Ruth and I wandered around the grounds and had a drink on the terrace overlooking the tranquil lake. It’s a peaceful haven, despite being just 10 minutes from the city centre.

If you like taking your dog along for mini breaks, there are dog-friendly rooms and some great walks - woodland strolls, winding paths and open lawns - in the grounds.

There are fun outdoor games too, including a giant chess set and Connect Four.

Our comfortable, spacious twin room, with a large double-bed each, overlooked the terrace and lake. Booked in for spa treatments the following morning, we enjoyed dinner in the restaurant, seated at the window with a lakeside view.

The newly designed menu - available in the Lakeside Bar and Gallery Restaurant - features locally sourced ingredients showcasing flavours of the region. Starters included butter chicken croquettes, salt and pepper squid, porcini mushroom arancini and panko halloumi fries.

We went for katsu cod goujons and buffalo cauliflower wings to share. The goujons, tender fish in a crunchy crust with a tasty spicy sauce, and battered cauliflower florets, in a zingy coating, were delicious, and just light enough.

Main courses included salmon fillet and new potatoes, chicken tikka masala and sweet potato and chickpea curry, as well as salads and rice bowls.

I chose sweet potato and red onion marmalade tart from the ‘signature dishes’ section (other options were a ribs and wings combo and lamb shank). The tart was lovely - melt-in-the-mouth short crust pastry, with a pumpkin seed topping, and potato gratin dauphinoise with steamed vegetables.

Ruth went for the ‘Build Your Own Burger’ option - step one: burger (beef, chargrilled chicken or plant), step two: topping (Monterey Jack or vegan cheese), step three: garnish and sauce. Served with fries and coleslaw, it got the thumbs-up from Ruth, who had the chicken option. “I don’t have burgers very often so it was a treat,” she smiled.

![]()'The best flavours of the region' are on the menu *(Image: Kenwood Hall Hotel)*

You can ‘build your own dessert’ too, with pancake, apple and raspberry tart or sticky toffee pudding layered with ice-cream, custard or cream and choices of sauces and toppings.

We were too full for dessert, but the ice-cream parlour looked impressive, with indulgent sundaes and milkshakes. The staff were friendly and efficient throughout our stay.

Next morning we enjoyed a hearty cooked breakfast before heading to the spa. The Health Club and Spa offers various treatments, including Swedish massage, facials, tinting and waxing. There are also specialised Maternity Massages and men’s grooming treatments.

The spa spans two floors, with cardiovascular and resistance machines upstairs, and a gym overlooking the lake.

![]()The hotel offers a range of spa treatments

I was a bit nervous about the hot stone therapy. I liked the idea of it, but wasn’t sure how it would actually feel.

Heat has long been used to ease muscle tension and pain; helping to increase blood flow to affected areas. The blend of heated stones and essential oils also aids circulation, improves energy levels and sleep and is said to reduce stress.

I was in safe hands with my therapist, Alison, who explained that this massage works by applying smooth, heated volcanic rocks on key points of the body, to “melt away tension and provide deep relaxation”.

Checking that the temperature was okay, Alison began to massage my back, neck and shoulders. It was warm and soothing, I soon felt relaxed. I’d expected to feel the stones placed on my back but it just felt like a really smooth massage. The heat was very soothing and I felt deeply relaxed. Heaven!

![]()Hot stone massage: Smooth, heated volcanic stones relieve muscle tension, aid circulation and help with sleep

Ruth went for the Indian Head Massage. Having never had a spa treatment before, she said therapist Molly put her at ease. Head massage works on pressure points on the head, neck and shoulders to release blocked tensions and stimulate circulation. “I felt so soothed afterwards, a bit floaty,” said Ruth, unwinding in the relaxation room. “I thoroughly enjoyed it. I’m now up for more pampering treatments!”

We followed the treatments with a swim in the pool and a dip in the Jacuzzi, steam room and sauna. Bathed in natural light, it’s a lovely, relaxing leisure space.

After a coffee in the bar we headed back, nicely chilled and refreshed. It had been a truly rejuvenating break, in a peaceful, tranquil setting. And only an hour or so from home, so no long journey or complex planning.
