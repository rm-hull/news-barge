A SCHOOL on Bradford's biggest estate has created a "calm oasis" for pupils but attendance and achievement remain areas requiring attention.

Carrwood Primary School, on Eversley Drive in Holme Wood, was inspected on June 30 - its first since joining the Leading Learners Multi Academy Trust in 2023.

Inspectors praised the school's work in supporting pupils' personal development and wellbeing, describing it as a particular strength.

They also found that teaching, the curriculum, early years provision, inclusion, leadership and governance were all performing at the expected standard, while safeguarding arrangements were found to be effective.

## Pastoral care praised

Inspectors singled out the school's pastoral support as a key strength, describing leaders' work with vulnerable families as "to be commended".

They said school leaders act swiftly to remove barriers to education and ensure families receive support when needed.

The report found relationships between staff and pupils are characterised by "warmth, trust and respect", while pupils benefit from a wide range of trips, visits, sports activities and cultural experiences.

Inspectors also praised opportunities for pupil leadership, including participation in pupil parliament and engagement with democratic processes.

Ofsted said: "The calm oasis that exists throughout the school helps to ensure pupils feel safe and nurtured, and allows them to thrive at school."

Headteacher Nicola Malt said the school was proud to see its nurturing approach recognised.

She said: "We are incredibly proud that inspectors recognised the strength of our pastoral care and the nurturing environment we have built for our pupils and their families.

"Care for our pupils extends well beyond the classroom, and it means a great deal to see that reflected in this report. A happy child that feels safe and welcome is one who is ready to learn!"

## Strong start for youngest pupils

Inspectors also praised the school's early years provision, saying leaders are determined to give children "the very best start in life".

The report highlighted a broad and ambitious curriculum, effective phonics teaching and a strong culture of inclusion, with pupils with special educational needs and disabilities receiving tailored support.

Staff told inspectors that Carrwood was "a very special place" where they feel they make a real difference to children's lives, while governors and trust leaders were praised for providing effective support and challenge.

## Achievement and attendance still a concern

Despite the positive findings, Ofsted judged both achievement and attendance as "needs attention".

Inspectors said published outcomes remain too low, with too many pupils, including disadvantaged pupils, achieving below expected levels in national tests.

While younger pupils are making stronger progress and catch-up support is having a positive impact, inspectors found that gaps in knowledge and foundational skills are not closing quickly enough in Key Stage 2.

Attendance was also highlighted as an ongoing challenge.

The report noted that too many pupils are regularly absent and that overall attendance remains below leaders' expectations despite efforts to improve it.

School absence stood at 9.3 per cent in 2024-25, compared with a national average of 5.2 per cent, while persistent absence was 34.2 per cent, significantly above the national figure of 13 per cent.

Inspectors acknowledged that leaders have a clear understanding of the issues and are already taking action.

Miss Malt said: "Inspectors recognised that we have an accurate and honest understanding of where we need to keep improving, particularly around attendance and outcomes at the end of Key Stage 2.

"We have already introduced additional maths lessons, handwriting support and catch-up activities, and we are working closely with families to improve attendance.

"We are determined to keep building on the strong foundations this report recognises."

## School 'on an upward trajectory'

Yvonne Brown, CEO of Leading Learners Multi Academy Trust, said the findings reflected the commitment of staff, pupils and families.

She said: "The Carrwood community should be rightly proud of their combined efforts to make the school such a special place where every child is seen, valued and included, as reflected in this report.

"The school is on an upward trajectory and is working with pace and purpose, supported by the Trust, to further strengthen attendance and attainment for all pupils."
