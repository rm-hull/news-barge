A family-run farm shop a few miles from the North Yorkshire border has come up with a simple way of showing customers exactly how local the food on its shelves really is.

Owen's Farm Shop & Café at Kirkby Stephen operates from Sandwath Farm, where Rob and Jill Owen have farmed since 2014.

Both come from local farming families and opened the shop and café as a way of selling produce from their own farm while showcasing food made by other producers from the surrounding area.

Among the products sold are the farm's own beef, lamb, pork and bacon.

But one of its more distinctive ideas is something it calls "Sandwath Miles".

Products from other businesses are labelled according to their proximity to Sandwath Farm, giving shoppers an immediate indication of how far the food has travelled before reaching the shelves.

That local-food philosophy runs through the wider business.

The farm shop sells meat produced at Sandwath alongside goods from local suppliers, while the adjoining café gives visitors somewhere to stop for breakfast, lunch or refreshments.

Customers have been particularly enthusiastic about the café's breakfasts.

Owen's currently has a 4.4 out of five Tripadvisor rating and is listed as the number one quick-bites venue in Kirkby Stephen on the site.

One customer who visited in May 2026 said it was among the "best all day breakfasts" they had eaten and particularly praised the flavour of the black pudding.

Another reviewer travelling between Lancashire and County Durham said they had driven the route for more than 30 years before finally following the farm-shop signs, describing Owen's as an "awesome find" and praising its soup, cheese scone and raspberry cheesecake.

A family visiting in August 2026 was similarly enthusiastic about lunch, highlighting the ploughman's, sausage roll, homemade quiche and cakes.

Other customers have described the café as somewhere they now intend to use regularly to break up journeys between the North East and Cumbria.

For Rob and Jill, the shop represents an extension of the farming tradition both grew up with.

They established the business to diversify the farm while giving customers direct access to the meat produced there.

The location adds to its appeal.

Kirkby Stephen sits in the Upper Eden Valley, close to the Yorkshire Dales and North Pennines, while the nearby A66 provides one of the main routes between County Durham, North Yorkshire and Cumbria.

That makes Owen's the sort of rural business which attracts both local customers and people travelling through the area.

Unlike a conventional supermarket, however, there is a much shorter story behind some of the produce.

The beef, lamb and pork sold by the business originates from the farm itself, while the Sandwath Miles labelling is designed to put a figure on the meaning of "local" for everything else.

It means shoppers are not simply presented with the word on packaging.

They can see how close to the farm a product was actually made.

Anyone making a special trip should check the latest opening times before setting off.
