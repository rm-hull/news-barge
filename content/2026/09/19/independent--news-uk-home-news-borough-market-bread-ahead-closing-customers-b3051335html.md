On a Wednesday afternoon in September, Borough Market is still as busy as ever. It is not difficult to see why – with offerings ranging from juniper-cured ham to chilli-stuffed Lebanese olives, it is one of London’s most-loved culinary hotspots.

The central London market promises to act as a beacon for sustainable food production and champion independent producers. But beneath it all, traders across the market suggest there is a growing sense of unease.

The feeling stems from the news that Bread Ahead – the famed artisan bakehouse – has been told to leave on the grounds of being “too successful”, according to founder Matthew Jones.

![Bread Ahead has attracted global attention for its unique takes on classic baked goods](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/17/8/47/Doughnuts.jpeg)

The bakery, which first appeared in this part of Southwark 13 years ago, was founded by the popularly termed ‘King of Doughnuts’, Mr Jones.

Specialising in avant-garde takes on classic baked goods, ranging from peach melba doughnuts to fig and roquefort sourdough pizzas, Bread Ahead has both garnered and sustained global attention for its decadent creations.

![Bread Ahead’s goods have received international attention](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/17/14/45/Bread-Ahead-Bakery-Kitchen-LR-91-(1).jpeg)

For many, the decision to shut down the bakery’s operations in its flagship brick-and-mortar location has sparked varying combinations of confusion, fear, and frustration.

Speaking to *The Independent*, Mr Jones warned of the precedent the move sets for local vendors.

“I think the decision sends a really strange message to the market,” Jones said. “That's what a lot of the other traders here have been saying – 'What about me? If I grow my business and become successful, does it mean I get thrown out?'

“Traders here are worried because a lot of them have got short-term leases, and they're always in fear of losing their lease.”

Other vendors who spoke to *The Independent* anonymously echoed a similarly stomach-churning question: how can a stall be closed for being “too popular”?

![Matthew Jones questioned what the decision means for the rest of the market](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/17/10/27/METTHEW.jpeg)

Dating back more than a millennium, Borough Market is run by a charitable trust governed by a board of volunteer trustees.

Traditionally dedicated to wholesale trade and independent food craft, securing a trader spot is notoriously competitive – applicants are vetted through a rigorous selection process that prioritises artisanal production, local sourcing, and environmental sustainability.

But in more recent years, the market has found itself managing increasing levels of congestion, massive queues, and a shifting consumer demographic looking for the next viral bite.

While long-term residents like Neal’s Yard Dairy and Kappacasein have anchored the market for decades, turnover among smaller pitches is built into the business model to encourage fresh talent and rotas of guest traders.

In market terms, Bread Ahead’s decade-plus tenure has placed it well beyond the category of a temporary guest pop-up. For Mr Jones, “Borough Market is home. It is where Bread Ahead began, and we want to stay.”

The sentiment is widespread.

Oskar Threlfall, a student visiting the market, was in the middle of biting into a creme brûlée doughnut when he said: “It does seem like they’re punishing someone for being successful – there must be something else behind it because why else would they get rid of something just for being too good?”

![Oskar Threlfall, right, questioned the logic behind the market’s plans to close the bakery](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/17/10/06/IMG_9949-2.jpeg)

Nichola Collete, a regular who has watched the market evolve, was equally baffled: “It seems like such a shame – it’s quite sad. I thought the whole point of Borough Market was to host independent places that make really good things. I just don’t get it; surely the market should want their stalls to be doing well?”

But for Amaan Faisal, another Bread Ahead customer, the proposed closing was less of a blow: “I do love Bread Ahead, but there’s quite a few of them around London.

“It’s sad they’ll have to close down, but it might give another independent place a chance to become just as popular.”

But not all customers agreed.

Adinda Vandeyken, a Belgian tourist who stumbled upon Bread Ahead’s doughy creations with her family on holiday, said: “It just sounds really weird. Why would it be shut down just because it’s doing well?”

![Bread Ahead when it first appeared at Borough Market](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/17/14/30/IMG-20240425-WA0032.jpeg)

Looking around, she added: “It’s not as if Bread Ahead is taking over the entire market. People come here to sample different stalls and walk around. It doesn't look like they're taking business away from anyone else.”

For Mr Jones, who started Bread Ahead as a small stand, the growing popularity of his bakery – which now hosts locations in Dubai, Riyadh, and Bangkok as well as seven branches in London – cannot be called anything but a success story.

But in Borough Market, a space that traders across the market say has built its reputation on independent hustle, something has changed. Success, it seems, may no longer be the recipe for survival.

![Adinda Vandeyken, right, expressed surprise at the market’s decision](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/17/10/49/IMG_9941.jpeg)

Borough Market told *The Independent* its terms do not allow its traders to enter franchises, or offer franchised goods for sale, and that a formal process is communicated to traders well in advance of a lease expiry date.

In a statement, the market added: “We apply this process fairly and consistently.

“The Borough Market Charitable Trust remains in contact with Bread Ahead, both directly and through its respective legal representatives, and discussions are ongoing about the business’s future at the market, including a potential departure date in 2028.

“We remain committed to engaging constructively with Bread Ahead as discussions about its future at the market continue.”
