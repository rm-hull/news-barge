AN AI platform that could transform drug development has been named a finalist in a prestigious competition.

ConsoneAI, based in Wetherby, Yorkshire, developed the technology to predict drug effectiveness across different genders and ethnic groups, and side effects in just 90 seconds.

The platform, named DioScor, uses artificial intelligence to model how new compounds might affect humans and animals, taking into account genetic, ethnic, and age-related factors.

Mo Kosar, founder and chief executive at ConsoneAI, said: "My team and I were extremely proud to be finalists in the RSC’s Emerging Technologies Competition;

"It was incredible to receive validation for the value of our work.

"It’s taking too long for essential drugs to get to market, and with our 90-second technology, our aim is to save as many lives as possible while reducing the dangerous gender and racial bias in drug research."

The technology was recently named a finalist in the Royal Society of Chemistry’s 2026 Emerging Technologies Competition, which recognises science-led solutions to global challenges.

ConsoneAI’s mission is deeply personal for Ms Kosar, whose family has experienced adverse drug reactions across three generations.

She said: "People are suffering because we still rely on a limited approach to medicine, where factors like gender, ethnicity, and body composition are treated as afterthoughts.

"This leads to dangerous dosing disparities, causing many to be under or over-dosed.

"Our family knows this pain all too well.

"Spanning three generations, we have experienced first-hand the adverse, sometimes devastating side effects of cancer treatments that simply weren’t designed with South Asian genetics in mind.

"We are on a mission to change this narrative – making drug development safer, more equitable, and fundamentally efficient for everyone."

Traditional drug development has often excluded women and underrepresented ethnic groups from clinical trials, resulting in treatments that may not be safe or effective for all populations.

DioScor aims to close this gap by predicting drug responses for a diverse range of people from the very beginning of the research process.

The platform could also help reduce the use of animal testing by mapping toxicity profiles from animals to humans.

In 2024, around 2.64 million animal experiments took place in the UK.

The Government has pledged £60 million to support alternative preclinical models, a figure higher than the cost of ConsoneAI’s technology.

The company’s innovation has drawn praise from industry leaders.

Ben Voysey, entrepreneurship manager at the Royal Society of Chemistry, said: "Our annual Emerging Technologies Competition aims to celebrate groundbreaking new technologies that address real-world problems, and we’ve been deeply impressed by the quality of this year's entries.

"ConsoneAI’s game-changing AI platform is not only an impressive technological innovation, but it also strongly aligns with the RSC’s commitment to overcoming systemic barriers and inequalities in modern-day science.

"We look forward to seeing this groundbreaking concept grow from strength to strength, with the potential to transform and improve lives on a global scale."

ConsoneAI is now working to further develop DioScor and expand its impact across the pharmaceutical industry.

The company hopes its technology will lead to safer, faster, and more inclusive drug development worldwide.
