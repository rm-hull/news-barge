Durham Jazz Festival says ticket sales are strong, with two performances sold out and several more nearing capacity ahead of next month’s first event.

The festival will bring three days of live jazz to venues across Durham city from Friday, October 23, to Sunday, October 25.

Organisers have called demand “fantastic” and urged audiences to book early.

![]()Emma Fisk's Hot Club Du Nord *(Image: Supplied)*

Alan Patrickson, one of the festival organisers, said: "The response to our first Durham Jazz Festival has been fantastic.

"Seeing Knats sell out following their Mercury Prize nomination is a real milestone, and we're now seeing several other shows getting close to capacity."

Newcastle jazz band Knats sold out their Fabio’s Bar show after being nominated for the 2026 Mercury Prize.

![]()Jo Harrop *(Image: Supplied)*

Their album, A Great Day In Newcastle, is shortlisted alongside releases by Olivia Dean, Paul McCartney and RAYE.

The sold-out show will take place on Saturday, October 24, two days after the Mercury Prize is awarded at Newcastle’s Utilita Arena.

Emma Fisk’s Hot Club du Nord is also sold out. The Friday lunchtime show celebrates the music of Stéphane Grappelli and Django Reinhardt.

![]()Left, John Lyons, Richard Turner and Alan Patrickson *(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Patrickson said the festival showcases local and national talent.

He said: "We created the festival to showcase the incredible jazz talent we have here in the North East alongside some of the most exciting artists from across the UK.

"We want Durham to be filled with live music, with something for dedicated jazz fans and people discovering the genre for the first time."

![]()Knats *(Image: Supplied)*

The festival will feature headline acts, established North East musicians and emerging talent.

A highlight is Ronnie Scott’s Jazz Club presents The Ronnie Scott’s Soho Songbook at Redhills Durham Miners’ Hall.

Durham-born vocalist Jo Harrop will return to the city to launch her album Love Now, Pay Later.

Other performers include Marvin Muoneké, a 2026 JazzFM Award nominee for Jazz Vocalist of the Year.

![]()Sula *(Image: Supplied)*

He will perform alongside Newcastle’s 19-piece Strictly Smokin’ Big Band.

The weekend will also feature Seb Rochford’s Finding Ways, The Pete Roth Trio with Bill Bruford, the Vasilis Xenopoulos and Paul Edis Quartet, and Janette Mason ReWired.

The festival will close with a special double bill at Redhills on Sunday evening, presented with Durham Brass Festival.

The bill features the Olivia Cuttill Quintet and King Pleasure & The Biscuit Boys.

Organiser Mr Lyons said the festival aims to appeal to seasoned jazz fans and newcomers.

He said: "The festival has been created to celebrate both the rich jazz talent of the North East and leading artists from across the UK, with a programme designed to appeal to dedicated jazz followers as well as audiences looking to discover the genre for the first time."

Mr Lyons also highlighted the range of venues and experiences on offer.

He said: "Performances will take place at a number of venues across Durham City, allowing audiences to move between venues and experience everything from intimate club-style gigs and late-night jams to big band performances and major headline shows."

Emerging acts include London jazz trio SULA, neo-soul artist Rivkala, vocalist Eleonora Kouneni and Newcastle collective SwanNek.

Tickets remain available for many shows, with 10 per cent off bookings for three or more eligible performances.

The Durham Jazz Festival website has the full line-up, artist details and ticket information.
