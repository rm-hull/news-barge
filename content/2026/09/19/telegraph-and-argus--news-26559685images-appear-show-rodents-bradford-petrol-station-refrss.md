Concerns have been raised after photographs appearing to show rodents near a Bradford petrol station were shared with the Telegraph & Argus.

The concerns relate to Phoenix Filling Station, 93 Listerhills Road, Bradford, where a resident said they had spotted rats near the site's waste storage area.

A resident contacted the newspaper claiming there were "so many rats" at the site and supplied a number of images and video footage.

The photographs appear to show several rodents in an area containing commercial waste bins behind the premises.

## Images show rodents near bin area

The images show large commercial waste bins in a service yard area.

In one photograph, a rodent appears to be visible on top of a waste bin among cardboard and other materials.

Another image appears to show at least two rodents on the ground near a drain cover between the bins and a traffic cone.

The photographs were taken during hours of darkness and show the rodents in close proximity to the waste storage area.

## Concerns raised by resident

The resident who provided the images claimed there were multiple rodents at the Listerhills road site.

The Telegraph & Argus approached the petrol station for comment after receiving the photographs and allegations.

During a telephone conversation, a spokesperson acknowledged a reported rat sighting and said pest control measures were in place at the premises.

They said: "We've got pest control here on site, and they come by regular to do that."

No further comment was provided.

## Pest control arrangements in place

The spokesperson said the business has a pest control contractor that attends the site on a regular basis.

The company did not comment directly on the photographs or on whether there had been any recent increase in rodent activity.

The Telegraph & Argus has seen multiple photographs which appear to show rodents in the vicinity of the site's waste bins.

## Why rodents are attracted to waste areas

Rodents are often attracted to locations where food waste, shelter and water sources are available.

Commercial premises typically use waste management and pest control measures to reduce the risk of infestations and manage any rodent activity.

The photographs prompted the resident to raise concerns about conditions at the site and contact the newspaper.
