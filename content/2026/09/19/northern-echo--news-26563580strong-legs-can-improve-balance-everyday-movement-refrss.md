**Our fitness expert Graham Low on how keeping your leg muscles strong can help with many everyday movements**

WHEN we talk about keeping fit, we often focus on our stomach, arms or weight. But there is one part of the body that deserves a lot more attention, and that is our legs.

Strong legs are important for so much more than exercise.

Think about your normal day. Getting out of bed, standing up from a chair, walking to the shops, climbing the stairs, getting in and out of the car and carrying the shopping all rely on your legs.

As we get older, we naturally lose some muscle and strength. This is a normal part of ageing, but it does not mean we have to accept becoming weaker.

Regular strength training can help maintain the muscle we have and make everyday movement much easier.

One of the simplest exercises is the squat. You do not need to use heavy weights. Even sitting down onto a chair and standing back up again is a great way to work your legs.

Step ups are another good exercise. Using a step or a low platform, you can work your legs while also challenging your balance.

Calf raises are often forgotten, but they are useful too. Strong calf muscles help with walking, climbing stairs and keeping you steady on your feet.

Strong legs can also help with balance. Your legs play a big part in keeping you stable when you walk, change direction or step over something.

This is particularly important because losing confidence in your balance can sometimes make people less active. They may stop going for walks or avoid certain activities because they are worried about falling.

The less we move, the weaker we can become.

It can become a bit of a cycle.

That is why keeping your legs strong is so important.

You do not need to spend hours in the gym. Two or three short strength sessions each week can make a real difference.

Walking is also fantastic. Try to get outside every day, even if it is just for a short walk. Hills and stairs can provide an extra challenge if you feel comfortable doing them.

And remember, strength is not just about exercise.

Eating enough protein gives your muscles the fuel they need to repair and stay strong. Foods such as eggs, chicken, fish, lean meat, Greek yoghurt, beans and lentils are all good choices.

The goal is not to have huge muscles or lift the heaviest weights. It is about staying strong enough to do the things you enjoy.

Strong legs can help you stay mobile, confident and independent for longer, and they are doing more for you than you probably realise.
