# Yorkshire fly-tipper snared after investigators plant GPS tracker inside hot tub

## A fly-tipper was snared after council investigators secretly planted a GPS tracker inside a hot tub during an undercover sting.

Kacper Kruk, 26, was caught after the device led officers straight to a street in Wakefield, West Yorks., where the rubbish had been illegally dumped.

He has now been ordered to pay almost £2,500 after admitting two waste offences.

Kirklees Magistrates' Court heard Wakefield Council launched an investigation in March after officers discovered fly-tipped rubbish in Castleford.

They traced the waste back to a resident who had paid what he believed was a legitimate company to take it away.

Anti-social behaviour officers obtained the firm's phone number and contacted it on WhatsApp, posing as customers who needed rubbish collecting.

A pick-up was arranged from a property in Castleford, West Yorks., on April 16.

But before the collection, investigators planted a tracking device inside the lid of a discarded hot tub and marked the rubbish so they could identify it later.

Kruk and another man then arrived in a white van.

He was handed £200 for the job and gave officers a business card before driving away with the waste.

Investigators watched the tracker as it moved across the area.

Just 45 minutes later, it came to a stop in Wakefield, where officers found the rubbish dumped beside household bins.

Investigators then set up a second sting using the phone number on the business card Kruk had handed over.

Kruk turned up again and quoted £400 to remove another load of rubbish.

But this time, officers were waiting and apprehended him.

Kruk, of Thornes Lane, Wakefield, later admitted owning the van and being present when the first load was collected.

He also admitted he did not have the waste carrier's licence needed to carry out the work.

Kruk pleaded guilty to depositing controlled waste without an environmental permit and transporting controlled waste without a licence.

He was ordered to pay a total of £2,421, including a £480 fine, £1,456 court costs and £292 compensation to the council.
