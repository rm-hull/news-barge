A York dentist has been struck off following a series of professional failings including poor hygiene during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Jenefar Kabir, a dentist with more than 20 years of experience, risked harming patients as she put social media and business growth above their safety, a professional disciplinary tribunal decided.

The committee members in their decision said Ms Kabir’s misconduct involved serious and wide-ranging breaches of professional standards, that involved medication, record keeping, patient consent, antibiotic prescribing, staff checks, and the treatment of colleagues.

She had contested the allegations.

Staff working at her practice Fresh Dental Smiles in Rawcliffe left because of what they experienced, the committee heard.

A dentist described experiencing immense stress and said staying at the practice would have risked her professional reputation.

A dental nurse said she was horrified by what she saw and left out of fear that association with the practice would harm her own career.

A dental hygienist felt so unsafe that she chose to make herself unemployed rather than continue working there.

A young trainee said the experience left her anxious and changed her desire to pursue a career in dentistry.

The decision sent to Ms Kabir said: "In the committee’s view, you had prioritised your personal motivation for expanding your business and increasing your practice’s social media presence over putting your patients’ best interests first.

It said that her actions represented a serious departure from professional standards for infection control, clinical practice, and behaviour towards colleagues.

The committee said: "Your misconduct brought the profession into disrepute and breached fundamental tenets of the profession."

It concluded there was a risk Ms Kabir could repeat the behaviour in future due to her ongoing lack of insight.

Infection control failures were described as particularly concerning by the committee.

The decision said: "These were not simply administrative oversights or failures.

"You personally reused single-use items and also instructed or permitted staff to reuse clinical gloves and other items, and permitted disposable masks to be used for inappropriately long periods."

The failures occurred during the Covid-19 pandemic when infection control standards were especially critical, said the committee.

The committee also said she showed a persistent lack of insight into the impact of her failings, including employing staff without proper checks and misconduct towards colleagues.

"The misconduct was not confined to an isolated reaction on a single occasion but occurred over a period of time and affected several members of staff."

While the committee acknowledged Ms Kabir faced personal challenges at the time, it said these did not excuse the full extent of her failings.

Ms Kabir’s actions took place mainly in late 2021 and early 2022.

Fresh Dental Smiles Ltd folded in January 2023 after a complaint about Ms Kabir was made to the General Dental Council.

Allegations that she had used rusted and blunt drill bits and used a high speed drill inappropriately were dismissed or dropped before the hearing.

Neil Sheldon, representing Ms Kabir, said she had no previous adverse findings in a 24-year career and was regarded as a competent dentist with experience in complex cases.

Mr Sheldon said there was considerable evidence before the committee to demonstrate that she was a sound and highly performing dentist who was highly regarded and who has carried out more complex dentistry without any reported concerns.

He said Ms Kabir had reflected on the case and taken steps to address the concerns raised.

Mr Sheldon said the four-year wait for the hearing had taken a personal and professional toll on her.

He said: "There is a strong public interest in maintaining the services of an otherwise experienced and competent dentist."
