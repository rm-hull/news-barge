*Five generations were united this week after baby Archie was flown 11,500 miles to Darlington to meet his Great-Great Grandma Molly.* * PETER BARRON**shared the joy*

AT 104, Molly Ingham has seen more momentous events in her life than most of us could ever dream of – but meeting her great-great grandson, Archie, was “extra-special”.

Archie was flown 11,500 miles from his home in New Zealand to meet Molly for the first time, in a family gathering that brought five generations together.

“Who’d have thought it? I honestly never thought I’d see this day – it's really quite something, isn't it?” said Molly, as she cradled Archie on her knee at Elderwood Care Home, in Darlington.

"It's a very long way for them to come, but I've been really looking forward to it – just having all the family together," she added.

Molly, who is looking forward to her 105th birthday on November 7, has had quite a life.

She’s reputed to have been the first woman to learn to drive a car in Darlington – a skill that proved invaluable during her war service when she became a driver for British Officers, including General Montgomery.

When she reached 100, she was chosen to be the “poster girl” of the flu jab campaign in Darlington.

And, on her 104th birthday last year, she made national headlines by revealing she’d once turned down a young Tom Jones for a dance when he was appearing at La Bamba nightclub in her hometown.

However, nothing tops meeting the latest member of her family. “It’s extra-special – he’s so lovely,” she smiled.

Alongside the oldest and youngest members of the family, this week’s remarkable gathering of five generations included Molly’s eldest daughter, Diana Chapman; granddaughter, Alison Gamble; and great-granddaughter, Rachel Pollock, who now lives in New Zealand.

![]()Five generations: Molly and Archie with Molly's eldest daughter, Diana Chapman; granddaughter, Alison Gamble; and great-granddaughter, Rachel Pollock *(Image: Chris Barron)*

Rachel, Archie’s mum, flew back to England with her husband, John, to visit her family and introduce the new addition, who is now nearly six months old.

“It’s hugely important for me to see my family, but this trip has been extra-special because it’s Archie’s first visit,” said Rachel, a teacher, who grew up in Croft-on-Tees.

“The biggest downside of living so far away is missing family, but it’s been lovely to see everyone.

"Molly was so excited to meet Archie – it was quite emotional seeing them together for the first time. Hopefully, we can come back in the not too distant future.”

![]()The moment Molly first met Archie *(Image: Alison Gamble)*

Rachel went to New Zealand on a working holiday in 2014, and settled on the North Island after meeting John, who works in the fuel industry.

Under her maiden name, Rachel Gamble-Flint, she became an accomplished rower while at Yarm School and went on to row for Great Britain.

Rachel's great-grandma, known as Nan, started life as Molly Marr, in Haughton-le-Skerne, on the edge of Darlington. She later moved to the Drinkfield area of the town, where her family kept a smallholding, with pigs and chickens.

She learned to drive at 16 when her then boyfriend, Ralph Ingham, provided her with a Riley car he’d cobbled together from old parts. The couple went on to marry and lived happily in Hurworth Place, and then over the river at Croft-on-Tees, until Ralph passed away in 1985.

Molly and Ralph had three daughters – Diana, Barbara, and Heather, who recently passed away. All three daughters were county swimmers, and Molly served for many years as President of Darlington Swimming Club.

She also has six grandchildren, nine great-grandchildren – and now a great-great grandchild!

"It's been incredible to have five generations of the family together," said Molly's granddaughter, Alison Gamble. "I think it was a bit overwhelming for Molly at first but I'm sure it meant the world to her."

![]()The five generations are joined by Rachel's husband, John, and Diana's husband, Barry *(Image: Chris Barron)*

Molly is clearly still enjoying life; taking part in games of cards, bingo, and 'Sing With The Stars' sessions at the care home.

Has she any lasting ambitions? "Well, I'd quite like to go on a cruise – anywhere the sun's shining will do," she said.

In the meantime, she's looking forward to her 105th birthday when she is due to receive a birthday card from King Charles to go with the one she received from Queen Elizabeth when she was 100.

"Not many get birthday cards from a King and Queen do they?" she suggested.

"Mind you, this is the best present," she added, giving Archie another cuddle.
