A quirky tearoom tucked away in one of North Yorkshire's best-known fishing villages has been recognised by Tripadvisor for 2026 after attracting more than 1,100 reviews.

Dotty's Vintage Tearoom sits on the High Street in Staithes, the picturesque coastal village between Whitby and Redcar.

Inside, the café embraces a vintage theme, with tea served in traditional china alongside homemade cakes, scones, lunches and locally roasted coffee.

It currently holds an impressive 4.8 out of five rating from 1,173 Tripadvisor reviews and is listed as the number one coffee and tea venue in Staithes.

![]()Inside Dotty's Vintage Tearoom *(Image: TRIPADVISOR)*

The tearoom has also received Tripadvisor's Travellers' Choice award for 2026.

The awards recognise businesses which consistently receive strong reviews from travellers and rank among the top-rated listings on the platform.

Dotty's describes itself as being right in the heart of the village and offers both indoor and outside seating.

Recent visitors have repeatedly highlighted its unusual interior, homemade cakes, generous portions and vintage crockery.

One customer visiting in September described finding a “real buzzing atmosphere”, while another summer visitor praised the tearoom's retro surroundings and generous lunches.

A visitor in May called it a “very quirky place” after returning to the café several times, praising its bacon rolls, coffee and cakes.

Meanwhile, another customer visiting the North York Moors with family in April said Dotty's had been their favourite tearoom of the trip after sampling cafés in several different places.

Scones appear particularly frequently in the reviews, alongside hot chocolates, toasties, cakes and pots of Yorkshire tea.

The café is also dog-friendly, with furry friends welcomed inside as well as in its outdoor seating area.

Its setting is likely to be part of its popularity.

Staithes is characterised by its tightly packed cottages and narrow streets running down towards the harbour, with Dotty's sitting directly on the village's High Street.

That does mean visitors arriving by car will generally need to park above the old village and walk down into Staithes before making the return climb afterwards.
