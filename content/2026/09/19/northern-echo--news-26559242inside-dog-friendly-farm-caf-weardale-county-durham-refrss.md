Tucked away in the Weardale countryside is a farm café where visitors can eat beside a wood-burning stove, browse vintage curios and even find something for their dog.

Bradley Burn Farm Café is based on the A689 around two miles east of Wolsingham in County Durham and occupies a large log-cabin-style building at Bradley Burn Farm.

Its timber interior gives the café a very different appearance from the typical high-street coffee shop, with a stove providing extra warmth during the colder months.

In warmer weather, there is also outside seating.

The café specialises in homemade dishes and baked goods, with scones repeatedly appearing among the things customers recommend.

Bradley Burn currently has a four out of five rating from Tripadvisor reviews, although more recent Google reviews aggregated elsewhere are considerably stronger.

A recent customer described it as a good place for a "delicious cheese scone", a light snack or cake and particularly praised how dog-friendly it was.

Another recent reviewer praised the café as clean and comfortable, with sensible prices, pleasant staff and the added attraction of antiques and collectables.

One visitor in January 2026 simply highlighted the "lovely food and coffee" and friendly staff, while another called it a quirky country café and curios shop and praised the dog-walking field beside it.

Longer-standing Tripadvisor customers have also singled out the homemade food.

One reviewer who had visited on several occasions praised the soup and cheese scones and described the atmosphere as relaxed, while another said the scones they ordered with coffee were particularly good.

Food is only part of Bradley Burn's unusual appeal.

The same building is home to a vintage and curios operation, meaning customers can browse old and unusual items before or after stopping for refreshments.

The café is also dog-friendly, which makes it particularly suited to people exploring Weardale with their pets.

Its rural setting puts it close to some of the dale's best-known scenery and walking country.

Wolsingham, a short distance away, acts as one of the gateways into upper Weardale, while the A689 continues west through villages including Frosterley, Stanhope and St John's Chapel.

That makes Bradley Burn an easy place to overlook while travelling through the dale.

Inside, however, the combination is unusual enough to distinguish it from a conventional roadside café.

Visitors can step inside a timber cabin, eat homemade food, look through vintage curios and bring their dog along at the same time.

For a small café sitting beside one of Weardale's main roads, Bradley Burn has developed a character all of its own.

Anyone planning a special visit should check the business's latest opening days and hours before travelling.
