A POPULAR restaurant has thanked its customers for all their tributes after a its well-known owner died.

Back in June, the Telegraph & Argus reported that Shabbir Ahmed Laher, founder of Samarkand Restaurant on High Street in Heckmondwike, had passed away peacefully at home.

Known affectionately as Sammy, he founded the restaurant in 1986 and spent more than 35 years building it into what it is today.

## 'We honestly don’t know where to begin'

In a recent tribute shared on social media, a spokesperson for the restaurant said: "We honestly don’t know where to begin, except to say thank you from the bottom of our hearts.

"Since losing our beloved Dad, Sammy, we have been overwhelmed by the incredible number of messages, comments, memories and tributes that everyone has shared with us.

"We have read every single one, and although this is an incredibly painful time for our family, your beautiful words have brought us so much comfort."

They described Mr Laher's lifelong dedication to the restaurant, and said: "For over 35 years, Dad dedicated his heart and soul to Samarkand.

"To him, it was never just a restaurant.

"It was his passion, his hard work, his pride, and such a huge part of his life."

## A place where customers became genuine friends

The spokesperson continued: "So many of you started as customers but became friends over the years.

"Some of you have been coming to Samarkand for decades, and hearing your stories about Dad has touched us more than we could ever explain.

"Knowing that Sammy made such a lasting impression on so many people means more to our family than you will ever know."

## 'Sammy was a special man'

Comments flooded in from customers too.

One person wrote: "Sammy was a special man.

"Many years ago, I took my son in there when he was at Hecky grammar school.

"He was doing a sponsored event for school.

"After our meal, I paid our bill, then Sammy sponsored my son for more than I had just paid.

"The world needs more people like Sammy."

Another former employee recalled Mr Laher's huge influence on their life.

They said: "Started work at Samarkand in 1992 at the age of 18 wasn’t just my first job – it was my initiation into the real world.

"I owe so much of my growth to his mentorship.

"Sammy will always hold a sacred place in my memory, cherished not just for what he taught me, but for the extraordinary person he was."
