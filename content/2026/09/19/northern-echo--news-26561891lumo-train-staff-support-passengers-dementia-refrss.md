A North East rail operator has pledged to train every member of staff to support passengers with dementia and make rail travel less daunting.

Newcastle-based Lumo is the first UK train company to partner with the Alzheimer’s Society to train its entire workforce in dementia awareness.

The Dementia Friends Sessions will help staff understand dementia and support passengers affected by it.

Amanda Spivack, regional fundraiser for the Alzheimer’s Society covering the North East and Scotland, said: "Train travel can be particularly challenging for those living with dementia.

![]()Newcastle rail operator Lumo has pledged to ensure every single employee is equipped to help passengers living with dementia travel more confidently on its trains *(Image: North News & Pictures)*

"Disruption or unexpected last-minute changes to train times can be especially disorientating and unsettling, not just for people living with dementia, but for those travelling with them.

"At the same time, a dementia diagnosis impacts the whole family and can create a sense of isolation – not just for those living with the condition, but also for their families and carers."

Lumo will give all employees the one-hour Dementia Friends Sessions by May 2027.

The first sessions will begin on September 21, World Alzheimer’s Day.

More than 41,000 people live with dementia in the North East, among around one million across the UK.

Dementia can affect memory, thinking and behaviour, making train travel especially challenging.

Ms Spivack said: "These sessions will equip Lumo colleagues with the knowledge and confidence to ensure that rail journeys are accessible and improve the whole travel experience for people living with dementia.

"We are delighted to see Lumo becoming one of the leading rail operators to engage their teams in our Dementia Friends programme.

"By investing in staff awareness and understanding, Lumo is demonstrating their commitment to creating a more inclusive and dementia-friendly travel experience.

"For many people living with dementia, accessible rail travel helps maintain independence and social connections, and we look forward to seeing other rail operators across the region follow their lead."

The sessions will raise awareness, reduce stigma and show staff how small steps can make a big difference to passengers with dementia.

Lumo says the training will teach staff to spot when a passenger may have dementia and offer help in clear, calm, simple language.

It also stresses patience and empathy when passengers are confused, upset or repeating questions.

Lindsay Gauntlett, head of people and culture at Lumo, said: "The dementia initiative is the next step for us.

"We have already done work helping to support passengers with ADHD and autism, and this is part of a broader inclusivity approach.

"We recognise that dementia, and caring for those with the condition, can be isolating and encourage people affected to travel.

"The message is, whoever you are, we will support you to travel with Lumo.

"The aim is to help people feel confident enough to travel to see friends and family, with Lumo providing a supportive environment.

"We're proud to be collaborating with Alzheimer’s Society, and to be leading the way on inclusivity within the industry."

All 250 or so of Lumo’s employees will take part in the training.
