STUDENTS are being warned of lesser-known driving offences that could cost them their licence.

With nearly two million undergraduates across the UK preparing to return to university, legal experts are highlighting four driving laws that could catch students off guard and lead to hefty fines or penalty points.

Abbie Upton, a criminal law expert at Richard Nelson LLP, said: "Before setting off on a long journey, drivers should make sure luggage and other belongings are properly secured and cannot move around the vehicle."

One of the lesser-known offences involves overloading a vehicle with belongings, which could breach the Highway Code if items obstruct the driver’s view or are not properly secured.

This could result in a fine of up to £1,000 and three penalty points.

Another risk is using a mobile phone in a drive-through queue.

While drivers are permitted to use a phone for contactless payment at a terminal while stationary, using it to check messages or change music is illegal and could result in six penalty points and a £200 fixed penalty.

Ms Upton said: "Mobile phone rules are an area where motorists can easily become confused, particularly when they are stationary.

"The safest approach is always to avoid handling your phone while driving and to make sure you understand when an exception does, and does not, apply."

Splashing pedestrians by driving through a puddle can also land drivers in trouble, with potential fines of up to £5,000 if the case goes to court.

Parking on pavements to unload boxes, especially outside student accommodation, is another common mistake.

In London and many other areas, pavement parking is banned and can result in a penalty charge notice of up to £130.

Ms Upton warned that new drivers are particularly at risk.

She said: "A high proportion of student drivers are still within their two-year probationary period.

"What many fail to appreciate is that a single infraction can cost you your licence.

"One six-point mistake, like touching your mobile phone in a drive-through queue, is enough to revoke your driving privileges immediately."
