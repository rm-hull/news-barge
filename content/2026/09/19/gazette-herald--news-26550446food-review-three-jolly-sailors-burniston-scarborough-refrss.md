Can you get a good pub meal in Scarborough?

I’ve been coming to this Queen of East Coast resorts for more than 50 years and I don’t recall ever having a pub lunch there- at least in the town centre.

Fish and chips? Yes! Mother Hubbard’s in Westborough was a regular favourite and more recently TripAdvisor led us to the Lifeboat Fish Bar, Fish and Chip Café and Take Away on the bottom of Eastborough and they too were excellent.

And Scarborough also has many decent restaurants.




But for pubs, eating out tended to be further out of town.

![]()The pan seared tuna steak had a pleasing tanginess about it. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

As a kid, I recall many a happy Sunday lunch at the Downe Arms Hotel at Wykeham, when we were caravanning at the nearby St Helens in the Park. It was the 1970s then and melon boat was the height of sophistication! We called back a few years ago and Sunday lunch was still good.

![]()The hake was delicious, with crunchy, crispy batter. Yum yum! *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

In more recent times, a drive out towards Whitby would take you through Burniston, past the Oak Wheel Pub on the Coastal Road and at the junction of the main A165 and A171 you would see the Three Jolly Sailors.

Both pubs are a decent size, especially the Three Jolly Sailors, having a dominant location by the junction. So in the pre-internet era, a full car park was a good sign that either venue were good, and they were, and that is how you did find a good pub, back in the day. Over the years neither have disappointed.

![]()Dad's roast chicken dinner is hidden under a huge Yorkshire Pudding *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Both venues continue to get good reviews online. The Three Jolly Sailors gets 4.5 stars out of five with Google and 4.3 stars out of five with TripAdvisor, and the Oak Wheel 4.5 stars on Google, 4.2 stars on TripAdvisor.

![]()More than enough for one! *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Now, it just so happened that this week, I have been staying in Burniston, in a cottage in the high street, literally over the road from the Three Jolly Sailors.

I called into the thriving inn with mum and dad moments after having a pint at the Bryherstones Country Inn at nearby Cloughton. There, the interior was traditional, the food looked lovely, it has rave reviews, and the staff were welcoming. We said we would return.

![]()Plenty of veg! *(Image: Darren)*

Anyway, at the Three Jolly Sailors, we were shown to our table in a large dining area, which was quite busy, with plenty of atmosphere. But as it was nearly 4pm the Sunday lunch only had the roast chicken left. Normally, there is also beef, pork and a 'nutless roast'.

However, the friendly server said the extensive ‘main’ menu was also available. That menu had a massive choice with all the pub classics and favourites, plus a few innovations. There was also a specials board.

Now, we had been unable to contact the woman who had the keys to our cottage, so whilst ordering drinks at the bar, I asked if the barmaid knew of the owner of our cottage.

![]()The exterior of the Three Jolly Sailors in Burniston. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Well, it turns out she did and the cottage owner ran a pub in Cloughton. Soon after, we were able to finally make contact and get the details of where to find the keys to the cottage.

Now, for drinks Dad had a pint of Timothy Taylor (£5.85), I had a pint of Perculier Assassin (£6) and mum had half of Guinness (£3.05).

Dad had the roast chicken (£18.95), mum had the catch of the day (£19.95), which was hake, and I had the pan-seared tuna steak (19.95).

Well, it wasn’t long before the meals arrived. The Three Jolly Sailors has plenty of staff, who are all kept busy.

A young lad neglected to bring dad his dish of vegetables but after a reminder 5-10 minutes later, he brough them and apologised, so he was forgiven, and dad was rewarded with a dish of vegetables as big as what a couple were sharing on the next table.

Now, as I have said before, as mum and dad are in their late eighties, their appetites aren’t what they used to be. And even I am not the pig I used to be!

![]()You won't miss the Three Jolly Sailors if passing through Burniston. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

However, I can say the hake was delicious, with a mild fishy flavour and a tender flake. It’s not a fish I have often, but maybe I should. The batter was deliciously crispy and a little crunchy and, dare I say it, it was far better than the haddock I had eaten at the Wetherby Whaler in Wetherby the day before. Praise indeed!

Mum’s chips were effectively done in the oven like small pieces of baked potato and they too were good. Lovely and soft in the middle. The peas were sweet and tender.

Dad says his chicken was tasty and tender but he has had better. He thought the exterior of the roast potatoes a little tough. But the other vegetables were fine.

Yes, the roast potato edge was a bit hard but once cut they were nice and soft inside. The other vegetables such as turnip, carrot, boiled potato, cauliflower, mange tout, etc, were all boiled or steamed as they should.

There was certainly plenty to go around. Indeed, dad said there were too much and only I was able to finish my plate.

The tuna steak had a fine lemony flavour and was served on cous cous, olives, tomato, and other vegetables. The contrast of flavours worked well, with an overriding pleasing tanginess. The portion was generous too.

Naturally, this meant we had no room for pudding. This is a shame as I am sure they and the starters would have been good. Dad did say to me there was far too much food and he doesn’t like waste.

Perhaps we should have gone at lunchtime through the week, when the Three Jolly Sailors has the Lunch Club where smaller portions of our pub favourites can be had for £11.50.

Well, I had already paid and the meal and drinks had come to a very reasonable £73.75. We were all ready for a lie down.

On the way out I thanked the barmaid for tracking down the owner of our cottage. She said we will have to return and I said we probably will as we are here till Thursday.

The Three Jolly Sailors is a lovely, traditional village pub, with plenty of choice and delicious food and good, friendly service. It is deservedly busy and well recommended.

I trust we will return but there again, there are other pubs nearby. Destination locations, who like the Three Jolly Sailors, attract their trade by excelling at their food, drink and service.

Now, whether you can get a good pub meal in Scarborough, I guess I will have to discover for myself another year. In the meantime, go to the villages just outside town. You won’t be disappointed.
