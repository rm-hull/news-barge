Concerns about breast cancer services at the region's NHS trust had been raised for more than a decade before the scandal sparked a major investigation, new papers reveal.

Warnings over surgical practices, delayed diagnoses and patient care were flagged repeatedly, the documents detail.

The County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust is reviewing thousands of patient records after a review of cases found hundreds of patients came to harm during treatment in the breast unit

The trust has published a detailed timeline of problems in its breast services as the board considers how far back to extend a major patient-care review.

The timeline records concerns dating back as early as 2012 about surgical training, unusually high procedure rates, delayed cancer diagnoses and patient complaints.

By 2018, the Northern Cancer Alliance had identified the Trust as a "regional and national outlier" because of high re-excision and benign breast surgery rates.

![]()University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City

The following year, a national NHS Getting It Right First Time review branded the Trust a negative outlier for high mastectomy and re-excision rates and low immediate reconstruction rates.

The disclosures appear in papers for an extraordinary Trust board meeting on September 24.

Board members must decide whether to extend the review of breast treatment from January 2023 to February 2025 back as far as 2015.

The Trust describes the investigation’s background as a "prolonged period of systemic governance and clinical concerns".

The chronology begins with training concerns in 2012 and 2015.

![]()County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust

In 2012, the centralisation of vascular surgery services at University Hospital of North Durham led to the loss of a combined vascular and breast surgical trainee post at Darlington Memorial Hospital.

In 2015, a trainee reported "limited opportunities to gain operative experience at University Hospital of North Durham" and said the supervising surgeon performed most procedures while the trainee watched.

The feedback contributed to the loss of that training post.

Further concerns emerged in 2018.

That year, the Northern Cancer Alliance called the Trust a "regional and national outlier for high re-excision rates and high rates of operations involving benign breast conditions."

![]()Darlington Memorial Hospital

The Care Quality Commission also raised formal concerns about delayed diagnoses, serious incidents, reporting delays, complaints and audit findings from the previous 18 months.

The Trust responded, and the CQC took no further action at that point.

In 2019, NHS England’s Getting It Right First Time programme, which examines variation in clinical practice, highlighted further problems.

The Trust was classed as a "negative outlier for high mastectomy and re-excision rates and low immediate reconstruction rates."

The concerns continued into 2021.

In March that year, external oncoplastic breast surgeons raised concerns about the high rate of benign operations.

National Cancer Patient Experience Survey results published in 2023 and 2024 also found County Durham and Darlington to be "an outlier across several areas of patient experience."

By May 2024, escalating concerns prompted another external review by the Northern Cancer Alliance of breast surgery carried out in 2023.

The review graded cases red, amber or green according to concerns about departures from evidence-based practice.

Around one-third fell into the red, highest-concern category.

The review found a 30 per cent re-excision rate at the Trust – double the 15 per cent regional average.

The report was issued to the Trust in June 2024.

![]()University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City. The Northern Echo interview CEO of the County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust, Steve Russell

The Royal College of Surgeons was then invited to examine the wider breast service, including radiology, surgery and non-surgical oncology.

Informal feedback from the review found "significant problems within breast surgery" and recommended "immediate external support".

The Royal College’s final report, issued in April 2025, identified a "decade of systemic failings".

The Trust says the findings ultimately contributed to the current large-scale lookback.

It also accepts that statutory Duty of Candour processes could have begun before 2025.

A February 2025 internal review revisited earlier reports and legal advice before prompting a review of patients treated in 2023, 2024 and up to February 21, 2025.

The Trust is now considering whether that review period goes far enough.

One option would extend the review backwards year by year towards 2015.

Another would commit the Trust to reviewing cases dating back to 2015 immediately.

The Trust says harm has already been found among patients treated before 2023 and accepts there are "likely to be further cases".

However, independent clinical experts advising the Trust say in the board papers that "examining significantly earlier cases is unlikely to uncover large numbers of patients requiring new treatment or substantially change what is already known about the service's failures".

The board will decide when it meets on September 24.

Around 1,500 patient records remain under review, while Durham Police continue to investigate whether criminal offences may have taken place.
