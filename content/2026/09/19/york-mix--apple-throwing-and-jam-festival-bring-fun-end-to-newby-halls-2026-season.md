Newby Hall is preparing to close its 2026 season – but not before ending with its popular Apple Day celebrations.

Taking place at the Ripon family attraction on **Sunday 27 September**, the day will feature the return of the hotly contested Apple Throwing Championships and the World Jam Festival and Awards.

Now in their fifth year, the Apple Throwing Championships will run from 1-3pm as competitors attempt to launch apples across the River Ure.

The longest throw secures a place in the grand finale and the chance to win the trophy. Last year’s winner, Lucas Robinson from Workington, recorded a throw of more than 80 metres to secure the title.

Visitors can enjoy pressing demonstrations, cider tasting and a display of heritage apple varieties from Newby’s orchards.

The World Jam Festival and Awards also return after attracting more than 200 entries last year from as far afield as Australia and Brazil. Judging is led by Michelin‑starred chef Andrew Pern, with categories including jams, preserves, chutneys, relishes, pickles, curds and fruit butters.

The winner of the ‘Best in Show’ trophy will also have the chance to have their entry stocked in Newby’s shop.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/The-Apple-Throwing-Championships-and-World-Jam-Festival-and-Awards-close-Newby-Halls-season-on-Sunday-27-September.-Pic-Helen-Tabor.webp)

Entertainment includes Harrogate Spa Town Ukes and Beijing‑based dancer Yulin Luo, plus a ‘Best Hat’ competition judged by the Mad Hatter.

Newby Hall head gardener Lawrence Wright said this year’s warm, dry summer has resulted in a smaller but highly flavoursome apple crop, with excellent concentrated flavour, but less juice.

“Newby’s orchards are filled with heritage varieties of apples – some dating back to the 1880s,” said Lawrence.

“As well as managing the historic varieties, we work in partnership with the Northern Fruit Group to plant new more manageable trees. The resulting fruit is used to make apple juice and cider, and bags of apples are sold in our shop.

“Smaller windfall apples help to feed the estate’s diverse wildlife, with visiting field fares and other migratory birds enjoying the harvest, along with insects, birds and mammals living along the river.”

Commercial director Stuart Gill added: “We’re proud to champion British apples and with more than 50 heritage varieties growing in our orchards, Apple Day is a brilliant way to celebrate the end of another fantastic season.”

Internationally recognised for its Chippendale interiors, Newby Hall is one of the UK’s finest Adam houses.

Tickets include entry to its 25 acres of award‑winning gardens, featuring one of Europe’s largest double herbaceous borders, the newly restored Edwardian rock garden and a woodland walk.

Families can also enjoy the adventure playground, miniature railway and permanent exhibitions of dolls’ houses and teddy bears.
