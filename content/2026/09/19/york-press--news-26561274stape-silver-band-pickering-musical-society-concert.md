A brass band concert will bring the sounds of stage and screen to life.

Stape Silver Band and members of Pickering Musical Society will perform together in Theatrical Brass at Pickering’s Kirk Theatre on Friday, September 25 at 7.30pm.

The show will raise vital funds for both groups.

Luke Arnold, director of the Kirk Theatre, said: "It’s a really important event for both the band and theatre, an opportunity for us to come together, dazzle the audience, and make some of the much-needed funds that keep both organisations going throughout the year.

"We’ll be putting on quite a show – and we’re really hoping that our local community will come out to support."

The evening will feature a mix of musical theatre favourites performed by the award-winning Stape Silver Band and singers from Pickering Musical Society.

Led by Robin Rutter, the band will play songs from shows such as Starlight Express, Grease, Cats, Annie Get Your Gun and Hairspray.

Robin Rutter, conductor of Stape Silver Band, believes the Theatrical Brass theme will particularly complement the combination of the band and Pickering Musical Society singers.

Performances from the band will be interspersed with vocal numbers by society members Rachel Anderson, Courtney Brown, Paula Cook and Danielle Long, accompanied by pianist Sue Knowles.

Mr Arnold said: "The audience can expect some really big numbers, from both the band and our singers.

"The programme is built around recognisable and well-loved tunes.

"We’ve focused on creating a really special, entertaining evening, whilst also giving the audience the opportunity to support two local volunteer-run organisations."

For Ms Anderson, the concert is a chance to return to a stage that holds personal meaning.

A long-time member of Pickering Musical Society, she is known for roles including Dolly in Hey, Dolly! and Eliza Doolittle in My Fair Lady.

Ms Anderson said: "It’s always such a special place to sing.

"It feels very much like coming home.

"I think so many of us – performers and audience members alike – feel like we just step into the theatre and it becomes a time and space to switch off from everything else and just be."

Tickets for Theatrical Brass are £13 and can be booked at www.kirktheatre.co.uk/events.
