The family behind of Teesside's most famous nightlife venues have announced they are stepping away after 35 years with the business.

In an emotional message to customers, Barry Faulkner Snr and Barry Faulkner Jnr said they would stop running Middlesbrough Empire, ending a chapter that began in the early 1990s.

The pair called it “the end of an era” but reassured club-goers that the Empire would continue under new owners.

Announcing the news, they said: "After nearly 35 years, the time has come for us - Barry Faulkner Snr & Barry Faulkner Jnr - to step away from running the Middlesbrough Empire.

"It's incredibly difficult to put into words what this place has meant to us.

"The Empire has never just been a nightclub. It has been a huge part of our lives and, judging by the stories, friendships and memories we've shared over the years, we know it has meant an awful lot to many of you too."

![]()The Faulkner's will step down as the owners of The Middlesbrough Empire after 35 years. *(Image: Paul Robinson)*

The Middlesbrough Empire has long been one of the most recognisable names in Teesside nightlife, attracting generations of revellers and hosting everything from club nights and live music to touring DJs and major events.

Most recently, the venue made headlines when it announced it would be hosting two album launch shows by Courteeners in October, which sold out within hours of tickets going on sale.

The Faulkner family also ran the legendary Sugar Shack, when internationally renowned DJs travelled to Teesside and put Middlesbrough on the dance music map.

Reflecting on their history, they said: "From the incredible Sugar Shack years, when some of the biggest DJs in the world came to Teesside, to every Friday, Saturday, live show and unforgettable night since - what happened inside those walls was only ever possible because of the people."

The pair also paid tribute to staff, DJs, promoters and customers who supported the venue over the decades.

They said: "You turned up. You filled the dancefloor. You brought the energy. You created the atmosphere and the memories. We all did this together."

While the announcement marks the end of an era for the Faulkner family, the venue itself is not going anywhere.

The family confirmed the Empire has been sold to new owners, who they say have plans to reinvent the club and bring fresh life to the landmark venue.

They said: "We are passing the baton to new owners who have exciting plans to reinvent the club, give this incredible venue a new lease of life and hopefully bring some of that glory back to Teesside once again."

Before leaving, the family are inviting generations of former customers back for one final celebration.

A farewell weekend will be held on November 12 and 13, with former staff, DJs, promoters, old regulars and newer club-goers invited to attend.

"Let's give our chapter of the story the send-off it deserves," they said.

"From the bottom of our hearts, thank you for the memories, the loyalty, the support and most of all, the love. It has been an absolute honour."

![]()Opening in 1899, Middlesbrough Empire has hosted huge names, including Charlie Chaplin. *(Image: PA)*

For many Teessiders, the Empire has been far more than a nightclub.

Since opening its doors in 1899, the Middlesbrough Empire has welcomed some of the world's most famous entertainers, including Charlie Chaplin, Harry Houdini and Bishop Auckland comedy legends Laurel and Hardy.

The club reopened as a multi-floor live music venue and nightclub in 1991, when the Faulkner's bought the club.

Since then, the venue has been the backdrop to thousands of first nights out, lifelong friendships and countless memories, making the Faulkners’ departure a major moment in its history.

While the Empire's future is secured, the new owners and their vision for the venue has yet to be announced.
