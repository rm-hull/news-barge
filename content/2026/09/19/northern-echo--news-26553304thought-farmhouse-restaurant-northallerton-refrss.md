I have been in plenty of farmhouses in my time.

Busy places, usually with a heap of wellies at the back door, an entrance full of overalls, waterproofs and woolly hats, and a kitchen where the single most important item is the kettle.

The opening of a new restaurant in Northallerton, simply called Farmhouse, therefore, piqued my interest, conjuring up visions of hearty bakes and mugs of tea.

What I found could not have been more different.

Farmhouse, slightly confusingly, was formerly the Governor’s House venue at the heart of the Treadmills development on Northallerton’s old prison site.

![]()The new Farmhouse restaurant on the Treadmills development in Northallerton *(Image: Hannah Chapman)*

It has relocated across the square to a unit next to Soju by Sumo, within the same building that houses the Everyman cinema, and is now called Farmhouse.

The brand has two other venues in Harrogate and Leeds, and at first glance, has retained a similar brunch-and-cocktails type of feeling as the old Governor’s House.

My sister Claire and I called in on a Sunday afternoon a week after the reopening, both having been caught in an unexpectedly ferocious downpour, and in need of food to recover.

It was well past lunchtime, but the place had a pleasant hum, with family groups, couples and friends all dining or enjoying drinks.

The glass frontage makes for a sleek first impression, which is continued inside with the minimalist décor – all dark wood panels, plain tiling and feature lights.

![]()Inside the new Farmhouse restaurant on the Treadmills development in Northallerton *(Image: Hannah Chapman)*

The main body of the restaurant is full of small, square tables, but we were shown to one of the horseshoe-shaped booths at the side.

Now, these do look great, and once you are in, they are great for a cosy chat. But the process of pulling the table out, sliding along the curved seat, and then pulling the table back in so we could reach our food and drinks required quite a bit of dexterity. As did extricating ourselves later.

The menu has more than plenty to choose from – no fewer than six different pancake/French toast toppings, and eight breakfast choices, plus six porridge/granola or toast-based options.

There are small plates, six main courses, burgers, four ways to have eggs benedict and a selection of salads and rice bowls.

Claire wanted something a little lighter, so chose the smoked salmon and scrambled egg (£14.95) from the breakfast menu, but I was feeling a tad hungrier, so went for the falafel flatbread (£15.45) from the mains.

The flatbread was a really good-sized portion – four chunky falafels on a large flatbread, drizzled in tzatziki, on a bed of hummus, roasted beetroot, pico de gallo and salad leaves.

![]()Falafel flatbread at Farmhouse restaurant on the Treadmills development in Northallerton *(Image: Hannah Chapman)*

The earthy, nutty falafel contrasted well with the subtle heat of the pico de gallo, while the tzatziki pulled everything together with a refreshing coolness.

Claire’s smoked salmon and scrambled egg, served with avocado, spinach and dill cream, was another decent portion.

The generous helping of salmon was set off well by the tangy dill cream, however the eggs, very pale in colour, didn’t have the strongest taste – a shame as it was otherwise an enjoyable dish.

![]()Smoked salmon and scrambled eggs at Farmhouse restaurant on the Treadmills development in Northallerton *(Image: Hannah Chapman)*

With two soft drinks (a raspberry lemonade at £4.50 and a large fresh orange and lemonade at £5.50) the bill came to £40.40. Probably about standard value at today’s prices.

The highlight of the whole experience was the staff, who were friendly, attentive without being overbearing, and all knew their stuff, even with the place only having opened under its new name a few days earlier.

I do remain confused about the Farmhouse branding, but that is not necessarily a criticism – I am no marketing expert, and now with three venues, it is clearly a growing business.

With a warm welcome on offer, the fresh, fun food offering fits nicely alongside Soju and the cinema. If the early signs are anything to go by, Farmhouse looks set to harvest plenty of success in its new home.
