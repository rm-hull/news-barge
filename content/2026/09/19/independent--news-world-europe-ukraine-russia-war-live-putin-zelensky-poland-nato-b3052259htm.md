Donald Trump has signed sweeping legislation targeting Russian officials with new tariffs meant to pressure Moscow for its ongoing war against Ukraine.

The bill enables sanctions on Russia's energy and defence industries, as well as its “shadow fleet” of tankers that evade existing sanctions.

Volodymyr Zelensky hailed the “critically important” legislation and thanked the US president, saying: “The best reward for everyone helping us put pressure on Russia to end its war will be peace.”

The bill, now called the "Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026," to ‌honour the late South Carolina Republican, passed the ‌House of Representatives on Wednesday and the Senate last month.

Russia targeted Ukraine’s capital with another missile attack on Friday evening, as explosions were heard across the capital.

In the southern port of Odesa, regional Governor Oleh Kiper said Russian forces launched two strikes on the city centre, the second while emergency crews were already at the scene.

A Russian glide bomb also killed two people and injured seven more, including children, after striking an apartment building in the northern Ukrainian city of Sumy.

## Zelensky thanks Trump for signing sweeping Russia sanctions bill

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky has thanked Donald Trump for signing legislation imposing further sanctions on Russia, saying the move would help increase pressure on Moscow to end the war.

In a statement, Zelensky recalled US senator Lindsey Graham’s calls during visits to Ukraine for maintaining pressure on Russia, including through sanctions and targeting countries that continue to buy Russian oil or provide financial and business services to Russian companies linked to the war.

“Peace comes through strength. Calm comes through security. And the best guarantee of security is resolve,” Zelensky said.

He thanked Trump and members of Congress who supported the legislation, saying the best way to honour Graham’s memory was to implement the law “fully and swiftly”.

The House passed the measure 262-159 earlier this week, with 58 Democrats voting in favour and seven Republicans against it. The Senate had approved the legislation 86-11 in August after more than a year of negotiations.

The Lindsey O Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 gives the president authority to impose tariffs of up to 100 per cent on the five largest purchasers of Russian oil or natural gas.

It also sanctions Russian president Vladimir Putin, government officials, oligarchs and Russian banks and financial institutions.

In a comment summing up the outcome ⁠of the meeting, Zelensky said on ​Telegram ⁠that 50 agreements had been signed for a total value of more than €1bn ($1.15bn). He gave few ⁠details, but said more than half the accords dealt with energy, ​particularly ⁠solar and wind power.

"The ‌Carpathians could become an energy supplier for the European Union, not just a transit region," Zelensky wrote. "And this directly ‌supports Europe's goal of energy independence from Russia."

## Two killed after Russian glide bomb hits apartment in Ukraine's Sumy

A Russian glide bomb hit an apartment building in the northern Ukrainian city of Sumy on Friday, killing two people and injuring seven, Volodymyr Zelensky and other officials said.

Three of those injured were ⁠in serious condition.

Zelensky, writing on the Telegram messaging app, denounced the "absolutely inhuman strike". He said emergency crews had rescued four people after the attack and said others could still be trapped under rubble.

"There ⁠was a direct hit by ​an ⁠aerial bomb between the third and fourth floors. Four people have already been rescued from under the rubble, including a child," ⁠he wrote. "There may still be people under the rubble."

Zelensky posted pictures ​on ⁠Telegram showing the shattered facade of ‌the building and rescue workers making their way through debris.

## US clears way for $2.7bn sale to Ukraine of air-defence upgrades

The US State Department has approved a potential $2.7bn military sale to Ukraine for air-defence development upgrades, weapons systems and related equipment and services.

The proposed package includes S-300 clone missiles, GAM-67 missiles, extended-range air-defence laser-guided rocket systems, launcher and mobile launching-system modification kits, and counter-drone system radars, according to a congressional notification issued on Friday.

The sale remains subject to congressional review.

If concluded, Ukraine would finance the purchase through a combination of European contributions and US foreign military financing appropriated during former president Joe Biden’s administration, the State Department said.

The notification comes as Donald Trump has stepped up pressure on Russia over its war in Ukraine. Earlier on Friday, Trump signed a sweeping Russia sanctions bill into law, paving the way for increased economic pressure on Moscow.

## Putin’s Russia threatens British targets in Ukraine over support for Kyiv

Vladimir Putin’s government has warned that British facilities in Ukraine will be considered “legitimate targets” if they are used to support long-range strikes against Russia.

The Russian foreign ministry summoned the UK’s deputy head of mission in Moscow Danae Dholakia to complain about British support for Ukraine in the war triggered by Mr Putin’s invasion four years ago.

The senior diplomat was warned that Russia could take “retaliatory measures” in response.

A statement issued by the Russian foreign ministry said Ms Dholakia was “issued a strong protest” over the supply of drones and other weapons systems to Volodymyr Zelensky’s forces in Ukraine.

“It was emphasised that in light of the United Kingdom’s policy the Russian side reserves the right to take necessary retaliatory measures as part of the exercise of the right to self-defence,” the statement said.

## Ukraine lashes out at Russian elections ‘held at gunpoint’ in occupied territory

Ukrainians in territories occupied by Russia are being “held at gunpoint” and forced to vote in parliamentary elections that “bear no resemblance to democracy,” the Ukrainian foreign ministry has warned.

For the next three days, 111 million people will head to the polls for Russia’s first parliamentary election since the since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022 - and one that includes the regions of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, as well as the annexed Crimean Peninsula.

“This is a farce orchestrated by the occupying state; it bears no resemblance to democratic elections and establishes no legal grounds for extending Russian jurisdiction to any part of Ukraine's territory,” a statement from the ministry said.

“No ‘vote’ organised by Russia at gunpoint alters the internationally recognised status of these territories. Temporary occupation confers no sovereign rights upon the occupying state.”
