Nearly 300 patients potentially affected by failures in an NHS breast service would have been missed by the Trust’s existing data and review criteria, documents reveal.

County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust says 283 people have been flagged for review despite falling outside its main review groups.

The Trust identified them through its breast services helpline, complaints, reports from other hospitals and clinicians’ concerns.

By September 2, the Trust had reviewed 240 cases.

Nineteen cases, around eight per cent, met the threshold for the statutory duty of candour, which applies when a notifiable safety incident causes or could cause moderate or severe harm or death.

![]()County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

The details are in papers for an extraordinary trust board meeting on September 24, where members will decide whether to extend the breast surgery review.

The current review mainly covers patients treated between January 2023 and February 2025.

Some of the 283 patients were treated before 2023. Others had no surgery at the Trust and would have been missing from its surgical data.

The Trust says the cases show why its “failsafe” safeguards must stay in place.

They include a patient helpline, incident reports from the breast team and other NHS trusts, and close links with other healthcare providers.

![]()University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City *(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

The board papers warn that vulnerable groups may be less likely to come forward, leaving patients to carry the burden instead of the NHS.

Patients and members of the public consulted on the review’s future also called for an end to what the Trust calls “inbound” engagement.

They said affected patients should instead be identified and “contacted proactively”, rather than expecting individuals to realise their treatment might need further examination.

The papers state: "The Trust must identify and contact affected individuals rather than relying on patients to come forward. Responsibility should not fall on patients."

By September 2, the dedicated breast services helpline had received 642 calls and emails.

![]()Darlington Memorial Hospital *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Launched in April 2025, the helpline lets former patients raise concerns about treatment outside the formal review criteria.

The Trust says the helpline has triggered reviews of people treated before 2023 and patients diagnosed through its breast services who had no surgery at its hospitals.

The findings come as the Trust considers four options.

They range from keeping the January 2023-to-February 2025 timeframe to extending the review to 2015.

The Trust acknowledges in its board papers that further cases of harm are “likely to exist outside the existing timeframe.”

It also says harm has already been found among people treated before 2023.

However, its independent Clinical Reference Group says extending the review much further back is “unlikely to identify large numbers of patients requiring further treatment or reveal substantial new clinical learning”.

Extending the review back to 2015 could bring about 4,500 cases into scope, with the Trust estimating the work would take five years at its current maximum rate of 80 reviews a month.

The Trust says older cases are likely to take longer because records from before October 2022 are predominantly historic paper files that were later scanned.

The Trust's latest figures show that 315 of 514 reviewed patients suffered harm, including 77 who suffered significant harm, while one patient is known to have died.
