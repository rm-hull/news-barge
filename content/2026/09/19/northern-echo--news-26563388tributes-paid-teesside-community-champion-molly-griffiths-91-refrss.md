Tributes have been paid to a Teesside woman who spent her later life bringing people together and tackling loneliness across the region.

North Ormesby resident Molly Griffiths died aged 91 on September 8, after decades of fundraising, organising social events and supporting older people across Teesside.

Known for her annual Christmas celebrations and long-running over-50s social club, Molly became one of the region's best-known community figures through her efforts to give people friendship, companionship and support.

Her over-50s club became the cornerstone of her work, providing a regular meeting place with laughter and activities for people who may otherwise have felt isolated.

Molly also organised trips across the UK and Europe, giving many people new experiences and lasting memories they otherwise may not have had in later life.

The club also proved important during a time when loneliness and isolation continue to impact many older people, offering a sense of belonging and extended family to those who need it.

Many of the friendships formed through the group are still strong to this day, with some members even finding their lifelong partners thanks to the weekly meetings.

Molly's dedication earned widespread admiration throughout Teesside, with tributes now flooding in, highlighting the impact she had on thousands of lives.

![]()Broadcaster Paul Goffy with Molly Griffiths. who passed away earlier this month aged 91. *(Image: Goffy Media)*

Broadcaster Paul 'Goffy' Gough, who supported many of Molly's events and helped promote her work on local radio, said: "Molly never stopped helping people. Whether it was through her Christmas celebrations, fundraising activities or the over-50s club, she was always thinking about others.

"She was loaded with warmth and totally understood how painful loneliness and isolation can be.

"It was a privilege to help promote her work because you could see first-hand the difference she was making to people's lives.

"She brought people together, created friendships and made sure nobody felt forgotten."

Molly took a genuine interest in people’s lives, with her home phone constantly busy with callers seeking advice, friendship or simply someone to talk to.

During the Covid pandemic, she continued supporting members and friends through some of the hardest times of their lives.

Molly is survived by her sons Michael, David and Dennis, daughter Denise and her grandchildren.

Her funeral service took place at Trinity Church in North Ormesby, Middlesbrough, at 10.30am on Friday, September 18, followed by burial at Thorntree Cemetery.

Molly’s legacy will live on in the friendships, support networks and community spirit she built across Teesside over decades of service.
