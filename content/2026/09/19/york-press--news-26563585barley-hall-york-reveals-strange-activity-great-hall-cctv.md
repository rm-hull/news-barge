A PARANORMAL detective case may need to be opened after some unpredictable activities were spotted by officials inside an historic building in York.

Barley Hall, a restored medieval townhouse on Coffee Yard off Stonegate, shared a moment of mystery recorded on its overnight security cameras.

It all started after a manager found a candle decoration broken on the floor of its Great Hall on the morning of September 8.




The team took to Instagram to broadcast the moment when earlier, at 2.47am the same morning, the ornament seemed to urgently slide off a table in the hall all on its own and crash to the floor with seemingly no influence from the natural world, before rolling across the tiles.

The video also shows that elsewhere in the hall, what seems to be curtain fabric making a jerky movement just before the fateful moment when the candle decoration embarks on its fateful journey.

There is no sign of any human activity in any of the footage, although The Press may also have missed some of the other supernatural elements of this 11-second clip as it was hiding behind its sofa from halfway through.

The Instagram post fuels the readers imagination further by going on to state: "After seeing the video online of a ghostly figure spotted in our window, our team took a look at our overnight CCTV.

"What do you think could have caused this?"

So far, the post has received more than 215 comments, including "err...cancels next volunteer slot," to "former residents still partying" and "I'd like to say it's the wind, but I'm not quite sure."

York Archaeological Trust, who rescued the building in 1987 and carried out extensive restoration before reopening it as a heritage attraction, declined to offer The Press anybody for comment when we contacted them earlier today.

Richard Jones, events manager at York Archaeology has described Barley Hall's Great Hall as a wonderfully atmospheric space, where its events are often hosted.

He said: "Barley Hall has been restored to its original splendour with stunning high ceilings and beautiful exposed timber frames.

"It has been decorated to replicate what it would have looked like as the Snawsell home around 1483 and boasts a magnificent Great Hall.

"Visitors to Barley Hall can make themselves at home and sit on the chairs and handle the objects and experience what it would have been like to live in Medieval England."
