The Crown Estate has revealed it is not involved in the current sale process of Dalton Mills in Keighley.

The statement follows a fire at the historic mills on Saturday, September 12, after which questions were raised about ownership and the proposed sale.

A Crown Estate spokesperson said: "In relation to Dalton Mills, we had been working towards a disposal of the property, but in this instance, the sale process was being delayed by issues beyond our control.

"These issues stem from a legal charge over the property concerning the former owner's debts which the trustee in bankruptcy is unwilling to release."

The Crown Estate understands that Bradford Council is now processing the sale, without its involvement.

The spokesperson said: "We now understand that the sale is being progressed by Bradford Council without The Crown Estate's involvement.

"Whilst The Crown Estate does not take any action that would constitute an act of management, we would not interfere with or prevent others, such as local authorities, community groups or any other party, from taking action to secure the site.

"We understand Bradford Council has previously taken action to secure the site."

## History of Dalton Mills ownership

Dalton Mills was bought in 2013 by Bellissimo Investments Limited for £10,000.

The company was dissolved in 2021, and Dalton Mills was automatically passed to the Crown.

The property became subject to escheat, a legal process which the Crown Estate described as an “incredibly complex legal construct”.

It effectively means the mills are ownerless.

The spokesperson said: "When land or property is subject to escheat, it falls to The Crown.

"The Crown Estate does not own or manage such land and is not responsible for its repair or maintenance.

"The Crown Estate's only involvement with such land is to effect a sale or transfer to a new owner, to an appropriate person or body."

## What is the Crown Estate?

The Crown Estate is an independent business with a collection of land and holdings that belong to the monarch as "corporation sole".

This means that Crown properties neither belong to the government, nor do they form part of the monarch's private estates.

Whilst the sovereign has official ownership of estates, it is not involved in their management or administration.
