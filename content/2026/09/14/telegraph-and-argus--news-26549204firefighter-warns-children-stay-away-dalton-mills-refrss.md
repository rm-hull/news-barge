A top firefighter is pleading with parents to keep "curious children" away from from a "very unstable" disused mill after repeated fires at the historic site.

Benjy Bush, Bradford district commander for West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, has urged families to keep children away from Dalton Mills in Keighley, describing the site as "dangerous."

The warning comes after a major fire broke out at the historic Grade II* site on Saturday, September 12, marking the fifth large blaze at the mill in five years.

### 'It's got a lot of hidden hazards'

Mr Bush said: "Local parents, please speak to your children.

"It might look like a fun place, but it’s got a lot of hidden hazards.

"It’s a dangerous place.

"There are holes in the floor.

"I understand it’s a derelict property and it attracts curious children, but I’d urge parents to speak to their children about keeping away from the area."

He said he would hesitate to send firefighters into the building due to its poor condition.

“I’m very hesitant now about sending any firefighters in there for any further incidents,” said Mr Bush.

Mr Bush said: "It’s very unstable now.

"I think it’s been well-documented how many fires there have been over the years and every time there is an incident, it becomes more unstable."

### Up to 70 firefighters in attendance on Saturday night

The latest fire was reported shortly after 8pm on Saturday and required a large emergency response.

Mr Bush highlighted how much of a strain the frequent fires at Dalton Mills place on the fire service.

He said: "It is very resource-intensive.

"On Saturday night, we had up to 60 or 70 firefighters, 12 fire engines and two aerial appliances in attendance.

"You have to remember that we have the rest of West Yorkshire to protect as well.

"It’s a big, big challenge every time we turn up to Dalton Mills and have a significant incident."

### 'It's been devastating for the people of Keighley'

Mr Bush reinforced the negative impacts the fire has had on the local community.

He said: "This has been another devastating fire at Dalton Mills.

"It’s been devastating for the town, for the people of Keighley, and also for us as a fire service.

"This structure is becoming more and more dangerous.

"Every time we attend, it’s a big concern and we’re hoping we can get the site as secure as possible.

"We’re hoping we don’t have to come back here."

### Residents evacuated as fire threatened to spread

Mr Bush acknowledged the frustration of nearby residents.

He said: "With the fire on Saturday night, we did have to evacuate some homes, as there was the potential for the fire to spread to a row of houses nearby.

"Thankfully, that didn’t occur and we were able to get those families in, but really, all they can do is exactly what they did.

"If they see any activity, phone the police and and if they see a fire, ring us as quickly as possible so that we can get on top of the incident as soon as we can."

### Dalton Mills once a 'thriving' place

Mr Bush called for improved security and lamented the loss of a "thriving" site.

He said: "It’s a shame.

"I imagine it was once a thriving place.

"It needs securing properly.

"We need to make it as difficult as we can to get into."

As reported by the Telegraph & Argus last year, the company which previously owned Dalton Mills went bankrupt and was dissolved via a compulsory strike-off in 2021.

This left the 19th century complex to become subject to an arcane law called escheat - meaning it effectively became ownerless - and it was given up to the Crown Estate.

### What happened at Dalton Mills on Saturday?

Reports of a fire emerged just after 8pm on Saturday.

Emergency crews, including ambulances and 12 fire crews, scrambled to the scene.

The fire took several hours to put out.

West Yorkshire Police said: "Police were called to reports of an industrial fire by West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service at around 8.28pm on Saturday 12 September.

"Officers were called to contain the scene at Dalton Lane, Keighley. At approximately 1.16am on Sunday 13 September the fire was extinguished.
