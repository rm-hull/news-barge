“I don’t regret anything” said the man sentenced for displaying a flag with an offensive message about York MP Rachael Maskell on the Bar Walls.

Speaker of the House of Commons Sir Lindsay Hoyle told York Magistrates' Court that Lee Moran’s actions were the type that undermined democracy and made MPs fear for their safety.

Deputy senior district judge Tan Ikram said he would have seriously considered jailing Moran but the law said he had to suspend the prison sentence.

He gave Moran, of Brunswick Street, South Bank, York, a 14-week prison sentence suspended for 12 months on condition he does 15 days’ rehabilitative activities. He must also pay Ms Maskell £70 compensation, £100 prosecution costs and a £154 statutory surcharge.

Moran lives on benefits and told the court he had lots of debts.

The judge told him: “Two MPs have been murdered and other members of Parliament are feeling fearful of expressing their political views. We live in a democracy where they are entitled to their views.

“There is a way to give political views and that is through reasoned debate with others and through the ballot box.

“Descending into this sort of criminal behaviour is a feature which I am increasingly seeing and it is an aggravating factor in this case.”

In his statement which was read to the court, Sir Lindsay said he was responsible for MPs' safety, he had twice received phone calls saying an MP had been murdered, and he knew of MPs who were preparing to leave Parliament because of the level of abuse they were receiving.

Outside court Moran said he intended to appeal against his convictions for a public order offence involving the displaying of a St George’s flag with the words “This one’s for you, Maskill” on the Bar Walls; using threatening words or behaviour towards a woman who feared he would throw her off the Bar Walls while he was displaying the flag; and sending an offensive message in a Facebook post.

He had denied all charges but been convicted at a trial at York Magistrates' Court last month.

He said he was making a formal complaint about the way the judge had conducted the trial and would also appeal against his sentence.

“I don’t regret anything,” he said and claimed that the prosecution had misinterpreted his actions.

Asked why he had displayed the flag he said: “Because I’m sick of being called a Nazi, racist, sick of being called far right.”

He said it was “disgusting” to mention the murders of the two MPs in connection with his case.

During the hearing, he repeatedly shook his head while prosecution lawyer Verity Barnes was describing what he had been convicted of. He also interrupted the judge as he was passing sentence saying he disagreed with the convictions.

He was told to respect the judgement of the court.

Defence solicitor Shawn Cooper did not give any mitigation after the judge said he could not jail Moran.
