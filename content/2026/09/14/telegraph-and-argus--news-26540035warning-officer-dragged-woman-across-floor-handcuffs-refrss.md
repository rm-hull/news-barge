A West Yorkshire Police officer used ‘unreasonable’ force after dragging a woman along the floor by handcuffs during an arrest at a Bradford flat, an investigation has found.

The incident occurred in October 2023, when two officers attended a property to arrest the woman over an allegation of threats to kill.

Prior to the incident, the woman bit one of the officers and was subsequently arrested on suspicion of assaulting an emergency worker.

The case was examined by the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) in July, after complaints from the woman and her partner over the officers' use of force and allegations of discrimination.

According to the IOPC, a struggle broke out after she refused to accompany officers to a police station for interview.

The watchdog found the woman's arrest was "justified, necessary and proportionate", but criticised aspects of the arresting officer's conduct as the situation escalated.

As a result of the incident the arresting officer was handed a written warning, lasting 18 months.

## What happened?

During the confrontation, the woman bit one of the officers and was later arrested on suspicion of assaulting an emergency worker.

The IOPC said the arresting officer warned the woman that irritant spray would be used if she failed to comply with instructions.

When she did not, the officer deployed the spray.

The woman's partner attempted to intervene in the arrest and became involved in a struggle with the second officer.

The couple later alleged the woman had been stamped on, and the man had been punched in the stomach.

The couple also alleged the woman’s baby, who was present during the incident, had been stamped on.

The woman’s partner reported that the child was taken to hospital with breathing difficulties because of the irritant spray.

The IOPC said body-worn camera footage did not support those claims. The child was later discharged.

After being handcuffed, the woman was pulled into the hallway outside the flat, where she complained she was bleeding and fell to the floor.

The officer then dragged her along a corridor by the central bar of the handcuffs before she was taken into custody.

The second officer used force in an effort to prevent the man from following.

The couple later complained about the officers' use of force, with the woman's partner alleging the incident was racially motivated.

## Actions ‘not in line’ with training

Investigators identified concerns about the treatment of the woman after she was restrained.

In its report, the IOPC said: "Dragging the woman using her full weight along the floor by the handcuffs was not" in line with training.

The watchdog concluded: "This use of force was unreasonable due to the increased chance of injury to the woman."

The investigation also found the arresting officer had failed to communicate effectively with the woman during the arrest, which "directly affected the officer's decision making and allowed the situation to escalate in the way it did".

Body-worn footage later captured the officer referring to the woman using what the IOPC described as "inappropriate language" while she was in custody.

Whilst acknowledging the officer was “understandably upset” after being bitten, the watchdog said the comments were “unacceptable” and "had the potential to undermine confidence in policing".

The investigation found no concerns relating to the use of irritant spray.

## Assault charge considered

The IOPC concluded the officer may have committed a criminal offence and referred the case to the Crown Prosecution Service to consider an assault charge.

Ultimately prosecutors decided there was insufficient evidence to provide a realistic prospect of conviction.

A misconduct hearing concluded in July 2026 and found the officer had breached professional standards relating to conduct, authority, respect and courtesy, and use of force.

The IOPC found no evidence that either officer had acted in a racially discriminatory manner, although the hearing found a partial breach of equality and diversity standards in relation to the woman's maternity status.

The arresting officer was given a written warning lasting 18 months. The second officer was cleared of any misconduct or criminal wrongdoing.
