A historic viaduct on a popular County Durham walking and cycling route is to be resurfaced.

The Grade II listed Hownsgill Viaduct near Consett, built in 1857, carries the Waskerley Way and Coast to Coast (C2C) cycle route across the Hownsgill Valley.

Durham County Council will replace worn-out concrete on the viaduct deck with new slabs after plans were approved on September 7.

The current surface has deteriorated and was further damaged during previous work to install anti-climb fencing.

Councillors have raised concerns about the surface's condition.

The refurbishment plan involves removing damaged concrete sections between the fencing and the central drainage channel and installing new slabs with reinforcing mesh to minimise future cracking.

Jagged edges on the existing surface will also be removed to improve the deck's appearance.

Planning officers stated that the work would preserve the viaduct's historic integrity while ensuring it remains safe and accessible for users.

The viaduct, designed by railway engineer Thomas Bouch, was part of the Stanhope and Tyne Railway and is now a key feature of the Waskerley Way.

Officers concluded that the repairs would improve conditions for users by reducing trip hazards and providing a smoother surface without harming the viaduct's historic character.
