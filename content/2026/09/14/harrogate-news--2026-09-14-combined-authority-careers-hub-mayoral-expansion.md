**HARROGATE** — Regional governance chiefs have confirmed a major structural overhaul of the district’s youth employment infrastructure, placing a multi-agency network directly under localized mayoral command.

The York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority has officially assumed strategic leadership of the regional Careers Hub network, tracking alongside a national policy shift toward localized authority-led educational planning. The governance transition was formally endorsed during a combined authority cabinet session on **Friday 11 September 2026**, shifting operational control away from centralized pipelines to align training directly with North Yorkshire employer deficiencies.

The system expansion represents a core pillar of Metro Mayor David Skaith’s statutory ‘Cradle to Career’ policy framework, which aims to establish integrated public service interventions from early-years development through to active corporate recruitment. The centralized hub currently intersects with all **75 secondary schools and colleges** across the unified boundaries, managing career pathways for approximately**50,000 students** annually.

## Mandatory Placements and Primary School Extensions

Under the newly approved operational expansion draft, the authority will introduce rigorous new delivery metrics across the regional network. Chief among the structural mandates is a requirement ensuring that every secondary school pupil across the Harrogate district secures the equivalent of two weeks of meaningful, vetted industry work experience before graduation.

Furthermore, the authority’s new delivery roadmap will actively extend career awareness frameworks downward into regional primary schools to build an all-age career model. Operational priority will be heavily weighted toward tracking and supporting economically disadvantaged young people, deployable through targeted resource allocations to dismantle structural barriers to technical progression.

**David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire:** “Everyone deserves the best start in life so they can have a fulfilling career, no matter their background. That’s why we’re investing in and expanding our Careers Hub, as we bring the service under our leadership. This is how we can inspire the next generation, tackle inequalities and ensure every student comes out of school ready and excited for the world of work.”


## Multi-Agency Integration and Local Corporate Outreach

The expanded local infrastructure will continue to operate in formal partnership with The Careers and Enterprise Company, a national independent body managing 44 regional education hubs across England.

By shifting the administrative core inside the combined authority, the hub will serve as a unified contact node connecting local independent schools, technical colleges, private training providers, and the National Careers Service. Local business owners looking to register as enterprise advisers, or firms looking to supply corporate work placement capacity to meet the new statutory quotas, can review localized engagement criteria directly through the central portal at York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.
