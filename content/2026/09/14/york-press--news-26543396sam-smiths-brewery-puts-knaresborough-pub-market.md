Sam Smith’s brewery appears to be selling a growing number of its pubs.

Among the pubs the Tadcaster-based brewery seeks to sell is the Wellington Inn at Knaresborough.

Estate agents Fleurets is marketing the freehold of the pub at 23 Briggate for £325,000.

But the sales particulars say this is for ‘non pub use only.’

Fleurets says the property features a bar, lounge and kitchen, plus 3-bed accommodation. There are also a garden and parking for 6-8 cars.

The Wellington Inn closed in 2022 and was the last of three Sam Smith’s pubs in the town. The brewery also owns the long-closed Marquis of Granby, on York Place, and Commercial Hotel, formerly the Borough Bailiff, on High Street.

The sale of the Wellington Inn at Knaresborough has generated concerns from the Facebook groups that comment on the affairs of Sam Smith’s Brewery.

Malton resident Tom Taverner, who compiles a national listing of which Sam Smith’s pubs are open or closed, has produced a list of nine Sam Smith’s pubs he says are on the market.

In addition to The Wellington Inn at Knaresborough, Tom also listed pubs in Barnsley, Wakefield, Rochdale and elsewhere, being marketed by Fleurets or BTG Eddisons.

This follows the recent auctioning of two Sam Smith’s pubs in Doncaster and Bradford for £230,000 and £262,000 respectively.

The sales have led to some debate on the Facebook groups, with some concerned it may see the brewery offloading many of its pubs rather than re-opening them.

One pointed out that former chairman, Humphrey Smith, who died several months ago, hardly ever sold pubs. Has the brewery now changed its policy on selling pubs, some asked?

Sam Smith’s Brewery has recently spoken of its ambitions to help regenerate Tadcaster.

This was raised at a town council meeting a month ago, where North Yorkshire councillor Kirsty Poskitt read out a letter from the brewery on its plans.

As the Press recently reported, the brewery plans projects including building 101 homes on Station Road, Tadcaster, to help fund the regeneration of properties in the town centre.

North Yorkshire Council, as the planning authority, has yet to approve the scheme, which is supported by Tadcaster Town Council.

Cllr Poskitt has posted online: “There was a commitment [from the brewery] to getting those buildings back into use.”

Last month, Christian Horton, Director of Samuel Smith’s Brewery, also told the Press: “The brewery continues to support a high-quality heritage-led regeneration of the town’s Conservation Area.”

The Press has approached Mr Horton about the brewery selling pubs and its efforts to fill its empty pubs.

Mr Horton told the Press he would prefer not to comment on commercial matters like pub sales, but he said “the brewery is continuing to look for new pub managers to reopen any closed pubs.”
