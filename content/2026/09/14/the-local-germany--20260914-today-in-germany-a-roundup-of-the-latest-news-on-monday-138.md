**Monday's top story: CDU holds off rivals in Lower Saxony district elections**

Germany’s centre-right CDU has emerged as the largest party in local elections in Lower Saxony. The vote, which determines the composition of district and municipal councils, is widely viewed as an important indicator of public sentiment.

Lower Saxony, a state in the north-west of Germany with around eight million people, will hold its next state election in 2027.

According to preliminary results, the CDU won 27.2 percent of the vote, ahead of the SPD on 24.6 percent. The far-right AfD finished third with 15.9 percent, marking its strongest ever performance in a local election in Lower Saxony.

The Greens secured 14.1 percent, followed by The Left on 6 percent and the FDP on 3.7 percent.

While both the CDU and SPD lost support compared with the previous local elections in 2021, the AfD more than tripled its vote share, rising from 4.6 percent. Voter turnout also increased sharply to 64.6 percent.

The result will be closely scrutinised nationally, particularly after the AfD’s landslide victory in Saxony-Anhalt a week earlier. Attention now turns to state elections in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania on Sunday, where the balance of power between the AfD, CDU and SPD is expected to be a central theme.

Germany’s homeownership rate has fallen to its lowest level in two decades, underlining the growing difficulty many households face in getting onto the property ladder.

A study by the Pestel Institute found that only 43.5 percent of people in Germany now live in a home they own, placing the country at the bottom of a European comparison. Researchers point to soaring construction costs, higher interest rates and the withdrawal of government support schemes as key factors behind the decline.

According to the study, the cost of building apartments has risen by 160 percent since 2000, far outpacing overall consumer price inflation. As a result, even many dual-income households on average earnings struggle to afford a home, particularly younger buyers.

The impact is most evident among those aged 25 to 45, with almost three-quarters now renting rather than owning their homes. Institute director Matthias Günther warned that homeownership is becoming increasingly unattainable for younger generations.

He also argued that the decline could have wider social consequences, linking rising rents to a greater risk of poverty in old age.

**Chancellor Friedrich Merz in Finland for arctic security talks**

European leaders are meeting in northern Finland on Monday for an EU-Arctic Summit as growing geopolitical tensions and security concerns bring renewed attention to the strategically important Arctic region.

The summit is taking place in Rovaniemi, just south of the Arctic Circle, and was expected to focus heavily on defence, regional security and Europe’s long-term Arctic strategy. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz is attending the gathering after holding talks with Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo on Sunday.

Security concerns are likely to dominate discussions. Finland, which shares a 1,300-kilometre border with Russia, joined NATO in response to a deteriorating security environment following Moscow’s invasion of Ukraine.

Finnish Ambassador to Germany Kai Sauer said Europe’s attention should extend beyond Greenland to the wider Arctic, referring to renewed international interest in the region and concerns sparked by US President Donald Trump’s past annexation remarks regarding Greenland.

**Germany tastes US Open glory but basketball bronze slips away**

Germany could celebrate a major sporting success on Sunday as Alexander Zverev claimed the first US Open title of his career, defeating American Ben Shelton 6-3, 7-6, 5-7, 6-2 in a four-set final.

The German tennis star was up against a highly partisan crowd in New York and remains a controversial champion following allegations of abuse made by two former partners, all of which he has denied. A 2024 court case in Germany ended without a verdict.

There was disappointment meanwhile for Germany’s women’s basketball team at the FIBA Women’s Basketball World Cup in Berlin. The hosts missed out on a historic bronze medal, suffering an 81-58 defeat to Spain in the third-place play-off.

A hiker’s fears of a bear encounter in southern Germany ended in relief after police determined the supposed predator was in fact a group of deer during the autumn mating season.

The incident occurred near Bad Hindelang in the Allgäu Alps, close to the Austrian border, when the walker called emergency services believing he was being pursued by a bear.

According to police in Kempten, officers could immediately hear the distinctive calls of rutting deer over the phone and were able to reassure the hiker.

The episode highlights both the dramatic nature of the annual deer rut and the enduring fascination with bears in Germany. Brown bears were wiped out in the country by the early 19th century, although small populations continue to live elsewhere in the Alps, particularly in Slovenia and northern Italy.

While wandering bears have occasionally crossed into Germany, including the famous bear “Bruno” in 2006, conservation groups say there is currently no permanent bear population in the country.
