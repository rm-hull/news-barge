A Middlesbrough primary school has been named among the country’s best for outdoor play, with a playground where children can build, explore and imagine.

Grangetown Primary School has been awarded its second consecutive OPAL Platinum Award - the highest level of accreditation available through the Outdoor Play and Learning (OPAL) programme.

The latest award puts the school on course to become an OPAL Honours School.

The award honours schools that transform playtimes and outdoor learning, giving children more freedom to be creative, adventurous and active alongside classroom lessons.

Inspectors said: "This is a remarkable and rare achievement that reflects your school's outstanding commitment to creating a rich, inclusive, and empowering culture of outdoor play."

  Grangetown Primary School in Middlesbrough has been awarded its second consecutive OPAL Platinum award. 

*(Image: GOOGLE MAPS)*

OPAL awards Platinum status only to schools that go above and beyond to transform children’s play.

Inspectors praised the school’s outdoor environment and the way staff have developed it to help children socialise, build confidence and challenge themselves.

They said: "The site now offers children powerful opportunities to develop a multitude of skills, socialise with friends, develop physical literacy and confidence and test their limits in meaningful and exhilarating ways."

The scheme’s values and the way Grangetown children look out for one another also helped win the award.

The report said: "Children feel trusted and cared for and through OPAL assemblies, you communicate and celebrate play, highlighting children who have shone or gone above and beyond, but often for simple reasons of being kind.

"Each member of staff I spoke to was clearly passionate about amazing play opportunities and the impact they have had within your community."

Inspectors singled out John Vickers, the school’s assistant headteacher and OPAL lead, for his leadership of the programme.

Inspectors said the programme’s benefits were clear across the school community.

"The impact on your children is clearly visible: they are more confident, adventurous, and connected," they said.

"Your behaviour and attendance have improved, and children are learning vital life skills through joyful, self-directed play."

School leaders said the accreditation was a moment for the whole Grangetown community to celebrate and showcase positive stories from the area.

One Year 5 pupil said: "We can build, explore, imagine and play together. That's why I love OPAL. It means we get to choose our own adventures."

OPAL mentor Lizzie Pringle formally presented the award at a special ceremony at the school on Thursday (September 10).
