ONE of Britain's oldest institutions "marked the beginning of a new era" with a relaunch featuring video art built around a conversation with a York-born star of stage and screen.

The Royal Society of Arts (RSA), founded in 1754 has launched a new visual identity, with one of its Fellows, Dame Judi Dench FRSA, featuring at the heart of the unveiling in a short video entitled "The Case for Human Imagination", which debuted this spring.

The Heworth-born icon, whose career spans seven decades, took part in an unscripted conversation with the society this Spring.

*(Image: Royal Society of Arts)*

Her words form the centrepiece of the RSA’s renewed mission to become "the home of creativity for the common good."

Charlotte Thomson, director of strategy and creative at the RSA, said: "The Royal Society of Arts is coming out of its shell to meet the moment.

"Its belief is timeless: imagination leads to impact.

"This storied institution inspires, excites, and brings together people from all walks of life.

"And it’s needed more than ever.

"Sitting down with Judi was just magic.

"Overflowing with wisdom and wit, who better to make the case for human imagination than Judi?

"And who could ever disagree with it?"


The video combines Dame Judi’s reflections with a stream of imaginative visuals drawn from the RSA’s own archive.

It highlights some of the society’s most notable inventions and public initiatives, including the lifeboat, the scandiscope, and the fireman’s ladder.

The RSA’s Fellowship includes figures such as Marie Curie, Vivienne Westwood, David Attenborough, Tim Berners-Lee, Bernardine Evaristo, as well as Dame Judi.

The new brand identity, designed by creative agency Baby Teeth, is intended to reflect the RSA’s commitment to creativity as a force for positive change.

Lynsey Atkin, founder and partner at Baby Teeth, said: "When the Royal Society of Arts was founded, what people lacked were the means – the access, the knowledge, the technology – to make their visions for a better world a reality.

"Today, in a world abundant with means, the RSA’s urgent call is for vision: to imagine a better, fairer world for all.

"Spending time with RSA Fellow Dame Judi Dench, talking about the importance of human imagination was – unsurprisingly – both a thrill and an honour.

"We live in challenging times, so to demonstrate her words of conviction and hope through the rich archive of the RSA has proved fascinating and energising."

An RSA spokesperson said: "In the words of Dame Judi, 'it's not enough to say "This is the only way it can be.

'No, no. That won't do'"

Over the coming months, the RSA will introduce the new brand across all communications, digital channels, and its physical premises.

It also plans to continue its events series, focusing on conversations that explore imaginative pathways to a better future.

The Royal Society of Arts is one of Britain’s oldest institutions dedicated to social progress and creative innovation.
