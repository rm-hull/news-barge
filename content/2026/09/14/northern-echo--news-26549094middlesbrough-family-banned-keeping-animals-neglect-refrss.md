A mother, her partner and her son have been banned from keeping animals after three Jack Russell terriers were found severely neglected, with one later put down.

Irene Pallent, 63, her partner Derek Lane, 59, and her son Stuart Pallent, 38, were sentenced at Teesside Magistrates’ Court on September 3 after admitting two animal welfare offences.

Police found the dogs at Leighton Road, Grangetown, Middlesbrough, in December 2023 and removed them before the RSPCA investigation began.

Sadie, an elderly female, was almost completely bald and appeared to be blind.

  Rosa when found 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

Derek Lane accepted joint responsibility for the dogs and agreed to hand them over to the RSPCA.

He declined an RSPCA interview and refused an examination by a vet of his choice.

Irene Pallent said Sadie belonged to her son and she owned the other two dogs.

She admitted knowing the dogs were in poor condition but refused an independent veterinary assessment and an interview.

Stuart Pallent also accepted joint responsibility for the dogs but declined an interview.

All three dogs had severe fur loss, overgrown nails and advanced skin disease.

A vet said the dogs suffered unnecessarily for at least six weeks because their skin conditions went untreated.

Sadie weighed just 3.8kg (8.37lb) and scored two out of nine for body condition.

She was blind, severely underweight, had numerous lumps, and showed signs of neurological problems, including walking in circles.

  Rosa in her new home 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

The vet recommended euthanasia because Sadie had advanced dementia and was suffering further.

Eight-year-old Rosy was underweight, with severe fur loss and overgrown nails.

The third dog, elderly Susie, had similar skin problems, overgrown nails and three mammary tumours.

In a written statement, the vet said: "Sadie, Rosy and Susie were all suffering from advanced skin disease.

"This was obvious from the very notable loss of fur, the thickened and reddened skin and the notable irritation to the animals particularly when handling them.

"There was also a notable odour."

The vet identified flea allergic dermatitis (FAD) as the most likely cause.

The statement continued: "Left untreated the skin becomes disrupted, the hair falls out, the skin becomes thickened and infected.

"This leads to further distress for the dog.

"Any responsible and humane person would have recognised that there was a problem causing all three dogs distress and would have sought appropriate advice and treatment."

Irene Pallent received a 17-week prison sentence, suspended for 12 months, and a 15-year ban on keeping animals.

  Rosa in her new home 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

Derek Lane and Stuart Pallent were each given a 16-week suspended sentence and a ten-year animal ban.

All three were ordered to pay £450 in costs and a £154 victim surcharge.

Rosy was cared for by the RSPCA’s Warrington, Halton and St Helens branch, where it took six weeks to stabilise her skin condition.

She has since been rehomed but remains under ongoing veterinary care.

Susie was put to sleep after failing to respond to treatment.

Natalie Hill, RSPCA chief inspector for Durham and Cleveland, said: "These little dogs suffered weeks of neglect because the defendants hadn’t provided the most basic of care.
