A man who carried out a revenge attack “out of rage and anger” by crashing his Range Rover into another car containing a woman he knew has been jailed for 23 months at Bradford Crown Court.

Gavin Thorpe’s car was seen reversing into the Seat as it tried to leave the small car park of the Park pub on Reevy Road in Wibsey on June 15 this year.

After damaging the other car, Thorpe drove briefly forward before reversing again and slamming into another car and a stone outbuilding, which partly collapsed onto the Range Rover.

He then jumped out of the Range Rover and chased two men who had escaped from the Seat.


He threw a stone at them and shouted, “Go f*** yourselves, boys” before fleeing the scene.

*(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Thorpe, 42, of Arkwright Street, Wibsey, appeared in court via video link from HMP Leeds.

He previously pleaded guilty to dangerous driving, assault occasioning actual bodily harm, and three counts of criminal damage relating to two cars and the outbuilding.

Prosecuting barrister Elizabeth Noble told the court how Thorpe had been in the pub at the same time as a woman who was known to him and who was subject to a domestic violence protection order.

That prevented Thorpe from contacting the woman.

Miss Noble said there was “interaction” between the pair and witnesses present suggested Thorpe behaved “unwantedly” towards her.

The conversation was overheard by two men who agreed to take her home.

She got into the back of the driver’s car, a Seat.

At that point Thorpe appeared and said something to the Seat’s front seat passenger who then asked the woman if she wanted to go with Thorpe, and she said no.

She also said the same to Thorpe, who went to his Range Rover.

As the trio in the Seat reached the exit of the pub car park, Thorpe reversed his Range Rover at speed and collided with it, causing serious damage.

He then drove forward before reversing at speed a second time, colliding with another vehicle in the car park as well as a stone outbuilding, which partially collapsed onto the Range Rover.

There followed the brief interaction between the three men before Thorpe ran off.

Miss Noble said: “This was a revenge attack that came out of jealousy and dissatisfaction with [the female victim].

“He was certainly reckless when he reversed towards [the Seat].

“The target was the vehicle because they had [the woman] within that vehicle.”

The court heard that the male passenger in the other car suffered neck and back pain from the crash and had to have an x-ray at hospital.

Thorpe was arrested two days later.

In an interview with police, he gave “no comment” answers to questions.

He later pleaded guilty at the magistrates’ court to dangerous driving, assault occasioning actual bodily harm, and three counts of criminal damage.

The court heard that Thorpe had six previous convictions for seven offences including for ABH and wounding, for which he received two years’ imprisonment in July 2024.

Mitigating, Saf Salam said Thorpe accepted colliding with an occupied vehicle and that the assault occasioning actual bodily harm arose out of that.

He described it as “all part and parcel of a short and continuous incident.”

He said no evidence had been offered to suggest that the man injured in the incident had suffered a fracture, had received prolonged treatment, or had spent time off work.

He said drug addiction had been a factor in Thorpe’s life but that since being on remand in prison he was drug-free.

He said Thorpe, a personal trainer who had also worked in the building trade, had an insight into drug misuse and its impact and that rehabilitation was capable through structured intervention.

Mr Recorder Patrick Palmer sentenced Thorpe to 15 months for dangerous driving plus six months for ABH and two months for criminal damage.

He also banned Thorpe from driving for three years and ordered him to pass an extended re-test before getting behind the wheel again.

He described his actions as “quite deliberate” and said: “This was an offence coming out of rage and anger.”

He added: “You are now building up a significant series of offences for crimes committed whilst subject to anger.

“You may wish to take as much help as you can whilst subject to custody.”

Recorder Palmer told Thorpe he would serve up to half of his sentence in custody before being released on licence.
