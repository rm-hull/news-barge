ONE of the North East’s best-known business leaders has praised the “incredible” progress of a development to transform a farm into a training and employment hub for autistic people.

Sir Peter Vardy has been a leading supporter of the vision created by the North East Autism Society (NEAS) for New Warlands Farm, near Burnhope, in County Durham.

As well as being a place where autistic people can develop employability skills, the site will become a working farm and tourism attraction as part of the £4.9m development.

  In March 2025, Sir Peter led a rallying cry to other North East businesses by pledging a £500,000 contribution towards the charity’s *Growing Futures* appeal.

He called on other businesses in the region to come together to match the donation, urging them to “be part of something very special”.

  Sir Peter Vardy (right) with NEAS CEO Paul McGinnety and former CEO John Phillipson 

*(Image: NEAS)*

The second phase of the 78-acre project started this summer with the construction of new buildings, including a farm shop, café and cider mill.

The site will also feature retail outlets, specialist play areas, wildlife areas, and a petting zoo, with internationally renowned artist Mackenzie Thorpe involved in producing the designs.

The scheme is on schedule for the farm to be operational by the end of the year, with a formal launch next April to celebrate World Autism Acceptance month.

During a visit to the farm, Sir Peter said: “I think it's fantastic the way the site's developed. The speed of construction is faster than anything I've seen in the last five years.

“To see the progress, and to think you're going to have it ready for the end of the year, I think is incredible.

  Sir Peter Vardy during a site visit at New Warlands Farm 

*(Image: NEAS)*

“Throughout the work of The Vardy Foundation, I've found that outstanding people do great work, and our aim is to support the outstanding people who make things happen.

“We invest in inspirational leaders, with fantastic vision, who can dream of what it will look like and make that dream a reality. It’s important to support the people that can do that.

“You invest in people. You invest in people who have passion, who have a vision of what can be done, and an ability to make it happen.”

NEAS officially launched the ambitious project in November last year, with the symbolic planting of a sapling from the felled Sycamore Gap tree at Hadrian’s Wall.

Mulberry Construction Group, architects BDM Ltd, and Turner and Townsend have come together to work on the development, while former NEAS CEO, John Phillipson, has stayed on as a consultant until the project has been completed.

John’s successor, Paul McGinnety, said: “Sir Peter Vardy has played an inspirational and incredibly generous leading role in this development, and it was a pleasure to show him the remarkable progress that is being made.

“It is exciting for all of us to see the vision becoming a reality, knowing that New Warlands Farm is going to be a place where lives are transformed for years to come.”
