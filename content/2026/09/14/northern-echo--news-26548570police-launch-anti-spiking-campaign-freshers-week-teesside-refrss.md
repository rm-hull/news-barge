Students starting college, sixth form or university this month are being urged to stay alert as police launch an anti-spiking campaign across Teesside.

Cleveland Police officers will attend events throughout September to raise awareness, offer safety advice and urge students to report spiking involving themselves or friends.

Spiking involves giving someone alcohol or drugs without their knowledge or permission, including by tampering with drinks or using needles - a crime carrying up to 10 years in prison.

People who have been spiked may suddenly feel dizzy, confused, extremely intoxicated, sick or drowsy, and should seek medical help immediately if they have concerns.

  Spiking can include substances slipped into drinks, but incidents of needle spiking are on the rise. 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

The campaign will cover colleges, sixth forms, universities, pubs and other licensed venues during freshers’ week and the first weeks of term.

Police are working with councils, education providers, venue operators and security staff to prevent spiking and catch offenders.

Sergeant Glyn Bavin, from Cleveland Police’s Licensing Support Unit, said: “This is an exciting time for students and young people as they take their next steps at education facilities across our force area, and we want to reassure them that police and our partners take a robust approach to tackling criminal behaviour like spiking.

“Our co-ordinated partnership approach sees us raising awareness of spiking, promoting student safety, and increasing visibility of reporting and safeguarding initiatives.”

Cleveland Police’s Engagement Team will visit freshers’ events, handing out anti-spiking bottle stoppers and pint-glass covers.

Students will also receive details of the Holly Guard personal safety app and the “Ask for Angela” scheme.

  Fresher's events are kicking off across the North East as students return to college and university. 

*(Image: The Northern Echo)*

Pubs in Stockton will receive anti-spiking beer mats through Pubwatch, while officers continue patrolling and visiting licensed premises in Middlesbrough.

Sergeant Bavin said the force wanted to help young people protect themselves and others while ensuring offenders were “identified and dealt with robustly”.

Police are urging anyone who thinks they or a friend has been spiked to report it and seek help immediately.

Anyone who witnesses crime or anti-social behaviour on a night out can contact Cleveland Police online or on 101. Call 999 in an emergency.
