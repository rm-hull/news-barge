A NEW project shining a light on ‘forgotten suffragettes’ will be launched this weekend.

Suffrage Sisters in Saltaire and Shipley aims to rediscover the stories of local women, and men, who campaigned for votes for women. The stories of two women - of Jessie Millar Wilson and Marion Best - who became leaders of local women’s suffrage organisations will be told as part of the event, at Kirkgate Community Centre in Shipley on Sunday, September 20, from 10.30am to 3pm.

The launch is part of Bradford Heritage Open Days weekend.

Suffrage Sisters will be opened by a special surprise guest - a descendant of a national campaigner who was both a suffragette and a suffragist! The event will include a range of craft activities for all ages, and there’s chance to dress up as a suffragette and have a photo taken in the ‘suffrage studio’.

Says Suffrage Sisters project leader Dr Jennie Kiff: “There will be a selection of costumes and impressive hats to choose from. If the hat fits, why not take a picture of yourself wearing it?

“Hats will be a key theme of this project; they will be used to tell the stories of local campaigners in our final event in 2027. We even have hats for gentlemen, as the founder of the first Men’s League for Women’s Suffrage lived in Shipley.”

Two of the project volunteers have been busy over recent weeks designing and making items that will be sold at the event, including bookmarks, fridge magnets, badges, bags, mugs, even ‘miniature suffragettes’. All proceeds from the sales will go towards supporting the work of this Suffrage Sisters project in the months ahead.

Suffrage Sisters is one of the many projects run by HerVote100, a not for profit CIC creating, supporting and advising on events celebrating the centenary of the Equal Franchise Act in 2028.

The Saltaire and Shipley group will officially start work at the end of September. “We already have a small group of enthusiastic volunteers and we hope our event will encourage more people to join us,” says Dr Kiff. “No previous experience is needed, as we will be showing people how to find our forgotten suffrage campaigners. We will also be exploring new and creative ways to tell their stories and share them with the wider community.

“We hope people will be inspired to become ‘history detectives’ and rediscover the stories of those whose names are missing from the history books. Suffrage Sisters is open to everyone and is free to join.”

As the national co-ordination of the Suffrage Sisters projects Dr Kiff has a busy time ahead: “Other Suffrage Sisters groups have been formed elsewhere, such as Belfast, Leeds and Preston, with many more across the country preparing to start work in 2027.

“Our Grantham branch launched this month and has had a tremendous response from the local community, including the grandson of one leading activist!. There may be people in Saltaire and Shipley who also have family links to local campaigners. It would be wonderful to meet them and include them in our Suffrage Sisters projects.”
