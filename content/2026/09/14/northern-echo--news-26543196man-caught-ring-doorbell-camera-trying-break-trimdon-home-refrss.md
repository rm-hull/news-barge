A drug addict with a record of 100 convictions is back behind bars after being caught on CCTV during a night-time crime spree.

CCTV captured Philip Hewitson tampering with the petrol cap of an Audi A3 on the driveway of a home in Dunelm Road, Trimdon Village, at about 10pm on Wednesday, April 29.

Durham Crown Court was told the owner was watching the CCTV footage as it happened and, when a security light was activated, Hewitson fled the scene.

In a victim statement, the homeowner said the incident raised concerns about home security and the safety of his pets.

Prosecutor Caroline McGurk said Ring doorbell footage showed Hewitson trying the front and back door handles of home in Northside Buildings, Trimdon Grange, shortly after 1am the next morning.

At least once, he pulled his sleeve over his hand to avoid leaving forensic evidence.

Miss McGurk said both doors were securely locked, so Hewitson could not get in.

Police attended and compared descriptions of the suspect from both incidents. They concluded the same man was responsible for both offences and searched the area.

Officers found Hewitson sleeping on Salter’s Lane, Trimdon Grange, at 2.30am.

He was arrested and made no comment in interview.

The 46-year-old, of Milton Road, Hartlepool, admitted vehicle interference and attempted burglary, having originally denied the latter charge.

He appeared for sentence by video link from Holme House Prison in Stockton on Friday (September 11).

The court heard he has 100 convictions for 243 offences, including 171 for theft and similar crimes and, now, eight burglaries or attempted burglaries.

Calum McNicholas, representing Hewitson, said he has fared well in prison while on remand and has used support services available to overcome his drug addiction.

“He is on a drug-free wing and earned enhanced prisoner status, which is progress in the context of a life of drug abuse and acquisitive offending.

“Although he has seven previous burglaries on his record, five were more than 20 years ago.”

Judge Amanda Rippon said Hewitson failed to complete the burglary only because he could not get into the property.

She said such crimes leave people feeling unsettled and unsafe in their own homes.

The judge said it was reassuring that Hewitson was tackling his drug addiction, but warned that failure could lead to more offending.

As she imposed an 11-month prison sentence, she warned Hewitson that failing to overcome his addiction would probably lead to more offending and further jail terms.

She told Hewitson that, under new sentencing guidance, he may serve only 33 to 40 per cent of the sentence in prison.
