KNITTED sheep and canal boats are bringing together community spirit and creativity in Skipton.

Mayor, Winston Feather, recently visited Richard’s Endeavour, an accessible canal boat operated by the charity Spirit of Endeavour, as part of the town’s Skipton Sheep Trail.

He was joined by two of the trail’s 20 woolly sheep, Ralph, and Winston, named after him, during his visit.

Bob Evans, Chair of Trustees and skipper with the Spirit of Endeavour, said: "It’s a real joy and privilege to be part of this project which continues to put smiles on the faces of people who live with disadvantage."

The Skipton Sheep Trail features 20 knitted sheep, including the Mayor’s, displayed around the town and and are being auctioned online until September 28.

Funds raised will support the charity’s work, helping to provide more inclusive canal experiences for people facing mobility barriers.

The charity is celebrating 37 years of community canal trips, originally launched by the former Skipton and Craven Action for Disability (SCAD).

It now operates under the governance of Spirit of Endeavour, which runs the purpose-built, wide-beam boat Richard’s Endeavour on the Leeds and Liverpool Canal.

Each season, the charity delivers around 120 recreational and therapeutic trips.

The boat is fully accessible, equipped with hydraulic lifts, a disabled toilet, and wide aisles to ensure comfort and safety for all passengers.

It can host up to 12 passengers, including space for four wheelchair users, and regularly serves care facilities, community groups, and families.

The charity is entirely volunteer-operated, from trustees to boat crews.

To maintain its schedule, Spirit of Endeavour is seeking additional volunteers with helming experience.

Anyone interested in volunteering can contact [bookings@spiritofendeavour.org.uk.](mailto:bookings@spiritofendeavour.org.uk.)
