The family of PC Tom Clough has expressed their heartfelt thanks to the people of Teesside for the support shown during his funeral on Thursday, September 10.

The family said the community’s response “meant the world” to them and was something they will “never forget”.

They said Tom would have been "amazed and so grateful" to see the support shown by those who lined the route from the funeral home to Kirkleatham Walled Garden, applauding as the cortege passed and paying their respects.

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

The family also thanked drivers whose journeys were disrupted, praising those who left their vehicles and stood with heads bowed as the cortege passed.

The family gave special thanks to neighbours who supported them, along with representatives of the Army, Civil Nuclear Constabulary, British Transport Police, Cleveland Police, the wider police family, fire and rescue, ambulance and NHS services.

   

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

   

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

The family also recognised the support of Redcar and Cleveland Council and staff at Kirkleatham Walled Garden.

In their closing message, they said: "To each and every one of you we are immensely grateful.

“Your support, love and warmth have helped our family navigate Tom’s loss, celebrate his life and honour the legacy he will leave across our communities.

"Thank you."

PC Clough was killed alongside colleague PC Matthew Blades when their marked police car was hit head-on by a VW travelling the wrong way on the A66 at South Bank on August 22, 2026.

His coffin, draped in the Union Jack and surrounded by flowers, was carried down Plantation Road as police officers stood shoulder-to-shoulder in tribute.

Hundreds of officers paid respects to their fallen colleague, many wearing a patch bearing a the thin blue line and the officers’ names and collar numbers - PC 2443 Blades and PC 3234 Clough.

44,000 people donated to the fundraiser, created by Cleveland Police Federation which closed today (September 1), at 9am, raising £1,203,932.

Organisers said money raised would be split equally between the two officers’ families, while the federation would continue to provide support.

They described the kindness and generosity shown by the public as “overwhelming”.
