**HARROGATE** — Regional transport and tourism heads have unveiled the official programme for a major autumn recreational campaign, opening up dozens of specialized outdoor events across the county border.

The annual **York Walking Festival** will formally commence on**Saturday 19 September 2026**, staging an extensive series of community group itineraries through to its closing day on** Sunday 4 October 2026**. Municipal coordinators at City of York Council have confirmed that all scheduled walks are completely free for the public to attend, though select high-demand slots will require advanced digital registration due to strict guide-to-passenger safety ratios.

The 16-day initiative has been designed to address public health and environmental targets simultaneously. Local authority health frameworks highlight NHS data indicating that regular low-impact walking routines can lower the risk of early mortality by up to 30 per cent, while providing crucial long-term mitigation against cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and strokes.

## From Bat Walks to Civil War Sieges

The 2026 scheduling introduces a diverse mix of historical analysis, environmental monitoring, and mental health programming. Key features across the initial launch calendar include:

- **River Foss Wildlife Wander (Saturday 19 September):** A five-mile scenic ecology trek tracing the riverbank from Haxby down into the city center.
- **University Bat Walk (Monday 21 September):** An evening wildlife logging expedition led by the East Yorkshire Bat Group tracking nocturnal species across the University of York campus.
- **The Guildhall ‘Wrong Uns’ Tour (Wednesday 23 September):** An active historical lookback at medieval law enforcement and crime infrastructure directed by regional specialist Richard Taylor.
- **Civil War Siege of 1644 (Friday 2 October):** A military history tour exploring the defensive layout and structural bombardment zones of the historic fortress city.

**Richard Taylor, The History Man and Festival Guide:** “The York Walking Festival offers a wonderful opportunity to step outside and uncover the city’s hidden stories and best-kept secrets. While many people know York for its Romans, Vikings and Saxons, my walks reveal a very different side of the city’s past, from lawbreakers to the dramatic siege of 1644. If you enjoy great stories and surprising history, we’d love to see you there.”


The festival will also serve as the official platform to launch *Walk York*, a new regional pedestrian group working alongside national charity Living Streets to campaign for wider public pathways, safer crossings, and reduced architectural barriers for wheelers and pedestrians.

**Cllr Kate Ravilious, Executive Member for Transport at City of York Council:** “York is a wonderfully walkable city. The York Walking Festival is about helping people discover just how enjoyable it can be to get outdoors, exploring the city and spending time with others. What’s more, walking is one of the simplest ways people can look after both their physical health and wellbeing. We hope everyone will take advantage of the opportunity to find new favourite routes, enjoy York’s parks and green spaces, and help York become a healthier and more environmentally friendly city.”


## Logistics, Accessibility, and Central Portal Access

Organisers have explicitly color-coded the 2026 route logs to ensure maximum accessibility. Several specialized loops—including the newly opened 1.2-kilometer York Rivers Trail launching on **Saturday 3 October**—are mapped as completely flat, step-free, and fully optimized for families with pushchairs and individuals utilizing mobility aids.

Participants attending longer rural trail dates are strictly instructed to bring appropriate waterproof footwear and independent hydration packs. The complete digital directory, individual booking forms, and last-minute weather scheduling updates can be accessed directly via the master travel grid at iTravel York Walking Festival Portal.
