A public inquiry into the refusal of a "ridiculous" plan for 600 new homes in Yarm is set to get underway tomorrow.

Taylor Wimpey lodged an appeal earlier this year after Stockton Borough Council threw out its plan for hundreds of new homes on the fields south of Green Lane.

The developer said it was "disappointed" by the decision, which followed months of backlash from residents over potential “chaos” on facilities and infrastructure.

The public inquiry is set to kick off tomorrow (September 15) at Stockton Council’s headquarters at Dunedin House, in Thornaby.

The inquiry will see the plans come under the scrutiny of barristers and expert witnesses and is expected to last up to nine days.

   

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

The £133m plans for the new estate were rejected this year by councillors, who voted 13-1, following almost 600 objections and even a ‘Save Yarm’ petition.

Taylor Wimpey lodged an appeal against the decision shortly after.

The inspector, George Underwood, expects to hear statements and evidence from the council and developer about landscape, roads, ecology, planning and infrastructure.

Taylor Wimpey argues permission should be granted for the “relatively modest” number of homes in a “sustainable location”.

But the impact of the sheer amount of homes on traffic and the plans to add two new junctions to the already busy Green Lane remain one of several major concerns.

Councillors previously criticised the suggestion that the development would add just one minute 27 seconds of time to a six minute 23 seconds journey in the mornings - and no increases in excess of a minute in the evenings.

The impact on Northern Powergrid’s electricity network is also a worry, with the area’s network having “limited capacity to support additional homes without upgrades”.

Councillor Andrew Sherris said residents are already “well versed in experiencing regular electrical outages” particularly on the East side of Yarm.

He said that, just last Sunday (September 6), Hutchinson Hobbs Farm Shop had been forced to close due to an outage, with emergency generators brought in to safeguard stock.

   

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

“The evidence presented by Taylor Wimpey does not demonstrate that electrical capacity for 600 homes is currently available or secured,” he argued.

Cllr Sherris said there were therefore concerns over how the developer would build 140 homes by 2031/32; 10 in 2028/29, 30 in 2029/30 and 50 in each of 2030/31 and 2031/32.

He said a permanent connection would depend on a sub station being built elsewhere - and that, while Mount Leven was proposed as a location, no planning application has been made.

Cllr Sherris previously argued the site was “far more” than just hitting golf balls, with significant investment being put into the facility in recent years.

The Northern Echo understands that the decision on the range will ultimately lie with the developer if the appeal is successful.

Taylor Wimpey previously said it believed the site to be “sustainable and suitable for development” and remained "committed to engaging with the process”.

A spokesperson said: "Matters relating to electricity capacity will be discussed through the Public Inquiry process, and as such it would be inappropriate for us to comment further on specific technical points ahead of that.

“We will continue to engage constructively with Stockton Council and all stakeholders.”
