A North East couple hope to help other families after the heartbreaking loss of their son.

Aleem Ditta and his wife Alina Pervez, both 25, from Middlesbrough, were devastated at the loss of their first child, Noah Aleem Ditta, in April this year.

They want to use their experience to help other bereaved families and give back to those who supported them.

The married couple are walking 5km in support of the James Cook Hospital NICU which treated their son.

Aleem and Alina will set off from Middlesbrough Cricket Club at 10.30am on October 11 and walk to James Cook Hospital.

The event, which will take place during Baby Loss Awareness Week, is not just to tell Noah’s story, they said, but for “every baby gone too soon,” and every family “who have experienced the heartbreak of baby loss.”

Baby Loss Awareness Week is a national campaign involving charities including Sands and Tommy's, providing anyone affected by pregnancy or baby loss with a safe, supportive space.

*(Image: ALEEM DITTA)*

“When you have a child, you plan so much ahead, you plan all these memories, and it’s almost been taken away from you,” Aleem said.

A scan at 20 weeks showed Aleem and Alina’s son was not growing as he should have been.

A second scan showed Noah had fetal growth restriction, meaning he was not receiving enough oxygen or nutrients to develop properly.

Noah was born by C-section on April 12, at 25 weeks and five days, weighing just 550 grams.

Aleem described it as the “best day of my life”.

“It was perfect,” he said, “he delivered the smallest of cries.”

Noah’s condition deteriorated despite round-the-clock care from the James Cook Hospital neonatal intensive care team.

Aleem said his lungs were unable to circulate enough blood around his body, despite oxygen being supplied.

Thirteen days after his birth, the decision was made to turn off Noah’s life-support machinery and allow him to die peacefully.

*(Image: Aleem Ditta)*

Aleem said: "The most difficult thing is walking out without your baby because that’s the last thing you possibly think about."

Aleem described seeing nurses provide round-the-clock care to critically ill babies in the James Cook Hospital neonatal intensive care unit as “incredible” but difficult to witness:

“It’s the most incredible thing you’ll ever see, the most incredible but disturbing place," he added.

Aleem and Alina thanked the NICU team for all of their hard work and compassion during what were some of the couple’s most mentally taxing days.

The couple are now fundraising in an effort to give back to the ward and its staff.

The couple have raised more than £1,800 for James Cook Hospital’s neonatal intensive care unit since launching their appeal on September 5. They said the unit supported them “like a family.”

Aleem said: "They went above and beyond what a nurse should have to do.

"They couldn't have done any more, for not just my baby, but any baby.”

The couple also want to raise awareness of baby loss in their community and promote unity with others during heightened tensions in Middlesbrough.

Aleem spoke about the stigma surrounding baby loss and said he wanted Noah’s legacy to bring people from different backgrounds together.

He added: "Within our specific kind of Pakistani heritage, it's very taboo to mention miscarriages or loss of babies, and that's totally the wrong way [to go] about it, because if you keep bottling things in it'll never be a good thing.

"It will eventually come out.”

The couple hope the event will unite people from different backgrounds behind an important cause at a tense time for Middlesbrough.

Aleem said racial tensions and division had grown in the town, and that he and his wife hoped to unite locals behind a cause everyone could support.

Aleem said the fundraiser had exceeded their expectations, with hundreds interested in taking part and more than £1,800 raised at time of publication.

The couple hope to make this an annual fundraiser, ensuring Noah’s life has a lasting legacy, helping other families who have experienced the loss of a baby while raising money for the staff and resources that supported them.

Aleem said: "We're quite amazed at the support that we've got.

"I'm very grateful as well.”

Aleem and Alina urged anyone struggling after a loss to seek help, including counselling and support from the NHS and neonatal team.

“Time gives you an opportunity to accept what’s happened and try and move on with your life as best as possible,” Aleem said.
