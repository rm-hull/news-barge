Bradford Council says it has not received any complaints about reports of late-night drumming in Frizinghall, despite residents claiming the noise left them unable to sleep.

The authority issued a statement after concerns were raised over loud drumming allegedly heard in a residential area of Frizinghall at around 11.20pm on August 28.

A video shared with campaigners appears to capture the noise, with one resident claiming the disturbance was so loud it "sounded like a bomb".

## Council says no complaints received

Responding to concerns, a Bradford Council spokesperson said the authority had not been made aware of the specific incident through formal complaints.

The spokesperson said: "We have not received any complaints about this issue in Frizinghall."

The statement comes after residents told the Telegraph & Argus they were becoming increasingly frustrated by what they described as repeated late-night noise disturbances in the area.

One woman said she had been woken suddenly by the drumming and was left shaken by the experience.

## 
  **Concerns over wider noise nuisance**

Although the council said no complaints had been received regarding the alleged drumming incident, it acknowledged that noise nuisance linked to celebrations and fireworks remains an ongoing issue across the district.

The spokesperson said: "However, we are aware of the noise nuisance caused by fireworks and celebrations which is a recurring problem across the district."

Residents who spoke to the T&A claimed noise from drumming, fireworks, loud music and vehicles had become increasingly common in recent years.

## 
  **'Bang Out of Order' campaign**

The council said it has sought to tackle concerns around inappropriate fireworks use through its ongoing "Bang Out of Order" campaign.

The spokesperson said the campaign aims to raise awareness of the impact that fireworks can have on residents, pets, vulnerable people and emergency services.

They added: "The campaign promotes responsible and considerate use of fireworks and encourages people to celebrate safely."

## 
  **Calls for stronger powers**

Bradford Council said it remains committed to working with communities and partner organisations to reduce nuisance and anti-social behaviour linked to celebrations.

The spokesperson said: "We are committed to working with local communities, partner agencies, and residents to minimise nuisance and ensure that celebrations can take place without causing unnecessary disturbance to others."

The authority also encouraged residents to take part in the Government's consultation on tougher firework regulations.

However, the council acknowledged its powers are limited under current legislation.

The spokesperson added: "Until stronger legislation exists, our powers to deal with nuisance fireworks are limited."

## 
  **Demonstration planned**

The latest concerns come after a demonstration outside Norfolk Gardens last weekend.

Campaigners say the event will focus on concerns surrounding fireworks, noise nuisance and anti-social behaviour, with several Bradford councillors invited to attend.

Residents hope the demonstration will encourage authorities to take further action to address repeated disturbances affecting communities across Bradford.
