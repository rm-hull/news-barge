Sam Smith's Brewery has put more Yorkshire pubs up for sale.

The Tadcaster-based brewery has placed several of its properties on the market, including the Wellington Inn in Knaresborough, the Albion Inn in Wakefield, and the Bulls Head Inn on Dewsbury Road in Wakefield.

The Wellington Inn, located at 23 Briggate, is being marketed by estate agents Fleurets for £325,000, with the listing specifying "non pub use only."

The property includes a bar, lounge, kitchen, three-bedroom accommodation, a garden, and parking for six to eight cars.

It has been closed since 2022 and was the last remaining Sam Smith's pub in Knaresborough.

The brewery also owns two other long-closed sites in the town: the Marquis of Granby on York Place and the Commercial Hotel, formerly the Borough Bailiff, on High Street.

The sale has sparked concern among members of Facebook groups who follow the brewery's activities.

The BUlls Head is described as a 2 storey rendered brick detached building under a pitched stone roof. It has single storey flat roofed and rendered sections to the front.

To the right side there is a car park for c.6 cars and to the front a tarmac area suitable for either external seating or an additional 4 parking spaces or so.

Tom Taverner, a Malton resident who maintains a national list of open and closed Sam Smith's pubs, has produced a list of nine Sam Smith’s pubs he says are on the market.

In addition to The Wellington Inn at Knaresborough, he also listed pubs in Barnsley, Wakefield, Rochdale and elsewhere, being marketed by Fleurets or BTG Eddisons.

This follows the recent sale of two Sam Smith's pubs at auction in Doncaster and Bradford for £230,000 and £262,000 respectively.

Some social media users have questioned whether the company is shifting its approach by selling off properties rather than reopening them.

One pointed out that former chairman Humphrey Smith, who died several months ago, hardly ever sold pubs.

Others have speculated whether the brewery's policy has changed since his passing.

Sam Smith's Brewery has recently expressed ambitions to support the regeneration of Tadcaster.

At a town council meeting last month, North Yorkshire councillor Kirsty Poskitt read a letter from the brewery outlining its plans.

The brewery has proposed building 101 homes on Station Road in Tadcaster, with the aim of funding improvements to properties in the town centre.

North Yorkshire Council has not yet approved the scheme, but it has the backing of Tadcaster Town Council.

Cllr Poskitt said: "There was a commitment [from the brewery] to getting those buildings back into use."

Christian Horton, director at Samuel Smith’s Brewery, said he would prefer not to comment on commercial matters like pub sales.

However, Mr Horton said: "The brewery is continuing to look for new pub managers to reopen any closed pubs."
