A York man has received a suspended prison sentence after being convicted of hanging an abusive flag on the city walls directed at York MP Rachael Maskell.

Lee Moran, 55, draped the St George’s Cross near Walmgate Bar with a message reading: “This one’s for you, Maskill”, which was a deliberate misspelling of her name.

The message was aimed at the York Central MP because she had spoken out about the hanging of St George’s Cross flags on lampposts during the Operation Raise the Colours campaign and had called for their removal.

Maskell said the flag, which was hung on 16 September last year, caused her to feel “distressed with the threat of being a target in political violence”.

Moran appeared at York Magistrates’ Court for sentence today (Monday) after being convicted of an offence under the Communications Act and two public-order offences following a trial.

Prosecutor Verity Barnes Operation Raise the Colours had spread throughout the country in what was seen as a patriotic call-to-arms by some but an “intimidating” anti-migrant campaign by others.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/york-city-walls-maskill-flag-16-sept-2025.webp)

Ms Maskell was vocal about calling for flags to be removed from lampposts in York, saying in a statement read out at the trial that they had caused division in communities and cost the council £250,000 to remove.

She also said that she feared the flag would “incite a larger group of people”.

She said that at the time she was “in a position of vulnerability due to my suspension of the Labour whip” following her rebellion over welfare cuts. Ms Maskell has since had the Labour whip returned.

“This has been my position from the start of the movement,” she said.

Moran, of Brunswick Street, said he disagreed with Ms Maskell and, writing on social media a week before he hung the flag from the city walls, he called the MP a “traitor to the English people” for suggesting the flags should be removed.

## MPs fear for their safety

Moran said he had deliberately misspelt Ms Maskell’s surname as “Maskill” on the flag but denied he did so in a threatening way, claiming was suggesting the MP was a “kill joy” or a “buzzkill”.

He said it was “over the top” for the message to be interpreted as a threat and that “only cynical people” would interpret it as a death threat.

Prosecutor Ms Barnes read out a statement from Sir Lindsay Hoyle, the Speaker of the House of Commons, who said that MPs were becoming increasingly concerned for their safety following the deaths of ex-Labour MP Jo Cox and former Tory MP David Amiss.

“Democracy itself is at risk as a result of threats (made to MPs),” he added.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/rachael-maskell-york-central-mp.webp)

He said he was aware of some MPs being reluctant to stand for election due to receiving threats from members of the public.

Mr Moran also fell to be sentenced for a public-order offence at the city walls on 16 September last year, the same day he erected the flag.

Ms Barnes said that a woman had been walking along Walmgate Bar when she came across Moran and the flag which was blocking her path.

“She accidentally knocked one of the poles and (Moran) hurled abuse at her,” added Ms Barnes.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/sir-lindsay-hoyle-commons-speaker-official-portrait.webp)

She said that the woman feared violence would be used against her.

The Communications Act offence, between 15 and 18 September, involved a heated discussion which Moran had with a named female on Facebook’s comments section after a photo of the flag was uploaded onto the site.

Ms Moran said that Moran had 45 previous offences on his record including public-order offences.

## Serious debt

His solicitor said that Moran was currently claiming Universal Credit and was in “serious debt”.

Deputy district judge Tanik Ram echoed Mr Hoyle’s fears about the current threat to MPs in today’s febrile political climate.

He added: “Two members of Parliament have been murdered and others MPs are feeling fearful of expressing a political view.

“We live in a democratic world where you are entitled to your views but others are also entitled to their views… and there is a way to disagree politically, through reasoned debate, without threat and through the ballot box.”

He told Moran: “Politicians have to feel safe in being able to express a political view and I say that of all political persuasions. I’m not singling out any political view.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/york-magistrates-court-close-tower.webp)

He gave Moran a 14-week prison sentence, but this was suspended for 12 months.

He told the defendant that had it not been for recent changes in the law, in which there is a presumption in favour of suspending jail sentences of 12 months or less, “I’d have seriously considered an immediate custodial sentence”.

He ordered Moran to pay £70 compensation to the woman he verbally abused on the city walls.

Moran was also ordered to pay £100 costs and a victim surcharge of £154.

Outside court following sentence, Moran said he intended to appeal against both conviction and sentence.

He told ***YorkMix***: “There was no intention of sending any sort of death threat (to Ms Maskell). It was just a play on words.”

He added: “I don’t regret anything. I’ve got no regret and I don’t apologise to nobody (sic). No threat or cynicism was intended by the flag.

“I wrote it to wind up Rachael Maskell, her bubble … her friends, her social circle.”
