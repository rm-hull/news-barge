York Minster will offer a behind-the-scenes glimpse into its world-renowned conservation work – as it opens its doors to their Centre for Excellence.

As part of England’s largest festival of history and culture, Heritage Open Days, visitors will have the opportunity to visit the Minster’s award-winning Heritage Quad to meet the expert teams of craftspeople and heritage specialists who care for one of the world’s most magnificent cathedrals.

Teams from across the Minster – including stonemasons, gardeners, scaffolders, and glaziers from York Glaziers Trust – will be on hand to showcase their extraordinary skills and share how they care for Minster on **Friday 18 September**.

Usually only visible from the City Walls, the Heritage Quad is an integral part of York Minster’s award-winning Centre for Excellence for Heritage Skills and Estate Management, home to the expert stonemasons and gardeners who care for the Minster and its precinct.

Visitors will be able to discover how traditional crafts and modern innovation come together to safeguard this incredible cathedral and its surrounding spaces for future generations.

This event will also provide an opportunity to learn more about several major projects currently underway, including the conservation of the iconic Rose Window; the restoration of the South Quire Aisle pinnacles, where visitors will be able to see newly crafted grotesques up close; and the Minster’s work to create a diverse habitat for wildlife within the city centre.

Alexander McCallion, director of works and precinct at York Minster, said, “We are delighted to open the doors of the Heritage Quad and showcase the craft skills, dedication and innovation involved in caring for the Minster.

“Visitors will be able to meet the people behind our conservation work, ask questions, and discover how we are combining heritage preservation with ambitious sustainability and biodiversity initiatives to ensure the Minster continues to thrive for generations to come.”

**The Heritage Quad will be open to visitors on Friday 18 September from 9:30am to 3pm**. Entry is free and no booking is required. 

The meeting point will be in Dean’s Park, located behind the Minster, opposite the Old Palace Library.
