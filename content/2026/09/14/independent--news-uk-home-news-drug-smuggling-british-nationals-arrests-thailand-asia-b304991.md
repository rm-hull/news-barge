The number of British nationals detained overseas on drug trafficking charges has surged more than fourfold over three years, official figures show, alongside a Government warning that penalties include life imprisonment and the death sentence.

Consular incidents involving suspected "drug mules" grew from 80 in 2023 to above 340 in 2025, with at least 160 recorded so far this year.

Over 200 UK citizens are presently held in foreign custody for smuggling cannabis originating in Thailand. Around 60% of those caught since 2024 are aged between 16 and 30.

This rise has been driven by cannabis detentions across south-east Asia, including Thailand, Malaysia, Cambodia and Laos, the Foreign Office said.

It added recruits are often told luggage destined for UK contains clothes, gold or cash – with suitcases actually containing up to 71kg of cannabis.

![British teenager Bella May Culley after being arrested in Georgia for smuggling cannabis from Thailand.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/05/16/7/08/r4ewfacscScreenshot-2025-05-16-at-08-43-36-copy.png)

Evidence from consular casework and law enforcement revealed a growing pattern of young British nationals being used by criminal networks, with the Government warning travellers to “think twice” before carrying luggage, packages or goods on behalf of others.

Some young victims have been targeted on social media with promises of “free travel, easy money or low-risk opportunities”, and are often told luggage is filled with clothes, gold or cash.

Research commissioned by the Foreign Office suggests some young travellers underestimate the risks, with a third of those surveyed saying they did not consider luggage packed by someone else as potentially dangerous,

In addition, 30% said they would consider travelling abroad with an item despite being unsure it was permitted.

Foreign Office minister Stephen Doughty said: “Far too many British nationals are being lured into drug smuggling schemes by criminal gangs that promise free travel or a quick way to make cash.

“The reality is very different, and detainees are warning about the dire living conditions they now face decades of in prison or, in some countries, the possibility of the death penalty.

“Organised criminals are only interested in their own profit and do not care about the devastating consequences for the people they recruit.

![British citizen Thomas Parker was also recently sentenced for drug offenses, in Denpasar in Bali.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/05/27/8/07/Indonesia-British-Drug-Trial-fr60ziub.jpeg)

“Our advice is clear: never carry drugs or items for others. Remember no holiday, payment or opportunity is worth risking your freedom, your future or your life.”

The Foreign Office said penalties abroad range from lengthy prison sentences to life imprisonment, whipping and, in some countries, the death penalty.

In warnings by people detained abroad, one British national held on cannabis offences said: “At the end of the day, it’s your freedom you’re risking, and once it’s gone, it’s not easy to get back.”

Another said: “When I was told about the job, it made it seem [like] everything will be okay and nothing illegal will happen, and [I would get] to enjoy a free holiday. […] You start to think about many things and moments you will miss out on. My heart hurts.”

One detained British national described feeling “a sense of mourning for the life I had”.

The Foreign Office added that legal proceedings can be lengthy and costly, with suspects spending months in detention before their cases reach court.

Prison conditions can be harsh, access to healthcare limited and British nationals have previously died while in custody in south-east Asia, it said.
