Volunteers at a Bradford nature reserve say they have uncovered a hidden trail that has left trees cut down, wildlife habitats disturbed and deep pits dug into protected land.

The Friends of Prince of Wales Park in Bingley say the unauthorised route has been carved into a steep section of the reserve, with concerns that years of conservation work could be undone by the damage.

## 
  **Trees cut down and deep pits dug**

A spokesperson for the Friends of Prince of Wales Park said the trail appears to have been built without permission and stretches across a significant area.

They said: “Somebody has decided they will remove some trees and cut trees down.

They’ve dug very deep pits, probably about five foot deep, to create enough soil to make banking.

They’ve made jumps out of cut pieces of wood and also this soil, and it goes on for quite an extensive way.”

The group believes similar activity had previously taken place elsewhere in the park before apparently moving location.


## 
  **Concerns for wildlife**

The park is designated as a Local Nature Reserve, and volunteers say they are particularly concerned about the impact on wildlife.

The spokesperson said hedgehog houses had been installed in the area and that the trail had been created just feet away from them.

They said: “The saddest thing for us is that in the area that we placed some hedgehog houses, they have dug no more than two feet away from them.

If there were hedgehogs there, that would dissuade them from staying and finding somewhere else.”

The group also raised concerns about the wider ecological impact, including damage to soil structure and habitats used by insects, fungi and other wildlife.

They added: “That’s one of the reasons why it’s a local nature reserve, to preserve all of the things from the very smallest to the largest creatures that live there.”

## 
  **Damage could take decades to recover**

Volunteers fear the environmental impact could last for years.

The spokesperson said: “We can make it look like it looked before, but the trees are dead.

You know how long trees take to grow.

I suspect maybe 20 years to get back to where it was.”

They added that while damaged areas could be restored visually, the loss of trees and disturbance to the ground would take much longer to recover.

## 
  **Volunteers frustrated by repeat problems**

The group says the incident is part of a wider pattern of vandalism and anti-social behaviour affecting the park.

According to the spokesperson, plants have previously been stolen shortly after being planted, while displays created for a Halloween trail were damaged or destroyed.

They said: “It’s very distressing for us and it’s very upsetting.

The community should be distressed as well because it’s our park.

It’s not just Bradford Council’s park, it’s our park.”

## 
  **Council says activity not permitted**

Bradford Council confirmed it is aware of the issue and is working with the Friends group and neighbourhood teams.

A council spokesperson said: “We are aware of this issue and are going to work with the park’s Friends of group and our Neighbourhoods team to discourage trail building.

"Cycling and disturbing the ground are prohibited by Parks and Green Spaces bylaws, unless the Council has given permission.

"We have not given permission for such activities in this park to either cycle in these areas or to create a trail.”

The council added that anyone who witnesses active trail building can report it to West Yorkshire Police on 101 as anti-social behaviour and criminal damage, or to the council via its customer contact service.

## 
  **Education rather than enforcement**

Despite their frustration, volunteers say they do not want to stop people enjoying the park and would rather help visitors understand why the reserve is important.

The spokesperson said: “We don’t want to stop them playing in the park. We don’t want to stop them enjoying it. We want to share it...if they understood what they were doing, then perhaps they would make the correct choices.”

They added that the park’s Local Nature Reserve status is relatively new and that some visitors may not fully appreciate its environmental significance.
