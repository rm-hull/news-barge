A NEW 'Home from Home' for families with babies receiving specialist neonatal care at Bradford Royal Infirmary is set to open next spring.

The facility, being developed by The Sick Children's Trust and Bradford Hospitals Charity, will provide free accommodation and support for families needing to stay close to their babies during treatment.

The project has moved a step closer to reality after the organisations revealed they have raised almost £2.5 million of their £3 million fundraising target.

## Construction well underway

The new accommodation will provide a warm and welcoming place for families whose babies are receiving care on the neonatal intensive care unit at Bradford Royal Infirmary.

Once complete, the facility is expected to support up to 500 families every year, allowing parents and relatives to remain close to their loved ones during some of the most difficult periods of their lives.

Nathan Grant, Director of Operations at The Sick Children's Trust, said: "Construction of the new 'Home from Home' is well underway, with doors set to open in Spring 2027."

Mr Grant said: "It will offer a free, warm and welcoming place to stay, along with vital support, for up to 500 families a year whose babies are receiving specialist care on the neonatal intensive care unit."

## Nearly £2.5m raised

Campaigners say the 'Keep Families Together' appeal has now raised almost £2.5 million towards its £3 million goal.

Mr Grant thanked members of the public, charitable foundations and businesses that have supported the fundraising campaign.

He said: "Thanks to immense public support, The Sick Children's Trust and Bradford Hospitals Charity's 'Keep Families Together' fundraising appeal for our new 'Home from Home' at Bradford Royal Infirmary has raised almost £2.5 million towards our £3 million target.

"We are delighted with this progress and offer heartfelt thanks to everyone who has helped us reach this point."

Among those recognised for major contributions were the Harry and Mary Foundation, Garfield Weston Foundation and Sovereign Healthcare.

## Final push for funding

Despite the progress, around £500,000 still needs to be raised before the appeal reaches its target.

Fundraisers are encouraging further donations to help bring the project to completion and provide vital support for families facing lengthy hospital stays with new-born babies.

Mr Grant added: "Every pound raised will make a world of difference to the families of neonatal babies being treated at Bradford Royal Infirmary."

The accommodation will be the first 'Home from Home' of its kind at Bradford Royal Infirmary and aims to ensure families can stay together while babies receive specialist hospital care.
