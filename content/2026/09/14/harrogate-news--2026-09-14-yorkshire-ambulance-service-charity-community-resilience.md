**YORKSHIRE** — Regional healthcare leaders have initiated a targeted emergency response program across select high-demand communities, deploying major national grant funding to combat deep-seated regional health inequalities.

The **Yorkshire Ambulance Service Charity (YAS Charity)** has officially launched a specialized community intervention project targeting two specific areas highlighted by severe population health needs:**Manningham in Bradford** and**Goldthorpe in Barnsley**.

Formally unveiled on **Monday 14 September 2026**, the initiative is backed by a dedicated two-year funding deployment from the national** NHS Charities Together**Community Resilience Grants Fund, which distributes a total**£1.94 million** pool across 14 chosen regional ambulance charities.

The deployment strategy relies heavily on data-driven targeting. Corporate ambulance logs indicate that these specific urban and industrial zones currently generate exceptionally high emergency ambulance demand while simultaneously tracking some of the poorest baseline health outcomes and lowest public medical intervention rates in the region. The project combines immediate first aid training with preventative public health messaging to mitigate long-term chronic illness trends.

## Combating Low Out-of-Hospital Survival Rates

The project’s immediate operational focus is to radically shift survival statistics for cardiac emergencies. Current regional medical data confirms that only **one in 10 individuals** who experience an out-of-hospital cardiac arrest survive to return home to their families. However, rapid community intervention via early identification, immediate cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), and prompt access to automated external defibrillators (AEDs) can drive survival probability past**50 per cent**.

To establish permanent infrastructure beyond the initial two-year cycle, the charity has appointed a dedicated Community Engagement and Resilience Officer. The operational roadmap relies on four core structural activities:

- **Train the Trainer Partnerships:** Equipping local volunteers, school staff, and community leaders with standard instructional modules to build an autonomous network of certified Community Trainers.
- **Public Defibrillator Infrastructure:** Auditing local population densities to install additional Community Public Access Defibrillators (CPADs) in high-risk zones.
- **Workforce Recruitment Drives:** Actively recruiting local residents into official Yorkshire Ambulance Service (YAS) pathways, including Community First Responders (CFRs), transport volunteers, and patient experience focus groups.
- **Employability Training:** Conducting structured life-skills and career courses designed to bridge local applicants directly into professional ambulance service employment tracks.

**Ripaljeet Kaur, Project Delivery Officer:** “These areas have been chosen because of population health needs, significant health inequalities and high ambulance demand. This project builds on existing initiatives such as Restart a Heart, but goes further by working alongside communities, listening to what matters to them and learning from patient experiences. We want to equip people with life-saving skills and give them the confidence to train others, so knowledge and support can continue within the community.”


The initiative has been actively welcomed by central charity executives, who note that health discrepancies place minority ethnic groups, impoverished neighborhoods, and isolated coastal or urban blocks at exponentially higher risk during sudden medical crises.

**Carey Taylor, Head of YAS Charity:** “The Charity’s aim is to engage with communities and to save lives, which this project helps us to achieve. As well as equipping people with skills, we hope that this project will make these skills accessible to all, leaving a lasting impact within these communities and creating lasting partnerships. We are also proud to be combining our core life-saving skills training with more preventative, public-health-related interventions.”


## Community Engagement and Registry Information

The YAS Charity operates as a legally registered independent entity under formal **Charity Number 1114106**, dedicated to expanding emergency resources and funding wellbeing provisions for frontline emergency service personnel.

Regional community groups, local schools looking to enroll in the Restart a Heart Schools network, or neighborhood volunteers looking to secure placement training can coordinate directly with the emergency engagement team via the dedicated communication gateway at [yas.communityengagement@nhs.net.](mailto:yas.communityengagement@nhs.net.)
