Concerns that walkers on the Coast to Coast National Trail are putting themselves at risk by attempting to cross the A19 on foot are set to be raised with the mayor of York and North Yorkshire.

Councillor David Hugill will highlight the issue when David Skaith attends the next meeting of North Yorkshire Council’s Richmond area committee on Monday.

Cllr Hugill, member for Hutton Rudby and Osmotherley, who also chairs Ingleby Arncliffe Parish Council, says walkers following the popular long-distance trail regularly attempt to cross the 70mph dual carriageway near the village.

A bridge further along the A19 offers a safer route, but few walkers appear to use it, possibly because it adds distance late in a tiring day.

Cllr Hugill said walkers sitting on the central reservation’s crash barrier and waiting for gaps in traffic was a “regular occurrence”.

He said the danger was especially serious because Ingleby Arncliffe is a popular Coast to Coast stop, where walkers stay overnight or take refreshments.

Cllr Hugill said he welcomed investment by the Combined Authority in a long-term approach to road safety combining infrastructure, enforcement and prevention, but added that a bridge for walkers across the A19 remained on the community’s “wish-list”.

He will tell the mayor that the dangers facing walkers do not end once they have crossed the dual carriageway.

Concerns have also been raised about speeding vehicles through the village and heavy traffic using narrow local roads as a shortcut to the A19 instead of travelling via the A172.

Cllr Hugill said this was putting both residents and visitors at risk, particularly because there is no pavement along the section used by Coast to Coast walkers.

He also claims there is little or no enforcement of the village’s 30mph limit or the national speed limit on roads around it.

Plans for a footbridge over the A19 near Ingleby Arncliffe have been discussed for years to give Coast to Coast walkers a safer crossing.

Feasibility work is complete and a preferred option has been identified, but no funding has been agreed.

The concerns are among several local transport issues Mr Skaith will hear when he meets the committee on Monday.
