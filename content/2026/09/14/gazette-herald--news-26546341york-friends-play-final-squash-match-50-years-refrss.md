When the squash ball hit the tin with a final hollow thud, silence fell over court four at the York Railway Institute.

For John Bridge, 86, and Trevor Elliott, 80, it was the sound of an era coming to an end.

After more than 50 years of shared sweat, laughter and competition, the friends stepped off the hardwood court on the evening of Tuesday, August 25, together for the very last time.

What started decades ago as a simple weekly fixture grew into the bedrock of their lives.

Over the years, the venue witnessed the quiet passage of time: their thick hair grew thinner, they carried a few more pounds, lightning sprints and reactions turned to calculated drops and cunning, they became grandads and great grandads and the world outside the court shifted dramatically.

Yet every week, they met in the confines of a 32' by 21' court to re-join their perpetual combat.

With the flush of exercise and released endorphins the two would repair to the RI bar and the company of friends and warm hospitality of the bar keep, Pete Watson.

The sanctuary of the Railway Institute – and the deep understanding between two men who knew each other's every move – remained untouchable.

"You don't only just share a court for more than five decades," Elliott said quietly, looking at the scuffed walls. "You share a life."

As the last game ended, there were no grand trophies or roaring crowds – just two old friends shaking hands and smiling at one another, for a lingering moment while they remember all their wonderful times and old friends at the RI.
