A West Yorkshire secondary school has been rated as "needs attention" in a recent Ofsted inspection.

Spen Valley High School, in Liversedge, was inspected on June 2 and 3, 2026.

Inspectors found that the school "needs attention" in several areas including achievement, attendance and behaviour, curriculum and teaching, leadership and governance, and personal development and wellbeing.

## 'Gaps in knowledge'

The report states: "Pupils do not make the progress that they should across the curriculum.

"This means that too many pupils reach the end of key stage 4 with gaps in their knowledge.

"As a result, pupils have not achieved as well as their peers nationally in public tests and examinations.

"This has been the case for a number of years."

## 'Attendance lower than it should be'

The report continued: "Pupils' attendance is lower than it should be.

"Too many pupils are regularly absent from school.

"They miss too many lessons, which results in them not making the progress that they should."

Inspectors found "teaching across the school is inconsistent" and that "staff do not always adapt their teaching effectively to pupils' starting points."

They said: "the governing body is committed to the school.

"However, governors are not sufficiently rigorous in overseeing all areas of the school's provision.

"As a result, underperformance has not been challenged in several key areas."

## Some pupils do not know what a democracy is

The report also states that "the school has a personal development programme that includes personal, social and health education lessons for pupils in Year 7 to Year 10, alongside daily coaching sessions and regular assemblies.

"However, pupils do not develop their knowledge of other important topics, including what it means to be a citizen in modern Britain.

"This means that some pupils struggle to explain, for example, what a democratic society is."

## Positives highlighted in the report

The report also states that "the safeguarding standards are met" and that "leaders are committed to ensuring that inclusion is at the heart of this school."

The report says "leaders have improved teaching" and that "pastoral care is a strength."

Not only that but "there is a range of support for pupils, including those known to social care" and that "pupils have a range of opportunities to explore their abilities and interests outside of lessons."

The report also said "leaders make sure that all pupils, including vulnerable pupils and those with special educational needs and/or disabilities, can participate in these wider opportunities.

"Leaders should ensure that teachers give pupils opportunities to practise and review what they have learned so that they develop deeper knowledge and understanding across all subjects, including within the personal development programme.

"Leaders should ensure that pupils who are at the early stages of reading receive the support that they need to progress.

"Leaders should further develop their analysis and use of attendance information to remove barriers to pupil attendance.

"Governors should rigorously assure themselves of the quality of the school's work so that they provide effective support and challenge to leaders, leading to sustainable improvements."

## Headteacher acknowledges challenges ahead

Headteacher Mr Kyle Audsley said: "The findings provide important recognition of the progress that has been made throughout the last academic year and affirm the clear vision and strategy driving improvement across the school. The report highlights our inclusive culture, high expectations and the positive changes that are already making a difference for our students.

"We were already aware of the areas raised by Ofsted and were already taking positive steps to address them. Some of these actions were newly introduced when the inspection took place and therefore their full impact had not yet been seen.

"However, we are confident that the benefits will become clear for present and future students alike. Whilst we are not complacent and remain determined to achieve even more for our young people, it is reassuring that Ofsted has recognised both the improvements already secured and the strong capacity within the school to deliver further progress over time.

## 'We are ambitious for our school'

"This outcome provides a clear platform from which to continue raising achievement and outcomes at Spen Valley High School. I am incredibly proud to lead this school and it is a genuine privilege to work alongside such a dedicated staff team, fantastic young people and a supportive community. While we are honest about the areas we must improve, I will always champion the people who make this school what it is.

"The care, commitment and determination shown every day by our staff and the way our students look after one another, are what truly define Spen Valley. We are ambitious for our school and for every child in our care. We will move forward with clarity, determination and a relentless focus on getting this right for our community, whilst holding on tightly to the values of inclusion, support and belonging that sit at the heart of everything we do."
