A runner faced the "worst experience" as she was subjected to racial abuse while taking part in the Great North Run over the weekend.

In her decade of loving running, Emma Kirk-Odunubi has never experienced anything like it, she claims.

The running blogger said a group of spectators booed her and shouted, "protect our kids, protect our England", about nine miles into the 13.1-mile race, which starts from Newcastle and ends in South Shields.

The viral woman, who boats over 44,000 followers, said in an Instagram video that the incident left her shaken, despite otherwise enjoying the event.

A police investigation has been launched following the incident.

  Emma Kirk-Odunubi. 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

Emma said: "Running people will say, 'don't let running get political'.

"When I am being booed because of the colour of my skin and me as a person doing something I adore, like running, that literally saved my life and that I've been doing for ten years...never have I come across that in my life, ever."

Despite the abuse, Ms Kirk-Odunubi praised the event, its organisers and the overwhelming majority of supportive spectators.

"To Great North Run, it was an incredible event aside from this," she said.

She added: "I had the best time. People were generally really lovely and encouraging. The crowds were incredible at the finish line, amazing.

"Everything else was so great. I couldn't just come on here and say I had an amazing race because it's just sat with me really funny."

   

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She added: "Hate has no place in running. I believe that to my core.

"I literally wrote a book about how running saved me, and the community of running helped to save me. So I don't know what this means, but I know that I need to share it and I need people to know that that's not what running is."

Addressing others who may have felt unwelcome during the event, she said: "If anyone on the Great North Run, on that course saw them and heard what they said and felt out of place, and that they shouldn't be there, you have every right to be on that course today, and you were all incredible, as is everyone else that is running."

Northumbria Police said officers are investigating the incident.

A spokesperson for the force said: "We have been made aware of information circulating online in relation to a participant being subjected to racial abuse during yesterday's Great North Run event.

"We want to make it clear we condemn all forms of discrimination.

"It has absolutely no place within our society, and we will take robust action against anyone found to be responsible.

"We'd ask anyone who was in the area at the time and saw anything, to report information to us to help inform our enquiries."

The force said witnesses can report information by direct message on social media or through its website.

The Great Run Company also condemned the behaviour and said it was working with police to establish what happened.

A spokesperson said: "We are shocked and saddened to hear about Emma's experience at today's Great North Run. We do not condone behaviour of this nature. We truly believe the event is for everyone.

"The event receives huge support from the local community. However, today clearly a small number of spectators didn't reflect the warm reception of the North East people.

"Over 200,000 people turned out in support. It's a real shame that a small subset have spoilt Emma's experience today."

The company said it was in contact with Ms Kirk-Odunubi and would pass any information to police.

The spokesperson added: "We are working with local authorities and the emergency services to understand more about what has happened.

"We will of course be taking learnings from this incident to try and mitigate this happening at future events."
