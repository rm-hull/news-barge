A motorist risked the safety of a railwayman when he jumped the lights at a level crossing and drove through its closing gates, York magistrates heard.

Peter Avery, 60, told the court he had expected the signaller to delay the closure to allow him through.

Georgina Fishwick, prosecuting, said Avery approached the York to Harrogate railway line at Cattal crossing at speed as the signaller was on the road manually closing the gates.

The level crossing lights were flashing, warning all motorists to stop as a train was approaching the crossing.

“He hasn’t slowed down or come to a stop,” said the prosecutor. “He has gone through the narrow gate caused by the closing of the gate and was fairly close to the signaller causing a risk of harm to him.”

Avery, of Prince Rupert Drive, Tockwith, pleaded guilty to careless driving on January 24 at Cattal Level Crossing.

York magistrates told him it was a serious offence. They fined him £769, ordered him to pay a £308 statutory surcharge and £85 prosecution costs and put six penalty points on his licence. He had no previous convictions.

Avery, who represented himself, said he regularly used both the Cattal crossing and the level crossing at Hessay near Long Marston, including when trains were approaching.

“Ninety-nine per cent of the time, the signallers on these crossings have seen me coming and they say ‘come on, come on quickly’ and let me through before they close the gates,” he said. “I made a bad judgement call.”

He said he had been the only vehicle on the road, he had seen the signaller at the gate and he had decided it wasn’t safe to slow down.
