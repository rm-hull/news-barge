**Changeable autumnal conditions are set to continue through this coming week, with spells of wetter and windier weather developing at times.** 

Monday will bring a short-lived burst of warmth and sunshine before temperatures drop to levels more typical for September later in the week.

A strengthening jet stream will bring deeper, low-pressure systems as the week goes on, with the potential for minor disruption towards next weekend.

Higher pressure looks set to build again for the end of the month, but prospects of a late-September heatwave remain uncertain.

## Strengthening jet stream to bring windier weather

![Two images of the jet stream position over the North Atlantic this week. On the left is the position at the start of the week, undulating and to the north of the UK. The second image shows the end of the week where the jet stream is straighter and crosses over the UK.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/72cc/live/aa0d10f0-ae91-11f1-a540-61c3f7fc4e6c.jpg)

A flatter and stronger jet stream, with speeds of more than 170mph, will bring deeper areas of low pressure to the UK later in the week.

During the start to the week, the jet stream will be relatively weak and loop to the north of the UK, allowing higher pressure to develop and a warm south-westerly airflow to take hold.

Later, the jet stream will take on a more southerly path, flowing faster and straighter towards our shores. This will mean lower pressure will form, bringing rain and brisk winds at times, and temperatures will drop.

## A cooling trend this week

Monday will be the warmest day of the week, with the air coming in from a south-westerly direction.

Many areas will stay fairly cloudy, with some rain around coasts and hills in the west. Despite this it will feel warm and rather humid, with temperatures climbing above 20C in many places.

Where the sun does break through, in central and southern parts of England, temperatures could reach as high as 26C.

![Two UK maps showing the temperature on Monday, ranging from 18C  in northern Scotland to 26C in southern England, and Thursday of 14C in the north and 20C in the south.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1920/cpsprodpb/f3be/live/1d561de0-af55-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

A change in wind direction will bring a drop in temperature though the week, but only closer to the seasonal average of 14-20C

By Thursday the winds will turn more westerly or north-westerly, bringing a fresher air mass and a cooler feel to the weather. Temperatures by this stage will reach around 14 to 16C in Scotland and Northern Ireland, and 17 to 20C in England and Wales.

## Will we see September out with a heatwave?

Forecasts are showing that areas of high pressure should have more influence on the weather for the last week of September. This may mean longer dry interludes and the chance of returning to above-average daytime temperatures, but the degree of temperature rise will depend on the positioning the high pressure relative to the UK.

While long-range forecasting is fraught with uncertainty and details are subject to change, there is currently nothing to indicate that a heatwave is on the cards.

Indeed, as autumn advances and the nights become longer, high pressure overhead could lead to some rather chilly mornings.
