The jury has retired to consider its verdict in the trial of a man accused of grooming, raping and sexually abusing a teenager.

Mark Faulds allegedly used alcohol, cigarettes and cannabis to make the girl more compliant, the court heard.

The 41-year-old faced 14 charges but was cleared of seven after the judge ruled there was no evidence to support them.

Judge Nathan Adams told the jury that the teenager’s evidence that sex was consensual meant they had to find Faulds not guilty on seven charges.

Faulds repeatedly denied having sex with the teenager or grooming her with alcohol or drugs.

Faulds’ DNA was found in semen on a condom in woodland, alongside traces of the teenager’s DNA, but the evidence did not prove intercourse.

The court heard that the teenager’s aunt secretly recorded her account after she said she would deny it if anyone else was told.

Faulds, of Oxford Street, Stanley, County Durham, denies the seven remaining charges.

The charges include rape, assault by penetration and causing a child to engage in sexual activity.

The trial continues.
