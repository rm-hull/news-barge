The transformation of a York building has been given the go ahead.

Plans to convert a James Street building into eight padel courts have been approved by City of York Council.

Northminster Developments Ltd’s plans stated they hoped to attract more people to the sport as it soars in popularity.

Council planning officers stated the conversion would provide new sports facilities in a sustainable and accessible location.

It comes as the developer’s plans stated there was a significant demand for padel facilities in York, according to a survey from its governing body the Lawn Tennis Association (LTA).

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/york-james-street-padel-site-gsv.webp)

The conversion is set to see the building, the southern part of which was previously used by neighbouring First Buses for storage and driver training, converted to house the courts.

Changing rooms and other facilities will also be included as part of the development.

The courts will open from 6am to 11pm, with up to 18 people employed as management, reception, cleaning and coaching staff.

There will be 38 parking spaces on site including two accessible bays and five with electric vehicle charging points.

Padel is played on an enclosed court about a third of the size those used for tennis with balls played off glass walls similar to squash.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/padel-paddle-tennis-px.webp)

The LTA stated it is easy to learn and sociable, contributing to its growing popularity.

Plans for the James Street site stated more than 400,000 adults played padel at least once in 2024, up from 129,000 the previous year according to LTA figures.

They added the sport’s governing body estimated demand in York could sustain 16 padel facilities, with the city currently home to seven.

Plans stated: “Padel is one of the fastest growing sports in this country and is embracing many players new to active sport.

“The LTA demand analysis indicates that there is very significant demand to play padel in the York area.

“The LTA describes the active efforts to promote broader participation and a more diverse padel playing community, providing the combination of sport and social activity.

“Padel’s social appeal supports health and wellbeing objectives, making it a valuable asset for the public health agenda and for community sports providers.”

Council planning officers stated the development would not result in the loss of employment land.

They added while the courts were likely to generate noise, there were no homes nearby that would be disturbed.
