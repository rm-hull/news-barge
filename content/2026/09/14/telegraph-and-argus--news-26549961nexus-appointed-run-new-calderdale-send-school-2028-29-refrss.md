A trust has now been appointed to run a new free school for children with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) in Calderdale, expected to open in academic year 2028-29.

South Yorkshire based Nexus Multi Academy Trust will run the new school on the former Threeways site at Ovenden in north Halifax – formerly Ovenden Secondary and then The Ridings School, and more recently housing community organisations before health and safety issues saw Calderdale Council close the site.

The previous Labour administration had earmarked around £20 million for a 200-place special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) school for primary and secondary age pupils and in July the council’s new Reform UK administration committed to delivering the new school.

The council ‘s view is that like other areas across the country, there are not enough school places in Calderdale for children and young people with special educational needs.

This means pupils sometimes have to go to schools outside Calderdale or into independent specialist schools, often meaning longer journeys to school, which are difficult for families and not the best value for council money.

The council and the Government’s Department for Education approved the development of the new specialist free school to ensure more young people can learn closer to home.

The new build will provide places for pupils aged four to 16 with a range of needs.

They will be able to continue both their primary and secondary education in the same school as they grow up, and will be supported with academic and vocational learning, therapeutic approaches and enriching activities to meet their needs, the authority says.

The council has worked with the Department for Education to carry out a thorough selection process to identify a multi-academy trust to establish and run the new school.

Nexus was chosen, with approval from the Department for Education, because of its strong track record in supporting pupils with special educational needs and disabilities.

All of Nexus’s current schools have been rated “good” or “outstanding” in the previous Ofsted inspection framework.

Calderdale Council’s Cabinet member for Children and Young People’s Services, Coun Tim Holden, said the creation of a new specialist free school is just one of the ways the council is supporting more children with special educational needs and disabilities.

“It will give them high-quality education close to home, with all the help they need to do well at school and be prepared for adulthood.

“The new school will build on and work alongside the great specialist provision already available in Calderdale and will be part of the outreach support for special education needs in mainstream schools.

“We look forward to working with Nexus and other schools across the borough to continue to help young people thrive,” he said.

Warren Carratt, who is Chief Executive Officer of Nexus Multi Academy Trust said they were “very proud and grateful” to have been appointed.

“Our Trust has a strong track record of co-producing great specialist educational provision, and this will be the fifth new special school that we’ve opened in the last decade.

“We look forward to working with the excellent providers already working in Calderdale, with children and families and with the local authority as we bring this new school to life,” he said.

The next steps are for the council and Nexus to work together to design the new school’s learning and outdoor spaces, and to consult with local schools, families and the community in 2027, said the council.

As part of the construction process, the project will aim to support local jobs and skills, increasing opportunities for local employment, said the authority.

Separately to this council-led project, through multi-million pound Government funding, an alternative provision free school partnered by the council and the Delta Academies Trust will be built, also in north Halifax, and will provide places for up to 125 primary and secondary-age children – the announcement was made in the spring with the exact amount awaited.

It will help children and young people aged eight to 16 who need additional support, with short-term placements giving them targeted help and the best opportunity to thrive when they return to their usual school, the council said at that time.
