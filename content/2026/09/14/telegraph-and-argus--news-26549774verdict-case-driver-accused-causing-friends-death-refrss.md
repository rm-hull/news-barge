A speeding driver has been convicted of causing the death of his friend as a result of a crash whilst they were racing on a Bradford road.

The jury of ten men and two women in the trial of Mohammed Awais Iqbal returned their unanimous verdicts after deliberating for four hours and 12 minutes.

They found him guilty of causing the death of Husayn Ali by dangerous driving, and of causing serious injury to Mr Ali’s passengers Bilal Hussain and Harris Butt on March 7, 2024.

Her Honour Judge Kirstie Watson told Iqbal: “You have been convicted by this jury of one count of causing death by dangerous driving and two counts of causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

“It is inevitable that the sentence I will pass will be a sentence of some length.”

Sentence was adjourned pending reports.

University student Mr Ali, 21, was pronounced dead in hospital three hours after his Skoda Fabia veered across Bolton Road to collide with a VW Golf travelling in the opposite lane shortly before 9pm on March 7, 2024.

The trial at Bradford Crown Court has heard that he was racing at 65mph with his friend Iqbal, who was following close behind in a Seat Leon Cupra, and failed to negotiate a bend on the road, which has a 30mph speed limit.

In an interview with police Iqbal, now 24, of Beechwood Drive, Wibsey, denied that he was involved in racing or reckless behaviour or encouraging anyone else to do it prior to the accident.

He said he had seen Mr Ali’s car “swerving from side to side” and had put on his hazard lights to warn him how badly he was driving.

He said he was not doing it to encourage Mr Ali and denied that the incident was anything to do with him.

The jury heard how Mr Ali was driving too fast for the road and that Iqbal was trying initially to keep up with him and then activated his lights to warn him to slow down.

But prosecuting barrister Gerald Hendron said the hazard lights on Iqbal’s Seat had been modified to flash out of sequence when they were activated.

CCTV footage screened in court appeared to show Iqbal’s car “blinking” its headlights intermittently as it drove down Bolton Road towards Bradford.

This, said Mr Hendron, “was an indication for the car ahead [Mr Ali’s Skoda Fabia] to go.”

In his trial opening last week, Mr Hendron said both men were racing or driving competitively with each other.

He said Iqbal was driving “far too close” to Mr Ali and would, by his presence, be likely to be pressuring him “to accelerate or at least maintain his speed as opposed to braking.”

During a brief journey along Bolton Road, both Mr Ali’s Skoda and Iqbal’s Leon accelerated.

However, the Skoda failed to negotiate a left-hand bend and deviated into the oncoming lane where it collided with the VW Golf driven by a man observing the 30mph limit.

The “almost head-on” collision, in which Mr Ali’s car was lifted off the ground and spun in the air before coming to a stop, was captured on CCTV.

Husayn Ali, who had not been wearing a seatbelt, was unresponsive and slumped over in the driver’s seat.

He was treated with CPR at the roadside and on the way to Leeds General Infirmary by paramedics, but he could not be revived at hospital and was pronounced dead around three hours later at 12.05am on March 8, 2024.

Mr Ali’s front seat passenger Harris Butt suffered a broken femur and a fracture to his chest.

Rear seat passenger Bilal Hussain suffered a broken sternum and a broken right elbow as well as cuts and bruises.

Judge Watson is expected to pass sentence on November 13.

Iqbal, dressed in a dark suit, white shirt, and dark tie, was released on conditional bail and handed an interim driving ban.
