A soundbar is one of the smartest upgrades you can make for your entertainment room. The latest models sound loud, clear, and distinct, with realistic sound that is truly transportive—a thunderstorm sounds like rain from every direction, a sword fight reverberates with every clang.

There are tons of options to choose from, but all of my top picks went through rigorous hands-on (and ears open) testing. I chose the best models in a few categories, including ones that are ideal for bass, small spaces, or expandability.

The Sonos Arc Ultra launched in 2024, but it’s still my soundbar champion, with the most pristine sound for movies, games, and music. With some of my other top picks outclass it in specific areas like surround sound or bass, the Arc Ultra’s great all-around performance makes it easy to recommend.

This model is a prime example of why soundbars are a great addition to any home theater. Dolby Atmos and DTS:X surround sound are convincing— the upward fighting and side channels make spear clanks and grunts in *Predator: Badlands* appropriately spatial. Play a song like*Want Want* by Maggie Rogers and you can feel the pounding electric guitars and synth in your bones.

And while the Sonos app has gone through ups and downs, room calibration *just works* without a lot of tedious tweaks and adjustments, especially compared to other brands I’ve tested.

While it’s not perfect—I would have liked an extra HDMI input and audio that is not quite so bright in the upper register—this is one powerful and sublime soundbar that’s an easy recommendation.

You won’t find a better surround sound experience than the one the LG Sound Suite H7 provides, especially if you add the optional LG W7 subwoofer and LG M7 satellite speakers. They aren’t cheap additions, but they create a very immersive experience all together.

The system uses Dolby Atmos FlexConnect so you can place the extra speakers anywhere you want. Using the LG ThinQ app, you can then group the speakers for Dolby Atmos surround sound that’s ultra-realistic. When I watched *The Last House*, which has an exceptional audio design, the system placed creepy sound effects across every corner of the room.

Setup takes a little patience—you have to configure each individual speaker, add it to your network, group them together, and calibrate the sound to your room. But it’s worth the effort. I’ve never heard planes sound so realistic in my viewing room than watching *Unbroken* with this system.

It’s not as bass-heavy as some of my other picks from Focal and Klipsch, and the full system of soundbar, speakers, and subwoofer is pricey, but it delivers an excellent surround sound experience.

Once I heard the soundtrack to *The Odyssey* on the Klipsch Flexus Core 300 soundbar, I was convinced it was the loudest and bassiest model around. But dialogue is also a strength—TV shows sounded crisp and clear even in chaotic scenes which can sometimes cause issues with lower quality soundbars.

Also, room calibration took a lot of effort and time.

## Best Premium Soundbar

Loud and powerful, the Focal Mu-So Hekla soundbar is a 34-pound behemoth you want for explosive set pieces in movies, (think anything with Tom Cruise flying airplanes or jumping off of buildings). There are 15 speakers inside, including three woofers that deliver powerful bass that matches the Klipsch Flexus Core 300.

A big plus is the upfiring audio drivers for Dolby Atmos. It was the first time I noticed a loud *clank* sound up by my ceiling when the main character in*Predator: Badlands* extends a spear to the sky. Only the LG Sound Suite H7 has better surround sound, but the Mu-So Hekla is louder and boomier (in a good way).

I loved the Focal app as well—it’s easy to use for selecting inputs, adjusting settings and room calibration.

For music, I played Fleetwood Mac at high volume and never heard any distortion or hiss. A test track by Maggie Rogers called “Want Want” sounds like a nightclub has come alive.

This is a large, heavy, premium soundbar with a premium price tag. With such a big upfront investment, it’s disappointing that it isn’t more expandable—you can only add a subwoofer, not side or rear speakers like the Sonos, LG, and Klipsch.

Still, for sheer sonic superiority, the Focal Mu-So Hekla is one of my all-time favorite soundbars.

**Why would you want to expand with more speakers?**

Several soundbars like the Sonos Arc Ultra and the Klipsch Flexus Core 300 allow you to add extra speakers and even a sub (or two!) to the standalone soundbar. While this might seem like overkill since the soundbar itself is capable of simulating surround sound effects, adding more speakers will help with realism. Spaceships will sound like they are flying from the back of the room and gunfire will ricochet from the left to the right.

**Why is Dolby Atmos so important?**

Dolby Atmos is the gold standard for surround sound audio, competing with the less common DTS:X specification. Soundbars use upfiring speakers to give you the impression that music and movie audio is coming from above you or from the sides. Note that Dolby Atmos is often only available at the premium tier level of streaming apps like Prime Video and Netflix.

**What is room calibration?**

Audio tends to bounce all around the room, echo down hallways, and escape through open windows. If you are in a tiny space, the sound won’t have anywhere to go. Room calibration is one way to compensate for room size and unusual architectural features. A soundbar like the Sonos Arc Ultra has a calibrate feature, where it emits a tone and then listens on its microphone, adjusting its audio for that particular room. Having tested many soundbars, good calibration can make a huge difference.
