Rail passengers across the North East will soon find it easier to complete their journeys when trains are cancelled, as the Government introduces new cross-operator ticket acceptance rules.

From Sunday (September 20), passengers whose trains are cancelled can board participating operators’ services at no extra cost.

Passengers with valid tickets for cancelled services can use another operator’s train leaving up to two hours before or after their booked service.

Major North East operators including LNER, Northern, TransPennine Express and CrossCountry are taking part, serving stations such as Newcastle, Durham, Darlington, Sunderland and Middlesbrough.

Rail Minister Lord Hendy called the changes a “simple, common-sense” reform that puts passengers first.

He said: "From this weekend, if your train is cancelled, you can get on another operator's service and carry on your journey without paying again."

The change should end the uncertainty many passengers face over whether another operator will accept their ticket after a cancellation.

In some cases, customers are forced to wait for the next service from the same company, buy a new ticket, or abandon their journey altogether.

The changes should particularly help North East travellers on busy long-distance routes from Newcastle and Durham to London, York, Leeds, Manchester, Birmingham and Edinburgh.

The expansion builds on existing agreements between publicly owned train operators.

From September 20, the scheme will also cover CrossCountry, Great Western Railway, Avanti West Coast and Chiltern Railways, extending protection across key intercity routes.

The reforms come in preparation for the planned establishment of Great British Railways, which will bring train operations and infrastructure together under a single public body.

The Government says the measure will help simplify the complex and outdated rail system.

Passenger watchdog Transport Focus has welcomed the announcement.

Chief executive Alex Robertson said passengers hit by delays and cancellations want to reach their destination without worrying whether another service will accept their ticket.

He added that clear information would be vital to helping people understand their options during disruption.

The Government is also investing £156 million in new ticketing schemes, including plans to bring pay-as-you-go travel to more than 100 stations in Greater Manchester, the Midlands and the South East by 2027.

A new Great British Railways website and app will let passengers buy tickets, check train times and find travel information in one place.

From September 20, these operators will take part in the expanded ticket acceptance scheme: Avanti West Coast, c2c, Chiltern Railways, CrossCountry, Gatwick Express, Greater Anglia, Great Northern, Great Western Railway, LNER, London Northwestern Railway, Northern, Southeastern, Southern, South Western Railway, Thameslink, TransPennine Express and West Midlands Railway.

East Midlands Railway is expected to join later.

Separately, one next of kin of each service person who died while serving will qualify for free National Rail travel to Remembrance events on November 7 and 8, 2026.
