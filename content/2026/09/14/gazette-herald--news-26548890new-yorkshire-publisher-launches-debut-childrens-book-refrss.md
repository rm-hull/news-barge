A new independent publisher has launched with its debut children’s title.

Shannon Publishers, based in Eastrington, opened its doors and released its first book, Pony Tales, a short children's title which tells the story of a young horse at her first show.

The publishing house was founded by Emma Swaine, from Cliffe, near Selby, who started the project as part of her master’s degree in publishing.

  


She said: "In one of my modules we had to look in depth at the process of publishing a book and then for my dissertation we had to undertake our own publishing project.

"I found the research into starting a press and the process of getting a book made and on shelves really interesting and so I thought why not give it a go myself for my dissertation."

  The publishers is named after her own much-loved horse, Shannon, which is the subject of her first short children's story Pony Tales 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The publisher focuses on stories set in the countryside.

She said: "It's inspired by my own happy memories spent outside, usually on the back of a horse.

"We are even named after my own horse, Shannon.

"Our aim at Shannon Publishers is to put a spotlight on authors and stories from the countryside."

Pony Tales follows Shannon at her very first horse show.

Emma said: "I thought it was a nice idea to have our first title relate to our namesake, plus as this project was originally just for my dissertation I only had the summer to write the book, edit it, proofread, draw the illustrations, typeset and get it printed.

"So a short children’s book seemed like an easier task than a full length novel."

Although it began as a student project, Emma says she now plans to continue developing Shannon Publishers beyond her studies.

She said: "I work full time, so Shannon Publishers is just a side, passion project that I’m trying to balance as well.

"However, there are plans for another publication later this year, a short novel for an older target audience than Pony Tales.

"And then I hope to open up to submissions and start working with some local authors."

  A labour of love, Emma hopes Shannon Publishing will open more doors for writers in the north 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Emma described the challenges and rewards of launching her own press.

She said: "It’s been hard work, a lot more goes into it than people might expect.

"Especially when you’re just starting out and most, if not all, the jobs fall to you.

"But it has been so exciting too; even just seeing the PDF proof before Pony Tales went to print was surreal.

"Seeing the final project come together, printed and bound, with an ISBN number like all other books in bookshops is really fulfilling."

She also hopes Shannon Publishers can help address the lack of publishing opportunities in Yorkshire.

"I’ve personally found the publishing industry to be very London focused, I felt like Yorkshire was completely left out of the industry to be honest", she said.

"There are so many great writers in the North and so many wonderful stories to be told, I just hope Shannon Publishers can help to shine a light on them and be here to celebrate the local area and our stories."
