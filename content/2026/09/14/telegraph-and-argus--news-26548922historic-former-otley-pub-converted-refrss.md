A historic former pub will be saved from decay after plans were approved for its conversion into housing.

Grade II-listed Royal Oak House in Otley town centre is set to be turned into a five-bedroom family house.

Leeds City Council gave planning permission for work on the Manor Square building, part of the town’s designated conservation area.

A design report said it was historically used as a public house and more recently as offices.

The project would include the demolition of a 20th century rear extension which was in a poor condition and damaging the original building.

The report said: “Its continued decay poses a direct risk to the historic fabric, and its removal is therefore both a conservation benefit and a necessary measure to safeguard the long-term condition of the listed structure.”

Refurbishment of the ground floor would include re-instatement of the gas supply, a modern kitchen and central heating. Five bedrooms would be created on the first floor.

The report said the conversion project would secure the future of the vacant building.

It said: “Economically, the proposal secures the long-term viable use of a vacant Grade II-listed building within Otley town centre.”

Occupants of the new home would be close to shops, jobs opportunities and transport links.

The report said: “It will contribute to housing supply through the effective reuse of an existing building and provide a well-designed dwelling that meets modern space and living standards.

“Bringing the property back into active residential use ensures continued investment in the historic fabric and supports the vitality of the town centre.”

The refurbishment would be designed to fit in with the building’s historic surroundings, the report by building consultancy Planning By Design said.

It said: “The proposal seeks to preserve and enhance the building’s significance while securing a viable long-term use.”
