**A jury has been discharged after failing to reach a verdict in the trial of a man accused of plotting to disrupt Boris Johnson's wedding reception.**

Tim Speers, 41, allegedly carried spray paint and superglue to the Daylesford Estate in Gloucestershire on 29 July 2022, on the eve of the former prime minister and Carrie Johnson's wedding reception.

Speers, from Bude, Cornwall, denied having items with intent to cause criminal damage and said he abandoned the idea to spray "Palestine" on the lawn by the time police approached him.

The judge discharged the jury and adjourned the case at Southwark Crown Court until 29 September, when prosecutors have been told to indicate if they want a retrial.

## More from Gloucestershire

During the trial, the jury heard that police stopped Speers and another man, who will stand trial at a later date, after the pair were spotted carrying cans of beer at the Cotswold estate.

They initially refused to tell police why they were there and then said they were on holiday.

Christiaan Moll, prosecuting, said police found a voice note recorded by Speers the day before his arrest in which he said he had been asked by two Palestine Action activists to take part in "action" and he "agreed to do it".

The jury - which began deliberations on Friday - had sent the judge a series of notes that said they were "stuck" and divided on the verdict.

The Johnsons were married in a private ceremony at Westminster Cathedral in May 2021.

They hosted their wedding celebrations at Daylesford House on 30 July the following year, setting up marquees and hay bales in the grounds.
