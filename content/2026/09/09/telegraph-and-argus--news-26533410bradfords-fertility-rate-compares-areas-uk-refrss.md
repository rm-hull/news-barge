Bradford has one of the highest fertility rates in England and Wales, according to newly-published figures.

Pendle in Lancashire and Luton in Bedfordshire were tied with the highest rate of 1.93 children per woman.

They were followed by Oldham in Greater Manchester (1.87), Bradford (1.84) and Barking & Dagenham in London (1.83).

Many of the lowest levels are in parts of London or locations with major universities.

The City of London had the lowest rate in 2025, at 0.37 children per woman.

This was followed by Cambridge (0.87), and then Islington and Westminster in the capital (both 0.90).

The fertility rate is defined as the average number of live children women would expect to have across their childbearing life.

The data has been published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS).

It shows other areas with the lowest rates in 2025 were Brighton & Hove (0.95), the London borough of Southwark (0.97) and Norwich (0.98).

Exeter, Oxford and York, along with Camden and Hammersmith & Fulham in the capital, all had rates of 1.01 children per woman.

Figures published earlier this year by the ONS showed the overall fertility rate for England and Wales has dropped to a new record low.

The rate fell from 1.41 in 2024 to 1.39 in 2025.

For a country’s population to remain stable over time – discounting any impact from migration – the rate needs to be around 2.1.

Some 12 of the top 25 local authorities with the lowest fertility rates in 2025 were in London.

In Wales, Swansea had the lowest rate at 1.18.

The highest rates were in Carmarthenshire, the Isle of Anglesey and Newport (all 1.48).

There were 585,396 live births in England and Wales last year, down from 594,677 in 2024 and the lowest number since 1977.

Live births fell in every region in England, with the largest percentage decrease in the West Midlands (down 3.1%) and the smallest in the North East (down 0.3%).

The average age of parents increased slightly year on year, the ONS data showed.

Mothers had a provisional standardised mean age of 31.1 in 2025, up from 31.0 in 2024.

Fathers had an average age of 34.0 last year, a rise from 33.9.

Parents have been getting older since 1975, when mothers were on average aged 26.4 and fathers were 29.5 years old.

The proportion of births where the mother was born outside the UK has been on a steady upwards trend for a number of years.

This proportion climbed from 20.8% in 2005 to 27.5% in 2015 and 34.6% last year.

India was the most common country of birth for non-UK-born mothers in 2025, the fourth year in a row it took the top spot.

It was followed by Pakistan, Nigeria and Romania.

Some 56.4% of births last year were to parents both of whom were born in the UK, down from 62.7% a decade earlier in 2015.
