A special brew is set to take centre stage at a popular beer festival in York next week.

York CAMRA members joined forces with Trig Brewery in Poppleton to create 100th Meridian, a Mountain IPA that will make its debut at the York Beer Festival, when it starts next Wednesday.

Steve Gorton, a member of York CAMRA, said: "The brewery has grown from Patrick’s dad’s garage to a unit on a business park in Poppleton where they regularly do two 10-barrel brews a week.

"Keeping with the theme of Trig, a marker on the top of a hill or mountain, our idea, with expert guidance from head brewer Patrick, was to produce a Mountain IPA which would bring together the best of West Coast and New England IPAs with a subtle balance of bitterness and hop flavours.

"We believe we have achieved this with rice, oats, and extra pale, chit and Vienna malt, enhanced by a combination of modern American hop varieties of Chinook, Centennial and Citra.

"The name: 100th Meridian is the geographical centre of the USA, which is a fairly mountainous region.

"The geographical theme and the 100th brew of beer at the brewery all bring this together."

The York Beer Festival will take place from September 16 to 19 at St Lawrence’s Church, located at the top of Hull Road.

This marks the fourth year the event has been held at this venue since relocating from the Knavesmire.

York CAMRA described St Lawrence’s as a "magnificent venue" and noted that the church is the second largest in York after the Minster.

The festival will feature more than 200 beers and ciders in a wide variety of styles and strengths, sourced from local, national, and international brewers.

In addition to beer and cider, attendees will also be able to enjoy gin, wine, and mead.

This year’s festival will also include a Locale blind-tasting competition, and a range of ticketed, tutored tasting sessions and presentations from local brewers and beer experts.

The full beer list will be published online shortly before the event, and a live beer list will be available throughout the festival.

Attendees will receive a free printed programme upon entry.

For the first time, CAMRA members will be granted free entry to the festival, in line with the organisation’s national policy.

Organisers have also confirmed that the event will be dog-friendly and wheelchair accessible.

York CAMRA has again partnered with York Rescue Boat to help raise funds for the charity during the festival.

Mr Gorton added: "The proof, of course, is in the tasting, so we are confident you will get to sample this ale and look forward to your feedback at the festival."
