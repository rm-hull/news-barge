Urban explorers have gone inside a former country house near Yarm that is set to become a five-star luxury hotel.

Judges County House Hotel, in Kirklevington, has remained empty since it suddenly closed its doors in March 2023, when all weddings and events were cancelled.

The venue, previously a rural retreat for the Richardson family of Hartlepool, operated as Judges from 1994 for almost two decades.

And, after being bought by property developer Chris Musgrave following its closure, the venue will soon become the “top drawer” Kirklevington Hall.

But last week, urban explorers went inside the hall, posting images of how the rooms look today, more than three years after guests were last welcomed through the doors.

Alongside a number of empty bedroom and office spaces, they found old pictures of the venue and a book of wedding photographs.

The owner of the hall declined to comment on the matter.

  Signs for the new Kirklevington Hall near Yarm 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Plans for Kirklevington Hall, which is set to feature a new restaurant, events pavilion, and garden lodges alongside a community of premium self-build homes, were approved in March.

Mr Musgrave and hotel and restaurant operator Kymel are putting a more than £25 million investment to breathe new life into the site, which was in a “poor state of disrepair” following its closure.

The development will include a luxury spa and wellness facilities, woodland lodges set within the grounds, a high-end events space within the historic walled garden, the conversion of the former stables for events and hospitality use, and a premium food and beverage offering.

Once operational, the hotel and spa will create a further 80 to 120 permanent roles across hospitality, wellness, events and estate management functions.

The construction phase is expected to support between 120 and 180 direct and indirect jobs.

  A sketch of the proposed development at Kirklevington Hall near Yarm 

*(Image: GSSArchitecture)*

Mr Musgrave said he wanted to celebrate the site’s heritage while “creating a destination of genuine regional and national appeal”.

Signs were erected for the venture and the new self-build plots just off the A67 in April, with reservations no being open.

Kirklevington Hall was originally constructed in 1888 and remained a private residence for the Richardson family for decades.

It was eventually adapted as lodgings for circuit judges who were visiting the North East to preside over court proceedings and was then developed into Judges.
