A Bradford man had a lucky escape after a skip lorry overturned outside his home, crushing his car and spilling rubbish into three gardens.

The EFR Skips & Waste Recycling lorry overturned around midday on Wednesday on New Lane in Laisterdyke, Bradford, near the junction with Dick Lane.

The crash spilled a mountain of rubbish into three gardens, crushed a car and smashed a horse box, causing major disruption.

Shocked homeowner Harry Davey said he felt “lucky” he was not in his car when the lorry overturned.


Mr Davey told the T&A: "I was in bed at the time. I’m usually up and sat in the car. I sleep much better in the car than I do in the house.

"Luckily, this time, I wasn’t sat in the car."

  Harry Davey's car was crushed when a lorry overturned on top of it


He told how he heard a loud bang then seconds later answered the door to concerned neighbours worried he may have been in his devastated car.

Mr Davey said: "Ordinarily you get used to hearing the bump of lorries travelling down the road.

"But then all of a sudden, I heard knocking at the door.

"It was the neighbours who came to check on me."

  New Road was closed after a lorry overturned and spilled rubbish into the gardens of three homes


The lorry crushed Mr Davey’s Vauxhall car, which he had recently spent £750 repairing.

He said: "I just spent £750 and put all brand new stuff on it.

"I also just filled it up with diesel."

Neighbours were shocked and feared for Mr Davey’s safety.

Khadija Haider, who lives nearby, said: "He’s usually in his car."

Ms Haider described the mess left by the crash.

She said: "I was told the Council will clean it up. I hope they come quick and take the rubbish away."

Another neighbour, Angelique, said she was not at home during the accident.

She said: "I count myself lucky.

"At least everyone was okay and the driver of the lorry was fine."

Residents said the driver of the skip lorry appeared shaken but was not seriously injured.

One resident overlooking the scene said: "I heard a loud bang.

"I understand the lorry wasn’t going fast but the load could have shifted when he went around the bend."

He said Mr Davey’s usual routine may have put him at risk of serious injury.

He said: "Usually [Mr Davey] is in the car, so it could have been a lot worse.

"The driver of the lorry all seemed okay, so I’m glad no one was injured.

"It’s unfortunate this has happened, but I’m glad no one was hurt."

Police declined to release further information about the incident.
