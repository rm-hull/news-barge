A man who was part of a large-scale anti-migrant riot was arrested for drink driving on the outskirts of York.

Ricky Cotton, 34, tried to evade justice by walking off from his vehicle and, after police found him, refusing to take a breath test at Fulford Road Police Station, York magistrates heard.

He was three times the drink drive limit, said Martin Butterworth, prosecuting, and had previously served a driving ban for a similar offence.

Cotton is currently on prison parole having been released partway through a 28-month prison sentence imposed at Sheffield Crown Court in 2024 for violent disorder.

He was among hundreds of rioters outside the Holiday Inn in Rotherham earlier that year, some of whom forced their way into the hotel where asylum seekers were living at the time.

A probation officer said because of his drink driving arrest, the probation service had ordered him to wear an alcohol abstinence tag for 120 days as part of his parole conditions.

Cotton, of Pearwood Close, Goldthorpe, Barnsley, pleaded guilty to failure to provide a sample of breath when suspected of drink driving.

He was given a 12-week prison sentence suspended for 12 months and banned from driving for three years. He must pay £85 prosecution costs and a £114 statutory surcharge.

Mr Butterworth said police searched for him after a man matching Cotton’s description was seen driving a car that crashed on June 23 and found him nearby.

They gave him a breath test at the roadside that gave a reading of 106 micrograms of alcohol in 100 millilitres of breath, arrested him and took him to Fulford Road Police Station for a breath test, the result of which could be used in court. He refused to take it.

The legal limit is 35 micrograms of alcohol.

Cotton, who represented himself, said he had nothing to say.
