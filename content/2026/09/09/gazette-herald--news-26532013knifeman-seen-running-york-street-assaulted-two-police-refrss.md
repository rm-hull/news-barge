A man armed with a knife chased another man down a residential street in York during the morning rush-hour, the city’s magistrates heard.

Phillip Bilsborough, 37, told a probation officer after he had pleaded guilty to four offences that he had had two knives.

Martin Butterworth, prosecuting, said when police arrested him shortly afterwards, Bilsborough tried to head-butt one officer and kicked another on the leg.

Defence solicitor Shawn Cooper said the 37-year-old had been drinking heavily for two days before the incident on June 23.

Bilsborough, of St Phillips Grove, Clifton, pleaded guilty to carrying one knife in public, two charges of assaulting police officers and a public order offence.

Magistrates said his offences merited the maximum sentence they could pass – 12 months – and suspended it for 12 months on condition he does six months’ alcohol rehabilitative treatment and 12 days’ rehabilitative activities.

They also ordered him to pay £100 to each police officer, £85 prosecution costs and a £187 statutory surcharge, totalling £472. They also confiscated and ordered destruction of the knives seized by police including weapons taken from his house.

Mr Butterworth said a member of the public alerted police at 8.30am on June 23 that Bilsborough, armed with a knife, was chasing another man along Bramham Avenue, Chapelfields.

CCTV captured Bilsborough running out of an address with a knife in hand pursuing another man.

  


“He is physically chasing someone else while heavily intoxicated in a public street,” said Mr Butterworth.

Officers went to the scene and arrested Bilsborough, who was abusive towards them and threatened them.

He went to head butt one officer but failed to make contact and lashed out with his leg, hitting the other officer on the shin. Neither officer was injured.

Mr Cooper said Bilsborough was “alcohol dependent” and drank daily.

“On this occasion, he had been drinking heavily for two days and has very little memory of what happened,” said the defence solicitor.

Bilsborough also had mental health problems.

“He is clearly an individual who needs assistance. He wants assistance to handle his alcohol intake and reduce it,” said Mr Cooper.
