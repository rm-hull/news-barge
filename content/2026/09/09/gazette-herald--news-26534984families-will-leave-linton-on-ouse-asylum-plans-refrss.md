A "significant number" of families intend to leave a village near York if plans to house 1,200 male asylum seekers there go ahead, a group opposing the proposals has warned.

The Linton Action Group also said the families would withdraw their children from local schools if the plans at the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse proceed.

A Home Office spokesperson said it is "working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country" as part of government measures to close asylum hotels.

The Linton Action Group, which has invited Home Office minister Anna Turley to the village to discuss the proposals, said Linton-on-Ouse is "the wrong place" to house asylum seekers, arguing that the small village population would be overwhelmed by introducing up to 1,200 new residents.

  RAF Linton-On-Ouse in North Yorkshire which the Home Office is planning to use to house asylum seekers in its quest to shut more hotels 

*(Image: Ryan McDougall/PA Wire)*

From a survey of 404 local residents, the group said 96 per cent were concerned about integrating asylum seekers into the village and 93 per cent believed the proposals would strain local services.

The survey also found that among families with children, 35 per cent said they were "very concerned" about the proposals and 22 per cent said they would move their family from the area if the plans go ahead.

Olga Matthias, spokesperson for the Linton Action Group, called the government's proposals an "insane plan" which is "killing our village".

"This survey shows residents aren't merely worried, many will be forced to uproot their families, or withdraw their children from school," she said.

The survey comes after the group wrote to Ms Turley over the proposals, saying Linton-on-Ouse is a "small, isolated rural village of around 600 adults and 100 children", and adding 1,200 male asylum seekers to its population would mean "approximately one woman for every six men and one child for every 18 adults".

"York is around ten miles away and Easingwold around nine. This is not dispersal across a large urban area with extensive infrastructure. The immediate impact will inevitably be concentrated upon one small rural community," the group added.

Plans to house 1,500 asylum seekers at the base in Linton-on-Ouse were previously considered in 2022, but the controversial scheme was abandoned following local opposition and a legal challenge from the then Hambleton District Council.

In June, however, Sir Keir Starmer's government announced that plans were once again being considered to house asylum seekers at the base as part of efforts to reduce the use of hotels as accommodation.

A Home Office spokesperson said: "The concerns of communities are central to our immigration reforms. That is why we are closing every asylum hotel and moving asylum seekers into alternative accommodation, including former military sites.

"We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, engaging with local authorities to reduce the impact on communities."
