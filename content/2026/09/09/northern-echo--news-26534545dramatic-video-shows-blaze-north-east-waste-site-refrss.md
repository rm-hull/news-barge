A fire at a North East waste site is believed to have been caused by a lithium battery, sparking fresh warnings about proper battery disposal.

The blaze broke out at North Shields Waste Transfer Station in North Tyneside at 7.55am on August 26, prompting a swift response from Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service, who arrived just after 8am.

In response to the incident, North Tyneside Council has released the footage below, showing how rapidly a fire can start and spread as they urge residents to dispose of batteries responsibly.


The site was evacuated and remained closed for part of the morning while it was made safe for staff and the public.

Councillor Willie Samuel, cabinet member for environmental sustainability at North Tyneside Council, said: "This fire serves as a stark reminder that something as small as a battery can have serious consequences when it ends up in the wrong place.

"Thankfully, our contractor’s team acted quickly and professionally, and we would like to thank both Suez and Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service for their support.

"Their actions prevented what could have been a much more serious incident."

Although investigations are ongoing, the fire is believed to have been caused by a lithium battery.

Lithium batteries are commonly found in household items such as vapes, power tools, mobile phones, toys, and other e-cigarette devices.

When damaged, crushed, or punctured, these batteries can ignite without warning.

Residents are advised not to place loose batteries in household rubbish or recycling bins, to remove batteries from devices before disposal wherever possible, and to use dedicated battery recycling points at supermarkets, retailers, council buildings, or household waste and recycling centres.

Electrical items containing embedded batteries, such as vapes and rechargeable tools, should be taken to appropriate electrical recycling facilities.

Paul Russell, area manager for community safety at Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service, said: "Incidents like this show just how important it is for everyone to think carefully about how they carefully dispose of lithium batteries.

"We don’t realise how many of our everyday items are powered by lithium batteries, from vapes and toys to laptops and mobile phones."

"The standout danger happens when these products with a concealed lithium battery are thrown away into household waste or recycling bins.

"They can then become damaged during collection and processing, potentially causing fires that put workers, firefighters and the public at risk."

Anyone unsure about how to safely dispose of a battery, especially if it is damaged, swollen, or overheating, is encouraged to ask site staff for guidance before disposal.
