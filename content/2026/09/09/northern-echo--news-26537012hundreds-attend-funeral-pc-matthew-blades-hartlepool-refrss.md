The heartbroken wife of PC Matthew Blades has described the “unimaginable” pain of losing her husband as hundreds gathered to pay their respects at his funeral.

This morning (Wednesday, September 9) the officer was laid to rest after a service at St Joseph’s Church in Hartlepool.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


Hundreds of people packed the church paying their respects to the 37-year-old father of two who died on duty. Standard bearers from every force across the country lined the route as PC Blades’s hearse made the journey to the church.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


His coffin was draped in the Union Jack flag. The hearse carried a floral tribute at its front: “Dad”.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


In a statement his family said: “Matt’s laughter may no longer fill the room, but the memories of his humour, his love and the happiness he brought us will stay in our hearts forever.”

There was silence as mourners bowed their heads as PC Blades’s coffin made its way slowly to the church, followed by his family as part of the ceremonial parade.

Hundreds of officers from Cleveland Police were on parade, many wearing a patch with the thin blue line and the officers’ names and collar numbers - PC 2443 Blades and PC 3234 Clough.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


In a full statement issued after the funeral by the Cleveland Police Federation, his wife said: “There are no words powerful enough to express the heartbreak of losing my beloved best friend, my husband and father to our beautiful boys, especially in such tragic and unimaginable circumstances.

“The heart of our family has been taken from us and from those who loved him far too soon.

“The pain of his absence will be felt every day, but so will the love he gave, the memories he created, and the precious place he will forever hold in the hearts of his family.

“He may no longer be here to hold our hands, but his love will remain with them for the rest of their lives. He will be loved, remembered and missed beyond words, today, tomorrow and always.

“He will be incredibly proud and thankful for all the support that’s being given to us as a family.

“We miss everything about him, but perhaps most of all, we miss his laughter, his silly jokes and the way he could always make everyone smile, even when we least expected it.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


“He was the joker of the family, the one who brought light and laughter into every room, and life feels unbearably quiet without him.

“Matt’s laughter may no longer fill the room, but the memories of his humour, his love and the happiness he brought us will stay in our hearts forever.

“We will miss him more than words could ever say, and we will love him always.”

Lauren Somerville, chair of the Cleveland Police Federation, said: “The police family came together shoulder to shoulder today, arm in arm to remember our brave colleague PC Matthew Blades.

“It was an honour and a privilege to attend today’s funeral on behalf of our colleagues across Cleveland.

“His family have shown incredible dignity and bravery since those awful events in August, and it was our privilege to play our small part in honouring Matt’s memory.

  PC Matthew Blades


“Matt was a devoted husband and father. An adored son and brother, son-in-law, and is remembered as the most fun uncle. He has been taken from his loving family far too soon and they remain squarely in our thoughts.

“The support and love we offer them does not end today. We will always be there to take care of them.”

Police Minister Sarah Jones attended the service on behalf of the Government and PC Blades’ coffin was piped into the service to the tune “Amazing Grace”.

Tributes to PC Blades were made throughout the hour-long funeral service, including from Cleveland Police Chief Constable Victoria Fuller.

Ms Somerville added: “Matt was a police officer who was loved deeply by his colleagues and clearly a cherished and much missed football coach.

“It was so moving to hear the stories of his humour and cheekiness. That’s the man who will be remembered fondly and we will never grow tired of hearing these stories.

“Policing Cleveland will continue, but we will ensure that Matt and Tom, and their legacy, will never be forgotten.”
