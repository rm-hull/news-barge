A new centre designed to support pilgrims and visitors has been unveiled in Durham.

The Cuthbert Centre for Pilgrimage and Prayer marks a major step in Durham Cathedral’s Vision 2033 and its commitment to Christian faith exploration.

Centred on the Chapel of the Holy Cross, it will welcome individuals and groups and help more people explore the Christian faith.

The Very Reverend Dr Philip Plyming, Dean of Durham, said: "The completion of the Cuthbert Centre for Pilgrimage and Prayer is a moment of great joy and an opportunity to give thanks to God.

"This project has been many years in the making, and it was wonderful to welcome so many of the people whose generosity, commitment and support have helped turn a shared vision into reality.

"At its heart, this is not simply about a building.

"It is about creating a lasting legacy that will help people encounter the richness of pilgrimage and prayer, deepen their faith, and find space for reflection and renewal."

Guests, supporters, donors, and community representatives gathered at the cathedral on Friday (September 4), to celebrate the centre’s completion and recognise the contributions that made the project possible.

The centre offers a dedicated and accessible space for pilgrims arriving in Durham.

Caroline Peacock, chair of the Friends of Durham Cathedral, said: "The Friends of Durham Cathedral, a community of more than 3,000 members around the world, are delighted, deeply honoured – and indeed moved – to be the principal funders of the Cuthbert Centre for Pilgrimage and Prayer.

"It is a privilege to help bring this vision to fruition and to support a Centre that will encourage pilgrimage, prayer and hospitality for future generations."

The centre includes step-free access for the first time, making it welcoming to all.

At the celebration event, long-standing guide and cathedral volunteer Lilian Groves MBE unveiled a commemorative plaque.

The Bishop of Durham, the Rt Revd Rick Simpson, offered prayers for the centre’s future ministry.

Visitors were also invited to Festal Evensong and a procession marking the anniversary of the Translation of the Relics of St Cuthbert.

The centre’s full programme will launch in spring 2027.

The Revd James Shelton, Director of the Cuthbert Centre for Pilgrimage and Prayer, said: "The completion of the new Cuthbert Centre facilities is a wonderful step along the way towards delivering our first full programme of pilgrimage and prayer activities, to be announced in the new year.

"I am excited to see the Centre begin fulfilling its potential as a place of welcome, learning, reflection and community, starting with our Open Day on Saturday 26 September.

"We look forward to welcoming all those who are curious to come and find out more."
