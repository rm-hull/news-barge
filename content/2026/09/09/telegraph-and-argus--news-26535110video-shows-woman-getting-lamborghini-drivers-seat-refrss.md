Video has emerged showing a Bradford woman accused of killing her friend in a 120mph Lamborghini crash leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights, moving around the vehicle and getting into the driver's seat.

Humaira Batool, 26, denies causing death by dangerous driving of Mohammed Shaan Hussain, 20, following the crash on the M62 near Ripponden, Calderdale, on December 21, 2022.

Batool initially told police she had been asleep in the passenger seat and awoke when the car rolled, the court heard.


But footage shown to the jury shows Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights in Bradford, walking around the vehicle and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain.

The court has been told that Batool kep "entirely shtum" about the change of driver until confronted with the video footage.

Batool claims Mr Hussain triggered a "boost" by tampering with the supercar's controls.

She told jurors that Mr Hussain pressed buttons moments before the 600bhp Lamborghini Urus began to "surge".

Jurors at Leeds Crown Court are expected to be sent out today (September 9) to consider their verdict following closing speeches from the prosecution and defence.

Prosecutor Christopher Dunn said Batool had got up in the middle of the night after seeing Mr Hussain in the Lamborghini outside.

He was initially the driver of the powerful supercar, but the pair swapped seats and the court heard she drove at more than twice the speed limit in dark and wet conditions.

The court heard the car had reached speeds of up to 143mph before the fatal crash.

Mr Dunn told the jury: "The defendant's driving fell far below what would be expected of a competent and careful driver."

In terms of the footage showing her getting into the driver's seat, Mr Dunn said: "She kept entirely shtum about that until she was forced to acknowledge it when she was confronted with unequivocal evidence proving that she was the driver at the relevant time."

Batool told jurors Mr Hussain had been handling controls inside the car and said: "Watch this, watch this" before reaching towards the centre console and steering wheel.

She claimed the vehicle repeatedly "boosted" as he "pressed the buttons multiple times", causing her to panic and freeze.

But a Lamborghini expert told the trial there were no buttons, levers or controls which could cause such acceleration.

Mr Dunn told the jury: "You may feel she embarked on a deeply distasteful attempt to put the blame for that grossly excessive speed upon the person who died as a result of her dangerous driving."

Defence barrister Richard Dawson argued the collision may have been a genuine accident for which nobody was criminally responsible.

He said: "Sometimes, accidents happen - accidents in the true sense where no one is criminally to blame."

Mr Dawson said high speed alone would not make a car suddenly leave the road and overturn, suggesting something else must have caused the catastrophic loss of control.

He said: "The prosecution have never been able to provide any crucial detail as to how or why the collision actually occurred. They don't know."

Mr Dawson said Batool did not claim to know exactly what Mr Hussain touched, only that the car surged afterwards.

Mr Hussain had hired the Lamborghini to collect a friend from HMP Leeds and picked up Batool from her Bradford home at about 4am.

The car was travelling at around 120mph as it passed Stott Hall Farm - the "house in the middle of the motorway" - before leaving the carriageway, hitting a barrier and overturning.

Motorists desperately tried to rescue Mr Hussain but were beaten back by fire and an explosion.

Batool escaped and was found on a grass verge with serious injuries, including burns to around 10 per cent of her body and a shattered tibia.

Mr Hussain died in the wreckage.
