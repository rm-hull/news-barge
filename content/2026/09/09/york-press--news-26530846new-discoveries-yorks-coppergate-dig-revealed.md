Fresh discoveries from one of York’s most famous archaeological digs are set to take centre stage at a major event.

The findings, made in the years since the original Coppergate excavation, will be the focus of this year’s Richard Hall Symposium, named in honour of the late archaeologist who led the dig.

The event will take place on 18 October 2026, at Tempest Anderson Hall in the Yorkshire Museum, running from 9am to 5pm.

Gareth Henry, director of public engagement at York Archaeology, said: "Many people may assume that archaeology ends when trenches and pits are filled in, but really that is just the start of the story.

"What follows is an extensive programme of research and exploration, piecing together clues in a 1000 year old jigsaw to help us understand what life was like so many generations ago."

The Coppergate Dig, which ran from 1976 to 1981, uncovered Viking-age remains and led to the creation of the JORVIK Viking Centre.

Mr Henry added: "We’re delighted to be bringing together many of the people who have worked on this project to share their stories and discoveries at the Richard Hall Symposium."

The full-day event will include talks from archaeologists and researchers who have worked on the project.
