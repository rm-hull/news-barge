Arizona’s Grand Canyon National Park announced a recovery plan for the canyon’s water system Tuesday as search operations continue following August’s deadly flash flooding.

River patrols are still trying to find one hiker along the Colorado River, which runs through the canyon, officials said in a statement.

Timothy Smith, a 53-year-old Texan and father of three, is the last person who remains unaccounted for after the flooding. He was supposed to return home Wednesday, his wife Carrie Smith wrote in a Tuesday Facebook post.

“These last nine days have been the hardest days that my family has endured,” Carrie wrote.

Park officials confirmed the deaths of Smith’s friends, 46-year-old John Guisti and 42-year-old Brent Rosenkranz, around the river on September 1.

![The search continues for Texas hiker Timothy Smith (top right) following deadly flash flooding in Arizona’s Grand Canyon National Park last month](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/09/16/11/missinggrandcanyon.jpeg)

The hikers were caught off guard by a deluge on August 29 that flooded the park’s Bright Angel Canyon and Phantom Ranch areas with five feet of water.

The fast-moving storm dumped nearly an inch of rain per hour there, forcing the dramatic helicopter evacuations of 80 people.

The flooding rain damaged 40 percent of the park’s main water supply system along the canyon’s South Rim, leading to water cuts for locals and forcing the closure of local hotels.

But now, the park says there is a path forward to restore water service - and it is expected to take weeks, instead of months.

![People cross a bridge amid flash flooding in Arizona’s Grand Canyon on August 29. A fast-moving storm forced the rescue of 80 people and killed two men](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/09/17/2026-08-31T152512Z_1735237809_RC2R9NAV4JM8_RTRMADP_3_USA-FLOODS-GRAND-CANYON.JPG)

Crews are working on a temporary pipeline to replace the one destroyed in the flooding. The new pipeline would have the same capacity as the previous pipeline, pumping approximately 500 gallons of water per minute.

“Engineers are in the canyon designing the temporary intake, the existing Transcanyon Waterline contract has been modified to support the work, and construction activities are beginning this week,” the park said.

The park is working with Arizona’s Department of Environmental Quality to meet federal water quality requirements and said teams were actively monitoring the system for leaks and unusual water use.

Officials also stressed that conservation remains “critical” while work moves forward. Every gallon saved by businesses, visitors and locals gives crews more time.

![Park members hike through the Grand Canyon South Rim’s Phantom Ranch area on September 5. Park officials say there’s a plan to restore the canyon’s water system, which was damaged in the flooding](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/09/16/40/photo_of_damage-20260905-193120.jpeg)

Bottled water is being passed out and handwashing stations and portable showers and toilets have been deployed throughout the community.

Tap water remains safe to use and there is no boil-water advisory in effect.

“Current conservation measures are offsetting approximately 137,000 gallons of potable water use each day, extending the amount of time available for crews to complete critical work on the water system,” the park wrote.
