BRILLIANT, brilliant and brilliant again: Operation Mincemeat is undoubtedly prime beef masquerading as a cheap cut and, more than any show I have seen in many a year, deserved the instant standing ovation it was given by a packed house.

This five-hander leaves you almost breathless such is its split-second timing, costume changes magnifique and seamless choreography and direction by Jenny Arnold and Robert Hastie.

With hints of Book of Mormon, Hamilton (that’s the rapping bit!), One Man, Two Guvnors (the Ray Cooney farce element) and Mel Brooks’ The Producers (the Nazi satire part!), it is safe to say that Operation Mincemeat is madness exquisite!

But therein lies its utter brilliance: based on a real story, this two-hour musical - I use the term loosely - somehow manages to turn one of the most pivotal events of World War II into a piece of entertainment that is brimming with brilliance.

First aired at the Edinburgh Fringe in 2019 before its West End debut in 2023 and then Broadway in 2025, the show tells the true story of Operation Mincemeat and the elaborate plot by MI5’s ‘Twenty Committee’ to fool Hitler into making a wrong strategic decision.

And he fell for it hook, line and sinker, moving thousands of troops from Sicily to Sardinia, paving the way for allied soldiers to capture the Italian island and use it as a beachhead for the D-Day landings.

But considering that said ‘ruse’ involved dumping a dead body into the waters off Spain to fool the Germans into thinking that they had discovered the remains of a crashed pilot and with him, plans to invade Sardinia (not Sicily, if you’re still with me?), it is hard to imagine that the four writers (David Cumming, Natasha Hodgson, Felix Hagan and Zoe Roberts) could somehow turn this into 21st century entertainment gold. But they have.

And they manage to pull off what only Mel Brooks could have in The Producers: a complete send-up of the Third Reich with singing, dancing, glitzy costumes and sequined swastikas, thrown in for good measure.

  Holly Sumpton as Ewan Montagu. Matt Crockett


There were no celebs, just five brilliant actors: the multi-talented Sean Carey as Charles Cholmondeley, Holly Sumpton as Ewen Montagu - a true all-rounder and stage anchor - the fantastic Jamie-Rose Monk (Johnny Bevan), Charlotte Hanna-Williams as Jean Leslie and the brilliant Christian Andrews as office manager, Hester Leggat...everyone playing several other characters to boot.

  Christian Andrews as Hester Leggatt. Matt Crockett


The stage was filled with raw talent, not ‘names’ and it was an absolute joy to watch, leaving me almost breathless by curtain down, not only for its complexity of thought, music, and, occasionally, rap style lyrics, but for its seamless delivery.

If ‘different’ is your by-word then Operation Mincemeat is for you. Expect the unexpected!

* Operation Mincemeat runs at Leeds Grand Theatre until Saturday, September 12.
