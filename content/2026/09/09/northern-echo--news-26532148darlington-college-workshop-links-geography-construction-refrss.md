A new workshop in Darlington is helping students see how classroom subjects connect to career opportunities.

Darlington College students recently took part in the Geography session, part of the Construction in the Curriculum programme led by Esh Group.

The programme aims to show students how subjects like geography are applied in the construction industry and can lead to specific job roles.

Dale Morris, technical director at Homes by Esh, said: "The geography session gives students an introduction to the same principles we consider when bringing forward new housing developments.

"From assessing site constraints and environmental factors to determining how land can be used most effectively – it reflects many of the decisions made within my role, they are just actioned on an appropriate scale for the workshop participants to take away clear insights to our sector."

During the session, students examined factors influencing housing development, including flood risk, biodiversity net gain, and drainage.

One student, Chantelle, said: "One thing I'd take away from today is how important geography is for construction and factors such as drainage, flood risks, and surrounding environments, and how they have an impact on where housing estates are built.

"The session has definitely made me more interested in the wider planning side of construction."

The workshop is part of Esh Group’s Constructing Local commitments, which aim to deliver social value in the areas where the company works.

Lee Chatterton, senior lecturer at Darlington College, said: "It’s really important that students are able to relate their studies to future career opportunities.

"It’s very important to promote the wide variety of roles available within the construction industry and therefore it’s invaluable to us that businesses like Esh extend the hand of friendship, allowing young people to discover new career opportunities, succeed as they move forward and ultimately inspire the next generation."

Construction in the Curriculum now covers maths, science, and geography.

Kate Marshall, social value manager at Esh Group, said: “Construction in the Curriculum helps bridge the gap between education and industry by showing students how classroom learning translates into real careers within construction and the built environment."
