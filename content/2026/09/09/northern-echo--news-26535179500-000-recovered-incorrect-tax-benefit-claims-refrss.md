A council crackdown on fraud has recovered more than half a million pounds in false and incorrect benefits and tax reduction claims.

Hartlepool Borough Council has reclaimed more than £500,000 following an investigation into false claims for the Single Person Discount (SPD) on Council Tax, as well as improper claims for Council Tax Reduction, Housing Benefit, and Universal Credit.

The SPD provides a 25 per cent reduction in Council Tax for properties occupied by only one adult.

Councillor Graham Harrison, who now plans to step down as leader of Hartlepool Borough Council, said: "We are committed to ensuring that everyone who is entitled to financial support receives it, and we actively encourage residents to claim all the benefits and discounts they are legitimately entitled to.

"We understand that some claims can be made incorrectly but in good faith.

"However, we will not stand by while a minority choose to deliberately abuse the system for their own gain, and we make absolutely no apology for taking a robust approach to tackling fraud."

The council began investigating SPD claims in April, resulting in a reduction of 944 discounts.

This equates to an annual saving of more than £402,000, based on the lowest Band A SPD rate of £426.15 per property.

Additional savings of £140,000 a year have been identified through investigations into Council Tax Reduction and Housing Benefit claims in the last four months alone.

The council previously warned residents of the impending SPD review and encouraged people to ensure their claims were accurate.

While some residents responded by updating their information, others did not.

The anti-fraud team continues to receive referrals and is handling appeals from individuals who have lost discounts or benefits as a result of the investigation.

One resident claimed they had never lived at a property linked to a disputed claim, but evidence indicated otherwise.

Another individual requested a refund for seven months of SPD, insisting they lived alone.

Instead, they were issued a bill of more than £5,000 after investigators found the claim to be false.

The council also reviewed capital held by benefit claimants in 20 targeted cases.

Seven individuals were found to have undeclared savings above the eligibility threshold, resulting in overpayments totalling £36,813.

Councillor Harrison said: "Those who deliberately provide false information, fail to declare changes in their circumstances or attempt to exploit public funds should be in no doubt that we will take strong action."
