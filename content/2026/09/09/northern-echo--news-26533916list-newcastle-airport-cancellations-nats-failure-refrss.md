Newcastle Airport passengers are facing further cancellations and delays as the fallout from a major air traffic control system failure continues to hit airports across the UK.

Three arrivals at Newcastle Airport have been cancelled or delayed so far today.

  **The affected flights are:**

- TUI flight TOM1257 from Tenerife, due to arrive at 2.50am, has been delayed.
- easyJet flight EZY237 from Bristol, due at 8.20am, has been cancelled.
- British Airways flight BA1328 from London Heathrow, due at 10am, has also been cancelled.

Three departures have also been cancelled at Newcastle Airport.

  **The cancelled departures are:**

- easyJet flight EZY5605 to Amsterdam, due to leave at 7.20am, was cancelled.
- easyJet flight EZY238 to Bristol, scheduled for 8.50am, was also cancelled.
- British Airways flight BA1329 to London Heathrow, due to depart at 11.05am, has been cancelled.

Newcastle Airport said a technical problem at NATS, which runs air traffic control across the UK, had affected its flights.

A Newcastle Airport spokesperson said: “We are aware of a technical issue affecting NATS which is impacting some flights at airports across the UK.

“While some departures from Newcastle are experiencing delays, our operations have not been affected to the same extent as those at some airports in the south of the country.

“Passengers are advised to check with their airline for the latest flight information.”

A small number of flights are scheduled at Teesside Airport today, and all are currently due to operate as normal. We will update this if that changes.

The disruption follows more than 1,300 flight cancellations at UK airports on Tuesday after a system issue hit National Air Traffic Services (NATS).

Tens of thousands of passengers were caught up in the chaos, with more cancellations and delays expected as airlines deal with the fallout.

NATS said engineers were working to restore normal operations.

A spokesman said: “Our engineers are on site and working urgently to resume normal operations as quickly as possible.

“We recognise the ongoing disruption to travel, for which we sincerely apologise.

“Flights are still departing and airspace is open, but recovery will take some time.

“We will provide further updates as soon as possible.

“Passengers should check the status of their flight with their airline.”

NATS later warned that the disruption had affected the aviation network and recovery would take time.

NATS chief executive Martin Rolfe said a thorough investigation into the incident would take place and ruled out a cyber attack.

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband called the disruption “frankly unacceptable” and said it must not happen again.
