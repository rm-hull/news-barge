**North Yorkshire Council has put forward 24 recommendations** following a review of its Home to School Travel Policy, which was overhauled in September 2024 to shift free transport eligibility from traditional catchment areas to the nearest suitable school with an available place. The service manages travel for about 11,000 pupils at an annual cost exceeding £52 million.

While the review found the policy is achieving budget savings and aligning with national guidelines, it acknowledged challenges for families, noting that 741 pupils were denied free transport who would have previously qualified, generating numerous appeals and Ombudsman complaints.

## Key Recommendations and Changes

- **Route Revisions:** The council proposes permanently removing five remote, high-altitude roads—Fleet Moss Road, Keld to Kirkby Stephen, Buttertubs Pass, The Stang, and Kidstone Pass—from the digital mapping (GIS) database due to severe weather and difficult terrain making them unreliable for transport calculations.
- **Operational Adjustments:** Proposed changes include a two-mile road journey discrepancy safety net for stage-one appeals, an extension of the notice period from 7 to 30 days for withdrawing paid spare bus seat permits, and updating school gate mapping every five years.
- **What Remains Unchanged:** The review does not recommend returning to catchment-based eligibility, automatic transport for younger siblings, or altering restrictions on faith school transport and split addresses.

## Statements From Council Leadership


**Cllr Annabel Wilkinson, executive member for education, learning and skills:**

We know that school travel arrangements are incredibly important to families and that the changes introduced in 2024 have affected some households more than others. When the policy changed, we promised to review how it was working in practice and to listen to the experiences of families. That is exactly what we have done.”

We have carefully considered feedback from parents and carers, schools and other stakeholders, alongside appeals, complaints and operational data. The review shows the policy is meeting its objectives, but it has also highlighted areas where we can make improvements. We are committed to learning from people’s experiences and ensuring families have clearer information and a better experience of the service.

Families should continue to make school preference decisions based on existing arrangements while the decision-making process is completed. To support families during the transition period, we will continue to use the appeals process and discretionary powers in exceptional cases. This means support can be considered where specific routes or circumstances cause difficulties, rather than waiting for any future policy changes to take effect.


## Next Steps

The recommendations will be reviewed by the Children and Families Overview and Scrutiny Committee and the executive in September 2026, ahead of a public consultation and a final full council vote in November 2026. Approved changes would not take effect before September 2027. Further details and the full report are available at www.northyorks.gov.uk/PIR.
