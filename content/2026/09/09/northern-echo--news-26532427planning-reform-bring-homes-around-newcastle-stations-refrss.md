More than a million homes could be built around train stations in Newcastle if planning rules were reformed.

Research by the Centre for Cities shows that 1.2 million homes are missing around inner-city rail hubs in Britain's 12 largest cities, including Newcastle, when compared with housing densities in other G7 countries such as France and Japan.

The independent think tank says that slow progress on the Prime Minister’s pledge to build more homes near stations is due to restrictive planning rules and unclear land ownership, which make urban development difficult and limit city control over adjacent sites.

Anthony Breach, director of policy and research at Centre for Cities, said: "Building a lot of homes around existing stations is one of the most effective ways to boost city economies.

"In a low-growth economy like Britain, it is one of the most glaringly obvious problems to fix."

According to the analysis, London is missing more than 500,000 homes, with Glasgow short by 183,000 and Manchester by 134,000.

Manchester is the best-performing British city outside London, adding 30,000 homes near inner-city stations between 2011 and 2021.

London delivered 300,000 homes in the same period.

Glasgow, Birmingham and Liverpool also saw growth but still lag behind international peers.

Centre for Cities recommends (in England) fast-tracking land transfers from Network Rail to mayoral authorities, expanding the use of Mayoral Development Corporations, and introducing Mayoral Development Orders to speed up planning.

Steve Rotheram, Mayor of the Liverpool City Region, said: "We’re already leading the country on station devolution in the Liverpool City Region – and showing what can be achieved when transport, housing and regeneration are planned together.

"We want to build on what we’ve started.

"Giving us greater control over stations and the land around them would open up more sites for development and help us build the homes our residents need, where the transport links already exist."
