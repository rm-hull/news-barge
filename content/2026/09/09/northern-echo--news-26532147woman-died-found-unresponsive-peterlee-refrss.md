A woman died after being found unresponsive on a grass verge in a County Durham town, an inquest has heard.

Victoria Jayne Kipling, from Hartlepool, was found by a member of the public on a patch of grass on Passfield Way, in Peterlee, on June 13.

Paramedics were called to the scene and administered CPR but the 53-year-old was sadly pronounced dead at the scene.

An inquest opened into her death at Crook Coroners’ Court on Tuesday (September 8) heard how Victoria’s cause of death was the combined toxic effects of a number of drugs.

This included morphine, pregabalin (used to treat epilepsy and anxiety), fluoxetine (antidepressant), diazepam and amitriptyline (for pain and migraines).

The post mortem was carried out by Dr Sam Cook at the Newcastle Royal Victoria Infirmary Mortuary on June 19.

   

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

A cordon was erected on Passfield Way following the incident earlier this year, where pictures showed three police vehicles surrounding a blue tent.

Durham Police said at the time that the force was not treating the death as suspicious, adding that a file would be prepared for the coroner.

Floral tributes were left at the scene following the incident, with one message reading: “Fly high, I hope you make them angels laugh as much as you did me. Gone too soon."

Another card paying tribute said: "You are the craziest person I have ever met, you would do everything for anyone, the most beautiful soul."

A force spokesperson said at the time: "Police were called just before 7.05am on Saturday (June 13) following reports that a woman had been found unresponsive on Passfield Way, in Peterlee.

"Paramedics were also called, but sadly the woman, who is in her fifties, was pronounced dead at the scene.

"Her death is not being treated as suspicious, and a file will be prepared for the coroner."

Senior coroner Jeremy Chipperfield adjourned the inquest until October 8.
