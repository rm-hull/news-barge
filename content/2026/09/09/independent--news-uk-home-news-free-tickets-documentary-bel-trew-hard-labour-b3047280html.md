*The Independent* is offering 10 pairs of tickets to a special screening at the ICA London of the moving new documentary Hard Labour: Inside the global crisis killing mothers and babies.

For centuries, pregnancy and childbirth were among the world’s greatest dangers for women. Over the past 25 years, major advances in healthcare have helped reduce maternal deaths by 40 per cent. But that progress is now under threat. Unprecedented international aid cuts, combined with the highest levels of conflict seen in a generation, are reversing years of hard-won gains.

In this new documentary, Bel Trew travels to some of the world's most dangerous places for pregnancy and childbirth, meeting mothers, babies and frontline healthcare workers as they confront the devastating consequences of conflict, poverty and collapsing healthcare systems.

The screening will take place at 6.30pm on Tuesday 15 September. The screening will be followed by a panel with Bel, Monica Ferro of the UN Population Fund (UNFPA) and Dr Alexandra Ridout, Clinical Lecturer in Women and Children's Health, Kings College London

**To be in with a chance of claiming the free tickets, simply complete the form below by 4pm on 7 September.**

Last year, Donald Trump slashed aid funding from the US, essentially shuttering the United States Agency for International Development, taking billions of dollars away from projects around the world. Those cuts were followed by other nations around the world, including the UK.

Recent aid reductions have cut off lifesaving services for many. According to the UNFPA, across 19 of the humanitarian countries where they operate, more than 11 million people have lost access to essential sexual and reproductive health and protection services, while almost 1,100 health facilities and mobile clinics have been forced to close and nearly 6,000 frontline health workers, including midwives, have lost their jobs.

Bel Trew, Chief International Correspondent at *The Independent*, said: “Over the last 25 years of global efforts, the world managed to bring maternal mortality rates down by 40 per cent. We were on track to end preventable deaths in childbirth. Until now.

“For this film we went to the worst places on earth to give birth – where the nightmare mix of unprecedented aid cuts and unprecedented levels of conflict have put the lives of mothers and their babies on the line. We hope this is a wakeup call to all.”

To mark the release of Hard Labour, *The Independent* is shining a light on the lifesaving work of the UNFPA to raise awareness of the urgent maternal health needs of millions of women around the world. UNFPA works with partners in more than 150 countries to provide access to a range of sexual and reproductive health services – including in dozens of humanitarian settings. As a global leader in maternal and newborn health, UNFPA trains midwives, and supplies hospitals with lifesaving maternal health medicines and supplies, including emergency birth kits. Making motherhood safer is at the core of UNFPA’s mandate and is a human rights imperative.

“No woman should die while giving life. Yet, every two minutes a woman dies due to pregnancy or childbirth, mostly in fragile and conflict-affected settings,” said Ms Ferro. “The good news is we know how to address this daily, global emergency. We need political will and investments in solutions that work – including universal access to midwives, which could prevent nearly two thirds of maternal and newborn deaths.”
