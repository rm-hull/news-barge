A historic primary school in Stockton has undergone a multi-million-pound transformation.

Oxbridge Lane Primary School has been fully refurbished in an £8.2 million redevelopment, funded as part of Stockton Borough Council’s wider £57.7 million investment in education.

Built in 1890, the school has been modernised to improve teaching spaces while preserving its original Victorian architecture.

Lauren Amerigo, headteacher at Oxbridge Lane Primary, said: "We pride ourselves on a happy, secure, and aspirational environment so that all our pupils can reach their full potential.

"These upgraded facilities will enhance our pupils' overall educational experience while ensuring the school maintains its character in a modern twist by staying on Oxbridge Lane."

The project included the addition of a glazed reception, a kitchen, solar panels, and improved early years provision.

A state-of-the-art kitchen has also been installed, allowing pupils to receive nutritious meals on site.

Councillor Clare Besford, cabinet member for children and young people at Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council, said: "We're immensely proud of our investment in schools across the borough and this spectacular transformation at Oxbridge Lane Primary School will certainly improve the overall educational experience for current and future pupils."

Brims Construction Ltd, a Stockton-based contractor, delivered the work alongside the council’s design services and asset management team.

Jeremy Marley, contracts manager at Brims Construction Ltd, said: "We are delighted to have worked on a Victorian building of such magnificent design and quality.

"Operating within a live school environment always poses unique challenges, and delivering this multi-phase project was no small feat.

"Thanks to the outstanding support, commitment and cooperation of our site team, the school, and Stockton-on-Tees Borough Council, we successfully navigated every stage without compromising on safety, quality or learning."
