Hidden pubs, scenic riverside strolls, and wide-open green spaces are among the quieter corners of York waiting to be explored by visitors and their dogs.

As autumn approaches, Jerry Rebbeck, a local expert from Wheelwrights York, has shared his favourite spots for visitors exploring the city with their dogs.

His recommendations include welcoming pubs, scenic walking routes, and peaceful green spaces that offer a quieter alternative to the busy city centre.

Mr Rebbeck said: "There are many pubs in York that are often overlooked when it comes to being dog-friendly.

"The Cross Keys, located on Goodramgate, is one example.

"The pub welcomes well-behaved dogs and provides water bowls and treats after a long walk around the city.

"The Ackhorne, located in St Martin’s Lane, is another hidden gem for dog owners visiting York.

"Tucked away down a cobbled lane off Micklegate, this cosy pub welcomes dogs of all breeds and provides treats and water for four-legged visitors.

"It’s a relaxed spot to stop off at after exploring the city, particularly for visitors looking to discover some of York’s less obvious places away from the main tourist trail."

For those looking to stretch their legs, Mr Rebbeck suggests beginning a stroll along the River Ouse from the city centre towards Millennium Bridge.

Once there, visitors can cross the bridge and return along the opposite bank.

He also recommends Clifton Ings, a large open grassland area next to the River Ouse, and The Knavesmire, home to York Racecourse.

Mr Rebbeck added: "It’s a great way to see a different side of York and it gives your dog a decent walk without having to travel far from the heart of the city."

For a peaceful escape, Mr Rebbeck points to Rowntree Park and Homestead Park as ideal green spaces for dog owners.

Rowntree Park welcomes dogs on leads, while Homestead Park offers open lawns, woodland walks, and a wildflower meadow.

Dog bowls are available near the public toilets, and an on-site café provides a place to stop and refuel.

For visitors in September, Mr Rebbeck highlights York’s first-ever city-centre dog show, taking place on Sunday, September 20, from 10am to 4pm in Museum Gardens.

He also notes that many ghost walks starting from The King’s Arms pub and Grape Lane are dog-friendly and begin nightly from 8pm.

River cruises departing from King’s Staith Landing or Lendal Bridge also welcome dogs at no extra charge.
