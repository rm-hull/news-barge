An MP has welcomed maternity services at York Hospital being upgraded from 'inadequate' to 'good' following an inspection.

York Outer Luke Charters said “staff should be really proud” but acknowledged “there is still more to do” after the inspection from the Care Quality Commission (CQC).

The CQC upgraded the rating of the maternity services at York Hospital following a two-day inspection in April.

It follows the watchdog improving the wider hospital site’s rating from inadequate to “requires improvement” last year.

  York Hospital 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Maternity services at Scarborough Hospital were also rated good overall after a separate two-day inspection by the CQC in April.

The director of midwifery at the York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, which runs York and Scarborough hospitals, said it was a “proud moment” for the trust and “for the women and families we care for”.

Mr Charters said: “This is such welcome news for families across York and a real credit to the midwives, doctors and wider maternity team who have worked incredibly hard to make these improvements.

“My wife and I experienced that care ourselves when our baby, Louis, was born at York Hospital. We were looked after with such kindness and reassurance at one of the biggest moments of our lives, and that is something our family will always be grateful for.

“Since becoming York Outer’s MP, I have heard from many constituents about their own experiences of maternity care. Some have shared really positive stories, while others have come to me with concerns. It is an incredibly important issue and every family deserves to feel safe, listened to and properly supported.”

The Labour MP added: “Moving from inadequate to good is a step forward. Staff should be really proud of what they have achieved, but there is still more to do.

“The trust now needs to keep listening to women and families, act on the areas where further improvement is needed and make sure this progress lasts.”

The York and Scarborough trust’s director of midwifery, Sascha Wells-Munro, said: “We know our work does not stop here. I want to assure all women and families that we will keep listening to you and learning from you. We will build on this progress, sustain the changes we have worked so hard for and keep looking at how we can improve.”

The trust’s chief nurse, Joe Hague, added: “This rating provides important independent recognition of the progress we have made in the quality and safety of care.

“It’s an important milestone, but our responsibility now is to sustain these improvements and continue getting better. We will take confidence from this report while remaining focused on providing consistently safe, high-quality and compassionate maternity care for every woman, baby and family who relies on us.”
