A major milestone has been reached in the development of a UK carbon capture and storage (CCS) network.

The Castorone, one of the world’s largest pipelay vessels, has arrived in the Southern North Sea to begin laying 136km of pipeline between Teesside and the Endurance storage site as part of the Northern Endurance Partnership (NEP).

The pipeline will transport captured CO₂ emissions from industry to a permanent storage site beneath the North Sea.

A spokesperson for the NEP said: "Just a few months ago, we welcomed the arrival of our linepipe into the Port of Hartlepool.

"Today, seeing Castorone arrive in the Southern North Sea to begin laying that linepipe is a powerful reminder of the pace and scale of progress being made.

"This is more than an engineering milestone.

"It is a significant moment for the East Coast Cluster, for industrial decarbonisation and for the UK's journey to net zero.

"The infrastructure we are building has the potential to support jobs, attract investment to the region and help secure a cleaner future for generations to come."

The Castorone, which is 325 metres long, will be powered by nearly 3,000 tonnes of marine biofuel during the offshore campaign.

Sonardyne, a specialist in underwater technology, will also deploy environmental monitoring landers on the seabed.

These autonomous devices will continuously track water chemistry and currents to help verify safe CO₂ storage.

The NEP said the use of biofuel and environmental monitoring technology reflects its commitment to sustainable construction and long-term marine protection.

The infrastructure will support the East Coast Cluster, a CCS project designed to help decarbonise industry in Teesside and the Humber.

The NEP said this work could protect existing jobs, create new employment opportunities and attract further investment to the region.

The project is seen as essential to delivering the UK’s net zero ambitions.
