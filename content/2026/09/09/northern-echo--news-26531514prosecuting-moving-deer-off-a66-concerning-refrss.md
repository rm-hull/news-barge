*“The process feels like it has been the punishment. It does not feel as if it is in the public interest.”*

As a self-confessed lover of nature, Guy Carpenter thought he was doing no wrong when he picked up the carcass of a dead deer from the A66 near Barnard Castle in June.

The 42-year-old had moved the animal from the central reservation with the aim of taking it home to eat - but was stopped in his tracks by officers who said he was breaking the law.

It was a situation that left the photographer and church magazine editor “unnerved” - and two months later when he was issued a court summons, his anxiety skyrocketed.

  Yesterday, Mr Carpenter appeared at __Peterlee Magistrates' Court__ for removing the carcass of a deer without the consent of the owner or occupier of the land, where an out-of-court resolution was sought and the hearing was adjourned. 

Mr Carpenter, who was a volunteer at the now closed Wetheriggs Animal Sanctuary, has now spoken out about the ordeal, questioning the public interest in taking the case to court.

“This attempt to criminalise a law abiding and responsible member of society for doing something that is perhaps slightly unusual but not what most people would consider criminal or antisocial is concerning,” he argued.

  Guy Carpenter

“For many years I have picked up roadkill, both to clear it off the road, and to make use of the meat, as I believe - along with many other like-minded people - it is responsible to use these available resources.

"I grew up in the Yorkshire Dales. I have a love of the natural world and I have always spent a lot of time with animals, and watching animals.

“I dislike seeing animals being crushed into the road, and rotting away on the tarmac without dignity.

“The prosecution has cost the taxpayer, and myself, significant amounts of both time and money and does not feel as if it is in the public interest.

“The process feels like it has been the punishment, and has taken a toll on my physical and mental health. I'm not sure I can identify who has benefited from it."

Concerns have since been raised over whether the prosecution is a waste of money, with a TaxPayers' Alliance spokesperson saying taxpayers will “struggle to understand why scarce police and court resources are being spent pursuing a case like this”.

  Mr Carpenter, who edits the magazine for churches in the Holmedale Benefice north of Richmond, was prosecuted by __Durham Police__ under The Deer Act 1991.

This states that it is an offence to take or move the carcass of a deer without the consent of the owner or land occupier.

It is understood that, under UK law, deer are considered ownerless until they are killed, after which it becomes the property of the land owner where it was found.

Following his appearance in court, Mr Carpenter was granted conditional bail and the hearing was adjourned until October 26, unless a resolution is found outside of court.
