SITCOM fans were devastated when Richard O'Sullivan died earlier this month.

The Man About The House star was one of the biggest names on British television during the 1970s.

But after walking away from showbiz at the height of his fame in 1988 he was hardly heard from again - except once.

His last appearance on television was as a guest on a 1999 edition of This Is Your Life held in honour of his co-star and great friend, Bradford-born George Layton.

O'Sullivan had himself been the subject of the show in 1974 when Layton was a guest.

The pair struck up a life-long friendship when they starred together in Doctor At Large, a popular spin-off from a series of British comedies released in the 1960s and 70s.

Despite the fact that O'Sullivan had lived in Brinsworth House in Twickenham, a retirement home for actors and entertainment workers following a stroke in 2003, he remained firm friends with Layton.

Last year, the two pals met up to celebrate O'Sullivan's 81st birthday and Layton posted: "Me & my dear pal Richard 'Ricardo' O'Sullivan celebrating his 81st birthday today!!

"We've been strolling (at a good pace) down Memory Lane - & it's a long lane, I can tell you. We can't remember where we first met but it's been a blast! Love you Richard..."

Layton was a familiar face on television in the 1970s and 1980s with roles in Minder and The Sweeney.

He was born George Michael William Löwy at Bradford Royal Infirmary on March 2, 1943, to Austrian-refugee parents.

As a child, he attended Belle Vue Boys' Grammar School in Bradford. He also started his acting career at the Bradford Civic Playhouse before training at RADA.
