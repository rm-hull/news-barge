A new mural has been unveiled in a York community – and more artists are being sought to help brighten up the area further.

The mural on the side of the Spar building in Lowther Street explores the history of the Groves.

It features a flat cap wearing fox – an animal often spotted in the area – apples, historic buildings and York’s well-known Bile Beans advertisement.

Street artist Tom Sledmore, known as SledOne, created the mural which was funded by the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority and commissioned by charity Door 84.

Tom is also the artist behind the “Welcome to the Groves” mural in Lowther Street completed in 2022.

A spokesperson for the Groves Association, which works to improve the area, said Tom had done a “really good job” of the new mural.

“People love it,” they said. “[The wall] was a bit run down before.

“It was really good that people also had a say in it. That’s what we wanted.”

They said the community has secured funding for four more murals but is still searching for artists to create them.

These murals will include a larger artwork beneath the cycle path in Huntington Road and smaller pieces elsewhere in the Groves.

The Groves Association spokesperson said the murals would be created by the artists working with community payback and youth justice services.

They hoped the new murals would encourage other artists to make their mark on the area.

The spokesperson also said the smaller murals would be a good opportunity for emerging artists to showcase their work in York.

“I would love it if we got some artists who have never done this before to give them a chance,” they said. “It’s got to be professional, but it could be a start for them to say, ‘I did this mural in the groves.’”

The murals will come as part of ongoing efforts to brighten up the Groves which residents said have helped to reduce crime in the area.

New planters are due to be installed in Lowther Street next week, with plans also in place for bunting and new lighting to be installed in the street.

  Tracy Ostle in front of the 'Welcome to the Groves' created by Tom Sledmore in 2022 

*(Image: Dylan Connell)*

Tracy Ostle, chair of the Groves Association, told The Press in July that the ongoing efforts to improve the Groves have resulted in people taking more pride in their community.

“The idea is to make it a pleasant area,” she said. “The more pleasant you make it, the less likely it is that people will use it for the wrong reasons.”

“I wouldn’t want it to be the Hebden Bridge of York,” she added. “But I want to light it up. It’s a bit grim.”
