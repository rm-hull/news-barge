Review – Lindisfarne Festival, Beal Farm – Northumberland

As the seasons change, and we bid farewell to the glorious summer weather we’ve encountered this year, the start of autumn brought one of the last festivals of the outdoor events season – that being Lindisfarne Festival in Northumberland.

Set against the causeway adjacent to Holy Island, thousands of revellers once again descended upon Beal Farm for the event’s annual gathering.

Festival punters (also known as Pilgrims) by the event’s fraternity made their annual pilgrimage to the Northumberland locale for three days of entertainment.

  Festivalgoers enjoyed Lindisfarne Festival at Beal Farm near Holy Island, where thousands gathered for a weekend of live music, entertainment and festival activities in Northumberland 

*(Image: Adam Kennedy)*

And whilst Beal Farm may not be quite as established as Worthy Farm and Glastonbury, that is the beauty and draw of Lindisfarne Festival.

It’s the boutique event’s small and friendly setting and programming that keeps the festival’s loyal fanbase returning year after year.

Whereas with bigger events, you have to walk considerable distances between stages, each of the stages at Lindisfarne Festival is a mere stone's throw away.

And what’s more, the diversity of the programming across the festival site sets the event apart from the rest.

Whilst the event may be small and friendly, don’t let that fool you in terms of the artists performing.

  Festivalgoers enjoyed Lindisfarne Festival at Beal Farm near Holy Island, where thousands gathered for a weekend of live music, entertainment and festival activities in Northumberland 

*(Image: Adam Kennedy)*

Across the course of the weekend, a whole raft of the entertainment world's finest graced the main stages of the event, each bringing something different to the equation.

Mainstage headliners included the likes of The Proclaimers and indie/rock royalty Razorlight.

Whilst dance music legends Faithless provided an explosive conclusion to the festival on Saturday evening, with anthems such as Insomnia and God Is A DJ soundtracking the final night of the event.

But it wasn’t just the headliners that pulled in the crowds; the undercard was equally as strong, with acts such as M People’s Heather Small, Soul II Soul, Elvana, Utah Saints and AC/DC tribute Back In Black all proving to be a big hit with the Lindisfarne crowd.

  Festivalgoers enjoyed Lindisfarne Festival at Beal Farm near Holy Island, where thousands gathered for a weekend of live music, entertainment and festival activities in Northumberland 

*(Image: Adam Kennedy)*

The beauty of an event like Lindisfarne Festival is how supportive it is to the local music scene.

Just one glance down the action-packed line-up and you would notice many names from the North East.

Shannon Pearl once again proved to be a popular choice on Friday afternoon, with the artist’s ethereal brand of neo-folk music enchanting the Lindisfarne crowd throughout.

Whereas acts like Leazes and Swindled also flew the flag high for the North East music scene over the course of the weekend.

  Festivalgoers enjoyed Lindisfarne Festival at Beal Farm near Holy Island, where thousands gathered for a weekend of live music, entertainment and festival activities in Northumberland 

*(Image: Adam Kennedy)*

Once again, Lindisfarne Festival proved that you don’t have to travel hundreds of miles to go to a music festival – sometimes the best events you will experience are right here on your doorstep.

We can’t wait for next year’s event already.
