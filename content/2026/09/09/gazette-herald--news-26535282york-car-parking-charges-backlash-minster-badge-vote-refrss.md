PARKING charges in York are putting off customers and tourists, a shop has claimed ahead of a decision on whether to cut some fees.

Mark Jones, of the Cleggs gift shop in Goodramgate, said his turnover fell by almost a third year-on-year following parking charge hikes in April 2025.

York Business Improvement District (BID) Chief Executive Andrew Lowson said a recent review into parking charges backed up traders' views that fees were discouraging people from visiting shopping areas.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, City of York Council's Labour transport spokesperson, said the recommendations of the review would be looked at carefully before a way forward is decided.

It comes after proposals to cut fees and increase discounts for residents' Minster Badge parking passes are set to go before the council's executive on Wednesday, September 9.

Permits would be £15 cheaper, costing £15 a year rather than £45 for two years, and discounts in community areas would increase to 50 per cent if the changes go ahead.

It would see discounts for the Bishopthorpe Road, East Parade and Rowntree Park car parks and on-street spaces including in Micklegate and The Groves rise from 30 per cent currently.

Discounts would not apply in city centre car parks run by the council.

The proposals follow an Arup review into the impact of parking charges in Bishopthorpe Road, Heworth, Micklegate and The Groves launched following a legal challenge over hikes last year.

Mr Jones said fewer local customers were coming to his shop following the increase to charges which are now £5 an hour for the first two from Sunday to Thursday.

A 10 per cent premium applies Fridays, Saturdays and during events when charges are £5.50 an hour for the first two in central council-run car parks.

The council said the hikes were needed to help tackle congestion by encouraging more people to walk, cycle or use public transport.

But Mr Jones said: "Those we speak to frequently say they now avoid coming into the city because of the high cost of parking.

"Tourists have also told us they intended to spend a full day in York but cut their visit to just a couple of hours because of the cost, some have even said they do not plan to return."

BID data showed year-to-date spending in July 2026 was down by 3.8 per cent compared to 2024 which the council has said is also down to wider economic factors.

The body's chief executive Mr Lowson said they were hoping for updates on a planned citywide Parking Strategy setting out the council's broader approach to the issue.

He added city centre businesses shared the concerns of those in the areas looked at in the parking review but proposed Minster Badge discounts would not apply across York.

The chief executive said: "Eighteen months ago, businesses across York were united in their concern about the substantial increase in parking charges.

"After a lengthy process, the review's findings confirm what traders have consistently told us - higher parking costs are discouraging local people from visiting York's shopping areas.

"The BID Board understands the need to reduce cars in the city centre in the long term, but this needs to be part of a phased approach with parking incentives for local people at off-peak times."

A council spokesperson said they had been legally required to only respond to the specific parts of York addressed by petitioners who triggered the challenge which prompted the Arup review.

They added there was a national trend of lower takings in shopping areas and the council's influence over parking locally was limited, with much of it privately managed.

A council report to Wednesday's executive meeting showed there had been a 2.5 per cent increase in passengers using York's Park & Rides and a 6.5 per cent rise in cycling levels last year.

A wider Parking Strategy is among the issues set to be discussed by the executive on Wednesday.
