A BRADFORD man who was involved in operating a significant Class A drugs line has been jailed for six years.

When officers arrested Abubakr Ahmed, 23, earlier this year he was found to be in possession of thousands of pounds in cash as well as a knuckle duster device capable of discharging an electrical current.

Judge Ahmed Nadim said the prosecution evidence demonstrated that over a period of about 13 weeks the drugs line had sent out thousands of bulk messages advertising the availability of Class A drugs.

“The telecommunications evidence attributed the operation of the drugs line to you,” the judge told Ahmed.

He said further expert evidence indicated that the line was responsible for the supply of substantial quantities of heroin and crack cocaine and generated significant criminal proceeds.

“The police also recovered from your address a knuckle duster capable of discharging an electrical current,” noted the judge.

## Knuckle duster to 'protect drugs'

Ahmed, of Leeds Road, Bradford, claimed he had become involved due to running up his own drugs debt and threats of violence towards himself and his family.

But Judge Nadim heard evidence from Ahmed earlier this week and rejected his account.

“I found you to be an unreliable witness,” the judge told Ahmed.

“I am satisfied that you were in possession of the knuckle duster in connection with you drug dealing activities.

"It was plainly retained to protect both the drugs and the proceeds of your offending.”

## Man jailed for 6 years

Ahmed admitted offences related to the supply and possession with intent to supply of Class A drugs as well as further allegations of possession of a prohibited weapon and possession of criminal property.

Judge Nadim described Ahmed as an immature young man and suggested that his limited drug use and his health difficulties may have contributed to his poor judgement and vulnerability.

“You will appreciate that drug dealing is a pernicious form of offending which causes immense harm to individuals, families and communities,” said the judge.

He said the drugs trade left a trail of misery which extended far beyond those directly involved in the transactions.
