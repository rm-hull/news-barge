Here's everything you need to know ahead of the Junior and Mini Great North Run as it kicks off this weekend.

The event, which takes place annually in Newcastle on the day before the main Great North Run, is expected to see more than 13,000 children participating on Saturday, September 12.

The Newcastle Quayside will be abuzz with children as young as three, along with their families, experiencing the atmosphere of the famous run.

The Mini, for ages 3–8 is a 1.2km dash and the Junior, for ages 9–16 is a 4km challenge.

### Where do I start?

The Junior and Mini Great North Run starts on Sandhill, on Newcastle Quayside.

### Travel Options

Metro: The start, at the bottom of Dean Street, is a short walk (approx. 10 minutes) from Central Station and Gateshead Metro Station. Up to three children under the age of 11 can travel for free on Metro and Shields Ferry services when accompanied by a fare-paying adult, see here for more details.

Walking: If you’re walking from Gateshead there will be access across the Swing Bridge to the start on Sandhill.

Car Parking: Should you need to park close by, there are plenty of car parks and on-street parking options available.

There will be no changing or baggage facilities provided on the day.

Toilets will be available in several locations across the Quayside.

The course is suitable for wheelchair users and there is a dedicated wheelchair start on the 1.2k course.

The event village is located across Guildhall Car Park and Quayside West Car Park and is open from 7am- 6.15pm.

Runners will be sent a T-shirt ahead of the event to run in and finishers will receive a medal.

The Junior and Mini Great North Run has grown in popularity over the years, providing young runners the chance to have their own Great North Run moment.

Adding a special touch to this year's celebrations, stars from the BBC show Gladiators will be present as official race starters.

Alex Gray (Apollo), Emily Steel (Dynamite), and Matty Campbell (Bionic) will be at the start line, ringing the famous starters' bell and cheering on the young participants.

They will be joined by the Great North Run mascot, Tyney the Tortoise.

The Junior and Mini Great North Run is just one event in a weekend packed with running activities across the North East.

The festivities kick off with the Great North Run 5K on Friday and culminate on Sunday with the main event, where 63,000 runners will tackle the renowned 13.1-mile route from Newcastle to South Shields.
