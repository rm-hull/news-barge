Silence fell across a packed street as hundreds gathered to say a final goodbye to PC Matthew Blades.

PC Blades died when a marked Cleveland Police vehicle crashed on the A66 at South Bank at 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

He was one of two officers killed in the crash, alongside PC Tom Clough.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


  Bagpipers lead the funeral cortege.


Just after 11am on September 9, Hutton Avenue in Hartlepool fell silent as the funeral parade for the much-loved officer, husband and father approached St Joseph's Church.

Pictures from the funeral parade show the sheer scale of the turnout, with family, friends, colleagues and members of the public gathering along the street to honour the officer who never returned home from his shift.

  Bagpipers lead the funeral cortege.


A sea of police officers in matching uniforms and white caps were seen walking in formation alongside the procession, paying tribute to their colleague and friend.

Other photos capture a convoy of black vehicles making its way through the packed residential street as mourners stood silently behind barriers.

Bagpipers dressed in traditional kilts led the parade, with the sound of Amazing Grace echoing through the area as the cortege approached the church.

One of the most poignant images shows the black funeral hearse carrying PC Blades' coffin. Resting on top was a floral tribute spelling out the word "Dad", while his white police cap sat above the coffin draped in the Union Jack.

Photographs also show emotional scenes among those gathered, with people wiping away tears, hugging loved ones and bowing their heads as the procession passed.

MP for Redcar & Cleveland Anna Turley said: "It was an honour to attend the funeral of PC Matthew Blades today and pay my respects and gratitude on behalf of the people of Redcar & Cleveland.

"It was such a powerful tribute to a wonderful man - clearly so loved and admired by colleagues and friends in Cleveland Police and beyond.

"But most of all a devoted husband and Dad who was rightly a hero to his sons. My thoughts remain with everyone who loved Matthew as we remember his life and honour his service."
