Hundreds of people stood shoulder to shoulder to say goodbye to an officer killed in the line of duty on the A66.

Just after 11am today (Wednesday, September 9), silence fell over Hutton Avenue as PC Matthew Blades' funeral cortege approached St Joseph's Church in Hartlepool.

Dad-of-two PC Blades died alongisde PC Tom Clough when their marked police car was hit head-on by a VW going the wrong way up the A66 at South Bank at 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


The closed road was packed with mourners, police officers, ambulance workers and members of the public.

Silence fell until bagpipers leading the cortege began playing Amazing Grace.

As the music echoed down the street, mourners wiped away tears, held hands, hugged loved ones and bowed their heads.

Officers, wearing black armbands embroidered with the names of PC Blades and Clough, embraced as they reflected on their colleague's life.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


Resting on top of the hearse was a poignant white floral tribute spelling out the word "Dad", while his white police cap sat proudly above the coffin draped in the Union Jack flag.

Officers in matching white-topped caps marched alongside the cortege, escorting their colleague and friend on his final journey to the church.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


As the bagpipes fell silent, pallbearers gently carried PC Blades' coffin towards St Joseph's Church.

Police officers from across the region stood in formation with medals gleaming in the sunshine.

A convoy of black vehicles followed as family and friends made their way to the church for a private funeral service.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


One resident who came to pay their respects said: "We wanted to show our respects to him and the police force.

"He was a football coach and dad, and we wanted to be here.

"It's been amazing to see so many come out, and I hope it shows what decent people the police force are who deserve to be respected."

Among those in attendance was Tracey, 67, from Hartlepool, who described the scenes as heartbreaking.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


She said: "I came down today to pay my respects to PC Blades and the police force who do such an incredible job of keeping our communities safe.

"It's heartbreaking, but I'm glad I was here for it and what an amazing turnout from everyone."

  Pc Blades, 37, died after a marked Cleveland Police vehicle was involved in a crash on the A66 at South Bank at 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

Redcar and Cleveland MP Anna Turley said: "It was an honour to attend the funeral of PC Matthew Blades today and pay my respects and gratitude on behalf of the people of Redcar and Cleveland.

"It was such a powerful tribute to a wonderful man - clearly so loved and admired by colleagues and friends in Cleveland Police and beyond.

"But most of all a devoted husband and Dad who was rightly a hero to his sons. My thoughts remain with everyone who loved Matthew as we remember his life and honour his service."
