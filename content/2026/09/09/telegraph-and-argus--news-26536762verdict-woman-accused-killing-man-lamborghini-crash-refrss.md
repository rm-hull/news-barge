A Bradford woman has been found guilty of causing the death of her friend in a horror crash involving a rented Lamborghini.

Humaira Batool, of Smiddles Lane, Bankfoot, was driving the hired Urus when it hit a barrier on the M62 and caught fire. Shortly beforehand, it hit speeds of more than 140mph.

The collision, near Ripponden in Calderdale, killed her friend Mohammed Shaan Hussain, aged 20 and from Bradford.

During her trial, the 26-year-old embarked on a "distasteful attempt" to blame Mr Hussain for the excessive speed of the car, suggesting he had meddled with the controls to make the vehicle "surge."

  Humaira Batool who has been found guilty today by a jury at Leeds Crown Court for causing the death by dangerous driving as she drove at speeds up to 143 mph in a Lamborghini supercar killing Shaan Hussain 

*(Image: Ben Lack)*

Despite Batool's denials, West Yorkshire Police always maintained she was responsible for her own actions and must now answer for those actions.

Detective Inspector Paul Conroy, of the Force's Major Collision Enquiry Team, said: “We welcome the verdict of the jury in finding Batool guilty of causing death by dangerous driving today.

"We always maintained she was responsible for her own actions in choosing to drive the car to speeds up to double the speed limit, and she must now answer for the incredibly dangerous driving she demonstrated on that night.

"We now await her sentencing on 2 November.”

Leeds Crown Court had heard how the Lamborghini was driven by Batool "at grossly excessive speeds of up to 143mph in wet conditions" in the early hours of December 21, 2022.

  Shaan Hussain, who was 20 and from Bradford, died after the black Lamborghini Urus in which he was travelling overturned on the westbound carriageway at Outlane between junctions 22 and 23 

*(Image: West Yorks Police)*

Batool, who was aged 22 at the time, was convicted of causing death by dangerous driving.

Seconds after it passed Scott Hall Farm - known as "house in the middle of the motorway" - the supercar smashed into a barrier, overturned and burst into flames.

Drivers who had just been overtaken by the stricken supercar stopped and desperately tried to save Mr Hussain.

But their rescue efforts were thwarted by a fire and an explosion.

Batool was later found on a grass verge near the motorway by a second group of witnesses.

Batool initially told police she had been asleep in the passenger seat and awoke when the car rolled, the court heard.

However, video was shown to the jury of Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights in Bradford, moving around the vehicle and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain.

  Footage shown to the jury shows Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights, moving around the Lamborghini and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain 

*(Image: CPS / West Yorkshire Police / SWNS)*

The court was told that Batool kept "entirely shtum" about the change of driver until confronted with the video footage.

After conceding that she was in fact driving, Batool then put forward a different reason why she was not responsible for the fatal crash.

During the trial, she claimed Mr Hussain triggered a "boost" by tampering with the supercar's controls.

She told jurors that Mr Hussain pressed buttons moments before the 600bhp Lamborghini began to "surge".

Prosecutor Christopher Dunn said Batool had got up in the middle of the night after seeing Mr Hussain in the Lamborghini outside.


He was initially the driver of the powerful supercar, but the pair swapped seats and the court heard she drove at more than twice the speed limit in dark and wet conditions.

The court heard the car had reached speeds of up to 143mph before the fatal crash.

Mr Dunn told the jury: "The defendant's driving fell far below what would be expected of a competent and careful driver."

In terms of the footage showing her getting into the driver's seat, Mr Dunn said: "She kept entirely shtum about that until she was forced to acknowledge it when she was confronted with unequivocal evidence proving that she was the driver at the relevant time."

Batool told jurors Mr Hussain had been handling controls inside the car and said: "Watch this, watch this" before reaching towards the centre console and steering wheel.

She claimed the vehicle repeatedly "boosted" as he "pressed the buttons multiple times", causing her to panic and freeze.

But a Lamborghini expert told the trial there were no buttons, levers or controls which could cause such acceleration.

Mr Dunn told the jury: "You may feel she embarked on a deeply distasteful attempt to put the blame for that grossly excessive speed upon the person who died as a result of her dangerous driving."

Defence barrister Richard Dawson argued the collision may have been a genuine accident for which nobody was criminally responsible.

He said: "Sometimes, accidents happen - accidents in the true sense where no one is criminally to blame."

Mr Dawson said high speed alone would not make a car suddenly leave the road and overturn, suggesting something else must have caused the catastrophic loss of control.

He said: "The prosecution have never been able to provide any crucial detail as to how or why the collision actually occurred. They don't know."

Mr Dawson said Batool did not claim to know exactly what Mr Hussain touched, only that the car surged afterwards.

Mr Hussain had hired the Lamborghini to collect a friend from HMP Leeds and picked up Batool from her Bradford home at about 4am.
