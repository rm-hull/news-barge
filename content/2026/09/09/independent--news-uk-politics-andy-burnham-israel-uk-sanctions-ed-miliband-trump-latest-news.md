Andy Burnham said Britain will continue to “act in support of our values” after Israel retaliated against UK sanctions on goods from illegal settlements in the West Bank.

“The suffering of the Palestinian people is a scar on the conscience of the world,” the prime minister said, adding that “Britain must be willing to lead.”

His statement came after Israel announced a raft of countermeasures, which included the closure of the British consulate in Jerusalem.

Foreign minister Gideon Saar said the Labour government “has left us no choice but to respond to its hostile actions”, as he also declared that 12 British officials, including Jeremy Corbyn, were banned from the country.

Earlier, foreign secretary Ed Miliband accused Israel of “turning a blind eye” to the ethnic cleansing of Palestinians living in the West Bank, who are increasingly under pressure to relocate from settlers.

"The British government agrees that there is ethnic cleansing of Palestinians in areas of the West Bank - perpetrated by settler terrorists.”

In what is the UK’s strongest intervention yet, the foreign secretary said it was for the courts to determine if Israel committed genocide in Gaza.

## UK sanctions must not be 'left as symbolic gestures'

Britain’s sanctions on Israeli settlements must “be enforced with teeth”, a Palestinian advocacy group has said.

The International Centre of Justice for Palestinians said it welcomed the “overdue step” from the government, but urged it to “ensure these measures are robustly enforced.”

“The ban on imports from illegal settlements should have happened years ago,” the group said.

“Measures covering settlement advertising and services such as finance, construction, and infrastructure could help disrupt the expansion and maintenance of the settlement enterprise, but only if they are enforced with teeth, not left as symbolic gestures.

“UK policy must confront enablers, not just the symptoms.”

## Government's sanctions will impact the safety of British Jews, says rabbi

A rabbi from Golders Green has described feeling “very saddened” by the UK government’s sanctions on illegal Israeli settlements, and believes they will have an impact on the safety of British Jews.

Sam Fromson told *The Independent* that the community was “feeling marginalised within British society and threatened,” adding: “I've got friends who are considering whether it is time to leave the country.”

He said: “This isn't a practical imposition on the Israeli government in any way. It's a minuscule amount of goods that are being sanctioned here.

“This is a symbolic political gesture to pander to a prevailing sentiment of a section of Labour voters, and it creates an increased negative environment for UK Jews through this political stunt.”

Mr Fromson said the announcement had made him feel “disconnected” from the British government and more doubtful about a long-term future for Jews in the UK.

“This type of action, this type of political posturing caters to the extremist elements in society and caters to people whose sole platform for political representation in the UK is an anti-semitic message, and this emboldens that deplorable minority.”

## Miliband’s ‘brave’ settlement trade ban has come too late, say West Bank Palestinians

The Independent’s Alex Croft writes:

West Bank Palestinians are welcoming the UK government’s unprecedented ban on trade with the Israeli settlements - but say it is long overdue and doesn’t go far enough.

On Tuesday, UK foreign secretary Ed Miliband confirmed a “new approach” in the UK’s relations with Israel, imposing a ban on goods coming out of Israeli settlements in the West Bank which Britain regards as illegal.

Palestinians in the West Bank praised the “brave” government for a step in the right direction, as residents face growing violence and repression from Israeli settlers and authorities.

“It's too late, but to come late is better than never,” says Basel Adra, filmmaker and director of the Oscar-winning documentary *No Other Land,* which documents life facing settler attacks and home demolitions in the beleaguered area of Masafer Yatta in the southern West Bank.

“It's the right thing, it's the moral thing, but it's too late, and we wish that all the international community, especially in Europe, will take the steps that the UK and this brave minister and prime minister did,” he tells *The Independent*.

## MP banned from Israel says sanction 'will not intimidate me into silence'

Richard Burgon, who was among the 12 people banned from Israel on Tuesday, said the sanction “will not intimidate me into silence”.

The Labour MP wrote on X: “I’ve repeatedly called for sanctions on Israel over its war crimes and ethnic cleansing, including by providing evidence to the International Criminal Court.”

“The decision of the Netanyahu government to sanction me today will not intimidate me into silence.

“I will continue to do what is right, speak out in defence of international law and stand up for justice for the Palestinian people.”

## ICYMI: Jeremy Corbyn and Diane Abbot among 12 British officials banned from Israel

Former Labour leader Jeremy Corbyn and Labour MP Diane Abbott are among the 12 individuals banned from entering Israel in the wake of the British government’s sanctions, the Israeli government has announced.

Earlier, Israel’s foreign minister said 12 publicly elected British officials will be banned from Israel along with others “who are involved in anti-Israeli and anti-Semitic activities”.

Also on the list in MP Zarah Sultana, Fahad Ansari, the director of Riverway to the Sea, MP Naz Shah, MP Hannah Spencer, MP Carla Denyer, MP Sian Berry, MP Ellie Chowns, MP John McDonnell, MP Richard Burgon, and MP Adrian Ramsay.

## UK must now deal with consequences of sanctions, says ex-cabinet secretary

A former cabinet secretary has said it is important the UK now “deals with the consequences” of the sanctions and maintain good relations with Israel.

Mark Sedwill told Sky News that the government must remember “we have an important defence and security relationship that needs to be preserved,” adding: “I hope that behind the scenes there is work going on to maintain that.

The ex-diplomat said: “We've got to look at the US relationship. What's the reaction going to be from Washington? We've had contrasting signals from there: Marco Rubio (US secretary of state) quite measured. The US ambassador in Israel, Mike Huckabee, quite inflammatory - let's see.

“That's the way the Trump administration sometimes works. It's difficult to interpret.”

## Netanyahu spokesman condemns Miliband's 'preposterous' claims

A spokesperson for Benjamin Netanyahu has labelled Ed Miliband’s claims as “preposterous”.

Doron Spielman told Sky News that the foreign secretary had essentially said “Jewish people are no longer allowed to live in Judea.

“There are hundreds of thousands of Jewish people in Judea. Most of them go to work on a daily basis. Their mothers and fathers are children,” he said.

He admitted there twas crime there and said Israel condemned “all crime, whether it's Jewish crime or Arab crime”.

“No one deserves to live under crime, to be threatened or to be intimidated. And therefore if there are Jews who are doing it, we condemn it, we bring them to justice. And if there are Arabs who are doing it, we bring them to justice.”

## 'UK and Israel governments putting politics before national interest'

The British and Israeli governments are more interested in politics than the national interest, the president of the Board of Deputies of British Jews has said.

Phil Rosenberg told Sky News: “I think actually we're stuck as people who care about that bilateral relationship with two governments who are more interested in their political interests than the national interest.

“You’ve seen this kind of tit for tat, and it’s been building for a while.

“I don’t think it serves the cause that we want to see, which is a path to a two-state solution, the cause of peace, de-escalation and the best possible relations we can see between the UK and Israel.”

## Government now face test of enforcing sanctions, says Amnesty

The government’s sanctions on Israel are “a significant change of course”, but must be properly enforced, the chief executive of Amnesty International UK has said.

Kerry Moscogiuri said: “Today’s announcement is hugely welcome. The foreign secretary promised a ‘comprehensive reset’ and today could be the sea change that is needed.

“The UK government has committed to concrete action and recognised Israel’s unlawful occupation and ethnic cleansing.

“Amid ethnic cleansing, genocide and apartheid against Palestinians, today’s measures are a genuine step towards accountability for Israel’s repeated and flagrant violations of international law.

“But today is not the end of the road. The test now is whether these commitments are properly implemented and enforced, and followed by the wider action needed to hold Israel accountable for its crimes and meet the UK’s obligations under international law.”
