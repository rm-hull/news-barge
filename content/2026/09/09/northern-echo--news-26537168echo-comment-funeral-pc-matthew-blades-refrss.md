THE remarkable turnout for PC Matthew Blades’ funeral in Hartlepool shows how the decent, law-abiding majority have great respect and gratitude for him individually and for the police generally.

Flowers came from Australia, France and the US showing how these sentiments are shared, and those members of the public who couldn’t be present personally have donated very generously to the Police Federation fund.

The same respect and gratitude will be shown to PC Blades’ colleague, PC Tom Clough, when he is laid to rest today.

The two officers were doing their duty, attempting to stop crime and to keep the public safe, when they had the horrific misfortune to be hit by a car driven the wrong way on the A66.

Just doing their duty, finishing their shift, serving the public, and never coming home. It is terrible.

But we need people like them, people brave enough to stand up and serve, people who know right from wrong.

While honouring the two officers, we must also salute their force. Cleveland Police has rarely been far from the headlines for decades now and it takes a special kind of person to serve in those communities.

It is a small force and the impact of the two men's loss on their fellow officers must have been immense – especially when they’ve had to turn in for work on an extremely challenging investigation.

The response of the public in the days since the tragedy will hopefully strengthen the force and provide a degree of comfort to the two officers’ families.

And, above all, it is with them that our thoughts rest.

PC Blades was only 37, a young father-of-two who should have had so much time ahead of him with his family. On his coffin were white flowers spelling out “dad” reminding us that although this was a police funeral there was immense personal loss at its heart.
