A church has repaired its roof and cut its carbon footprint after securing a £37,000 grant.

Christ the King Church in Thornaby, built in the 1960s, needed urgent repairs to its roof after 60 years of use led to leaks and damp affecting the church, hall and community café.

The church successfully applied for funding from Enovert Community Trust through the Landfill Communities Fund, allowing the work to go ahead.

Father Pat O’Neill, parish priest, said: "We are extremely grateful to Enovert Community Trust for supporting this important project.

"The roof repairs have addressed a significant maintenance issue and helped to protect a building used for worship, pastoral care and community activities throughout the week.

"Thanks to Enovert’s generosity, Christ the King can continue to provide a safe and welcoming space for local people and community groups in Thornaby."

The church is open daily for religious services, café visitors and activity groups and is also regularly used by Christ the King School and St Patrick’s College.

Angela Haymonds, trust manager of Enovert Community Trust, said: "Christ the King Church is a pillar of Thornaby society, and the Trustees were delighted to be able to support the repairs to its roof."
