NATIONAL Rail said measures have been to support customers affected by the recent air traffic outage.

The rail industry's national passenger information service said there is "flexibility" for ticket holders whose travel plans were disrupted by a technical issue with air traffic control, as reported by The Press.

Affected customers will be able to use their rail tickets to travel at alternative times or claim a fee-free refund.

This move comes in the wake of a major system issue that led to the cancellation of 1,300 flights to and from UK airports on Tuesday, including Leeds Bradford and Manchester.

  


A National Rail spokesperson said: "If you have been affected by a flight cancellation or amendment and have a train ticket dated Tuesday, September 8 or Wednesday, September 9, your original train ticket can be used instead on the new date for which your inbound / outbound flight has been rebooked for."

Those who have already started their journey can use their existing ticket to return home on the same day in the event of a flight cancellation.

Customers are required to present their airline ticket when using their rail ticket at an alternative time or date.

For those who no longer wish to travel, a fee-free refund will be offered within 28 days of the date of travel.

This also applies to abandoned journeys or if a new ticket had to be purchased to travel to or from a different airport station due to a flight diversion.

The National Air Traffic Service (NATS), which provides air traffic control services to 15 UK airports, was hit by the technical problem.

### 
  **'Inevitably, things do go wrong in complex systems'**

Flight cancellation and delays continued into Wednesday, affecting tens of thousands of passengers.

Heathrow Airport had already cancelled 246 flights by 6am on Wednesday, and Gatwick faced 33 cancellations.

The chaos is expected to continue as airlines deal with the knock-on effects.

Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander is scheduled to speak with Martin Rolfe on Wednesday before addressing MPs in the Commons for the second reading of the Civil Aviation Bill.

Speaking to BBC Radio 4’s Today Programme, Mr Rolfe apologised for the issue, stating: “Inevitably, things do go wrong in complex systems.

“We never want them to. We always hope they won’t, but we absolutely plan for them to and make sure we handle them properly.”
