Airports have warned of further disruption on Wednesday after more than 1,000 flights to and from the UK were cancelled due to a “technical issue”.

Tens of thousands of passengers at airports including Heathrow and Gatwick faced travel chaos on Tuesday, with departures severely delayed because planes could not take off safely.

Bristol Airport has told passengers to expect more disruption on Wednesday, while Heathrow has also warned passengers of potential delays.

About four hours after the disruption began, the National Air Traffic Control Service (Nats) said engineers had identified the problem and had fixed it.

However, it was not clear how long it would take for systems to return to normal after the widespread delays and cancellations.

A Nats spokesperson apologised for the delays and advised passengers to check with their airline before travelling to airports.

Ryanair and Wizz Air slammed Nats as “not fit for purpose” and called for urgent reform of the group.

*“* A critical national infrastructure provider should not repeatedly bring the aviation system to a standstill. Nats is simply not fit for purpose in its current form,” a Wizz Air spokesperson said.

## Angry airlines call for Nats chief to resign

Nats’ chief executive officer Martin Rolfe has been under fire from Wizz Air and Ryanair after the latest issue which saw some passengers trapped on planes on runways for hours.

Neal McMahon, Ryanair’s chief operating officer, called on Mr Rolfe to resign, saying: “How many more failures do passengers have to suffer before overpaid failure Nats CEO Martin Rolfe accepts responsibility for his repeated mismanagement and resigns?

“Over three years after Nats’ catastrophic 2023 system meltdown, it’s clear that nothing has changed, with UK passengers once again suffering avoidable ATC (air traffic control) delays of up to eight hours today due a critical Nats ATC system failing.”

The head of Wizz Air UK told Sky News she was "beyond angry". She said "urgent reform" of Nats was needed over "systemic" failures.

Transport secretary Heidi Alexander is set to speak with Mr Rolfe on Wednesday before she addresses MPs in the Commons for the second reading of the Civil Aviation Bill.

## Luton Airport says it is open but flight operations could be distrupted

Luton Airport has said it is “open and operational” following flight disruption at some airports.

“Some flights may still be subject to disruption and passengers are advised to check their flight status directly before travelling to the airport.”

## Wednesday will be 'horrible' for passengers

Travel journalist Simon Calder has warned that Wednesday is going to be “horrible” for passengers as a result of the knock-on effects from yesterday’s mass cancellations.

“Already, British Airways is saying some of its flights are going to be up to nine hours late in and out of Heathrow.

“There are going to be dozens of cancellations in the morning of flights coming into Heathrow because, basically, the flights didn’t go out tonight.”

He added: “Every single one of these tens of thousands of passengers, they’ve got really important reasons for being where they need to be, and it is simply shocking.”

## Nats chief says he takes full responsibility for incident

Nats’ chief executive officer Martin Rolfe said he “always accept(s) full responsibility” when asked if he was considering his position after the incident.

He told Sky News: “I am the CEO of a piece of critical infrastructure, so it is my job to take responsibility for that.

“What I absolutely take responsibility for is keeping people safe and delivering the best service we possibly can on a day like today.

“You will understand my focus is entirely about how we actually resolve this issue, get things back to normal, help our airlines, our airport customers and the travelling public.”

## Nats says system issues 'will take time to recover'

In its latest update, Nats said work is continuing to assist airlines and airports in recovering from yesterday's disruption.

“This has been a very complex recovery and has created difficulties for the whole aviation network, from which it will take time to recover,” it said.

It apologised for the disruptions and urged people to check their flight's status before leaving.

## Flightradar24 says 1,300 flights cancelled

Flight monitoring website Flightradar24 said 1,300 flights had been cancelled to and from UK airports on Tuesday after a system issue hit Nats, which provides air traffic control services to 15 UK airports.

The website added that 177 flights were cancelled early on Wednesday, nearly all of which were at Heathrow Airport.

Ryanair said more than 65,000 of its passengers had been disrupted and it had cancelled more than 50 scheduled flights.

The boss of an air traffic control provider will speak to the Transport Secretary on Wednesday as disruption is set to continue.
