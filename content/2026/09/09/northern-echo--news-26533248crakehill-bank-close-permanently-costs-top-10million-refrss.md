A County Durham road shut for years could close for good as damage worsens and repair costs soar.

Durham County Council is considering closing Crakehill Bank in Hamsterley, shut since 2022 after a major landslip.

The embankment has continued to move, pushing estimated repair costs above £10 million.

Initial repairs were estimated at £1.7 million, but investigations found the ground was even less stable.

The council’s cabinet will decide on September 16 whether to ask magistrates for a permanent closure order.

Tim McGuinness, the council’s cabinet member for rural, farming and transport, said: "Since the landslip was first discovered, we have been meticulous in exploring options to repair Crakehill Bank and we know the community has been hopeful that the road could reopen.

"Sadly, the slippage of the embankment has escalated beyond what we had expected and repairs are no longer viable, especially given that there is no guarantee that further subsidence will not occur.

"This has been a difficult decision and one that has not been made lightly.

"It is not possible to reopen Crakehill Bank and guarantee that the public will be safe or that the road will not slip again.

"Therefore, we are considering proposals to close the road."

A council report says repairs could not guarantee the road’s long-term stability because of the nearby river and difficult groundwater conditions.

If the closure is approved, the council would partly remove the carriageway and carry out other work to stabilise the site.

The existing diversion route would become permanent, with upgraded signs, road markings and maintenance.

The C31 is one of only three roads serving Hamsterley, linking the village with Witton-le-Wear and providing access to local services and businesses.
