**North Yorkshire Council has unveiled plans to invest £18 million** into expanding its temporary accommodation delivery scheme by almost 25 per cent. The revised strategy increases the number of proposed safe housing units from 90 properties to 112 homes countywide, targeting a critical shortage of single-occupancy spaces and smaller properties for couples and single people experiencing homelessness.

The strategic financial move aims to drastically reduce the local authority’s current reliance on high-cost emergency commercial bed and breakfast (B&B) and hotel placements. Council financial models project that once the 112 council-owned properties are fully occupied, the expanded program will save taxpayers up to £2.7 million annually in ongoing emergency accommodation expenditures.

## Skyrocketing Costs and Surging Local Demand

The localized response addresses a severe national housing trend. Government figures from June 2025 revealed that over 132,000 households across England were residing in temporary accommodation—the highest volume documented since records began, showing a 7.6 per cent increase year-over-year alongside rising numbers of dependent children in transient housing situations.

Within North Yorkshire, local demand has spiked exponentially, with total emergency accommodation spending rising by more than 400 per cent in recent years. Historical outlays grew from just over £500,000 during the 2019/20 financial cycle to upwards of £2.1 million by 2022/23. The authority currently possesses access to 191 dedicated temporary accommodation units, though the existing provision is poorly distributed geographically across the county.

## Statements From Council Leadership

**Cllr Simon Myers, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for housing, stated:**

Everyone deserves access to safe, secure and suitable accommodation when they are facing difficult circumstances. The demand for homelessness support and temporary accommodation has increased significantly in recent years, and we are seeing a growing need for smaller one-bedroom homes.

Our work in this area has received national recognition from the Government – and we will not stop there. We have already made strong progress as part of a proactive approach to delivering new temporary accommodation across North Yorkshire. We remain committed to supporting vulnerable households while reducing our reliance on hotel and B&B accommodation, which comes at a significant cost to the taxpayer.

By increasing our delivery programme, we can provide more people with appropriate accommodation as part of a long-term solution, while continuing our commitment to preventing homelessness and rough sleeping.


## Current Work and Executive Decisions

The expanding development initiative forms a core component of the council’s first unified homelessness and rough sleeping strategy, which was formally ratified last year. As part of this ongoing program, the local authority has already brought 20 new temporary homes online, with an additional 40 properties currently undergoing structural improvement and repair works ahead of final occupancy.

The revised £18 million spending framework and the 112-property expansion blueprint are scheduled for formal consideration and debate by members of the council’s executive committee during their next sitting on Tuesday 15 September 2026. The full executive council report can be viewed and downloaded via the North Yorkshire eDemocracy Portal.
