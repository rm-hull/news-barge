Durham County Council has approved plans to turn a family home into a shared HMO, despite "worries" from neighbours about "a series of issues".

The scheme will convert a family home on Newton Drive, Framwellgate Moor, into a four-bedroom house in multiple occupation (HMO).

Approved on Friday, September 4, the application drew objections from neighbours and the City of Durham Trust over parking, noise and living conditions.

The Trust called the parking plans "unsafe" and urged the council to refuse the application.

While a nearby resident warned the change could affect the street, saying: "We are worried about how these changes will affect our day-to-day life and the impact this could cause to our street."

The resident also complained about rubbish and parking, telling the council: "We have already experienced HMO properties in our street, and that caused a series of issues, especially regarding accumulated rubbish and untidiness."

They added that a shared house could mean "higher levels of comings and goings, visitors, and general living noise compared to a single-family dwelling".

Council officers said the proposal would not have an unacceptable impact on neighbours or the wider area.

They said the number of similar properties nearby was below the council’s threshold and that environmental health officers had raised no objections.

The plans were approved with conditions requiring soundproofing, bin and cycle storage, and a maximum occupancy of four residents.
