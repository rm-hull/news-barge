RESIDENTS are demanding that the construction of cycle lanes along a key route to Bradford be paused, over fears that the impact of the build was not “properly considered”.

Roadworks to improve the A652 Bradford Road transport corridor in Dewsbury are expected to last for around 50 weeks, with construction currently underway.

The initial hope was that the build would enhance pedestrian and cyclist safety along the route by introducing shared facilities for cyclists and pedestrians and creating a one-way system along Victoria Road and Rink Street.

Yet just months into construction, both residents and councillors have called for an urgent pause to the project, amid fears that the roadworks have made the new routes “beyond dangerous”, branding the move an “absurd” idea that “won’t work”.

Sharon McNulty, 59, has owned Grilled and Filtered, on Bradford Road, for 22 years – but now, looking out at mounds of tarmac and “absurdly wide roads,” she fears for her business.

  Cafe Owner, Sharon McNulty, 59, has owned 'Grilled and Filtered' for 22 years and fears the road "won't work" in practice


Sharon told the Local Democracy Service: “There is no way trade from the people on the job will cover the costs we are losing, now there is no street parking.

"It’s not what was promised to us at all, and I don’t think it will work.”

She also raised concerns that the cycle lanes were a “waste of time”.

Sharon said: “I have only ever seen two cyclists take this road.

"There is no point making the pavements this wide… All it will do is make the road more unsafe. It’s beyond dangerous now.”

  Roadworks to improve the A652 Bradford Road Transport Corridor began earlier this year


This was echoed by Councillor for Batley East and Group Leader of the Kirklees Independents, Habiban Zaman, who has urged the council to revisit the plans for the road’s construction.

Despite being part of the Labour Group that approved the initial plans, Cllr Zaman warned that she was not in favour of the road to begin with and actively voted against the corridor’s approval.

Now, Cllr Zaman is hoping that the new Reform UK Cabinet and Kirklees Council will listen to the petition.

Cllr Zaman said: “The traffic is chaos, there are parking issues, businesses are complaining – it’s affecting so many people.

“The Kirklees Independents Group are now asking for a pause, a review, and for the council to listen to residents”.

In response, Cllr John Hardie – Cabinet Member for Regeneration, Highways and Transport – explained that there were ongoing discussions between councillors.

Cllr Hardie said: “We are well aware of the situation and I have been discussing it with one of the independent councillors. It is also being discussed at present within the cabinet.”

Kirklees Council were contacted for further comment.
