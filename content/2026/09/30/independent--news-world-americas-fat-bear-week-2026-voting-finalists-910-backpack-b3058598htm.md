Backpack has officially been crowned the chunky champion of Fat Bear Week after an intense competition with Bear 910.

Officials at Alaska’s Katmai National Park and Preserve, where over a dozen brown bears competed to be named the heaviest, announced the winner Tuesday night.

“We have a new fat Bear Champ! Bear 89 nicknamed ‘Backpack,’” park officials wrote on Facebook.

Backpack won the finals with 103,334 votes compared to Bear 910’s 98,253 votes, according to explore.org, whose nature cameras captured the Fat Bear Week contestants.

“Your votes, your posts, your favorite bear campaigns, and your shared love for Katmai’s wild places turned this week into a collective story shaped by thousands of voices,” the park officials said. “It’s a reminder that just like America’s 250th anniversary, traditions endure because people keep them alive.”

![Backpack has officially been crowned the chunky champion of Fat Bear Week after an intense competition with Bear 910](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/1/43/aa7aa92e-5f31-4cca-9713-3395b710100e.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The competition, currently in its 12th year, honors the resilience of the 2,200 brown bears living in the preserve along the Alaska Peninsula, which stretches from the southwest region of the state toward the Aleutian Islands.

Established in 2014, Fat Bear Week is an interactive initiative to educate the public about coastal brown bears, the close relatives of grizzlies.

Chunk — officially recognized as Bear 32 — won last year’s title at an estimated 1,200 pounds. He was knocked out during the first round of this year’s competition by Bucky, who later lost a direct faceoff to Backpack.

A total of 16 bears entered this year's single-elimination, bracket-style tournament to compete for the heavyweight crown.

![The competition, currently in its 12th year, honors the resilience of the 2,200 brown bears living in Alaska’s Katmai National Park and Preserve](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/1/27/b197bac4-9065-4e92-9dd9-2447041c1cb1.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"Fat bears are successful bears," explore.org explains on its website. The animals gorge on the abundant salmon that return to the Brooks River beginning in late June. It is along the river that they build up the vital fat reserves required to survive winter hibernation. To be included in the tournament, bears must frequently visit the river area.

In midsummer, adult male brown bears generally weigh between 600 and 900 pounds. By the time hibernation approaches, following months of feeding on migrating and spawning salmon, large males can weigh significantly more than 1,000 pounds.

Female brown bears are approximately one-third smaller. Bears may consume as many as 30 fish each day during this time of year, working relentlessly to eat a year’s worth of food in approximately six months.

During their winter hibernation, the bears go without eating or drinking and lose a third of their overall body weight.

![Backpack won with 103,334 votes while fellow finalist Bear 910, pictured, received 98,253 votes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/1/57/fe557f9d-d4bf-4915-8c18-8a5ec43f264f.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Contenders in Fat Bear Week also included five bear families, reflecting the record number of families and cubs present at the river this summer.

A mother bear must feed and protect her young while consuming sufficient calories to sustain herself through hibernation. Cubs frequently face the danger of attacks by other bears because of territorial behavior or food scarcity.

Bear 132 and her three spring cubs successfully reached the semifinals, but ultimately lost to Bear 910.

Contestant profiles identify Bear 132 as one of the largest and most experienced mother bears at Brooks River. In mid-July, one of her cubs became separated from the family for several days and roamed the river corridor alone. They were reunited a short time later.
