This is a really good month for everyone who is both a science-fiction fan and a *New Scientist* reader. Not only do we have a new sci-fi novel due from the magazine’s columnist Annalee Newitz (with a slime mould as its protagonist), but new books from New Scientist Book Club alumni Adam Roberts and Claire North to look forward to. And there’s more: Audrey Niffenegger (*The Time Traveler’s Wife*) is returning to her world of causal loops in a sequel to her bestselling 2003 novel, while Becky Chambers is here to bring us some more sci-fi fun. Time to book a few days of holiday to binge through all this…

Fans of *The Long Way to a Small Angry Planet*, rejoice: this month, there’s a new novel out from the mighty Becky Chambers, and it sounds great. Set on the planet Fortune, where a poisonous atmosphere alters the nature of life, we follow the adventures of Cora, a pilot who has a dangerous condition thanks to her bond with an alien animal, Colibri. Then Cora, in need of treatment but unwilling to seek it if it means losing her connection to Colibri, meets a botanist, Signy, who might be able to help her.

I have chortled over Jasper Fforde’s literary detective novels ever since the first, *The Eyre Affair*, starring Thursday Next. The publication of* Dark Reading Matter* marks Next’s eighth and final outing, in which she’s up against some Martians who have broken out of their H.G. Wells novel, as well as problematic Reality Field Distortion experiments. The title, by the way, refers not so much to the hypothesised physical phenomenon but to the “realm where deleted books and unrealised literary ideas end up”.

Audrey Niffenegger, author of *The Time Traveler’s Wife*, has written a sequel to that bestseller, following the life of Alba, the daughter of Claire and Henry. Like her father, Alba is a time traveller, but instead of having one great love like Henry does,* New Scientist*, and I urge you not to miss it. A new novel *New Scientist*’s tech columnist? And a wild-sounding one at that? We are spoiled for sure. The book follows the adventures of Gardenpath, who is a slime mould from an ancient civilization as well as a graduate student in biology. Gardenpath wants to discover something “truly novel in the universe” to obtain the highest possible honour in their exams, so travels to a remote and dangerous planet and discovers … a strange multicellular animal living in a city they call Pompeii. Newitz has written about the research they did for this book in a recent column – you can here to see if it tempts you.

This comes to us from the small literary publisher Fitzcarraldo, a press that punches way above its weight in terms of choosing incredible writers – think of literature Nobel winners Annie Ernaux, Svetlana Alexievich, Olga Tokarczuk, Elfriede Jelinek). *The Floating World* tells of a billionaire called Morel, who is building a refuge for the super-rich on an island in the Aegean. A struggling journalist is asked to come and work there, but – and I imagine you’ll be as unsurprised as I was by this next plot line – discovers Naomi Alderman’s*The Future*, so I’ll definitely be trying this one.
