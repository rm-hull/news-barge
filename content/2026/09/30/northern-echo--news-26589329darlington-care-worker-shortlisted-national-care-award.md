A Darlington care worker is celebrating after her idea for a town-wide Care Home Olympics earned her a shortlist place at one of the UK’s top care awards.

Jemma Redhead, wellbeing lead at Grosvenor Park Care Home, is a finalist for Care Activities Co-ordinator/Facilitator of the Year at the 2026 National Care Awards.

Her nomination follows the second Darlington Care Home Olympics, which brought residents and staff from eight homes to Firthmoor Community Centre.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21513179.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jemma Redhead and a participant *(Image: Supplied)*

The event began last year with five care homes and 30 residents, promoting teamwork and friendly competition.

Ms Redhead said: "I was absolutely thrilled to find out that I’d been shortlisted for a National Care Award.

"One of the reasons I moved into activities was because I wanted more time to sit, listen and really get to know the person behind their care needs."

Ms Redhead said she uses residents’ lives, interests and hobbies to create meaningful experiences.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21513181.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jemma Redhead and a participant *(Image: Supplied)*

She said: "I love finding out about people’s lives, interests and hobbies and then creating experiences around the things that really matter to them.

"It’s also really important to me that residents stay connected with the wider community.

"I’m really proud of how the Care Home Olympics has grown and I’m already looking forward to bringing it back next year and hopefully getting even more Darlington care homes involved."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21513185.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The winners celebrate *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Redhead moved to Darlington at 13 and joined Grosvenor Park nearly five years ago.

She worked in a nursery before becoming a care assistant and later moving into activities.

She moved into activities to spend more time with residents and get to know them beyond their care needs.

Ms Redhead now delivers personalised activities based on each resident’s interests and life story.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21513184.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darlington Care Olympics *(Image: Supplied)*

She builds links with community organisations and helps residents stay connected to Darlington.

Ms Redhead arranged an alternative train journey to the seaside for two wheelchair users who could not join a coach trip.

She discovered one resident’s passion for vintage cars and arranged a visit to a classic car show on her day off.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21513182.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jemma Redhead and a participant *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Redhead recently completed her Level 3 Diploma in Adult Care and won the Resident Wellbeing Award at the Marton Care Awards.

She was also a finalist in this year’s Stars of Social Care Awards.

Angela Hutchinson, home manager at Grosvenor Park Care Home, said: "We’re incredibly proud of Jemma.

"One of the reasons I moved into activities was because I wanted more time to sit, listen and really get to know the person behind their care needs."

"What makes Jemma special is that she doesn’t tend to accept that someone has to miss out.

"If there’s a barrier, she will look for another way of doing things.

"To see an idea she started here in Darlington grow into an annual event involving eight care homes has been fantastic, and being named a National Care Awards finalist is thoroughly deserved recognition for the difference she makes every day."

The National Care Awards winners will be announced at a ceremony at London’s Park Plaza Westminster Bridge on Friday, November 6.

Marton Care Homes runs Grosvenor Park Care Home, which provides short- and long-term residential and dementia care.
