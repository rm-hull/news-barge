American troops are scheduled to exit their final military posts in Iraq on Wednesday, a pullout welcomed as a triumph by Iran and its partners. Their sway runs deep across the country, where over two decades of fighting claimed 4,500 U.S. lives.

Nevertheless, the United States has retained practical power over Iraq's oil revenue since its 2003 invasion. That financial leverage grants Washington immense control over affairs in Baghdad, carrying significant implications for regional dynamics involving Iran.

## How the Federal Reserve Bank of New York controls Iraq’s oil wealth

At the center of the issue is how the Federal Reserve Bank of New York handles Iraq's oil earnings.

Following the 2003 military campaign, the American-led Coalition Provisional Authority (CPA) created the Development Fund for Iraq (DFI). Housed at the New York Fed, this mechanism was established to gather energy income intended for national rebuilding and redevelopment projects.

![Members of the Iraqi security forces, participate in a military parade marking the withdrawal of the US-led coalition from Iraq](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/12/2297304051...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The initiative was also structured to safeguard these assets from legal actions and claims originating from Saddam Hussein's regime.

Formed under an executive directive from then-President George W. Bush, the system has been extended by every president since. The DFI was ultimately converted into a Central Bank of Iraq account at the New York Federal Reserve, where it continues to operate.

## Why Iraq remains financially bound to the U.S. despite pushing for full sovereignty

Generating roughly 90 percent of its total state budget, crude oil is Iraq's primary economic resource, giving Washington considerable influence over the nation's political and financial stability.

When Iraqi leaders requested the withdrawal of American forces in 2020, Washington reportedly threatened to block access to Baghdad's New York Federal Reserve accounts, forcing the government to back down.

![The Federal Reserve Bank of New York building is seen in Manhattan](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/05/29/09/2026-05-19T155853Z_1514850147_RC2ECLAE42LH_RTRMADP_3_USA-FED-PERLI.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Although Iraq has gained greater authority over its financial matters since the initial occupation years, this connection underscores the US's lasting influence on the Iraqi economy, even as the country tries to assert full political independence and national sovereignty.

## Iraq’s secret weapon against Iran

Iraq's financial stability has been secured by the mechanism, which protects public funds, government sources told Reuters anonymously.

According to officials, the framework strengthens international trust in oil revenue oversight, facilitates access to American currency required for commercial trade and imports, and shields state income from external legal claims, creditor demands, and broader economic shocks.

![In his second term, President Donald ​Trump has pursued a ⁠campaign of maximum pressure on Iran](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/30/06/2026-09-29T204321Z_881298470_RC28TNAPOKLU_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The setup stabilizes exchange rates and reinforces faith in the national economy, helping fortify local financial institutions while asserting broader economic control, officials added.

It also gives the government leverage to push back against factions, including Iran-backed groups, that demand fewer restrictions on dollar access. Last year, the U.S. targeted several Iraqi banks and figures with sanctions over allegations of money laundering for Tehran.

## How U.S. dollar curbs are squeezing Iraq's economy

Heavy curbs on U.S. dollar supply led to a parallel, informal market for dollars, creating a price spread between the central bank's official exchange rate and the black-market rate. The ​price difference is essentially a risk premium for dealing outside the formal system. In his second term,

​Trump has pursued a ⁠campaign of maximum pressure on Iran, with Iraq often caught in the crossfire as Tehran has used it as a vital economic lung. The war he launched with Israel against Iran on February 28 added further pressure on Baghdad.

The Federal Reserve Bank of New York continues to hold custody of Iraq's oil earnings.

To supply American dollars, the CBI traditionally relied on dollar auctions, officially known as the foreign currency window. This allowed private banks and exchange firms to bid daily for U.S. currency using Iraqi dinars.

Under heavy U.S. pressure, Iraq officially shuttered this auction framework in early 2025, driven by a broader crackdown targeting the alleged siphoning of dollars to sanctioned parties, particularly Iran.
