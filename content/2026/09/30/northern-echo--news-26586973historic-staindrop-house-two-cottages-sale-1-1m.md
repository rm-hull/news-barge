A Georgian mansion has gone on the market in one of County Durham's most sought-after villages, steeped in more than 1,000 years of history.

Staindrop House, an 18th-century property in the heart of Staindrop, has been extensively renovated while retaining a wealth of period features, including high ceilings, sash windows and elegant reception rooms.

The six-bedroom home offers nearly 7,000 sq ft of space, with formal and informal rooms for family life and entertaining.

Highlights include a bay-windowed drawing room with a fireplace, a vaulted dining room, a games room opening onto a terrace, a morning room with garden access and a large kitchen with a central island and Belfast sink.

The house stands in Staindrop village centre, close to Raby Castle, one of the North East's best-known historic landmarks.

Staindrop lies between Barnard Castle and Darlington and has more than 1,000 years of history.

At its heart stands St Mary's Church, parts of which pre-date the Norman Conquest, while the village retains its traditional medieval layout centred around a large village green.

Raby Castle has towered over the surrounding landscape for centuries.

Once a powerful northern stronghold of the Neville family, the estate was later acquired by Sir Henry Vane the Elder in 1626, with the Vane family maintaining their connection to the castle ever since

St Mary's Church contains surviving Anglo-Saxon masonry, while Norman arcades were added in the 12th century.

The Nevilles' connection with Raby Castle ended after the Rising of the North in 1569, before the estate passed to the Vane family in 1626.
