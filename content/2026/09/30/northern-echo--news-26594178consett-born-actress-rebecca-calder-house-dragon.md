While many film fans will recognise her face from major productions including House of the Dragon, Wrath of Man and The Conjuring: Last Rites, some may not realise actress Rebecca Calder has roots in County Durham.

Born in Consett, Calder has built a career across television, film and theatre, working alongside some of the industry’s biggest names.

Growing up, Calder lived in several countries and was educated in both New Zealand and Australia before returning to England at the age of 19 and later settling in London.

Calder began her professional acting career on stage at London’s Arcola Theatre in 2007 before moving into television, with early roles in Casualty, Primaeval, The Bill and Doctors.

Calder's breakthrough came as her screen career gathered pace through the 2010s.

She appeared in the horror film Altar before earning widespread praise for her lead performance in 2015's Love Me Do.

The role won her Best Actress awards at both the European Independent Film Festival and Cardiff Independent Film Festival.

Since then, she has featured in a string of high-profile productions.

Her credits include Guy Ritchie's King Arthur: Legend of the Sword, the critically acclaimed BBC and HBO drama I May Destroy You, action thriller Wrath of Man starring Jason Statham, Liam Neeson's Memory and the historical film Hilma.

More recently, Calder has become known to fantasy fans through her appearances in HBO's House of the Dragon.

She has also continued to establish herself within the horror genre, starring in Broken Bird and taking on the role of Janet Smurl in The Conjuring: Last Rites.

Despite a career that has taken her across the world and onto major film and television sets, Calder remains one of several performers with strong links to County Durham who have found success on the international stage.

From her beginnings in Consett to roles in globally recognised franchises, Rebecca Calder has quietly built a career that places her among the North East's most successful acting exports
