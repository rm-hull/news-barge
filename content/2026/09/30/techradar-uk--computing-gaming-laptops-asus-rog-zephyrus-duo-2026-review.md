### TechRadar Verdict

We’ve seen dual-screen laptops before, but they’re still something of a rarity, especially in a full-fledged gaming laptop. So it’s impressive what Asus has managed to achieve here, packing two 3K OLED displays (with touchscreen functionality no less), a detachable keyboard, and an RTX 50 series GPU into a form factor that isn’t as cumbersome as you might imagine. It also performs exceptionally well, although battery life is underwhelming at best. It’s a niche prospect for sure, but if you must have two displays in a laptop and don’t want to sacrifice graphical power, the ROG Zephyrus Duo is unmatched.

## Asus ROG Zephyrus Duo (2026): Two-minute review

The ROG Zephyrus Duo is a mighty gaming laptop with an outstanding spec, which includes not one but two 3K OLED touchscreens.

It looks, though, bely its capabilities. There isn’t much branding or brashness here, and the dark colorway keeps things modest. It also lacks the sleekness of some models, with its various jutting edges and angles sullying its contours. The materials don’t seem all that premium, either. However, the ROG Zephyrus Duo is still a solidly made machine, and considering what lurks beneath, it’s surprisingly thin and compact.

The way the dual displays are set up is clever and well engineered, too. The keyboard can be detached to reveal the second screen, and there’s a sturdy stand underneath the unit to prop up both displays. These solutions provide plenty of scope for your preferred setup, and the various hinges operate smoothly while providing plenty of stability.

![Three-quarter view of ASUS ROG Zephyrus Duo 2026 on a desk with pink wall in background](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/V5yJZMW9xXoqELQzHrPQe7-1200-80.jpg)

What’s more, there’s a generous selection of ports on the ROG Zephyrus Duo, matching some of the best gaming laptops on this front. In addition to two Thunderbolt 4 USB-C ports and two USB-A ports, you also get an SD card reader and an HDMI output.

My review unit was the top-spec model with the RTX 5090 GPU, so naturally its gaming performance was superb. It ran *Cyberpunk 2077* between 50-60fps on the highest preset and at full resolution. There was a lot of fan noise and heat, but the former isn’t too disruptive, and the latter is confined to the underside of the unit.

Both displays are as vivid and as sharp as you would expect given their OLED technology and 3K resolution. Their touchscreens are responsive and sufficiently smooth, although they can feel a little grabby at times. They’d be better served by a stylus, but unfortunately you’ll have to provide your own, since the ROG Zephyrus Duo doesn’t come with one.

The keyboard functions well, whether attached to the bottom screen or detached and connected via Bluetooth. The keys have a generous travel and a tactile feel, which makes them great for typing. However, they feel a little cramped when gaming in the WASD position, and it’s a shame the layout isn’t full size, given the space available. The integrated touchpad works well for cursor navigation, thanks to its large surface.

As with most gaming laptops, the battery life of the ROG Zephyrus Duo is a weak point. It didn’t even last four hours when I ran a movie on a continuous loop, a poor result even by the measly standards of your typical gaming laptop.

The ROG Zephyrus Duo certainly demands a large chunk of money, but it's an undeniably high-end device with just about the best performance you could wish for in a portable gaming machine. What’s more, there aren’t many dual-screen laptops built for gaming at this level, so the ROG Zephyrus Duo is a must if that’s a priority for you.

## Asus ROG Zephyrus Duo (2026) review: Price & availability

* **Starts from $4,499.99 / £3,999.99 / AU$8,999**
* **Available now**
* **Incredibly expensive**

Given its almighty spec, it shouldn't come as a surprise that the ROG Zephyrus Duo has a price tag to match. Exact model specifications vary with region, but the starting price is $4,499.99 / £3,999.99 / AU$8,999. For this, you get an RTX 5070 Ti GPU, 32GB of RAM, and 1TB of storage.

If a dual-screen setup is a must for your laptop, you could also look at the Lenovo Yoga Book 9i. It too comes with Bluetooth keyboard, but also comes with a mouse and a stylus, both of which the ROG Zephyrus Duo omits. Additionally, it costs a lot less than ROG Zephyrus Duo, although the big drawback is that it doesn’t have a dedicated GPU, so it isn’t one for heavy gamers.

If you don’t need dual screens, but just want an all-conquering gaming laptop, then the Razer Blade 18 is about the best we’ve tried in this category. We were overawed by its performance, while we also loved its keyboard, display, and form factor. It too has a seriously daunting price, though.

## Asus ROG Zephyrus Duo (2026) review: Design

* **Understated looks**
* **Smart and versatile dual-screen setup**
* **Generous port selection**

The ROG Zephyrus Duo cuts a rather unassuming figure, with its dark gray colorway and lack of branding. On the lid, the diagonal strip that’s become the mark of the Zephyrus line doesn’t add much interest. But the matte finish on the lid is a nice touch.

However, the rest of the unit’s materials don’t look or feel particularly premium. Its form is quite utilitarian, too, lacking the totally flat and flush surfaces of more sleek laptops. Thankfully, though, it feels very solid, and although the top display enclosure is quite thin and has some flex to it, it’s sturdy enough.

The ROG Zephyrus Duo is actually quite a discreet device. There’s no denying its thickness, but when you consider it hides a second screen and a detachable wireless keyboard, as well as featuring up to an RTX 5090 GPU, it’s quite impressive Asus has managed to make it this compact. It’s still expectedly heavy, though.

Two 3K OLED touchscreen displays are the standout feature of the ROG Zephyrus Duo. You can set them up in various ways: by reclining the main display back past 180 degrees so you can prop the unit like a tent, or leave the laptop in the standard position and simply remove the keyboard to use the display underneath. This would be perfect for drawing or handwriting while you keep your eyes on the main display. It’s a shame, though, that the ROG Zephyrus Duo doesn’t come with a stylus despite supporting them, although if you’re an enthusiastic creative you may already have one you can use.

![Close-up of ports on left-hand side of ASUS ROG Zephyrus Duo 2026 with pink wall in background](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/R3jeAYpzTH6RPyzG8Lb4oH-1200-80.jpg)

The hinge offers a smooth pivot and provides plenty of stability, no matter what angle you have the main display at. However, it doesn’t allow for a full 360-degree rotation, so the ROG Zephyrus Duo can’t be used like a tablet — although this wouldn’t exactly be practical, given its size and weight.

Perhaps the best way to make use of the ROG Zephyrus Duo’s dual displays is to unfold the integrated kickstand underneath the unit so that both displays are stacked on top of each other. This stand provides a reassuring stability and, despite how thin it is, it doesn’t feel flimsy.

Switching between dual- and single-screen use is made easy thanks to the removable keyboard. It’s magnetically attached to the bottom screen, so it simply requires a light pry to detach it. At the same time, it’s also very stable when in place; I didn’t experience any wobbling or displacement when using it.

When detached, it connects to the ROG Zephyrus Duo via Bluetooth, which you enable by flicking a switch on the side of the keyboard. It charges when attached to the bottom screen, but you can also charge it using the USB-C port on its side when it’s not.

Its build quality is less impressive than the rest of the ROG Zephyrus Duo, though. The soft outer material feels cheap and is prone to scuff marks and the like, while the board itself is quite flimsy. However, this is a price worth paying for its lightness, and it remains stable while typing.

Given its real estate, the layout is disappointingly compromised. It seems like there’s space to include a number pad or at least more navigation keys. The arrow keys are inconveniently small, too, although the integrated touchpad is a good size at least.

I was also glad to see a number of ports on board the ROG Zephyrus Duo. In addition to two Thunderbolt 4 USB-C ports and two USB-A ports, there’s also a full-sized SD card reader, an HDMI port, and a 3.5mm headset jack. These ports are spread quite evenly across both sides of the unit and are sensibly positioned.

My review unit was equipped with the top-tier RTX 5090, which, combined with the Intel Core Ultra 9 and 64GB of RAM, made for blistering performance. I was getting around 30fps on *Cyberpunk 2077* with the maximum Ray Tracing: Overdrive preset and the maximum 2880 x 1800 resolution selected, with DLSS set to Balanced. This jumped to between 50-60fps when I enabled Frame Generation on top.

As expected, though, fan noise accompanied this performance. Thankfully, it’s not too disruptive, but more troubling is the fact that the fans can whir up sporadically, even when fairly lightweight tasks are being undertaken, such as browsing the web.

Scorching temperature can also afflict the ROG Zephyrus Duo, although mercifully the heat is confined to the underside of the base, with the commonly contactable surfaces, such as the keyboard and bottom screen, remaining perfectly comfortable to touch. Using the kickstand also helps to dissipate some of the heat.

As you would expect, the two 3K OLED displays are a visual treat, with plenty of sharpness, contrast, and vividness. They both have small bezels, which helps them to maximize their respective 16-inch canvases, and they do a reasonable job of keeping reflections at bay thanks to their high brightness levels.

Their touchscreen functionality is also quite good. Although they can be a little grabby at times, they’re smooth enough for the most part. They’re also quick to respond to inputs.

The keyboard is a tactile affair, with low-profile keys that offer more dampening and travel than you might expect, but they're still snappy, making for a fast and satisfying experience. They’re comfortably spaced for typing, although I found gaming in the WASD position a little cramped and awkward. The large touchpad makes general navigation practical, though.

## Asus ROG Zephyrus Duo (2026) review: Battery life

* **Short-lived, even by gaming laptop standards**
* **Quick to charge**

For such a large and feature-packed gaming laptop, it’s no real surprise that the ROG Zephyrus Duo isn’t a particularly enduring machine. However, it drains faster than many other gaming laptops. It lasted just over three and a half hours in our movie playback test, and this was with just the main screen active.

To put this result into context, the Lenovo Legion 7i Gen 10, another 16-inch gaming laptop, lasted almost eight hours in the same test, while the Razer Blade 18, which is 18-inches, lasted closer to eight and a half hours.

Fortunately, the ROG Zephyrus Duo is quick to charge, taking between 60-90 minutes to fully replenish, which is more impressive.

**You want two displays**  
I don’t think I’ve seen a more powerful laptop with dual displays, and their clever versatility is an added bonus.

**You want a high spec**  
With heaps of RAM and storage space, not to mention a top-tier CPU and GPU, there aren’t many portable devices more powerful.

**You’re on a budget**  
With that godlike spec comes an unholy price tag.

**You want a long battery life**  
Not many gaming laptops can keep going all day, but the ROG Zephyrus Duo is particularly short lived.
