Russia plans to raise its defence spending to more than £150bn next year to bolster its war on Ukraine, according to a draft budget due to be submitted to parliament tomorrow.

Total defence spending will amount to £450bn (50 trillion roubles) in the next three years, reported Reuters, as Putin doubles down on his now four-and-a-half year war with Russia's European neighbour.

It comes as Germany identified Ukraine’s air defence shortage as its “weakest point” heading into the winter. German chancellor Friedrich Merz made the remark during a briefing in Berlin, where he said he was speaking personally with several allies to try and secure more supplies.

Sharing his concerns about securing Kyiv, he said: “If Ukraine falls, peace and freedom throughout Europe will be under threat.”

Meanwhile, Russian air strikes killed three, including a child, and injured another seven people in the Ukrainian capital of ⁠Kyiv and the surrounding region early on Wednesday, officials said.

Poland's Air Traffic ⁠Authority said on ⁠Wednesday ​that ⁠airports ⁠in ​Rzeszow ⁠and ‌Lublin temporarily suspended air ‌operations due ‌to ⁠military aviation activity during Russian strikes on ‌Ukraine.

## Ukraine captures 250 Russian soldiers using 'zombie car' operation – report

Ukraine has captured 250 Russian soldiers as prisoners of war using “zombie cars”, reported the *Telegraph* citing a senior defence official.

Brig Gen Andriy Biletsky told the outlet that his troops reclaimed nearly 68 square miles of land in north of the city of Lyman in the military campaign named Operation Vivaldi.

He said that his command turned Soviet-era armoured vehicles into unmanned zombie cars to blew up a heavily fortified position, leading to the surrender of an entire Russian unit.

While vehicles have been used as improvised explosive devices, the new technology allows to steer them to the target in a “suicide” attack, without needing a driver to man it.

“The enemy was deceived and missed the real strike,” said Brig Gen Biletsky, who commands 3rd Army Corps.

The claimed surrender of 250 Russian soldiers is the one highest totals reported in a single operation.

## Russia claims massive overnight strike hit Kyiv communications centre and energy facilities

In Kyiv, Russia said it struck the De Novo ⁠data centre, which it described as a major ​internet ⁠traffic exchange hub supporting ‌the Ukrainian armed forces.

In the wider Kyiv region, Russia said it hit the Kyivska ‌power substation near Nalyvaikivka, the Kyiv ‌hydroelectric power plant in Vyshhorod, the Trypilska thermal power plant and an energy storage facility in Horenychi.

Ukraine is yet to comment on the specific claims but said its emergency services are responding in Kyiv.

## Germany identifies air defence shortages as Ukraine's 'weakest point'

Ukraine’s shortage of air defence missiles is currently its weakest point, the German Chancellor said on Tuesday, adding that Berlin is working with its partners to bolster Kyiv’s capabilities before winter sets in.

Friedrich Merz made the remarks during a briefing in Berlin, reported by DW.

Merz said he was raising the issue and trying to secure fresh supplies for Kyiv in discussions with multiple heads of state.

“At the moment, this is the weakest point. I hope that we will be able to prepare Ukraine for the upcoming winter, including in the field of energy supply,” he said.
