## Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless review: Two-minute review

The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless is (as the name suggests) the second iteration of Corsair's Harpoon RGB Wireless, sporting a redesigned casing and improved internal components.

Back when we reviewed the Harpoon RGB Wireless in 2019, we gave it a well-deserved four and a half stars, citing its excellent value for money, solid ergonomics, and good battery life. If I cast my gaze back seven years to the heady days of the pre-COVID era, I do remember wireless gaming mice being more of a luxury item, and the Harpoon brought this functionality to a wider audience thanks to its extremely competitive pricing.

But times have changed, and it's now far easier to get your hands on a wireless gaming mouse from a household-name PC hardware brand without breaking the bank. Mice like the MSI Versa 300 Wireless 8K, Cooler Master MM311, and Logitech G305 Lightspeed Wireless have made the market a lot more competitive than it was in 2019, so does the Harpoon V2 Wireless earn its place among the best gaming mice?

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/dR5fwUgZuSk5rUXiczMDJX-1200-80.jpg)

For the most part, I'm pleased to say that yes, it certainly does. Considering the price point, this is an exceptionally well-constructed mouse that offers great gaming performance and a decent (though admittedly not expansive) feature set. I do have one particular issue with the Harpoon V2 Wireless, which you've probably already deduced from the headline of this article, but it's far from a dealbreaker.

Let's address that issue right away, then. It's small. Not tiny – I've personally reviewed more ridiculously compact mice than this – but definitely on the smaller side for a gaming mouse. Compared to the Logitech G Pro X2 Superstrike and NZXT Lift Elite that I use regularly at home, it's almost a full centimeter shorter in the body.

This isn't inherently a massive problem, as the ergonomic design of the Harpoon V2 Wireless makes it easy to grip and move around. But as a palm gripper, I found that my fingers rested too far forward in the most comfortable position, prompting me to adopt more of a claw-type grip, which proved a bit uncomfortable in longer sessions. The thumb buttons are also a bit further forward than I would expect; most mice with two thumb buttons will keep the split between them lined up with the back edge of the main mouse button, but they're closer to the front here, which made pressing the back one feel a bit less intuitive than I'd like.

Now, I'm a big guy. I'm 6'4", and the meat paddles I call hands are obviously going to be bigger than what the vast majority of PC gamers are using to manipulate their peripherals. With that in mind, I'm not going to knock Corsair down too hard here – because outside of this personal gripe, the Harpoon V2 Wireless is a generally great gaming mouse. But it bears mentioning, so if you're a similarly club-fisted PC gamer, this won't be the right mouse for you.

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/q7EpGW4dj4wbGDVC6rEnAX-1200-80.jpg)

Now, let's get to the good stuff. The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless has a sleek matte plastic body with two translucent main buttons. Despite its small size, it nonetheless offers a sensible amount of weight with very balanced distribution, preventing any uneven handling for users who regularly lift the mouse clear of the pad. Each side has a textured triradiate patterning that greatly helps with grip, though I do worry that it might easily become dirty with extended use. The shape is asymmetrical, with no left-handed version available – sorry, southpaws.

In terms of buttons, you get the two main pointer buttons, two thumb buttons, a textured rubber scroll wheel with regular click input (no tilt inputs), and an additional button behind the wheel that is set to switch between DPI profiles by default. All of these are remappable using the downloadable Corsair iCue software, but even better, you can achieve whatever hardware tweaking you desire (including adjusting DPI presets, RGB lighting, and custom button macros) via the Corsair Web Hub without needing to install any additional software.

If you'll allow me a moment to go on a brief tangent, I'd like to wax lyrical about the Web Hub for a paragraph. Corsair isn't the only PC hardware manufacturer to have recently embraced the notion of browser-based equipment tweaking, but it's certainly on the cutting edge. Keychron and Royal Kludge offer similar web-app tools, but they're limited to keyboard tweaking only. Corsair's Web Hub offers a broad range of customization options with zero downloads required, and I love it for that.

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/S2TbvHEAgAkhnxLuSPYdAX-1200-80.jpg)

Anyway: back to the Harpoon V2 Wireless. The outer casing feels pleasingly durable, with a good, firm click to the two main buttons, which are rated for 100 million clicks. The thumb buttons are a little spongy, but not to a concerning degree; it's rare to see a gaming mouse with genuinely sturdy thumb buttons, to be honest.

The scroll wheel also has a good, robust click, and a satisfyingly smooth rotation that doesn't feel *too* smooth; I could still get a feel for the individual increments if I slowed down, but scrolling up and down web pages or text documents was nice and swift.

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/UdbYB4xJJ6HwMdnVDN3G2X-1200-80.jpg)

The underside of the mouse also houses the sensor, which is Corsair's own Marksman model and supports up to 26,000 DPI. Yes, sensitivity limits in gaming mice are getting a bit silly these days, but I'm sure there's an esports pro out there somewhere who can actually tell the difference between a 22K and a 26K mouse. I found the sensor to be excellently responsive and accurate during my testing, playing a number of games including *Marathon*,* Warframe*,* Marvel Rivals*, and* Valorant*.

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/E9EeYSLR6aSai8nXPAdNuW-1200-80.jpg)

Battery life is also excellent. Corsair advertises up to 140 hours of use, and while that probably means disabling some extra features like the RGB lighting, I didn't need to charge the Harpoon V2 Wireless even once during the week I spent testing it. As far as battery longevity goes, you won't need to be charging it often, which is great.

It charges via a high-quality braided USB-C to USB-A cable that comes in the box, and naturally you can use it in this wired configuration if that's what takes your fancy. Wireless connection offers two modes: Bluetooth or a 2.4GHz connection via a USB dongle. This sort of dual-mode wireless connectivity is pretty common in gaming mice these days, since the 2.4GHz connection offers lower latency than Bluetooth.

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/qctBKzzFADf3pwfAgfGcCX-1200-80.jpg)

On the topic of that USB dongle: it's concealed in a slot on the underside of the mouse when not in use, which is accessed by completely removing one of the two PTFE skates. I did find that having the skate be removable like this (as opposed to a separate slot for the dongle between the skates, which is commonly seen on other wireless mice) meant that it was a bit more susceptible to debris like dust and fibers getting stuck under the edges. I do have a dog who is a notorious shedder, so users without pets might not find they experience this problem, but it was irritating to repeatedly need to remove hairs and dust from the skate.

Still, that's genuinely a very minor criticism. The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless is an excellent choice of gaming mouse for the majority of users, delivering great performance and a durable, high-quality design at a competitive price point, so if your hands are average-sized or smaller, this mouse is an easy recommendation.

## Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless review: Price & availability

![The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless gaming mouse pictured on a dark marble worktop.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/6TbpM8a9BneKk8vEx8gy4X-1200-80.jpg)

* **$69.99 / £64.99 / (around AU$100)**
* **Available now worldwide**
* **Solid value proposition**

The Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless is slightly more expensive than its predecessor, priced at **$69.99 / £64.99 / (around AU$100)** and available directly from Corsair in the US and UK (Australian buyers will need to use third-party vendors like Amazon).

Despite the minor price hike, I remain strongly inclined to say that the new Harpoon remains very good value for money. Wireless gaming mice aren't the pricey products they once were, but considering the seven years of inflation we've had since the original Harpoon RGB Wireless hit the market, I'd say that price point is highly competitive. Additionally, gaming mice are something you'll often see discounted during sale events like Black Friday, and even a mild price cut would take this mouse into genuine bargain territory.

## Buy it if…

**You want something comfortable**  
With 80g of weight and a sleek, ergonomic chassis featuring grippy side panels, the Harpoon V2 Wireless is comfortable to use for all grip types.

**You want straightforward performance**  
Other gaming mice might offer more bells and whistles, but the Harpoon V2 Wireless brings strong performance at a reasonable price.

## Don't buy it if…

**You want plenty of inputs**  
The Harpoon V2 Wireless only has six programmable buttons, so gamers looking for lots of input options might be disappointed here.

**You've got big hands**  
While it's still usable, PC gamers with particularly large paws might want to avoid this one, since it's very compact.

I replaced my usual mouse, the Logitech G Pro X2 Superstrike, with the Corsair Harpoon V2 Wireless for one full week, using it not only for my daily work at TechRadar but also for personal projects and gaming.

I played a wide range of games, including *Marathon, Warframe, Esoteric Ebb*, and my current addiction,* Slay the Spire 2*. I found that the Harpoon V2 Wireless was responsive and (mostly) comfortable, though lengthier sessions with the claw grip I had to use with this mouse did result in some minor hand cramp – though of course, this won't be a problem for regular claw-grippers, or users with smaller hands.
