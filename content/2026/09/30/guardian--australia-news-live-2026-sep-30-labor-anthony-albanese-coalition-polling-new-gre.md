Alan Kohler will retire from the ABC at the end of this year, capping a three decade-tenure at the national broadcaster.

Kohler joined the ABC in 1995 as a business correspondent and has been the lead finance presenter for the 7pm news since 2002. His trademark signoff – “And that’s finance” – has greeted viewers for years.

He said in a statement:

I love the ABC, always have, even more so after I joined it.

I find it very hard to imagine this wonderful place not being part of my life, but I’m 74 and it’s time to spend more time with my family, including the six grandchildren.

NSW police allege three arrests in mistaken identity shooting of Sydney student ‘hired guns’ linked to organised crime

NSW police are holding a press conference after two men and a teenage boy were charged in relation to the alleged mistaken identity shooting of Sydney student Marco Tapia.

Three people, aged 22, 23 and 16, were arrested in Junee, near Wagga Wagga, yesterday.They were charged with a series of offences: murder, two counts of special aggravated break and enter and committing a serious indictable offence – weapon and possessing an unauthorised prohibited firearm.

Two others were previously arrested in relation to the shooting and charged with murder, boys aged 14 and 16.

Det Supt Joe Doueihi said police will allege they were contracted as “hired guns” by organised crime groups to commit the shooting. He said Tapia was a “completely innocent party” in the shooting and alleged there was an intended target who resided on the same street. The alleged target has links to organised crime groups, Doueihi said.

He went on:

These investigations are complex and take some time to go through the evidence. There are thousands and thousands of hours of CCTV, telecommunication information, download, and reviews of communication devices, forensic evidence, canvassing witness statements.

Once we have the opportunity to go through all of that and put it all together, we were able to identify that there was a strong case [allegedly] implicating these three males in this offence.

Six Australian universities in top 100 in global rankings

The University of Melbourne is the top Australian university in the Times Higher Education rankings this year, one of six local institutions in the top 100.

Melbourne Uni clocked in at No 34 on the rankings, which evaluate teaching environments, research, contributions to industry and innovation and international outlook.

The others who ranked highly were:

The University of Sydney at 51

Monash University at 61

The University of Queensland at 73

UNSW Sydney at 75

Australian National University at 77

The rankings said Australia bucked a larger trend of western universities falling in the rankings, remaining “relatively stable”. A release accompanying the news said:

Although Australia’s overall score declined slightly as a result of falls in research productivity, doctorate-staff ratio, student-staff ratio and research strength, there were improvements in research and institutional income, along with reputation and citation impact.

Overall, the University of Oxford ranked No 1 for the 11th year in a row.

‘Give and take’ relationship with AI giants, Australian Signals Directorate chief says

Abigail Bradshaw, the director general of the Australian Signals Directorate, said Australia is beholden to AI companies that are willing to give the country access to sophisticated models, but added partnerships work “both ways”.

Bradshaw spoke to RN Breakfast this morning after the OpenAI breach of a Medicare website, saying Australia has “unique insights” to offer such companies that are valuable for technology giants. She said:

It’s a give and take here. And I think what’s important also is to look at the content of that apology. It’s not just the headline. The apology is important. We shouldn’t miss that moment.

But actually, look at the content of what OpenAI have set out there. That is not insignificant. They are agreeing actually to sharing, for example, preliminary findings. They have taken a really significant step in slowing down the release of the next model to ensure that guardrails and sufficient safeguards are put in place. They have given an offer of practical support.

And in fact, that is consistent with our experience, with our engagement with not just OpenAI, but the other labs, Anthropic and others, over the last six months … And I think that’s a really important precedent for the way this relationship proceeds into the future.

Australian Signals Directorate chief says OpenAI’s ‘substantive’ steps key to future security after Medicare breach

Abigail Bradshaw, the director general of the Australian Signals Directorate, said yesterday’s apology by OpenAI after a hack of a Medicare website was important, but the “substantive” steps the tech company plans to better safeguard sensitive information is the bigger takeaway.

OpenAI apologised for the breach yesterday and said an executive will front parliament next week to speak about the episode and what will be done to prevent it from happening again.

I think that the message out of this is – this is a demonstration, in fact, about how Australia can participate in developing new norms, new behaviours, and new protocols which are beneficial to Australians but, in fact, are shaping norms which will impact people around the world.

She added the signals directorate had been working to defend Australian data from emerging technology and “fortify” itself.

There has been work and engagement over the last six months utilising – including models that aren’t publicly available – to fortify the sensitive networks of, for example, financial institutions, health institutions, energy providers.

That process enabled us to – with great speed – identify vulnerabilities in code and patch those prior to the rollout of that really important national process.

Wife of Australian detained by Vietnam says she has concerns about his welfare

Chloe Dang, the wife of a missing Vietnamese Australian democracy campaigner, said she hasn’t slept for days after her husband went missing during a trip to Cambodia.

Tran Hiep, 50, was due to return to Bangkok, Thailand, earlier this month and reunite with his wife, but never returned. Dang said the government has since confirmed he has been detained by Vietnamese authorities on what have been described as “fabricated” charges.

Dang said she hasn’t heard anything from her husband, but found out yesterday from Vietnam that he was in the country. She said she does not know where he is being held, or the status of his wellbeing.

“My most concern is his wellbeing as well, because it’s been 12 days and we hadn’t heard anything,” she told ABC News Breakfast. “He’s an Australian citizen. He went into Cambodia legally. And now, the accusation is he tried to pass the border [into Vietnam] illegally.”

She’s asked the Australian government to do everything they can to secure his release, saying officials have been assisting her and her family.

Three charged over mistaken-identity shooting of Sydney student

Two men and a teenage boy have been charged in relation to the alleged mistaken identity shooting of Sydney student MarcoTapia – including the person who allegedly held the gun – police say.

Tapia, 23, was shot inside his family’s home in Glen Alpine early on 1 September. Police have alleged perpetrators had the wrong home and person.

The trio – aged 22, 23 and 16 – were arrested in Junee, near Wagga Wagga, yesterday and charged with a series of offences: murder, two counts of special aggravated break and enter and committing a serious indictable offence – weapon and possessing an unauthorised prohibited firearm.

Detectives will allege in court the three – one allegedly armed with a pistol – entered the home and confronted Tapia, before he was fatally shot.

All three will appear in court today – the teenager will appear in children’s court.

Two other teenage boys have been charged and remain before the courts.

Albanese to announce next stage of Melbourne airport rail link

Benita Kolovos

Early works are beginning on the second stage of the long-awaited train line to Melbourne airport – a 7.6km stretch of the train line from Albion to Keilor East, where a new station will be built.

AnthonyAlbanese will be in Melbourne today with Victorian premier, BenCarroll, to announce that site investigations, procurement and development work will begin in the coming weeks.

Built alongside the Jacana freight corridor, this stage of the project will feature a 500m rail bridge to carry trains over the Maribyrnong River.

Community engagement and early designs will follow next year, with major construction on the Keilor East station to begin in 2028 and finish in 2031.

The first stage of Melbourne Airport Rail, which involves the creation of the “Sunshine superhub” and the construction of new stations at Tottenham and Albion, is expected to be completed in 2030.

Home Affairs orders rapid review of government-wide cyber systems after OpenAI hack

OpenAI’s Medicare breach has prompted a government-wide assessment of cyber systems to bolster Australia’s public sector against further AI threats – deliberate or not.

Home affairs directed all government departments and agencies to undertake a “rapid” stocktake of legacy systems, to formulate risk management for legacy technology and to report their compliance back to the department.

The directive prioritises systems of government significance – the government’s critical systems – with a review due by the end of the year, while other systems will be assessed by March.

The acting home affairs minister, RichardMarles, said AI is “changing the environment in which we operate at extraordinary speed.”

We can’t wait for an old system to fail before replacing it. We need to identify vulnerabilities and deal with them before they can be exploited.
