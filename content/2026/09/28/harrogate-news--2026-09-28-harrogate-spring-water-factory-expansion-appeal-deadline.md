**Harrogate Spring Water** is facing a critical 19-day countdown to salvage its controversial factory expansion plans, with no formal appeal yet lodged against the local authority’s decision.

Under statutory planning regulations, developers are granted a strict six-month window from the date of a decision notice to appeal a refusal to the national Planning Inspectorate. Following North Yorkshire Council’s unanimous rejection of the Harlow Moor Road expansion earlier this year, that drop-dead deadline is officially locked in for **17 October 2026**.

An independent review of active planning inspectorate records confirms that, as of today, the water bottling giant has not submitted any formal appeal documentation or notified the council of an active legal challenge.

### PR agency blames ‘admin error’ for freezing out The Harrogate Informer

The revelation comes as the developer actively coordinates regional media briefings and private factory tours in Harrogate tomorrow morning to deliver a self-described “significant strategic update.”

However, the staging of the closed-door event has already sparked controversy. The *Harrogate Informer* understands that local ward councillors representing the affected community have been entirely left off the guest list for tomorrow’s briefings.

Furthermore, despite our position as a primary independent digital news service for the district, the *Harrogate Informer* was also entirely excluded from the initial scheduling loop. It was only after this publication directly challenged the company and its Newcastle-based PR agency,**Cool Blue**, regarding the blackout that handlers scrambled to offer an interview slot, claiming that our omission was down to an “administrative error.”

### Private briefings scheduled ahead of drop-dead deadline

The timing of the sudden PR push—falling within the final two weeks of the statutory appeal window—has sparked intense speculation among community groups and environmental campaigners over whether managing director Richard Hall will announce a last-minute legal challenge or withdraw the current expansion plans entirely to avoid a costly public inquiry.

The proposed expansion has faced fierce opposition from local residents and conservationists due to the required felling of community-planted woodland at the neighbouring Rotary Wood site. Opponents argue that bypassing a local, unanimous democratic refusal via a national appeal would severely damage the brand’s localised marketing efforts.

If Harrogate Spring Water fails to submit its formal appeal documents before midnight on 17 October 2027, the current refusal will become legally binding, forcing the developer back to square one if they wish to expand the facility in the future.

*The Harrogate Informer has requested an on-the-record virtual interview with Harrogate Spring Water Managing Director Richard Hall tomorrow afternoon.*
