## Princess Royal jokes with Sir Kevin about joining him on a horse

The Princess Royal joked with Sir Kevin Sinfield that she could accompany his running team on a horse as she joined hundreds of supporters in York on the latest stage of his final endurance event to raise money for motor neurone disease charities.

As the pair chatted, Sir Kevin agreed with the royal visitor that the term “fun run” was a “contradiction”.

Anne said: “I can run but I don’t do it for fun. I’m past that stage.”

Sir Kevin said: “Well, you’re welcome to join at any point”, to which the Princess Royal replied: “I’ll stick with horses.”

## Sir Kevin given royal welcome as he arrives in York

If you're just joining us, here's what's been happening today:

* The Princess Royal greeted Sir Kevin Sinfield as the rugby legend arrived in York during his latest endurance challenge for motor neurone disease (MND) charities.
* Anne welcomed Sir Kevin at the University of York this morning on the second day of his 7 in 7: The Grand Finale challenge, which will see him try to complete an ultra-marathon every day for seven days.
* Sir Kevin, who has already raised more than £11 million for MND causes, ran to the university from Shiptonthorpe, near Market Weighton.
* This week's challenge is Sir Kevin's seventh endurance venture inspired by former Leeds Rhinos team-mate Rob Burrow, who died in 2024 after living with MND for four-and-a-half years.

## ‘It was amazing’ - woman after completing mile with Sir Kevin

A woman whose husband with diagnosed with MND last year said it was “amazing” to see the support for Sir Keivn in York.

Hilary Casey, 65, from Scarborough, ran pushing her husband Bob, 63, around the track at the University of York – and they were even joined by Sir Kevin.

“I was chatting to [Sir Kevin] to say my friends were in the crowd and if I cuddled him it would make them really, really jealous. And he gave us a cuddle!” Hilary said.

“It was amazing,” Hilary said after completing the mile with Sir Kevin “It’s lovely – so uplifting. It’s been an amazing day.”

Bob praised Sir Kevin for keeping MND in the spotlight through his challenges, saying: “It was really good to be involved in things like this because it keeps it in the public eye.”

Bob also said that knowing about Rob Burrow’s MND diagnosis meant he had an understanding of the disease when he was diagnosed.

“It made the diagnosis easier to deal with because I knew so much more about the disease,” Bob said.

Hilary added: “People are getting help quicker now because people are understanding what MND is [because of Sir Kevin’s efforts].”

Hilary said she has supported the MND community since running the first Rob Burrow Leeds Marathon in 2023.

She said Bob had been in the crowd watching her complete the race, not knowing that he would be diagnosed with MND two years later.

## Sir Kevin brings ‘hope and love’ for people with MND, says father of man diagnosed with disease

The family of Tom Davies were among those who joined Sir Kevin in running a mile at the University of York during the 7 in 7: The Grand Finale 2026 Challenge.

Tom’s dad, Simon Davies, praised Sir Kevin for the “the hope and love” he brings to people with MND by raising awareness of the disease.

“To make people aware of it is tremendous,” he said.

Tom, from Sheffield, who was diagnosed with MND two years ago aged 29, said he wanted to come to the university to support Sir Kevin.

“I’m happy he puts in so much for effort for people with MND,” Tom said.
