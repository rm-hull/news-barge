## Swiss voters reject proposal to strengthen country’s neutrality

Swiss voters have overwhelmingly rejected a proposal championed by a populist party to strengthen the country’s vaunted neutrality.

Official figures published by the government showed 70 per cent of voters, as well as majorities across all 26 of the country’s regions, turned down the measure.

Supporters of the measure, spearheaded by the Swiss People’s Party, claimed the European country had drawn dangerously close to Nato and the EU, particularly concerning the conflict in Ukraine.

## What comes next for North Korea soldiers transferred from Ukraine to South Korea?

Two North Korean soldiers captured while fighting for Russia against Ukraine are now in South Korea, raising questions ​over how they will be investigated, protected and resettled, and the legal basis for their transfer.

The soldiers are expected to undergo questioning and security screening before the South Korean authorities decide whether to grant them protection and begin the resettlement process.

Seoul's Unification Ministry said on Monday their cases would be reviewed under relevant laws and past practices, suggesting that the North Korean Defectors Protection and Settlement ⁠Support Act would apply.

The investigation and temporary protection may last up to 90 days, with a possible extension in unavoidable circumstances.

The soldiers’ military backgrounds and firsthand knowledge of North Korea’s deployment to Russia could result in more extensive debriefing.

Those granted protection usually receive resettlement training before entering South Korean society.

The unification minister normally determines whether a North Korean arrival qualifies for protection, although the NIS director can make the decision on national security grounds.

Yu told Reuters the sensitivity of the case could also keep them out of public view longer than other North Korean arrivals.

Their physical and ⁠psychological condition is also likely to be assessed.

## Inside Ukraine’s fortress cities – and the ex-convicts keeping them from Putin

Even as Ukraine and Russia intensify mass aerial attacks against each other, the unrelenting slaughter on the battlefields below continues, particularly in Ukraine’s Donetsk region, which Vladimir Putin has been obsessed with seizing since he launched his invasion.

Two large cities, Slovyansk and Kramatorsk, and a number of smaller towns in the region remain in Ukrainian hands and have become known as the “fortress belt”. The area is key to Ukraine’s overall security.
