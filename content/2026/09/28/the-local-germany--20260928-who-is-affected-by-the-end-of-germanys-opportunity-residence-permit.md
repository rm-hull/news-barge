For tens of thousands of long-term residents in Germany living on "tolerated" status, the Opportunity Residence permit (*Chancen-Aufenthaltsrecht)* was supposed to provide a route out of legal limbo and towards permanent residence.

Now, nearly four years after the scheme was introduced, that pathway is drawing to a close.

*(Note that this is a different residence permit to the Opportunity Card, which was designed for skilled immigrants looking to move to Germany for employment.)*

Here's a look at where things stand, how successful the scheme has been and what permit holders should do next.

**New court ruling throws spotlight on the scheme's final chapter**

Some immigrants who applied for Germany's Opportunity Residence permit before the deadline may now find that their application leads nowhere.

A ruling this month by the Higher Administrative Court in the state of Hesse said that applications still awaiting a decision after the scheme expired cannot automatically be granted under the old rules.

That could leave some would-be applicants in legal limbo despite having applied on time. The court argued that lawmakers failed to include protections for pending applications when the scheme ended at the close of 2025.

While the ruling applies directly in Hesse, it raises wider questions for people elsewhere in Germany whose cases have not yet been resolved.

At the same time, many of those who obtained an Opportunity Residence permit are approaching the end of their non-renewable 18-month residence period and face an important deadline to secure a more permanent status.

For anyone in this position, the court ruling comes a reminder that the clock is ticking to secure a more permanent status before their permit runs out.

**What was the Opportunity Residence permit?**

The Opportunity Residence scheme came into force on December 31st, 2022.

The scheme targeted people who had spent years in Germany with a so-called tolerated stay, or *Duldung*. A* Duldung* does not provide a regular right of residence. Instead, it is typically granted when someone is required to leave Germany but cannot currently be deported for practical or legal reasons.

As a result, some people end up spending many years in Germany working, attending school and raising families while still lacking a secure immigration status.

The Opportunity Residence permit aimed to give some of these long-term residents a realistic chance to regularise their status. In practical terms, it meant that people who had lived in Germany for at least five years by October 31st, 2022 – and met certain conditions – could receive an 18-month residence permit.

During that period they could work and build the foundations which would allow them to apply for a longer-term residence status.

**How many people used the scheme?**

By the time the programme closed, around 90,800 people had received an Opportunity Residence permit, according to figures from Germany's Central Register of Foreigners.

Around 34,500 former permit holders had already moved on to a longer-term residence permit by June 2026 according to Mediendienst Integration, achieving the programme's main goal of helping people transition out of tolerated status and onto a more secure legal footing.

Not everyone succeeded. Around 10,000 people who had previously held an Opportunity Residence permit had reverted to tolerated status by the end of 2025.

Even so, the figures suggest the scheme provided a pathway to more permanent residence for tens of thousands of long-term residents who had previously lived in legal uncertainty.

The Opportunity Residence permit was introduced as a temporary measure aimed at addressing the large number of people who had spent years living in Germany on tolerated status.

People who met the conditions could apply until the scheme expired at the end of 2025.

Critics argued that the scheme rewarded people who had spent years in Germany without securing a permanent residence status. Germany's most recent federal coalition agreement – comprised of the conservative Christian Democrats and centre-left Social Democrats – did not include plans to continue or replace the programme.

**A broader shift in Germany's integration policy?**

The end of the Opportunity Residence scheme also comes against a backdrop of wider changes in Germany's approach to integration.

This year, for example, the federal government has moved to limit access to state-funded integration courses and language programmes.

A draft federal budget for 2027 would reduce funding for integration courses from €1.1 billion to €590 million, prompting sharp criticism from education providers and integration organisations.

Meanwhile, access to courses has already been restricted for some groups of immigrants and refugees.

The Opportunity Residence scheme may have come to an end, but people who already hold a permit still need to pay close attention to their own deadlines.

Under Section 104c, each individual Opportunity Residence permit is valid for 18 months and can’t be renewed.

In most cases, permit holders are expected to transition to a residence permit under either Section 25a or Section 25b of the Residence Act before their current permit expires.

According to guidance from Berlin's immigration authorities, applicants should apply for a follow-up permit well before their Section 104c permit runs out – ideally four to six weeks in advance.

The exact requirements vary depending on the route being pursued, but generally include a combination of German language skills, identity verification and evidence of integration, while some routes also require applicants to be largely financially self-sufficient.

Those who do not qualify for a follow-up residence permit risk falling back into tolerated status (*Duldung*) once their 18-month Opportunity Residence permit expires.
