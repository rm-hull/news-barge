Petrol prices increased by more than 10 cents a litre last week to reach a national average of 237.5c/L, as global oil supply disruptions intensify.

The price of diesel, which is being squeezed by a shortage of refining capacity and high demand from commercial users, rose more than 13c to 286.7c/L, according to the Australian Institute of Petroleum.

Economists have linked the recent price rises in crude oil, which affects petrol prices, to the enduring conflict between the US and Israel against Iran, and recent Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries.

Elevated fuel prices are a major household expense and inflation trigger given they drive up costs across goods and services in the economy, pushing central banks, including in Australia, to consider rate hikes to keep a lid on inflation.

‘Not all good news’ amid whale conservation anniversary

Forty years on from an international moratorium on commercial whaling, the global community is meeting to consider new, unforeseen threats, AAP reports.

The International Whaling Commission’s 70th meeting began on Monday at Hobart, with delegates from some 63 member nations in attendance. The commission was founded in 1946, at a time when whale populations had been decimated by decades of unregulated industrial whaling.

While populations have in many cases rebounded – particularly since a 1986 moratorium on commercial whaling – whales remain under threat globally.

“It’s not all good news,” environment minister Murray Watt told delegates.

A humpback whale breaching. Photograph: Pacific Whale Foundation/Cover Images/ASSOCIATED PRESS

Among the main issues to be debated at the week-long meeting is a quarter-century old push for a whale sanctuary in the southern Atlantic ocean.

The proposal, which was first raised by Brazil in 1998, would extend an existing protected area in the Southern Ocean, protecting whales from West Africa to South America.

But pro-whaling nations have refused to back the plan, with some walking out of previous meetings to scupper votes.

Whale carcass prompts shark warning at Perth beach

Perth’s popular City Beach is closed after a whale carcass washed up this morning amid concerns it could attract sharks.

Surf Live Saving WA reported the carcass around 7.30am this morning Perth time. Officials said sharks may be present along the WA coast throughout the year, but people should exercise additional caution at this time.

Officers from the WA Department of Primary Industries and Regional Development and other agencies are monitoring the situation. The Town of Cambridge is working to remove the carcass.

SharkSmart, which monitors shark activity, has issued shark advice for the City Beach area due to the carcass.

Cafes and shoppers at a crossroads as surcharge ban looms

A ban on card surcharges is supposed to deliver savings for Australian shoppers, but the reality could be quite different if retailers raise prices and the banks claw back losses by axing rewards.

AAP reports that the reform, driven by the Reserve Bank of Australia, comes into effect from Thursday, when anyone paying with a debit or credit card will be spared the surprise of extra charges at the till.

Under the new rules, retailers must either absorb the cost or build it into their prices.

They could also offer a discount for cash or payment services such as PayID, which some experts think could lead to savings of up to 10% off the transaction price.

However, dealing in cash means keeping a float and taking it to the bank at the end of the day, which can be difficult given the general decline in branches across the country, and does lay a burden on small business owners.

For those sticking to tap and go, there’s a good chance some businesses will raise prices to offset the merchant fees they still pay to the banks and payment providers to process those payments, which they used to recoup under surcharges.

Paramedics to take the lead in Victoria mental health call outs

Paramedics will take the lead in mental health emergency call outs – not police – under a new agreement announced by Victoria’s health minister, Ingrid Stitt, this morning.

Under the new mental health crisis response protocols, signed by Ambulance Victoria, Victoria police and the health department, these call outs will be treated as a “health-led” response rather than a law-and-order matter.

Victoria’s mental health royal commission recommended in 2021 that paramedics become first responders to Triple Zero calls for mental health crises. In May, the New South Wales government accepted a similar recomendation by both a parliamentary inquiry and an internal police review.

Under the Victorian agreement, police and protective service officers will attend mental health call outs to support paramedics when needed rather than leading the initial response.

People in mental health crisis will also be transferred to hospitals by family members, mental health services, non-emergency patient transport or ambulances, rather than in the back of a police car when it is safe to do so, under the new arrangements.

Stitt said:

When someone is experiencing a mental health crisis, they deserve a response that puts their health and wellbeing first while keeping them and the community safe.

Mike Baird to step down as chair of Cricket Australia

Former NSW premier Mike Baird will step down as the chair of Cricket Australia at the body’s next annual general meeting in October.

Baird has been chair since early 2023 and led Cricket Australia’s push to privatize the Big Bash Leagues clubs. Both NSW and Queensland have opposed the plan.

In a statement, Baird said:

I believe Australian cricket continues to be well led by the CA Board. Their mix of significant experience and passion for the game gives me great confidence for the future. …

I have been incredibly proud to play a role in the oversight of cricket on the CNSW Board and, over the past six years, the CA Board, including almost four as chair.

David Boon, a former Test batter, will take over as interim chair on 28 October as a process to find a new full-time chair begins.
