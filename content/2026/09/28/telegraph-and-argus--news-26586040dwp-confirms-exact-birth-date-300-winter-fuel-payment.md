The Department for Work and Pensions has confirmed the exact rules for the 2026/27 Winter Fuel Payment, with your date of birth and circumstances during September 21 to September 27 determining whether you qualify.

Anyone born on or before June 27, 1960 could be eligible for the payment, which is worth between £100 and £300 towards heating bills.

The seven-day period from September 21 to September 27 was the official qualifying week for this winter's payment.

That means the date is particularly important for anyone close to the State Pension age threshold.

If you were born after June 27, 1960, you will not qualify for the Winter Fuel Payment for winter 2026/27 under the current rules.

For those who do qualify, the amount they receive will also depend on their age and household circumstances during the qualifying week.

## The birth date that matters

The DWP's cut-off is clear: you need to have been born on or before June 27, 1960.

The qualifying week has now ended, meaning the Government has the snapshot it needs to determine eligibility for this winter.

For people who live alone, or who live with someone who is not eligible for the payment, the basic rates are:

* £200 if you were born between September 28, 1946 and June 27, 1960

£300 if you were born before September 28, 1946.

The calculation becomes more complicated for couples and people receiving certain benefits.

## Why September 21 to 27 matters

The qualifying week is not simply a date by which you have to reach a certain age.

The DWP uses your circumstances during September 21 to September 27, 2026 when working out whether you qualify and how much you should receive.

This can include your living arrangements and whether you receive certain benefits.

There are also circumstances that can rule someone out, including normally living outside England, Wales or Northern Ireland, being in prison for the whole qualifying week or being in hospital receiving free treatment for the whole week and the preceding year.

## When will the Winter Fuel Payment money arrive?

Most eligible people will not need to make a claim.

The DWP says letters will be sent in October or November, telling people how much they will receive.

Most payments will then be made automatically in November or December 2026.

Anyone who thinks they qualify but does not receive a letter or payment should check whether they need to make a claim.

## The £35,000 rule

There is another important detail pensioners should not overlook.

People with total individual income above £35,000 can still receive the Winter Fuel Payment, but HMRC will reclaim it.

The Government says your partner's income does not count towards your individual £35,000 threshold. HMRC can recover the payment by changing your tax code or through Self Assessment.

So while the qualifying week has now passed, pensioners should not assume that receiving a Winter Fuel Payment necessarily means they will ultimately keep it.

With winter approaching and household energy costs still putting pressure on budgets, knowing exactly which rules apply could make a significant difference to how much money is available to cover heating bills.
