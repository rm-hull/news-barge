Created by Steven Antin and adapted from his 2010 film of the same name, Burlesque The Musical takes audiences deep into a glittering underground world.

Starring Corrie favourite Faye Brookes as Tess, Gracie O'Brien as Ali and Ryan Carter as the charismatic MC Sean, the production - which I saw at Bradford's Alhambra Theatre - delivers a visually spectacular night of entertainment that feels less like watching a musical and more like stepping inside the club itself.

Going into Burlesque, I already had a soft spot for the 2010 film, so I was really curious to see how it would translate to the stage.

One of the show's greatest triumphs is undoubtedly its costumes. From the dazzling Dirty Martini numbers to Ali’s lightning-fast quick changes and Tess’ seemingly endless parade of breathtaking outfits, every detail contributes to the illusion that the audience has genuinely entered a world of burlesque. The performances are directed straight at the crowd, creating an immersive atmosphere that gives every routine an extra spark. This is driven particularly well by Ryan Carter’s engaging role as Sean, whose role as MC helps blur the line between stage and audience.

I also particularly admired the regular cabaret-style interludes a bright and authentic addition to the world of Burlesque. Similarly, they were not only entertaining but also serve a clever practical purpose, allowing seamless set changes and technical transitions to take place behind the scenes without disrupting the momentum.

While the production largely succeeds in expanding the story, not every addition lands quite as effectively. The romance between Jack and Ali feels overdeveloped compared to its role in the film. While fans of the original may appreciate its inclusion, I found the relationship occasionally pulled focus away from the more compelling dynamic at the heart of the show.

By contrast, I loved the decision to introduce a storyline suggesting a deeper family connection between Tess and Ali is inspired. It added so much emotional weight to their relationship and supported the bond that forms between them. Although some elements stretch believability, particularly the idea that Tess would have had little involvement in Ali’s life during her time at the club, the emotional payoff is strong enough to make the storyline one of the production’s highlights.

The cast deserve enormous praise. Faye Brookes brings warmth, authority and vulnerability to Tess, while Gracie O’Brien impresses in her professional debut as Ali, carrying the role with confidence and charisma. Ultimately, the combination of a star studded score, glittering spectacle, and thrilling vocals made me want to watch Burlesque all over again.
