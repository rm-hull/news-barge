# Police given special powers in Leeds after 'man seen with machete'

## This is in effect for an initial 24 hours

Police have been given special stop and search powers in two Leeds areas after a machete "incident".

Senior officers at Leeds District have authorised the use of Section 60 of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 in Chapeltown and Harehills. The order is intended to detect and deter the carrying of offensive weapons and to prevent serious violence.

It follows an incident earlier today (Monday, September 28) in which a man was seen with a machete on Leopold Street.

West Yorkshire Police said: "Increased stop and search powers have been put in place temporarily in Chapeltown and Harehills to prevent serious violence and keep communities safe following an incident earlier this afternoon."

The authorisation came into effect at 4pm for an initial 24 hours. Whether it continues will depend on regular reviews.

The order lets officers search people or vehicles for offensive weapons or dangerous instruments without needing their usual grounds to suspect each person searched. It covers an area shown on a map released by the force.

Officers are increasing their patrols and presence in the area to deter any further incidents and reassure the community.

A police spokesperson added: "Maintaining the trust and confidence of the community is vital and we are continuing to liaise with key community representatives, including the district's Independent Advisory Group, to ensure appropriate scrutiny and accountability around the use of this additional power."
