Two friends from County Durham have set off on an extraordinary walking challenge in a bid to raise money and awareness for men's mental health

Brad Whitmore, 30, from Crook, and Jack Stead, 24, from Durham, are walking 277 miles from Barnard Castle to Loch Ness to raise money for Mind.

The friends aim to raise £1,500 for Mind, with a focus on men’s mental health.

The friends, who have known each other for six years, began their hike from Stang Lane, south of Barnard Castle, on September 1. They plan to cross the North Pennines and Northumberland National Park before travelling through Edinburgh, Dunfermline and Perth, then around the Cairngorms to Loch Ness.

The walk began as a “mental health journey” before the pair decided to raise money for Mind and awareness of men’s mental health.

Brad said connecting with nature helps him relieve stress and urged people to “take time out of things they're doing, get out of the rat race a little bit and just enjoy themselves, take in the scenery".

  The route takes the friends through some of the North's most striking landscapes 

*(Image: BRAD WHITMORE)*

He said: “It is really good, even if you're not in a terribly bad place with your mental health, and you just need a little change.”

The pair initially aimed to reach Loch Ness within a month by walking 10 to 15 miles a day.

After more than a week on the trail, Brad said the pair had decided to take the walk at their own pace rather than race to Loch Ness.

“I think originally we were just thinking how fast we could do it, but as we've come through, we've decided to go a little bit slower and just take in the sights,” he said.

He said the walk mirrors the ups and downs of a mental-health journey and has taught them to appreciate the beauty around them.

  Some of the dramatic countryside views enjoyed by the pair on their walk north 

*(Image: BRAD WHITMORE)*

“It’s also something that's good for people who are struggling with mental health, instead of just rushing through it and steaming through,” he added.

“It might take us a little bit longer than what we'd anticipated, but I think it's because we want to enjoy the scenery and the spots.”

Despite the changeable weather, the pair are enjoying the scenery, clearing litter from popular spots and sharing their journey on social media to encourage others to do the same.

Brad said that one campsite last week was a “local spot for the young kids, all full of rubbish and cans and everything".

He added: "We took a picture and then cleaned it up, took another picture, just to get it tidied up. We like to leave things tidy and pack it all up away."

Brad and Jack have enjoyed the walk so much that they are considering returning on foot if they raise enough money, taking the total distance beyond 550 miles.
