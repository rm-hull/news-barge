A North-East woman who says yoga transformed her life has taken the next step in her journey by opening her own studio in Darlington.

Rachel Tomlin welcomed her first clients to Sanctuary Yoga on Coniscliffe Road last week after years of teaching in community venues across the region.

The studio offers Hatha yoga for all ages and abilities, with Rachel aiming to make newcomers feel as welcome as experienced practitioners.

  The entrance to Sanctuary Yoga on Coniscliffe Road, Darlington 

*(Image: KENZIE HARRISON)*

She describes her classes as a “middle ground” between slower yoga styles and intense fitness sessions, aimed at improving wellbeing and helping people escape everyday pressures.

Rachel said: "We don't rush through anything. I do try and weave a little bit of philosophy through, not too much, though, because I don't want to scare people off."

"People come to my workshops to get a little bit more of that."

The opening marks a major change for Rachel, who until this year combined yoga teaching with a 30-hour-a-week NHS job.

After accepting voluntary redundancy, she decided to dedicate herself full-time to the practice that she says has had a profound impact on her own life.

An NHS colleague introduced Rachel to yoga at a workshop in the early 2000s, but having her first child meant she could not practise regularly.

After a series of stressful events and her mother’s death in 2016, Rachel struggled with her health and wellbeing.

Encouraged by a close friend, she returned to both the gym and yoga and quickly found herself drawn back to the practice.

"I was hooked," she said.

A later Lake District workshop deepened her interest in yoga’s philosophy and techniques, inspiring her to train as an instructor and qualify in 2019.

Since then, she has spent four years building a client base through classes held in village halls and community centres across the region.

  Inside Sanctuary Yoga, which opened on Coniscliffe Road in Darlington this month 

*(Image: KENZIE HARRISON)*

Rachel said yoga transformed her self-confidence and self-esteem.

"I noticed that I was really hard on myself. I was my own worst critic and I was completely getting in my own way," she said.

"From practising yoga, I started to recognise that and it took a long time, but I managed to turn that around."

She links much of that growth to the yogic principle of Ahimsa, a Sanskrit term often translated as non-violence.

"We don't go around punching each other, we don't do that, but it's actually about the violence we cause ourselves," she said.

"It's if you walk into a room and you go, 'stupid woman, you've forgotten what it is that you were looking for'. It's things like that."

Alongside improving fitness, flexibility and strength, Rachel said many of her clients have reported benefits including greater mental clarity, increased patience and a reduced reaction to stressful situations.

While her current timetable in Darlington is still taking shape, she hopes eventually to offer two classes a day from Tuesday to Friday, alongside monthly Saturday sessions and seasonal workshops.

Looking ahead, Rachel said she would also like to introduce online classes once the studio is fully established.

The studio’s monthly themes follow the seasons, with September focused on slowing down after summer’s intensity.

Rachel said: "We've had the high energy of the busyness, the holidays, the lack of routine, the heat and lots of light, and now it's just time to start finding a little bit of slowness, a bit of steadiness.

"We might have got knocked off track somewhere, which is fine. That's what it's for, to just try and bring ourselves back."

Many clients are women with grown-up children seeking a new focus, but Rachel stresses that her classes are open to everyone.

"My target audience is predominantly women aged 40 to 60 or 70, their children have probably grown a bit.

"They're probably getting to that point in their career where they've given it 100 per cent and are feeling quite dissatisfied," she said.

"But what I would really like to see is more men doing yoga. I do have a few men that come and they love it."

More information about Sanctuary Yoga is available at santcuaryyogadarlington.co.uk.
