Today’s Object of the Week is a collection of artworks inspired by some evocative historic landmarks, offering a fresh perspective on places that have captured imaginations for centuries.

Few places in the North of England combine beauty, history and atmosphere quite like the ruined abbeys that lie scattered across the North Yorkshire landscape.

Set in secluded valleys and rolling countryside, the ancient abbeys have attracted visitors, historians and artists for generations.

They have inspired countless paintings, sketches and photographs, each seeking to capture something of their enduring character.

Now, a new collection of artworks is providing an opportunity to see these familiar landmarks through a different lens.

  Artist Simon Crawford, whose new exhibition explores Yorkshire's historic Cistercian abbeys 

*(Image: RYEDALE FOLK MUSEUM)*

Simon Crawford’s Shining Lights of the North exhibition features paintings and sketches on display at Ryedale Folk Museum.

The works are inspired by three of North Yorkshire’s best-known monastic ruins - Rievaulx Abbey, Byland Abbey and Fountains Abbey.

All three were founded by the Cistercians, a religious order that came to England in the 12th century.

Their monasteries became centres of worship, learning and farming and played a major role in the medieval economy.

Henry VIII dissolved the abbeys in the 16th century, but their ruins remain among the region’s most recognisable landmarks.

  Rievaulx Abbey, reimagined by artist Simon Crawford as part of Shining Lights of the North 

*(Image: RYEDALE FOLK MUSEUM)*

Rievaulx Abbey, near Helmsley, was the first Cistercian monastery established in the north of England and grew to become one of the wealthiest abbeys in the country.

Byland Abbey was famed for its ambitious architecture, while Fountains Abbey remains one of Britain’s largest and best-preserved monastic ruins.

Over the past year, Mr Crawford has immersed himself in these historic landscapes, following the route known as the Shining Lights of the North Way.

The walking trail links the three abbeys and reflects journeys once made by the monks who travelled between them centuries ago.

  Simon Crawford's painting of Fountains Abbey explores the site's history and landscape settin 

*(Image: RYEDALE FOLK MUSEUM)*

Instead of focusing only on the buildings, Crawford captures the changing moods of the surrounding landscapes.

By returning throughout the seasons, he has recorded how light, weather and atmosphere transform familiar scenes, producing a series of works that reflect both the passage of time and the enduring appeal of the locations.

The exhibition also acknowledges a much longer artistic tradition.

North Yorkshire’s abbey ruins have been sketched and painted by generations of artists, including celebrated names such as JMW Turner, John Sell Cotman and Thomas Girtin.

Their dramatic settings, coupled with the sense of history embedded in the stones, have long proved irresistible subjects.

  Byland Abbey is among the historic landmarks featured in Simon Crawford's latest exhibition 

*(Image: RYEDALE FOLK MUSEUM)*

Jennifer Smith, director of Ryedale Folk Museum, said: "Simon’s work gives visitors the opportunity to look again at these extraordinary places.

“The exhibition really connects the present-day landscape with its history and stories across centuries.”

For visitors, the exhibition presents a chance not only to admire the paintings but also to reflect on the remarkable history of the abbeys themselves.

The exhibition runs at the Art Gallery at Ryedale Folk Museum in Hutton-le-Hole until Sunday, November 1.

Entry is free, the exhibition is outside the museum’s paid area, and it is open daily except Fridays.
