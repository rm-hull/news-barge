EFFORTS to bring an empty York supermarket building back into use have taken a step forward amid fears over the plans.

An application to install two signs featuring Greggs branding on the eastern end of the former Iceland, in Fulford Road, has been approved by City of York Council.

Council planning officers stated the advertisements would not adversely affect the appearance of the empty supermarket, neighbouring buildings or the wider area.

But a related application for the full conversion into an outlet of the bakery chain has sparked objections over noise, traffic and its location close to local schools.

The council is yet to rule on the application to change the front of Unit 2C of the building and install equipment including air conditioning and extractors.

It comes after both plans were lodged in June.

They follow the council's refusal of plans to convert the whole building into a McDonald's drive-thru in October 2024.

Council planning officers ruled it would be too close to local schools and would undermine efforts to promote healthy lifestyles.

The McDonald's plans sparked a backlash from locals who claimed it would cause noise, odour and traffic problems.

A separate application from Dalian Properties Ltd to divide the empty Iceland into three separate units was approved in April.

The proposed Greggs would open from 6am to 7pm from Monday to Saturday and from 8am to 6pm on Sundays.

In the plans which are currently awaiting a decision, the company stated it would see part of the building brought back into use following Iceland's closure in 2023.

Plans said: "Investment into this part of York should be welcomed, particularly where visual improvements to the area are to be made, enhancing and improving the vitality and viability of this existing, minor retail area.

"The subdivision of the site allows for the previously developed, brownfield land to be brought back into use, whilst also bringing additional, employment opportunities to the area.

"With respect to highway safety, it is not considered that the proposal raises any issues relating to those either travelling on foot or in vehicles adjacent or around the site."

But six objections have been lodged to those plans, including from Labour Fishergate ward councillor Conrad Whitcroft, over concerns about its 6am opening time and noise from the bakery.

Cllr Whitcroft said he had been contacted by a number of local residents about the plans.

He added that while opposition was more muted than that sparked by plans for the McDonald's drive-thru, legitimate concerns remained over opening hours, noise, traffic and other issues.

Cllr Whitcroft said: "Some residents have highlighted previous experiences of early morning noise associated with the site and are concerned about the potential for further disturbance to nearby households.

"Comparisons have also been drawn with the public debate surrounding the former McDonald's proposals, residents have asked whether a bakery and caf� use may similarly attract significant patronage from school-aged children given the site's location."

One objection was lodged against the approved plans for the Greggs signs over light pollution which could affect wildlife.

Council planning officers stated the building had already been used commercially.

Officers stated: "The signs will be set back from the site boundary and there is ample separation from ground level the highway.

"The illumination will remain static and conditioned for lights to be angled to prevent glare, reflection, or distraction to highway users and illuminated during opening hours only."
