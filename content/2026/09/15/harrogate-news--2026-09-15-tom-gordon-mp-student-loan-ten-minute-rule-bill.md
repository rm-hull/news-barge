**WESTMINSTER** — Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon is spearheading a legislative challenge against the Treasury, introducing a fresh parliamentary mandate to force a total structural review of the national student finance framework.

The Liberal Democrat representative will officially present his specialized Ten-Minute Rule Bill on the floor of the House of Commons on **Tuesday 15 September 2026**. The legislative bid follows a cross-party briefing session held within Portcullis House on** Monday 14 September 2026**, which drew coordinated support from multi-agency campaign networks including RethinkRepayment, the Young Liberals, and the Good Growth Foundation.

The draft bill takes direct aim at the financial terms governing “Plan 2” higher education loans. National debt tracking logs confirm that an estimated **5.4 million graduates** across the United Kingdom remain actively impacted by successive ministerial directives to freeze salary repayment thresholds, a policy mechanism that critics state operates as a retroactive contractual penalty on historical student cohorts.

## Confronting the Treasury Framework

### The Plan 2 Repayment Controversy Explained

Introduced for university cohorts entering higher education between 2012 and 2022, Plan 2 student loans were originally marketed under the statutory promise that the salary repayment threshold would rise annually inline with national average earnings inflation. However, successive ministerial interventions tore up those terms retrospectively, freezing the repayment threshold at **£27,295**.

By preventing the threshold from rising alongside high public inflation cycles, the policy acts as a stealth tax on middle-income earners. It forces graduates to repay 9 per cent of their earnings on a much larger portion of their salary than originally contracted, while standard compound interest rates on the outstanding balances continue to accumulate at punitive rates.

The timing of Gordon’s legislative intervention intersects directly with a major parliamentary auditing cycle. Immediately preceding the MP’s speech on Tuesday afternoon, Treasury Select Committee Chair **Dame Meg Hillier** is scheduled to deliver a definitive statement detailing the committee’s independent report into the broader stability of higher education funding models.

Gordon, who holds a personal Plan 2 student loan balance and was the first member of his family to enter higher education, stated that the current framework functions as an artificial barrier preventing younger demographics from accumulating capital or hitting standard milestones such as property acquisition. Student advocates argue the state is actively profiting from punitive interest accruals on historical educational contracts.

## Campaign Alliances and Statutory Provisions

The proposed statutory review has secured formal backing from the executive leadership of the National Union of Students (NUS), who are urging a wholesale shift back toward sustainable public investment in higher education infrastructure.


**Oliver Gardner, Founder of the RethinkRepayment Campaign:**

The Government needs to commit to meaningful action to make the repayment terms more affordable for graduates, so they have a chance of building a life for themselves in the UK.
