Rugby league supporters came together at Eastmoor Rugby Club to honour the legacy of Rob Burrow while raising money for a charity dedicated to supporting people affected by motor neurone disease (MND).

The charity event, organised by The Rugby League Daily, saw players, supporters and volunteers unite in aid of the Rob Burrow 7 Trust while helping to raise awareness of a disease that has left a lasting impact on the sport.

For organisers, the day was about much more than rugby.

"We've wanted for a while to do something to raise funds for a cause that is close to the hearts of the rugby league community," a spokesperson for The Rugby League Daily said.

"What better way to do so than by raising funds for the RB7 Trust to pay homage to a legend of our great game in Rob Burrow and also be able to have fun with it too."

## A disease that has left a lasting mark on rugby league

The rugby league community has rallied behind MND awareness since Burrow's diagnosis, with countless fundraising events taking place across the country.

Organisers said the need for awareness and research funding remained as important as ever.

"It's massively important. Sadly there is currently no cure for MND and we want to help change that," the spokesperson said.

"It's heartbreaking to see anybody suffer from this terrible disease as we have sadly seen first hand with the late Rob Burrow and now more recently with Jai Arrow.

"It's important we do our part to keep Rob's legacy alive by banging the drum on MND awareness and this event played a part in helping raise vital funds towards the goal of one day finding a cure for MND."

## Supporters stepped onto the pitch

The centrepiece of the day was a charity rugby league match involving 42 players.

While many participants had rugby league experience, others had never played the sport before and signed up purely to support the cause.

The teams had been assembled earlier this year after organisers put out a call for volunteers.

"We had a total of 42 players taking part, some of whom had never played rugby before but wanted to make an appearance for a good cause," the spokesperson said.

"The game was special for supporters and I'm sure they got a few laughs out of watching the lads play."

## Months of work behind the scenes

While visitors enjoyed a day packed with entertainment, fundraising stalls and rugby league action, organisers said months of preparation had gone into making the event happen.

Since February, volunteers had worked to source raffle prizes, secure sponsorship, arrange activities and coordinate dozens of participants.

"It certainly caused stress at times but it was worth it once the event came together," the spokesperson said.

The day featured raffles, a tombola, fundraising collections and entertainment from the Wakefield City Emeralds, who performed before the match and during half-time.

## Businesses and clubs rallied behind the cause

The event also received strong backing from across the rugby league community.

Professional clubs donated signed shirts, match tickets and memorabilia, while businesses from across West Yorkshire sponsored the teams and supported fundraising efforts.

"We could not be any more grateful to all of these businesses that supported us throughout the journey," the spokesperson said.

## Hoping to make a difference

As the event drew to a close, organisers said success would not be measured solely by the amount of money raised.

Instead, they hoped everyone who attended left knowing they had contributed to a cause that meant so much to Burrow and his family.

The spokesperson said: "Everybody came away from the event feeling like they'd made even a small bit of difference.

"We've raised a good amount in the hope of making a difference to those affected by MND."
