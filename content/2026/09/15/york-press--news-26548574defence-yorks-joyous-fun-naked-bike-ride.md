  *York’s naked bike ride is a “protest against car culture” and the dangers faced by cyclists – but some people have “become less tolerant” to the unusual event in recent years, writes John Cossham who has been involved with the event – as both organiser and participant – for almost 20 years.*

THE World Naked Bike Ride has been going for over 20 years worldwide, as a protest against car culture and the dangers that cyclists face when getting from A to B. It has also become a celebration of the human body, one of which we all have, and should not feel ashamed of or be made to feel ashamed of – and has become a regular fixture in the ‘naturist’ calendar. As it’s become clearer that climate change is an existential threat to many species and ecosystems on earth, cycling is increasingly seen as an eco-friendly alternative to fossil-fuelled transport, so the protest also celebrates zero-carbon travel.

The York ride started 20 years ago and for most of that time has been a joyous and fun event, supported by the police who share our concerns about cyclist safety – many of them are cyclists and none of them enjoy going to deal with the results of a collision between a lump of metal weighing hundreds or thousands of kilos and travelling at speed, and a vulnerable body protected only by thin clothing.


However over the past few years some people have become less tolerant, and appear to have confused non-sexual nudity with pornography and sexual offences. The York ride had a single protester one year asking us (while filming!) as we got ready if we’d be prepared to do the ride clothed. Her fears were that women and children could be harmed by seeing naked people cycling past, and members of the public ‘do not consent’ to seeing this type of thing. We were all polite (as was she) but we explained our various reasons for riding naked.

This year, intolerance in our communities seems to have grown – people are gathering and shouting at refugees who’ve left their country, many because they face death if they stay, and even criticising the RNLI whose job is simply to rescue people at sea. So this year the York ride was warned that there might be a ‘counter protest’, potentially with violence. We didn’t want to call off the ride as that would mean the intolerant people had ‘won’, and we got an assurance from the police that they’d be around and would deal with things appropriately.

As we were gathering near Millennium Bridge before setting off on Saturday afternoon (September 12), a young woman approached, followed by three or four men, one brandishing a can of lager. She started by calling us paedophiles and perverts, and telling us that nudity is illegal – which is factually wrong. Several riders responded to this telling her that it wasn’t illegal, at which lager-man said that if he took a pee ‘over there’ he’d get arrested. Which he might – as urinating in public may be an offence in some circumstances – but that’s different from simply being naked in public. I offered to explain the law to the woman – but she said she didn’t want to know!

So this is what the law actually is – and it depends on the intention behind the nudity. If the intent is to cause alarm or distress, then it could be prosecuted under ‘outraging public decency’ or ‘indecent exposure’ laws. However, just being naked is not against the law, and is perfectly legal if there is no sexual intention and it’s just a naked hike, art installation or protest. However, some people seem to be confused between non-sexual nudity and the sort of nudity which may happen when there’s sexual activity. And describing naturists and naked cycle protest participants as perverts and paedophiles is ridiculous and typical of the misinformed over-reaction that is becoming more prevalent these days.

The handful of protesters told us they were going to stop us getting into town to prevent people from seeing our ‘disgusting bodies’. They waited on the bridge, assuming we’d be going over to the west side of the city. However our route took us in the opposite direction up towards the university, much to the annoyance of the lone protester who ran after us shouting inane insults.

The ride went without a hitch until we arrived at the top of Stonebow, and the half dozen self-appointed guardians of decency decided to blockade the road to us and all traffic, and we stopped for at least 15 minutes. A massive crowd appeared, causing far more people to see what the protesters were up in arms about. What an own goal, a complete backfire. The cyclists waited patiently but after 10 minutes someone called the police and when they turned up the protesters disappeared. We finished the ride at Clifford’s Tower.

However there was one more issue which made this ride a bit different. As we were getting ready to go, a man turned up with a Palestine flag. I want to stress that the World Naked Bike Ride is apolitical and that this man’s cause is separate to the core issues of the ride.
