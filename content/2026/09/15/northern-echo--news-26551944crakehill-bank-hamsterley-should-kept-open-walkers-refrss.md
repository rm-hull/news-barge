People are calling for a County Durham road to remain open as a footpath or cycle path if it can no longer be used by vehicles.

Crakehill Bank in Hamsterley has been closed since a major landslip in 2022 and could see a permanent shutdown, as spiralling repair costs and an unstable embankment have dampened hopes of ever reopening it to cars and lorries.

  **Council leaders will decide on September 16 whether to ask for a permanent closure order** – a move supported by some, but greeted with concern by many local residents calling for access to remain for walkers, cyclists, and horse riders.

It comes as Durham County Council said the underlying ground was exceptionally unstable – repairs now could top £10 million, a budget far exceeded from early estimates.

The update has angered regular road users, who note the closure now forces pedestrians, horse riders, and everyone else onto the fast-running A68.

   

*(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

There are voices pressing decision-makers to at least grant access via the remaining healthy road surface, rather than consigned everyone to riskier options.

Linda Davis said: "This definitely needs to remain viable for foot/cycle/horse usage. Ridiculous that we have been unable to use a width of road far wider than most bridleways for all this time, forcing us to navigate the very fast A68.

"Of course they should reroute the road further in, but from what is being said that is obviously not going to happen, so at least show some sense and make it viable to some users."

A separate call for cautious optimism comes from David Ramsay, who said: “Should be kept open for cyclists and walkers, even if closed to traffic.

"Photo shows that half the road is in sound condition. Wider than most footpaths.”

Echoing Ms Davis, Mike Charlston said: "Absolutely agree, should be maintained as a footpath / cycle route. But the junction with the A68 properly closed."

Tempers have frayed as time goes on without a fix – with one section of residents suggesting that closing the route has less to do with safety and infrastructure concerns than a general reduction in public services.

Steven Matthews said: “This has been left and left for literally years. How much of the rest of our infrastructure does this have to happen to? We’ve patched potholes in roads that ought to be resurfaced, a Toft Hill bypass, discussed for years, kicked into the long grass then shelved.

"I wouldn’t mind but we’re paying more in council tax than ever before for a seemingly shrinking level of service.”

## River and Environmental Concerns

Around the landslip location, some users wonder whether good maintenance practice might have included taking action to regulate the river months or even years earlier.

Alistair Paul Fisher said: “Had the river Wear been maintained as it used to in days of old to seasonally re-align the flow and prevent erosion to north and south banks upstream the A68 viaduct then the C31 slippage would not have occurred.”

Sharon Graham said: “Could you just not move part of the road away from the slippage?”

It is an idea proposed by several commenters, who observe road relocation schemes work elsewhere so seems logical here, provided cost and safety can be prioritised.


## 'Repairs are no longer viable'

Previously, Tim McGuinness, the council’s cabinet member for rural, farming and transport, said that other options had already been explored and closure seemed to be the only viable solution.

"Since the landslip was first discovered, we have been meticulous in exploring options to repair Crakehill Bank and we know the community has been hopeful that the road could reopen," Cllr McGuinness said.

"Sadly, the slippage of the embankment has escalated beyond what we had expected and repairs are no longer viable, especially given that there is no guarantee that further subsidence will not occur.
