BRADFORD Council bosses are pushing for the new Weaver Network bus service to be rolled out in the district much sooner than planned.

In the coming years, bus services across West Yorkshire will be brought under public control as part of a franchising scheme.

It will mean that West Yorkshire Combined Authority, and not bus companies, will decide on frequency, fares and routes.

But franchising will not be rolled out in Bradford until a year and a half after it is rolled out in other parts of West Yorkshire.

Bradford Council Leader Stephen Place (Reform UK) recently told a meeting of the council’s Executive that he would push West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin to bring the Bradford roll out forward.

## When will each area in West Yorkshire get Weaver Network?

According to West Yorkshire Combined Authority, “Phase 1” of franchising will roll out in Kirklees, Dewsbury, Huddersfield and parts of Leeds in spring 2027.

This will be followed by Phase 2 that will include Five Towns (Castleford, Featherstone, Knottingley, Normanton, and Pontefract), parts of Leeds and Wakefield, will roll out in early 2028.

Phase 3, which includes Bradford, Calderdale and parts of Wakefield, will not be rolled out until late 2028.

## Positives for it being rolled out in Bradford last

A report going to last week’s Executive meeting acknowledged there would be some benefits to Bradford being the last of the areas for franchising to be rolled out in.

It said: “For Bradford district, the introduction of franchising presents a significant opportunity to address long-standing challenges around service reliability, network coverage, ticketing integration and passenger experience.

“As Bradford is currently expected to join the franchised network during Phase 3, planned for October 2028, the district will benefit from lessons learned during the earlier implementation phases elsewhere in West Yorkshire.

“This phased approach will also provide further opportunity to work with WYCA to ensure that local priorities, including access to employment, education, healthcare and district centres, are reflected in the future network design.

“Franchising will also support greater integration between bus services and other major transport investments, including Mass Transit, rail improvements and active travel networks, helping to create a more seamless public transport system across the district and wider city region.

“It is recognised that the bus franchising programme is a lengthy and complex process and where opportunities may arise to accelerate bus service and bus infrastructure improvements for the district the ambition remains to explore delivery options within Phase 2 of the programme.

“A key benefit for Bradford will be the transition towards a zero-emission bus fleet.

"Through investment being coordinated by WYCA, together with £8 million of funding secured by BDMDC for the electrification of the Bowling Back Lane depot from the Joint Air Quality Unit, approximately 90 electric buses are expected to be introduced into the Bradford fleet from 2027.

“This will represent one of the largest investments in cleaner bus technology in the district and is expected to deliver significant improvements in air quality, reduce carbon emissions and support the council's wider environmental and public health objectives."

## Bradford Council leader wants Bradford to be in Phase 2

Speaking on the report, Cllr Place said he was “continuing to press the Mayor (Tracy Brabin) to accelerate bus franchising so Bradford moves from Phase 3 to Phase 2.”

Bus franchising will be discussed at a meeting of WYCA’s Weaver Network Board on Thursday.
