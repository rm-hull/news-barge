People are being invited to have their say on plans aimed at protecting and enhancing one of County Durham's most important wildlife sites through the future introduction of grazing cattle.

Durham County Council is considering introducing GPS-controlled conservation grazing at Waldridge Fell, a rare lowland heath near Chester-le-Street.

The plan aims to improve biodiversity and support long-term management of the site, which is designated as a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) by Natural England.

Jeff Talbot, clean and green manager at Durham County Council, said: "Grazing is the most effective and sustainable way to protect and maintain habitats.

"It’s a method that has been used to shape our landscapes for centuries, and by introducing it at Waldridge Fell, we can further enhance this beautiful and important heathland.

"We know Waldridge Fell is a popular place for locals and visitors to walk and connect with nature.

"That’s why we are keen to hear what people think of the plans and are encouraging residents and anyone with an interest in conservation to take part in our consultation."

A consultation has been launched to gather public feedback before the council applies to the Secretary of State for permission to install two new sections of perimeter fencing in the southern part of the fell.

The fencing, which requires permission due to the site’s status as Common Land, would secure areas for grazing while maintaining public access through gated entry points.

Cattle would be contained using GPS technology, reducing the need for physical barriers within the heathland.

Key benefits of conservation grazing include controlling scrub and dominant species that could outcompete native heathland plants, and maintaining a mix of vegetation heights and ages to support a wider range of wildlife.

This includes birds, reptiles, invertebrates, and plants.

The consultation is open until Monday, October 26, and is available on the council’s online engagement platform, Let’s Talk County Durham.

Paper copies of the survey can also be requested by emailing [letstalkcountydurham@durham.gov.uk](mailto:letstalkcountydurham@durham.gov.uk), and will be available at upcoming drop-in sessions.

Details of these events will be shared in due course.
