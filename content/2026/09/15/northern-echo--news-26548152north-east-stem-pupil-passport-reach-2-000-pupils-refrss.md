A new initiative designed to inspire more young people to pursue careers in science, technology, engineering and maths is being rolled out across the North East after a successful pilot programme.

The Pupil Passport, developed by RTC North and the North East STEM Foundation (NESF), aims to encourage students to explore STEM pathways by recording their participation in related activities throughout their education.

The programme follows a successful pilot and will involve around 2,000 pupils from 20 schools across the region this year, with plans for further expansion.

  Left, Jamie Ollivere, chief executive of RTC North, Tania Cooper MBE and NESF trustees Amy Smith and Dawn Gaskins at the charity’s annual fundraising dinner where the collaboration was announced. 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Tania Cooper MBE, chair of NESF, said: "We exist to ensure every young person in the North East, regardless of background, has access to meaningful STEM opportunities.

"Working alongside RTC North on targeted projects allows us to broaden our reach, strengthen our links with industry and create even more opportunities for young people."

Students receive a stamp in their Pupil Passport each time they take part in workshops, competitions, employer encounters, STEM Ambassador sessions, industry visits or careers events.

This record supports participation in nationally recognised CREST Awards, project-based awards that recognise STEM achievement, which can strengthen applications for university, apprenticeships and future employment.

The scheme is the first joint project from a wider strategic partnership between RTC North and NESF, who will continue to deliver their own established programmes while working together on selected initiatives.

Janine Marshall, head of business development at RTC North, said: "This partnership with the North East STEM Foundation is about alignment, not duplication.

"By working together, we can amplify the impact of STEM activity across the region, connecting education, industry and opportunity, while respecting the independence and unique strengths of both organisations."

NESF specialises in delivering employer-led STEM outreach and increasing access to high-quality experiences, while RTC North focuses on connecting education with industry through regional programmes such as the STEM Hub, STEMFest, and the CREST Awards.

By pooling their expertise, the organisations hope to improve employer engagement, raise awareness of STEM careers, and create a more streamlined pathway from classroom learning to the workplace.

The Pupil Passport is expected to help students demonstrate their curiosity, skills, and commitment to STEM through a structured, long-term record of achievement.

It also aims to support employers in developing a skilled talent pipeline by exposing students to real-world STEM environments and career options from a young age.

The programme marks the first of several planned joint ventures between RTC North and NESF, as they work to build a more connected and effective STEM ecosystem across the North East.
