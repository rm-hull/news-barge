THE decision to scrap £400,000 of culture funding to re-open a district tip will be further scrutinised by a cross-party committee of Councillors.

Earlier this month, Bradford Council’s Reform Executive voted to re-open the Sugden End tip in Cross Roads, near Keighley, by the end of the year.

The decision was welcomed, but concerns were raised over how the Executive planned to pay for it.

The decision would see £400,000 funding for cultural events “de-prioritised.”

Now the Executive’s decision has been “called in” by Bingley Councillor Susan Fricker, and it will be discussed at a meeting of the Regeneration and Environment Scrutiny Committee next Tuesday (September 22).

It will give Councillors from all parties a chance to debate the plans before either deciding to accept and “release” the decision – or send it back to the Executive to be reconsidered.

Rather than the re-opening of the tip, Cllr Fricker’s reasoning for the call in relate to the scrapping of culture funding.

The £400,000 funding, included in a budget that was voted through ahead of this May’s local elections, was set aside to “support the delivery of events across the district to promote local heritage, culture and arts and drive increased footfall in district centres.”

Cllr Fricker’s reason for the call in points out that the funding was split into two pots.

£200,000 was for events, and would be “Invested to help local groups deliver more events and festivals across the district, driving increased footfall to district high streets and bringing people together to celebrate where they live.

“Everything from local heritage projects to markets to arts events, small and medium festivals, live music, food, cultural events and more, in line with what local communities want.”

The other £200,000 is for “strategic support for local organisations running events - revenue investment for local community cultural organisations to maintain their impact helping animate district centres and high streets.”

  Cllr Fricker’s call in states: “The funds were designed for communities to run local events, to support increases in footfall to high streets and town centres, to support local businesses, our creative industries and to enhance local pride in place. 

“The termination of these funds will have detrimental impacts upon local communities, local businesses, our creative industries and the reputation of the council.

“The report to the Executive and the meeting of the Executive failed to give any consideration to the impact of terminating these funds, there was no relevant cost/benefit analysis.

“The report to the Executive and the Executive meeting failed to give any consideration to alternative sources of funding for the HWRC proposals.”

At last week’s Executive meeting, Councillor Imran Khan, leader of the Labour group, said alternative funding should be found to re-open the tip, rather than cutting arts funding. He said: “It doesn't have to be one or the other."

But Council Leader Stephen Place said re-opening the tip would help the people of Bradford, particularly the Keighley area.
