A Palm Desert woman accused of savagely beating her 77-year-old husband to death with a heavy metal curtain rod—potentially during a bitter dispute over his life savings—is scheduled to finally face a judge today for formal arraignment after days of medical delays.

Vicky Kyung Mi Kim, 65, faces first-degree murder charges along with weapons and great bodily injury enhancements in the gruesome slaying of Richard Kaufman inside their upscale Enclave Condominium complex. Kim remains behind bars at the Robert Presley Jail in Riverside on $10 million bail. She has missed three previous court dates due to undisclosed medical holds within the county correctional system, leaving officials uncertain if she will appear at the Larson Justice Center in Indio on Tuesday morning.

The tragic killing follows a documented history of severe domestic abuse that ended in fatal consequences. According to the Riverside County District Attorney’s Office, Kim had repeatedly assaulted Kaufman in the past. Despite fierce objections from prosecutors who fought for protective orders to shield the vulnerable senior, Judge John Ryan converted Kim’s previous felony domestic abuse charge into a misdemeanor in January. The judge waived her eight-day jail sentence and declined to place her on probation.

![Suspect Vicky Kyung Mi Kim shown in her mugshot](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/09/15/15/55/Vicky-Kyung-Mi-Kim.png)

Sheriff’s deputies rushed to the couple's home near Gateway and Gerald Ford Drive around 10 a.m. on Sept. 3 following reports of a deceased person. Upon arrival, deputies discovered a disoriented Kim standing outside with a bystander. Kim allegedly claimed her husband was "very sick" and "sleeping" following an early-morning fight.

Inside the residence, investigators uncovered a blood-soaked nightmare. Blood spatter covered the hallway and living room walls, while evidence suggested someone had made a desperate attempt to clean the crime scene. Deputies found Kaufman’s body wrapped in a blanket or rug, displaying signs of decomposition and horrific, widespread blunt force trauma across his head, face, and torso.

Detectives seized a large metal curtain rod featuring a heavy, blood-caked circular finial on the end as the primary murder weapon.

Investigators confirmed deputies and Adult Protective Services had previously responded to multiple domestic violence calls at the residence, with Kaufman listed as the victim in every single instance. Beyond physical violence, authorities were probing ongoing financial elder abuse allegations against Kim.

Officials revealed that both Bank of America and JP Morgan Chase had reported red flags after Kim allegedly tried to force her elderly husband to transfer massive sums of cash out of his accounts. Adult Protective Services had open financial fraud investigations into Kim at the time of Kaufman's death.

If convicted as charged of first-degree murder, Kim faces life in prison.
