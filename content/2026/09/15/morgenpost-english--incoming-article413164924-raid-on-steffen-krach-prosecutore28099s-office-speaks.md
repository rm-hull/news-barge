Contrary to what had previously been assumed, the Hanover public prosecutor’s office is not investigating Steffen Krach on suspicion of taking bribes. According to information obtained by the Berliner Morgenpost, the investigation into the Berlin SPD’s lead candidate concerns anti-competitive agreements under Section 298 of the German Criminal Code. Kathrin Söfker, spokesperson for the Hanover public prosecutor’s office, confirmed this to our editorial team.

She said the alleged offence was being investigated in conjunction with alleged trade-secret violations, breaches of confidentiality obligations and two cases of accepting benefits. This also marked a change in how the Hanover public prosecutor’s office communicated about the case. On Wednesday, it had declined to provide details about the background to the investigation, citing investigative considerations. Krach is presumed innocent.

Another unanswered question was why prosecutors had carried out the searches eleven days before the election in Berlin and four days before the local elections in Hanover. In response to an inquiry from the Berliner Morgenpost, the spokesperson said that the public prosecutor’s office had received initial information about the alleged events in mid-August. She was unable to say what kind of information this was or how and whether it had been passed on to prosecutors, citing the ongoing investigation.

### Public Prosecutor’s Office: Initial Suspicion Has Strengthened

Following a legal review of the case, the findings were considered sufficiently substantiated to justify opening an investigation into Steffen Krach on September 3. To prevent evidence from being lost, the public prosecutor’s office immediately applied for a search warrant, the spokesperson said. It was issued on September 7. On September 9, the offices of the Hanover Region authority, Steffen Krach’s home in Schöneberg and the Berlin SPD’s headquarters were searched. Data storage devices were seized during the searches, the spokesperson said.

At a press conference at midday, Steffen Krach assumed that the investigation concerned allegations of corruption. The SPD politician dismissed them as unfounded. “I have never accepted any money in a private capacity. And I have also never accepted a donation as SPD chairman here in Berlin,” Krach said. “I have nothing to hide.” Investigators could inspect his bank accounts if they wished.

### Authority in Lower Saxony Examines Disciplinary Proceedings

In the evening, it emerged that the municipal supervisory authority in Lower Saxony was also conducting a preliminary inquiry into possible disciplinary proceedings against Krach. A spokesperson for the Interior Ministry confirmed this. The authority had learned of the public prosecutor’s investigation on Wednesday afternoon. A preliminary inquiry is routinely conducted whenever such a case comes to light. This does not amount to any prejudgement of the case; the presumption of innocence applies. Bild had previously reported on the matter.

According to the authority, the preliminary inquiry is examining whether there are sufficient grounds to initiate disciplinary proceedings. If disciplinary proceedings were opened, they would probably be suspended until the criminal investigation was complete and any possible court ruling had been issued, the spokesperson said.
