Teesside University has risen 24 places to its highest-ever position in a UK university league table.

The university, which has campuses in Middlesbrough and London, is 32nd in the UK and second in the North East in the Guardian University Guide 2027.

The guide attributes the rise to improvements in teaching quality, student experience and graduate outcomes.

Interior Design ranked first in the UK, while sports science and social work both came second.

Animation and Games Design ranked third nationally, while media and film studies came fourth.

The rankings measure student satisfaction, continuation rates, value-added scores and graduate career prospects.

The Guardian reported that 83 per cent of Teesside graduates moved into highly skilled jobs or further study.

Around 58 per cent of graduates stay in the Tees Valley, while almost 90 per cent remain in the wider North East.

The university said this keeps skills and talent in the region while supporting local economic growth.

  Vice-Chancellor and Chief Executive, Professor Mark Simpson 

*(Image: Teesside University)*

Professor Mark Simpson, Vice-Chancellor and Chief Executive of Teesside University, said: "This is a landmark achievement for Teesside University and reflects the dedication of our staff, students and graduates.

"These results demonstrate the impact of the high-quality education, support and opportunities we provide.

"We are committed to ensuring our students not only succeed during their studies but graduate with the skills, experience and confidence they need to thrive in their careers."

"As a civic university, we are particularly proud that so many of our graduates choose to build their futures here in the Tees Valley and the wider North East, contributing their talent and expertise to the continued growth and prosperity of our region."

Teesside University holds a Triple Gold rating in the Teaching Excellence Framework and has also been rated Outstanding by Ofsted.

Last year, the university was named Times Higher Education University of the Year and Daily Mail Modern University of the Year.

The latest Guardian ranking is the university’s highest since the league table began, continuing its upward trend.

Professor Mark Simpson, a former student who previously served as deputy vice-chancellor, succeeded Professor Paul Croney after 11 years in the role.
