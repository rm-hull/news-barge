Reform UK's Dan Thomas has quit as the party's Welsh leader

**Reform UK's Dan Thomas has quit as the party's Welsh leader, after he was arrested on Sunday on suspicion of assault and controlling or coercive behaviour.**

Gwent Police confirmed that a 45-year-old man from Aberbargoed, in Caerphilly, was bailed pending further enquiries.

In a statement issued by his party, the Member of the Senedd for Casnewydd Islwyn said he was "standing down" from his position due to personal reasons.

He said he had been "released without charge" and had "done nothing wrong".

Thomas was appointed Welsh party leader by Nigel Farage in February.

Plaid Cymru, which runs the Welsh government, said: "Reform UK is descending into chaos."

Thomas did not take part in First Minister's Questions earlier today.

He was first elected to the Senedd in the elections in May, where his party went from having two politicians in Cardiff Bay to 34.

Reform came second behind Plaid Cymru in the vote, with Thomas becoming leader of the opposition in the Senedd.

Thomas said: "It has been the honour of my life to have been the leader of Reform Wales and see this party go from zero to 34 elected members and become the largest opposition group in Welsh history.

"With deep regret, I am now standing down from this position due to personal reasons.

"Over the weekend, I was reported to the police and was released without charge."

He added: "I will continue to serve my constituents in Casnewydd Islwyn and will fully back whoever the group selects as its next leader."

The news came on the same day that Reform MS Sarah Cooper-Lesadd defected to Plaid Cymru.

Gwent Police said: "On Sunday 13 September a 45-year-old man, from the Aberbargoed area, was arrested on suspicion of assault and engaging in controlling/coercive behaviour.

"He has been bailed pending further enquiries."

Plaid Cymru Senedd group chair, Marc Jones, said: "Reform UK is descending into chaos."

He added: "Sarah Cooper-Lesadd's decision to leave and join Plaid Cymru says it all - Reform is interested in division and headlines, not governing Wales."

Ken Skates, leader of Welsh Labour, told the Press Association: "This is quite incredible news and many questions will need to be answered.

"Dan Thomas was a personal pick by Nigel Farage and this will surely demonstrate the appalling judgement of the Reform UK leader."

Mims Davies, the Conservative Shadow Secretary of State for Wales said: "Reform are hitting the news for all the wrong reasons - massive crypto donations, on top of the jailing of Nathan Gill for pro-Russian bribery.

"All this raises serious and deep questions about Reform's judgement and leadership at every level."

Welsh Liberal Democrat Leader, Jane Dodds, said: "The wheels are rapidly coming off Reform in Wales and the entire country."

Dan Thomas was appointed by Nigel Farage in February, and appeared with him during the Senedd election campaign

Thomas grew up in Blackwood in the south Wales valleys.

He said in February that both his grandfather and great-grandfather were miners, and he had "fond memories" of growing up in Wales - but left when he became an adult because "good opportunities" there were "few and far between".

He worked in financial services in London and became a councillor, and later Conservative leader, at Barnet council in the north of the capital.

During this time Thomas stood for the Conservatives in the Islwyn constituency at the 2010 and 2017 general elections, coming second to Labour's Chris Evans on both occasions.

Thomas then led Barnet council from 2019 to 2022, eventually becoming opposition leader after Labour won the local election there until May 2024.

In June 2025 Thomas defected from the Conservatives to Reform, and at the end of the year resigned from the council.

Local media quoted him saying he moved away from Barnet so that he and his wife could raise their young sons in the countryside and live closer to their families in south Wales.
