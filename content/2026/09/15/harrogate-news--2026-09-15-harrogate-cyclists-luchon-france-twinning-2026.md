**HARROGATE** — A coordinated contingent of regional endurance athletes has returned from the French Pyrenees after completing one of Europe’s most challenging mountain cyclosportives to commemorate a historic international civic partnership.

Ten cyclists representing the unified Harrogate training community successfully completed the grueling **La Lapébie** road race in Harrogate’s official twin town of**Bagnères-de-Luchon**. Staged on** Sunday 13 September 2026**, the event drew a mass-start field of approximately 600 international riders into the high-altitude border terrain, testing regional athletes against severe mountain passes and extreme micro-climates where mid-afternoon track temperatures peaked at** 36C**.

The specialized expedition was organized by Dennis Richards on behalf of the **Harrogate International Partnerships** framework, which provided core administrative and financial backing. The selected roster brought together competitive and club riders from major local athletic networks, including Harrogate Nova, Harrogate Tri Club, the Prologue Junior Racing Team, and the Wheel Easy ‘Contrarians’ group.

## Conquering the Col du Portillon and Superbagnères Slopes

The event structure forced competitors to choose between two highly demanding, timed mountain routes governed by strict algorithmic cutoff times. The standard *Eth Camino* layout required riders to complete 105 kilometers of road work encompassing 2,227 meters of vertical ascent before looping back into the Luchon valley floor.

The maximum endurance tier—the *Eth Gran Camin*—extended the mountain trail to 121 kilometers, forcing riders over the iconic category climbs of the Col des Ares, the Col de Menté, and the Col du Portillon along the Spanish frontier, before culminating in a brutal summit finish at**Superbagnères**. The final mountain pass demanded 3,380 meters of total vertical climbing. Harrogate representative** Emma Dooley** secured a prominent placement within the international field, finishing as one of only nine female cyclists globally to successfully complete the full maximum-tier route.


**Rob Beagley, Harrogate Cyclist:**

It’s been a fantastic weekend. Luchon is a beautiful place, and it’s an honour to have flown the flag for our wonderful town of Harrogate.



The high-altitude finish line at Superbagnères holds a prominent position in modern professional sports tracking, having recently served as the official high-mountain finish for Stage 14 of the 2025 Tour de France. The annual cyclosportive itself serves as a living memorial to the French Lapébie racing dynasty, celebrating the legacy of 1937 Tour de France champion Roger Lapébie.

## 73 Years of Shared International Civic Twinning

The successful mountain deployment serves as a prominent milestone marking the **73-year formal twinning relationship** established between Harrogate and Bagnères-de-Luchon in 1953. Beyond their matching historical roles as premier European spa towns, the two communities maintain shared institutional links centered around outdoor sports engineering, thermal health science, and regional tourism.

The international expedition secured vital corporate backing from the local independent business community, with financial and logistical underwriting supplied by property agents Verity Frearson, Hornbeam Park Developments, and investment firm Endless.
