A LONG-STANDING charity has announced a new name after more than a century of service.

Saint John of God Hospitaller Services (SJOG) is now The Do Good Charity, reflecting its mission in clearer terms.

The organisation provides specialist care, supported living, and residential services across the UK, including in Leeds and Bradford.

Paul Bott, chief executive of The Do Good Charity, said: "We're changing our name because we want everyone to feel welcome.

"But we're not leaving behind the values that made us.

"The Do Good Charity puts our purpose into plain English and, importantly, it is also an invitation.

"We’ve been quietly doing good since 1880.

"Now we need people to know who we are because we want to share what we learn more widely.

"We want more people, communities, charities, supporters and partners to work with us so that together we can do more good."

In Roundhay, Terry Yorath House provides 24-hour residential and respite care for people with physical disabilities.

Bede’s Close in Thornton, near Bradford, offers care in a four-bedroom home for people with learning disabilities.

The charity’s origins trace back more than 450 years to Saint John of God, whose legacy inspired the name change.

In the UK, The Do Good Charity began its work in 1880.
