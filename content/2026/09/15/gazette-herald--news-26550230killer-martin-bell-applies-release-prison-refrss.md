THE sister of a woman who was beaten to death then buried at a Yorkshire beauty spot has spoken of her anger at the killer's bid to be released from prison.

Martin Bell attacked Gemma Simpson, 23, with a hammer and stabbed her at his Harrogate flat in May 2000 before dismembering and burying her body at Brimham Rocks, near Pateley Bridge.

In 2014, Bell confessed to the killing and in December that year was sentenced to life imprisonment with a minimum term of 12 years for manslaughter on the grounds of diminished responsibility.

The Parole Board confirmed a hearing is to take place - but Gemma's sister Naomi Carrack said Bell is "not somebody we want walking the streets of society", as reported by the BBC.

Bell attacked Gemma, from Leeds, with a hammer, bludgeoning her body before stabbing her in the back and the back of the head.

When he realised Gemma was still alive, he dragged her body to the bath and laid her face down, where he left her for four days.

He confessed in July 2014, walking into a police station in Scarborough to admit to the killing before telling officers where he had buried her body.

He pleaded guilty to manslaughter with diminished responsibility and was sentenced at Leeds Crown Court later that year.

A psychiatric report carried out after Bell's arrest diagnosed him with a psychotic mental illness and, during his trial, the court heard he told Gemma that God wanted him to kill her.

Bell, from Scarborough, was given a minimum term of 12 years, but this was reduced to 11 years and 205 days because of the time spent in custody.

In 2025, the parole board recommended he be moved to an open prison, but now he is applying for full release, the BBC reported.

Gemma's sister told the BBC he should not be released and said she has not been given any evidence to suggest Bell has been rehabilitated.

She said that the time he has spent behind bars is neither "fair nor just".

"I don't have any hope [his application] will be rejected because of how fluid it's been for him to go from regular prison to open prison," she said.

The Parole Board told the BBC a hearing had been listed for his parole review and was scheduled to take place in September.

"Parole Board decisions are solely focused on what risk a prisoner could represent to the public if released and whether that risk is manageable in the community," a spokesperson said.
