Plans to turn a County Durham house into a children's home have been approved despite "considerable concerns about safety" from neighbours.

A property on Old Durham Road in Sedgefield, will be converted from a family home to accommodate up to two young people aged between eight and 17 after Durham County Council green-lit plans on September 7.

The council argued the move would address a shortage of small children's homes in the area, providing local accommodation for looked-after children.

However, the proposal faced opposition from neighbouring residents.

One resident said the change would have "a significant and unacceptable impact on the safety, amenity, and character of our residential environment".

They added that it had caused "considerable concern regarding our daughter's safety and our own peace of mind."

Another resident argued the scheme would be "fundamentally incompatible with the quiet, residential nature of the immediate neighbouring family homes" and warned it would have a "detrimental impact on neighbourhood security."

Despite these objections, Durham County Council found there was not enough evidence to deny the application.

The local authority noted that Durham Constabulary had raised no objection, and environmental health officers believed the property was unlikely to generate more noise than a large family home.

Approved plans will be limited to a maximum of two children under 18, with at least two staff members on site at all times.
