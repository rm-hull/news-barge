Three people have been hospitalized as a massive swarm of bees invaded an Intel manufacturing campus in Arizona and terrorized employees.

Fire crews in Chandler, about a 30-minute drive southeast of Phoenix, raced to Intel’s Ocotillo campus Monday morning following reports of one person with a “large amount of bee stings” and multiple people stung by the swarm, according to ABC15 Arizona.

Ten people were treated by first responders on campus, including three taken to the hospital in stable condition, the Chandler Fire Department said in a statement shared with *The Independent*. 

It’s unclear what caused the bees to become agitated.

![Three people have been hospitalized as a massive swarm of bees invaded an Intel manufacturing campus in Arizona and terrorized employees](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/14/20/14/intel-ocotillo-fab-feat-16x9.jpg)

Intel got its start in the late 1960s and soon after created the first microprocessor. It has since grown its global business and currently makes AI chips as demand for the technology grows.

The tech firm is based in Santa Clara, California, but has facilities across the country and across the world. Intel calls Arizona its “U.S. manufacturing powerhouse.”

In March 2021, Intel announced plans to build two new chip factories in Chandler, one of which is up and running. The company says it brings $10.7 billion into Arizona’s economy every year.

A hiker in Phoenix, Arizona, was stung more than 100 times by bees in April.

![A hiker in Phoenix, Arizona, was stung more than 100 times by bees in April and had to be rescued by fire crews](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/14/20/41/660280441_1369655458536258_8957948924738398299_n.jpg)

The Phoenix Fire Department rushed to Lookout Mountain following reports of a hiker repeatedly stung by bees.

“Crews located the adult male, who reported over 100 stings and was unable to continue his descent,” the fire department wrote on Facebook at the time.

The man was rescued by fire crews in a helicopter and taken to the hospital in critical condition. It’s unclear whether the hiker survived the bee attack.

“Avoid disturbing hives, skip scented products when outdoors, wear light-colored clothing, and if you encounter a swarm - run away quickly and protect your head and face,” the fire department warned.
