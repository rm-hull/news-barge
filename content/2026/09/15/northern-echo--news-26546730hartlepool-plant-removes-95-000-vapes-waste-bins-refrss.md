Households across County Durham and Teesside are being warned to keep dangerous items out of their recycling bins, with discarded vapes singled out as a serious fire risk during National Recycling Week.

Richard Nicol, Biffa’s resources division commercial chief, is urging residents to keep hazardous and non-recyclable items out of their recycling bins.

He said: "They’re a fire risk and a risk to life.

  Richard Nicol, manager of Biffa's recycling plant in Hartlepool_ 

*(Image: Biffa)*

"If I could ask for one thing people take from this article, it’s never to put a vape in the recycling bin again."

Mr Nicol oversees Biffa’s Teesside materials recycling facility at Tofts Farm Industrial Estate in Hartlepool.

Staff at the Hartlepool site have removed 95,000 vapes from household waste in just four months.

Lithium batteries inside vapes pose a serious fire risk.

  Richard Nicol, manager of Biffa's recycling plant in Hartlepool_ 

*(Image: Biffa)*

Mr Nicol said: "Lithium batteries never fully run out of charge.

"They’re a fire hazard in bin wagons and at recycling centres.

"We can lose whole bales of recyclable material because of a single vape."

Around 1,200 battery fires are reported across the UK each year, many involving items such as vapes.

Biffa recommends taking vapes to household recycling centres, retailer drop-off points or vape-shop recycling bins.

  Richard Nicol, manager of Biffa's recycling plant in Hartlepool_ 

*(Image: Biffa)*

The waste industry is calling for a vape deposit scheme, with shoppers paying a small deposit and getting it back when they return the vape for recycling.

Mr Nicol has also shared a list of items that should never go in a recycling bin.

It includes vapes, electrical goods such as kettles and toasters, fans, lamps, pots and pans, tins of paint, clothes, broken light bulbs, used nappies, Pyrex dishes, chargers and electric toothbrushes.

Large pieces of wood, which can damage machinery, and dead animals should also be kept out of blue bins.

Recycling bin items include cans, plastic bottles, glass bottles and jars, rinsed food trays with the film removed, and cardboard with as much tape removed as possible.

The Hartlepool plant processes 2,200 tonnes of household waste a week for four councils, including all of County Durham.

Mr Nicol said: "We’re proud of our record. But there are ways the public can help us work more efficiently and safely."

He said many people mean well but often put the wrong items in the bin.

He said: "It’s called wish-cycling.

"People put things in the bin hoping they can be recycled, without really knowing whether they can be or not."

Electric toothbrushes and anything containing a battery may seem recyclable, but do not belong in blue bins.

Mr Nicol said: "The key things we’re interested in are always glass, plastic and cans."

His message for National Recycling Week is simple.

He said: "If in doubt, leave it out."

For recycling advice and a guide to what belongs in your blue bin, visit Recycle Now.
