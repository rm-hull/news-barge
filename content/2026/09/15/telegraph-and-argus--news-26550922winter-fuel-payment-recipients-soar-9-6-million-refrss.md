The number of pensioners receiving the Winter Fuel Payment has soared by 9.6 million in just one year, new figures reveal, after the Government dramatically widened eligibility for the cash.

Some 10.9 million people in England and Wales received the payment in winter 2025 to 2026, according to new Department for Work and Pensions statistics.

That is a huge increase from the 1.3 million who received the payment in the previous winter.

The latest figure is also around 290,000 higher than the 10.6 million recipients recorded in winter 2023 to 2024, when the payment was universal for most pensioners.

The dramatic rise follows a major change to the rules.


## Why did so many more pensioners get Winter Fuel Payments last year?

In winter 2024 to 2025, the Government restricted the Winter Fuel Payment to pensioners receiving certain means-tested benefits.

But for winter 2025 to 2026, most pensioners living in England and Wales once again became eligible, provided they met the qualifying criteria.

Those born on or before September 21, 1959 were eligible, although pensioners with income above £35,000 had their payment recovered by HMRC through the tax system.

The DWP figures do not include subsequent HMRC recoveries and exclude pensioners who opted out of receiving the payment before it was made.

## 11 million people benefited

The number of beneficiaries was even higher once eligible pension-age partners were taken into account.

There were 11 million Winter Fuel Payment beneficiaries in winter 2025 to 2026.

The payment was worth between £100 and £300, depending on the recipient's circumstances.

Of all recipients:

- 37 per cent received £100
- 9 per cent received £150
- 38 per cent received £200
- 17 per cent received £300

The majority of recipients were under 80, with 7.7 million people – 70 per cent – falling into this age group.


## Thousands opted out

Despite the much wider eligibility, 53,000 pensioners opted out of receiving their Winter Fuel Payment last winter.

The Government's income-based recovery system also meant that some higher-income pensioners could receive the payment before HMRC reclaimed it.

An HMRC impact assessment estimated that 2.2 million pensioners, including those in Northern Ireland, would be affected by these recoveries.

The DWP said its latest statistics only cover payments made by the department and do not include money subsequently recovered by HMRC.

## Where did most pensioners receive the payment?

The new figures also reveal a stark regional breakdown.

North Yorkshire had the highest number of Winter Fuel Payment recipients, with 150,000, followed by Birmingham and Cornwall, both with around 140,000.

At the other end of the scale, the Isles of Scilly had just 500 recipients, followed by the City of London with 1,200 and Rutland with 10,100.

The figures cover people living in England and Wales. Winter Fuel Payment has now been devolved to Scotland, which operates its own Winter Heating Benefit.

The Winter Fuel Payment was introduced in 1997 to help pension-age people with their heating bills.

It is an annual tax-free payment designed to help with the cost of heating a home during the winter.

The latest figures provide the clearest indication yet of the scale of the Government's decision to reverse the sharp restriction in eligibility introduced the previous year.
