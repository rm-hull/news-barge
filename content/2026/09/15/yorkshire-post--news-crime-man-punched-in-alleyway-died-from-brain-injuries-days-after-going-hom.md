# Man punched in alleyway died from brain injuries days after going home

## A trio of men have been jailed after the death of a man “unaware of the catastrophic injuries he had suffered” in an alleyway attack.

Aiden Matthews, 31, was attacked on New Years Eve in Norton but tragically returned home “believing he would recover”.

However, Aiden was later taken to hospital where he died of a brain injury a few days on from the attack.

Three men – Declan Moor, Max Cashman and Lee Bradshaw – were jailed for manslaughter at Teesside Crown Court on Monday September 14.

![Aiden Matthews, 31, was taken to hospital where he died of a brain injury a few days on from the attack.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmExYjU3ZmYyLTM0MmItNDI3ZC1hZDI1LWE1NDcyZWNkMDQ1YzpiYjdkODA5Ny05ZmJjLTRhOWMtOTkzMC0wNTdkNTVkMzdhNTE=.png?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Cashman, of no fixed address, was sentenced to six years in prison after pleading guilty to manslaughter.

Bradshaw, of Vale Drive in Thornaby, was sentenced to five years and four months after pleading guilty to manslaughter.

Moor, of Centenary Crescent in Norton, was sentenced to six years and six months after he was found guilty of manslaughter following a trial.

After the sentencing, Aiden’s family said: “The pain of losing Aiden has broken us as a family and our lives will never be the same again.

![Three men – Declan Moor, Max Cashman and Lee Bradshaw – were sentenced at Teesside Crown Court on Monday September 14.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmM4NDJkYzAxLTQ2NzktNDBkNi05ZGFhLTVhODQ4MTI4M2FmMDo4OTZkNzRmYy05ZTA0LTRiODUtYmVjZS1hZWJmMDFmMDhlMGI=.png?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



“All the memories we would have created with him are now only something we can dream of.

“Aiden didn’t deserve to have his life cut short. The actions of these men that night have devastated our family, and our lives will never feel the same again.”

Senior Investigating Officer, T/Superintendent Deb Fenny from Cleveland Police’s Homicide and Major Enquiry Team, said: “This has been a complex and challenging investigation, and I am pleased that we have been able to secure justice for Aiden Matthews and, most importantly, for those who loved him.

“Nothing can undo the devastating loss of Aiden. He was a son, a brother, a father and a friend, and the pain his family have endured since his death is unimaginable. Whilst today's outcome cannot bring Aiden back, I hope it provides his family with some measure of closure and reassurance that those responsible have now been held accountable for their actions.

“Throughout the investigation and court proceedings, Aiden’s family have shown extraordinary courage, dignity and resilience. They have had to repeatedly relive the circumstances surrounding Aiden’s death whilst listening to difficult and distressing evidence. Their strength throughout has been remarkable.

“What makes this case particularly tragic is that Aiden was unaware of the catastrophic injuries he had suffered following the assault. He returned home believing he would recover, when in reality his injuries would prove fatal just days later.

“I would like to thank everyone involved in the investigation for their professionalism, commitment and determination in securing this outcome. Most importantly, our thoughts remain with Aiden’s family and friends as they continue to come to terms with their loss. I hope they can take some comfort in knowing that justice has now been achieved on Aiden's behalf.”
