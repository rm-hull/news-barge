A MAJOR study into menstrual health is underway in Bradford.

The Born in Bradford research programme, based at Bradford Teaching Hospitals, has launched CycleTrack to better understand menstrual experiences and improve care for women.

The study will gather information on the full spectrum of menstrual health, from those with no difficulties to those living with pain, heavy bleeding, endometriosis and fibroids.

Salma Chopdat, community research coordinator at Born in Bradford, said: "For too long, women's experiences have been overlooked, leaving gaps in our understanding of how menstrual health affects everyday life, wellbeing, and healthcare outcomes.

"Now BiB participants can help us to change this by sharing their experiences now or in the past.

"As a thank you for taking part, participants will also receive a £10 voucher."

The study aims to identify factors influencing menstrual symptoms, understand why some people have more severe experiences than others, and help improve diagnosis and treatment.

Eligible Born in Bradford participants aged 18 or over who currently have, or have previously had, periods are invited to join the study.

CycleTrack is a collaboration between Born in Bradford and the universities of Exeter and Bristol.

It is supported through Wellcome Leap's Missed Vital Sign programme.
