Former Senate GOP leader Mitch McConnell returned to Capitol Hill on Monday, his first return to work – and the first time he has been seen in public – since the Kentucky senator was hospitalized in June after suffering a fall.

The senator said in a statement that he would cast a vote Monday evening, when the chamber was set to vote on a nomination to fill an opening on a district court bench in Ohio, shortly before he spoke to reporters on the Hill.

“Today, I’ll cast my first Senate vote since I took a bad fall back in June. I’m really looking forward to being back on the Senate floor and seeing my colleagues,” said McConnnell in a statement released to news outlets.

“My recovery has been a long and often frustrating process, and the lingering effects of childhood polio haven’t made it any easier. I’m still not quite back to 100%, but I’ve assured Leader Thune that, as I continue with physical therapy on the advice of my doctors, I will do my best to be present for tough votes when our Conference needs me,” his statement continued. “Elaine and I are very grateful for all the continued well wishes from our fellow Kentuckians, and I’m glad to get back to more of business as usual on their behalf this month.”

McConnell, 84, remained in the hospital and later a rehab center for weeks before he was discharged in early August. During that time, he faced criticism including from his state’s Democratic governor, Andy Beshear, and others for letting information about his condition slowly trickle out while questions about his illness and ability to serve intensified. McConnell later said that he suffered pneumonia after his initial hospitalization.

The octogenarian senator has suffered numerous falls on Capitol Hill the past few years, sometimes in front of reporters. A childhood polio sufferer, he was at times pictured using a wheelchair more recently before his latest fall in June or being helped up and down Capitol stairways.

Beshear repeatedly called on McConnell to release a video statement confirming his ability to return to work, but was rebuffed. The senator’s return to Capitol Hill was left a mystery until the Senate gaveled in Monday afternoon, with his aides only telling reporters that they “hoped” he would be able to return this week, unsure if it would occur.

The Senate is set to remain in Washington for three weeks before once again leaving town ahead of the midterm elections. The House of Representatives is set to depart at the end of the week, making progress on key legislative priorities a difficult prospect.

![McConnell leaves his townhome in D.C., his first public appearance in months](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/14/23/2026-09-14T221220Z_1859829277_RC2AJNADJ5W5_RTRMADP_3_USA-CONGRESS-MCCONNELL.JPG)

Video taken outside McConnell’s D.C. townhome showed him being wheeled out to a waiting car late Monday afternoon in front of a crowd of spectators and reporters, some of whom shouted questions while one person shouted “welcome back, senator”. He did not take questions or address reporters as he was quickly helped into a waiting van.

Moments later, McConnell arrived at the Senate, where he made a brief statement and did not take further questions from journalists.

McConnell was hospitalized in June after a fall at his home, and the senator’s office released spare updates over the ensuing weeks as questions of his ability to serve mounted on social media. The senator’s office released a photo of him and his wife, former Transportation Secretary Elaine Chao, in late July as calls for proof of his progress kept being leveled by the likes of Andy Beshear, the governor of Kentucky, but resisted calls from Beshear for the senator to release a statement on video.

“On the advice of my doctors, I’ll maintain an intensive regimen of physical therapy from home during the state work period, and I’ll continue to engage with my staff and colleagues on important Senate business,” McConnell wrote in a statement released by his office last month.

Activists, journalists and everyday voters alike have made age a greater issue in the 2026 cycle after the prior presidential election cycle came to be defined by the withdrawal of incumbent President Joe Biden from the Democratic ticket after a disastrous debate.

The senator has also worked through other instances of health challenges in recent years, and in one particularly alarming 2023 episode he was seen on camera freezing up at a press conference before his Republican colleagues whisked him away out of concern.

McConnell, before his hospitalization in June, was also seen on C-SPAN cameras sitting on the Senate floor, largely not speaking to others, for around six hours during a period of Senate debate.

Another veteran member of the Senate GOP caucus, Sen. Lindsey Graham, died at the age of 71 earlier this year, in what was a shock to many of his colleagues. In the House, Rep. David Scott died in April at the age of 80 after reportedly suffering multiple episodes of disoriented behavior on the Hill.

McConnell retired as leader of the GOP Senate caucus in January 2025. He announced last year that he would not seek re-election for another term, and will exit the Senate at the end of the year.
