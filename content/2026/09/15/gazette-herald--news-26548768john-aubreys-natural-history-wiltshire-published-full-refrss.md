A 17th-century manuscript never before published in full is finally being published in full.

Natural History of Wiltshire, written by Wiltshire-born antiquary John Aubrey, is being printed 370 years after he began the work in 1656.

The book is the earliest known natural history of an English county, predating Robert Plot’s Natural History of Oxfordshire by more than 20 years.

Louise Ryland-Epton, the historian who edited the book, said: "Published only in reduced form, Aubrey's Natural History has never had the chance to be judged as the book he actually wrote.

"What it shows is a serious and curious observer of the natural world, who left a record of the flora and fauna of seventeenth-century Wiltshire that exists almost nowhere else."

Aubrey, best known for his Brief Lives, spent 40 years compiling the manuscript but never saw it published.

Until now, only a heavily edited 19th-century version has been available.

The full text reveals a wide-ranging and unusual collection of observations.

While centred on Wiltshire, Aubrey’s writing draws on global knowledge spanning millennia and covers everything from soil and weather to local folklore, architecture, and garden design.

He wrote about petrified shells found in a landlocked county, ghost stories, folk remedies, and even mused on the idea of a university in Wiltshire founded by Greek philosophers.

Aubrey recorded environmental changes such as the enclosure of land and the burning of downland, noting their effects on wildlife, people, and the landscape.

He viewed people and nature as part of one interconnected system.

He also gave weight to the observations of farmers, servants, and women—alongside those of Royal Society members like Robert Hooke and Christopher Wren.

Some of his ideas were ahead of their time.

Aubrey believed fossils were the remains of once-living creatures and argued that the earth had been shaped by ancient catastrophes and was far older than commonly thought.

The most complete version of the work survives in a fair copy made for the Royal Society in 1691.

The new edition, published by Hobnob Press, marks the 400th anniversary of Aubrey’s birth.

A book launch will take place at Chippenham Museum on Wednesday, September 16, from 4.30pm to 6.30pm.

The event is free and open to all.
