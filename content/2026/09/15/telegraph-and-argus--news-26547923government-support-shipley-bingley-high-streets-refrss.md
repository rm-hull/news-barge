*This week's MP coumn is written by Shipley MP Anna Dixon*

Local businesses are the heart of our communities - I’m proud to support them.

Our high streets aren't just where we shop.

Shipley Market Square and Bingley High Street are places where young people get their first work experience, where small business owners take a risk on an idea, and where communities come together.

But, for far too long, our town centres have been hollowed out and left to decline.

Under successive governments over recent decades, we’ve seen bank branches close one after another, shopfronts become vacant, and police not get the support they need to tackle anti-social behaviour on the high street effectively.

  Shipley town centre 

*(Image: Alan Barber: T&A Camera Club)*

In Shipley, that has meant empty units and shop fronts that used to house clothes shops, butchers and grocers, being replaced by betting and vape shops.

Since even before I was elected the Member of Parliament for Shipley, revitalising our local high streets has been one of my top priorities.

It’s a job that starts locally, but its success also depends on the decisions we make in Westminster.

Locally, I’ve been working hard to change this - lobbying our new Reform UK-led Bradford Council to ensure they make full use of the strong new High Street Rental Auction powers granted by this Labour Government, to prevent more commercial properties from sitting empty for years on end.

I’ve worked with our local police on tackling crime and anti-social behaviour, because no independent business can thrive if staff and customers don't feel safe, and I've strongly welcomed the increase in neighbourhood policing and the summer crackdown on street drinking that we saw in Shipley last year, along with new powers to tackle shoplifting, which have started to make a real difference.

And I worked closely with Bradford Council under the previous Labour administration to deliver a major redevelopment and upgrade of Shipley Market Square.

I’ve also taken this cause to Parliament too, speaking up for towns like ours and making the case directly to Government ministers that places like Bingley and Shipley have huge untapped economic and cultural potential; we just need to back them properly.

That backing is now certainly on its way.

Since Andy Burnham entered Downing Street, the Labour Government has moved to put money and power back into the hands of local high streets.

Business rates for pubs, clubs and live music venues have been cut by 20%, easing the pressure on some of the businesses that give our town centres their character and their nightlife.

VAT has been removed from household electricity bills, cutting household overheads meaning more people have money to spend on our high streets at the end of the month.

Plans have been introduced to require planning permission for new vape shops and give local councils stronger enforcement powers to block betting shops from clustering on high streets.

Alongside this, the Government has committed millions of pounds to communities across the country through the Pride in Place programme, with funds available to charities, town councils, community groups and non-profits across the Bradford District.

Local authorities like ours have also received a boost in funds, thanks to the Government’s introduction of the ‘Fair Funding Formula’, a change that I campaigned for and will bring hundreds of millions extra to our city in the coming years.

Years of neglect and underfunding of communities like ours cannot be undone overnight, and I won’t pretend that everything is fixed.

I know many business owners are still watching their costs closely and still rightly want to see more action taken to save on their bills, and I'll keep holding government to account on that.

We also need to do more to level the playing field between our high streets and online retail for good, not just through one-off reliefs but through the kind of fundamental reform of business rates that's been promised for years and is now, firmly, back on the agenda.

But things are changing.

Just in Saltaire, for example, we have seen several great new local businesses opening their doors, such as The Baby Bean, the Love Apple Cafe, and Bows and Beams Cafe.

And in Bingley, we have seen Mulligans expanding, and the Brick Banks Café and the regular farmers market, opened only last year, thriving.

For the first time in decades, our high streets have a government on their side that sees them as places worth investing in.

We can now look ahead and see a genuine path to high streets in Shipley and Bingley that are safer, busier and full of the kind of independent businesses that local people want.

As an MP who will always put their constituency first, I will keep fighting, in Westminster and on the ground in Shipley and Bingley, to make sure that investment lands where it's needed.
