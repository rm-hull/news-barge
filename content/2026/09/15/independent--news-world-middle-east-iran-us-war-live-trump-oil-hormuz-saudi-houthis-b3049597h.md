US President Donald Trump has said he is “open” to negotiations with Iran, but stressed the final decision rests with him.

In a post on Truth Social he said: “The failing Nation of Iran wants to make a deal, quickly and badly. I will determine whether or not the U.S.A. will choose to engage - The concept of which we are open to.”

It came after he insisted the steep rise in Diesel prices is a result of the war between Ukraine and Russia and not Iran.

Oil and gas prices have climbed higher after Saudi Arabia shut down a vital oil pipeline in response to a series of drone attacks. The price of a barrel of Brent crude oil increased by around 3% to 108 US dollars (£80) on Monday morning.

Oman announced late on Sunday that planned talks with Iran about the future of the Strait of Hormuz had been cancelled “in the interests of consensus”, scuppering hopes of a diplomatic breakthrough in the near term.

Iranian officials had said earlier that they would attend the gathering with Gulf Arab states to present an agreement with Oman on the future governing of shipping routes through the Strait, which carried about a fifth of global oil supplies before the war.

## Iran should not be distracted by mixed signals from Trump, Rezaei says

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, said Iran should not be distracted by mixed signals from Donald Trump.

“Don’t get distracted by the US president’s mixed signals—from ‘no negotiations’ to ‘we’re ready to talk,’” Rezaei wrote in a post on X.

“The stakes around oil and the straits have changed. Damage control won’t stop what’s coming,” he added.

“No talks until Iran’s conditions are met. Period!”

## Saudi Arabia lift warnings after alerts in six cities

The Saudi Arabian Civil Defence sent an all clear on Tuesday after issuing alerts for potential danger in six cities including Yanbu, the kingdom's main Red Sea oil port, where millions of barrels a day are loaded.

Saudi Civil Defence said the danger had passed in Jeddah, Abha, Jazan, AlUla and Taif after the areas received emergency alerts amid an uptick in attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis.

The Houthis have stepped up attacks on Saudi cities and energy infrastructure while advancing along Yemen's Red Sea coast towards the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a key shipping route.

## Australia's energy minister to visit Saudi Arabia as Middle East conflict escalates

Australian energy minister Chris Bowen said he would travel to Saudi Arabia next week to meet his counterpart, Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman, as an escalation in the Middle East conflict raises concerns over global energy supply disruptions.

Australia imported 84 per cent of its petroleum product demand last year, official data shows, leaving it exposed to global shocks. Canberra has been moving quickly to shore up supplies since the US-Israeli war with Iran began in February.

"What we are seeing in the Middle East is deeply concerning," Bowen told ABC News late on Monday.

Australia currently holds 41 days' worth of petrol reserves, Bowen said, adding, "The approach of the Saudi Arabian government will be key for ensuring that fuel security overcoming weeks and months."

Bowen ruled out the possibility of a fuel tax cut, after the Labor government in March halved the fuel excise for three months to help families cope with surging costs.

"That excise cut was temporary ... we're not going to respond to every daily movement on the world oil price."

## Israeli strikes on southern Lebanese town kill 13 as fears of escalation mount

Israeli strikes on a southern Lebanese town killed at least 13 people including six children on Monday, Lebanon's health ministry said, in one of the deadliest days of Israeli bombardment in recent weeks.

Fears of a renewed Israeli military campaign are mounting in Lebanon after days of escalating strikes despite a June ceasefire that was meant to halt fighting between the Israeli military and Lebanese armed group Hezbollah.

A woman who said she was related to at least three of the dead said one attack hit a residential building as children were getting ready for school. The Israeli military said it struck people seen transferring weapons in the area of the town, Kfar Rumman.

At the site, Reuters reporters saw children's schoolbooks strewn between chunks of concrete where the building once stood. Rescue workers with heavy machinery had worked throughout the morning to pull bodies out from underneath the rubble.

Eight-year-old Mustafa Farhat lost his grandparents and his mother in the strike, locals said. Rescue workers were still searching for his father's body on Monday morning while the boy lay in the hospital being treated for his wounds, they added.

## Explained: What the escalating war between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis means for Trump in Iran

Yemen’s Houthi rebels have forced a lightning offensive to the Red Sea coast, assuming control over another vital waterway in the region and threatening to expand the frozen US-Israeli conflict with Iran to the east.

By securing a position overlooking the narrow Bab el-Mandeb strait, the Iran-aligned group has opened a second pressure point, further squeezing Saudi Arabia’s oil exports already diverted from the Strait of Hormuz.

The sudden land grab over the last week has been accompanied by an aerial campaign against military instalments in southern Saudi Arabia, shifting the balance of power after more than a decade at war.

## EU condemns Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia

The EU commission president condemned the Houthi attack against Saudi Arabia on Monday.

In comments on social media, Ursula von der Leyen said the bloc stands “in solidarity with our Gulf partners in the face of relentless attacks by Iran’s proxies”.

“We will continue working with our partners for safe, free navigation through the Strait of Hormuz and for stability in the Middle East,” she said.

## Former Trump aide says his constant posting shows his ‘brain rot’

A former White House aide has likened President Donald Trump’s recent run of bizarre posting on Truth Social to “watching his brain rot in real time” amid growing exasperation over his online trolling.

The president has regularly made headlines through his social media output but caused particular concern over the Labor Day weekend when he posted no fewer than 60 posts in 10 hours, many of them AI-generated memes of highly inflammatory topics.

They included the president surrounded by robots, playing the stock market and showing allied countries on a map draped by an American flag.

“He floods the zone with so much of it that we’ve become numb to just how bizarre it is,” ex-White House deputy press secretary Sarah Matthews told *The New York Times*.

## Formula One set to make late call on Middle East season finale

Formula One is prepared to wait before deciding whether the current season can conclude in the Middle East.
