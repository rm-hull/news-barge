A new drive-thru which is set to open in York has taken a step forward.

Plans to install 27 signs featuring KFC branding at the drive-thru in Monks Cross Shopping Park have been approved by City of York Council.

The application from developer Lionacre sought approval to put up the signs on and around the building which is by the Lidl supermarket, in Monks Cross Drive.

Council planning officers stated the scale, height and design of the signs were acceptable and would not have a negative impact on public amenity or safety.

It comes as the new drive-thru was being built during the summer.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/kfc-drive-thru-monks-cross-signs-pd-2.webp)

A licensing application to supply late night refreshments from the building was lodged by Marsdens Catering of Sheffield Ltd in August.

It stated the drive-thru would open for takeaways and deliveries from 9am to 5am daily.

The licensing application closed for public comments on Tuesday, September 8.

A vacancy for a team leader was listed for the site on KFC’s website for £13.70-an-hour and closed for applications on Sunday, August 16.

The American fast food fried chicken restaurant chain is yet to speak publicly about the new Monks Cross site.

Plans for the drive-thru’s signs include an archway, menu board and KFC lettering on the building.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/kfc-drive-thru-monks-cross-car-park-pd.webp)

They also include signs featuring the restaurant chain’s bucket-shaped logo and stickers with its ‘finger-lickin’ good’ slogan.

Council planning officers stated the signs would not be overly-dominant or out-of-place in a largely commercial area.

Officers said: “Given the 27 branding signs are constrained to around the KFC store, drive-thru and the entrance into the car park which is set 30m from the eastern elevation it is considered that the signs are appropriately placed.

“It is assumed that vehicles will not be approaching the site at high speeds and given the presence of existing advertisements for other stores, the proposed branding signs are not anticipated to be a harmful distraction to drivers.”
