A new chapter for arts and culture in the North East has begun with the opening of a contemporary art gallery in a landmark city centre venue, marked by an exhibition celebrating the life and work of a Sunderland-born artist.

The Northern Gallery for Contemporary Art (NGCA), now housed in Culture House Sunderland, launched its new space with Audrey Amiss: Kitchen Sink Realism, an exhibition developed in partnership with the Wellcome Collection.

The exhibition opened on Saturday, September 12, and features previously unseen paintings, drawings, and archival material from the late artist, born in Fulwell in 1933.

Steve Weatherell, Audrey Amiss’ nephew, said: "Both Audrey and my mother were incredibly proud of their Sunderland roots and thanks to this exhibition, which displays a number of her pieces inspired by the city, it feels as though the circle is complete.

"It’s something which we had always dreamed might be possible and we’re very pleased that Sunderland has recognised that she’s very much a daughter of the city and that it has been so happy to welcome her home."

Audrey Amiss studied at Sunderland College of Art -now part of the University of Sunderland’s School of Media and Creative Industries - before earning a scholarship to the Royal Academy in London.

Her work documented everyday life through painting, drawing, journalling, collage, and campaigning, creating an extensive body of work that continues to resonate with audiences today.

Kevin Petrie, professor of creative practice at the University of Sunderland, said: "It’s fantastic to be able to showcase the work of this important artist in our new gallery space.

"NGCA gives our creative students the chance to work alongside the exhibition curator, and in many cases artists themselves, to learn first-hand about careers and professional practice in the arts.

"We’re planning a range of exhibitions of professional artists, academic creative work and student projects for our new gallery space, demonstrating all the ways in which creativity can challenge, inspire, and delight."

Audrey Amiss: Kitchen Sink Realism traces the artist’s life from her working-class upbringing in Sunderland to her later years in South London.

The exhibition features early works depicting locations and subjects from across Sunderland and the wider North East.

Nick Malyan, chief executive of Sunderland Culture, said: "It’s incredibly exciting to open Northern Gallery for Contemporary Art’s new home with the work of such a remarkable Sunderland-born artist.

"Audrey Amiss’ story and her work are deeply connected to the city, so bringing her work back to Sunderland feels like a fitting way to begin this new chapter for NGCA.

"We’re grateful to Wellcome Collection for helping us bring Audrey’s work to new audiences and shine a light on an artist whose work deserves to be much more widely known."

The NGCA's new home in Keel Square is designed to support exhibitions, learning, and collaboration, strengthening the link between education and the city’s cultural life.

The exhibition will run at NGCA until Sunday, January 17.
