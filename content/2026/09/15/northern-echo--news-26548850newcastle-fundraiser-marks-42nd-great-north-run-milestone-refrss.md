An inspirational runner has completed his 42nd Great North Run while continuing a lifelong fundraising effort that has generated an estimated £80,000 for a children's charity close to his heart.

David Stabler, 71, from Newcastle, has spent decades raising funds through the half marathon for Action for Children, a charity he has supported since childhood through his church.

His connection began at Sunday School, where he sold the charity’s “Sunny Smiles” booklets door-to-door with his mother.

  David Stabler receives Action for Children Award from the charity's vice-president Ken Deeks 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Stabler said: "I came to running fairly late.

"I was chatting to a colleague at work and he was enthusing about the Great North Run and I suddenly realised that if I too took to the streets I could raise funds for NCH (now Action for Children) by asking church friends and work colleagues to sponsor me for a charity that has always been close to my heart."

Since the Great North Run began in 1981, Mr Stabler has taken part 42 times, fundraising for Action for Children in at least 38 of those events.

  David Stabler taking part in the Great North Run 

*(Image: Supplied)*

His dedication was recognised earlier this year when he was named an “Outstanding Supporter” at Action for Children’s annual Stephenson Awards, which honour the contributions of supporters, volunteers, and staff.

For about 25 years, Mr Stabler served as the charity’s regional door-to-door collection treasurer and now acts as a trustee for three charitable trusts supporting Action for Children’s work in the North East.

These trusts help fund children’s homes and a specialist domestic abuse counselling programme in the region.

Alongside his fundraising work, Mr Stabler is also a local preacher, district property officer, and church treasurer.

He said: "I know the little I can do to make a difference is only a drop in the ocean, but on the other hand if there are a lot of drops in the ocean that can help change a life."
