A County Durham mum-of-four says her family fled their home after the ceiling collapsed and filthy water poured through the roof.

Jess Partington says the nightmare at her Livin Housing property on Lyne Road, Middlestone Moor, near Spennymoor, peaked when the ceiling above the front door collapsed earlier this month.

The 34-year-old said water began "raining down" into the hallway, flooding the bathroom and leaving chunks of ceiling across the floor. The provider has since re-boarded the ceiling but Jess said she feels "completely let down".

  Jessica Partington, who has issues with her house in Middlestone Moor 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Her four children, aged three, seven, nine and 15, had to be taken out of the house due to the damage.

Pictures show a huge hole in the ceiling, with timber, pipes and wiring exposed above the hallway.


Other images show broken plaster and debris scattered across the floor after the collapse.

Jess said: "It was horrendous. The ceiling just couldn't take any more water, and then it came down.

  The hole in the ceiling at the Lyne Road property 

*(Image: JESSICA PARTINGTON)*

"There was water pouring through the hole above the front door, the bathroom was flooded, and there were debris everywhere. My children had to be evacuated from their own home.

"You don't expect to be standing in your hallway watching water come through the ceiling and wondering what is going to collapse next.


"How can anyone be forced to live in a place like this?"

The latest incident follows months of complaints about the property - where Jess has lived for two years.

She first complained after noticing a strange smell coming from the loft.

  The debris that rained down from the ceiling after it collapsed 

*(Image: JESSICA PARTINGTON)*

The mum later found an exposed, uncapped pipe and claimed foul odours drifted through her home whenever neighbours used their toilets, sinks or showers.

But she says the bad smell has since become a full-blown "housing nightmare."

Jess claims damp and mould have spread through the property, alongside problems with the roof, brickwork and loft.


She says scaffolding has been erected around the property several times, but the underlying problems remain unresolved.

After the incident on Thursday, September 3, Jess says Livin Housing boarded up the damaged roof section, but only after four days.

She said: "We were left like that for days; it's completely unacceptable.

  Jessica Partington 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"I just can't understand how a family with four children can be left in these conditions.

"This hasn't happened overnight. I've been reporting problems and reporting problems, and it feels like nobody listens until something really serious happens.

"Now the ceiling has actually come down. What has to happen next for them to take more action and properly fix the issues?"

  The exposed pipe in the loft 

*(Image: JESS PARTINGTON)*

While the roof is boarded up, the 34-year-old says the carpets at her property are "sodden" with potential sewage-tinged water, and the longer-term issues remain unresolved.

Jess claims she commissioned her own roof survey, which she says raised serious concerns about the property’s condition.

She said the survey describes parts of the building as "structurally unsafe".

  The exposed ceiling after it collapsed 

*(Image: JESSICA PARTINGTON)*

The mum says she is furious that she and her children have been left to deal with the aftermath.

She added: "I feel completely let down.

"I feel like people in bad housing situations are taken advantage of because landlords know they don't necessarily have anywhere else to go.

"We have four children here, and one of them has serious asthma.

  Jessica Partington, who has issues with her house in Middlestone Moor 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"I shouldn't have to keep fighting just to make sure the roof over their heads is safe."

In response, Paul Thompson, head of property services at Livin said: "Miss Partington reported a blocked toilet that had overflowed on September 3, resulting in water damage to the downstairs ceiling. We attended within our emergency priority timescale and carried out work to make it safe.

"The ceiling was subsequently re-boarded the next day and fully rectified within five days of the original report. This issue was not caused by a defect with the property.

"Unrelated remedial works to the roof and attic were scheduled for September 8. However, we were unable to complete the work as planned because access to the property was not granted.

"We remain committed to carrying out these repairs and are ready to do so as soon as access can be arranged with Miss Partington's cooperation."

Jess previously said she feared damp and mould could aggravate her children's health.

Livin Housing previously said it was aware of Jess’s circumstances and was addressing the issues through its repairs and damp and mould procedures.
