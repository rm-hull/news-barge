A jury has heard how a man groaned and then staggered away after he was stabbed with a 17cm chef’s knife in a doorstep altercation.

Mason Jones, who was 30, died on March 1 this year several hours after he was stabbed outside a flat on Main Street, Burley-in-Wharfedale.

John Robinson, 31, of Derry Hill in Menston, is on trial at Bradford Crown Court. He denies murder, manslaughter, and having a bladed article in public.

In a videotaped police interview played on the second day of the trial Mr Jones’s friend Will Clarke described how the pair had been drinking all day in Bury, and each taken half a gramme of cocaine.

He described himself as “probably black-out drunk”.

They arrived in Burley-in-Wharfedale after being invited to go to the flat of Annabel Ramsay, Robinson’s on/off girlfriend.

Following arguments, they were “booted down the stairs” of her flat and spent some time wandering around the surrounding area.

After trying and failing to locate Mr Clarke’s friend’s home, where they hoped to re-charge their phones, they returned to Miss Ramsay’s flat.

By then Miss Ramsay had made good on her threat to call Robinson, who travelled from his home in Menston to join her.

The jury heard that she was alleged to have previously told both Mr Jones and Mr Clarke: “Wait ‘til John comes down. You’ve both f***ed it then.”

Mr Clarke said Mr Mason could be “argumentative” when drunk and added: “I am the same myself. We get silly when we’ve had too much to drink.”

The jury heard that Mr Jones had told Mr Clarke: “This guy’s getting hard with me. Come back with me. This guy’s getting cheeky.”

He said: “[Mason] said some guy was starting on him, which was obviously John.”

The jury heard that the pair were bickering, with Robinson at a window above an alleyway leading to the flat.

Mr Clarke described how Miss Ramsay came out of the flat screaming and punching Mr Jones, who retaliated. Mr Clarke described it as “scuffling about”.

He said John Robinson, who was holding the knife, came out of the flat around five seconds later.

The jury heard that what happened next occurred in the space of 30 seconds.

Mr Clarke, standing “shoulder-to-shoulder” with Mr Jones, saw the knife and stepped back.

He said: “[John] has come out pretty fast. He has basically ran out and gone to Mason.

“Mason didn’t move because he didn’t see the knife.”

It was during these few seconds that Mr Jones was stabbed in the stomach.

Mr Clarke added: “Me and Mason both run off. I’ve heard Mason groan and seen him holding his [abdomen].

“He’s gone like that [demonstrates looking at the wound] and obviously it’s terrible. I’ve looked at it and then carried on running to the main road.”

He described trying to catch Mr Jones as he was falling over onto the pavement, and people coming out onto the street who tried to help.

During cross-examination by defence barrister Ian Henderson KC, Mr Clarke said Mr Jones “groaned a bit” as he was stabbed before staggering off.

He added: “He lifted his top up and I saw his injury.”

Challenged by Mr Henderson as to whether he saw the actual moment of the stabbing, Mr Clarke replied: “I saw it all. I don’t have any confusion about what happened.”

Asked if the injury could have been caused by an accidental collision – when John Robinson slipped as he came out of the flat in the immediate moment before he made contact with Mason Jones – Mr Clarke replied: “No. There was nothing accidental about it.”

The trial continues.
