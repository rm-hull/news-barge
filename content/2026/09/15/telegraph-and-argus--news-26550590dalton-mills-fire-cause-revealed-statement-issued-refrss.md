THE fire service has confirmed the cause behind a huge blaze at a historic Bradford district mill used for Peaky Blinders and Downton Abbey filming.

The blaze broke out at Dalton Mills in Keighley just after 8pm on Saturday, destroying the roof and causing significant damage to the site.

12 crews and up to 70 firefighters were called to the scene.

## What was the cause of the Dalton Mills fire?

  Firefighters were still tackling the blaze the following morning 

*(Image: T&A)*

The cause of the latest fire at Dalton Mills has been confirmed as arson.

A spokesperson for West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service said: "The fire has now been recorded as suspected deliberate ignition."

## Top firefighter warning

A top firefighter is pleading with parents to keep "curious children" away from the "very unstable" disused mill.

Benjy Bush, Bradford district commander for West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, has described the site as "dangerous."

Mr Bush said: "Local parents, please speak to your children.

"It might look like a fun place, but it’s got a lot of hidden hazards.

"It’s a dangerous place.

"There are holes in the floor.

"I understand it’s a derelict property and it attracts curious children, but I’d urge parents to speak to their children about keeping away from the area."

He said he would hesitate to send firefighters into the building due to its poor condition.

“I’m very hesitant now about sending any firefighters in there for any further incidents,” said Mr Bush.

  Dalton Mills in the aftermath of Saturday's blaze


Mr Bush said: "It’s very unstable now.

"I think it’s been well-documented how many fires there have been over the years and every time there is an incident, it becomes more unstable."

## A history of arson attacks

Dalton Mills has a recorded history of arson.

Two deliberate fires were started within six days of each other earlier this year.

In March 2022, a major blaze ripped through the Grade II*-listed mill, destroying much of the interior and causing an estimated £15 million worth of damage.

The former mill, which dates back to the 1860s, required a huge emergency response from firefighters as flames spread through the vast Victorian building.

Investigations later led to the conviction of two teenagers for arson.

Court proceedings heard that the youths had entered the site during a break from college before the fire started.

The incident placed a heavy strain on emergency services and raised further concerns about security at the largely vacant complex.
