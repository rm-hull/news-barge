Plans to tackle graffiti which has been scrawled on the side of an historic York building for years have been lodged.

An application to paint a mural on the side of 54, Monkgate has been submitted to City of York Council.

The mural would feature imagery inspired by the history of the surrounding area including the Grey Coat School and Monkgate & Jersey Diary Company, with the final design yet to be decided.

Applicant Duncan Marks, of the York Civic Trust, stated a high-quality mural would attract less graffiti than the blank rendering repeatedly applied to the wall in recent years.

The Grade II-listed 19th Century building’s wall facing onto Agar Street has been targeted by graffiti since at least 2009.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/54-monkgate-york-graffiti-mural-plan-4.webp)

The application for listed building consent to paint the mural on the wall follows the commissioning of the work and engagement with the community.

Engagement including through workshops was done to ensure the mural’s design was linked to the wider area’s heritage.

York Civic Trust, York Business Improvement District (BID), ward councillors and York St John University have all aided the development of the plans.

The concept design drawn up for the mural features the silhouette of a young girl carrying books.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/54-monkgate-york-graffiti-mural-plan-2.webp)

Plans stated the details of the design are not final and are subject to change.

The mural would be applied using breathable paint and pressure washing, abrasive cleaning and other methods would be avoided to prevent damage to the listed building.

It is thought have been built in around 1840.

The artwork would be painted in the autumn, depending on the weather, if the plans are approved.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/54-monkgate-york-graffiti-mural-plan-5.webp)

The application stated it did not claim the mural would stop graffiti in the future.

But it added the idea was based on research showing artwork elsewhere had made buildings less attractive to potential vandals.

Plans state: “The approach seeks to minimise potential effects by confining the work to the specified rendered area, selecting a site-responsive composition and commissioning an experienced muralist with a proven track record of delivering site sensitive murals in public spaces.

“The mural is intended to contribute positively to the experience of Agar Street by replacing recurrent graffiti tags with a coherent, community-informed artwork.

“Murals can provide placemaking and communal value where quality, location and local narrative are carefully considered.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/york-street-art-mural-lowther-street-the-groves-tom-sledmore-1-dd.webp)

It follows the unveiling of a mural depicting fox wearing a flat cap with a jar of Bile Beans in The Groves earlier in September.

The artwork, on the side of Lowther Street’s Spar, was designed by artist Tom Sledmore and explores the history of the Groves area.

Paintings on utility boxes designed by children from five schools were unveiled across the Holgate area in June in a separate project.
