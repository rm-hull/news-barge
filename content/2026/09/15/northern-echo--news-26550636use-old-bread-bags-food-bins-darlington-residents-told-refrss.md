People can use old bread bags if they run out of biodegradable liners for their new food waste bins, Darlington Borough Council has said.

The advice has been issued to thousands of households with the new bins, which were introduced earlier this year.

Darlington Borough Council said residents do not need to buy special biodegradable liners once the free rolls supplied with their food waste caddies have been used.

Instead, households can use plastic bags, including bread bags, or small bin liners bought from supermarkets.

The authority said the bags do not need to be biodegradable because they are removed during the treatment process.

Residents can also line caddies with newspaper, although the council warned outdoor bins may need cleaning after collections to prevent smells.

In an update to residents, the council said: "When your free biodegradable food caddy liners run out, there are a number of things you can use.


"A plastic bag – it doesn't have to be biodegradable as the bags are removed at the treatment plant. Even an old bread bag can work."

The council also thanked residents for taking part in the scheme, saying increasing amounts of food waste are being diverted away from landfill.

"We are collecting more tonnes of recycling each week, which is all avoiding landfill and being turned into energy to power homes and businesses, fuel and fertiliser for local farms."

The food waste collection service was introduced following a controversial delay earlier this year after the council said it had been "let down" by a supplier and did not have all the specialist collection vehicles required for the launch.

The scheme was originally due to start at the end of March before being pushed back until June.

Around 50,000 households across Darlington received kitchen caddies, outdoor food waste bins and a roll of 52 biodegradable liners as part of the Government-backed recycling initiative.
