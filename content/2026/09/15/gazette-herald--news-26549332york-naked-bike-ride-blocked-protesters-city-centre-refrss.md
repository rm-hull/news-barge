Dozens of naked cyclists were blocked by protesters during their annual ride in York, an event participant has said.

John Cossham, who joined this year’s York World Naked Bike Ride on Saturday afternoon (September 12), said the group was stopped in Pavement by a “half dozen self-appointed guardians of decency”.

He said the counter protesters stopped the group – cycling naked around York in protest against the dangers faced by cyclists while raising awareness about the environmental impact of fossil-fuel-dependent transport – for about 15 minutes.

John, writing in The Press, said: “A massive crowd appeared, causing far more people to see what the protesters were up in arms about. What an own goal, a complete backfire.

“The cyclists waited patiently but after 10 minutes someone called the police and when they turned up the protesters disappeared.”

He said the nude cyclists then went on to complete their around 11km route at Clifford’s Tower.

John added that the group also faced backlash from around five counter protesters before setting off from near Millennium Bridge at 4pm on Saturday.

He said the cyclists, who have crossed Millennium Bridge in previous years during the naked ride, decided to reroute towards the University of York to avoid the protesters.

It came after John claimed one of the protesters started “calling us paedophiles and perverts, and telling us that nudity is illegal – which is factually wrong”.

“I offered to explain the law to the woman – but she said she didn’t want to know,” he claimed.

John said public nudity is not illegal in itself, but offences can arise where there is an intent to cause alarm or distress, or where the conduct is considered sufficiently lewd or obscene, potentially engaging laws on exposure or outraging public decency.


John, who has been involved with the York Naked Bike Ride as both an organiser and participant for almost 20 years, said: “Just being naked is not against the law, and is perfectly legal if there is no sexual intention and it’s just a naked hike, art installation or protest.

“However, some people seem to be confused between non-sexual nudity and the sort of nudity which may happen when there’s sexual activity.

“And describing naturists and naked cycle protest participants as perverts and paedophiles is ridiculous and typical of the misinformed over-reaction that is becoming more prevalent these days.”

John said the naked bike ride in York started 20 years ago “and for most of that time has been a joyous and fun event, supported by the police who share our concerns about cyclist safety”.

But he said people have “become less tolerant” in recent years and “appear to have confused non-sexual nudity with pornography and sexual offences”.
