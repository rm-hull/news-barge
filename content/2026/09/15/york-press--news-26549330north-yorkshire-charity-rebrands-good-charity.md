A social care charity whose UK story began in North Yorkshire more than 140 years ago is entering a new chapter after adopting a new name inspired by its centuries-old heritage.

Saint John of God Hospitaller Services (SJOG) has rebranded as The Do Good Charity, reflecting its mission to serve others with compassion, hospitality and respect.

Its roots trace back more than 450 years to Saint John of God, who dedicated his life to caring for the poor and sick.

  Paul Bott, chief executive officer of The Do Good Charity 

*(Image: Guzelian Pictures)*

The religious order founded in his name became known in Italy as the Fatebenefratelli – the “Do Good Brothers.”

Paul Bott, chief executive of The Do Good Charity, said: "SJOG was familiar to the people who already knew us, but it did not necessarily tell someone encountering us for the first time who we were or what we stood for.

"The Do Good Charity puts our purpose into plain English and, importantly, it is also an invitation.

"We’ve been quietly doing good since 1880.

"Now we need people to know who we are because we want to do more of it, and share the things we have learnt.

  Front row, from left, Nicola Wilkin, Kay King, Jamie Hope. Back row, from left chief executive Paul Bott and Omolade Saibu, support worker in Darlington 

*(Image: Guzelian Pictures)*

"We want more people, communities, charities, supporters and partners to work with us so that together we can do more."

The charity began its work in the UK in 1880 in Scorton, North Yorkshire, and still operates supported-living services in Scorton, Catterick, and Leyburn.

Today, The Do Good Charity supports more than 1,800 people each month across 60 communities.

It provides housing, care and specialist support for disabled and autistic people, individuals with complex needs, and older people.

The charity focuses on helping people live as independently as possible, with a supported-living model that prioritises personal choice and community involvement.

  Charity staff, form left, James Lindley, Malcolm Thompson, Charlotte Waistell, Amy Griffiths, Jodie Brodie-Myers. People the charity supports, from leftNeville Fill, Damian Rodgers, Emma Catterson. 

*(Image: Guzelian Pictures)*

Though the name has changed, Mr Bott said the organisation’s core approach remains the same.

He said: "It’s a new name, but the same heart since 1880.

"The 500-year-old philosophy of hospitality is still the bedrock of our work."

For the fifth consecutive year, 100 per cent of its CQC-regulated services were rated good or outstanding across all areas.
