Sir Kevin Sinfield is set to be welcomed to Bishopthorpe Palace in York as part of his seventh and final fundraising endurance challenge for the motor neurone disease community.

The former Leeds Rhinos captain's visit forms part of the 7 in 7: The Grand Finale challenge, when he will run an ultra-marathon each day for seven days to raise funds and awareness for people affected by MND from September 27.

Sir Kevin, 45, will start at Betfred Super League champions Hull KR and will visit the other 11 English top-flight rugby league grounds before concluding at Old Trafford in time for the Super League Grand Final on October 3.

He has already raised more than £11 million for MND causes, inspired by his late friend and Leeds Rhinos teammate Rob Burrow, who died in 2024 after living with the disease for four-and-a-half years. Burrow wore the number 7 shirt during his career as a scrum-half for the Leeds Rhinos.

  Kevin Sinfield with his former teammate Rob Burrow who died in 2024 aged 41 (Image Danny Lawson PA Wire) 

*(Image: Danny Lawson/PA Wire)*

He will arrive in York from Shiptonthorpe, near Market Weighton, on Monday, September 28. He and his team will call at the University of York, LNER Community Stadium for a visit to newly-promoted York Knights, then pass York Minster and York Racecourse on their way to the finish line at Bishopthorpe Palace.

  Sir Kevin Sinfield will finish day 2 of his challenge at Bishopthorpe Palace, pictured. 

*(Image: Haydn Lewis)*

After completing day two of the challenge at the palace, the champion fundraiser will then set off the following morning for the DIY Kitchens Stadium, home of Wakefield Trinity.

Archbishop Stephen Cottrell said: "It is an honour to welcome Sir Kevin and his team to Bishopthorpe Palace.

"As they prepare to set off on Day 3, I will be pleased to bless Kevin and all those working alongside him, and to pray for strength and encouragement for the days ahead.

"This extraordinary challenge is raising vital awareness and support for people living with Motor Neurone Disease, and I hope that Kevin and his team will feel the encouragement of all those who come out to support them along the route.

"I wish them every blessing and safe running as they make their way through the rugby league communities across the North of England finishing his extraordinary challenge at the Grand Final in Manchester."

  The Archbishop of York Stephen Cottrell will bless Sir Kevin Sinfield and his team before they set off on day 3 of the challenge. 

*(Image: Photo: x)*

As part of the challenge the fundraiser is also hoping to sell 7,777 pairs of sports socks for the newly-launched Sock It To MND campaign in a further boost for MND charities.

He has teamed up with kit supplier Oxen Sports, which has been big supporter of his past challenges. Just 7,777 pairs of limited edition socks will go on sale, with Oxen asking athletes, supporters and organisations to post a photo or video on social media, with the hashtag #Sockit, in a further show of support.

Profits from the sock sales will go to MND charities and Sir Kevin's fundraising bid.

He has encouraged the public to support the challenge, saying that every card, message, hug and handshake "has fuelled us to do more and go even further in our fight against MND".

He has already completed six endurance challenges and was at York recently for the second day of the Ebor Festival, which saw 17 runners compete in the ‘Sir Kevin Sinfield 7 In 7 Grand Finale Handicap’.

  Kevin Sinfield outside York Minster after completing the ultramarathon in December 2023.

"We’ve got to keep going, we’ve got to keep fighting and keep trying to pull people together to help," he said.

The Archbishop will bless Sir Kevin and his team before they begin day three of the challenge.

Donations have already topped £340,000.
