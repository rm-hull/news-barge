**WESTMINSTER**— Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, presented a 10-Minute Rule Bill to review and fix student finance today.The Bill, co-sponsored by MPs from the Liberal Democrats, Labour, the Green Party, and Plaid Cymru, calls on the Chancellor to specifically review loan terms and conditions alongside repayment thresholds.


**Tom Gordon MP, addressing the House, said:**

It is not social mobility but social injustice, with interest. Students with the least borrow the most, graduate with the biggest debts, and are charged interest on every penny of it.



Addressing the House, Tom hit out at the chilling effect the system has on social mobility. He shared the story of his university friend whose loan balance rose by almost £2,000 whilst on maternity leave, unable to earn or make repayments.

Commenting on the Government’s lukewarm response to the Treasury Select Committee’s report, *Student loans: Broken and Unfair?*, he warned that the current path creates deeper uncertainty, leaves graduates in limbo, and deters disadvantaged young people from higher education.


**Tom Gordon MP added:**

My message to the Government is simple: commit to a genuine fix on student finance. No party can claim the moral high ground. So, every party can own the solution.



The Bill was passed onto its second reading with the potential to be debated on January 15th, subject to government support and the parliamentary schedule.


**Lord Shaffaq Mohammed, Lib Dem Spokesperson for Education in the House of Lords, said:**

My son said to me: ‘Dad, I’m paying £170 per month, and my loan balance hasn’t changed from when I graduated, how is that fair’. It’s unjust. For him and the thousands who have been paying it off and have seen their balance go up because of interest rates.



The full list of cross-party co-sponsors includes Tom Gordon, Vikki Slade, Liz Jarvis, Dr Danny Chambers, Christine Jardine, and Layla Moran from the Liberal Democrats; Cat Eccles, Cat Smith, Ruth Cadbury, and Alex Sobel from Labour; Siân Berry from the Green Party; and Ann Davies from Plaid Cymru.



### Addendum: What is the 10-Minute Rule Bill and why is student finance being challenged?

**What is a 10-Minute Rule Bill?**

 This is a parliamentary mechanism that allows backbench MPs to introduce a new piece of legislation. The MP is given ten minutes to make their case in the House of Commons chamber. While these bills rarely become law without direct government backing, they are highly effective tools for raising the profile of an issue, forcing a public debate, and demonstrating cross-party support.

**The Current Student Finance Issue**

 The debate centers heavily on the high interest rates applied to student loans and the government’s decision to freeze or alter repayment thresholds (the salary level at which graduates must start paying back their loans). Because interest accrues even while students are studying or on career breaks, many lower and middle-earning graduates find that their monthly repayments fail to cover the accumulating interest. As a result, their total debt balance increases year over year, even while they are actively paying it off.

**What Tom Gordon’s Bill Demands**

 The proposed legislation specifically calls on the Secretary of State to launch a formal review of the system in England. It demands that the Treasury look at:

 • Realigning repayment thresholds with average national earnings.

 • Reviewing the fairness of interest rates and repayment structures.

 • Assessing the long-term impact of retrospectively changing loan terms after they have already been issued to students.
