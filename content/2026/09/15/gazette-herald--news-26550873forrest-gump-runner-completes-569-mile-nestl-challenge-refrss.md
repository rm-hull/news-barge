Ultra-marathon runner Rob Pope - dubbed the "real-life Forrest Gump" - has completed a 14-day, 569-mile coast-to-coast charity challenge through York and the UK headquarters of the world's largest food and beverage company.

Pope ran the route with Nestlé to highlight how food is produced and help people reconnect with the journey of food from farm to fork and support The Felix Project, the UK’s largest food redistribution charity.

Pope set off from Ayrshire on September 2, on a route which took him through York and the Haxby Road factory site on day six —the home of KitKat—and finished in Brighton today (September 15).

  


He met farmers, factory workers and communities involved in producing and distributing food.

Mr Pope said: "When I set off from Ayrshire, I knew I was going to learn more about where our food comes from.

"What I didn't expect was just how many people I would meet along the way who play a part in that journey—from farmers who care deeply about what they grow, to factory teams who take great pride in the products they make.

"But what makes reaching the finish line really mean something is knowing those 500-plus miles will help provide the equivalent of 175,000 meals through Felix.

"Meeting the team there and seeing the work they do to get food to families who need it most really brought the journey full circle for me."

The Taste Trail was part of Nestlé UK & Ireland’s Made by Britain campaign.

The campaign aims to reconnect people with food’s journey from farm to fork and spotlight those behind it.

For each mile run by Mr Pope, Nestlé donated more than 300 meal equivalents to The Felix Project, resulting in more than 175,000 meals.

Dr Emma Keller, head of sustainability at Nestlé, said: "The Taste Trail has been about much more than the miles Rob has covered.

"It's been about the people behind our food—the farmers, factory teams and local communities whose work often goes unseen.

"We're proud that Rob’s Taste Trail challenge will help support the important work Felix does, while bringing people closer to the story behind the food they eat every day in Britain."

After the 14-day run, Pope said he was struck by how many people help produce everyday goods, the pride workers take in their jobs and food’s power to bring communities together.

Pope said: "Seeing the work being done with Felix, the UK’s largest redistribution charity, and the devotion to what they do, really brings home why this journey matters.

"Ultimately, it’s about helping make sure good food gets to people who need it."

During his York stop, Mr Pope visited Nestlé’s KitKat factory to learn how 100 per cent British milk and wheat are used to make the chocolate bar.
