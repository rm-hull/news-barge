PASSENGERS using mobility scooters can now travel on all types of trains in Northern’s fleet.

The change follows an update by Northern, one of the UK’s largest train operators, which now permits smaller mobility scooters on its Class 158 trains.

Previously, these models were excluded due to concerns about manoeuvrability and concerns they could become stuck.

Alex Hornby, customer and commercial director at Northern, said: "We are determined to create an accessible railway for everyone and continue to make further improvements for our customers across Northern.

"With more people using smaller scooters, we decided it was time to review the rules and find a new way forward to enable more passengers with mobility scooters to safely board more services."

Northern introduced its mobility scooter policy in 2020, but at the time, most scooters were too large to turn in the narrow vestibules of Class 158 trains.

As the number of passengers using smaller, lighter models has increased, the operator decided to reassess its policy.

Following consultation with passengers and staff, further testing with different scooter models, and a reassessment of more than 150 stations, Northern concluded that suitable scooters could now be accommodated on the Class 158s.

Passengers with existing mobility scooter permits who meet the size requirements are being contacted and issued new permits specifying that their scooters are safe for use on Class 158 trains.

Mr Hornby thanked those who contributed to the policy review.

He said: "I would like to thank Janet Kidd, Tony Jennings and Sue Buckingham, as well as Shopmobility Leeds for their support in making this happen."

Northern has already issued free permits to more than 3,000 passengers using around 330 different types of mobility scooters.

These permit holders can travel from 262 stations across the Northern network.

The policy change comes as Northern prepares for the largest investment in its fleet to date.

The operator plans to purchase up to 450 new trains in three phases, with around 130 trains expected in the first phase.

Contracts are planned to be awarded by early 2027, with the first trains scheduled for delivery in 2030.

A key focus of the investment is improving level boarding by ensuring new carriages meet requirements at platforms that conform to the network standard.

Northern operates 2,650 services a day to more than 500 stations across the North of England, making it the UK’s second-largest train operator.
