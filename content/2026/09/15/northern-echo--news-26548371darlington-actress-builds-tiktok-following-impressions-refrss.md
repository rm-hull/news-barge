Meet the Darlington actress, singer and impressionist turning a backstage habit into a growing social media career.

Georgia McIntyre, 34, started dancing at two and singing at six. She attended the Italia Conti Academy of Theatre Arts at 16, training in musical theatre.

McIntyre discovered her talent for impressions during long rehearsal days.

She said: "There's a lot of waiting around when you do musical theatre if you're doing tech runs or rehearsals. So in the green room I just started doing my impressions and it kept everyone entertained.

"I would do impressions of the teachers at college and things like that. I like to people-watch and I can't have a conversation without doing the voices of the people I'm talking to.

  Georgia McIntyre. 

*(Image: GEORGIA MICINTYRE)*

Her mum encouraged her to start posting videos on TikTok.

The account has attracted more than 3,000 followers and 100,000 likes.

"I've been quite lucky.

"I've only been doing it for a few years.

"It just keeps snowballing."

McIntyre, from Darlington, continues working in the performing arts while teaching Pilates at Rockliffe Hall.

Her days often involve juggling both careers.

"I'm recording my impressions of Saffron and Freddie from TOWIE today and then I'll be going to Rockliffe.

"That's just a standard Wednesday for me."

Television presenter Alan Carr is her favourite celebrity to impersonate.

"He's just such a funny person and I've got loads to go off with his mannerisms, his energy, his look.

She added: "It's an impressionists dream."

"The same with the GC (Gemma Collins).

"I love doing her and she's so iconic.

"The phrases go down well online and in live performances."

McIntyre has performed her impressions on stage but admits she is still finding her feet in that part of the industry.

"I'm a theatre girl. I've always grown up in the theatre, so I'm kind of winging it.

"I would love to do more theatre with my impressions, but I'm kind of the new kid on the block, so I'm just seeing what happens.

One of McIntyre’s recent theatre credits is Joe McElderry’s Something in the Water, a showcase of North East music, theatre, comedy and television talent.

The show returned to Newcastle Theatre Royal this summer for two performances hosted by the South Shields singer, with funk-soul group Groovetrain providing the music, and raised money for Newcastle Theatre Royal’s Creative Engagement programme and North East Music Opportunities CIC (NEMO), creating opportunities for young people across the region.

The production featured Danny Adams, Si King, Jason Cook, Michael Marouli, Adam Strong, Christina Bianco and Carly Burns.

McElderry said the event celebrated North East talent and created opportunities for future performers.

Georgia said: "I saw that Joe was putting on the show and it was all about North East talent and I loved it straight away.

"So I applied and sent him my impressions and then I got invited to do an in-person audition.

"Then I got told they'd love to have me on.

"The talent on that stage was unbelievable, I got imposter syndrome.

"I'm really grateful for these opportunities and anything else that comes my way, I'll just grab it."

The appearance marks another step in McIntyre’s varied career, with her impressions winning audiences online and on stage.

Georgia thanked her parents for their support.

She said: "When I film videos of my impressions, I don't post them without my mum and dad's approval.

"I just want to thank them for being the best supporters I could ask for."

Miss McIntyre said she is looking forward to the future.

"I love making people laugh so I think the next step in my career would be to do a comedy.
