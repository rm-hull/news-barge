Shoppers visiting Asda Express stores across the North East will notice a major change on supermarket shelves.

It comes as the retailer rolls out digital price labels nationwide, which will impact several stores in the North East.

Asda has announced that more than 517 Asda Express convenience stores across the UK have now been fitted with electronic shelf-edge labels, replacing traditional paper price tickets in what some customers have described as the "end of an era".

The move affects all nine Asda Express stores in the North East and forms part of a wider investment in new in-store technology.

The supermarket giant said it has installed around 1.2 million electronic labels across its convenience estate.

The digital displays allow customers to view pricing, promotions, unit costs and, in some cases, ingredient and allergen information directly from the shelf.

Asda said the technology replaces the need for colleagues to manually swap more than 120,000 paper price labels every week.

The retailer believes the change will free up staff to focus on serving customers, improving stock availability and maintaining stores.

The new labels can also provide additional information for products such as bakery items, helping shoppers make informed decisions about ingredients and allergens.


## Concerns about dynamic prices

The rollout comes as supermarkets across the UK increasingly adopt digital shelf-edge technology.

Tesco has begun a major programme to install electronic labels across thousands of stores, while Morrisons, Waitrose and Co-op are also introducing similar systems.

However, the technology has sparked debate among some shoppers, with concerns raised that digital pricing could make it easier for retailers to change prices throughout the day between customers picking up an item and reaching the checkout.

Asda has not indicated that its new labels will be used in that way, saying the focus is on improving efficiency and making product information easier to access.

The retailer is also trialling shelf-monitoring cameras in five larger stores ahead of a wider rollout.

The technology can identify gaps on shelves, low stock levels and items placed in the wrong location, automatically alerting staff so products can be replenished more quickly.

Asda said the measures are designed to improve product availability and help ensure shelves remain stocked for customers.
