A VETERAN of Bradford Movie Makers, and the much-loved star of an acclaimed film about the historic club, has died, aged 93.

Colin Egglestone joined Bradford Movie Makers in 1976. Founded 94 years ago, it is one of the world’s oldest amateur film-making clubs and was the focus of Bafta-longlisted documentary A Bunch of Amateurs, screened in cinemas worldwide.

Colin’s love of film-making started as a child when his grandad gave him a projector. “It was a 35mm wind-up, with a candle in it,” Colin told the T&A in 2023. He made films on Standard 8, moving on to sound: “I was always filming family holidays,” he said. “I’ve got all my family on film; at picnics and barbecues I used to pan the camera round.”

  Colin winding film. He initially made films on Standard 8


Founded in 1932 as Bradford Cine Circle, the club hit the big screen in a multi award-winning A Bunch of Amateurs, which had standing ovations at screenings and audience Q&As. The film documents highs and lows at the Little Horton club and its struggle to keep going.

Colin died peacefully in hospital. His fellow members of Bradford Movie Makers have paid these tributes to him:

Dave Marshall said: “Colin was born the year after the club was founded. He was a self-employed cabinet maker and made loudspeaker cabinets for Wharfedale’s Prestige Speakers. He was an honour guard for Queen Elizabeth in Germany and also made dashboards for Jowett cars.

“Colin was what Bradford Movie Makers represents - friendship, camaraderie, helping each other and true Yorkshire grit. He was the keystone of the club. He was the keystone of Bradford Move Makers and a major star of A Bunch of Amateurs.

"It was a pleasure to do his 90th birthday buffet and commission 40 special ‘Colin mugs’ for members and guests. He will be greatly missed by all of us."

  Colin was the longest-serving member of Bradford Movie Makers.


Judith Simpson said: “Colin’s loyalty to the club over its years of hardship is remarkable. As a new member he built the projection box; later he served as president. When the club was struggling with vandalised premises, Colin was always one of the first to volunteer to clean, paint or repair.”

Edmund Davies described Colin as “someone we all should have in our lives; he was kind, respectful and very loyal to Bradford Movie Makers - or, as he still called it, the Cine Club. He was the lifeblood of the club.”

Joe Ogden said: “Colin was a strong force at the club, for 40 years plus. A quiet gentleman who worked hard for the club, not afraid of change when needed, doing the jobs that need to be done. He was a link to remind us at Bradford Movie Makers of our roots in cine film."

Harry Nicolls said Colin was "a credit to the club".

Added Harry: "He was an inspiration. By the time I started making films it was all digital and Colin was a great link with the past, when filming meant film."

  Colin was a member of the film-making club for 50 years.


Phil Wainman recalled: “When I was a new member I put my name down to become President. It was a little nerve-wracking. Colin came up to me and said, ‘You’ll do great. We need some new thinking’. I was the youngest member of the club and was very pleased to have the blessing of the oldest. Colin set an example I endeavoured to follow.”

Kim Hopkins, director and co-producer of A Bunch of Amateurs, said: “Colin was the beating heart of Bradford Movie Makers. When it was released in cinemas across the country, we had hundreds of messages about him. He seemed to steal the nation’s heart. ‘I wanted to reach through the screen and give Colin a hug’. ‘Colin, the world is a better place with you’. And my favourite: ‘Colin is a national treasure’.

  Colin, right, with Movie Makers club members at the Bradford premiere of A Bunch of Amateurs.


People from all over the world, of all ages, seemed to connect with this old man with his walking stick, flat cap and dewy eyes. But more than anything, it was his passion for cinema that shone so brightly on the big screen.”
