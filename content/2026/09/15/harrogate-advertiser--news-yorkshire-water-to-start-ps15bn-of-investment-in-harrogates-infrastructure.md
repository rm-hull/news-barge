# Yorkshire Water to start £1.5bn of investment in Harrogate's infrastructure

## Ground work to start on The Stray in Harrogate

Harrogate is set to become part of one of Yorkshire's largest infrastructure investment programmes, as Yorkshire Water prepares to begin ground investigations on The Stray as part of a £1.5 billion programme aimed at reducing storm overflow discharges across the region.

![Water](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/jpim-static/image/ugc/2026/09/15/08/Water_1789403290_9294c046-1b785b705fa919ab.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



A spokesperson for Yorkshire Water said: "The local work, due to start on 17 September, represents an early step towards improving the resilience of Harrogate's wastewater infrastructure and protecting nearby watercourses.

"Contractors Ward and Burke will drill three boreholes between Montpellier Hill and the Esplanade over around six weeks.

"Each borehole will be around 30 metres deep, allowing engineers to understand ground conditions and monitor groundwater levels before determining the most appropriate permanent solution for reducing discharges into Oak Beck."

The investigation follows ecological and arboricultural surveys, with Yorkshire Water committing to reinstate The Stray once the work has been completed.

The spokesperson added: "While the immediate activity is relatively limited, the project highlights a much wider challenge facing Harrogate and other growing communities.

"Wastewater infrastructure needs to cope not only with existing demand but also with increasingly unpredictable rainfall, urban development and changing environmental requirements."

Yorkshire Water says its network is being tested by a combination of urban growth and climate change, making investment in capacity and resilience increasingly important.

The £1.5 billion programme will run between 2025 and 2030 and target more than 450 storm overflows across Yorkshire.

It follows an earlier £180 million investment covering more than 100 storm overflows. According to Yorkshire Water, that earlier programme contributed to a 24.5 per cent reduction in storm overflow discharges during 2025, while the duration of discharges fell by 33 per cent.

"For Harrogate, investment of this scale could have implications beyond reducing pollution," added the spokesperson.

"Modern water infrastructure can support the long-term development of towns by providing greater resilience as housing, commercial activity and population levels change.

"Improvements to drainage and wastewater systems can also help reduce the risk of flooding, protecting homes, businesses, roads and public spaces during periods of heavy rainfall.

"The programme also demonstrates how major infrastructure projects increasingly rely on detailed planning and project coordination.

"Ground investigations, environmental assessments, engineering design, construction and reinstatement all need to be carefully managed before permanent works can be delivered.

"Technology is likely to become increasingly important as water companies modernise their networks."

Yorkshire Water's wider investment programme includes new monitoring equipment designed to provide more information about network performance.

The company plans to install 92,000 monitoring devices across its wastewater network, helping its teams identify problems earlier and respond more quickly.

It states that this shift towards smarter infrastructure could ultimately change how Harrogate's water network is managed.

"Instead of relying primarily on reactive maintenance, increasingly detailed data can allow engineers to identify emerging issues, understand how infrastructure behaves during extreme weather and make better-informed decisions about where investment is required.

"The environmental benefits are also potentially significant. Oak Beck and other local watercourses are part of the wider network of rivers and waterways that could benefit from reduced storm overflow activity."

Yorkshire Water's programme includes a range of engineering approaches, including underground storage, surface-water separation, sustainable drainage systems and nature-based solutions.

"For Harrogate, the current work on The Stray therefore represents more than a short-term construction project.

"It is an early stage in a much larger effort to modernise infrastructure and make the town's wastewater network better equipped for future demands.

"As development continues across Harrogate and North Yorkshire, the ability to maintain reliable, resilient infrastructure will become increasingly important.

"The £1.5 billion investment provides an opportunity to address some of those challenges while supporting a cleaner environment and a more resilient built environment for the years ahead."
