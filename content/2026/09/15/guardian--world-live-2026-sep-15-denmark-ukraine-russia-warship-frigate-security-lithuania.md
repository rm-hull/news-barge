Lithuania to send a note to Belarus about drone incursion as investigation continues, minister says

Meanwhile, Lithuanian foreign minister Kęstutis Budrys said that Lithuania will deliver a note to Belarus to set out what it knows about the overnight drone incursion into its airspace.

He added that the work to determine the type and ownership of the drone was underway. “We will take other actions based on that,” he said in comments quoted by Lithuanian broadcaster LRT.

Budrys added that Nato’s eastern flank “must be strengthened and its resilience increased” amid growing tensions with Russia.

Helicopter incident between Russia and Denmark reflects rising tensions, expert says

Miranda Bryant

Nordic correspondent

Experts said that Monday’s incident on the Danish straits reflects increasing tensions between Denmark and Russia that is slowly moving towards Cold War levels.

Ebsen Salling Larsen, a military analyst at the Center for Joint Operations at the Danish Defence Academy, said it is normal for Danish ships, helicopters or planes to go out to monitor Russian state-owned ships and warships. Sometimes this prompts Russian crew to point weapons at them or use a tracking radar.

But Monday’s incident, during which Russian flares were fired at a Danish helicopter, was unusual and marked a “change in pattern”.

“There’s an ongoing tension and the incident yesterday talks to that increased tension somewhat,” said Salling Larsen. “If you go back to the cold war you have more incidents of ships bumping into each other and so forth. We are slowly reaching that level again.”

The show of force, he said, was a reaction to Danish support for Ukraine and the Baltic states.

It is unlikely that Denmark would close the Danish straits, he said, because Copenhagen wants to follow international law and has its own vested interests in being able to move freely.

“That would be a Pandora’s box we would rather not open,” he said. “We are a small state with a large merchant fleet so we have some interests there as well.”

Russian frigate issued no warning or attempted radio contact before firing flares at Danish helicopter, minister says

Meanwhile, Denmark’s defence minister Jeppe Bruus told Reuters that no warning or radio contact was made before a Russian frigate fired flares towards a Danish military helicopter.

He also said the flare from the frigate passed “within a matter of meters” from the Danish helicopter.

Bruus said it showed Russia’s increased willingness to take risks in its aggressive behaviour.

Russia's ambassador blames Denmark for helicopter incident

After speaking to the Russian media (12:25), Moscow’s ambassador to Copenhagen Vladimir Barbin was somewhat more incendiary in his comments to the Danish media.

He told Danish broadcaster DR that essentially it was the Danish helicopter that provoked the Russian ship, branding it a “reckless” behaviour.

He also accused Denmark of showing “no interest … in preventing the situation from escalating.”

'Moscow continues attempts to destabilise Europe,' Ukraine's foreign minister warns

Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha responded to the incident in Denmark, declaring Kyiv’s “full support” after “unacceptable actions targeting a Danish helicopter.”

“Moscow continues its attempts to destabilise Europe, not just Denmark. Joint and decisive action is required and Ukraine is ready to contribute to all efforts aimed at strengthening the protection of Europe from Russian threats,” he said.

Russia to 'carefully study' alleged incident involving a Danish helicopter

Russian ambassador to Denmark Vladimir Babin said Russia would “carefully study” the alleged incident involving a Danish helicopter.

In comments to Russian state news agency Ria Novosti, Barbin said “the Russian side will carefully study all the circumstances of the incident.”

But he implied that Denmark was at fault, saying he “demanded that the Danish side take the necessary measures to prevent such incidents from recurring in the future,” RIA reported.

Vilnius airport hit by delays after brief disruption caused by 'unidentified object'

Separately, Vilnius airport was forced to temporarily suspend its operations this morning after another “unidentified object” was reported in its vicinity.

The 30-minutes disruption resulted in delays to flights to/from Copenhagen, Tel Aviv, Venice, and Warsaw, among others, LRT said.

We are getting a bit more detail about the drone incident in Lithuania (10:25).

A drone shot down over its territory last night may have strayed off course, according to presidential security adviser Deividas Matulionis, public broadcaster LRT reported.

The key question of whether its incursion into Lithuanian airspace was intentional or not will be part of an investigation.

But Lithuania’s State Defence Council will meet on Wednesday to review the country’s air defence setup, he said.

Separately, defence minister Robertas Kaunassaid the drone was downed near a pumped-storage hydroelectric plant, part of Lithuania’s critical infrastructure.

The objects caused failures across the network, affecting several level crossings which disrupted road traffic, Dutch rail infrastructure operator Pro Rail said in a statement.

It explained that as a result, “our computer system identifies a section of track as occupied, even though there is no train present.”

“Because safety always comes first, signals automatically turn red. As a result, trains must stop or slow down. Level crossings can also malfunction,” it said.

It did not comment on who could be behind the incident.

Morning opening: Russia-attributed security incidents intensify across Europe

Jakub Krupa

Denmark has summoned Russia’s ambassador after a Russian warship fired two emergency flares at a Danish army’s helicopter in the Baltic Sea in the latest of incidents attributed to Moscow.

The incident took place during what Denmark said was “a routine” monitoring of the frigate.

“Reckless action from Russia towards Danish military during a routine overflight yesterday. Russian actions are meant to intimidate and divide. They will not succeed. We will not waver,” prime minister Mette Frederiksen said.

Denmark's prime minister Mette Frederiksen, leader of Denmark's Social Democrats. Photograph: Henning Bagger/Ritzau Scanpix/AFP/Getty Images

Denmark’s foreign minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said the frigate’s behaviour was “completely unacceptable.”

The incident took place amid worsening security situation in Europe.

Separately, Nato fighter jets stationed on the alliance’s eastern flank shot down a drone – believed to be flying from Belarus – over Lithuania last night.

“With Russia intensifying its aggression against Ukraine, such readiness is vital for our region,” Lithuania’s president Gitanas Nausėda said.
