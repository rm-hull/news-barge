Plans for safety improvements on a York road where an elderly woman was killed by a lorry are set for a decision.

Works worth £220,000 proposed for Outgang Lane, in Osbaldwick, would include improvements to footpaths, installing street lights and putting in double yellow lines.

A City of York Council report stated the road had become busier following a growth in traffic and a traveller site, with improvements needed to reduce the risk of accidents and injuries.

But two objectors claimed they could make the road more dangerous, fuel antisocial behaviour from the neighbouring traveller site and drive congestion into Osbaldwick.

It comes as plans for the works are set to go before a council transport meeting for a decision on Tuesday, 22 September.

The works are part of £5.25 million in improvements planned for York’s three permanent traveller sites up to 2030.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/outgang-lane-osbaldwick-york-aerial-gsv-1000x667.webp)

A council report on the proposals for Outgang Lane stated the road currently suffered from obstructions to and a lack of footpaths, poor lighting, potholes, inconsiderate parking and flooding.

It added the road had become busier since the Outgang Lane travellers site had grown from temporary accommodation to a permanent residential community.

Officials stated safety concerns had also been on the rise since an accident in the road which killed 73-year-old Betty Elizabeth Smith in January 2020.

An inquest in 2023 heard the resident of the travellers’ site died after a lorry carrying a skip reversed into her at the entrance to a waste management business.

The report stated: “Residents and businesses have very strong concerns about the danger to pedestrians.

“The 2020 incident escalated tensions between residents and industrial estate businesses.

“Residents are now more resentful towards motorists, whose inconsiderate behaviour negatively impacts on the road safety for pedestrians.”

But the plans prompted objections from Keyline, a business based at the Outgang Lane industrial estate, and Restore Britain ward councillor Mark Warters.

Keyline claimed the changes would create unnecessary and incredibly dangerous interactions between pedestrians and vehicles including lorries travelling to and from their premises.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/cllr-mark-warters-york-suit-ldrs.webp)

The businesses added they feared antisocial behaviour they were already experiencing from travellers could get worse.

It stated: “We are extremely concerned that your proposals will exacerbate these problems, making it increasingly difficult for us to operate effectively from this site.

“This is clearly unacceptable to a long-standing, successful business operator in the area.”

Cllr Warters claimed putting double yellow lines in the road would merely move traffic problems into Osbaldwick itself.

The councillor stated: “The industrial estate and Outgang Lane operated perfectly well for decades before, there is no need to create further chaos.

“All the proposals will do is result in the further urbanisation of Osbaldwick Village and Murton Way.”
