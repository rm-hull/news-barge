## York rivers trail invites residents to shape its future after reaching 6,000 people

Residents are being encouraged to help shape the future of the York rivers trail after the family-focused project reached more than 6,000 people and generated over 750 digital conversations during its first season *(Image: Supplied)*

Residents are being invited to help shape the future of a popular York rivers trail after the project reached more than 6,000 people through family-focused channels during its first season.

The York rivers trail was launched earlier this summer and has already sparked more than 750 digital conversations, reaching more than 6,000 people through family-focused channels.

Councillor Jenny Kent, executive member for environment and climate emergency at City of York Council, said: "York's rivers have always shaped our city, and the York rivers trail helps bring that relationship to life in new and engaging ways.

  Following the Foss 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"This autumn programme builds on the trail's successful launch by creating opportunities for people to explore, learn, reflect and share ideas about the future of York's rivers."

As the project enters its next phase, residents and visitors are invited to take part in activities during the York Walking Festival, York Environment Festival, and York 50+ Festival.

The programme includes guided walks, workshops, exhibitions, and creative sessions aimed at exploring themes of nature, water quality, climate resilience, and community action.

  An exhibition 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Mark Henderson, flood risk manager at City of York Council, said: "As we move into autumn and river conditions begin to change, the York rivers trail provides an opportunity for people to better understand how York lives with water.

"It helps make those connections visible, linking upstream landscapes, local action and the river through York into one shared story."

The trail features parks, gardens, historic spaces, and community stories, and can be explored year-round at locations including Homestead Park, Leeman Park, Museum Gardens, and Rowntree Park.
