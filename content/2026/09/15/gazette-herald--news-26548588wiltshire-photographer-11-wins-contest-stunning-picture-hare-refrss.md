An 11-year-old boy from Wiltshire has won a national photography competition.

Joseph, from Marlborough, took first place in The Week Junior Science+Nature magazine’s annual Scavenger Hunt Photo Contest with a striking image of a hare in a harvest field.

His winning photograph is featured on the cover of the magazine’s 105th edition.

A spokesperson for The Week Junior Science+Nature said: "Immediately we thought 'here’s our winner', the symmetry of this shot is amazing and the direct eye contact and battered ear gives it such character."

Joseph also won camera equipment from Canon UK, a nature book bundle from Bloomsbury Children’s Books, and tickets to the Photography and Videography Show 2027.

The winning image will be displayed alongside work from other young photographers at The Photography and Videography Show at ExCeL London, taking place from March 13 to 16, 2027.

His entry was selected from more than 550 submissions.

The Week Junior Science+Nature is a monthly magazine aimed at readers aged eight to 15.

It encourages children to explore, ask questions about, and engage with the natural world.

This year’s contest invited children across the UK to "Focus on Nature" and capture images that reflect the beauty of their surroundings.

Entries were judged by a panel of experts, including freelance wildlife photographer Ellie Rothnie and 11-year-old conservationist and author Aneeshwar Kunchala.

The panel selected Joseph’s photo for its "symmetry", "direct eye contact" and the hare’s "battered ear", all of which stood out to the judges.

The contest forms part of the magazine’s wider mission to inspire children to spend more time outdoors and develop a deeper appreciation for the environment.

Joseph’s win highlights the impact of creative challenges in helping young people notice and share the beauty of the world around them.

His success also demonstrates the potential of youth photography to capture compelling moments in nature.

All winning and runner-up photographs will be exhibited at the upcoming London show.

They will appear alongside prize-winning images from professional photographers.

This gives the young winners a rare opportunity to display their work to a wider audience.

It also celebrates the growing talent and enthusiasm among the next generation of nature photographers.
