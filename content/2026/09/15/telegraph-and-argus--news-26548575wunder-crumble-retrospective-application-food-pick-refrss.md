A MAJOR Bradford food business has applied for retrospective permission for a kerbside pick up service it says only serves “a limited number of customers.”

Wunder Crumble is a dessert business that runs two sites in the city

A restaurant on Great Horton Road and a storage and distribution facility on Ingleby Street called Belgravia House.

Since late last year, the Ingleby Street site has also been used as kerbside collection business – used by customers who drive to the building, pick up food and drive away to eat it elsewhere.

This coincided with the business organising a Winter Market and a New Year's Eve celebration - which attracted 3,000 people - near the site.

They also hosted a free drive-through fan zone experience during the World Cup this past summer.

Now the company has submitted a retrospective planning application to retain this “sale of hot and cold food and beverage” use.

If approved, it will also allow the business to keep “external areas for temporary food and beverage stalls.”

## 'Small number of customers'

The application form says the changes first came into effect in November 2025.

It claims that the main use of the building will remain storage and distribution, and that only a “small number” of customers will ever attend the unit for kerbside pick up.

## What does the application say?

Documents included in the application say: “The primary function of Belgravia House remains the storage and distribution of desserts.

“The same business operates a larger restaurant operation on Great Horton Road, with Belgravia House principally functioning as a supporting storage and distribution facility for that operation.

“The customer-facing element at Belgravia House is deliberately limited in scale and is not intended to operate as a conventional restaurant.

“A small number of customers may attend the premises to collect dessert products.

"The operation is principally undertaken on a kerbside collection basis, whereby customers arrive, park within the surrounding parking areas and collect their orders without the need to enter or occupy the premises for any significant period.

“A limited number of tables and chairs have also been provided within the segregated area of the premises.

“These are intended to provide a modest facility for customers rather than create a conventional dining establishment.

“The scale of this customer-facing element is therefore ancillary to the principal storage, production and distribution function of the premises.

“The operation is therefore not expected to generate the level of customer activity, deliveries or traffic associated with a typical standalone restaurant or takeaway.

“The customer-facing element is intentionally limited and comprises occasional kerbside collections, a small number of tables and chairs and occasional orders through third-party delivery services.

“It is not intended to operate as a large-scale restaurant or takeaway from the premises.”

The plans say the business will open until 2am.

A decision on the application is expected next month.
