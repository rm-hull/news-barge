A major new phase of work is under way to restore passenger train services to one of North Yorkshire's most scenic railway routes.

Wensleydale Railway has launched a further programme of track and infrastructure improvements on the five-mile stretch between Leyburn and Redmire, which has been closed to the public since 2020 because of the deteriorating condition of the line.

Some of the track dates back to before the First World War and has now reached the end of its operational life, requiring extensive repairs before passenger trains can safely return.

Nick Keegan, fundraising and marketing manager at Wensleydale Railway, said the project represented one of the organisation's biggest engineering schemes in recent years.

"This project is arguably the railway's largest civil engineering project in recent years and it's a testament to the hard work of our incredible volunteers, and the generous funding support we have received so far, that we are now approaching the halfway mark in our plans," he said.

The railway's board has set a target of restoring passenger services to Redmire by 2028.

"Reopening five miles of railway is not the end goal," Mr Keegan added.

"We want to unlock Redmire's potential.

"We see this work as the foundation for a much wider regional development opportunity, with the railway playing a pivotal role in connecting Leyburn, Redmire and the wider Wensleydale visitor economy."

Since 2021, volunteers and specialist contractors have been carrying out work to bring the route back into use.

The project has included drainage improvements, earthworks, embankment stabilisation, vegetation clearance, sleeper replacement and major sections of track renewal.

The latest phase will see specialist contractor Lampitt Rail Services work alongside railway volunteers to upgrade infrastructure between Leyburn and Wensley.

Once completed, attention will turn to the final stretch between Wensley and Redmire.

However, railway bosses say the most challenging and expensive phase still lies ahead.

Detailed surveys have identified a need for extensive track and sleeper replacements, ballast renewal, drainage works, earthworks improvements and the replacement of several farm crossings.

The cost of completing the final phase is estimated at around £420,000.

So far, the railway's "Return to Redmire" project has cost approximately £250,000, funded through support from charitable trust members, shareholders and public donations.
