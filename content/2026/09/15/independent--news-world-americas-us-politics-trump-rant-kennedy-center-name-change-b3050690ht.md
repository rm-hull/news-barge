President Donald Trump ranted Tuesday that the Kennedy Center is “in a virtual state of collapse” and that a falling chunk of concrete and steel left a security guard “60 seconds away from certain death,” as he lashed out on Truth Social just before a federal judge permanently blocked having his name inscribed on the building for a second time.

“The Kennedy Center is in a virtual state of collapse, and has been for many years,” Trump wrote in a lengthy screed. “A large piece of concrete and steel just dropped down, in a thud, from the ceiling of a heavily trafficked main hallway. If the Building were open, innocent people would have been killed. A security guard had just passed the area, one minute prior to this ceiling collapse. He was, therefore, 60 seconds away from certain death.”

Trump did not mention that the Kennedy Center’s own communications office, describing the same Grand Foyer ceiling incident after storms hit Washington earlier this month, said only that a section of ceiling had come down and that “no one was hurt.”

The post came not long before U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper issued an order blocking the Kennedy Center’s Trump-appointed Board of Trustees from inscribing “renovated and restored by President Donald J. Trump" on the building's main facade or renaming the center’s campus “President Donald J. Trump Plaza” in what is the latest defeat for a White House that has spent the better part of a year trying to attach the president’s name to the building.

Trump, who has installed himself as the Center’s chairman, blamed the building’s purported condition on the litigation rather than on his own administration’s decision to keep it open without a full renovation underway.

“The Judge has made us keep it open even though there are no performances, and no people that would have the Courage to even go in,” he wrote, before pivoting to a familiar attack line: “A very hostile and conflicted Judge (What else is new?) seems like he won't let that happen, in which case, unfortunately, the Building is destined to doom. Isn’t that too bad? We have already wasted more than 6 months ‘playing around’ in Court. Time is our Enemy!”

He closed by noting the Board — which he described as “comprised of some of the most prominent and respected people in our Nation” — was “meeting again” on Tuesday, adding: “It will be interesting to see what happens!”

What happened, according to Cooper’s 44-page memorandum opinion, was another loss for the administration.

The order enforces a permanent injunction Cooper issued on May 29 requiring the removal of Trump's name from the Center after Congress's Kennedy Center Act — which the judge noted bars “additional memorials or plaques in the nature of memorials” from the building's public areas — went unheeded by a Board that voted in August to try again. That August 13 resolution proposed inscribing Trump’s name on the building, renaming the grounds “President Donald J. Trump Plaza” and eventually adding a third inscription crediting a “Trump Kennedy Center Fund” once its endowment hits $100 million.

Cooper blocked the first two outright, rejecting the administration’s argument that the injunction applied only to the building itself and not the surrounding campus.

![Workers in June removed parts of the the signage for the venue after a federal judge rejected a bid by Kennedy Center board and the Justice Department to halt the Trump-led attempt to rename the venue after himself](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/13/19/06/GettyImages-2280577682.jpg)

“The Kennedy Center's public spaces honor President Kennedy and President Kennedy alone,” he wrote. “Alone means alone.”

He was similarly unmoved by the government's warning that the Center could face financial ruin without Trump’s name attached, noting officials had “failed to support th[e] assertion” of irreparable harm “with any specific facts or evidence” — a finding the D.C. Circuit had already made once, in July, when it denied the administration a stay. The Endowment Fund inscription survived for now only because the fund, by the administration’s own account, “has not yet started raising money.”

Cooper ordered the Board to give the court and Democratic Rep. Joyce Beatty, who is leading the legal battle, at least 60 days' notice before installing it, should the $100 million ever materialize.

The ruling capped a summer of escalating filings in *Beatty v. Trump*, the suit the Ohio Democrat brought as an ex officio member of the Kennedy Center’s board after the panel first voted to rename the institution in December. When ticket sales sagged and the administration abruptly announced in February that it would shutter the Center “ostensibly for renovations,” in the words of a Beatty court filing, she amended her suit to block that, too. 

The Board’s renewed push in August — after Cooper's May injunction — prompted the Department of Justice to warn the building could be “required to be taken down,” which Beatty’s attorneys cast in an August 26 filing as a threat rather than a warning: “Defendants have not-so-subtly said: ‘This is a nice Kennedy Center you have here, and what a shame should something happen to it.’ This is a breathtaking assault on the rule of law. It is delusional. And it must stop.”

![Trump on September 15 claimed a security guard was ‘60 seconds from death’ when a large chunk of concrete and steel fell from the ceiling of the Kennedy Center](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/15/17/51/Truth-Social-Kennedy-Center-ceiling-falling.png)

Cooper had heard arguments from both parties about the new changes last month, with Beatty’s legal team arguing that the board is illegally flouting his order against sticking Trump’s name on the building with a “breathtaking act of defiance.”

A draft resolution states that the venue “is in a dire financial position” and has “exhausted its fiscal resources” that could force its imminent closure — unless Trump can “step in and raise the necessary funds to keep the Center from bankruptcy during its much needed renovation.”

The board had been set to review a new list of 10 ways to put Trump’s name on the building, but the item was abruptly removed from Tuesday’s agenda, which instead focuses solely on the building’s closure.

“The law is extremely clear: The John F. Kennedy Center is named for President Kennedy — and no one else,” Rep. Beatty said in a statement shared with *The Independent* after Tuesday’s ruling.

“But President Trump is holding the Kennedy Center hostage unless he can put his name on this sacred memorial. The Court just rejected this proposal for the second time. This unlawfulness is as breathtaking as it is wrong, and it needs to stop,” she added.

Her counsel Norm Eisen said “legal sanity has once again prevailed over Trump’s vanity.”

“This is a victory for the memory of President Kennedy and for our nation,” he said.
