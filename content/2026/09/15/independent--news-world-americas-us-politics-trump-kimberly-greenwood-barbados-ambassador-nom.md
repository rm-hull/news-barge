President Donald Trump on Monday nominated Kimberly Greenwood, the wife of "God Bless the USA" singer Lee Greenwood, to serve as the U.S. ambassador to Barbados and the Eastern Caribbean.

Kimberly Greenwood’s name was included in a broader roster of diplomatic nominees that the Trump administration formally transmitted to the U.S. Senate for confirmation.

After the announcement later on Monday, Kimberly Greenwood took to Instagram to share a photograph of herself, her husband, and Trump, while publicly thanking the president for putting her forward for the position.

In her online statement, she wrote: "Mr. President, thank you for believing in me and entrusting me with this extraordinary opportunity to serve,"

Her husband, Lee Greenwood, has long been recognized as one of Trump’s favorite musical artists. His signature patriotic track has served as Trump's chosen walk-on music at political gatherings for years. In addition to political appearances, Trump has entered into commercial endeavors alongside Lee Greenwood, collaborating to market a $59.99 "God Bless the USA Bible,".

In a separate post published to Instagram to praise his wife following her diplomatic selection, Lee Greenwood pointed out that she had worked alongside Trump for a period of 18 years. This professional relationship took place when the president owned the Miss Universe Organization, during which Kimberly Greenwood served as a director for state-level beauty pageants.

If the Senate confirms her appointment, Greenwood’s duties will require her to serve simultaneously as U.S. ambassador to Barbados and, without extra pay, as ambassador to several other Caribbean nations. These countries include St. Kitts and Nevis, St. Lucia, Antigua and Barbuda, Dominica, Grenada, alongside St. Vincent and the Grenadines.

Handing out sought-after ambassadorial posts to key political supporters and financial campaign donors is a long-standing routine embraced by presidents from both major American political parties.

For example, Democratic President Joe Biden appointed Roger Nyhus, a prominent donor to the Democratic Party and Seattle-based business leader, to the same combined diplomatic role that Trump has now designated for Kimberly Greenwood.

Generally, political appointees drawn from the donor and supporter ranks exercise minimal influence over major foreign policy decisions. However, on occasion, their actions in diplomatic posts have created political headaches for the administration in power.

In addition to the Caribbean posting, Trump also announced Monday the nomination of outgoing Rep. Wesley Hunt, R-Texas, to serve as his primary diplomatic envoy to Riyadh.

Hunt is currently nearing the end of his second term representing a district located in the Houston region. In the March 2026 Texas Republican primary for the U.S. Senate, he placed third behind candidates Ken Paxton and John Cornyn.

If confirmed by the Senate, Hunt will take office in Riyadh during a fraught period in the Middle East. The regional landscape is currently marked by the ongoing war involving the U.S., Israel, and Iran, which continues with no clear resolution, as well as escalating military clashes between Saudi Arabia and Iran-backed Houthi rebels based in Yemen.

Before embarking on a career in politics, Hunt served as an officer in the U.S. Army, serving as a diplomatic liaison stationed in Saudi Arabia.

Posting on the social media platform X, Hunt stated that he understood "the strategic importance of the partnership between the United States and Saudi Arabia and the profound responsibility of this position."

The White House further announced a series of additional nominees on Monday to fill existing ambassadorial vacancies in Haiti, Somalia, Djibouti, and Suriname.

Looking back at political appointees from Trump’s first term, several donor envoys made headlines. Gordon Sondland, a hotel executive who donated $1 million to the inaugural committee, was appointed as chief envoy to the European Union. Sondland later delivered critical testimony against Trump during his initial impeachment proceedings, which focused on allegations that Trump sought assistance from Ukrainian officials to undermine Biden ahead of the 2020 presidential election. Trump subsequently removed Sondland from his post.

In another instance, Jeffrey Ross Gunter, a donor turned diplomatic envoy, surprised residents in the relatively low-crime city of Reykjavik, Iceland, by requesting to retain armed bodyguards.

Meanwhile in Britain, Ambassador Robert "Woody" Johnson was confronted with allegations that he attempted to influence officials to move golf’s British Open to a Scottish resort owned by Trump, alongside facing accusations of making racist and sexist remarks.
