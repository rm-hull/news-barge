More than 300 motorcyclists are set to ride in convoy through the Tyne Tunnel as part of a charity fundraiser supporting life-saving first aid services across the North East.

The North East Bikers Tyne Tunnel Run, taking place on Sunday, September 20, will raise funds for St John Ambulance, with all proceeds going toward supporting the charity’s work in the region.

Riders will gather at the A1 Washington North Bound Service Station before setting off toward Cramlington and then down the A19 to regroup at the Tyne Tunnel.

TT2, the company that operates the tunnel, will pause traffic to allow the full convoy to pass through together.

Lynn Horrocks CStJ, County Commissioner, Northumbria & Durham, said: "I am looking forward to this event.

"It promises to be loud and green, shining a spotlight on SJA’s work, especially during the Save a Life September campaign.

"The fantastic North East Bikers are not only raising awareness but also helping to fund our volunteers’ invaluable work in communities.

"Their commitment makes a real difference, and I am deeply grateful for their efforts."

The ride will finish at the Last Post Memorial Bar in Thornaby, where St John Ambulance volunteers will be on hand to meet the riders and offer free first aid demonstrations.

The charity’s community support vehicle, Marnie, which was funded by a donation from TT2, will also take part in the event.

Bertie Badger, the organisation’s mascot, will travel in the vehicle rather than on a motorcycle.

Lynn Horrocks CStJ, county commissioner for Northumbria and Durham, also thanked TT2 for its help in making the event possible.

Ms Horrocks said: "I’d like to thank TT2 for all their help and logistical support in organising for the bikes to travel through the Tyne Tunnel.

Riders are asked to contribute £10 to take part in the event, which includes a free T-shirt and supporter patch.
