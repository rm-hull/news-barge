The opposition has accused the Berlin Senate of not taking the consequences of the cyberattack seriously enough. “This IT disaster, probably the most consequential Germany has seen in a long time, really needs to be investigated with the utmost seriousness,” demanded Tobias Schulze, leader of the Left parliamentary group, at the final session of the state parliament before the House of Representatives election.

Stefan Ziller of the Greens said the theft of more than five terabytes of data should have been noticed earlier. The IT security problems had been known, but too little had been done in recent years. “We have been grossly negligent in simply relying on hope,” Ziller said. There was no time to lose in investing more in IT security. “The state network finally needs modern, unified monitoring.”

### AfD to Seek Committee of Inquiry into Cyberattack

AfD MP Marc Vallendar described the cyberattack as a “worst-case scenario for data protection”. “If this happened to a private company, it would face fines.” According to Vallendar, a systemic problem had allowed the data theft to go unnoticed for a week. Vallendar announced that the AfD would seek to establish a committee of inquiry to investigate the incident.

Left parliamentary group leader Schulze said that the cybercriminals had been able to access servers belonging to the two affected Senate departments for days. “That is the real scandal. There were no firewalls, nothing stopped the hackers,” Schulze said. The cybercriminals have since made huge amounts of data available on the dark web.

“Just imagine: employees’ personal passwords and mobile phone numbers have been published online. Job application documents, health certificates and employment contracts are online, as are personnel files and written warnings,” said the Left parliamentary group leader. But files concerning the security of public authorities had also been stolen.

“Plans for the locking systems of highly protected critical infrastructure buildings are online. There are construction plans for prisons online, as well as vulnerability analyses of our electricity and water networks,” Schulze said. “You don’t even want to imagine it.”

### Left Parliamentary Group Leader: Terrorists and Saboteurs Are Rubbing Their Hands

“Anyone planning attacks, sabotage or espionage now gets everything they need delivered straight to them,” Schulze warned. “Intelligence services, terrorists, neo-Nazis, saboteurs and organised crime are already rubbing their hands,” the Left politician said.

“We need segmented and encrypted networks and servers,” Schulze demanded. Centralising IT in Berlin must become a priority. The Left and the Greens are also calling for an expert panel to be appointed by October 1 to present a proposal for overhauling Berlin’s IT architecture.

The multi-day cyberattack on parts of the Berlin administration’s data network came to light on August 14. The two Senate departments responsible for construction and transport were affected. The hacker group Rhysida attempted to extort the state of Berlin, demanding 30 bitcoins, equivalent to around two million euros. The Senate did not pay. After their ultimatum expired, the cybercriminals published a large proportion of the stolen data sets.
