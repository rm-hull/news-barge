A surprise fireworks display at Steeton Hall has sparked upset in the community after a 10-minute show was staged during a wedding celebration on Saturday, September 12.

Neither hotel staff nor guests were informed in advance, and the display was reportedly organised by a friend of one of the bridal party.

A member of the Steeton Hall management team said: "If you're going to do it, you really do need to let us know because we need to let the community know.

### "I was really upset"

"I logged straight into Facebook to apologise, we’ve got a nursing home nearby.

"I was really upset, I just wished I’d been told."

The display began around 9pm after the DJ asked guests to gather outside for what was described as a "big photoshoot."

Management said the couple had initially requested fireworks but later cancelled the plan.

Steeton Hall does allow fireworks, but have a procedure in place to make sure the necessary health and safety measures are met, they would also put a notice out on Facebook apologising for any inconvenience.

### 'We weren't aware of it'

The hotel posted an apology on social media shortly after the incident, which read: "Incredibly huge apologies from Steeton hall hotel, somebody has surprised our brides tonight with a firework show to celebrate, we weren’t aware of it we are so sorry to all those affected by the noise, we weren’t informed about it."

The hall received several complaints, including from a man claiming his daughter was autistic and epileptic, and had a fit as a result of the display.

He then reportedly said "I will never come in again, I will never support you."

She described the situation as "heartbreaking" and emphasised the importance of local support for the business.

### Social Media Response

While comments on the Facebook post were largely supportive, some raised concerns about animal welfare and the lack of notification.

Sara Fairlamb said: "How does someone set up a surprise firework display on your grounds without asking permission or without any staff noticing?

"Pretty sure commercial premises need liability insurance for fireworks as the standard policies don’t cover it.

"If anyone had been injured or a fire started you would have been held responsible.

"Really poor from whoever organised it not to have at least asked permission."

   

*(Image: Northern Powergrid)*

### Concern for animal welfare

Sandra Tracey Waite commented: “It is near farmland. All animals are distressed by fireworks. Thank you for your apology it's not your fault. Clear contracts need to be in place for future events”

The incident comes amid ongoing discussions about anti-social firework use in the wider Bradford area.

In June, Bradford Council launched a "Bang Out of Order Campaign" to address the misuse of fireworks following years of complaints about noise, sleep disruption, and distress caused to vulnerable people and pets.

The campaign includes working with local wedding venues, and supporting Government proposals for tougher firework laws and lower noise limits.
