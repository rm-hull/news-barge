The Tyne and Wear Metro has been immortalised in a vibrant new public art project which has transformed a busy North East subway into a colourful gallery celebrating a city's creativity and transport heritage.

Located beneath the 55 Degrees North roundabout, in Newcastle, the 75-metre-long gallery features 39 bespoke pieces by 90 art students from Newcastle College, with four artworks depicting the classic Class 599 Metro trains, which were retired from service in June.

Lynne Dickinson, station delivery manager at Nexus, said: "It’s great to see Metro is part of this new art project.

"The students have done an amazing job.

"Our original trains served the public in North East England for 46 years.

"They became part of everyday life in our region.

"It’s fantastic to see young people taking inspiration from their legacy and bringing them to back to life through the medium of art.

  The Tyne and Wear Metro has been celebrated in a new 75-metre public art gallery beneath Newcastle's 55 Degrees North roundabout 

*(Image: Nexus)*

"We hope that people enjoy going to see this exhibition.

"It’s a great collaboration by our colleagues at NE1, Newcastle City Council and Newcastle College."

The students’ designs draw from the 1960s pop art movement, with Andy Warhol’s bold and colourful style serving as a key influence.

The project, titled "55 Creative - Connecting people from spaces to places," is a joint effort by NE1 and Newcastle City Council to revitalise the subway network beneath the roundabout, previously known as the Swan House roundabout.

This pedestrian underpass connects Newcastle’s main shopping area with the Quayside.

To prepare for the project, 100 volunteers spent 780 hours whitewashing the walls of three subways using 400 litres of paint.

In total, more than 1,500 square metres of subway will be painted and refreshed.

Metro operator Nexus said it underlines how the Metro is at the heart of the communities it serves.
