A paedophile has been jailed for eight years after being found guilty of a string of sex offences against a teenage girl.

Matthew Watson, 26, was sentenced at Newcastle Crown Court on Friday, September 11, after being convicted at a trial there the previous month.

Watson was said to have plied the girl with drugs and alcohol before assaulting her several times at locations across Newcastle.

The girl told trusted people in her life what had happened, and it was later reported to Northumbria Police.

After a complex investigation by Northumbria Police’s Safeguarding Department, Watson was charged in April last year.

A jury at Newcastle Crown Court found him guilty after the trial in August of 11 child sex offences, including sexual activity with a child and causing or inciting a girl to engage in sexual activity.

Watson, of Tewkesbury Road, Newcastle, returned to court on Friday, September 11, and received the eight-year sentence.

He must also register as a sex offender for life and comply with a Sexual Harm Prevention Order (SHPO).

Speaking after the sentencing hearing, Detective Constable Claire Ashurst, who led the investigation, said: “It’s clear that Matthew Watson is a sexual predator who has taken advantage of this vulnerable girl for his own sexual gain.”

Calling the girl a “victim-survivor”, Detective Constable Ashurst said she had shown, “great bravery and dignity through the entire investigation and court process”, which ended with her abuser behind bars.

Det Con Ashurst continued: “The sexual abuse of children is horrific and, as a force, we’ll continue to relentlessly bring those who commit these offences to justice.

“No-one should ever have to experience this type of criminality, but please know if you have, we’re here and we’re ready to listen.”

Anyone who has experienced sexual abuse, whether recently or in the past, can contact police through the ‘Report’ pages on the Northumbria Police website or by direct message on social media.

But anyone unable to report online should call 101, or 999 in an emergency or if a crime is ongoing.

To report a crime anonymously, the independent charity Crimestoppers can be contact by ringing 0800 555 111 or through its website.
