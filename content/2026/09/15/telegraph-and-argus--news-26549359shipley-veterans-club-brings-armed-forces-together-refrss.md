LAUGHTER, stories, and friendly rivalry filled the room as veterans gathered for breakfast.

The Shipley Veterans Breakfast Club, which meets on the second Sunday of each month at The Pride of Shipley, welcomed members of Royal Naval Reserve unit HMS Ceres during its latest gathering.

Mark Sugrue, the club’s founder and a former member of the RAF, said: "The whole idea is very simple. Put veterans and serving personnel around a table, add breakfast and a few brews, and the conversation takes care of itself.

"We were particularly pleased to welcome members of HMS Ceres.

"Keeping that connection between today's Armed Forces and those who served before them is really important to us."

The club offers a relaxed environment for veterans, reservists, serving personnel, and their families from Shipley, Bradford, and the wider region.

Mr Sugrue stated that anyone connected to the Armed Forces is welcome.

He said: "You don't need to join anything, fill in a form or even know anyone.

"If you've served, are serving, are a reservist or part of the wider Armed Forces family, just walk through the door.

"Someone will soon pull up a chair."

The gatherings are made possible through the club's partnership with The Pride of Shipley, whose landlord, Scott Isherwood, said: "We're proud to host the Veterans Breakfast Club and support the Armed Forces community in Shipley.

"There's always a fantastic atmosphere when they're here.

"Plenty of stories, plenty of laughter and usually a bit of friendly rivalry between the Services.

"The door is open and we'd love to see even more veterans come along."

Mr Sugrue encouraged attendees to support the venue in return.

He said: "Local pubs like The Pride of Shipley are an important part of their communities.

"Scott and the team look after us extremely well, so we'd encourage everyone attending to support them in return – preferably by ordering a breakfast."

Veterans Breakfast Clubs are informal, with no membership fees, formal meetings or requirements to wear medals, badges or club clothing.

They provide a space where veterans can connect with others who understand military life without needing to explain it.

The club also hopes its growing relationship with units like HMS Ceres will help attract younger veterans and reservists.

All veterans, reservists, serving personnel, and members of the wider Armed Forces community are welcome to attend.
