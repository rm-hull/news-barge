Plans to turn a house in a Consett cul-de-sac into a children's home have been refused, after attracting dozens of objections from residents.

An application, which was thrown out by Durham County Council on September 7, sought permission to change a detached property at Stuart Court into a children's home.

It would be for up to four children aged between seven and 18.

Council documents show 35 objections were submitted, with residents raising concerns about the suitability of the location, the scale of the proposal and its impact on the surrounding neighbourhood.

One resident argued that the development would be "out of keeping with the quiet residential nature of the cul-de-sac".

There were also concerns that the proposal could lead to increased traffic and parking pressures.

Another objector questioned the need for a four-bed children's home, stating: "The council's own strategy identifies a need for smaller homes.

"This proposal does not appear to meet that need."

Concerns were also raised about the concentration of children's homes in the wider area, with one resident claiming there was "already a significant number of similar facilities nearby".

Several objections focused on safeguarding and support arrangements, with residents saying there was insufficient information about how young people would be matched to the home and managed on site.

Planning officers ultimately agreed that key information was missing.

In a report recommending refusal, officers noted that Durham County Council's Children and Young People's Services team objected to the application.

The service said the proposed four-bed model did not align with the authority's identified need for smaller residential settings.

Officers also found there was no evidence to show the development would meet an identified local need and highlighted the absence of a Locality Crime Risk Assessment, which is required to demonstrate that a location is safe and suitable for vulnerable children.

Although highways and environmental health officers raised no objections, planners concluded the scheme failed to comply with County Durham Plan policies.

The refusal notice states the applicant had not demonstrated a need for the development and had failed to show the site would provide a suitable environment for vulnerable young people.
