The demolition date for a much-loved café in a North Yorkshire seaside village has yet to be confirmed.

Sandsend Café, located in Sandsend near Whitby, will be replaced with a larger, modernised venue following damage from a vehicle collision in May 2024.

A company spokesperson said it is hoped that the café will close this winter, but this is yet to be decided.

  


They said: “We hope to have a confirmed date at the end of September.

“This depends on two of our contractors.”

The original café, which has been in operation since 1978, has long been a staple of the Sandsend community and a popular stop for visitors to the North Yorkshire coast.

  Sandsend Café has been serving customers since 1978 

*(Image: North Yorkshire Council)*

It is set to be rebuilt with a new first floor and additional space to improve its kitchen area.

Plans are also in place to increase customer seating, with 140 covers in total – 50 of which will be inside and a further 90 outside.

The plans received consent from North Yorkshire Council’s planning department on Tuesday (June 16).

Speaking on behalf of the company in plans submitted to the council, Saddington Taylor Planning said the ageing building had suffered damage following the incident in 2024.

They added: “Given the construction method used for the building which utilises interlocking timber logs, a Building Surveyor has indicated that the only way to rectify the area of damage would be to take the entire structure down and rebuild it.”

After the replacement café is built, more customers will be able to sit and enjoy sea views year-round - thanks to the structure’s glazed roof.

The business will continue to sell beach toys and trinkets and cater for takeaway services.

The site does not have car park facilities, but the developer said that most customers make their way to the café on foot through the village.
