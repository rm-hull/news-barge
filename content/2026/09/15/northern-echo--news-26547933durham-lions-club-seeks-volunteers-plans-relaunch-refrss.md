A long-established Durham charity which has raised hundreds of thousands of pounds for local good causes over the past 50 years is appealing for new members to help secure its future.

Durham Lions Club, part of Lions Clubs International, may be forced to close if it cannot recruit new members.

The charity is planning a relaunch and will hold an open evening on Monday, September 28, in the hope of attracting new volunteers.

Jackie Robson, acting president of Durham Lions, said: "Unfortunately, old age and ill health has caught up with the members of Durham Lions Club, so we are relaunching the club.

"Durham Lions has been extremely successful over the past 50 years and there is no reason why it cannot continue to be successful.

"We would like to invite anyone who lives, works or studies in Durham, or in the surrounding towns and villages, and who is interested in helping their local community to come to our open evening.

"They can meet our Lions and find out what we do.

"Everyone is welcome."

The club has supported charities including St Cuthbert's Hospice, Willow Burn Hospice, the North East Air Ambulance Service, Learning for Life, and Northumbria Blood Bikes.

Volunteers also take part in litter picks, food bank support, youth schemes, and recycling glasses for use in developing countries.

Durham Lions is part of Lions Clubs International, the world’s largest service organisation. Founded in the USA in 1917, it is now present in more than 208 countries and geographic areas.

Lions Clubs bring together women and men from the age of 18 to serve their communities, meet humanitarian needs, encourage peace and promote international understanding.

The Durham Lions open evening will take place at Alington House, 4 North Bailey, Durham, DH1 3ET, starting at 7pm on Monday, September 28.

More information about Durham Lions is available at www.durhamlions.co.uk or by emailing [secretarydurhamlions@gmail.com.](mailto:secretarydurhamlions@gmail.com.)
