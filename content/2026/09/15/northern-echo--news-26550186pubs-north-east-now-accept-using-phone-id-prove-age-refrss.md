Businesses across the North East can now choose to accept digital proof of age when selling alcohol under new rules.

Customers can now use their phones to prove they are over 18 instead of carrying passports or driving licences.

The Government says the system will make age checks easier while protecting privacy, because customers will only need to confirm that they are over 18 - not provide their name or address.

   

*(Image: Getty)*

The rules cover licensed venues including pubs, restaurants, festivals and concert venues.

However, venues will not be required to accept digital proof of age, and customers can continue using passports, driving licences and other forms of physical ID.

Digital proof-of-age services use certified technology to check that an ID is genuine and belongs to the person using it.

Customers would show staff a QR code to scan or tap their phone against a compatible reader, before sending a confirmation to staff that the customer has passed the age check.

The Government says the system could cut fake or borrowed ID use and speed up checks at busy times.

   

*(Image: PA)*

Digital Government Minister Stephanie Peacock said: "From pubs and restaurants to music venues and festivals, licensed businesses carry out millions of age checks every year.

"This groundbreaking change in the law will mean you do not need to carry a physical ID, nor hand over sensitive personal information such as your name or address just to prove your age, allowing everyone to feel safer on a night out."

Industry leaders welcomed the move as a boost for businesses and customers.

Allen Simpson, chief executive of UKHospitality, said: "The roll-out of the voluntary digital proof of age will provide an easier experience for customers, allowing them to verify their age securely from their phones.

"We look forward to working with the Government to ensure the roll-out of the scheme, with multiple supplier options available, is cost-efficient and not burdensome for the hospitality sector."

   

*(Image: Getty)*

Julian David OBE, chief executive of techUK, added: "We welcome the coming into force of digital identity as a trusted way to prove your age when buying alcohol.

"This is a practical, everyday example of the value that robust digital identity services can bring to consumers, cutting friction and making lives easier, while maintaining high standards of security and privacy."

The change follows a 2024 Government consultation in which 72 per cent of respondents backed digital identities for alcohol age checks.

Ministers say the updated licensing rules will let venues use certified digital proof-of-age services while protecting against underage sales.

The licensing changes apply across England and Wales, although the Government expects it will take time for the technology to become widely adopted.

Customers will need to authenticate themselves each time they use a digital ID, after which the service can generate a token such as a QR code that is valid for about 30 seconds.
