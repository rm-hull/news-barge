Questions have been raised over the future of a long-awaited supermarket development in Darlington after work on the site appeared to grind to a halt.

The former Elm Ridge Garden Centre site, on the corner of Coniscliffe Road and Carmel Road South, has been earmarked for a new Sainsbury's Local and office space following the popular business's closure in 2023.

Demolition has taken place and planning permission was granted, but the development has stalled, fuelling residents' speculation about the delay.


One local source has claimed the delay could be linked to a historic Quaker covenant affecting the land.

The source claimed an old covenant bans alcohol sales on the site, which was once part of the gardens of two Pease family homes built in the 1860s.

They also claimed to have seen similar clauses in deeds relating to a number of properties along Coniscliffe Road.

The Pease family played a significant role in Darlington's Quaker heritage, with many properties and developments in the town linked to the influential family during the 19th century.

Darlington Borough Council has confirmed work cannot continue until an update is received from the developer but said any issues relating to covenants would fall outside the planning process.

  Work has started on the new Sainsbury's store which has since halted. 

*(Image: Peter Reimann)*

A council spokesperson said: “We continue to work with the developer and are awaiting an update before further work can commence.

“Any potential issues relating to possible covenants would be a civil matter, dealt with outside of the planning or licensing processes.”

The site has been vacant since Elm Ridge Garden Centre closed in March 2023 after decades of trading.

Demolition began later that year for a mixed-use development with a Sainsbury's Local on the ground floor and offices above.

Plans submitted by developer Ward Estates included 14 offices, four meeting rooms and a 44-space car park.

*(Image: Peter Reimann)*

Supporters said the scheme would revive the vacant site, while objectors warned about traffic and another convenience store in the area.

The proposal was approved by Darlington Borough Council's planning committee in October 2024.

The cause of the delay and a possible restart date have not been confirmed.

The Northern Echo has asked the developer and Sainsbury's for comment.

The proposed store would have a 260-square-metre net sales area, less than half the former garden centre's 870-square-metre retail floor space.
