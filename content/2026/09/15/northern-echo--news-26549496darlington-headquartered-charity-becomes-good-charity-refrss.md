A national social care charity with an 80-year history in Darlington has unveiled a new name as it sets out plans to expand its impact while keeping the town at the heart of its operations.

Saint John of God Hospitaller Services (SJOG) will now be known as The Do Good Charity.

  Front row, from left, Nicola Wilkin, Kay King, Jamie Hope. Back row, from left chief executive Paul Bott and Omolade Saibu, support worker in Darlington 

*(Image: Guzelian Pictures)*

Known as one of the UK’s largest and longest-standing social care charities, it has operated out of its Darlington headquarters since 1946.

Paul Bott, chief executive of The Do Good Charity, said: "Since 1946, Darlington has been the proud anchor for our national work.

"This town has headquartered a charity that impacts tens of thousands of lives across the UK."

  Paul Bott, chief executive officer of The Do Good Charity 

*(Image: Guzelian Pictures)*

The rebrand is part of a wider strategy that aims to raise the charity’s national profile while remaining rooted in the North East.

Despite supporting more than 1,800 people every month across 60 UK communities, employing 562 staff, and reaching more than 97,000 people through digital services in 2025, many remain unaware of its work.

Mr Bott said: "The Do Good Charity puts our purpose into plain English and, importantly, it is also an invitation for others to 'Do Good' in their community.

  Charity staff, from left, James Lindley, Malcolm Thompson, Charlotte Waistell, Amy Griffiths, Jodie Brodie-Myers. People the charity supports, from leftNeville Fill, Damian Rodgers, Emma Catterson 

*(Image: Guzelian Pictures)*

"We’ve been quietly doing good since 1880, and we now want more people, communities, charities, supporters and partners to work with us so that together we can do more good."

The charity’s UK roots date back to 1880 in North Yorkshire.

It was founded by St John of God, a Portuguese soldier-turned-healthcare worker who established a global order that now operates in 54 countries and supports seven million people each year.

The new name also connects with the order’s identity in Italy, where it is known as Fatebenefratelli, or "the Do Good Brothers."

In Darlington, The Do Good Charity operates services including Lindisfarne Court in Haughton-le-Skerne and Rockliffe Court in Hurworth, which together offer 18 supported-living homes for people with physical and sensory disabilities.

Its work extends across the Tees Valley with disability and autism support services in Middlesbrough, Billingham and Stockton.

This includes Dalby View in Coulby Newham and specialist autism services at Fairfields and The Old Vicarage in Stockton.

The charity moved into its current headquarters on Barnard Street in 2025.

Its core values—hospitality, compassion and respect—will remain unchanged, and it will continue to support people of all faiths and none.

Mr Bott said: "We're not leaving behind the values that made us.

"Doing good has been at the heart of this organisation for generations."

For the fifth consecutive year, all of the charity’s CQC-regulated services were rated Good or Outstanding.

The rebrand is part of a new strategy to 2030, built around three commitments: Do Good, Do More Good and Do It Well.

The Do Good Charity plans to build on its legacy and invite more individuals, communities and organisations to join its mission.
