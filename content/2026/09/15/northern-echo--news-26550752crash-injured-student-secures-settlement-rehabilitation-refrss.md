A college student who suffered life-changing brain and spinal injuries in a crash has secured a settlement to fund his rehabilitation.

Joshua Kay-Shaw, from Ripon, was seriously injured when the car he was travelling in lost control and crashed into another vehicle.

The 18-year-old York College student was travelling to an accounting exam when the crash happened on Marston Lane, York, in December 2023.

Joshua was airlifted to hospital with a fractured breastbone, four broken ribs, a punctured lung and liver and spleen injuries.

He was placed in an induced coma for four days.

*(Image: FAMILY)*

Joshua underwent surgery for traumatic brain and spinal cord injuries and spent four months in hospital.

After initially losing all useful movement in his legs, Joshua can now walk short distances with crutches and a specialist splint but uses a wheelchair for longer journeys.

Joshua and his family instructed serious injury lawyers at Irwin Mitchell to help him access specialist rehabilitation and rebuild his life.

At the time, Joshua was an accounting apprentice at his grandad’s practice while studying for his qualification.

His injuries stopped him from returning to his apprenticeship or completing his qualification.

After leaving hospital, Joshua suffered severe pain in his legs, back and hips, along with memory and concentration problems, fatigue and disrupted sleep.

He said: "My last memory is leaving for my exam and being wished good luck. I have a vague memory of my mum telling me that I was in hospital and that I had been in an accident. I now realise that I was in a coma when I heard this and was not fully awake.

"When I first came out of the coma, it was all a blur. My first clear memory was of all my family coming to see me on Christmas Day. My mum and stepdad came to see me most days, and a couple of my friends.

  Joshua, who also suffered a fractured breastbone, four fractured ribs, a punctured lung and injuries to his liver and spleen, was airlifted to hospital 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

"After I left hospital, I was okay at using a wheelchair and could move around, but the wheelchair I’d been provided with was heavy, uncomfortable and caused me pain."

Joshua spent around four months at STEPS Rehabilitation Centre in Sheffield, where he regained independence in many areas of daily life.

Funding secured through Irwin Mitchell enabled Joshua to buy a lightweight specialist wheelchair, greatly improving his mobility.

Joshua lived with his grandparents when the accident happened, and they adapted their home to meet his needs, including a specialist wet room.

In late 2025, he moved into a rental property with his new girlfriend.

He added: "While therapy has helped, I still experience periods of confusion. My short-term memory has been really bad, and I find it difficult to concentrate and process information.

"One positive that's come out of the accident is that I'm much more outgoing. I think it's because I've come to realise that there is more to life and my attitude has changed.

"I've also been trying to make sure I spend time with my friends, and I've been out in their cars with them a few times.

“I know I still face many challenges, but I’m determined to try and regain more of my independence and not to be defined by what happened.

"I hope by sharing what happened to me it will make other people take care on the roads, especially young people. It's not just your life you're taking into your own hands when you get on the road; it's also the lives of other drivers, passengers and pedestrians."
