Nigel Farage said Reform has done “nothing wrong” following allegations they breached political donation rules by having polling paid for by an overseas company.

Senior Reform UK officials James Orr and Dan Jukes have stepped down and the party has launched an investigation after a report broadcast by Channel 4 News claimed Reform took funding from a US firm for three polls it commissioned earlier this year costing £32,500.

The purported donors were in fact undercover reporters from investigative group Verbatim posing as an American financier and his UK-based son.

Mr Jukes was filmed meeting a man posing as the British-based son of a rich American businessman and was heard appearing to suggest a way to get around electoral law to secure a foreign donation of £500,000 for Reform UK, according to undercover filming broadcast by Channel 4.

Mr Farage told LBC that everything was “above board”, insisting that "no illegal transaction occurred".

Meanwhile, Mr Farage is to announce that he will make Parliament “sit day and night” until all legislation is passed if Reform UK wins the next general election.

## 'The media are coming for us', Lee Anderson says

Reform UK’s party chairman Lee Anderson has claimed “the media are coming for us” amid more allegations the party breached political donation rules.

Speaking to delegates at Reform’s party conference in Birmingham, he said: “Have you noticed that the media are coming for us? Has anybody noticed that?

“They came for Nigel (Farage) and they’ve failed. They came for Richard (Tice) and they’ve failed.

“And now they’re coming for the rest of us. But we will not be defeated. We will stick together.”

## Anderson: 'We will not be defeated by the media'

Lee Anderson has taken a swipe at the press following Channel 4’s donations sting.

He seemingly hinted to the undercover investigation, telling his party’s conference that the media were “coming for the rest of us”.

“Have you noticed that the media are coming for us? Has anybody noticed that?

“They came for Nigel and they failed, they came for Richard and they failed, and now they’re coming for the rest of us,” he said.

“We will not be defeated. We will stick together and we will beat them.”

## Reform media hostility increases as Zia Yusuf pulls out of event

Reform’s home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf has pulled out of a Spectator fringe event following complaints of unfair coverage.

His interview with former cabinet minister turned Spectator editor Michael Gove was due to be one of the highlights of the conference fringe in Birmingham.

The decision comes amid growing tensions between Reform and the media over recent coverage of scandals involving senior party members including Nigel Farage.

Already a number of journalists had been banned from entering the conference.

## Reform wants to boot out ‘money-grabbing’ government

Reform wants to boot out the “money-grabbing” Labour government at the next election, it has said.

‘We are going to boot out this incompetent, money grabbing, dystopian Labour government,” David Bull, the party’s former deputy leader said.

“Not only must we win the general election, we must also be prepared to hit the ground running in that first 100 days.”

## Zia Yusuf pulls out of fringe event following complaints of unfair coverage

Zia Yusuf has reportedly pulled out of a Spectator fringe event at the Reform conference following complaints of unfair coverage, accoding to Daily Mail’s political editor.

The home affairs spokesman was due to sit down with editor Michael Gove at 3pm today.

In what was supposed to be one of the highlights of the conference, the pair were going to discuss “Reform's ambitions for government”.

## Farage says 'someone missing' from Reform conference in tribute to Ann Widdecombe

Nigel Farage has kicked off his conference speech with a tribute to the late Ann Widdecombe.

“Someone is missing,” he said. “The irrepressible, unique, totally extraordinary Ann Widdecombe is missing.

“We thought the only appropriate way to start this conference was to pay tribute to Ann.

“She was, in my opinion, the best known female politician in Britain since Margaret Thatcher.

“Ann Widdecombe didn’t need autocue, she didn’t need notes, she took to the stage with a microphone and she bashed out the message.

“She said things not to court short-term popularity, she said things that she believed truly and deeply in her heart.”

He also spoke about her time on Big Brother, Strictly Come Dancing, and her ability to laugh at herself.

“Her political association with me started in 2019,” he added.

## Pollster claims Reform have not peaked and could get 40 per cent of vote

More in Common director Luke Tryl has addressed a fringe meeting at the Reform conference in Birmingham and offered a little hope to the embattled party.

According to Mr Tryl there are “a sizeable amount of Reform considerers” and if all of them voted Reform they could get “between 37 and 40 per cent in a general election.”

However, he noted that the party is in a worse state than a year ago because the vote on the right “has fractured” and is “no longer dominated by Reform.”

“Some have gone to Restore Britain and some back to the Conservatives,” he noted.

His comments reflected a fall by about 10 points in the polls from a year ago with Mr Farage now under investigation over donations.

However, he warned that a deal with Rupert Lowe’s restore Britain or Kemi Badenoch’s Conservatives would see a fall in the Reform vote.

He warned: “If they do a deal with Restore they lose traditional Conservative voters but if they do a deal with the Conservatives they lose 2024 Reform voters.”

He also questioned whether they have peaked as a party despite the slide in the polls.

“My answer is that a better way to look at this is to look at the core drivers support for Reform, which is lack of trust in politics, which is a sense that those positions of power do not understand how the social contract works. None of those fundamentals have gone away.”
