!["Putin growing more reckless as his inner circle shrinks, warn UK security insiders," reads the headline on the front page of the i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/8252/live/a5471670-a7d8-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

Nigel Farage "caught on camera in foreign donations sting" features on the i Paper's front page. It writes: "Reform UK leader filmed by undercover reporters allegedly welcoming party donations that may breach rules." Elsewhere, Russian President Vladimir Putin is "growing more reckless" according to UK security insiders who tell the paper he "is speaking to a small inner circle and none of the more sensible voices are still there". It has sparked fears that Putin "might 'last out' against Britain or Nato allies", according to the paper.

!["Argentina threat to Falklands oil fields," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2048/cpsprodpb/0f5d/live/348648b0-a7de-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

"Argentina threat to Falklands oil fields" is the Daily Telegraph's headline, reporting that Argentine President Javier Milei "plan for sanctions on British firm" is "set to trigger new row over future of islands". Writing ahead of Milei's address, the paper understands "he is preparing sanctions on offshore oil drilling in the region" amid "increasing speculation that Buenos Aires and Washington are discussing a deal to give US access to the oil". The islands are "British territory but Argentina lays claim to them", the paper explains, with the UK going to war in 1982 due to tensions over the islands.

!["Trump: I won't help Britain if Argentina invades Falklands," reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/943/cpsprodpb/c3ae/live/3d23f120-a7de-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

The Independent continues by quoting US President Donald Trump, who has told GB News: "I won't help Britain if Argentina invades Falklands". He cites frustrations with the UK over the Iran war, saying "your country was not there to help me".

!["Thought this summer was a scorcher? Next year's going to be... Hot as EL," reads the headline on the front page of the Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/e558/live/b758f130-a7d8-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

"Thought this summer was a scorcher?" asks Metro, adding: "Next year's going to be... hot as EL" (Niño, that is). "El Niño tipped to blow apart temperature records," is the headline warning from UN chief António Guterres, whom the paper quotes as saying: "El Niño is being supersized before our eyes. The science leaves no room for doubt - the planet is in uncharted waters, and those waters are heating up." The paper also embeds satellite images showing "three huge storms fuelled by El Niño moving across the Pacific".

!["Britons warned to get ready for biggest El Niño in a millennium," reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/77a0/live/387d9a90-a7de-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

"Britons warned to get ready for biggest El Niño in a millennium," is the Guardian's take on the UN warnings. It cites Britain's national emergencies ministerm, Angela Eagle, who advises "storing food and water in case of winter power cuts". Elsewhere, the paper pays tribute to Gloria Steinem, a "trailblazing feminist campaigner", after she died aged 92.

!["Europe faces winter with lowest ever gas reserves as Gulf-led shortages bite," reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2048/cpsprodpb/7278/live/9ebf0790-a7d8-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

"Europe faces winter with lowest ever gas reserves" is the dire warning splashed on the Financial Times' front page due to "Gulf-led shortages". The paper says time is "running out to fill shortages", which could leave European countries "without a buffer" in the event of a shock – listing off scenarios like "disruptions to liquefied natural gas supplies, a very cold winter, or low power production from renewables". The paper also carries a story that "Business tied to US citizen and Chelsea owner [Todd] Boehly gives £75,000 to Reform" UK.

!["Army halts training exercises to save money," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/2e64/live/b7475640-a7de-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

Military sources inform the Times: "Army halts training exercises to save money." They explain that "troops were told to 'cease activity' with immediate effect after commanding officers received a directive to halt 'collective training' unless their units were about to be deployed". The paper continues: "Ministry of Defence "accused of 'national dereliction of duty'."

!["Kemi: We must act now before it's too late," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/d4c8/live/af35cff0-a7d8-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

"Tory leader calls for defence spending to rise every year as Russian threat grows" carries the Daily Express, summarising Kemi Badenoch's intervention: "We must act now before it's too late".

!["Met chief stunned by release of serial criminals," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/6d70/live/2ae3b360-a7de-11f1-aed2-8d6da8d75094.jpg)

The Daily Mail says the head of Scotland Yard is "stunned by release of serial criminals". Met Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley says he is "flabbergasted" that "serious knife and firearm offenders" are freed on bail "again and again".

!["Reform: Back Britain... with our Chinese tat. Farage's No2 slates foreign imports while party flogs them on merch stall," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/f0e8/live/4079d2e0-a7de-11f1-a291-b542ee92de7c.jpg)

The Daily Mirror says Reform UK "are accused of hypocrisy for championing British industry while their conference merchandise stalls sell tat made in China". It quotes criticism from Liberal Democrat MP Max Wilkinson.

!["The great Guinness robbery," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1680/cpsprodpb/3f30/live/abafdd80-a7d8-11f1-b109-879e35c24276.jpg)

"The great Guinness robbery" is the Daily Star's top story, headlining on "truck gang steals 70k pints". The paper writes: "Someone is gonna wake up with a hell of a hangover this morning after two lorry trailers with 800 barrels of Guinness were nicked."

!["Exclusive: Diddly squat riad, EWEDUNNIT?" reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/0e13/live/46e0c530-a7de-11f1-b109-879e35c24276.jpg)

Finally, the Sun goes for a classic "Ewedunnit?" – reporting: "Furious Jeremy Clarkson last night revealed 11 sheep had been stolen from his farm." Police are investigating "the suspected late-night raid at Diddly Squat in Oxfordshire," the paper explains. The TV host is pictured cradling one of his lambs, with the 66-year-old's words for the "rustlers" printed: "put them back".
