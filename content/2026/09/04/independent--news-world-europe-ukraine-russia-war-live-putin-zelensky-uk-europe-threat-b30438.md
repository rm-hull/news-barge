Russian drones hit a Coca-Cola plant outside Kyiv on Thursday, president Volodymyr Zelensky said.

Officials said there were no injuries in the attack, which took place near the town of Brovary, east of the capital.

“Today, the Russians have hit a Coca-Cola plant with their drone. Coca-Cola is such an enemy that they set it on fire with their Shaheds,” the Ukrainianleader said in his nightly video address, referring to Iranian-designed drones.

“It's a clear Russian signal to America. The Russians cannot be unaware that this is an American facility.”

Andy Hunder, president of the American chamber of commerce told Reuters: “The company is working with the relevant authorities to assess the situation. Ensuring the safety and well-being of people remains top priority.”

Meanwhile, Vladimir Putin dismissed Russia’s ballooning deficit as “not a critical risk” to the economy despite growing alarm over the state of public finances due to the war.

"Considering that we have one of the lowest levels of national debt in the world, there is nothing critical here," Putin said at an economic forum in the Russian far eastern city of Vladivostok.

The Russian economy slowed sharply last year and the government has been forced to raise taxes and increase borrowing to finance Moscow’s war effort.

## Russia to close German cultural centres in retaliation

Russia will close German cultural centres in Moscow, St Petersburg and Yekaterinburg, foreign minister Sergei Lavrov said on Thursday, in retaliation for Germany’s decision to shut Russian diplomatic and cultural facilities.

Lavrov said Berlin “could not have failed to understand” that closing the Russian House cultural centre in Berlin would mark the end of Germany’s cultural presence in Russia.

The move comes amid escalating tensions between Moscow and Berlin over alleged Russian sabotage activities. Germany has accused Russia of a hybrid campaign targeting the country and said it had become a “daily target” of the Kremlin.

Berlin blamed Moscow for an attempted drone attack at Leipzig/Halle Airport on 4 August. An explosives-laden drone was found near a Ukrainian aircraft at the major freight hub and was later defused.

Separately, Bavarian police said on Thursday that incendiary devices had been thrown at a construction site near a building in Munich housing several companies, including firms from the defence sector. A brief fire caused no significant damage. Two Bulgarian nationals were arrested, although police have not confirmed their suspected involvement.

## Huge fire at oil depot in Russia's Sochi after Ukrainian strike, local authorities say

A Ukrainian drone strike caused a fire at an oil depot in the Russian Black Sea port city of Sochi, authorities in the Krasnodar region where the city is located said on the Telegram messaging app.

Videos showed a huge ball of fire and smoke emanating from the depot as residents shot video from a nearby high-rise building.

## US to seek back payment from Europe for Ukraine aid, Trump says

President Donald Trump on Thursday said he would ask European nations to pay back the United States for military aid and munitions previously sent to Ukraine.

"We are taking them for ourselves, the U.S.A., rather than selling them to others, but the sales to Allies will soon again, begin," Trump wrote.

"Hundreds of Billions of Dollars was given to Ukraine and Nato, free of charge, that Europe would have paid for — If they were only asked, but we will be asking for that money, though somewhat belatedly!" he added, blaming the administration of his Democratic predecessor Joe Biden.

## Russia hits energy production sites, Ukraine's Naftogaz says

Russia attacked Ukraine's Ukrnafta production sites, the country's state oil and gas company Naftogaz said.

The attack caused significant damage to equipment, it added in the statement, which did not give a location.

## India ready to help end Russia-Ukraine war, says foreign minister on visit to Kyiv

India is ready to help bring an end to the Russia-Ukraine war, the country’s external affairs minister S Jaishankar said on Thursday during his visit to Kyiv.

“If India can be of any help, in any manner, we are there,” he said, standing alongside his Ukrainian counterpart Andrii Sybiha, Reuters reported.

He is on a three-day visit to Ukraine and Poland from 3 to 5 September.

## Zelensky says Ukraine expects meeting with US negotiators in coming days

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Thursday that U.S. negotiators trying to broker a possible solution to the 4 1/2 year-old war with Russia were due to visit both Ukraine and Russia soon.

"There are preliminary dates. We expect to see representatives of the U.S. president here in Kyiv," Zelensky said in his nightly video address.

"There will be a meeting with them in Moscow and in Kyiv. We are expecting a meeting with them in the coming days. It is very important."

## Russian Black Sea strikes throw cold water on hopes for peace

Russian forces have struck two cargo vessels carrying weapons and military equipment near Odesa on Thursday, according to statements from Moscow’s defence ministry to state news agencies.

The ministry stated that high-precision strikes targeted the vessels alongside nearby port infrastructure and military warehouses.

Ukrainian officials have not yet confirmed the extent of the damage, though regional authorities reported that Russian air raids wounded 17 people in the Odesa region overnight.

The reported strikes throw cold water on earlier diplomatic signals from both Moscow and Kyiv hinting at renewed prospects for peace negotiations.

Just hours prior, both Russian president Vladimir Putin, and Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha had expressed cautious optimism regarding diplomatic engagement.
