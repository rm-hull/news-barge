A £2.7 million investment has delivered major improvements to almost every believe housing home in a County Durham village.

Believe Housing, one of the largest not-for-profit housing associations in the North East, upgraded 70 properties in High Pittington through a comprehensive programme of internal and external works.

David Taylor, assistant director of assets at Believe Housing, said: "We understand that this type of large-scale improvement programme can cause disruption while work is taking place.

"That's why we plan these programmes carefully, carrying out as much of the necessary work as possible at the same time.

"While that can be challenging for customers in the short term, it helps avoid several separate rounds of work in future."

The two-and-a-half-year project, delivered in phases, included new kitchens, bathrooms, central heating systems, rewiring, insulation upgrades, and external enhancements such as new roofs, solar panels, and smart render to replace pebble dash.

The homes are now more energy-efficient and better suited to meet the long-term needs of residents.

The works were managed by Believe Housing and delivered by Sunter Limited, with around 60 to 70 operatives involved.

The scheme also provided hands-on experience for apprentices and helped Believe Housing staff gain new qualifications.

Mr Taylor said: "This was a major investment in High Pittington, and we thank customers and residents for their patience and support throughout the programme.

"We're also pleased the work supported a local family-owned business and helped create opportunities for people to develop their skills and careers in construction."

The investment also delivered broader economic and employment benefits, supporting a regional contractor and workforce, creating jobs, and enabling skills development.

Emily Axon, a project surveyor with Believe Housing, completed a Level 3 qualification and a Higher National Diploma (HND) during the programme.

Ms Axon said: "It was a great opportunity to change roles and learn while studying and gaining hands-on experience.

"I've really enjoyed it, and my background working with customers has definitely helped me in this role."

Houghton-le-Spring-based Sunter Ltd also created two apprenticeship roles as part of the project.

John Walker, health, safety, environment, and quality manager at Sunter Ltd, said: "The security of long-term work through investment in social housing gives us the confidence to take on apprentices and develop our workforce in the region."

A similar investment programme is now underway in Bishop Auckland.
