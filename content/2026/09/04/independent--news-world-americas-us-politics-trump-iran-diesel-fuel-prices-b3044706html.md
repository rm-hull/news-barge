Fuel costs hit an unprecedented high in the U.S. on Friday, with diesel soaring to a record national average of $5.85 a gallon as an ongoing six-month war with Iran continues to disrupt global fuel flows.

Because freight and delivery networks rely heavily on diesel, these rising expenses mean higher transportation costs for a vast array of everyday consumer goods.

Commercial enterprises across multiple sectors are absorbing steeper bills, with several already passing the burden on to buyers through added fees on online orders and packages in the mail. Consequently, shoppers are likely to see increasing sticker shock trickle onto store shelves.

Supermarket aisles are among the first to feel the strain, particularly for produce, meat, and other perishable foods that require frequent hauling and restocking—or rely on diesel-powered farm equipment during harvest. Although it can take time for all of those expenses to fully trickle down to everyday buyers, the pressure is building.

Experts warn that price hikes could mount the longer diesel remains expensive, affecting a range of products transported by diesel trucks, trains, and boats, including clothing, cosmetics, and furniture.

Confronted by an intransigent Iranian government that has refused to back down in the face of a massive military campaign, President Donald Trump appears to have settled on a dual-prong approach combining economic pressure with the potential of a devastating escalation in force.

![Commercial vessels lie anchored in the Strait of Hormuz off Bandar Abbas, Iran](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/04/10/Iran_War_Strait_of_Hormuz_35355.jpg)

Having launched "Operation Economic Outcast" just last week to try to isolate Iran from its remaining global trade partners, the Trump administration also resumed strikes in recent days, prompting Iranian retaliation that has renewed concerns of an all-out regional war.

However, the combination of intense sanctions and an off-and-on bombing campaign over the six-month conflict has not bowed the Iranian leadership, leaving the administration struggling to find a way to wind down the conflict.

Tehran has dug in its heels—to Trump's frustration—as energy prices rise, the global economy roils, and poll numbers on the administration's handling of the war dip ahead of November's midterm congressional elections.
