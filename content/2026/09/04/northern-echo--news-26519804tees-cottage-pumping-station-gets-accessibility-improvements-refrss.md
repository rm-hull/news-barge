A historic pumping station in Darlington has improved visitor access thanks to new funding.

Tees Cottage Pumping Station, a 177-year-old waterworks site, received support through the Unlocking Access scheme, which is run by the Tees Valley Combined Authority.

The funding allowed volunteers to improve pathways across a site where surface water flooding and drainage issues had made access difficult, especially for wheelchair users, people with pushchairs, and those with limited mobility.

*(Image: Tees Valley Combined Authority)*

Martyn Brown, a volunteer and former chemical engineer, said: "The Unlocking Access fund has really put the cash in our pockets to buy the gravel and to rent the machines.

"Without it, the job would have taken us much longer.

"We'd have had to budget out of our limited funds to do a bit this year, a bit next year and a bit afterwards.

“But this has allowed us to do the full-scale job across the site"

The improvements were made possible by volunteers who laid gravel and used machinery to create smoother routes across the site.

An access survey supported by the scheme also helped the team understand how it can improve its signage.

The pumping station, built in 1849, supplied drinking water to Darlington and Teesside until its closure in 1980.

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said: "Our museums, attractions and cultural venues are a huge part of what makes our region special, and everyone should have the chance to enjoy them.

"This support is about making practical improvements which open doors to more people, and it’s great to see Tees Cottage Pumping Station using it to make such a historic site easier to visit.

"The volunteers there do a brilliant job keeping this important piece of our region’s history alive, and this funding will help even more people experience it for themselves."
