A man left his partner in financial difficulty after continuing to demand money from her for drugs even as their 16-month relationship petered out, a court heard.

During a course of coercive and controlling behaviour against the victim, Sean Hutchinson denied using violence, but admitted verbally abusing her, sending messages to her friends telling them not to associate with her, blocked others on social media, pestered her for money and told her what to wear.

Durham Crown Court heard that even when she moved into a refuge, in Nottingham, he contacted her and arranged to meet in Leeds, which she agreed to do, out of fear.

She eventually moved back to the North East, to a refuge in Darlington.

Martin Towers, prosecuting, said even though she left him, Hutchinson continued to seek money from her.

She agreed to many of his requests, often transferring money to him and his father, to relay to Hutchinson.

Mr Towers said between June and September last year she estimates paying him £1,300, plus £500 in November and other lesser amounts.

Despite her ending the relationship in November, Hutchinson did not leave her alone, as there were attempts to speak to her using unknown caller identities.

Although Hutchinson was arrested on January 14, he was bailed on condition that he did not contact her, but a week later he sent her a Snapchat message saying the police would not believe her.

He was arrested again and denied all the allegations, claiming his ex-partner was acting like a "woman scorned", as he was, by then, with someone else.

Hutchinson, 44, of Stocks Green, Newton Aycliffe, admitted a charge of controlling and coercive behaviour on the day he was due to go on trial, in July.

A victim statement was read to his scheduled sentencing hearing, on Thursday (September 3), in which Hutchinson's now ex-partner said she feels she has lost everything, including her financial security.

She said she moved to four different safe houses and was left in debt, while she feels she is just, "a shell of the person I used to be".

Despite requesting a lifetime restraining order, the victim said she is terrified he will attempt to breach any court protective order in future.

Mr Towers said the defendant has 79 past offences on his record and is in breach of a 21-month sentence, suspended for two years, which was imposed in January 2024.

Calum McNicholas, for Hutchinson, told the court: "Clearly drug use has been a key trigger for this behaviour.

"He has been in custody for almost nine months since January and has been engaging with the services available to those in prison, while performing two different jobs, in waste management and the stores."

Mr McNicholas said the defendant would like the opportunity of undergoing a drug review monitoring period to help him avoid a return to illicit substance misuse.

Judge Amanda Rippon said it was "perfectly obvious" that the defendant's offending and demands for money from his ex-partner were almost all down to his drug use.

She asked the Probation Service to carry out an assessment of the defendant to see if he is suitable to undergo a drug rehabilitation programme.

Judge Rippon told Hutchinson if the assessment is positive she intends to make him subject of a two-year drug rehabilitation programme, with monthly reviews at court to assess his progress.

She remanded Hutchinson to remain in custody pending the outcome of the assessment being presented to the court, on Wednesday, October 7.
