The former manager of a Filey care home has been jailed after she stole more than £50,000 from vulnerable residents and their families.

Michaela Golding, 58, from Greenfields Avenue, Osgodby, was sentenced to 32 months in prison at York Crown Court today (Friday).

Golding offered help to residents’ relatives who were struggling to manage care home payments and finances.

In several instances, money intended for care fees was instead paid directly into Golding’s personal bank accounts.

She also admitted to altering invoices from the podiatry service who came to take care of the residents, inflating the costs and taking the extra from the residents’ personal expenditure.

Originally, Golding denied taking the extra for herself, saying it was placed in a tin in the office as petty cash.

But when confronted with the evidence Golding admitted responsibility for the offences. From one victim alone, she took just over £27,000.

She pleaded guilty to fraud by abuse of position and false accounting. The offences took place between October 2022 and June 2024.

## ‘I feel deeply betrayed’

In a personal statement delivered at court, the son of one of Golding’s victims revealed the impact on him and his family.

“When I first discovered that money had been taken from my mum, I experienced a mixture of overwhelming emotions,” he said.

“Initially, I felt intense anger, which was then replaced by an overwhelming feeling of sickness.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/york-crown-court-oblique.webp)

“My mum was a very proud woman, and to know that this happened during the final years of her life causes me great distress. I still struggle to comprehend how someone could do this to her.

“My mum was living in a care home and was being looked after by people whom I believed I could trust – people who were supposed to protect her at a time when she was at her most vulnerable.

“My mum was the most wonderful person in my world and deserved kindness, dignity, and proper care. Instead, she was taken advantage of by someone who abused their position of trust.

“I feel deeply betrayed by Michaela Golding. Whenever I spoke to her, she came across as caring and attentive, while all the time she was stealing from my mum.”

Fraud investigator PC Emma Harris, from the North Yorkshire Police economic crime unit, said: “Golding’s offending was despicable and the effect on the families cannot be underestimated.

“She was placed in a position of trust to take care of vulnerable people and act in their best interests, instead she took advantage of them for her own financial gain.

“The victims’ families should be praised for their dedication and patience in achieving justice for their family members who fell victim to Golding. I hope today’s sentence goes some way in offering closure and help them now move forward and finally grieve for their loved ones.”
