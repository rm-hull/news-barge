A mother and daughter’s clothing business in West Yorkshire was used in fake TikTok adverts for a weight-loss product.

Nicola and Antonia Dexter, who run Style by Eleven in Ossett, Wakefield discovered that their promotional photos had been stolen and manipulated for use in fake TikTok adverts promoting a weight loss product called “Pinkies.”

Images of Nicola had been digitally altered to make her appear overweight, and were then used to falsely endorse weight loss capsules and "food noise patches" sold through a linked website.

  A real picture of Nicola Dexter posted to socials 

*(Image: Antonia Dexter)*

  The fake picture posted by the 'Pinkie' account 

*(Image: Antonia Dexter)*

Antonia Dexter said: "We mainly post of Facebook and Instagram, we’re not overly active on TikTok."

The scam came to light when a customer alerted them to the fake account.

Antonia said: "We got a message from a customer asking if we knew about this account. It was really active, every day they were posting images"

"A few people thought that it was us.

"That was what was most concerning, we were really shocked.

"Mum’s never been any other size so it was really strange."

Some of the fake posts had gone viral with tens of thousands of views, one video reaching 190,000 views.

  The fake posts on the 'Pinkie' TikTok account 

*(Image: Antonia Dexter)*

Antonia said their main concern was not the images themselves, but the potential harm to customers who might be misled into buying unsafe or non-existent products.

She said there were people in the account’s comments acting as her mum and answering people.

"We tried posting videos with our sign in the background to get it watermarked, but they just edited it out of the video.

"It makes you wonder what you can really do to keep things safe online."

Despite spending a month reporting the account to TikTok and submitting two cease and assist forms, the Dexters received little response.

Antonia said: "It took ages, we kept just getting these automated emails.

"Eventually we heard back from them and they said it doesn’t violate our terms even though we’d sent them hundreds of pieces of evidence."

She said: "We got in contact with West Yorkshire Police, who put it to their fraud team, but we hadn’t heard anything back"

  The front of their shop style by eleven 

*(Image: Antonia Dexter)*

After the Dexters contacted the BBC, the broadcaster got in touch with those behind the account. Within two hours of that approach, the TikTok profile had been removed.

Antonia said: "Getting the media involved clearly scared them off."

She also warned that the Pinkies brand had used videos from other businesses in similar scams, and that some TikTok accounts promoting the product remain active.

Despite the ordeal, the Dexters plan to continue using social media, their Facebook page boosts over 20,000 followers.

She said: "We get most of our custom from Facebook Lives, so if we stopped posting that would be the end of our business really."
