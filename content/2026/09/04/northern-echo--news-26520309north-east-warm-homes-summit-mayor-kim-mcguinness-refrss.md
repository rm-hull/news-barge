Action, not talk, is needed to lift families out of fuel poverty.

That was the message from Mayor Kim McGuinness at the North East Warm Homes Summit in Newcastle.

Held at The Common Room in the city centre, the summit took place on Tuesday, September 1, and brought together political leaders, energy experts, and industry representatives to discuss how investment in warmer homes can tackle fuel poverty, create jobs, and build regional supply chains.

The summit comes as Ofgem announced a 4 per cent rise in the energy price cap starting in October, pushing costs to a three-year high and deepening concerns for vulnerable households.

Mayor Kim McGuinness said: "We are not just talking about fuel poverty in the North East, we are doing something about it.

"That means turning ambition into action, working together to make homes warmer and more energy efficient, and ensuring that the investment into a region makes a difference to people’s lives."

The summit is part of a wider national push, with the UK Government's £15bn Warm Homes Plan aiming to upgrade five million homes and lift one million families out of fuel poverty by 2030.

Ross Armstrong, chief executive of Warmworks, said: "Fuel poverty is a national social crisis.

"There are still too many people living in damp, cold homes that they cannot afford to heat.

"With a cost of living crisis bearing down and energy prices set to go up again, this winter will pose a significant challenge for those who are living in fuel poverty."

The first panel discussion focused on immediate actions needed to address the energy crisis this winter, with panellists highlighting the urgent need for better targeting and delivery of support for at-risk households.

The summit also heard from a local homeowner who shared how home energy upgrades had transformed their quality of life.

A second panel explored the wider economic benefits of tackling fuel poverty, including job creation, apprenticeships, and growth in regional supply chains.

Stew Horne, group head of sector intelligence and external affairs at Energy Saving Trust, said the transition to low-carbon homes could also help protect families from future energy shocks.

He said recent research showed that heat pump users on smart tariffs could save around £330 per year

The summit closed with a keynote from Mayor Kim McGuinness.

She said: "At last year’s Summit, the conversation was about solutions.

"This year it is about delivery."
