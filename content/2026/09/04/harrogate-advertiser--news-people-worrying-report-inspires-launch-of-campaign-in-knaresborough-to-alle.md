# Worrying report inspires launch of campaign in Knaresborough to alleviate 'hidden loneliness' among older people

## A new campaign to tackle loneliness in older people that “isn't always visible” is being launched by Harrogate Neighbours and Knaresborough Library.

A recent report by North Yorkshire Council highlighted social isolation and loneliness as a significant concern for older people, particularly those living in rural communities.

It called for more community initiatives that promote companionship and social engagement.

In response, not-for-profit care provider, Harrogate Neighbours and Knaresborough Library, have joined forces to launch Puzzles for Pensioners.

(Picture contributed)](https://www.harrogateadvertiser.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmNlMjIyN2ZiLTA4NTctNDU0OS04NWMyLWY1ODA0NmQyNTQ5NjpiNDUyNGQwZi0xMTk5LTQ3OTgtYTVjNC01NDg5YjhlZWE1ZTc=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



The campaign, funded by the Knaresborough Rotary Club, is calling on local people to donate unwanted jigsaw puzzles and games to Knaresborough Library from Thursday, September 4.

The donated puzzles will be delivered to those who opt in, as well as those receiving Harrogate Neighbours' Meals on Wheels service in Knaresborough, giving them an enjoyable activity to complete at home.

Jamie Nicholson-Ruther from Harrogate Neighbours said, "For many of the people we visit, a friendly chat can be just as valuable as the meal itself.

"A puzzle gives us something to enjoy together, sparks conversation and creates a real sense of achievement when it's finished.

"We're hoping local people will have a look in their cupboards and donate any puzzles they're no longer using."

In addition to the donation of puzzles, Jamie Nicholson-Ruther will spend time this month visiting older people who opt in to the scheme, playing puzzles together while providing valuable companionship and a friendly face.

By encouraging conversation alongside stimulating activities, the initiative aims to help older people remain mentally engaged, improve wellbeing and identify any changes in health or mood before they become more serious concerns.

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for libraries, Coun Simon Myers, said: "This initiative is about much more than puzzles.

"It's about creating moments of connection, bringing joy into someone's week and helping people continue living independently in their own homes for longer.

“Loneliness isn't always visible. Many older people can go days, or even weeks, without meaningful conversation, and that's something we want to help change."
