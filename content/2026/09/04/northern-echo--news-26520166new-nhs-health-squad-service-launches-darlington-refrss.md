A NEW service is bringing healthcare directly to people who need it most.

Launched in Darlington, the Health Squad is delivered by County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust and commissioned by Darlington Borough Council.

The service is designed to reach individuals with complex needs who struggle to access traditional services due to barriers such as stigma, discrimination, poverty, or past trauma.

By focusing on person-centred, flexible care, the Health Squad aims to ensure no one falls through the gaps.

Gill Hunt, chief nurse at County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust, said: "Health Squad is taking personalised, compassionate support directly into local communities, building relationships and reaching people who may not otherwise engage with health services.

"By understanding what matters to each individual, we can provide tailored support that works for them and help address the wider factors that influence health including housing, finances and social circumstances."

Since launching in June 2026, the team has already supported more than 40 individuals, including “Robert”, who was helped to register with a GP and attend an appointment to access medication for an underlying mental health condition.

Robert also received support to engage with housing services and begin the process of securing suitable accommodation to support his long-term wellbeing.

The service operates Monday to Friday across a growing number of community venues in the town, with appointments also available at locations convenient to the service user.

Referrals currently come through partner organisations, though residents can also self-refer through the council website.

Caroline Todd, Manager at Kings Church Food Bank, one of the host venues, said: “This is an invaluable, much needed service.

“Most of our clients have some sort of health crisis and can find it difficult to keep up with regular appointments and meetings.

“Seeing a regular face from the team shows that someone actually cares about their needs and is helping people grow in trust and get the help they need.”

Councillor Matthew Roche, Darlington Borough Council's cabinet member for health and housing, said: "Disadvantage and inequality in our Borough results in a number of barriers in accessing essential services.

"This leads to worse health outcomes for many residents.

"By offering targeted support in the community, Health Squad is helping to tackle persistent health inequalities and is a great example of joined up working."
