## We found out if people are happy spending 50p to use public toilets in York

Lindsay and Mick Morgan were visiting York from the city of Tauranga on the North Island of New Zealand, where they say using local authority-run loos is free of charge. *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Locals and visitors to York might find it hard to pin down the location of some of the city centre public loos, but nobody seems to object to spending 50p to “spend a penny”.

The Press visited the City of York Council public convenience in Silver Street on Wednesday (September 2) after a reader’s letter last week claimed that a shortage of toilets in the city centre was to blame for two women urinating in public just outside the city walls in Lord Mayor's Walk.

Some of the comments on that story said there should be more council-run facilities to cope with the destination status York has with its visitors, and one asked about the adequacy of direction signage and another reminded of a need for facilities to be open 24/7.

  Users of York's public conveniences do not object to spending 50p to spend a penny, they said, provided they get a good service and cleanliness 

*(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Then, in a story published in June, the city’s loos were ranked second in a national study of the number of public toilets per capita.

One reader said despite this, there was a severe lack of open accessible facilities when and where people actually need them and another said there were far fewer public toilets now than in the past.

Others highlighted this study made no account for the millions of tourists coming into York each year.

City of York Council lists five council-run public toilets within the city walls on its website (if you include Nunnery Lane), with St George’s Field just outside and Union Terrace a little further.

The entry fee has recently increased to 50p, and City of York Council said they are operated by Healthmatic under contract.

The ones with half-a-mile’s walking distance of the old loos in Parliament Street are Coppergate, St Leonard’s Place, Rougier Street and St George’s Field.

We asked the people if they knew how many council-run loos were in the city centre and where they were, their views on the 50p charge, and whether shops, restaurants and cafes were right to limit the use of their facilities to paying customers.

  Lindsay and Mick Morgan were visiting York from the city of Tauranga on the North Island of New Zealand, where they say using local authority-run loos is free of charge. 

*(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

It seems the cost to spend a penny in York’s public conveniences is on a par with other European cities, and provided the customer is getting their money’s worth, it’s an acceptable price to pay.

And 100 per cent of those 10 or so people we spoke to said it was absolutely correct for coffee shops, pubs and restaurants in York to limit the use of their facilities to paying customers only.

Nick and Jaz, both 26, from Queensland in Australia are in the UK celebrating their engagement and they’d also been to France and Greece as part of their trip.

The couple passed Silver Street and admitted that the coffees and pastries they were carrying were bought from a local bakery partly so one of them could spend a penny.

Jaz said: “We’re used to the charge from travelling, we’ve been to the UK a handful of times, and paying still does feel a bit strange.

“I feel like all the places we’ve been to, in Italy or Greece, for example, it’s now just the norm to pay.”

Nick said: “I really only have a comparison with Australia, where as far as I can see the public loos are all free.

“The use of toilets in shops over there is also for customers only, although you could probably get away with not paying for anything at a fast-food restaurant.”

Edwina and Adam Brady from Heworth were in town for the day with their son and said that council facilities should be to the same high standard as those in the nearby shops and department stores.

Edwina said: “I think the accessibility of public toilets has been an issue in York for a long time.

“I heard it being brought up in a radio phone-in recently, and if you’re in town and you have to go, then it still feels like you have to go somewhere to buy a drink to do so.”

Adam said he knew there were some in St Georges Field car park and another out in Rowntree Park but couldn’t say where the others closer to Silver Street were.

Both Edwina and Adam said they both remembered the former public loos in Parliament Street which were demolished before the Silver Street toilets opened.

Edwina said: “50p is fine if you get the right service but if you pay over the odds for poor service then you have every right to complain.”

The couple said facilities in Girona in Spain where they have holidayed are more family-oriented than in York, in the town, and not just at the airport.

  Edwina and Adam Brady from Heworth, with their son 

*(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Lindsay and Mick Morgan were in York on their travels from Tauranga on the North Island of New Zealand, with Lindsay here to trace some of her Viking roots.

Lindsay was unable to pay cash at Silver Street turnstile which caused her and at least one other potential user a problem.

She said: “They’re not manned either which was a problem, and it seemed like tailgating might be the only way to get through.”

Mick said: “We’ve just paid £19 for five hours parking in Nunnery Lane and you’d think the public toilets in the city would be free on that basis."

Lindsay said: “I think shops should limit their WC use to customers only. At the end of the day they have to pay for the staff to come and clean them.”

Geraldine, a community nurse who has lived in the Heworth area of York for more than 30 years said she thought there should be more public toilets in the city centre.

She said: “I have no problem paying if there is soap to wash your hands and equipment to dry them.”

She added that the Silver Street public convenience was the only one she knew of in York’s city centre.

She said she would go into a pub and ask to use the facilities if she needed to and said people are usually very gracious if that happens.

Colin and Sheila Richardson were in York for the day from Scarborough and had paid a £1 fee for them each to use the Silver Street facility as their Royal Association for Disability and Rehabilitation (RADAR) key - which opens locked disabled-accessible toilets in the UK – had been left in the car.

Sheila, 77, said: “I quite understand why businesses limit the use of their toilets to paying customers only – they have to pay their staff after all.

Colin, 82, said: “We used to live in York and remember the Parliament Street public toilets, and we know this one now.

“There were always an adequate number of toilets in every town and city and it’ strange to see how many have closed or fallen by the wayside.”

A City of York Council spokesperson said: “We recognise that public toilet facilities play an important role for residents and visitors, and we want them to remain accessible, safe and well maintained.

“Under the terms of its lease agreement with the council, Healthmatic is responsible for operating these facilities, including setting the entry charge.

“This is the first change to the fee since April 2014, with the charge increasing from 40p to 50p.

“Healthmatic has advised that the increase reflects rising operating costs, including staffing and utility costs.

“The council will continue to work closely with Healthmatic to support the provision of reliable, good-quality public toilet facilities across the city.”
