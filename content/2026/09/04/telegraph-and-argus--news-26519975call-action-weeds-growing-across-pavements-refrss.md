A WARD councillor has called for action over weeds growing across pavements in a Bradford area.

Labour Councillor Joanne Dodds, who represents the Great Horton ward, said the footpaths look neglected and dirty.

Cllr Dodds is especially concerned now that schools are going back.

## 'I have never known it so bad'

She said: "In the 20 years I have been a councillor, I have never known it so bad.

"After an incredibly hot summer, weeds are growing out of pavements and kerbs across Great Horton.

"Some paths are covered with clumps of foliage that could pose a serious trip hazard and makes it difficult to get a pram, wheelchair or mobility scooter across and forcing people to go on the road instead.

"It’s an accident waiting to happen."

## No Bradford Council action

Councillor Dodds said she has repeatedly reported the problem to Bradford Council, but no action has been taken.

She has also raised concerns about overgrown snickets, saying brambles and weeds are posing a hazard to children and members of the public.

She said: "I have reported snickets and footpaths are thick with brambles and weeds, and I have heard from parents about their children being stung by overgrown nettles.

"Some of these snickets are well-used and, with the return to school this week, it is vital that they are kept clean, clear and safe."

## 'Get the basics right'

Despite raising the issue multiple times, Councillor Dodds said the areas have yet to be cleared.

She said: "People expect the local authority to get the basics right.

“I have been unable to get any answers as to when they will be done, or any explanation why the contractor has apparently not completed any weed-spraying in the Great Horton ward at all, and why are snickets and footpaths are not routinely kept clear

"It is simply not good enough."

## Why weeds can present a hazard

- 
    **Narrowing the path** : Weeds growing through cracks or along pavement edges reduce the effective width of a footway, and a route that is fine for an able-bodied walker may become too tight for a wheelchair or pushchair to pass.
- 
    **Uneven, unstable surfaces** : Weed roots can lift or crack paving slabs, creating trip hazards and bumps that are hard to navigate with wheels or a cane. Accessible footways need to be smooth, firm and step-free, and vegetation damage undermines that.
- 
    **Blocked drainage** : Weeds can block drains, leading to standing water that adds a slip risk and can further erode the surface over time.
- 
    **Forcing people into the road** : Where an obstruction is severe or the pavement is already narrow, pedestrians - including wheelchair and pushchair users - may have no option but to move into the carriageway, putting them at risk from passing traffic.
