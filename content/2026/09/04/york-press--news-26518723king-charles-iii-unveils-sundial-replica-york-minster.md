A replica of the historic Kirkdale sundial has been unveiled by King Charles III and President Emmanuel Macron.

Crafted by York Minster’s team of craftspeople, the replica was unveiled at the opening of the British Museum’s Bayeux Tapestry exhibition on Wednesday, September 2.

Judith McNicol, managing director of the British Museum, said: "The Kirkdale sundial, created in the 11th century when Edward the Confessor was king, is an amazing North Yorkshire survivor from those turbulent times.

"As we celebrate the opening of the Bayeux Tapestry, we were thrilled to work with the amazing stonemasons at York Minster to create a replica sundial.

"They have the heritage craft skills, as well as the cutting-edge technology, to deliver an Anglo-Saxon sundial that is so beautiful, of such high quality and at such pace."

The project highlights the capabilities of York Minster's Centre of Excellence for Heritage Craft Skills and Estate Management, which blends traditional craftsmanship with modern conservation technology.

Alex McCallion, Director of Works and Precinct at York Minster, said: "The Kirkdale sundial is one of the most important surviving artefacts from Anglo-Saxon England; recreating it has been a wonderful opportunity to demonstrate what can be achieved when combining traditional craft skills and modern technology."
