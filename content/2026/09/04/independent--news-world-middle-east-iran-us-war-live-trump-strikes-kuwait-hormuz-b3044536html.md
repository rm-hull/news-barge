JD Vance has faced backlash after appearing to dismiss reports that a US strike killed five people, including two children, at a wedding gathering in southern Iran.

When asked about whether a US strike was responsible, he said “sometimes things happen”.

He said: “I will say that the Iranian state media has not been a very good scribe about what’s happened in the conflict thus far, so I’m extremely sceptical of this.”

The toll was confirmed not by the Iranian government but by the Iranian Red Crescent, a member of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement.

“What I can say with 100% confidence is that, unlike the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps] IRGC, the United States never targets civilians in combat,” Vance said. “We never will do that. We never have done that, and unfortunately, obviously, sometimes things happen.”

Meanwhile, president Donald Trump insisted he had done the “right thing” with the war.

It comes amid fears Trump is planning to extend Washington’s war on Iran, after *The Wall Street Journal* reported that the Pentagon was extending the presence of troops in the Middle East into next year.

## US in turmoil: Trump faces Iran war nightmare as diesel hits record $5.85 and prices threaten to soar

Fuel costs hit an unprecedented high in the U.S. on Friday, with diesel soaring to a record national average of $5.85 a gallon as an ongoing six-month war with Iran continues to disrupt global fuel flows.

Because freight and delivery networks rely heavily on diesel, these rising expenses mean higher transportation costs for a vast array of everyday consumer goods.

Commercial enterprises across multiple sectors are absorbing steeper bills, with several already passing the burden on to buyers through added fees on online orders and packages in the mail. Consequently, shoppers are likely to see increasing sticker shock trickle onto store shelves.

## Iranian officials acknowledge in private that US pressure is working

Three senior Iranian sources have told the Reuters news agency that the US campaign to throttle Iran’s economy by blockading its oil exports is growing increasingly difficult to withstand.

While Iran’s clerical rulers have managed to get around harsh sanctions for decades, the latest US movements have left them in a far more sensitive position, with few remaining channels to secure foreign currency or buy goods, the sources said.

Iran publicly maintains that it has plenty foreign currency reserves and is not meaningfully affected by the US crackdown on sanctions evasion.

The latest measures expanded secondary sanctions that target countries doing business with Iran in a bid to stop it clearing dollar transactions needed for both oil sales and to finance crucial imports of goods and raw materials.

That effort is making Iran's existing sanctions evasion networks - front companies, unregistered tankers and smuggling - too expensive to use, the three senior sources said.

One of the sources said Iran only has another two months’ supply of gasoline, which has to be imported due to limited refining capacity.

## Araghchi defends Iran over strikes on Jordan

Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi takes to social media to respond to his Jordanian counterpart’s recent comments criticising Iran for strikes on Jordan and neighbouring countries.

The Jordanian minister Ayman Safadi said that attacks on his country came within two hours of the start of the war, in February, suggesting the attack was not reactionary.

Araghchi writes: “How much time does the Jordanian Foreign Minister believe Iran should wait before responding to an aggressor that respects neither Arab sovereignty nor Iranian sovereignty?”

He adds: “And is he truly unaware that Arab airspace, lands, and waters were used in the initial American attacks that resulted in the killing of innocent Iranians?”

## Evidence points to US weapon in deadly strike on Iran wedding celebration

The United States is investigating a deadly strike on a wedding gathering in southern Iran after weapons experts reviewing verified images and video concluded the blast was likely caused by a direct hit from a U.S. munition, Vice President JD Vance said Thursday.

Five people have died following Tuesday's strike on a home hosting the celebration, according to Iranian state television, which reported Thursday that a 22-year-old woman had died from her injuries. Iranian officials said nearly 70 others were wounded.

Speaking to reporters Thursday, Vance confirmed U.S. authorities were examining what happened.

"We're investigating it because obviously we care. We want to know," Vance said.

## This morning: Asian benchmarks mostly rise after tech stocks lead rally on Wall Street
