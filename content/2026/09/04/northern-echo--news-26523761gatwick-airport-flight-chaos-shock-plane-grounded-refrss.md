A Darlington woman has spoken of her shock after her Norse Atlantic Airways flight to New York was forced to make an emergency stop on the runway.

York Press reporter Alice Kavanagh, originally from Darlington, was onboard Norse Atlantic Airways flight Z0701, bound for New York’s John F Kennedy International Airport (JFK), from London Gatwick Airport at 1.05pm on Friday (September 4).

She described how her plane had ground to a sudden halt after gaining reported speeds - according to The Aviation Herald - at 138mph for an anticipated take-off.

Alice, now lining in York, said: "Honestly, the whole situation was pretty scary.

“We were starting to speed off for take off and all of a sudden, the pilot slammed the breaks on the plane.

“It took of a few minutes then they explained that they’d spotted a technical fault."

Alice said passengers remained in their seats while engineers tried to understand the nature of the fault.

Emergency services, including fire and rescue crews, were called to the runway.

Alice said: "It’s so lucky that the pilot spotted it.

“We couldn’t even drive off the runway - god knows what would happen if we’d taken off.”

Alice praised the "fantastic" crew aboard the plane, who she said did a "great job" at keeping everyone calm.

The incident brought London Gatwick Airport to a standstill for about 50 minutes as the Norse Atlantic Airways plane was towed from the runway.

A London Gatwick spokesperson told ITV: "The runway was closed for 50 minutes this afternoon (Friday, September 4) due to a technical issue with a departing aircraft.

"The aircraft was towed from the runway, which reopened shortly afterwards following a full safety inspection.

Alice and passengers of the Z0701 flight are now in the airport waiting for a new plane to New York, which is expected to be ready in an hour and a half.
