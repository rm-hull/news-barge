## Households in Great Britain can now plug approved solar systems directly into a socket, although the bargain-priced revolution may need a little longer to arrive.

After weeks of waiting, and with only a few days of the meteorological summer remaining, plug-in solar finally became a thing in the UK last week.

And the arrival of new legislation that allows people to plug money-saving solar panels directly into household sockets has been something of an anti-climax so far, it's fair to say.

Kits are available right now, so you can order a system online today and have it up and running within moments of it arriving, but prices are not quite what we were expecting.

It had been mooted by industry insiders that we'd soon see the arrival of a sub-£300 solar system when the government finally gave the green light for them to be plugged in, but that hasn't quite happened yet. In fact, quality kits from reputable sources can be costing double that. But, shopping around, there are still some relatively affordable options.

Below we'll take a look at some of the best options on the market right now.

## But first: what does a plug-in solar system need?

The elements in a plug-in solar bundle are pretty simple. You'll need at least one solar panel, a microinverter, and some cable.

The solar panels turn sunlight into electricity and the microinverter makes sure that electricity is safe and stable before it enters the household wiring.

The moulded three-pin plug all kits must include has to be plugged in to a fitted UK three-pin socket (not an extension cable) and as soon as you flick the switch, the microinverter kicks into life and any energy you harvest is yours to enjoy, for free.

## Will prices come down?

Plug-in solar has only been available for (legal) use for a week or so, and it's fair to say this is the honeymoon period for retailers, with the topic buzzing, demand high, and competition low.

What we need to wait for now is the big boys to get involved. Right from the outset, Lidl threw its hat in the ring as a retailer lining up to sell kits, so just keep your eye on the infamous "middle of Lidl" for a bargain.

That will, hopefully, start some competition and decent £250 systems will be a thing.

## Is it worth buying it? How much will I save?

Let's say you invested in a 400w plug-in solar system, and you were able to grab one for, say, £400.

There are so many variables, such as whether the panels face the sun and for how much of the day, and then the time of year, your current electricity tariff, the efficiency of the panels, and even the ambient temperature.

But it's just about possible to eke out a £100 annual saving from 400 watts in a good year, and you usually get a 10 year warranty on your kit. And 400 watts is enough to cover a background load for most homes, so it'll cover general day-to-day use.

A good inverter and a quality solar panel should easily outlast its warranty, but your system would hopefully pay for itself by the end of year four, and then you're quids in for every other year you keep it for.

Even if it only lasts you 15 years, which is pessimistic, that's a healthy return on your investment.

Although, if you're anything like me, you'll have expanded it by then. Getting free energy from the sun is properly addictive.
