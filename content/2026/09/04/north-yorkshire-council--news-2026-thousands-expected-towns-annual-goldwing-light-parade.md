![Goldwing motorcycles will be on display on Scarborough Pier on September 12 prior to the annual parade of machines in the resort which raises money for the Yorkshire Air Ambulance.](https://www.northyorks.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-09/Gold1.jpg) 

Thousands of visitors are expected in Scarborough next weekend as the popular Goldwing Light Parade returns to the town.

The free event, which has attracted as many as 12,000 people previously, will take place on Saturday 12 September, bringing together motorcycle enthusiasts and families along the South Bay seafront.

It raises thousands of pounds each year for the Yorkshire Air Ambulance service.

The Goldwing motorcycles will be on display on Scarborough Pier between noon and 4pm, before the main parade gets underway at 8pm, travelling via Spa Bridge, Foreshore Road and Marine Drive before reaching Peasholm Gap and returning to the pier.

The parade is expected to feature about 100 Goldwing motorcycles decorated with spectacular neon and LED lighting. Riders travel from across the country to take part in the procession, which will make its way around Marine Drive, creating a dazzling display around the bay.

Our executive member for arts and culture, Cllr Simon Myers, said: “Events such as this play an important role in attracting visitors to our coast and boosting the local economy.

“The combination of the illuminated motorcycles and entertainment creates a spectacle that helps showcase Scarborough as a vibrant destination and raises vital funds for the air ambulance’s work. We look forward to welcoming visitors from across the region and beyond."

To help ensure public safety, a number of road closures and traffic restrictions will be in place during the event.

Foreshore Road, Sandside and Eastborough, from Globe Street to the seafront, will be closed from 7.30pm until 10pm. Marine Drive and Royal Albert Drive will be subject to rolling closures from 7.30pm until approximately 8.45pm to allow the parade to pass safely.

Organiser Danielle Scott said: “This year marks my fourth year organising the parade, and I'm incredibly excited for what promises to be another fantastic event.

“Preparations are coming together really well for what will be a spectacular display of lights along Scarborough's beautiful seafront.

“Visitors can expect bikes decorated with lights galore, music, and even the occasional fancy dress costume, creating a fun and vibrant atmosphere for all ages.”

Due to safety concerns and in line with government advice following a spate of devastating wildfires across the country, the traditional fireworks display at the end of the parade will not now go ahead.
