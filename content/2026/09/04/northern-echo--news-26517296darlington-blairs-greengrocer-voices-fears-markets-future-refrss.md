A greengrocer who has spent three decades at one of Darlington Market’s best-known stalls says its closure will be a “huge loss” for the town, warning older shoppers will lose a vital place to buy fresh produce.

JJ Blair and Sons will serve customers at its stall for the last time at Darlington Indoor Market on October 9, ending more than 150 years of family trading in the town.

Known simply as Blairs to generations of customers, the business was founded in 1875 and has passed through five generations of the Blair family.

The closure follows the death of long serving owner Robin Blair, who died aged 81 in July after a short illness. It has been confirmed the business would close because there was nobody left to carry it on.

  Robin Blair served the community for decades. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Christine Hanlon, who has worked at the stall for 34 years and will retire when it closes, said Mr Blair’s death had left a gap which could not be filled.

She said: “The closure is because Robin died and there is nobody else to carry it on. The business goes with him.

“Robin did everything for his customers and it is the end of an era.

“He was a good boss and he was a good man. He was fair. Coming to work after he died has felt so cold. You would always hear him shouting names and chatting to customers. It is a huge loss for the town.”

Mr Blair was Darlington’s longest serving market trader and had worked at the stall for more than 70 years, after first helping in the family business as a child.

Ms Hanlon said the loss of Blairs would be particularly felt by older shoppers, many of whom called in to buy small amounts of fresh fruit and vegetables.

  JJ Blair and Sons 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She said: “It is such a shame for the older generation. They have got nowhere to go. Most of the time they just want one carrot or a few sprouts, but most of the shops are on the outskirts now.

“People came here for the fresh produce. Why should you be forced to shop at the supermarket?”

She said some customers had wrongly assumed the stall was already closed following Mr Blair’s death.

  Hundreds attend the funeral cortege of Robin Blair 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Ms Hanlon said: “Trade has been dwindling down now because people think we are already closed with Robin gone. But the stall is still here for now.”

She also had concerns about the wider future of Darlington Indoor Market.

Christine said: “You cannot call it a market anymore.

“It feels like investors have done nothing with this market. Robin was paying £2,500 a month for both stalls. That is just for rent.

  JJ Blair and Sons 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

“The market could have so much potential. Keep rents down, help people get on their feet and give people a reason to come in.”

The Blair family nursery on Barmpton Lane will remain open for the time being, selling plants and flowers grown by the family.

A spokesperson for Darlington Borough Council said: “We are saddened to learn of the closure of JJ Blair and Sons following the death of Robin Blair.
