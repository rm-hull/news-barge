A creative director has launched a public memorial project to honour the life of a homeless man who died in tragic circumstances in Bradford city centre.

Ethan Asante, 30, from Lidget Green in Bradford, created the tribute to Stephen Cameron, who had spent 18 years living on the streets.

The installation was placed outside the former NatWest doorway, close to where Stephen often sat and near where he died in a collision on Market Street on July 12.

  The memorial outside Barclays Bank which honours the life of Stephen Cameron


Stephen, who was in a wheelchair, was struck by a blue Nissan Qashqai that had mounted the kerb.

Mr Asante, founder of creative agency Proof of Lifes, said: "Following Stephen’s death, I installed a public life record on the former NatWest doorway where his friends had laid flowers.

"When I arrived, I was surprised to discover that every tribute had already been cleared away.

"It made the question behind my project feel even more urgent: What remains of a life after the flowers have gone?"

  Ethan Asante has installed a memorial outside Barclays Bank to honour the life of Stephen Cameron


The display features a reconstructed portrait of Stephen, an original poem and a QR code inviting people to share their memories.

Mr Asante used his skills as a graphic designer and 3D artist to create the portrait using video clips and screen grabs.

He said the installation was intended to offer a "first-person account" of Stephen’s life.


Mr Asante said: "He was on the streets for 18 years.

"We all saw him, but nobody paid attention to what his life was like.

"We all knew he was there but never asked who he was."

  Ethan Asante has installed a memorial outside Barclays Bank to honour the life of Stephen Cameron


Mr Asante said he understood that Stephen died one day after his 50th birthday.

Mr Asante also would like the display to highlight issues such as inequality and homelessness.

The memorial project forms part of a wider campaign, Poverty Cannot Cancel Calling, which aims to expand the concept into a pilot scheme in Bradford and eventually reach seven northern towns and cities.

Mr Asante is seeking funding, homelessness charity partners and public locations for future installations.

He said: "Most charities feed the homeless, hand out blankets and set up temporary accommodation.

"My campaign aims to use our gifts because everybody carries a story.

"For example, a photographer can preserve the face of a homeless person.

"Ask yourself ‘What can I give?’ Everyone has a gift or talent, and the best you can do is optimise it."

Mr Asante founded the agency as Moments by TOBY in 2022 after losing six family members during the Covid-19 pandemic.

The business originally focused on creating beautifully designed funeral booklets and memorial keepsakes.

It has since evolved into Proof of Lifes, which aims to capture and preserve the stories of individuals whose lives might otherwise go unrecorded.

The collision also seriously injured a 31-year-old man and left a 13-year-old boy with minor injuries, while a 30-year-old man was released on bail after being arrested.
