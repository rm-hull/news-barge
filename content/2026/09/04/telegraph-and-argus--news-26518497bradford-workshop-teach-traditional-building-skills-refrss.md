A HANDS-ON workshop will give people the chance to try their hand at traditional lime mortar skills.

Organised by the team behind the Bradford Heritage Action Zone (HAZ) scheme, the free training sessions will be held on September 23 in the Courtyard at Darley Street Market in Bradford city centre.

They will be delivered by Womersley’s Ltd.

Cllr Andrew Judson, Bradford Council’s executive member for economy, regeneration, and planning, said: "Bradford has a rich historic built environment, and maintaining these buildings requires specialist skills and knowledge.

"We're delighted to offer these practical workshop sessions, through the Bradford Heritage Action Zone programme, to give local people the opportunity to learn about caring for heritage buildings from experienced professionals.

"With a background in the building trade, I appreciate the importance of preserving and sharing knowledge of traditional heritage methods, ensuring these valuable skills are not lost as the construction industry continues to change."

The workshops are part of the wider Bradford Heritage Action Zone scheme, a multi-million-pound regeneration project launched in late 2024 to preserve and revitalise the city’s historic core.

The HAZ programme includes educational events to support traditional conservation techniques.

Attendees will learn the fundamentals of building conservation and gain hands-on experience with different types of lime mortars commonly used in heritage restoration.

Two sessions are available: a morning workshop from 10am to 12.30pm or an afternoon session from 1.30pm to 4pm.

Each 2.5-hour workshop is open to anyone interested in learning about lime mortars and their practical use in preserving historic buildings.

Participants will work with hot lime mortar, non-hydraulic lime mortar, and natural hydraulic lime under the guidance of experts.

The training will cover mortar preparation and mixing, joint preparation, application techniques, and achieving a professional finish.

Attendees will also learn why lime mortars are vital for the long-term care of heritage buildings.

Lisa Kinch, historic places advisor at Historic England, said: "Bradford's historic streets and buildings form a vital part of the city's character and heritage.

"Through the Heritage Action Zone programme, Historic England is proud to support projects that help people understand, value, and care for their local historic environment, while bringing new life to the heart of the city.

"These free workshops offer a valuable opportunity to connect with Bradford's heritage, learn traditional conservation skills, and help safeguard the city's historic buildings for future generations."

The workshop is for adults aged 18 and over, and places are limited.

To book, email [BradfordHAZ@bradford.gov.uk.](mailto:BradfordHAZ@bradford.gov.uk.)
