A medic who was forced to flee Sudan after suffering persecution and torture has spoken of how a Teesside volunteering scheme offered him a fresh start.

Ahmed Bakheet, 40, lives in Stockton and now works as a sonographer, thanks to a volunteering programme at the University Hospital of North Tees after years of being shut out of healthcare due to conflict and persecution.

Political instability forced Ahmed to leave Sudan in 2010, so he could find opportunities that would secure a future for his family.

He began working as a radiographer in Saudi Arabia, where he also completed a master’s degree in diagnostic medical ultrasound.

  Ahmed Bakheet. 

*(Image: Helpforce)*

But after returning to Sudan to find his family as conflict escalated, Ahmed was devastated to find that his two children and his brother had been shot and killed by government forces.

Soon afterwards, he was arrested for political activism and spent six months in prison, where he says he was tortured.

"I lost a part of myself during my incarceration," he said. "You don't forget those experiences."

After his release, Ahmed returned to the UK to start again, but found there were no job opportunities available to him.

Homeless and destitute, he spent more than three years unable to work while waiting for a decision on his asylum application.

He was eventually granted refugee status in June 2025, and quickly joined the Helpforce Volunteer to Career programme at the University Hospital of North Tees.

Ahmed started as a ward assistant before moving to radiology, where staff recognised his skills and helped him in his return to the profession.

He said: "It helped me remember what I could do. Step by step, I started to feel like myself again."

The volunteer programme gave Ahmed the recent NHS experience, professional references and renewed confidence needed to secure a role as a sonographer with a private healthcare provider.

He now performs advanced pregnancy scans and splits his time between Essex and his home in Stockton, where he lives with his wife Refga, teenage son Mo and baby son Ezzeldin.

Looking back on his experience, Ahmed said: "I'm proud. After everything, to be able to work again and help people means a lot."

Helpforce chief executive Amerjit Chohan said Ahmed’s story showed what medically qualified refugees can bring to UK healthcare when given the right support.

Ramit Imtiaz, an advanced practitioner in radiology at University Hospitals Tees, also praised Ahmed, calling his journey “incredibly inspiring”.

Ramit said: "Volunteering gave Ahmed the opportunity to reconnect with healthcare in the UK, while allowing us to see first-hand the skills and commitment he had to offer.

"His journey shows how pathways like this can benefit both individuals and the NHS."

The Volunteer to Care initiative is a collaboration between North Tees and Hartlepool, South Tees Hospitals, County Durham and Darlington, and Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys trusts, and Helpforce.

The programme allows people to gain hands on experience and provides a pathway to a career in healthcare.
