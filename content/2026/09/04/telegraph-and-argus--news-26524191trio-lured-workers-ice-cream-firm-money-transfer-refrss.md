TWO brothers and one of their employees have been all been jailed after they lured two other workers to their ice cream company premises in Bradford and accused them of stealing from the business.

Bradford Crown Court was told that the two complainants, who both denied stealing anything, had been asked to attend at the Super Whippy premises on Lister Lane back in May 2023 on the pretext of “company training”.

But when they arrived the pair were verbally abused, assaulted and accused of stealing from the business.

## Money transferred then threats

Prosecutor Nathan Davis said despite their denials the two complainants were each forced to transfer money over from their bank accounts.

“They were eventually allowed to leave the premises and threatened if they reported it to the police the defendants knew where they lived,” said Mr Davis.

## Men pleaded guilty on day of trial

Brothers Yousaf Akhtar, 32, of View Croft Road, Shipley, and Yasar Akhtar, 36, of Lister Lane, off Bolton Road, were each jailed for 40 months after they admitted two charges of blackmail.

Their employee Haseeb Mahmood, 33, of Hastings Terrace, Little Horton, admitted the same charges and was jailed for 36 months.

When the defendants were interviewed about the offences they replied “no comment”, but in a prepared statement Yasar Akhtar said the allegations were lies.

But on the day, the men were due to stand trial in June this year all three pleaded guilty to the blackmail charges.

## Defendant 'under stress' from long hours

Barrister Nadim Bashir, for Yasar Akhtar, submitted that the injuries suffered by the complainants were “relatively minor”.

He said his client had resigned as a director of Super Whippy Limited in October 2024 and was now devoted to caring for his elderly father and mother who both had health problems.

Barrister Charles Blatchford, for Yousaf Akhtar, described the offending as “a deeply unedifying incident”, but said it had been short-lived.

He said his client was a man of positive character who had helped at community events.

“It’s clear when you read the references and the pre-sentence report that this is a one-off for a man who has never been in trouble before,” said Mr Blatchford.

“At the time he had been working extremely long hours and was under stress from the amount of work which the company was doing and suffering from fatigue.”

Mr Blatchford said the defendant was ashamed of not doing the right thing and submitted that it was essentially a loss of temper.

## Men jailed

But Judge Ahmed Nadim said the offending must have occurred as a result of some level of planning and it was not a “spur of the moment” action.

Mahmood’s barrister submitted that there had been no sophisticated planning involved and his client now had caring responsibilities for his wife.

Jailing the three men, Judge Nadim said Mahmood had acted under the influence of his long-standing employers and his culpability was somewhat lower than his co-defendants.

He also imposed a five-year restraining order which bans the defendants from having any contact with the two complainants.
