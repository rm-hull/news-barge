Parents in North Yorkshire are being offered practical advice to support children who are anxious about returning to school.

Emily McIver, an education mental health practitioner from the Wellbeing in Mind team, is sharing tips to help families in North Yorkshire ease the transition back to the classroom.

She recommends re-establishing routines, especially around bedtime and mornings, to help children adjust.

Ms McIver said: "Going back to school after the summer break can feel challenging for many families and can bring mixed emotions.

"It's normal for children and young people to have lots of feelings.

"There'll be some that are really nervous, but also lots that are excited and others that aren't bothered at all.

"All these things are completely fine and normal."

Parents are also encouraged to create a dedicated "worry time" each day for children to talk about their concerns.

Ms McIver added: "Having a dedicated time will help children manage their worries by focusing on them during a set time each day, instead of letting them distract them all day."

The Wellbeing in Mind team, part of Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust, supports students with mild to moderate mental health issues in schools across North Yorkshire and York.
