A former Durham Police officer slammed his partner’s head against a wall and subjected her to repeated violence and domestic abuse, a misconduct panel has found.

The officer, who cannot be named because of a reporting restriction, was found to have assaulted his former partner, known as Ms A, during their relationship.

On September 22, 2024, the former officer was found to have hit Ms A in the face, causing injury and bruising, banged her head against a door, pinned her to the floor and injured her hand.

He was also found to have caused injury and bruising to Ms A’s eye and chest and damaged a wall at the home they shared.

The panel found the allegations amounted to assault and domestic abuse.

It said it was “unanimous” in its view that Ms A had been assaulted by the former officer on September 22.

The report said the panel found “that the version of events she provided to her brother was the truth”.

It highlighted body-worn video footage from police officers who attended Ms A’s home after her brother called 999.

When officers questioned Ms A about injuries to her face and hand, she told them: “I don’t want to say anything - I don’t want him to get into trouble. I love him to pieces and I don’t want to get him into trouble. He will lose his job and I don’t want to get him into trouble”.

The panel said Ms A later claimed she had tripped over suitcases, but found this was an attempt to protect the former officer.

The report said the panel believed she was “processing the situation she faced” before inventing an explanation following questioning by police.

It also noted that when officers told Ms A they believed the former officer had caused her injuries, she did not deny it.

Instead, the report said, she repeatedly expressed concern that he would get into trouble or lose his job.

The panel said: “The panel were of the view that were there an innocent explanation, Ms A would have had no cause to be concerned for the officer.”

It concluded that her concern for the former officer “corroborated the assault as reported to her brother”.

The report also said Ms A repeatedly told officers she did not want him arrested.

She said: “I do not want him arrested” and “I don’t want to stop him from coming home”.

The panel said: “In short, Ms A was only concerned with protecting the former officer.”

It found this explained the difference between what Ms A told her brother and what she told police.

“Her statements to her brother were the truth, her statements to the police were invented to protect Former Officer A from getting into trouble with the police,” the report said.

The panel also considered messages and photographs exchanged between Ms A, her brother and the former officer to be corroborative of the assaults.

It found the former officer’s reaction when he was arrested was also “corroborative of an assault having occurred”.

The panel found all the allegations of violence and domestic abuse proven.

It said the conduct amounted to a breach of the Standards of Professional Behaviour and found the former officer had a “high degree of culpability”.

The report said: “Former Officer A was wholly blameworthy for his actions, which involved serious violence against a woman who was entitled to be regarded as vulnerable.”

The panel also found a “high degree of harm”, citing Ms A’s physical injuries, “on-going damage to her health”, psychological distress and degrading treatment.

The repeated nature of the violence was among the aggravating factors identified by the panel, alongside the vulnerability of the victim and “violence against women”.

The report said there were no mitigating circumstances.

It found the former officer had shown no remorse and said that even if the relationship had been “toxic”, that would not mitigate his behaviour.

The panel concluded that his conduct amounted to gross misconduct.

The former officer had resigned from Durham Police earlier this year and did not attend the hearing.

The panel said that if he had still been serving, he would have been dismissed.

Instead, it directed that his name be placed on the College of Policing barred list.
