Middlesbrough Council will move forward with its “bold new vision” for the town including a new theatre and hotel after its Executive approved the bid.

The biggest changes to the town “in a generation” were unveiled last month as part of a bid to remodel and reshape the town for young people across 15 major projects.

  One of the headline projects includes the __demolition of the crumbling Civic Centre__ to make way for the “most technologically advanced theatre in the country”. 

  There are also plans for a new hotel at the __derelict Gurney House__, near the A66, to meet “sky high” demand and remove redundant retail floorspace across the town. 

On Wednesday (September 2), the authority backed the plans after they were presented to the council by Mayor Chris Cooke.

Following the decision, he said: “The town centre plan is backed by more than 5 million pounds initially and of seed funding, which we've managed to reprofile as we've got government grants in [and] as we've managed to acquire more investment from Middlesbrough.

“That means that we're able to free up existing money to be able to do these projects. It's really important that we get this off the ground.

“Within one small space, we'll now have a music theatre, late-night venue, a theatre and a museum. And that's really a lot of the ingredients that you need for a successful town centre.

“There will be more projects to come as we roll through the process so keep following for updates.”

The ten-year-plan aims to address how the way people work, shop and spend their leisure time is “changing more rapidly than ever before”.

The council said that significant grant funding at a regional and national level for the projects, which are at different stages of development, will also be vital.

The demolition and repurposing of current retail units is one of the key projects, with the council saying it only wants to retain a third of the current retail space in the town.

The plan also includes building of thousands of homes in Middlehaven and Gresham and, inspired by other projects across Europe, removing and replacing empty buildings with attractive urban spaces.

  Plans for the Academic Centre of Excellence for Surgery at James Cook University Hospital

The headline theatre project would see a roughly 2,000 seat venue built just off Centre Square, with one of the top theatre operators in the country developing the site.

It would be inspired by the Troubadour Theatre at Canary Wharf in London, where The Hunger Games on Stage is currently being held.

Other projects include a major taxi hub, playable spaces for young people, and an Academic Centre of Excellence for Surgery at the James Cook University Hospital.

The proposals come alongside a new ‘Place Leaders Partnership’ aiming to deliver long-lasting change has brought together the council, Teesside University, Middlesbrough College, Tees Valley Combined Authority and Middlesbrough Football Club.

Significant employers including AV Dawson and Behaviour UK also joined Cleveland Police, NHS organisations and the North East Chamber of Commerce on the partnership.

It aims to make Middlesbrough “the most creative place for young people to live, learn and realise their future”.

A consultation involving 1,300 young people, their parents and carers informed the plans which aim to respond to challenges felt nationwide and drive key themes.
