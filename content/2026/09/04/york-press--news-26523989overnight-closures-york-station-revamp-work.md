There will be more overnight road closures as ongoing work to revamp the area outside York station takes a step forward.

City of York Council said there will be overnight road closures in Station Road on Monday, September 14, and Tuesday, September 15, to allow for works at the crossing area in front of the station’s entrance.

The council said the section of road directly outside the station will be closed from 8pm to 5.30am both nights.

It means there will be no through traffic past the station, with drivers facing a diversion along the York inner ring road.

  The area in Station Road, York, that will be closed overnight on September 14 and 15 

*(Image: City of York Council)*

A council spokesperson said drivers will be able to access the station’s entrance during the closure.

Taxis, Blue Badge holders and anyone picking up or dropping off passengers in the short station’s stay car park will continue to have access at all times, they said.

It will only be taxis that are able to access York station’s entrance from the Blossom Street end of Station Road and on exit from Tea Room Square, the council said.

All other vehicles must turn left in the direction of Lendal Bridge after leaving the station.

  Cllr Kate Ravilious, City of York Council's executive member for transport 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Cllr Kate Ravilious, the council’s executive member for transport, said it is “hugely grateful for everyone’s continued patience while our contractors finish off the highways work. Like everyone, we are keen to see the works finished as quickly and safely as possible.”

She added: “Over recent days and weeks people will have noticed more and more areas opening up, including the seating next to the city walls and it has been great to see people starting to use and enjoy them.”

The council has faced criticism over a £34 million overspend on the Station Gateway project work, and from campaigners who have questioned why new cycle lanes outside the station are yet to be opened.

  A closed cycle lane outside York station 

*(Image: York Cycle Campaign)*

Cllr Ravilious said the latest closures “come as we near the end of the highways elements of work and will allow us to open up more areas. This will include the cycle lanes, which we are aiming to open by early October.”

  The council has said it **understood “residents’ frustration”** over the costs involved with the Station Gateway scheme, the bill for which currently stands at £60.1 million.

The council insisted that it has “strengthened governance arrangements for current and future major projects”, meaning “cost increases are far less likely to happen”.

  It followed more than 1,400 people signing an **online petition** calling on the council to explain “how a £26 million project became a £60 million project”.

  It also came after The Press revealed that some of history’s most iconic structures, such as the Eiffel Tower and the Empire State Building, were **built in less time than the Station Gateway project.**
