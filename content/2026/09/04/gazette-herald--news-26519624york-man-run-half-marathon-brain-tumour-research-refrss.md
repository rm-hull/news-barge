A York NHS worker is preparing to take on a half marathon for a cause close to his heart.

Piotr Pacych will compete in the Robin Hood Half Marathon on September 27, to raise funds for Brain Tumour Research after being diagnosed with a brain cavernoma in 2024.

The 26-year-old said: "When I was first told of the diagnosis, I was frightened.

"But I was lucky.

"Although I had symptoms like headaches, the condition did not threaten my life.

"While I was fortunate, many people diagnosed with brain tumours or cancer are not.

"Some face a difficult battle to prolong their lives, and that’s why this cause means so much to me."

He works as a personal assistant in the research and development team at Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust and has pledged to use his experience to support future research through fundraising.

As a mark of solidarity, Mr Pacych has shaved his head and will remain bald until race day in support of those affected by brain tumours.

He said: "I want to raise funds to support research that could bring hope to people who are less fortunate than me.

"Shaving my head is my way of showing solidarity.

"Brain tumours kill more people under 40 than any other cancer, yet historically only a small proportion of national cancer research funding has been allocated to this disease.

"Breakthrough treatments and advances in diagnosis could improve outcomes and help people live longer, fuller lives, which is why I’m running for such a good cause."

Mr Pacych began training for the half marathon in May, gradually building up his endurance after starting with short jogs and walks.

Despite struggling with shin splints, he now runs at least 10km twice a week and has competed in shorter races to prepare for the event.

He said: "I’ve never really been much of a runner, but I really enjoyed taking part in our Trust’s TEWV 10k last year, and it kind of inspired me to do more.

"In August I completed the Jane Tomlinson 10k Run for All in York, in a time of 58 minutes, which I was pleased with.

"It was hot but I kept a steady pace, which was good training.

"I’ve never actually run a full half marathon before, but I’m determined to get across the finish line and raise as much as I can for a charity which helps so many people."

Brain Tumour Research is a national charity that funds scientific research and supports a network of Centres of Excellence across the UK.

Donations can be made through Mr Pacych's Brain Tumour Research fundraising page: [https://www.justgiving.com/page/piotr-pacych-1](https://www.justgiving.com/page/piotr-pacych-1)
