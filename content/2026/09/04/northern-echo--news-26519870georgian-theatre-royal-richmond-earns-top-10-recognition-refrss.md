A Georgian playhouse in Richmond has been named one of Britain’s most underrated wonders.

The Georgian Theatre Royal in Richmond earned its place in The Telegraph’s top 10 list, which celebrates lesser-known British landmarks that deserve greater national attention.

Travel writer Paul Bloomfield described the theatre as "miraculously intact" and praised its rare glimpse into the theatrical world of the 18th century.

Jo Mawhinney, chief executive of the Georgian Theatre Royal, said: "We couldn’t be more pleased to see the Georgian Theatre Royal recognised by The Telegraph.

"The theatre is a remarkable survivor, but it is also a living, working theatre.

"Visitors can step through the doors and experience a building that has been at the heart of theatrical life in Richmond for more than 235 years.

"It’s particularly lovely to see Richmond itself celebrated as part of the recommendation.

"The theatre is just one of the many reasons to visit our wonderful town, and we hope the feature encourages more people to discover us for themselves."

Built in 1788 by actor-manager Samuel Butler, the theatre is the oldest working theatre in its original form in the UK.

It remains a vibrant venue, hosting drama, comedy, music, family entertainment, and community productions throughout the year.

Visitors can also take part in the Georgian Theatre Experience, an immersive guided tour that explores the building’s history, the lives of Georgian actors and audiences, and its historic stage scenery.

The Telegraph feature highlighted not only the theatre but also the wider appeal of Richmond, including its Norman castle, Easby Abbey, and regional delicacies like Wensleydale cheese and Yorkshire tea loaf.

The Georgian Theatre Royal was listed alongside High Force in County Durham, providing a strong regional connection.

Details about tours and upcoming performances are available at www.georgiantheatreroyal.co.uk.
