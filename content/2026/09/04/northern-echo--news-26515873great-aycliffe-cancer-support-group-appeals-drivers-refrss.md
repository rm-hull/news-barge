A County Durham cancer charity has warned its vital patient transport service is at risk as it urgently appeals for more volunteer drivers.

Great Aycliffe Cancer Support Group provides free transport to cancer patients across County Durham and Darlington, where demand has soared.

Its volunteer drivers take patients to chemotherapy, radiotherapy, scans, surgery and other hospital appointments.

  Allison White from Newton Aycliffe who is looking for people to transport Cancer patients to Hospital 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Founder Allison White BEM said some patients would not know how to reach treatment without the service.

She said: "Our free patient transport service is absolutely vital. We are taking cancer patients from across County Durham and Darlington to chemotherapy, radiotherapy, scans, operations and other hospital appointments.

"For some patients, without our volunteer drivers, they simply wouldn’t know how they were going to get to their treatment."

The charity is urgently seeking more drivers to keep the service running.

  Allison White from Newton Aycliffe who is looking for people to transport Cancer patients to Hospital 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Allison, 49, from Newton Aycliffe, set up the group in 2014 after being diagnosed with leukaemia in 2009.

The group has become a lifeline for thousands of patients, carers and relatives across County Durham, Darlington and North Yorkshire.

It also provides emotional support, information and social activities.

But the charity is under growing pressure. Allison said monthly running costs have soared from around £400 when the group began to more than £6,000.

Allison said: "When we started, it cost about £400 a month. Now it is well over £6,000, which just goes to show how busy we are and also how needed the service is."

  Allison White from Newton Aycliffe who is looking for people to transport Cancer patients to Hospital 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She added: "We always try to go the extra mile for them and that is the whole aim about what we do."

Cancer patient Sophie Harding, 41, knows how vital the transport service is.

Sophie was diagnosed with bilateral multifocal grade three, stage three breast cancer in September 2025.

She has since undergone chemotherapy and surgery and is now receiving daily radiotherapy at James Cook University Hospital in Middlesbrough.

Reaching appointments has been a major challenge for Sophie and her fiancée, Emma Leach, 37.

  Sophie Harding with her fiancee Emma Leitch 

*(Image: PROVIDED)*

Emma lost her driving licence on medical grounds because of a chronic illness, leaving the couple worried about how Sophie would get to hospital.

Great Aycliffe Cancer Support Group stepped in, with volunteer drivers taking Sophie to chemotherapy and oncology appointments at James Cook and Bishop Auckland Hospitals, her surgery in Hartlepool and her daily radiotherapy appointments.

Emma said the charity "just saved us, really", while Sophie called it a "godsend".

Emma said: "The charity treats Sophie like a human, and me, and we found that quite often you don't get that from others, if that makes any sense.

"We are just so grateful for this charity."

Sophie is walking 10,000 steps a day to raise money for the charity before she finishes radiotherapy.

The charity is run entirely by volunteers and pays drivers’ mileage expenses.

Drivers must be aged 25 or over, have their own car and hold a full UK driving licence.

Allison said drivers can make a difference even if they can spare only a few hours.

She said: "Every new driver means another cancer patient we can say “yes, we can get you there” to."
