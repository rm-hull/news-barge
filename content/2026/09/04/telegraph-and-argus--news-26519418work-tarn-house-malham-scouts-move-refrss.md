The 'mountain retreat' of a Victorian philanthropist is undergoing restoration.

Historic Tarn House, at Malham, in the Yorkshire Dales, is being renovated before its new scouting tenants move in later this year.

The former home of philanthropist and MP Walter Morrison, the propertywill be handed over to South Craven Scouts after the work is completed.

The district scout group, which stretches from Settle to Glusburn, including Upper Wharfedale and Skipton, has signed a 20-year lease with owners, the National Trust, for both Tarn House and the nearby High Stables.

Together, the buildings will become an outdoor hub offering residential stays for scouts and other young people to enjoy and learn about nature and the outdoors.

Grade two listed Tarn House, which is passed by the Pennine Way, is currently surrounded by barriers which bear information telling the history of Tarn House from its origins in the 1780s as a shooting lodge.

After decades as a field studies centre, the building came back under the direct care of the National Trust once again in 2023. Now - after securing the new tenancy in July - the charity is taking the opportunity to carry out some essential conservation works.

Craig Gillon, facilities manager for the Malham Tarn Estate, says the work will ensure health and safety compliance as well as restoring decorative elements.

He said: “We have worked with architects to restore the building in a way that will preserve original features while keeping the maintenance manageable.”

The work needed includes a new fire alarm and fire safety work, and ensuring all external and internal fixtures are repaired to National Trust standards.

One particularly exciting update will be repairs to the iron and glass canopies at the front of the mansion looking out to the tarn, says the charity.

“These much loved features will be restored to their former glory,” said Mr Gillon.

“We are excited to get the building ready, so lots of new memories can be made and further generations can connect with this amazing landscape."

As the 19th century 'mountain retreat' of Walter Morrison, the estate was visited by influential figures of the day including Charles Darwin and author Charles Kingsley who it is said was inspired while there to write The Water Babies.

  Tarn House pictured in 2013 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

In 1946 it was gifted to the National Trust by the niece of Mr Morrison after it failed to sale at auction. It was used as a centre for the Field Studies Council for more than 70 years until 2022.

It is hoped the High Stables will be used by the scouts for the first time at Easter next year.

Andy Stronach, South Craven District, Lead Volunteer, said: "This is a very exciting opportunity for South Craven Scouts and we are very grateful to the National Trust for choosing us as their preferred tenant. It will be great to be able to offer a Scout site in the Yorkshire Dales for everyone to enjoy.

"The new site will play an important role in expanding access to nature-based experiences, supporting the growth and development of young people across the region while making full use of the natural landscape around Malham Tarn."
