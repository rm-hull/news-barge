FLU vaccinations are now underway to protect vulnerable groups in West Yorkshire ahead of winter.

The first phase of the programme began this week, with children and pregnant women among the first to be offered the free jab.

Vaccinations will be delivered in pharmacies and community settings to improve access.

Dr James Thomas, GP and medical director at the West Yorkshire Health and Care Partnership, said: "The flu vaccine takes a matter of minutes but is a crucial way of protecting yourself, particularly for those groups who are most vulnerable to serious illness.

"By getting vaccinated you can help prevent yourself and those around you becoming unwell and needing urgent healthcare services during winter."

He also encouraged pregnant women to speak to their healthcare providers.

Dr Thomas said: "If you’re pregnant, speak to your maternity team, GP or community pharmacy about getting protected, and if your child is eligible, please take up the offer when it comes."

Flu is more than just a bad cold and can lead to serious illness, especially in children and pregnant women who are at higher risk of complications.

Most school-aged children will receive the nasal spray vaccine at school.

Parents of two and three-year-olds, and children in clinical risk groups, will be invited to book an appointment at their GP practice.

Pregnant women can get the jab at any stage of pregnancy.

Adults generally receive the vaccine by injection.

From October 1, additional eligible groups will be invited for vaccination.

This includes people aged 65 and over, adults with certain long-term conditions, care home residents, carers, close contacts of people with weakened immune systems, people experiencing homelessness, and frontline health and social care workers.

Appointments can be booked through the National Booking Service.

From October, parents of two- and three-year-olds will also be able to book appointments at participating community pharmacies through the same service.

Young people are also being reminded to book first and second doses of the MenB vaccine under a one-off programme.

First-year university students are around seven times more likely to develop meningococcal disease compared to their non-university peers.

The offer for first doses runs until December 31, 2026, with second doses available until March 31, 2027.

The MenB vaccine is available through participating community pharmacies.
