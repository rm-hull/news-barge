A man who subjected a woman to a "terrifying and traumatic" sexual assault has been jailed for five years.

Matthew Thomas Banks, 40, of Wheldrake, near York, was sentenced at York Crown Court on Friday, September 4, after admitting four sexual assaults on the day he was due to stand trial.

Following his arrest Banks had initially denied the offences during police interviews.

However, forensic evidence supported the victim's account and linked him to the crimes.

In a personal statement, the woman said: “I feel less of a human, less of a woman, and damaged goods because of this event. It has affected almost every aspect of my life and it will take a lot of time to fully heal from this.”

  


She said five years later she continues to suffer from panic attacks, night terrors, flashbacks, anxiety and episodes of dissociation, leaving her struggling at times to reconnect with the present.

Hannah Turner, prosecuting, said the police investigation had been delayed by three years because Banks had initially refused to give police a sample of his DNA so that they could link him forensically with the crime. He finally provided one in 2024. There was further delay because the officer then investigating the case had been on sick leave.

Banks, of Broad Highway, Wheldrake, pleaded guilty to four sexual assaults on the day he was due to stand trial.

He was jailed for five years and put on the sex offenders' register for life.

For Banks, Nicholas Hammond said he had pleaded guilty. He handed in references from Banks’ employer and others which he said “speak to a man very different from the man who behaved in this way on that night in 2021".

Detective Constable Sarah Scott, of York and Selby CID, said: "Banks subjected his victim to a terrifying and traumatic ordeal, which she showed immense courage in reporting to police.

"Not only did he commit these serious offences, but he continued to deny them, only admitting his guilt on the first day of trial. In doing so, he caused further distress to the victim, who faced the prospect of reliving her experience in court.

"Despite the significant impact these offences had on her, the victim remained committed to seeking justice. She bravely provided detailed accounts to investigators and supported the prosecution throughout the case, helping to ensure Banks was held accountable for his actions.

"I hope this sentence provides some closure to her, and reassures other victims that sexual offences are treated with the utmost seriousness by both the police and the courts. North Yorkshire Police remains fully committed to supporting victims, bringing offenders to justice, and protecting those who have experienced sexual violence and abuse.”
