Plans have been lodged for a flagship science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM) centre in a “major milestone” for Darlington's Science Park.

The "world-class" facility is hoping to deliver a “transformational education and skills” designed to support the region’s workforce.

It will feature specialist STEM spaces including science laboratories, data science and immersive learning facilities, and a flexible 200-seat lecture theatre.

Located withing Darlington's Central Park, it will be situated alongside major organisations in the sector including Teesside University, CPI and Innovation Central.

Today (September 3), regeneration specialist Cityheart has confirmed that a planning application for the centre has been formally submitted.

Delivered by Darlington Borough Council as part of a partnership with Cityheart, the centre will help address growing demand for STEM skills.

  Plans for a landmark STEM Centre at Darlington Science Park 

*(Image: CITYHEART)*

It also hopes to create clear pathways into high-value careers while supporting employers across Darlington, Teesside, and the wider North East.

Darlington College was "instrumental" in developing the original concept, with the scheme being funded through the government’s Growth Mission Fund and Pride in Place.

The project forms part of a wider programme of investment and regeneration across Darlington and will further strengthen Central Park’s position as a leading hub for education, research, innovation and enterprise.

A decision on the plans is expected before the end of the year.

James Litherland, development director at Cityheart, said: “This is an important step towards delivering a facility that will create lasting opportunities for people across Darlington and the wider region.

“Since signing our development agreement with the Council in May, we have wasted no time in bringing the STEM Centre forward at pace.

“We are proud to be working with fantastic partners on a state-of-the-art facility that will help connect education directly with the industries driving economic growth and opportunities across the region.”

Councillor Chris McEwan, deputy leader of the council and cabinet member for economy at Darlington Borough Council, said: “The submission of this planning application, which will now be subject to due process, marks another significant milestone in the delivery of a project which, if approved, will have a lasting impact on our town.

“A STEM Centre will support local people in accessing the skills, education and opportunities needed to secure high value careers in science, technology, engineering and mathematics, while supporting businesses to recruit and develop talent locally.”

David Gartland, principal and CEO of Darlington College, said: “This investment represents a significant opportunity for both the college and the wider community, helping to equip people with the knowledge and technical skills required for the industries of tomorrow.

“We look forward to continuing to work with our partners as the project progresses and to delivering a building that will bring lasting benefits.

“The development will create world-class facilities for learners and strengthen the skills pipeline that local employers need to support future growth.”
