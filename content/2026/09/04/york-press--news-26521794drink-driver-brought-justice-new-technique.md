The police investigation that led to the conviction of a drink driver who ran over tragic teenager Bailey Chadwick made history.

Joseph Stott, 25, was not arrested until four days after Bailey’s death near Pateley Bridge on July 20, 2025, and therefore could not be breathalysed to produce evidence of him drink driving.

But he pleaded guilty to driving while unfit through drink after the police used a technique called “forward calculation” and other evidence to show that his driving must have been affected by alcohol.

It is the first time North Yorkshire Police has secured a conviction using the technique.

Detective Inspector Jill Cowling, of the force's serious collision investigation unit, said: “This investigation required a significant commitment from officers and staff across North Yorkshire Police, and I would like to acknowledge the professionalism, dedication and determination shown by all those involved.

“I am also immensely proud of my investigation team and the many specialist departments who contributed their expertise, including roads policing officers, forensic specialists, intelligence staff, communications teams, and police staff who worked tirelessly to ensure every possible line of enquiry was pursued.

“While the outcome at court cannot ease the pain suffered by Bailey's family, it demonstrates our commitment to conducting thorough and professional investigations and holding offenders accountable for their actions."

Stott, formerly of Dairy Lane, Darley, Harrogate, and now living at an undisclosed address in West Yorkshire, also admitted driving without insurance. He was given a 12-month community order and banned from driving for 20 months at Harrogate Magistrates' Court yesterday.

He was never charged with causing Bailey’s death because, the court heard, a prosecution collision investigator declared the collision was unavoidable. The 19-year-old was lying in the road on the B6265 near Pateley Bridge in Nidderdale in the dark, wearing dark clothing.

The court saw CCTV of Stott buying round after round of wine, Jagermeister, beer and cocktails and drinking as he and his partner spent hours in two pubs in Headingley before he drove home.

It also saw film of Stott slurring his words after leaving the pubs. The prosecution said the footage also showed that he was unsteady on his feet – this was disputed by the defence.

With the "forward calculation" technique, a prosecution expert used the CCTV to estimate the level of alcohol in his body when he drove home. The defence disputed his calculations, but did not produce its own expert.

Det Insp Cowling said: “Before anything else, I want to acknowledge Bailey's family and loved ones.

"The loss of a child, sibling, family member or friend in such sudden and tragic circumstances is something no family should ever have to endure.

“Throughout this investigation, our thoughts have remained with Bailey's family as they continue to navigate the profound grief, trauma and loss caused by his death.

“I thank the community of Pateley Bridge and the wider public for the support they provided throughout what has been a complex and extensive investigation.

"The information, assistance and patience shown by local residents and members of the public were invaluable in helping investigators establish the circumstances surrounding this collision.

“Bailey's death is also a stark reminder of the devastating consequences that can arise when individuals choose to drive after consuming alcohol.

“Every road death leaves behind families, friends and communities whose lives are changed forever.

“Road safety is everyone's responsibility, and we would urge all motorists to consider the potentially life-changing consequences of their decisions before getting behind the wheel.

“Finally, I would like to pay tribute to Bailey. At the centre of this investigation was a young man whose life was tragically cut short. Our thoughts remain firmly with those who knew and loved him."
