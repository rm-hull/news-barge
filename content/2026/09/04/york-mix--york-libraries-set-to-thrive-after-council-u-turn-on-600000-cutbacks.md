A contentious council plan to force York’s library service into £600,000 worth of cuts has been dropped.

It means the service, run by Explore York, will be able to “focus on the future” and “thrive”, its bosses have said.

The Labour-run City of York Council announced in 2024 that it intended to cut its funding to Explore York by £300,000 a year for two years – even though that meant tearing up an agreed contract.

The administration said it was left with no choice due to a ‘financial black hole’ left by the previous Lib Dem-Green administration.

But the Lib Dems then launched a campaign to Save Our Libraries. The cutbacks were later deferred for a year.

Now, a report to next week’s City of York Council executive meeting reveals the council has dropped the plan, saying “now is not the right time to make changes to the service”.

**Chief executive of Explore York J** enny Layfield welcomed the news.

“Explore is a trusted and valued part of life in York,” she said. “We are proud of the difference our staff, volunteers, members and supporters make every day and pleased that we can now focus our attention on the future of the service rather than potential reductions.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/jenny-layfield-chief-executive-explore-york-libraries-archives.jpg)

“As a community-owned organisation, we believe local libraries and archives have an important role to play in supporting learning, culture, heritage and community life across York.

“We look forward to working with communities and partners to ensure these treasured local assets continue to thrive for future generations.”

## ‘Major victory’

**Cllr Andrew Hollyer, York Liberal Democrat spokesperson for housing, planning and safer communities,** said: “This is a major victory for York’s libraries and for the more than 3,000 residents who joined our campaign against Labour’s £600,000 cut.

“Labour insisted savings had to be made from the library service. We rejected that choice and warned that it could put staff, local services and York’s mobile library at risk.

“Libraries are at the heart of their communities. They tackle isolation, help people access computers and support, provide places to learn and bring residents together.

“Labour’s own report now recognises that this is not the right time to make changes to the service. That would not have happened without thousands of residents making their opposition clear.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/explore-york-central-library-interior-ground-floor.webp)

“This is a Liberal Democrat campaign victory, but above all it belongs to everyone who signed the petition and stood up for York’s libraries.”

**Executive member for economy and culture, Cllr Pete Kilbane** said: “While we welcome cross-party input into decision making, York Liberal Democrat Party’s campaign was so riddled with inaccuracy and alarmist claims that it hindered rather than helped this process.

“Days after our decision was published, Cllr Widdowson was still claiming that the libraries were facing cuts.

“We appreciate everyone’s passion for our libraries and sympathise with those who were duped by the Liberal Democrats’ misleading arguments.

“As a responsible administration we cannot bury our heads in the sand and pretend the council’s finances are fine. So, we have worked with Explore to look at options for delivering efficiencies and sustainable savings.

“At the same time, we are changing the way services are delivered in York so they’re more efficient and better connected to local communities.

:In this context, the library savings proved difficult to achieve while maintaining the key role that libraries play in our neighbourhoods. We look forward to continuing to work with Explore to provide a great library service to the people of York.”
