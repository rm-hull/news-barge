A consultant radiologist who spent decades transforming cancer care has retired after nearly 40 years of service.

Dr William Thompson, 69, from Stockton, played a key role in developing breast imaging and screening services for patients across Teesside during his career at the University Hospital of North Tees.

He qualified in 1980 and began working at the hospital in 1988, where he set up the first breast imaging service.

Dr Thompson said: "I happened to arrive at exactly the right time and put myself forward for something I never expected would become such a major part of my career.

"I thought it would take up a small amount of my time, but as it turned out, it became my life's work."

Before his arrival, patients had to travel to Middlesbrough or Leeds for mammograms.

Shortly after he started, the hospital acquired its first mammography machine through the North Tees Women’s Cancer Appeal.

Dr Thompson became the hospital’s first consultant radiologist for breast imaging.

In 1990, North Tees was chosen as the site for the new breast screening centre in the Tees Valley as part of the national NHS Breast Screening Programme.

Dr Thompson led breast imaging services for nearly 30 years before stepping down in 2017.

He said: "I'll miss the work and I will miss the people.

"I've worked alongside supportive, talented colleagues and I wish them all well for the future."

Colleagues praised his dedication and leadership at a retirement meal.

Throughout his career, Dr Thompson said he was driven by the difference his work could make to patients.

He said: "But ultimately, everything we do should help patients.

"One day, any of us could find ourselves on the other side of that interaction, and I want people to be treated with the same care and respect that I would hope to receive myself."
