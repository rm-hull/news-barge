Passengers are once again travelling between Saltburn's seafront and town centre, after one of the town's most iconic attractions reopened following a major restoration project.

Saltburn Cliff Tramway reopened on Friday, August 28, after its historic carriages were replaced and major engineering upgrades completed.

The attraction closed in January for work to secure its future while preserving its Victorian character.

The new-look carriages arrived back in Saltburn in July, paving the way for the tramway's return to service a month later.

Visitors can now travel between Saltburn's seafront and town centre in newly built carriages that have been carefully modelled on the Victorian originals.

The replacement carriages have been individually handcrafted by Teesside craftsmen around steel frames, preserving the tramway's historic look with modern construction methods.

The new carriages feature oak interiors, arched ceilings and larger windows, giving passengers panoramic views of the coastline.

Two stained-glass windows created by local artist Chloe Buck in 1991 have been restored and reinstated, with the remaining panels due to be installed later this year.

Engineers have also upgraded the tramway to improve reliability and safety.

The work includes a new hydraulic braking system, upgraded buffers, improved hydraulic equipment and safer door mechanisms.

The steel undercarriages were stripped, serviced and restored, while larger water tanks should make the system more efficient without changing its traditional water-balanced operation.

Both stations were also upgraded, including repairs to the original water-fill valve and restoration of the ship's wheel used to operate the tramway.

At the lower station, repaired flooring and an improved entrance have created a more welcoming arrival point for visitors.

A spokesperson for the attraction said: "What an incredible day as we welcomed passengers back to the Saltburn Cliff Tramway for the first time following its refurbishment.

"Seeing the tramway back in operation and filled with smiling passengers once again was a proud moment for everyone involved in the project.

"Thank you for your patience during the refurbishment works and for giving such a warm welcome to the tramway's beautiful new handcrafted carriages."
