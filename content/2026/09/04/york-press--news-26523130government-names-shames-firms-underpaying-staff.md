A top York restaurant and a city dental practice have been ‘named and shamed’ in a government list of employers underpaying staff.

In total, around 660 employers were named for failing to pay staff the National Minimum Wage, with £4 million returned directly to workers.

Some 27,000 workers had been denied the pay they were legally owed, with penalties totalling £7 million issued to the employers responsible.

The government says this week’s naming round is the latest step in its commitment to publish naming lists more regularly so that bosses are swiftly held to account and make improvements as soon as possible.

Since first launching in 2011, more than £100 million in penalties have been issued to over 5,200 employers, leading to over £66 million being repaid to 650,000 workers.

In York, Roots York Restaurant Ltd, is listed for failing to pay £614.36 to 18 workers.

Mr Robert D Glover, trading as The Mount Dental Practice, also failed to pay £1,361.07 to 2 workers.

The list also says Comforting Hands Recruitment Ltd, Harrogate, also failed to pay £17,980.98 to 29 workers.

Roots York Restaurants Ltd is the group headed by Michelin-starred chef Tommy Banks. It runs Roots York on Marygate, the Black Swan at Oldstead and The Abbey Inn, Byland.

A company statement said: “We are truly sorry to the 18 members of staff who were underpaid and very grateful to the National Minimum Wage Naming Scheme list for bringing the discrepancy in our company handbook's wording to our attention.

“The miscalculation related to a genuine oversight around company uniform. While we provide our staff at our pub, The Abbey Inn, with aprons and T-shirts, we ask them in our handbook to wear their own jeans and trainers. Since these are everyday items, we hadn't recognised the requirement to reimburse staff for buying and wearing their own items.”

“We now understand that any uniform stipulation, however loose, should be recognised in our team's pay. All affected employees have now been fully reimbursed, and we have sought guidance from both the Fair Work Agency and an independent employment specialist to ensure our processes are fair, accurate, and fully compliant going forward.”

The statement added: “We value our staff wholeheartedly - they are the beating heart of our business. We aim to do everything within our means to reward our employees fairly for the incredible work they do every day.”

The Mount Dental Practice declined to comment to The Press.

Minister for the Future of Work Kate Dearden said: “Underpaying your staff is illegal, and we will not let workers foot the bill for their boss failing to follow the rules.

“We’re making sure workers get the hard-earned pay they deserve, and where employers fall short, they will be held to account.

“Every employer should check their payroll now and reach out to Acas if they need further support.”
