A vicar has issued an amnesty of 5pm today (September 4) after a carved oak statue of St Cuthbert was stolen from a church in the Bradford district.

The statue, which has been in place at St Mary’s Church in Oxenhope since 1938, was taken sometime between 9am on Monday, August 31, and 5pm on Tuesday, September 1.

The church's vicar told the T&A that the theft has "deeply saddened" the church community, particularly its older members who give up their time maintaining the site.

## 'A place of peace to pray'

The Rev Denise Angela Hayes, vicar of St Mary’s Church, said: "St Mary’s is open every day between 9am-5pm as it provides a place of peace to pray.

"Different people from our community and visitors to the area use church and value the quiet space if offers."

Rev Hayes is baffled why anyone would have taken the statue - which is of little value.

  The vicar is hoping the statue is returned 

*(Image: T&A)*

## 'The first time anything has been taken'

She said: "This is the first time anything has been taken and for me the saddest part is the effect it has had on our older church members.

"Our congregation give great love and care to the church, several of them are well into their eighties and are still serving opening and closing church daily, maintaining both the inside and the grounds and this has hurt them.

"I cannot understand why St Cuthbert has been taken, whether it was a prank/dare or because someone thinks it’s valuable.

## St Cuthbert has stood for nearly 90 years

The missing figure is one of several carved statues that has stood in the Grade II listed church for more than 80 years.

St Cuthbert, depicted holding the head of the Northumbrian King Oswald, has been in situ for just under 90 years.

Rev Hayes said that St Cuthbert, like all the other statues, is a key part of the church’s heritage, holding great meaning for the congregation.

## How was he stolen?

The church's vicar explained to the T&A that the figurine was "situated high up over our main communion table" and, meaning made of oak, was by no means light to carry.

She added: "It was screwed down but it’s been twisted off.

"Thankfully there was no mess, and nothing else was taken.

St Cuthbert is approximately 2ft tall, so not easy to hide or carry.

"There must be people who know who has taken him."

## St Cuthbert amnesty until 5pm

The theft has prompted the church to offer a chance for the figure to be returned without consequence.

Rev Hayes said: "We are having a St Cuthbert amnesty until 5pm Friday so if anyone has taken him as a dare or prank, they have this opportunity to return him.

"We can all make wrong choices without thinking about the consequences of our actions, I’d like to think whoever took St Cuthbert did this and on reflection would like to return him."

## Who was St Cuthbert?

St Cuthbert was a seventh-century Northumbrian monk, bishop and hermit who became one of the most important saints in northern England.

Associated with Melrose and Lindisfarne, he travelled across Northumbria preaching before retreating to Inner Farne, where he died in 687.

Following Viking raids, his community carried his remains across the region before they were brought to Durham in 995; his shrine at Durham Cathedral became an important place of pilgrimage.

He is remembered as the patron saint of Northumbria and as a lasting symbol of the region’s Christian heritage.

## The Saint's link to Yorkshire

St Cuthbert actually had a direct historical link with Yorkshire through Ripon, where he served as guest-master at a new monastery in the mid-seventh century before returning to Melrose after St Wilfrid took control of the community.

His relics were also taken to Ripon briefly in 995 as monks fled renewed Viking threats before continuing to Durham.

His long-lasting regional influence is reflected in Yorkshire church dedications, including St Cuthbert’s in Wrose and churches or joint dedications in places such as Fishlake, Crayke and Bolton Abbey.
