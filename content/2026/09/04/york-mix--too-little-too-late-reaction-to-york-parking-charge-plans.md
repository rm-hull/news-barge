Plans to cut some parking charges in York do not go far enough to reverse the effects of previous hikes, opposition councillors and a trader have said.

City of York Council opposition Liberal Democrat economy spokesperson Cllr Ashley Mason and Conservative Cllr Mike Nicholls both said the Labour administration should have brought in cheaper half-hour tariffs.

Ruth Phillips, co-owner of Bishopthorpe Road’s Good Food Shop, said proposed cuts to Minster Badge costs would not provide enough support to residents and traders struggling amid difficult economic times.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, the council’s Labour transport spokesperson, said short-stay parking offers would clog up York’s after last year’s hikes had begun to get people out of their cars.

It comes as proposals to cut the cost of Minster Badge residents parking discount permits are set for a decision on Wednesday, September 9.

Permits would be £15 cheaper, costing £15-a-year rather than £45 for two years, and discounts in community areas would increase to 50 per cent if the changes go ahead.

It would see discounts for the Bishopthorpe Road, East Parade and Rowntree Park car parks and on-street spaces including in Micklegate and The Groves rise from 30 per cent currently.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Bishopthorpe-Road-12.webp)

Reduced rates for Minster Badge holders would not apply in city centre car parks run by the council.

The proposals follow an Arup review into the impact of parking charges in Bishopthorpe Road, Heworth, Micklegate and The Groves launched following a legal challenge over last year’s hikes.

Fees across council-run car parks were hiked in 2025 as part of council efforts to cut car journeys in York by a fifth by 2030.

But the move provoked a backlash from residents, traders and opposition councillors and charges were later lowered at the Bishopthorpe Road car park and for on-street parking in Micklegate.

**Cllr Ravilious** said the latest changes aimed to strike the right balance between helping residents and local businesses and cutting congestion in York.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/york-councillor-ashley-mason-micklegate-bar.webp)

But Liberal Democrat economy spokesperson **Cllr Mason** said the evidence was clear that parking charges were too high.

The Liberal Democrat said: “Instead of wasting money proving the problem exists, Labour should have put it towards making the first 30 minutes of parking cheaper and helping local businesses.

“Local businesses are already being squeezed, the last thing they need is Labour making it even more expensive for their customers to park.

“The proposed Minster Badge discount is welcome, but for many it is too little, too late.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/york-councillor-mike-nicholls.webp)

Conservative **Cllr Nicholls**, of Bishopthorpe ward, said previous hikes had unfairly hit people on lower incomes hardest.

The Conservative said: “The refusal to look again at having some free half-hour slots is a mistake and misunderstands the nature of shopping.

“People popping by will do this in their car rather than scheduling bus trips.

“This small custom is vital for small shopping areas in particular such as on Bishopthorpe Road that a number of my residents use.”

Shop co-owner **Ms Phillips**, also of the Bishopthorpe Road Traders Association, said their calls for low cost short-stay parking had been ignored despite being recommended by Arup’s review.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/parking-sign-micklegate-york-sept-2025-2.webp)

The trader said: “A reduced price Minster Badge doesn’t go far enough in our view.”

Labour Transport Executive Member Cllr Ravilious said they had drawn up proposals for Minster Badges to make parking in shopping areas more affordable after listening to residents’ and traders’ feedback.

She added the needs of the one quarter of York households who do not own a car and visitors coming on public transport also needed to be taken into account.

Cllr Ravilious said: ” Introducing free or 30 minutes parking rates would encourage more short trips and clog the city up even more than it already is, and significantly worsen bus service reliability, so we’ve ruled this out.

“The increase in parking charges has already changed travel habits, with a sizeable increase in walking, cycling and bus use over the last year, all against the backdrop of challenging economic conditions and low consumer confidence.

“Meanwhile, additional parking income is enabling this Labour administration to repair more roads and continue to support vital bus services to rural and suburban areas.”
