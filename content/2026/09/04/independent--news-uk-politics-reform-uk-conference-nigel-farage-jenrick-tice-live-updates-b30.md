Nigel Farage is to announce that he will make Parliament “sit day and night” until all legislation is passed if Reform UK wins the next general election.

The announcement is set to be made in his keynote speech at the party’s conference in Birmingham.

He will also blast the Tories for their “failed” 14 years in government and highlight that Andy Burnham “wants 10 more”.

To bring home his argument, he will state that “time is running out to save Britain” and that the country “doesn’t have 10 years to be saved”.

In the first of his two speeches at the conference, he will say: “Within days of winning the general election, we will introduce dozens of Bills and force Parliament to sit all through summer for seven days a week to pass them.”

It comes as Reform UK has been reported to the police over allegations it breached political donation rules by having polling paid for by an overseas company.

The party took funding from a US firm for three polls it commissioned earlier this year costing £32,500, Channel 4 News reported.

## Company linked to Chelsea FC co-owner Todd Boehly gives £75K to Reform

A company tied to to Chelsea FC co-owner Todd Boehly donated £75,000 to Reform earlier this year

Electoral Commission registrations, which were published today, revealed that Elridge Capital Management Services (UK) LTD gave Nigel Farage’s party the money earlier this year.

Mr Boehly is the chief executive of Eldridge Industries, an American holding company headquartered in Miami.

Eldridge Capital Management Services is ultimately controlled by Mr Boehly and he is listed as a former director.

## Trump suggests he wouldn’t back UK if Argentina invaded Falkland Islands

Andy Burnham is facing increasing pressure to ramp up defence spending after Donald Trump suggested he would not back Britain if Argentina invaded the Falkland Islands, in retaliation for the UK’s stance on the Iran war.

As the row over the sovereignty of the archipelago deepens, Mr Trump again hit out at what he sees as a failure to support his actions in Iran when asked if he would come to the aid of the UK.

“Your country was not there to help me,” he told GB News on Thursday, just days after he said he was reviewing the United States’ position on British sovereignty over the islands.

## Holborn and St Pancras by-election could be ‘make or break’ for Zack Polanski, top pollster warns

The Holborn and St Pancras by-election could be “make or break” for Zack Polanski if he is selected as the Green Party’s candidate, polling guru professor Sir John Curtice has warned.

He told *The Independent* the contest would be a “severe test” of the Green Party leader’s abilities, warning it is likely to be a “tough gig” for the party given how successful Labour has been in the seat historically.

It comes after Mr Polanski, who is currently not an MP but sits on the London Assembly, said he wants to stand in the contest, triggered by the resignation of former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer.

## Lisa Nandy to meet TikTok amid ‘epidemic’ of dangerous behaviour on social media

Lisa Nandy has said she will meet TikTok “in the coming days” as she told MPs there is more to do to address an “epidemic” of young people using social media to encourage dangerous behaviour.

It comes after two police officers died last month when their vehicle collided with a car being driven the wrong way along a dual carriageway in Teesside, killing five young men as well.

## Majority of Britons agree with Burnham that Brexit was ‘wrong turn’ for UK

The majority of Britons agree with Andy Burnham that the country took a wrong turn by leaving the European Union, but they are split over his belief that Margaret Thatcher’s premiership set the country on the wrong path.

Addressing the House of Commons for the first time as prime minister on Tuesday, the former mayor of Greater Manchester took aim at deindustrialisation and privatisation of the 1980s, and the impact of austerity and Brexit on regions of the UK outside of England’s South East and London.

He listed the “series of wrong turns” the UK has made since over the past 40s years and warned that Brexit had embedded the problems in the last decade.

## Reform reported to police over claims of funding rules breach in undercover sting

Reform UK has been reported to the police over allegations it breached political donation rules by having polling paid for by an overseas company.

Nigel Farage’s party took funding from a US firm for three polls it commissioned earlier this year costing £32,500, Channel 4 News reported.

The purported donors were in fact undercover reporters from investigative group Verbatim posing as an American financier and his UK-based son.

## What to expect from the Reform conference

The two-day conference in Birmingham is expected to hear from senior Reform figures setting out their own pledges for their first 100 days in office, as well as an address from Jordan Bardella, leader of the French National Rally.

Nigel Farage is expected to announce that any frontbenchers who fail to achieve those targets “will be sacked”.

He is also due to make his own promise to focus on reducing the cost of living and lead on efforts to end small boat crossings in the Channel.

This summer saw the lowest number of crossings in six years, while the overall number for the year is at its lowest since 2021, but the subject remains a key issue with 16,513 people making the journey so far this year.

## Recap: Reform will win trust of financial markets by being party of 'fiscal responsibility'

Nigel Farage’s party will build credibility with the financial markets by being the party of fiscal responsibility, Robert Jenrick said.

Speaking from the party’s conference, the party’s business spokesperson said: “I want Reform to be different to that so we have to earn the markets’ trust and respect in the period between now and the general election, and in the days immediately after the general election.

“I think it will require urgent action by a chancellor to say it’s different now, we’ve got this, we’re going to behave in a responsible way.”

He added: “Reform is the party of fiscal responsibility.

“The Conservative Party ceded that ground for the first time in a very long time with the events of the mini-budget a couple of years ago.”

If elected, he said his party would unveil a budget within its first 100 days.
