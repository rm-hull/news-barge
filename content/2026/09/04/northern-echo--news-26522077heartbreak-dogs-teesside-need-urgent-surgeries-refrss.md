An animal shelter has launched an urgent fundraising appeal after three dogs in its care were found to have serious medical conditions.

Maxi’s Mates Rescue, at Carlin Howe Farm, says it is facing mounting pressure as increasing numbers of dogs arrive needing specialist care in veterinary treatment.

The Teesside charity, dedicated to stray and abandoned dogs, is now "desperate" for help.

A spokesperson for Maxi's Mates Rescue said: "We really need your help. It’s been a while since we’ve had to ask for help like this, but things are getting into a very difficult state at the moment.

"We have so many dogs coming into our care who need extra help, and we’re really struggling under the pressure.

"Some arrive with medical problems that their previous owners were unaware of, while others come in because owners have passed away with no history at all, making it impossible for us to know what they may have been dealing with.

"At the moment, we have three dogs who need medical attention."

  Maxi's Mates Rescue is appealing for donations to fund urgent surgery for two dogs with serious health conditions. 

*(Image: Maxi's Mates Rescue)*

Sadly, one of the dogs has now reached a point where nothing can be done to correct the situation they are in.

Maxi’s Mates Rescue added: “The dog is not in pain but will have to live with it; that is heartbreaking for us.”

However, the charity hopes treatment can still transform the lives of two other dogs.

One dog has a tumour in its nose which requires surgery, while another has two benign lumps that, although not cancerous, could cause severe problems with passing urine if left untreated.

The animal shelter says both dogs need operations and it is determined to give them the chance of a healthier future.

The appeal comes at a time when the charity says its finances are already stretched by the day-to-day costs of running the kennels and caring for the dogs in its charge.

  Maxi's Mates Rescue is appealing for donations to fund urgent surgery for two dogs with serious health conditions. 

*(Image: Maxi's Mates Rescue)*

A spokesperson added: “We desperately want to give these two dogs the treatment they need and the chance to move forward into happier, healthier lives.

"But our funds are already stretched covering the everyday running costs of the kennels and caring for all the dogs in our care.

"These unexpected veterinary bills are going to be a huge additional burden for us.”

The rescue charity is now asking members of the public to donate whatever they can towards the dogs’ treatment costs.

They said: “Every £1 will help us get these two dogs the medical intervention they desperately need.”

Those unable to donate are being encouraged to share the appeal to help it reach as many people as possible.
