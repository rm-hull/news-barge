## Meet the teenage York busker impressing crowds and cruise ship agent

Jack Bailey performing a Nat King Cole number in Davygate yesterday (Wednesday, September 2) *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

A teenager busker in York is looking to follow in his opera singing mum's footsteps by building a musical career and has already attracted the attention of a cruise ship booking agent.

The Press wanted to find out more about the personalities behind the thriving busking scene in York city centre so we picked out a performance at random from a popular spot at the junction of Davygate and Stonegate on Wednesday morning.

Jack Bailey, 18, from Dunnington, performs across York city centre and we caught up with him after an 80-minute slot which earned praise and applause from passers-by.

Musicians, singers and street performers can be found in places such as Parliament Street, King's Square and St Helen's Square, although no busking pitches are fixed as such and performers are encouraged to spend no more than two hours at one pitch.

The city council says it welcomes buskers and street entertainment to York streets, recognising that they "add colour and vibrancy to public spaces, providing enjoyment for residents, visitors and businesses".

  One of a regular flow support from an audience impressed by Jack's performance in Davygate 

*(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

Jack said: "I have been singing from quite a young age and really enjoy performing for crowds, and it's a good way to make money.

"My older brother James also does it, and we explored busking together when I started out.

"I sing in quite a lot of places in York, and of all them, this one here is the best, with the acoustics created by the taller buildings and also a lot of people queuing outside Betty's.

"It can be hard to get this spot, and there’s lots of good singers in York too.

"If you are in a show, you would do a lot a vocal warm-up before going out to perform, but when you have a busking set list, that's less important as you can build yourself up as you go."

He has also attracted the attention of industry professionals.

Jack said: "I’ve had a cruise ship booking agent come up to me and give me a card.

"He wanted me to fly out with a week’s notice, so we couldn’t make that work, but I am still in touch with him."

The young performer recently completed his studies at CAPA in Wakefield, a specialist performing arts college.

He has been accepted onto an actor musicianship course at Rose Bruford College, a conservatoire in London.

He said: "The busking means maybe I’ll have a bit of money to take with me."

The 80-minute set earned Jack "quite a lot", he said.

Music runs in the family and doesn't end with his brother.

Jack said: "My mum is a professional opera singer, and the family love coming to see us sing.

"My mum taught me, and I am grade eight qualified, which is the highest scale."

Jazz numbers are a focus when performing on the streets and The Press heard a brilliant cover of L-O-V-E, a Nat King Cole song and also a smooth rendition of Dream a Little Dream, recorded by Ella Fitzgerald and Louis Armstrong amongst others.
