A BRADFORD firm says it did not pay minimum wage over an issue with staff wearing black trousers.

Creative Child Ltd, trading as Bamboo Kids, BD4, were one of seven businesses in the city on a Government named and shamed list.

It failed to pay £1,377.26 to 18 workers.

A spokesperson for Creative Child Ltd said the issue related to a previous policy requiring staff to wear black trousers.

The spokesperson added: “We genuinely didn't realise that specifying the colour of the workwear affected the wage calculations, but they said we should have been making sure that employees having to buy black trousers didn't take us under the National Minimum Wage.”

The spokesperson also said all affected staff were repaid in full, additional payments were made, and the company's uniform policy was revised.

## 'Uniform requirements' says another business

Megaplay (Bradford) Ltd in BD8, which failed to pay £1,218.66 to 14 workers, gave a similar explanation, stating: “The underpayment identified by HMRC arose from the way our staff uniform requirements were treated when calculating National Minimum Wage.

“At the time, we were not aware that the costs associated with our uniform requirements could have an impact on National Minimum Wage calculations.”

The company said the issue was a “genuine misunderstanding of the regulations” and that all affected employees were repaid immediately after it was brought to its attention.

## What else have companies been saying?

Speaking to the T&A, BD4 company City Glass Works Ltd, which failed to pay £2,500.80 to a worker, said its case involved a worker renting accommodation provided by the business.

A company spokesperson said the employee had been paid at the applicable wage rate, but National Minimum Wage accommodation offset rules created a calculated shortfall.

They said: “This was not a deliberate attempt to underpay the employee, and the issue arose from our misunderstanding of how the separate rental arrangement affected the minimum wage calculation.”

The company said the worker had been repaid in full and that additional compliance checks had since been introduced.

A spokesperson for Crown Foods (Bradford) Ltd in BD8, which failed to pay £1,495.82 to 17 workers, said the underpayment arose from an error in calculations rather than a deliberate decision to underpay staff.

The company said the money was repaid shortly after HMRC raised the issue.

The spokesperson said: “As soon as we were alerted, we made the payment.

“It wasn't deliberate. It was a mistake in working things out.

“We reviewed the system and made changes to prevent it happening again.”

IBI Healthcare Ltd, trading as Lister Pharmacy, BD8, which failed to pay £1,971.59 to one worker, declined to comment when contacted by the Telegraph & Argus.

PB2 Ltd, BD7, which failed to pay £747.86 to 11 workers, and Fashion Wize Ltd, trading as Lister Schoolwear, BD9, which failed to pay £1,003.55 to 28 workers, also did not comment.
