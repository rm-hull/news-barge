A proposal to turn offices into a pub and hotel at Wynyard Park is being considered by Stockton Council.

Wynyard Park Ltd has applied for planning permission to change the use of offices’ at Concordia House to a public house on the ground floor and an 18-bed hotel at the first floor.

The scheme proposes external changes to the building near Hanzard Drive and Glenarm Road including the installation of timber and stone cladding and re-roofing.

Josh Woollard, senior planner from the applicant’s agent Lichfields, says in a letter to the council: “The proposed development would give rise to wider public benefits, including maximising the range and choice of services/facilities within the Garden Village, enhancing the provision for the local residents.”

The proposed pub and hotel is near land identified as a “local centre” in the Wynyard Masterplan. Mr Woollard wrote: “The Wynyard Masterplan goes on to explain that the Wynyard Park Local Centre should contain a mix of commercial uses ensuring Wynyard has sufficient access to retail, food and drink uses including restaurant/café use, public house and hot food takeaways.

“It goes on to advise that development should ensure the needs of the population are met whilst also considering the use of upper floors to ensure effective use of land.

"Given the Wynyard Masterplan does not identify the site for any particular purpose and the proximity to the designated local centre, it is considered that the principle of the development, i.e. the new development and the proposed mix of uses, would be acceptable.”

He said the proposed development’s materials would be in-keeping with the local centre and there would be “no impact on residential amenity in terms of overbearing impact, overshadowing or loss of privacy”.

He added “noise does not present a constraint to development” with noise limits, enhanced glazing and acoustic ventilation.

He said there would be enough parking for customers: “The development will provide local facilities for local residents and therefore it is assumed that a proportion of people using the facilities will walk or cycle.

"Whilst some new trips would be generated by the development, it is considered that the impact on the wider highway network would be negligible.”
