A dangerous driver is due to be sentenced after a man was left with a broken rib following a head-on crash.

Brandon William Turnbull admitted causing serious injury by dangerous driving at a recent hearing before magistrates.

It follows a crash on the A183 between Chester-le-Street and Bournmoor, at about 8.10pm on December 28, last year.

The defendant was at the wheel of a Mercedes A180 involved in a head-on collision with a Mazda vehicle travelling in the opposite direction, close to White House Farm.

Both vehicles sustained extensive damage.

The Mazda driver, a man in his mid-40s, was taken to hospital by ambulance, suffering serious internal injuries and a fractured rib.

Turnbull is said to have fled the scene on foot following the crash, despite suffering injuries himself.

The search for the driver, involving a police helicopter and drone, was carried out in farmland in the vicinity but the defendant was not located.

A man in his mid-20s, who had attended at the University Hospital of North Durham, in Durham, for treatment to cuts and grazes, was arrested on suspicion of causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

Following his admission before magistrates last month, Turnbull, now 27, of Grasmere Road, Chester-le-Street, appeared at Durham Crown Court for sentence on Friday (September 4).

But Judge Amanda Rippon said an updated medical assessment of the victim and his impact statement had just been presented to the court.

“Mr Turnbull is a young man whose case is deserving of very careful consideration and because the medical evidence and victim personal statement have just been uploaded, I haven’t been able to read them and assess them.

“This type of offending is the type which normally receives a significant prison sentence.

“The defendant, who is 27, has no previous convictions.

“The simple fact is that it would be wholly inappropriate of me to cobble together now the proper way to sentence this young man.

“The victim’s personal statement, itself, can have a profound impact and it requires me to think very carefully whether or not the sentence can be suspended, as legislation now allows that for sentences of up to three years.

"So, I’m not going to sentence him today.

“I’m not going to rush judgement.

“This is a nuanced case and needs careful thought.

“He’s certainly at risk of an immediate prison sentence, but I just don’t have time to do this justice today.”

Apologising to the defendant for not being able to pass sentence at Friday’s hearing, the judge added: “I hope you understand I take my job extremely seriously and I have only just had all of the information I need.

"I need time to consider it.”

She adjourned the sentencing hearing for two weeks when she said the defendant should be ready to learn his fate.

Judge Rippon granted him bail to return to the court on September 17.
