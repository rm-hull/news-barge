Visitors staying overnight in York and North Yorkshire could soon be charged a £1-a-night tourist tax under new proposals backed by the region’s Mayor.

A new report commissioned by Mayor David Skaith suggests introducing a flat £1 levy per room each night to help counteract the strain millions of tourists place on the area.

If introduced, the report says the tax could raise up to £26 million every year.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/david-skaith-mayor-york-north-yorkshire-city-walls.webp)

Those funds could build better infrastructure, upgrade public transport, and support the region’s tourism and hospitality trades.

The move comes after months of fierce pushback from hospitality bosses. Leaders across York’s hotel and pub trades have previously hit out at the plans, warning that extra costs could punish independent venues and put off price-sensitive holidaymakers.

However, the report by the York and North Yorkshire Policy Lab argues that small charges do not deter visitors from top-tier destinations.

It points to cities like Edinburgh, Manchester, Paris, and Barcelona, where visitor numbers stayed strong after similar levies were brought in.

To protect small family businesses, rural retreats, and campsites across the county, experts are recommending a simple digital system alongside potential fee exemptions for lower-cost stays.

#### Report stats

**Key facts and figures from the report include:£1 per night charge:** Proposed flat fee for overnight accommodation.

**Targeted spending:** Revenues would be legally protected so funds cannot be swallowed up by general council budgets.

**£26 million annual boost:** Estimated funds generated to reinvest in regional transport, streets, and tourism.

**No immediate start:** Powers are expected to be granted under the English Devolution Bill later this year, with full implementation unlikely until**2027 or 2028** at the earliest.

Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, David Skaith, believes the plan strikes the right balance between welcoming visitors and protecting communities.

“York and North Yorkshire has something for everyone. Millions visit every year, and that’s why a visitor levy could be a total game changer for us,” he said.

“Thriving communities and thriving destinations go hand in hand, but welcoming so many visitors puts pressure on the infrastructure and services our residents rely on.

“We must get this right for our county so we can invest in our communities, and our hospitality and tourism businesses. That’s why I’m committed to keep talking with the sector as the details come together.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/york-tourists-phones-shambles-crop.webp)

Academic lead for the report, Professor Brendan Paddison from York St John University, stressed that the money must deliver visible benefits for local residents and traders alike.

“Any future levy would need to be simple and proportionate, underpinned by transparent governance and meaningful engagement with businesses,” he said.

“Crucially, there must also be a clear commitment that any revenue raised is invested back into the places and visitor economy that generate it.”

With national government consultation results due shortly, Mayor Skaith is set to hold further discussions with North Yorkshire hotel owners, B&B operators, and tourism groups over the coming months before any final decisions are made.
