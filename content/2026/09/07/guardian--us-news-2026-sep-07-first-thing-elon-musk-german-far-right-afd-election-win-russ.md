Good morning. A far-right leader who has won an important state election in Germany has thanked Elon Musk for his support, as voices in Europe warn the result is a serious moment for the continent. No far-right party has led or formed a government in Germany since the defeat of nazism at the end of the second world war.

Ulrich Siegmund, the Alternative für Deutschland’s lead candidate, thanked Musk on X after the tech billionaire said “Well done!” in German to the AfD co-founder Alice Weidel. The populist, far-right, anti-immigration party won the vote in the eastern state of Saxony-Anhalt.

During its campaign, the AfD sought to capitalise on a mood of nostalgia for the communist East Germany and discontent among east Germans. The party has promised a remigration and deportation offensive, to ban rainbow flags in schools and halt the building of new windfarms. It wants to change the slogan of the state from “Think Modern” to “Think German”, and reintroduce the singing of the country’s national anthem in schools.

- 
  **Will the AfD be able to form a state government?** Despite its resounding victory, the party fell just short of a majority. It was seeking an absolute majority in the state legislature to govern alone and overcome the so-called “firewall” of the mainstream parties, which refuse to work with it. The final results showed the AfD falling three seats short, with 39 lawmakers in an 83-member legislature.
- 
  **What has been the reaction in Washington and Moscow?** Donald Trump posted a screenshot of the election projection without comment on his Truth Social site. The Russian presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev took a screengrab of Trump’s post and praised a “historic win for the pragmatic AfD”. The AfD is pro-Kremlin, urging a stop to support for Ukraine and pushing for Ukrainians in Germany to no longer receive refugee status.

## **Iran war likely to drag on for another six months,former US defense secretary warns**

![Leon Panetta.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/41383e2a4b1a80e2e4ab5a21fcb91e6a5a5afad5/0_0_2800_2240/master/2800.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Donald Trump’s war with Iran is likely to drag on for another six months, the former US defense secretary Leon Panetta has said, adding that it risks becoming another “forever war in the Middle East”, with no clear end in sight.

“It’s no mystery that the United States and Iran are caught in a terrible stalemate, in which there really are very few options available to bring this war to an end,” said Panetta, who also served as CIA director under Barack Obama. “I think the likelihood is this war is going to continue for another six months without being resolved,” he added.

- 
  **Who else has been saying Trump’s war may not achieve its aims?** The US energy secretary, Chris Wright, said on Sunday morning Washington might not be able to reach an agreement with Tehran that would prevent Iran from having nuclear weapons. “We are strangling their economy to try to bring either a change in policy from the existing regime or a new regime,” he said.

## **Flock surveillance cameras spur rare bipartisan backlash**

![Flock Safety camera.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/e4d1851cb1d29f8deb1840d7271a1a9965ac6d0d/272_0_2999_2399/master/2999.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Growing factions from both sides of the political aisle in the US are pushing back against surveillance cameras made by a single company, a rare response in a sharply divided country with a president pushing an unwaveringly pro-tech agenda.

There are about 120,000 Flock cameras across the US, which conduct roughly 2bn scans a month. The company is staring down controversy after reports of abuses of its cameras by police officers in multiple states, who have used them to stalk former lovers and track people seeking abortions. The network the cameras feed into is also frequently used by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
