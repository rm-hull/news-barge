Richard Hammond has said he is “scared” he may face worsening memory loss due to a brain injury sustained in a crash near York almost two decades ago.

The presenter, 56, said there are lingering affects after he crashed a jet-powered dragster called Vampire at nearly 320mph while filming for Top Gear at the former RAF Elvington airbase.

Although the incident left him with serious head injuries, he recovered and returned to the show in early 2007.

In an interview with the Sunday Times, Hammond, when asked if he was concerned the brain injury will increase the risk of developing dementia earlier, said: “Brain-wise, yeah. I mean, I worry. I’m scared. Of course. That was a big impact.

"Will it lead to onset of dementia? I don’t know. Every time I lose my keys, and I do lose things, I think, ‘Oh Christ, is that brain injury, or is that just I’m 56?’ And we all do it. So yeah, there is a legacy from that. I will carry that for ever.”

Hammond got back in the reconstructed dragster as part of a video for the DriveTribe YouTube channel, which he co-founded with Jeremy Clarkson and James May, in 2022 and described the experience as “genuinely weird”.

He told Good Morning Britain: “It came about [because] I had the guys from DriveTribe over at my place and we were trying to plan a look ahead to the year.

“And one of them said, ‘You do know that car has now been restored and rebuilt just down the road from where you live?’ And it seemed too good an opportunity, so we rang them.

  Paramedics and police at the scene of the crash in Elvington, near York, in which Richard Hammond was injured while filming BBC in 2006

“It was all a bit of fun until the moment came – and you can see it on the little video on the platform – and I got in and I did go a bit quiet. It was, ‘Oh, hang on’.

“It’s weird. I remember the last time I got into this, but I don’t remember the last time I got out of it. So technically I must still be in it. It was a strange feeling.”

Hammond, who now presents Richard Hammond’s Workshop on Discovery Plus, was brought up in North Yorkshire and left Ripon Grammar School in the middle of his A levels before studying visual arts at Harrogate.

His first broadcasts emanated on BBC Radio York. "It took me about a week just to put one piece together," Hammond once told an interviewer. "And I only got paid about £10, so I worked part-time in a bookshop and a petrol station and collected chicken eggs on a farm."
