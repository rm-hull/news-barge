AN outdoor bar next to a medieval ruin in York has been given the go ahead.

Plans to build the free-standing oak bar as part of works to turn The Little Ale House’s rear courtyard into a beer garden have been approved by York Council.

Bar operator Harrogate Tipple’s application stated the works would respect the area’s surroundings including the neighbouring Grade I-listed Norman House, which is thought to be York’s oldest surviving domestic building.

City of York Council planning officers stated the bar was appropriately scaled and sensitively located and would not result in unacceptable noise or disturbance.

It follows City of York Council’s decision to grant listed building consent for the works, paving the way for the latest plans lodged in July.

The rear courtyard which would house the beer garden and bar is next to Norman House, a ruin which dates to the 12th Century.

The listed building consent plans were supported by York Civic Trust and Historic England who stated they would provide a great opportunity to provide information about the medieval building.

Harrogate Tipple’s latest plans would see the existing concrete and asphalt surface of the service yard replaced with new materials such as brick paving or stone flags.

A dead tree is set to be removed and moveable planters and pots would be put into the new beer garden.

A mix of timber benches, picnic tables and stools would provide seating and festoon and wall-mounted lights would also be installed.

The free-standing outdoor bar would be closed by timber cladding beneath a pitched slate roof.

The beer garden would open from 10am to 11pm.

Plans stated the redevelopment would allow views of Norman House and York Minster from the current courtyard to be opened up for patrons to enjoy.

The application stated: “The existing courtyard is visually unattractive and inaccessible to patrons.

“Creating a small beer garden would support the viability of the new business, provide an outdoor meeting space in keeping with York’s tradition of courtyard inns, and give the public the opportunity to appreciate the Norman remains from within a managed environment.”

Council planning officers stated the bar would be in an area already home to similar venues and there were no homes nearby which would be disturbed.

The plans follow the opening of The Little Ale House in the Grade II*-listed 46-50 Stonegate in December.

It was previously home to Little Betty’s Café which closed in 2021.
