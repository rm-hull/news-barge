WORKS to modernise parts of an historic York hotel have been proposed as part of plans for a refurbishment.

Two buildings in the Middletons Hotel complex would be redecorated, their en-suites upgraded and air conditioning improved and added if an application lodged with York Council is approved.

Operator Daniel Thwaites’ plans stated the improvements were necessary to bring 55 and 56, Skeldergate up to the rest of the hotel’s standards.

Plans lodged with the council propose refurbishing all bedrooms and their en-suites both buildings, along with installing new air conditioning systems and upgrading existing ones.

Window glazing would be replaced and outside doors swapped for ones matching those at the Judges Lodgings which is also run by Daniel Thwaites.

A new accessible bedroom would be created on the ground floor of 55 Skeldergate and one of the existing rooms would be enlarged.

A footbridge at the back of 55, Skeldergate would be removed and both buildings would be served by a new energy-efficient boiler.

The Middletons Hotel site dates to the 17th Century when Lady Anne Middleton founded an almshouse in York in 1660, the first woman in the city to do so.

The complex was rebuilt several times during the 18th and 19th Century and went on to serve as a hospital which closed in 1970, before being turned into a hotel.

No. 55 Skeldergate, also known as Chaplin House, is believed to date to the 17th Century but there is no documentary evidence about its early occupants.

The Grade II-listed building was home to York City Art Gallery Director Hans Hess in the 1950s and 1960s.

No. 56 dates to the end of the 18th Century and is Grade II*-listed.

It is thought to have been designed by John Carr, the architect behind York’s Bootham Hospital and Crown Court, for Ralph Dodsworth who served as Sheriff and Lord Mayor.

Plans stated the proposed works had been carefully considered to elevate the hotel’s offering.

The application stated: “This includes necessary services improvements such as new air conditioning to meet modern expectations for hotel guest comfort and control.

“The scope for No. 56 is conservative due to the special interest of the building and limited capacity for change.

“No. 55 has a greater scope as there is more capacity for change.

“The removal of the external bridge will open the rear and enhance the relationship with other hotel buildings.

“Together, the proposal aims to provide cohesive improvements and add to the collection of accommodation that creates the Middletons Hotel complex.”
