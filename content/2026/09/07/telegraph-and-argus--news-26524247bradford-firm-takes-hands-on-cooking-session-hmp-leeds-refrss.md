A HANDS-ON cooking session inside HMP Leeds is helping inmates build skills for a fresh start.

The session was delivered by a professional chef from Wunder Crumble by MyLahore, a Bradford-founded business that has been working with prisons for several years.

Inmates learned how to prepare and cook chicken jalfrezi from scratch, giving them an insight into working in a professional kitchen.

Ishfaq Farooq, director at Wunder Crumble by MyLahore and the MyLahore Group, said: "This isn't something that has started with one cooking session.

"For a number of years, we have been working with different prisons and their employment teams, attending prison job and careers fairs, speaking to individuals about opportunities within hospitality, sharing our experience as an employer, and looking at practical ways businesses like ours can support people preparing for life after release.

"The HMP Leeds cooking session was another way of taking that support a step further.

"Rather than simply talking about hospitality as a career, we wanted people to experience it, learn a practical skill, and hopefully discover a passion for the industry."

The company has built relationships with HMP Leeds, HMP Wealstun, and other prisons.

Mr Farooq emphasised the wider role businesses can play in their communities.

He said: "Corporate social responsibility shouldn't simply be something written on a website.

"Sometimes it means giving your time, sharing your skills and experience, attending a jobs fair, mentoring somebody, or being prepared to consider suitable individuals on their merits and, where appropriate, giving someone who is genuinely trying to create a different future the opportunity to prove themselves."

He also highlighted the value of second chances for those leaving the prison system.

Mr Farooq said: "We strongly believe that people who genuinely want to change direction and rebuild their lives deserve an opportunity to do so.

"Hospitality can be a fantastic industry for this, providing practical skills, confidence, teamwork, and discipline, as well as the possibility of developing a long-term career."

He praised the staff at HMP Leeds for their support and commitment.

Mr Farooq said: "We would also like to recognise and thank the team at HMP Leeds for making initiatives like this possible.

"Everyone deserves the opportunity to demonstrate that they can make a positive change.

"Sometimes all that is needed is for somebody to open the first door."
