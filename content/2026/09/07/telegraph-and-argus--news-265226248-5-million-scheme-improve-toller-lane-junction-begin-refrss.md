FUNDING to improve traffic flow in area of Bradford that a councillor has referred to as “a dump” has been approved.

Councillors from across West Yorkshire approved an additional £6.6 million for a junction improvement scheme - where Toller Lane, Whetley Hill, Whetley Lane and Carlisle Road - come together on Thursday.

The decision by West Yorkshire Combined Authority’s Investment Committee means the total amount pledged towards the scheme stands at £8.56m.

During the meeting Councillor Ian Eglin (Baildon), a member of the Council’s Reform UK Executive, welcomed the funding, saying that area of the city was “a bit of a dump to be honest.”

## When will work begin and for how long?

Now the funding has been approved, work is expected to begin next month and last until November 2027.

The junction improvement scheme was first proposed seven years ago, but funding issues have led a lengthy delay in the project moving forward.

Since it was first proposed, the estimated cost of the scheme has risen by 20 per cent.

## Work to the junction will include:

- Two new bus priority signals
- Segregated left-turn slip lanes onto Toller Lane from Whetley Lane and Carlisle Road approaches.
- Eight new signal-controlled pedestrian crossings and one new Toucan crossing.
- Permanent closure of Cross Road to simplify junction layout and minimise traffic conflicts, contributing to efficient signal staging.
- Footway widening and enhancements along Church Street and Whetley Lane.
- Fifty metres of new cycle lane between Cross Road and Church Street.
- One bus stop shelter upgrade.
- Planting 54 trees and 3,345 square metres of soft landscaping.
- 8,509 square metres of highway resurfacing.

## 'It is a bit of a dump'

During discussion of the item, Cllr Eglin said: “I often use the roads in the area where these works are planned, and it does need improvement.

“It is a bit of a dump to be honest.

“The congestion and traffic round there will benefit from having this scheme.”

## What other councillors have said

Councillor Ralph Cohn (Cons, Airedale) said he had been reading comments about the scheme online.

He said: “A significant number of residents are not in favour of this scheme, mainly on the basis of costs, but also the idea that the junction was fine until recently.”

He said the figures included in the report – that there had been 31 casualties resulting from car crashes in the area over the last five years – showed that this idea that there were no issues with the junction was most likely “mistaken.”

Councillor Sarah Courtney (Lab, Hebden Bridge) praised the scheme for its efforts to encourage more people to walk and cycle.

She said: “When it comes to good health, the nearest thing to a silver bullet is being a bit more active each day. This will lead to a reduction in casualties and an improvement in air quality.”

Referring to traffic congestion, Councillor Martin Hey (Green) said: “This area is one of the worst in the country – that side of Bradford moving into Calderdale, so I’m keen we pursue this.”

He said as a new member of the committee, the numbers being spent on the scheme seemed “quite startling.”

But members were told that analysis showed the scheme would be “high value for money” – with every £1 invested leading to a return of £3.37 in economic benefits.
