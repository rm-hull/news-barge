MOST people want to be remembered for kindness, not career success.

That’s according to new research from Remember A Charity, which found that 92 per cent of UK adults care about the legacy they leave behind.

Nearly two-thirds said being a good friend, partner or family member was the most important part of their legacy, while just one in 10 said career success mattered most.

Lucinda Frostick, director of Remember A Charity, said: "This Remember A Charity Week, we’re inviting people to think about how they want to be remembered and what the concept of legacy means to them.

"What this research shows is that when people think about their legacy, it’s rarely glory or wealth that springs to mind.

"It’s being there for family and friends, helping others in need, and the everyday shared moments that define who we are and what we really care about."

Other priorities included helping others in times of need and passing on values and life lessons, which ranked higher than professional achievements.

The findings come as more than 200 charities join together for Remember A Charity Week, running from September 7 to 13, to encourage people to reflect on the question: "What's My Legacy?"

The campaign also aims to highlight how charitable giving can form part of a lasting legacy.

Nearly two-thirds of respondents said supporting causes they care about is important to them, and 22 per cent have included or plan to include a charitable gift in their will.

Currently, one in six estates at probate includes a charitable gift, raising around £4.5 billion each year for charities across the UK.

Actress Sherrie Hewson, who is supporting the campaign, said: "For me, legacy isn't about what we leave behind for ourselves, it's about the impact we have on others.

"It's about keeping alive the memory of the people we love and supporting the causes that matter to us."

Regional and generational differences also shape what people want to be remembered for, from making the best Sunday roast in Wales to telling great stories in Scotland.
