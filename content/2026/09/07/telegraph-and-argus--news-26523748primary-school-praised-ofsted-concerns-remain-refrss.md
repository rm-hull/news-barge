An "improving school" in Bradford where pupils feel safe and well supported still has significant work to do on attendance, teaching and achievement, Ofsted inspectors have said.

Knowleswood Primary School, located on Knowles Lane in Holme Wood, was praised by the education watchdog for its safeguarding, pastoral care and inclusive ethos.

The report concluded that achievement, attendance and behaviour, curriculum and teaching, and leadership and governance all require further improvement.

Responding to the inspection, headteacher Kyrstie Stubbs said the report reflected "both the progress we have made and the work that still lies ahead".

She said: “We are particularly pleased that inspectors recognised the strength of our pastoral support, our commitment to inclusion, and the positive experiences we provide for our pupils through personal development and early years education.”

## School faced 'very challenging' circumstances

Inspectors described Knowleswood as "an improving school" and said pupils benefit from stronger support since the school joined Exceed Academies Trust.

The report found children are cared for well, feel safe and enjoy positive relationships with staff.

Chief executive of Exceed Academies Trust, Duncan Jacques, said the school had faced "very challenging" circumstances before joining the trust and that leaders were proud of the progress made.

He said: “We are pleased that Ofsted has acknowledged the school's strengths in safeguarding, inclusion, personal development and early years provision, alongside the positive culture and pastoral support that exists throughout the school community.”

Ofsted highlighted the school's work to ensure disadvantaged pupils and those with special educational needs and disabilities are able to take part in school life.

Inspectors said leaders had successfully removed barriers that could prevent some children from accessing school trips and wider opportunities.

The school's pastoral provision was described as "a real strength", while inspectors also praised pupils' personal development.

Children were found to have a strong understanding of issues including healthy relationships, online safety and diversity, and are encouraged to take on leadership roles through groups and activities across the school.

## Attendance and achievement 'needs attention'

The report also identified ongoing challenges.

Ofsted said some pupils have not achieved as well as they should over time, with weaknesses in teaching and assessment meaning gaps in learning are not always identified quickly enough.

Inspectors also found that too many pupils do not attend school regularly.

Although leaders have taken steps to improve attendance, persistent absence remains significantly above the national average.

Mr Jacques said: "We are equally clear about the areas where further improvement is needed and remain fully committed to supporting the leadership team, governors and staff as they continue to drive improvements in teaching, attendance and pupil outcomes.”

Ofsted said leaders have established plans to address the issues identified, but it is too early to see the full impact of those changes.
