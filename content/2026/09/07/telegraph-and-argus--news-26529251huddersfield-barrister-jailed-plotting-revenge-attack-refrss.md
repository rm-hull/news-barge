A BARRISTER has been jailed for plotting a violent revenge attack.

Samreen Akhtar, 38, was sentenced to three years and three months at Manchester Crown Court after being convicted of encouraging or assisting an offence.

## What happened?

Akhtar was a barrister at the time of the offence on September 15, 2021 whose client Tabish Ali, then of Clare Road, Cleckheaton, had been shot and injured in an incident in Blacker Road, Birkby, Huddersfield, the previous day.

She was recorded on a telephone call to Ali putting forward a plan to find the person responsible for the shooting so they could be caused serious injuries.

Ali was subsequently charged in October 2021 with the murder of Jamal Nedd, who had been shot and fatally wounded outside his home in Ripon Avenue, off Bradford Road, Fartown, Huddersfield, on December 19, 2020, as he drove his mother to work.

Ali was later convicted of his murder and given a life sentence along with two other men in July 2022.

Their trial had been told that the murder came as part of an escalating ‘turf war’ between drug dealing gangs, which had involved multiple violent and firearms incidents since 2018.

The attempted murder shooting of Ali in 2021 was believed to have been linked to this ongoing feud.

## Barrister found guilty

Akhtar, of Sufton Street, Birkby, Huddersfield, was arrested by detectives in February 2022 and charged in March the following year with encouraging or assisting the commission of an indictable offence, namely inflicting grievous bodily harm with intent, under the Serious Crime Act 2007.

She denied the offence but was convicted by a majority jury verdict following a five-day trial in June.

## What police said

Detective Inspector Paul Hutchinson of West Yorkshire Police said: "As a professional working in the criminal justice system, Samreen Akhtar would have been acutely aware of her legal and moral responsibilities, but she chose to act outside of those to plot revenge with a dangerous criminal involved in a violent gang feud which included the brutal execution of a rival, for which he was later convicted.

"In doing so, she became a criminal herself and showed a complete disregard for the standards that are expected of those in the legal profession, and also for the significant negative impact that gang-related violence and criminality have on our communities."
