AN Aldi supermarket is planning to boost security after facing an increase in theft and vandalism.

Aldi has applied to the council to install replacement shuttering around its store on York Road in Seacroft.

It would be among a string of measures to protect the site from damage if planning permission is approved.

A design report by the supermarket chain said: “The Aldi food store has been facing ongoing security challenges, particularly outside of trading hours.

“Due to damage at the site and issues with anti-social behaviour the applicant is proposing to install security shutters to deter this behaviour.”

The company said there had been multiple disturbances at the entrance to the store and break-ins through the glazed shop front.

The report said: “A significant concern has been the entrance to the food store, where incidents of theft, vandalism and other disturbances have been reported.

“This activity not only creates an unwelcoming atmosphere but also raises concerns for the safety of staff and customers.”

West Yorkshire Police previously made recommendations including a review of CCTV, repairs to perimeter fencing and more lighting.

A police inspection report said there were four burglaries and six theft-from-shop offences during a three-month period last year.

An assault and one public order offence were also recorded at the store.

The police report, dated April this year, said: “Aldi in Seacroft has seen a considerable increase in burglaries in the recent weeks.”

Aldi said the perforated shuttering would improve security while maintaining visibility and maintaining the store’s frontage.

Plans were revised after the council first raised concerns over the appearance of the proposed shuttering.

Aldi’s report said: “The design ensures that the entrance remains well-lit and visually open, addressing concerns raised by repeated anti-social behaviour.”

Leeds City Council is considering a full planning application for the shuttering, which is out for consultation until September 22.
