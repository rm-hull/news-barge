The political row over school closures has reignited after several Middlesbrough schools announced early finishes ahead of another funeral linked to the fatal A66 crash.

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch accused the Government of letting gang violence dictate the lives of law-abiding families.

Her comments followed several Acklam schools announcing early finishes ahead of Theo Rae’s funeral on Monday (September 7).

  Theo Rae, 17 and Cole Robert Worthy, 17. 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

Acklam Whin Primary School and Green Lane School will close at 1.15pm after lunch.

Acklam Grange and Outwood Academy Acklam will finish at 12.15pm, while Newham Bridge Primary School will close at 1pm.

  Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch has refreshed her shadow cabinet (James Manning/PA)


Badenoch launched a fresh attack on Education Secretary Lucy Powell. Writing on X she said: "It’s obscene that this is happening again.

"It is the duty of government to keep schools open, yet the Labour Education Secretary is allowing gang violence to dictate the lives of law-abiding citizens.

"Lucy Powell should be ashamed that she has refused to intervene. This sets a terrible precedent and sends a message to criminals and thugs across the country that this Labour government is too weak to keep its people safe from them.

"It is also a kick in the teeth to the police officers being asked to defend parents, teachers and children from the very thugs involved in the violence that killed two of their own.

"It was bad enough that schools closed early last week but it is completely unfathomable that the Labour government has not stopped it happening again this week.

"If they don’t act now, this will only get worse."

  Lucy Powell 

*(Image: Richard Townshend/UK Parliament/PA Wire)*

Lucy Powell hit back, branding Badenoch’s intervention "an ill-judged attempt at political point-scoring".

On X, the Education Secretary replied: "Another ill-judged attempt at political point-scoring from Kemi.

"School attendance and keeping schools open is my absolute priority, as is the safety of all children.

"We've been in close contact with headteachers, schools and local authorities in Redcar and Cleveland, and Middlesbrough to support them to keep schools open and with sensitive child protection issues.

"These are difficult decisions for headteachers to make, but I have been assured no further closures will be needed.

"The Conservatives used to champion school autonomy. Now, apparently, they want me signing off every decision a headteacher makes. I trust and support headteachers.

"If you want to talk about shame, start with huge cuts your government made to local policing and services in Teesside – they’re still living with the consequences."

Cleveland Police has warned of traffic disruption around Middlesbrough ahead of Theo’s funeral.

Superintendent John Wrintmore said disruption was expected from 1pm to 4pm as the funeral cortege travelled through Middlesbrough to St Bede’s Chapel on Acklam Road.

He said Ladgate Lane, Ormesby Road, Cargo Fleet Lane and Acklam Road were among the roads likely to be affected.

He added that police were aware of the closures but stressed they were not made on Cleveland Police’s recommendation.

"We are aware of some school closures in the area and have liaised with partners in the local authorities regarding the expected disruption today. Again, these closures have not been made on the recommendation of Cleveland Police."

Theo Rae was one of five men killed when a Volkswagen Passat collided with a marked Cleveland Police vehicle near South Bank on August 22.

The Passat was on the wrong side of the road when it hit the police vehicle, killing PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38.

  Pc Matthew Blades, 37, and Pc Tom Clough, 38 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

The latest closures come just days after schools shut early for the funeral of another crash victim, Jakub Matusiak.

Cleveland Police said Jakub’s funeral on Friday passed without incident and caused minimal traffic disruption.

The force said no schools, services or businesses had been told to close; individual organisations made their own decisions after risk assessments.

  Police at the scene of the A66 crash 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Superintendent Wrintmore said: "Today’s events have passed without issue and very minimal traffic disruption.

"Our policing operation to ensure public safety will take the same approach next week when more funerals are taking place.

"We will continue to work closely with our partners to minimise disruption in these areas and keep the public safe.

"No schools, services or businesses have been instructed to close, and any decision to do so has been made by individual organisations after their own risk assessments."

He added: "We would like to thank our communities for their continued cooperation and support. Our visible police presence in the area will continue throughout the week to provide reassurance and allow everyone to continue with their daily lives as normal."

  From left to right: Makai Saddington, 18, Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jakub Matusiak, 23, Michael Robert Cahill, 23. 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

Theo’s funeral will be held at 2pm at St Bede’s Chapel, Teesside Crematorium, on Acklam Road.

Makai Saddington’s funeral will be held on Tuesday, September 8, followed by Cole Worthy’s on Wednesday, September 9.

The funerals of PC Blades and PC Clough are also due to take place this week.

Ms Powell previously described "huge anxieties and worries in the community" and called the situation "very difficult and delicate".

She told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme: "As Education Secretary, I want schools to be open. I want pupils attending schools all the time.

"And these headteachers of these small number of schools have taken a very difficult decision with the local authority and the police for the safety of their school community to close early.

"What I would say is that there are huge anxieties and worries in the communities."

She also said she had contacted local MPs and Home Office ministers to secure extra police and resources to ease tensions and improve safety.

Police investigations into the crash remain ongoing, with 27 people arrested in a crackdown on serious and organised crime since the incident.
