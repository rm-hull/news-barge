A public meeting has aired opposition to plans substantially expanding a pig farm operation in Loftus.

Applicant Andrew Corrigan, of Feelgood Pigs Limited, operating from Highfields Farm, in South Town Lane, wants to add two new sheds with the equivalent gross internal floor space representing approximately 1,943 square metres in size.

The sheds and associated infrastructure would triple the number of weaner pigs able to be reared on the site from 900 to 2,700.

Weaner pigs are separated from their mother and given a diet of specialised solid feed in order to fatten them up for market during which time they can grow up to 30kg.

Plans submitted to Redcar and Cleveland Council described the proposal as complying with local and national planning guidelines.

They said Highfields Farm was an established breeding and rearing operation and the new development would “enhance the farm’s economic stability and accommodate increasing demand”.

However, a meeting organised by campaigning organisation Communities Against Factory Farming (CAFF) at nearby Easington Village Hall heard concerns about pig welfare, as well as problems with increased smells and the prospect of further expansion in the future.

Clare Robson, from Loftus, who was among those who attended, said: “I am deeply concerned about their confinement, staffing levels, slurry storage, ammonia emissions and the risk that three sheds could eventually become four, five or more.”

Retired Easington resident Elizabeth Wilmot described “thousands of piglets living over vats of their own excrement”.

She said: “The smells and the flies will be horrendous.”

Mr Corrigan was also present at the meeting and is understood to have spoken to a number of residents in an attempt to address some of the issues raised.

A statement issued on his behalf by Sam Harrison, a director with Harrison Pick planning consultants, said Highfields Farm was “not a new farming operation arriving in the countryside on a previously undeveloped site”.

The statement said: “Prior to submission, the proposal was assessed against the relevant environmental controls and technical requirements, and the applicant is satisfied that it complies with them.

“It now falls to the Local Planning Authority, together with its statutory and technical consultees, to scrutinise that information and determine the application on its planning merits.”

The statement said suggestions that “three buildings will somehow quietly become four or five are unfounded”.

It also said Mr Corrigan was concerned about the way the application was being presented by CAFF.

The statement explained: “Local residents are, of course, entirely entitled to raise concerns about a development proposed close to where they live, and many of those attending the recent meeting asked perfectly reasonable questions.

“There is, however, an important distinction between genuine local concern about the particular effects of this proposal and a wider national campaign against livestock production.

“The planning application should be assessed on the evidence relating to Highfields Farm, rather than on objections of principle to livestock farming generally.”

It said preventing domestic agricultural production of this nature would not, in itself, reduce consumer demand for pork and it could mean a greater reliance on imported produce involving longer supply chains and production under lower animal welfare and environmental standards.

Documents attached to the planning application addressed technical aspects stating: “The proposed buildings will be used as pig accommodation on a fully slatted slurry-based system, operating in the same manner as the existing and approved pig accommodation at the site.

“Slurry storage is self-contained within the buildings underneath the slats, providing 6 months storage capacity for slurry.

“The slurry is disposed of through spreading onto arable land as a fertiliser, utilising an organic and sustainable source of fertiliser for the land.

“The proposed buildings will include an automated auger feeding system and non-drip nipple drinkers.

“Ventilation is based on high velocity ridge mounted fans and side inlet vents.

“Pollution management for the proposed units is strictly regulated by the Environment Agency.”

Further submitted analysis suggested “minimal additional traffic” would be generated with an extra two vehicles a week on average expected.

Meanwhile, a dispersion modelling study produced on behalf of the applicant, assessing potential odour, said the closest residences were at another farm 540 metres to the west of the proposed pig rearing sheds.

It suggested there was no guidance on what level of odour might be problematic or constitute a statutory nuisance, but it was expected to fall below Environment Agency guidelines for “moderately offensive” odours.

More than 55 comments have already been lodged by neighbours.

Jo Lazarus, a campaign coordinator at CAFF, said: “Cramming so many animals together in such a stressful environment is deeply cruel and could have a serious negative impact on the local community.

“Thankfully, Loftus and Easington residents are coming together to make their voices heard and stop this factory farm expanding.”
