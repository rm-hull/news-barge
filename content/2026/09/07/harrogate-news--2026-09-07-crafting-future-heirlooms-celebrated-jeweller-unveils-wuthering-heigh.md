What does it take to make an heirloom of the future? That is the captivating question playing on the mind of designer jeweller Joanne Gowan as she prepares for a major exhibition in North Yorkshire later this month.

**Pateley Bridge**, the local artisan has already spent several years developing a single, monumental piece. She intends for it to eventually earn that prized status of a family heirloom through what she describes as the slow process of thinking.

**Joanne Gowan:**

I had the idea to produce a Wuthering Heights piece about two years ago when I moved to North Yorkshire, celebrating the first connection I had with the area from reading the Brontë novels from the age of 10.

This Emily Brontë piece sort of ate into my subconscious on so many levels: spiritual ideas, eternal love, passion, the wild of the landscape, and the contrast of good and evil, all wrapped into one.



When Gowan joins a curated group of the UK’s top contemporary jewellers and silversmiths for the three-day **Acquire** selling show at Ampleforth Abbey, visitors will have a rare, exclusive opportunity to view her work-in-progress. The creative journey behind the piece spans from the rugged Yorkshire moors to the historic gemstone cutting centers of Germany.


**Joanne Gowan:**

Initially, I took some images from illustrations of Cathy in early book editions and contacted my gem dealer in York, R.M. Weare. They sourced a highly talented gemstone carver in Idar-Oberstein, Germany.

The rock crystal carving then took over six months to develop, with lots of input back and forth during the cutting process.

Meanwhile, I re-read the novel several more times to narrow down the exact elements I wanted to include. After that, a lot of the designing happens in my head. Over the months I get a clear mental vision, and then on the right day, I sit down and work it all out on paper.


## The Vision: A Masterpiece in Precious Materials

The final design is a complex vignette, or picture piece, engineered to be worn as a high-end brooch or pendant, or displayed standalone as a miniature work of art. It captures the haunting, pivotal moment when Cathy appears at the window—a scene that defines the emotional landscape of the entire novel.

Framed within a window meticulously carved from soapstone, the structure mimics the box bed window of Ponden Hall near Haworth, widely believed to be Emily Brontë’s real-world inspiration for the scene.


**Joanne Gowan:**

Cathy, rendered in rock crystal, will appear to be coming through the leaded glass. The Ash tree leaves knock against the window, representing the moment when Nelly speaks to Heathcliff on the morning of Cathy’s death and he leans against the tree. In the foreground sill is a sprig of Yew with its toxic red berries, representing the bridge between mortal death, endurance, and the supernatural spirit. Alongside it sits a bunch of yellow crocuses—the earliest spring flowers brought to Cathy’s bedside by Edgar Linton, representing renewal and hope. This beautifully ties to the end of the novel, when the children of Cathy and Heathcliff reach an eventual mutual connection.



The breath-taking final piece will be constructed from a rich palette of premium materials: rock crystal, soapstone, sterling silver, 18ct yellow and red gold, black rhodium plating, platinum, vivid rubies, and yellow sapphires.

## A Creative Home in North Yorkshire

For Gowan, hosting this exhibition in North Yorkshire brings her creative journey full circle. Her current home and workspace in **Pateley Bridge** provide daily fuel for her artistic vision.


**Joanne Gowan:**

Many completed pieces of jewellery in my studio have the influence of the Yorkshire landscape running within them; it has been an influence on my work for very many years. So now, I am delighted that I can call this place my heartfelt home. Since Neolithic times, people have felt the joy of creating and wearing jewellery—its possession seems to be an intrinsic part of the human condition. It feels like the perfect time for me to create an iconic art piece of Wuthering Heights. I am always happy to discuss my work and create bespoke pieces for individual clients that speak to them in a personal, life-affirming way.



The original design sketch and the German-carved rock crystal component will be publicly displayed for the first time at the Acquire event, where the finished masterpiece will be open for official commission.
