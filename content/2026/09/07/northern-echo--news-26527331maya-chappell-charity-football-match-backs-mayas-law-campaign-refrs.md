Families and supporters came together at a charity football match for murdered County Durham toddler Maya Chappell, raising money for children and backing the campaign for Maya’s Law.

The Northern Echo is backing Maya’s family in its call for stronger child-safeguarding measures and a change in the law.

Maya’s Football Match was held at Consett AFC on Sunday, September 6, with dads, uncles, grandads and other supporters playing to raise money in the two-year-old’s memory.

  From left, Gemma Chappell, Dylan Thompson, Kerrie Robinson, Rachael Walls, Durham police and crime commissioner Joy Allen and Grace Thompson at the start of the Maya's Law charity football match at Consett AFC 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

Maya’s great-aunts, Gemma Chappell and Rachael Walls, organised the event and are leading the campaign to improve how child-safeguarding information is shared across the UK.

The match brought families together and raised money for causes close to Maya’s family.

The captains were Maya’s uncle and Rachael’s fiancée, Michael Thompson, and Maya’s cousin and Gemma’s son, Bradley Lee Chappell.

Players each paid £10 to hire the venue, with the rest going to the children’s hospital at Newcastle’s Royal Victoria Infirmary, where Maya died in September 2022.

  The Maya's Law charity football match at Consett AFC 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The money will also fund memory boxes for families after a child’s death and support staff caring for them through what organisers called “unimaginable circumstances”.

Adults paid £2 to watch the match, while children got in free.

The fundraiser is part of Maya’s family’s campaign to protect other children in her name.

  The Maya's Law charity football match at Consett AFC 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

Maya suffered catastrophic injuries at her home in Shotton Colliery and died in September 2022.

Michael Daymond, Maya’s mother’s partner, was convicted of her murder and jailed for life with a minimum term of 20 years in December 2023.

Maya’s mother, Dana Carr, was jailed for nine years after being convicted of allowing her daughter’s death and of child cruelty by neglect.

  The Maya's Law charity football match at Consett AFC 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

Since then, Gemma and Rachael have campaigned with The Northern Echo for “Maya’s Law”, a proposed Child Risk Disclosure Scheme to help officials share and act on safeguarding information about adults who may pose a risk to children.

A petition supporting the change passed 100,000 signatures and triggered a Westminster debate, while MPs backed calls for stronger safeguards.

Maya’s family welcomed information-sharing measures in the Children’s Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 but says more is needed and wants Maya’s Law established separately.

  The Maya's Law charity football match at Consett AFC 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

The match came days after the family won another important victory in its campaign.

The Government confirmed on Sunday, August 30, that prisoners convicted of causing or allowing a child’s death would be excluded from early-release changes.

The announcement means Carr will not become eligible for the substantially earlier release Maya’s relatives had feared.

Gemma, a police officer from Consett, described the change as “a weight lifted off our shoulders” after weeks of uncertainty.

She said the decision showed the Government had listened to victims and their families, but vowed the campaign would continue.

Gemma has said the family “won’t stop” until Maya’s Law is in place.

A review published in May 2024 found missed opportunities after Maya’s father raised concerns about domestic abuse, while saying there was no evidence her death could have been prevented.
