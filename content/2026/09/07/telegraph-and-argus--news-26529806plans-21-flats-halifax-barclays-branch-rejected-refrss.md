Last month proposals were submitted by a developer to sub-divide a former Barclays Bank town centre branch into four commercial units.

Now a new application for the Commercial Street, Halifax, building – this time for prior approval to convert other parts of the building into 21 flats – has been rejected by planners.

Darsons Homes Ltd wants to convert the ground and basement floor levels into four commercial “Class E” units.

Under planning rules in England, a Class E unit is a commercial property that can be used for things such as a shop, office, cafe or restaurant, gym, health service, nursery, or certain light industrial uses.

But Calderdale Council planners have refused the developers prior approval permission for separate proposals to turn the first, second, third and fourth floors from offices into 21 flats.

As things stand, says the council, due to the proximity of the habitable room windows of the flats facing out to the back – out onto Powell Street – they would have inadequate levels of natural light.

Additionally, insufficient information has been submitted to demonstrate that the proposed fourth floor flats, which would be created within the existing attic space, would have minimum required minimum floor to ceiling height, said the planning officers.

A prior approval application is a request for the council to check specific impacts of a development that may be allowed under national permitted development rights before work can go ahead without a full planning application.

So in light of that, it is a case of back to the drawing board.

The shop-space application, number 26/00541/FUL, is still to be determined -supporting papers can be viewed on the council’s Planning Portal.

Barclays closed its Commercial Street branch in Halifax two years ago – it was the bank’s last remaining branch in the borough.

The bank said it was closing the branch because customer behaviour had changed significantly and most customers were using digital or alternative banking services instead.
