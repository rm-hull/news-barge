A teenager travelled more than 150 miles to confront and fatally stab a man, a court has heard.

Sulaiman Tajzai, 18, from Leeds, is on trial at the Old Bailey accused of murdering 26-year-old Farmanullah Sherzad in Westminster, London, on March 27.

The court heard that Mr Sherzad had previously been accused of stalking by members of Tajzai's family.

Lisa Wilding KC, prosecuting, said: "His actions that day were neither a coincidence nor spontaneous.

"He travelled to London armed with that knife, waited for his target outside his home, and then confronted him in a sustained and brutal attack that led to Mr Sherzad’s immediate death."

Police were called shortly before 10.15pm to Abbey Orchard Street, where they found Mr Sherzad "lying on his side in a pool of blood with a large kitchen knife stuck in the left side of his chest."

He had suffered multiple stab wounds and died from his injuries.

The court was told that Tajzai had purchased the knife earlier that day from a shop near his home in Leeds.

He was seen on CCTV arriving at London King’s Cross station just before 5pm, wearing a medical face mask, a distinctive jacket, and dark gloves.

Footage then tracked him walking across the city to Abbey Orchard Street.

Ms Wilding told the court that the background to the incident involved a "pre-existing connection" between Mr Sherzad and Tajzai’s cousin’s wife, which had "turned sour."

On September 22 last year, the cousin and his wife reported Mr Sherzad to police in Leeds, showing an officer an image of him and text messages in Arabic.

However, the wife deleted the texts, refused to sign a statement, and left the police station.

Last October 3, police officers attended the couple’s home in Leeds and found Mr Sherzad sitting on the floor with his wrists tied together with fabric and no shoes on.

He was arrested on suspicion of stalking and trespass with intent to commit a sexual offence.

Mr Sherzad later claimed he was in a relationship with the wife and had travelled to Leeds after she invited him via Snapchat.

Following his release, the defendant’s cousin and his wife contacted the police multiple times and were later told there would be “no further action” against Mr Sherzad.

The court also heard that Tajzai had previously travelled to London with his cousin on January 9 and 21.

On those occasions, they reportedly spent several hours near the convenience store where Mr Sherzad worked in south London, as well as near his home.

Tajzai, of Temperance Hall, Leeds, denies murder and possession of a knife.

He is being assisted in court by an interpreter.

The trial at the Old Bailey continues.
