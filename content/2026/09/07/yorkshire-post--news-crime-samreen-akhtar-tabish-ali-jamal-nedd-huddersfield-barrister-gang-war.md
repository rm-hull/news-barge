# Barrister caught plotting revenge over Yorkshire shooting as part of gang turf war

## A barrier who plotted a revenge attack with a career criminal who had been shot as part of a violent turf war between drugs gangs has been jailed for more than three years.

Samreen Akhtar was the barrister on behalf of Tabish Ali, who had been shot in an incident in Blacker Road in Huddersfield on September 14, 2021.

The following day, she was recorded on a telephone call where she put forward a plan to enact retaliation for the shooting by finding the personal responsible and seriously injuring them.

A month after the conversation, Ali was charged in relation to murder of Jamal Nedd, who had been shot and killed outside him home in Ripon Avenue, Huddersfield, on December 19, 2020.

He was jailed for life alongside two other men after being convicted of murder in July 2022.

His trial heard of an escalating turf war between drug dealing gangs which had involved multiple shootings since 2018. The shooting of Ali in 2021 had been linked to the violent feud.

The 38-year-old barrister was arrested in February 2022 and charged with encouraging or assisting the commission of an indictable offence, namely inflicting grievous bodily harm with intent, under the Serious Crime Act 2007 in March 2023.

![Samreen Akhtar has been jailed for more than three years](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/09/07/14/10/Samreen-AKHTAR.jpg?trim=625,0,161,0&width=640&quality=65)



She denied the offence but was found guilty by a majority verdict following a five-day trial in June earlier this year.

Akhtar, of Sufton Street in Huddersfield, was jailed for three years and three months at Manchester Crown Court on Monday (Sep 7).

Detective Inspector Paul Hutchinson, who led the investigation, said: “As a professional working in the criminal justice system, Samreen Akhtar would have been acutely aware of her legal and moral responsibilities, but she chose to act outside of those to plot revenge with a dangerous criminal involved in a violent gang feud which included the brutal execution of a rival, for which he was later convicted.

“In doing so, she became a criminal herself and showed a complete disregard for the standards that are expected of those in the legal profession, and also for the significant negative impact that gang-related violence and criminality have on our communities.

“West Yorkshire Police remains firmly committed to targeting serious and organised criminals under our ongoing Programme Precision initiative, and we will continue to relentlessly pursue those involved, no matter what their role or occupation, to keep our communities safe.”
