A “greedy” care home manager cheated residents and lied to their nearest and dearest as she used their money to fund her extravagant lifestyle, York Crown Court heard.

Michaela Golding, 58, diverted fees intended to pay for a dying woman’s care into her own bank account, charged residents for care they had not received, duped the dyslexic relative of one resident into paying nearly £10,000 into her own bank account, and charged another for a “retainer fee” that did not exist, said Emily Jenkins, prosecuting.

She kept stealing for 20 months until the management at the Filey care home uncovered her crimes. By then she had stolen £50,128 and left the son of one deceased resident with years of probate problems as he tried to finalise his mother’s estate.

All care home fees had to be paid directly to the care home owner company’s head office, but Golding had persuaded the family of one elderly resident to pay her fees to her directly, claiming she would forward them to head office.

None of the £27,631 paid to her by the resident's son was forwarded and the resident’s fee account went more than £27,000 into arrears. The theft was only discovered when the son was going through his mother’s accounts after her death for probate.

“He felt deeply betrayed by the defendant who came across as caring and attentive whilst at the same time she was stealing from his mother,” said Ms Jenkins.

In a statement released by police after the case, the son said: "My mum was a very proud woman, and to know that this happened during the final years of her life causes me great distress. I still struggle to comprehend how someone could do this to her.

"My mum was the most wonderful person in my world and deserved kindness, dignity, and proper care. Instead, she was taken advantage of by someone who abused their position of trust."

Ms Jenkins said when interviewed by police for the second time in February 2026, Golding said she had stolen “out of greed” and used the money to “support a lifestyle beyond her means”.

Police discovered she had spent the money on credit cards, holidays, Amazon and Next purchases and furniture, among other items, said Ms Jenkins.

Golding, of Greenfields Avenue, Osgodby near Selby, pleaded guilty to fraud by abuse of position between October 12, 2022 and June 12, 2024, and false accounting between March 30, 2022 and November 27, 2023.

She was jailed for 32 months and faces a confiscation hearing next year as the authorities try to reclaim the money.

Defence barrister Tom Harris said: “These offences are a blemish and a stain on what is otherwise a very creditable record of working in the care system for 37 years.”

York Crown Court heard Golding started working for Woodlands care home in Filey on April 24, 2021, and became its manager in December 2021. She left the company on September 27, 2024 after being on “sick leave” since July 2024.

“She is no longer in employment in the care sector and never will be employed in the care sector or in a position of responsibility again,” said Mr Harris. She was appalled by her actions.

She had been suffering from poor mental health at the time of the offences, probably due to the “emotional, physical and other abuse she had suffered growing up”.

Judge Simon Hickey said it was difficult to see how someone suffering from a mental health crisis could have carried out such “methodical and systematic” crimes.

Ms Jenkins said all four victims of the fraud offence had since died.
