A "slurring" driver left a woman needing life-changing surgery after ploughing into her in a head-on smash.

Darlington's Daniel Smith was driving three passengers in a Skoda Octavia towards oncoming cars on Middridge Road, near Newton Aycliffe, shortly after 8pm on Saturday, April 4.

The driver of one car heading in the opposite direction managed to take late evasive action, but the driver in the car behind, who was driving home from work, was unable to get out of its way in time.

Durham Crown Court was told she was rendered unconscious in the head-on crash between the Octavia and her Peugeot 308.

Other drivers at the scene went to her aid while emergency services were called.

Ellen Wright, prosecuting, said witnesses noted two males who got out of the Octavia seemed to find it funny, treating it as a joke.

When the Peugeot driver regained consciousness, she recalls a woman from the vehicle behind calling for the emergency services.

That witness said she could hear the clinking of bottles, and 12 discarded nitrous oxide canisters were later recovered from the roadside.

The injured driver was taken by ambulance to hospital, where, in the accident and emergency department, she collapsed due to internal bleeding and had to have life-saving surgery, including the removal of her spleen.

She required a blood transfusion because of the ruptured spleen and spent 14 days in hospital recovering but, subsequently, had to go back daily to have her dressing changed after developing an infection.

Miss Wright said police arriving at the accident scene were informed Smith had been driving the Octavia and his DNA was recovered from its air bag.

Police approached him and he appeared intoxicated, with his speech slurred and his pupils dilated.

A drug wipe taken at the scene provided a positive result for the presence of cocaine and he was arrested.

Miss Wright said despite the positive roadside drug wipe, a subsequent toxicology report failed to provide sufficient evidence to charge Smith with drug driving.

She told the court no test is available to check for the presence of nitrous oxide in the system.

The 31-year-old defendant, of Severn Way, Darlington, admitted causing serious injury by dangerous driving, at a plea hearing on July 21.

He was said to have nothing similar on a relatively short past criminal record.

Friday’s (September 4) sentencing hearing heard the statement of the 54-year-old victim, who outlined the life-changing effect the accident has had on her.

She said she found getting in a car in the weeks afterwards to go back to hospital to have her dressings changed daily was distressing.

The victim said she felt she has lost her independence and suffered flashbacks, affecting her sleep, leaving her feeling lethargic during the daytime.

She has been unable to return to work and can no longer go swimming or take her dog on long walks.

The victim said she rarely leaves the house and is on antibiotics to prevent infections, while she can’t eat a lot of food and no longer enjoys eating.

Many other aspects of her life have also been affected in the months since the crash and she still suffers frequent pain, despite the medication.

Penny Hall, for the defendant, said he can’t recall the lead up to the accident and why he ended up on the wrong side of the road, but added that, “his bad driving seems to have been short lived.”

She said the defendant claims he had not taken cocaine that day but accepts it can remain in people’s systems for several days.

Miss Hall said the defendant was not one of those laughing at the scene and was immediately aware what had happened and was in shock.

She said he is genuinely remorseful for his actions and the consequences of his driving that evening on the victim.

The two-and-a-half months he has spent in custody have been his first taste of life behind bars, which he has found “difficult and unpleasant”, but he has tried to be, “a model prisoner”.

She added that he has, “a good work ethic”, having worked all his life.

Judge Amanda Rippon told Smith: “By driving as you did, you exposed innocent road users, your own passengers and even yourself, to an obvious grave risk of death and/or very serious injury.

“The impact of your offending on that lady has been, quite simply, horrific.

“Every aspect of her life has been affected in every way possible and she has suffered permanent irreversible injury and has a lifelong dependency on medication.

“I’m satisfied the seriousness of the offence means the only appropriate sentence will be one of immediate custody.”

Imposing a 32-month prison sentence, the judge told Smith it would have been a three-year sentence but for his guilty plea.

She also banned the defendant from driving for a total of 40 months.

Smith was told under current sentencing legislation it is likely he will spend no more than 40-per cent of the sentence in prison before being released on licence.

Five people were taken to Darlington Memorial Hospital with five ambulances called to the scene.

A spokesperson for North East Ambulance Service (NEAS) said: "We were called to a road traffic incident on Middridge Road, Newton Aycliffe, shortly before 8.30pm on April 4.

"We dispatched five double-crewed ambulances and a clinical team leader. Five patients were taken to Darlington Memorial Hospital."
