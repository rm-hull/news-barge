Customers say it is "such a shame" as a busy Barnard Castle food spot is stopping its takeaways.

Rising costs, complaints and "disrespectful" behaviour aimed at staff have reportedly led to the decision.

Babul’s, on Market Place, will stop offering takeaways from September 14 as it focuses on its restaurant and bar.

In a message to customers, manager Zak Ahmed said financial and operational pressures had made the takeaway service increasingly difficult to sustain.

He said: "We know this will be disappointing for many of our customers who have supported our takeaway service over the years, and we are genuinely sorry that we can no longer offer it at this time.

"There are a number of reasons behind this decision, and we feel it is important to be open and honest about them."

The restaurant, owned by Shuhel Ahmed, pointed to the hidden costs of running a takeaway operation, including packaging, administration, and staffing, at a time when businesses are facing mounting overheads.

The manager also acknowledged that balancing takeaway demand with seated service had become a growing challenge.


Online, telephone and in-person orders put kitchen and front-of-house staff under heavy pressure during busy periods, risking delays for both diners and takeaway customers.

Babul’s also reported more negative online reviews about takeaway orders and "disrespectful" behaviour towards staff.

The restaurant stated: "Sadly, we have also experienced an increase in occasions where members of our team have been spoken to in a rude, disrespectful or verbally abusive manner, particularly over the telephone when dealing with takeaway concerns.

"We completely understand that things can go wrong and that customers can feel frustrated or disappointed.

"We will always listen, take responsibility where appropriate and do our best to resolve genuine concerns.

"However, our staff should never have to accept abusive or disrespectful behaviour while simply trying to do their jobs."

The manager also said there have been claims that portions have been reduced.

He said: "Our takeaway portion sizes have not been reduced. They remain the same portions that we serve to customers dining in the restaurant.

"However, we have made some adjustments to recipes and preparation methods over time, these changes have been made to align with the food that is served within the restaurant.

"With certain dishes, this can mean they appear to have less sauce than they may have done previously.

"We completely understand why this may have caused some disappointment or confusion, and we appreciate the feedback we have received."

Despite the disappointment of losing the takeaway option, many customers backed the decision.

One customer wrote: "So sorry to hear this - We hope you’re all ok and we’ll miss your takeaways but will just have to come in to eat instead."

Another person commented: "Very sad to read this - we have thoroughly enjoyed all of our meals with you over the years, whether in the restaurant or as a takeaway."

One person wrote: "Such a great shame that your business has suffered as a consequence of rising prices and very disappointing to learn that your staff have been verbally abused- aberrant."

Babul’s has not ruled out bringing back takeaways if it can find a reliable, financially sustainable model that meets customers’ expectations.

The owner added: "For now, we sincerely hope you can understand why we have had to make this difficult decision.

"To everyone who has ordered from us, supported us, recommended us and continued to choose our small independent business over the years, thank you.

"Your support genuinely means more to us than you probably realise, and we never take it for granted."

Despite ending takeaways in Barnard Castle, the business continues to grow, with the Darlington branch continuing unchanged and a new Durham venue in the pipeline.
