Negotiations between Ukraine and the US have been hailed as “very substantive” by Zelensky, as the Trump administration renewed its push to end Russia's four-and-a-half-year war in Ukraine.

US peace envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff met with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky in Kyiv on Sunday, after holding three hours of talks with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Saturday in Moscow.

Witkoff, President Donald Trump's envoy, said they had "heard new ideas" in Moscow, which they did not detail, and they hoped a new round of US-mediated Ukraine-Russia talks would be announced "in short order."

The high-stakes diplomatic effort comes as Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov issued a stark escalation in rhetoric, claiming accusations from Berlin that the Kremlin coordinated a recent drone attack at Germany's Leipzig airport represent "by and large the start of real war".

As Donald Trump’s administration has stepped up efforts to broker an end to the Ukraine war, Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said the talks were “substantive, constructive and extremely frank”, but gave no indication of any breakthrough.

Putin said he had ordered Russian forces to halt strikes on Kyiv after receiving a request through diplomatic and intelligence channels, while Ukraine has said it will similarly refrain from striking Moscow through Monday.

## US peace envoys' visit to Ukraine sparks cautious optimism on the streets of Kyiv

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner have visited Moscow several times in their roles as peace envoys. But Sunday marked their first visit to Kyiv in their capacity as US negotiators.

Their visit has long been sought by Ukraine's leadership, and on the streets of Kyiv, which has been bombarded heavily in recent weeks, some residents were hopeful.

“The issue can be solved in the blink of an eye. We want peace. I've fought myself, so I know what war is like. We need peace, because there's no other way,” said military veteran Stepan Yermak.

In previous talks, the sides have remained far apart on key issues, including the future of the eastern Ukrainian region of Donbas.

Russia has demanded Ukraine's withdrawal from a belt of towns that it still controls there, but Kyiv has refused, reasoning that it can still hold on.

“The question of Donbas... I believe, is the question of this war,” Zelensky told reporters on Sunday.

## US envoy Witkoff says he ‘feels good’ about Ukraine peace talks

US envoy Steve Witkoff said on Sunday that he felt good about peace talks in both Moscow and Kyiv.

The senior envoy from the Trump administration who held talks with Ukraine yesterday said that the US hoped to find a diplomatic solution to the war.

Hours earlier, president Volodymyr Zelensky said his talks with US peace negotiators were “very substantial”.

The two sides discussed security and economic guarantees for Ukraine and also supplies of air-defence missiles and a winter support package.

He also hoped that trilateral talks, including Ukraine, Russia and the US would restart soon.

## One killed and three injured in Russia-held Sevastopol

A girl was killed and three other people were wounded in a Ukraine drone attack on the Russia-held Black Sea port city of Sevastopol late on Sunday, officials said.

The attack was confirmed by Moscow-appointed governor Mikhail Razvozhayev on his Telegram channel.

## Zelensky says window of opportunity for peace exists before 2028 US election

The Ukrainian president, who believes Washington will be key to any peace deal, has said a window of opportunity to make peace exists before the US begins focusing on its next presidential election in 2028.

The US envoys held several hours of talks with Ukrainian officials. British, French and German national security advisers, who were also in Kyiv, joined for the final hour. The US-Ukrainian discussions were due to continue on Sunday evening.

During a break, Volodymyr Zelensky said officials discussed the possibility of security and economic guarantees for Kyiv, as well as a post-war "prosperity plan."

"In the last 10 to 12 months, I believe we have got stronger," Mr Zelensky said, adding that the U.S. and Europe had noted this.

## ‘Overnight we all became war correspondents’: Ukraine’s local newspapers face battle to stay alive

In the weeks leading up to Vladimir Putin’s invasion of Ukraine, the headlines in a Kyiv newspaper looked much like they would anywhere else.

There was some debate over whether Russia would actually attack its neighbour. But then, outside of politics, there was everything else: the tourists stuck abroad, changes to school meals and the spread of the Omicron variant of Covid.

On 24 February 2022, everything changed as Russian tanks rolled across the border. Businesses closed or relocated, martial law saw hundreds of thousands of working-age men recruited for military service, and around one million people fled the country in the first week.

## Putin has no interest in peace – and Trump is falling into his trap

Steve Witkoff is the real estate guy with zero diplomatic experience who Donald Trump appointed in 2024 as his personal “peace emissary”.

Jared Kushner is the president’s son-in-law, but did at least play some role in one of President Trump’s few foreign policy achievements – the Abraham Accords process that promised to deliver some stability to the Middle East during the first Trump term. As a team, they have enjoyed little success in securing the “settlement” to the war in Ukraine that Mr Trump so desires.

There is little reason to suppose that their latest exercise in shuttle diplomacy will succeed where their previous efforts failed. Indeed, the prospects for an end to the war, let alone one that will prove just and lasting, are as dismal as at any time since Vladimir Putin launched his ill-fated “special military operation” more than four and a half years ago – its duration now longer than the Soviet Union’s four-year “great patriotic war” against Adolf Hitler.

Just as in that epoch-defining conflict, it is the civilian population of Ukraine that will face some of the harshest consequences as winter approaches.
