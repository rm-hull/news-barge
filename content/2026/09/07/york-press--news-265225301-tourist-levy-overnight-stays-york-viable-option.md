A £1 visitor levy on overnight stays in York and North Yorkshire is a “viable option” worth exploring, a new report commissioned by the county’s mayor has said.

Mayor David Skaith said the levy, dubbed a “tourist tax”, could be a “game changer” for the county by raising up to £26 million which would be reinvested locally.

But critics, including the county’s Conservative MPs, have slammed the proposals, saying they are “simply wrong” and may deter people from visiting York and North Yorkshire.

The report by York and North Yorkshire's Policy Lab, a partnership between the combined authority and York St John University, found that modest levies do not deter people from visiting appealing destinations.

It pointed to visitor growth remaining steady in places that have already implemented a levy on overnight stays, such as Edinburgh, Manchester, Paris and Barcelona.

The report recommended a simple low-cost digital platform with a flat £1 a night fee and potential exemptions for low-cost stays in a bid to reduce the burden on small operators and campsites.

*(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Skaith, who leads the combined authority, said millions of people visit York and North Yorkshire every year, “and that’s why a visitor levy could be a total game changer for us”.

"Thriving communities and thriving destinations go hand in hand, but welcoming so many visitors puts pressure on the infrastructure and services our residents rely on,” the Labour mayor said.

"We must get this right for our county so we can invest in our communities, and our hospitality and tourism businesses. That's why I'm committed to keep talking with the sector as the details come together."

  


Professor Brendan Paddison, dean of York Business School at York St John University, said the report’s findings “suggest that a visitor levy is a viable option worth exploring further, but the detail matters”.

“Any future levy would need to be simple and proportionate, underpinned by transparent governance and meaningful engagement with businesses and communities,” he said.

“Crucially, there must also be a clear commitment that any revenue raised is invested back into the places and visitor economy that generate it, with benefits that are visible to businesses, visitors and communities across York and North Yorkshire.”

Prof Paddison added: “Good policy starts with good evidence. This report is not about making the case for or against a visitor levy, it is about giving the mayor, businesses and communities the evidence they need to have an informed conversation about what could work for York and North Yorkshire.”

The report comes after Conservative MPs Kevin Hollinrake, Rishi Sunak, Julian Smith and Alec Shelbrooke wrote to Mr Skaith earlier this year, saying the visitor level proposals “should not be taken forward”.

They argued that the levy would hit people taking family holidays in the county and the “family businesses that depend on them, at a time when both are already facing significant cost pressures”.

The MPs also said there is a “lack of clarity around who would ultimately bear the burden of the levy” which is a “recurring concern raised with us”.
