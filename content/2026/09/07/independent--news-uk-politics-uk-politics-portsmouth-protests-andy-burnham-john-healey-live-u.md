Police are hunting to identify offenders after police were injured and cars were damaged in anti-migration protests.

Hampshire and Isle of Wight police said on Monday that “a number of police, Coastguard and Border Force vehicles were damaged” after hundreds of far-right demonstrators descended on Portsmouth.

“A small number of officers were treated at the scene for injuries, but no-one in attendance from any agency or the public was seriously injured.”

They said no arrests have been made at this time, however they had launched an investigation to identify offenders responsible for any assaults to officers, criminal damage to vehicles, or other criminal offences.

It comes after Reform UK chairman Lee Anderson urged those attending protests to “do it the right way” and condemned “attacks on our police”.

He said: “If you’re going to protest then be careful, do it the right way then you have my full support because the left are just looking for an excuse to label you all as far right thugs. You're not, you are decent patriotic Brits who've had enough.”

The government has meanwhile condemned far-right protesters who blocked roads and faced up to riot police over the weekend as “thuggish and intimidating”.

## Danny Tommo interrupts interview with Zia Yusuf at Reform UK conference

The founder of the group behind the anti-immigration protests at Dover on Saturday has interrupted an interview with Reform UK’s Zia Yusuf in Portsmouth.

Daniel Thomas, known as Danny Tommo – an associate of ring-wing activist Tommy Robinson – spoke to the Reform UK home affairs spokesperson at length, following Mr Yusuf’s at-times heated interviews with news outlets.

The Patriot Platform group has been criticised for wearing masks and all-black clothing in Dover across the political spectrum, including by Reform UK leader Nigel Farage.

Mr Thomas, who founded Patriot Platform in July, dashed around the large crowd of reporters and said: “You have to understand, Yusuf, that what we’re trying to do and the reason the Patriot Platform has been created is not because of violence. We’re not looking to intimidate anyone.

The reason they’re covering their faces is because we know they’re going to be targeted.”

He added: “What Nigel Farage has done is not good. When you call people disgusting, yeah, you have to understand the reasons of why this is happening.”

## Farage 'needs to be careful with his words,' far-right activist says

The founder of a group of protesters who blocked roads in Dover on Saturday has told Reform UK’s Zia Yusuf his party leader Nigel Farage needs to be “careful with his words”.

Interrupting Mr Yusuf during an interview in Portsmouth following another protest on Sunday, Daniel Thomas, known as Danny Tommo, accused Mr Farage of calling protesters at the Unite the Kingdom rally in London earlier in the year “knuckle-draggers”.

Mr Thomas, who founded Patriot Platform in July, said: “You have to tell Nigel… we had a million people on the streets of London in every colour, every creed, every background, every ethnicity… he called us knuckle-draggers.

“I understand Nigel is going to be prime minister one day and I understand he has to set a standard, which will basically be that you know he can’t just come out and say everything’s great and these guys are fantastic, but Nigel needs to be very careful with his words.”

Mr Yusuf said he was at the scene to listen to people, and told Mr Thomas: “Nobody has done more to raise this issue, and taken a load of flack for it, than Nigel Farage.”

He added: “I will take that message, you have my word.”

## Portsmouth resident: migrant protest 'closest I've ever been to an insurrection'

A Portsmouth resident said the protest over migrant arrivals on Sunday night was “the closest I’ve ever been to an insurrection”.

Sue Dewey watched the Eastney scenes unfold from her balcony, and said: “It was a very surreal night, that was the closest I’ve ever been to an insurrection.”

The 68-year-old added: “I’m not a rebel, I’m not an agitator. I am very unhappy about the way things are unfolding, I think that it’s not great for this country – these people are illegal, they’re coming from a safe country, the French are enabling them, we’ve just given them a load more money to enable them even better.”

Ms Dewey told reporters: “They (the migrants) were held up for about 12 hours by the agitators. They didn’t let them go until about 4am.”

On Monday afternoon Ms Dewey was seen taking several life jackets which had been left on a gangway at Eastney.

She said: “They’re just sitting there, I mean the police have said somebody’s coming to collect them but they don’t belong to anybody.”

Ms Dewey said her neighbour took some life vests at about 7am on Monday which prompted her to think “we’ll get some for the (boating) club because the club’s been inconvenienced by this”, adding club staff had to stay on sofas there overnight because they could not leave the street.

## Job losses at Jaguar Land Rover 'deeply concerning', minister says

## Only crime committed in Dover protest was criminal damage to the road, Kent police say

Kent Police said that the only crime committed during Saturday’s protest in Dover was criminal damage to the road.

In a statement Kent Police Assistant Chief Constable Nigel Brookes insisted the gathering by 200 people dressed in black and wearing face coverings was spontaneous.

He said: “We are of course disappointed with the significant traffic disruption for several hours on Saturday morning in Dover. We will always support people’s right to peaceful protest, and we will always seek to police proportionately, fairly, and reasonably. We do not take account of why people are protesting, or the rights, wrongs, or reasonableness of that as that is not a matter for the police.

“Saturday’s protest involving approximately 200 people developed rapidly and, based on the information available to us at the time, was treated in-line with national guidance as spontaneous.

“Officers attended, engaged directly with those involved, and worked to restore the free movement of traffic and access to the port. By midday those involved had dispersed, and normal traffic flows resumed, with traffic clearing fairly quickly. Extensive traffic management plans are in place given the frequency of disruption on the roads at the frontier.

“It is important we distinguish between conduct we find objectionable and disruptive and conduct which constitutes a criminal offence. The right to peaceful protest is an important part of our democracy and applies irrespective of the political views being expressed. Equally, that right is not unlimited, and if offences are committed, lawful police conditions are breached, or disruption crosses the legal threshold we will take action. Based on our current assessment, the only associated offence we are investigating is criminal damage to a road.

“There has been some discussion about the fact that protesters on Saturday wore face coverings. To do so is not, in itself, sufficient to establish a criminal offence. Police powers concerning face coverings have specific statutory requirements, and officers must satisfy those requirements before exercising them. There is no need to wear a face covering in Kent, if your plan is to peacefully express your views. We will examine the intelligence process, but it is important to note that we do not as a matter of course monitor peaceful protesters, and their political viewpoints are not taken into consideration.

“Moving forward, we will seek to identify and engage with those who attended to understand their future plans in a bid to support their rights to peaceful protest and balance them with the needs of the local community, who we remain steadfast in supporting and protecting.”
