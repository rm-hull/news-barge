Durham Jazz Festival has received a large amount of interest ahead of its debut weekend in October.

The first edition of the three-day festival is scheduled from Friday 23 to Sunday October 25 in Durham city centre.

It will feature acts such as Newcastle-based band Knats, jazz vocalist of the year nominee Marvin Muoneké, and emerging London-based group SULA.

The festival is advertised for both passionate jazz fans and audiences unfamiliar with the genre.

It will bring together nationally known jazz veterans, North East talent and burgeoning artists for a weekend full of music across 13 Durham venues.

  Organisers of the Durham Jazz Festival, which will be held in October 

*(Image: Durham Jazz Festival)*

John Lyons shared the organisers’ excitement for the weekend, praising the amount of local talent being showcased: "The festival has been created to celebrate both the rich jazz talent of the North East and leading artists from across the UK.

"[There is] a programme designed to appeal to dedicated jazz followers as well as audiences looking to discover the genre for the first time."

Two acts have already sold out, Knats and Emma Fisk’s Hot Club Du Nord, and now organisers are urging audiences to book tickets early due to high demand for other acts.

Tickets remain available for several performances around the city, with a 10 per cent discount available when booking three or more shows at once.

Organiser Alan Patrickson said: "The response to our first Durham Jazz Festival has been fantastic.

"Seeing Knats sell out following their Mercury Prize nomination is a real milestone, and now we’re seeing several other shows getting close to capacity.”

  It's the first time the jazz festival has come to the city 

*(Image: Durham Jazz Festival)*

Newcastle jazz band Knats were nominated for the 2026 Mercury Prize following their second album, A Great Day in Newcastle, with the award ceremony taking place in Newcastle’s Utilita Arena just two days before the group are scheduled to perform in Fabio’s Bar.

Britain’s Got Talent semi-finalist Marvin Muoneké will play alongside Newcastle orchestra.

The Strictly Smokin’ Big Band at the Gala Theatre following Muoneké’s 2026 nomination for Jazz Vocalist of the Year at the JazzFM awards.

The variety in venues will allow audiences to roam the city between shows, said Mr Lyons, and offer different environments for each act’s performances- “from intimate club-style gigs and late-night jams to big band performances and major headline shows.”

Other highly anticipated acts include Jo Harrop, originally from Durham, announcing her homecoming album Buy Now, Pay Later, plus The Pete Roth Trio featuring Bill Buford, and contemporary jazz musician Eleonora Kouneni.

Another name on the bill attracting attention is Ronnie Scott’s Jazz Club: The Ronnie Scott’s Soho Songbook, inviting some of London’s legendary jazz scene to perform at the festival.

The closing act on Sunday evening will be in association with Durham Brass Festival, featuring Olivia Cuttill Quintet and King Pleasure & The Biscuit Boys at Redhills Durham Miners’ Hall, with organisers promising a “riotous and fun party evening” to wrap up the weekend’s festivities.
