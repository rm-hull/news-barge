A BRADFORD legal firm has been refused retrospective planning permission for new signage on a listed building.

Khan Solicitors had applied for retrospective planning permission to replace the frontage of Bridge House – the firm’s Sunbridge Road offices.

The application also sought to replace the timber window frames of 51 windows of the upper floors of 12-20 Sunbridge Road with black aluminium.

The building is Grade II listed.

The application said the works were needed to improve “security and the energy efficiency of the building by way of its ability to retain heat.”

But planning officers have now refused the application.

Referring to the signage above the door, planning officers said: “The effect of the signage is strident, projects forward from the face of the masonry apertures and is incongruous.

“The signage causes harm which is not balanced by any public benefit.”

Referring to the plans for the windows, officers said: “Replacing them with aluminium frames would remove the original joinery and introduce frames with no decorative profiles or finesse, resulting in windows that no longer complement their surrounding masonry.

“The application notes that multiple properties in the area have windows with black aluminium frames, however these buildings are not listed.

“The presence of these windows on a non-listed property is not sufficient to justify the harm they would cause to the listed building.”
