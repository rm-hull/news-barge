President Donald Trump has said that the US is prepared to launch another attack on Iran at “any time”.

"We took out all of the new equipment that they tried to build along the Strait of Hormuz - some defensive, some offensive,” he told reporters in the Oval Office on Wednesday.

“It was a very heavy attack last night, and we're prepared to do another one any time we want.” He added that a renewed campaign would not last “too long”.

It came as Iranians and others in the region feared a return of the conflict in earnest after the biggest exchange of fire between Tehran and Washington since July.

US attacks hit the southern coast of Iran while Tehran hit back at US assets in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait and Iraq.

Iran accused Washington of hitting a wedding party, killing four people and wounding dozens, while its health minister later said 18 people in total had been killed and 108 injured in the latest wave of US strikes.

The US military said the targets of its attacks were air defences, radar systems, maritime assets, mine-laying capabilities and communications sites on Tuesday.

## Trump floats renaming Strait of Hormuz as ‘Trump Strait’

President Donald Trump suggested on Wednesday that the Strait of Hormuz should be renamed after him, claiming the key waterway is now under US control.

“Now that we have it under USA control, should we change the name Hormuz Strait to TRUMP STRAIT???” Trump said in a post on Truth Social.

The waterway has become a flashpoint in the renewed conflict between the US and Iran. The latest escalation began after US forces struck what an American military spokesperson said were Iranian Revolutionary Guard forces preparing to lay more sea mines in the strait.

Trump last month also vowed to declare the Strait of Hormuz US territory.

## Trump aides trying to pause Iran war to save GOP from midterm election disaster: report

Senior advisers to US President Donald Trump are attempting to prevent the military conflict with Iran from worsening ahead of November's midterm elections to curb potential Republican losses at the polls, according to four individuals knowledgeable about the discussions.

However, this strategy is already facing severe pressure as exchange strikes between the United States and Iran persist.

White House figures are considering an increase in military operations following the November 3 vote, the sources said, who asked not to be named when describing internal considerations, though a full resumption of major warfare remains far from certain.

## Australia calls Iran’s Hormuz shipping restrictions ‘unacceptable’

Australia’s deputy prime minister and defence minister Richard Marles has called for the Strait of Hormuz to be reopened, saying Iran’s restrictions on shipping through the key waterway were “unacceptable”.

“What we need to see is the Strait of Hormuz open,” Marles told ABC News from Washington, DC.

It came as he met US defence secretary Pete Hegseth in Washington DC, on Wednesday.

He said Iran’s actions were “fundamentally in contravention” of the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea and the principle of freedom of navigation.

“We do want to see a de-escalation to what’s occurred in the Middle East, but we need to see the Strait of Hormuz open and global fuel supply chains return to normal,” he said.

## US says Navy has redirected 86 commercial vessels under Iran blockade

The US military's Central Command said it continues to enforce its blockade against Iran, with US forces having redirected 86 commercial vessels.

Centcom said in a social media post that the US Navy has also disabled three vessels and boarded two to "ensure compliance."

"The US Navy continues to strictly enforce the US blockade against Iran," it said in the post.

## Iran reports 18 killed in latest US strikes

Iran’s health minister said 18 people were killed and 108 wounded in US strikes across the country on Tuesday night.

The latest reported deaths add to the 3,636 people documented as killed in Iran as of April 7, including 1,701 civilians, according to the Human Rights Activists News Agency, an independent Iranian rights group.

The US has reported 18 military personnel killed and more than 750 wounded.

## Reported wedding attack draws UN condemnation

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said he was “deeply alarmed” by reports of civilian casualties, including the reported strike on a wedding ceremony in Iran.

His spokesperson Stephane Dujarric said he called for an immediate cessation of military action.

## Kuwait says air defences intercepting Iranian missile, drone attacks

Kuwait’s air defences are responding to Iranian missile and drone attacks, the country’s military said on Wednesday.

The General Staff said explosions heard in the country were caused by air defence systems intercepting what it described as hostile targets, according to a post on X.

## Iran's rial drops to new low of 2.2 million to the dollar, tracking websites show

Iran's currency has dropped to a record low of more than 2.2 million rials per U.S. dollar, two websites tracking the free market rate showed on Wednesday, amid renewed military strikes.

The rial has dropped about 10 per cent in the past week. The troubled currency has lost about half of its value in a year of tightening U.S. sanctions and U.S. and Israeli attacks that began in February.

## US says 17 million barrels oil transited Strait of Hormuz on Monday

US secretary of energy Chris Wright said on Tuesday that 17 million barrels of oil transited the Strait of Hormuz on Monday.

He said that this marked the highest level of crude oil to pass through the waterway since the Iran war had reduced flows.
