PLANS have been submitted for the old Fulton's frozen food store in Otley town centre.

Mr Azhar Hussain, of Numu Properties Ltd, is seeking planning permission from Leeds City Council to bring the vacant building at 1-9 Market Place back into use through a scheme involving internal alterations, accessibility improvements and external refurbishment works.

The application is asking for change of use of the second floor from Class C3 to Class E and the use of the whole building as office accommodation within Class E.

## What would the building be turned into?

The proposal would see the building's basement, ground, first and second floors used as office space. While much of the building already falls within the commercial Class E use category, permission is required because the second floor has a historic consent for residential use.

According to the application, the development would provide a reception area, meeting rooms and office accommodation across the upper floors, with staff facilities and storage space located in the basement.

The plans would create a total of 48 desk spaces.

No extensions or structural additions are proposed, with the applicant instead focusing on refurbishing the existing building, with work already underway.

Externally, the scheme would involve removing and replacing deteriorating white render on a like-for-like basis and repainting the property white.

The existing blue aluminium shopfront would also be replaced in the same style and colour, while branding associated with the former frozen food retailer would be removed and replaced with more traditional signage.

Most upper-floor windows would be renewed on a like-for-like basis, with no changes to their size or appearance.

The building lies within the Otley Town Centre Conservation Area, although it is not listed.

Planning documents state the proposals have been designed to preserve the building's appearance and contribution to the historic streetscape while removing prominent corporate branding.

The application also includes measures to improve accessibility.

A lift would be reinstated using an existing lift shaft, level or ramped access would be retained at the entrance and an accessible toilet would be provided on the ground floor.

Planning documents state the development would bring workers and visitors into the town centre, helping to support local businesses and increase footfall in Market Place.

The application can be viewed on Leeds City Council's Planning Portal (Ref: 26/04808/FU) and is out for consultation until September 24.
