A COUNCIL opposition leader has bit back at Reform UK’s policy of banning Pride flags on town hall buildings.

On Wednesday, it was announced that Kirklees Council had approved the flag policy.

A report made by Kirklees Council stipulated that only the Union Flag, Flag of St George, Civic Flag and specific flags associated with ‘named days’ will be permitted to be flown from Civic buildings owned or managed by the local authority.

This move – which was made without the prior approval of ward councillors – is now facing backlash from the Leader of the Kirklees Council opposition, Green Party Cllr Andrew Cooper, who branded the move a “divisive and unnecessary diversion”.

Speaking on the new policy, Cllr Cooper said: “With so many problems facing the people of Kirklees, it is disappointing that the first thing Reform decided to do is create division when there are so many other issues that need their attention.

“The council is facing an overspend in its budget of £13,6 million, demand for services for elderly and vulnerable people is soaring.

“Fly-tipping and the state of Kirklees roads continue to be big issues of concern in our communities, yet the first thing Reform does is try to whip up division over which flags we do or don’t fly on council buildings.”

Cllr Cooper went on to explain that under the previous Kirklees protocol on which flags should be raised on town halls, it would be a decision between the Kirklees Mayor, the Council’s Chief Executive and the Leader of the Council.

Cllr Cooper said: “Cllr Sarah Wood, as Leader of the Council, would still have a say over what flags would have been flown anyway. So why the need for this policy?”

“I guess the intended targets of this Reform policy will be the Pride flag and flags expressing support for Ukraine and Palestine.

"The fact is that Kirklees has never raised a Ukraine or Palestine flag, and when the Pride flag has been raised on town halls, there hasn’t been an outcry or demands that it be taken down.

“…This announcement simply stokes division in our communities and is a diversion from the real issues that they should be dealing with.”

Speaking on the ban, Reform UK Councillor Sarah Wood, Leader of Kirklees Council, assured new policy was in place to “set out a clear approach across Kirklees”, while focusing on the “symbols that unite us all.”

Cllr Wood said: “This is about having a straightforward approach to flags and celebrating the things we have in common: our country and our community.”
