A Teesside man with fond childhood memories of Roseberry Topping will climb the iconic hill 51 times in memory of his late mum.

Dean Smith, 40, from New Marske, will spend up to 30 hours repeatedly climbing Roseberry Topping this weekend in a challenge designed to match the height of Mount Everest.

Roseberry Topping and the surrounding Teesside countryside are tied to Dean’s childhood days out with his mum, Denise, who died from blood cancer in 2022.

  Dean Smith will climb Roseberry Topping 51 times, matching the height of Everest, in memory of his mum Denise. 

*(Image: Blood Cancer UK)*

Dean said: "I went there with Mum as a kid. We used to drive out for days together.

"We'd go up Roseberry Topping, over to Captain Cook's Monument, stop at Great Ayton for an ice cream and a Petch's pie, then sit by the river and feed the ducks.

"It's all those nostalgic things you remember, so that whole area means a lot to me.

"To be taking this on there, in Mum's memory, makes it even more special."

Doctors diagnosed Denise with myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS), a form of blood cancer, in 2018.

She suffered serious complications, including sepsis, before dying on May 30, 2022.

"My mam was the toughest person I've ever known. She could turn the darkest moments into something positive," Dean said.

"This is the best way I know how to honour her by challenging myself in her name.

"Whenever I'm on those dark, tough climbs, I remember my mam's strength.

"If she could battle through what she did, I can push through too."

  Dean and his mum, Denise, who passed away in 2022. 

*(Image: Blood Cancer UK)*

Dean plans to start at 5am on Saturday, September 5, and climb through the day and night without sleep until he reaches approximately 8,849 metres - the height of Everest.

He added: "This is the only way I can do Everest without actually going to Everest.

"I'll see the sunrise, go right the way through, see another sunrise and hopefully finish around midday on the Sunday.

"I'm going to be absolutely knackered by that point."

"Quite a few people want to come and do the final climb with me, and I think that's when I'm going to need them the most," he said.

Dean is currently living with a hernia, while also recovering from a back injury sustained at the beginning of the year.

After taking a break from major fundraising challenges in 2025, Dean has now lost more than three stone since January while training for climb.

The Roseberry Topping climb is part of Dean’s 'Super September' - a 10,000-metre swim at Saltburn the following week, followed by the 161-mile Coast to Coast route.

Dean says his wife, Leanne, and their wider family have made it all possible.

Dean said: "If mum was here now, she'd be telling me I'm stupid, but she'd know why I was doing it."

"Mum fought a long, hard battle. I can't change what happened to her, but I can keep her memory alive and turn something awful into something positive.

"When it gets dark, when it hurts and when I'm wondering why on earth I'm climbing Roseberry Topping again, that's what I'll be thinking about."
