A leading figure in social care is set to retire after nearly 25 years of service.

Jenni Wood, chief executive of Durham County Carers Support (DCCS), is stepping down after a career helping thousands of unpaid carers across the region.

She joined Durham and Chester-le-Street Carers Support before becoming CEO of DCCS following a merger 14 years ago.

Ms Wood said: "Being an unpaid carer for family, friends and neighbours is better recognised now and we are reaching more people and professionals.

"To have a job where you can make a difference every day has been a gift."

Ms Wood, who lives in Cockfield and is herself a carer, began her career in social research with the Office of National Statistics before moving into adult literacy and supporting children with learning disabilities.

During her 14 years as chief executive, the number of carers supported by the charity has grown from 8,000 to 34,000.

Her role will be split between Jacqui Coulson, who will oversee resources, and Liz Johns, who will manage operations.

Ms Coulson said: "I’d personally like to thank Jenni for her dedication and commitment to DCCS.

"Jenni and I first started working together following the merger in 2012, so we’ve shared a huge amount of change and development."

Chair of the board, Janet Potts, said: "Jenni has fundamentally shaped DCCS and has left an extraordinary 24-year legacy.

"Her enthusiasm shone through from the beginning, and we all have a great deal of admiration for her."

Ms Wood now plans to enjoy her retirement with family and friends, and pursue her interests in folk music, the theatre, and singing with Heart Songs, a women’s group at Barnard Castle’s Hub.
