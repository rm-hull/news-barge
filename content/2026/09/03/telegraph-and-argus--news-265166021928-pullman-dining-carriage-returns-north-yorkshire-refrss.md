A FULLY restored 1928 Pullman dining carriage has returned to service.

Car 79, the oldest Pullman dining coach at the NYMR, and the only one of its type remaining, is now running on the North Yorkshire Moors Railway (NYMR) after a 10-year restoration project.

It is now in use on the railway’s Autumn Pullman dining services.

Laura Strangeway, chief executive at NYMR, said: "Car 79 is a truly special part of the NYMR heritage, so it’s incredibly exciting to see this beautifully restored carriage return to service.

"The scale and craftsmanship involved in the project has been remarkable, and we’re so glad passengers can now experience it for themselves."

Passengers can enjoy a three-course meal on board while travelling through the North York Moors.

Car 79 was part of an order of 29 all-steel Pullman cars for the London and North Eastern Railway (LNER) and remained in service until 1965.

It was donated to the South Yorkshire Area Group of the NYMR in 1973, before arriving at the railway in May 1990.

Restoration work included major roof repairs, all-new roof side panels, a bogie overhaul, bodywork restoration, rewiring, marquetry repairs, and interior refurbishments.

Ms Strangeway said: "The Autumn Pullman services are the perfect opportunity to step back in time whilst travelling on history through the stunning moors.

"We hope diners will enjoy the unique character of Car 79 as much as we do."

The journey departs from Grosmont Station at 12.35pm and travels 18 miles to Pickering Station, with a return to Grosmont.

The full experience lasts around two hours and 45 minutes.
