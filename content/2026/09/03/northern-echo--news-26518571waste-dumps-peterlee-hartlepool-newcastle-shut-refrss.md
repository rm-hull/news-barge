Three mounding waste dumps featuring almost 3,500 tonnes of rubbish across the North East have been shut down.

An investigation has been launched after three illegal waste dumps were discovered in Peterlee, Hartlepool, and Callerton in Newcastle in the last few weeks.

The sites all contained shredded household waste - with the Environment Agency (EA) saying the Hartlepool dump, which was reported on August 18, weighed an estimated 2,500 tonnes.

Both Peterlee, which was reported on July 17, and Callerton, near Newcastle Airport, which was reported on August 24, were estimated to feature 400 tonnes of waste.

  The waste dump in Newcastle 

*(Image: ENVIRONMENT AGENCY)*

The EA said it acted quickly to close down the sites and put concrete blocks in place. Plans are being made with the landowners to clear the space.

Mark Rumble, environmental crime team leader at the EA, said: “We’ve taken quick action to block access to these sites to prevent further dumping and investigations are underway.

“Illegal waste sites are a blight on our communities and residents should not have to live next to eyesores and put up with the smells.

“We have stepped up enforcement action with our 10 point plan and I want to make it clear that we’ll do everything possible to ensure those involved in dumping this waste will be held accountable.”

Minister for Circular Economy and Waste Crime Emma Hardy said: “Residents deserve to be free from the blight of waste crime – and the decisive action taken in this case is an example of our crackdown on these criminals.

  The waste dump in Peterlee 

*(Image: ENVIRONMENT AGENCY)*

“The Government is shutting down the gangs running illegal waste sites, with a stepping up of enforcement action and more officers on the frontline.

“My message to the criminals involved in waste is clear: we are coming for you, and we will take your profits, and we will use them to clean up, because the times of people getting away with these crimes are over.”

Illegally dumped waste poses a risk to communities and the environment and undermines legitimate businesses that dispose of waste responsibly, as well as being costly for landowners.

It’s estimated waste crime costs the UK over £1 billion every year.

The Environment Agency is urging anyone with information about the three sites, those involved in waste crime in the region, or anyone who sees suspicious activity to get in touch.

Mark added: “I’d urge people to report information straight away to us or anonymously though Crimestoppers.

“Acting quickly gives us the best chance of investigating effectively and preventing further illegal dumping.”

You can report suspected waste crime to Crimestoppers on 0800555111 or the Environment Agency incident line on 0800 807060.
