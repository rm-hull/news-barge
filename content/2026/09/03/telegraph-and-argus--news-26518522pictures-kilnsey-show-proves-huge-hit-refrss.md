Thousands turned out for a much-loved country show despite a burst of rain — but organisers had to add an extra race after one attraction proved far more popular than expected.

Kilnsey Show and Sports, in the Yorkshire Dales, returned for another glorious celebration of all things great and rural this week with a big crowd on its sprawling grounds beneath the landmark crag.

Many thousands flocked to the Dales' premier agricultural event on Tuesday and this year one southern visitor commented on its Facebook page: "Our five hour journey from Wiltshire was WELL worth it. Congratulations to everyone involved!"

  Watching the action in the main ring 

*(Image: Tim Smith)*

Most folk were from closer to home.

Mark Walsh from Bradford brings his family to the show every year and explained why: "We live in a city and think it's so important for our kids to get up close to animals and understand what nature and the countryside is all about," said Mark. "Whenever they see the gigantic bulls they go wide-eyed and say 'Wow - look at that!' and to be honest, I do just the same."

  The Sheep Show 

*(Image: Tim Smith)*

Probably relishing a bit of damp after months of dry heat, the premier livestock on show looked both magnificent and content. The busy sheep pens were home to the best in the business and visitors were surely impressed by the love and care lavished on what they might previously thought of as "just a sheep".

Similarly, when it comes to cattle, people are always struck by the sheer size and scale of the beasts. Even grown adults gasp.

  Demonstrating a horse gin, which powered farm machinery used for threshing corn and other processes, 

*(Image: Tim Smith)*

Show regular and Skipton MP Sir Julian Smith was amongst those who watched the sheepdog trials, many equine events, the vintage vehicle parades and also the famous Kilnsey Crag race, and commented:

"It was fantastic to attend the Kilnsey Show again this year and is a brilliant celebration of our rural communities, local farming, traditional skills and the many local businesses that make such an important contribution to our local economy.

"I would like to thank everyone involved in organising this year's event. The hard work of the volunteers, committee members, exhibitors and participants has once again delivered a wonderful day for residents and visitors alike."

  Runners in the Kilnsey Crag Fell Race for under 17s 

*(Image: Tim Smith)*

Show chairman, Chris Windle, was very pleased with the day and with his success in the produce tent. For the second year running he got first place for his flavoured vodka - with damson - and a very respectable second place in the 'cake made by a gentleman' with his lemon and blueberry cake in a hotly contested class of 22.

"The show went very well. We had a decent crowd, there were a couple of sharp showers after lunch, but it was dry apart from that and we've had some very good comments, people seemed to enjoy themselves.

"Everything went ahead as planned and the ground was good and dry, no one got stuck in the car park. The harness racing, which is probably a unique end to a show, went very well and they had to add an extra race because of all the entries."
