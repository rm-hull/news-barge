Russian former president Dmitry Medvedev has threatened Germany with a “direct strike” on military production sites after Berlin accused Moscow of being behind a drone attack on Leipzig Airport last month.

Medvedev, the deputy chair of Russia’s security council and a routinely outspoken provocateur, wrote on Telegram that Germany’s leadership “deserve a direct strike on all German military equipment production facilities ... and quite possibility on several other locations in Berlin as well”.

In an ominous addendum he suggested: “Every terror has sponsors. At the moment, these are the heads of Western countries, primarily European ones. And they often travel to Kyiv by train...”

Workers last month found a drone laden with explosives and a detonator at the airport, which is a base for several Ukrainian cargo planes.

Germany characterises the attempted attack as part of hybrid warfare by the Kremlin meant to cause divisions in countries that back Ukraine. Russia denies involvement.

Chancellor Friedrich Merz has promised President Volodymyr Zelensky that Ukraine will have Germany’s continued support following the discovery.

Russian drones tore into central Kyiv on Wednesday, wounding 11 people on the seventh day running of attacks.

## Moscow responds to Ukraine's warning on risky Russian airspace

Russia vowed to head off any disruption to civil aviation on Wednesday after a warning by Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky that its drones had made Russian airspace dangerous to use.

Zelensky has warned that Ukraine is responding to Russian attacks meant "the war started by Russia is closing its own skies" but his forces are not targeting civil aviation.

Andrei Nikitin, Russia's transport minister, was quoted as saying by the Interfax news agency: "We are constantly improving flight safety and airport security requirements, and we have solutions for all of this.”

“We are working in close contact with the Ministry of Defence and the Russian National Guard. And I believe that we will not allow any significant disruption to civil aviation,” he said.

## Flights to Russia cancelled as disruptions seen at Moscow airport

Flight tracking website Flightradar24 data showed disruptions yesterday at Moscow's main airport, while some airlines cancelled flights though they did not specify the reason for doing so.

Moscow's Sheremetyevo International Airport showed 21 cancelled flights and 235 delays, while the city's Vnukovo airport had 22 cancelled flights and 94 delays, according to Flightradar24.

"We continue to closely monitor the evolving situation in the region and adjust our flight schedules and routings as necessary," airline flydubai said in a statement.

“As the situation remains dynamic, our teams are continuously assessing operational requirements. We recognise that some delays and diversions may occur... The safety of our customers and crew remains our highest priority,” they said.

Turkish carrier Pegasus Airlines said it had cancelled some flights to Russia for "technical and operational" reasons, without giving further details.

Vnukovo airport said Pegasus Airlines had cancelled six arriving and six departing flights at the hub on Tuesday.

The carrier said overnight flights into Wednesday were impacted, but other ongoing flights were "operating as scheduled".

## Ukraine asks UN aviation agency to support ban on flights in Russian airspace

Ukraine has urged the UN's aviation agency to ask its members to fully ban operations in Russian airspace, a day after president Volodymyr Zelensky warned it was "becoming completely unsafe".

Ukraine would only target Russian military facilities and not threaten civilian aircraft, Zelensky added, but he stressed that the growing number of Ukrainian drones in Russian skies should "be taken into account".

In a letter to International Civil Aviation Organization chairman Juan Carlos Salazar, a copy of which was exclusively shared with Reuters, Ukraine's infrastructure minister Mykola Kalashnyk said Kyiv believed the risk of tragedies in the skies should be minimised.

Ukraine has asked ICAO to encourage aviation authorities and airlines to safeguard civil aviation, "including by facilitating the full prohibition by ICAO member states of civil aircraft operations within the airspace of the Russian Federation".

## Russian air attack damages multi-story residential building in Ukraine's Odesa

A Russian air attack damaged a multi-story residential building in Ukraine's Black Sea port city of Odesa in the early hours today, said Serhiy Lysak, the head of its military administration.

At least eight people were injured in the attack, Lysak said.

A Reuters witness reported an explosion on Wednesday in the capital Kyiv amid continued Russian air strikes.

## Russia vows to fend off disruption to civil aviation after Ukraine's threats

Russia vowed to head off any disruption to civil aviation on Wednesday after Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenksy said Kyiv’s drones had made Russian airspace dangerous to use.

Mr Zelensky warned airlines and insurance firms that the presence of Ukrainian drones as it responded to Russian attacks meant “the war started by Russia is closing its own skies”. He said he was not targeting civil aviation.

And Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said on Wednesday the country had also officially notified the International Civil Aviation Organisation that Russian airspace was not safe.

## Citibank’s London branch fined £4.7m for breaching Russia sanctions

Citibank’s London branch has been fined £4.7m for breaching Russian financial sanctions rules in 2022.

The division of the US-based investing banking group processed hundreds of payments between accounts linked to designated Russian companies and individuals, the Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation (OFSI) found in an investigation.

In total, 970 payments worth about £19.7m were considered to be breaches.

They included processing payments for designated Russian banks Alfa-Bank JSC, PJSC Gazprombank and Credit Bank of Moscow.

Most of the transactions occurred between February and November 2022, following Russia’s invasion of Ukraine.

## Kyiv creates underground storage bunkers to protect fuel from attacks

Ukraine is creating a network of underground fuel storage facilities as its energy system comes under daily Russian attack, energy minister Denys Shmyhal has said.

The priority was to ensure an uninterrupted supply of diesel fuel, primarily for defence needs and critical infrastructure, he said.

In recent weeks, Russia has intensified attacks on fuel stations, destroying or damaging around 300, primarily in frontline regions.

Mr Shmyhal said Ukraine and Romania had agreed to expand fuel supply capacity to Kyiv via both rail and river ports.
