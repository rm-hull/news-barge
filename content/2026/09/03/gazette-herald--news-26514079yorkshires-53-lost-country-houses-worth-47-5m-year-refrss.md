Dozens of lost country houses could be costing the region millions in lost heritage value each year.

New analysis by Family Tree magazine estimates that 53 lost country houses in Yorkshire may represent around £47.5 million in annual heritage value, with £31.7 million potentially linked to local spending.

Rounton Grange, an Arts and Crafts house designed by Philip Webb for the Bell family and associated with explorer and writer Gertrude Bell, is one of the clearest local examples of what was lost.

It was demolished in the 1950s.

Helen Tovey, editor of Family Tree, said: "Rounton Grange is exactly the kind of place that shows why lost country houses still matter.

"It was not just a grand home.

"It was a workplace, a local landmark and part of Yorkshire’s story."

She said the loss of such estates erases more than just architecture, highlighting their importance to local identity and family history.

Ms Tovey added: "That is why family history is not just names and dates.

"It is about putting people back into the places, jobs and communities that shaped their lives."

The analysis forms part of broader research into Britain’s lost country houses and the economic and social impact of their disappearance.
