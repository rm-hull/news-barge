Nigel Farage will try to shake off ongoing scrutiny about funding both he and Reform UK have received as the party’s conference begins.

The Clacton MP has just returned to parliament after winning a by-election he called in his constituency in protest at the Commons’ standards inquiry against him over a donation from Christopher Harborne.

On the eve of the Reform conference, which begins in Birmingham on Thursday, Mr Farage told the BBC his party is up against a “complainant society” and suggested that he would not get a fair hearing on whether he broke parliamentary rules for failing to declare the cash, because the MPs on the committee have already prejudged him as guilty.

Reform’s conference begins as it fell behind the Conservatives into third place in the polls -–its lowest performance in the last 18 months.

In a new voting intention poll from More in Common on 1 September, the right-wing party has landed on 22 per cent, four points behind Labour and one point behind the Tories.

## Three-course meal and champagne with Farage: What £3,000 gets you at Reform's conference

Supporters of Reform have been offered the chance to sip champagne with Nigel Farage at his party’s conference this week.

The “platinum pass”, which costs £3,000 + VAT per person, is the most expensive and exclusive ticket for the conference, and offers access to the party’s “key players, senior leadership team and MPs.

“At the centre of the Platinum experience is our extraordinary bespoke 20m × 10m double-deck hospitality structure—the largest B-Matrix stand of its kind in Europe.

“This impressive, purpose-built space will provide Platinum guests with a luxurious environment in which to meet, network, dine and relax away from the main conference floor while remaining at the centre of the action,” the party said.

Ticket holders will get an exclusive champagne breakfast attended by Nigel Farage on Saturday, a three-course meal with senior figures, and “high-level networking” opportunities.

The cheapest tickets for the conference are £25 for under-25 members, while general access is £50.

## Reform to unveil its plan for first 100 days in government

Reform will unveil its plan for its first 100 days in government if it is elected at the party conference today.

The conference is kicking off with its first ever “business day”, which includes a full day of “business-focused sessions, fringe events and panel discussions” with around 1,000 business delegates.

Richard Tice told Sky News: “This is our business day, our first of three days....There is well over 5,000 people expected hearing our plans for the first 100 days of a Reform UK government.

“Britain's broken, it needs fixing, it needs business like people, with a can-do, positive constructive attitude.”

Mr Tice said his party would be discussing how to reduce taxes, limit regulations on small businesses, and reinvigorate city centres among other things.

Addressing their 18-month low approval rating, he told Talk TV: “Polls go up and down. If you look every week at the council by-elections, we are consistently getting more votes than our national average.”

## Journalist who revealed £5M donation among three barred from Reform conference

Reform has barred at least three journalists from its conference in Birmingham today, all of whom have reported negatively of the party and its leader.

The Guardian’s City editor, Anna Isaac, who first revealed that Mr Farage accepted an undeclared £5m gift from Christopher Harborne, was among those not given accreditation.

Investigative reporter Peter Geoghegan, who authors newsletter Democracy for Sale, and broadcaster Michael Crick also had their applications declined.

Reform told The Guardian that Mr Geoghegan and Mr Crick were refused accreditation because the party viewed them as minor figures.

The party did not say why Ms Isaac was exluded.

Mr Geoghegan said his accreditation was denied five minutes after he published a story claiming Isabel Oaskeshott had privately called Reform mega donor Christopher Harborne “untrustworthy”.

British journalist Ms Oakeshott, whose partner is Reform MP Richard Tice, wrote on X: “More than 1000 journalists successfully applied for accreditation for @reformparty_uk party conference.

“Of three rejects, one broke the law; another has only one agenda; and the third is not a journalist. This is a non story.”

## Majority of Britons believe Reform is untrustworthy - poll

Around six in ten Britons believe Reform is an untrustworthy party, serving their own interests, prejudiced and extreme, a YouGov poll has found.

A survey ahead of the party’s conference today revealed that 56 per cent of people also saw the party as incompetent and caring only about a select few rather than caring about ordinary people.

When Britons were asked to describe Nigel Farage in their own words, some 71 per cent used at least one negative term, with the most frequent being accusations that the leader is a racist, used by 15 per cent of those surveyed, or a liar, used by 12 per cent.

Meanwhile, 27 per cent use at least one positive term to describe Mr Farage, with his perceived patriotism (5 per cent) and honesty (4 per cent) being the top compliments paid to him.

Some 19 per cent of Britons feel that he looks like a prime minister in waiting, down seven points since June.

## What's happening at Reform's conference today?

Reform’s three-day conference in Birmingham is kicking off at 11am on Thursday.

Tickets range from between £25 to £3000, with the most expensive access including a champagne breakfast with Nigel Farage.

At the main stage, the conference begins just after 11am with tributes to the late Ann Widdecombe.

Reform MP Lee Anderson will then speak at 11.30am, followed by the party’s home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf.

At 12.30pm, Jordan Bardella, the president of French National Rally, a far-right political party, will speak.

Matt Goodwin is up at 3pm, followed by Sarah Pochin at 3.15pm, Richard Tice at 3.30pm, before Nigel Farage addresses supporters at 3.50pm.

## Analysis: Beleaguered Reform are hoping for a big reset this week

*Political editor David Maddox writes:* 

After dominating British politics for most of the last two years, Reform UK arrive in Birmingham with a myriad of problems.

Nigel Farage’s party is no longer leading in the polls and in one case was even pushed into third place behind the Tories.

Their pitch to the country that they are the inevitable next government has already come apart.

Meanwhile, Mr Farage has looked tired and angry over pressure on donations he received which has led to an ongoing investigations by the standards commissioner.

Reform believe there is some sort of establishment plot against them and have even banned some journalists from attending the conference in Birmingham. But they are lacking in policies, have question marks over the depth of their talent and are now up against a new Labour leader and prime minister in Andy Burnham who has shown he can take them on.

The deputy leader Richard Tice has said that the aim this week is to get the country to “love Nigel Farage again.”

The bigger problem is that while Mr Farage is adored by some he is hated by many more and is arguably the most divisive figure in politics.

Added to that a new party on the right - Restore Britain led by former Reform MP Ruper Lowe - is eating away at Mr Farage’s core support.

## Support for Reform at 18-month low

Reform has fallen to their lowest performance in opinion polls in more than 18 months amid intense scrutiny over undeclared donations to its leader.

Nigel Farage’s party will now be looking to regain support at the party conference on Thursday after sliding into third place.

The new More in Common voting intention poll puts the party at 22 per cent, with Tories in second place at 23 per cent, and Labour in the lead on 26 per cent.

It is the party’s lowest approval rating since January 2025.

## Farage warned he could face stiffer penalty after claiming probe into £5m crypto donation is rigged

Nigel Farage has been warned that he could receive a “stiffer penalty” after he claimed that the result of an inquiry into his undeclared £5m donation from a crypto billionaire is rigged against him.

The leader of Reform UK has just returned to parliament after winning a by-election he called in his Clacton constituency in protest at the Commons’ standards inquiry against him over the donation from Christopher Harborne.

He suggested to the BBC that he would not get a fair hearing on whether he broke parliamentary rules for failing to declare the cash, because the MPs on the committee have already prejudged him as guilty.

He told the BBC: “Well, I mean, that investigation, the outcome. Have you seen who is on the committee? I think we know the outcome already.”

But former Labour deputy leader Baroness Harriet Harman, who chaired the Privileges Committee inquiry into the Boris Johnson Partygate scandal where the former prime minister received a stiffer penalty for his attacks on the process, warned Mr Farage could face the same fate, if he is found to have breached the rules.
