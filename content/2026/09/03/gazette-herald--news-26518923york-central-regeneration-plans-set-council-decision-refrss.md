PLANS for more than 1,000 homes, a hotel, offices, shops, a park and leisure spaces proposed as part of York’s biggest regeneration scheme are set for a decision.

Proposals for the York Central site, which also include a new entrance to York Station and a public square, are set to go before councillors on City of York Council on Thursday, September 10.

Applicants York Central Ltd, Network Rail and Homes England stated it would see one of the UK’s largest brownfield sites transformed into a thriving place to live, work and explore.

Council planning officers stated the plans would deliver a highly sustainable and well-connected mixed-use neighbourhood and recommended that they be approved.

The plans come as part of the wider York Central regeneration project which was first granted outline approval in 2019.

The £2 billion scheme, led by developers McLaren Property and Arlington Real Estate, covers 45 hectares and would see at least 2,500 homes built.

It would grow the city’s economy by a fifth, adding around £1.1 billion, and create around 6,500 jobs.

The expansion of the National Railway Museum with a new Central Hall is also part of the project.

The application set to go before the council’s Planning Committee on Thursday is the final part of the first phase of the project.

Earlier parts included infrastructure for the site and a six-storey Government Hub office building set to be completed by 2028.

  An impression of an aerial view of Coal Drops Square in York Central towards the Western Station Entrance 

*(Image: York Central Ltd)*

The latest application features a 213-bed three or four star hotel, with the operator currently unknown.

It would face onto the new Coal Drops Square public and be next to the new station entrance at the western end of York Station featuring a 300-space cycling hub.

A seven-storey building with shops on the ground floor and an Innovation Hub office block in the upper levels is also included.

The sixth floor would feature terraces with views looking towards York Minster and the city’s walls while the first and second floors would have space for small start-up firms.

Three plots would be set aside for residential development.

One would feature eight blocks with 144 homes provided in 19 townhouses and 125 apartments, along with four commercial units.

  An impression showing how homes in York Central could look 

*(Image: York Central Ltd)*

Another would be set aside for 508 build to rent homes in blocks with ground floor shop and leisure units.

Two further plots would feature a total of 362 homes available on the open market or for social rents, along with a convenience store on a 430sqm site.

Changes made since the plans were lodged include increasing the overall number of homes by 15 and reducing the number of three-bed ones on one plot.

A play area has been added to one housing block and changes to parking and walking, wheeling and cycling routes have also been made in parts of the site.

Six comments have been lodged on the plans, including one in support, three general responses and two objections.

Objections related to separate but related plans for the new Wilton Rise footbridge linking York Central to Holgate.

Another said the proposed hotel would be overbearing and severely restrict natural daylight in Coal Drops Square.

Council planning officers stated the plans contributed positively to the overall York Central development.

Officers said: “The proposals deliver a highly sustainable, well-connected and distinctive mixed-use neighbourhood that responds appropriately to its context, provides high-quality public realm and landscaping and supports a significant modal shift away from the private motor vehicle.

“The scheme successfully balances the delivery of housing, employment, transport infrastructure, public open space and associated community benefits, whilst preserving key views, respecting heritage assets and creating an attractive and inclusive environment.”
