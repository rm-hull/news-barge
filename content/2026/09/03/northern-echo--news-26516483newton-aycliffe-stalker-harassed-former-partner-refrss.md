A stalker who repeatedly harassed his former partner has avoided an immediate jail sentence.

Lee Winward struggled to accept the end of his relationship before launching a campaign of stalking, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The 37-year-old turned up uninvited at her home, slept in his car nearby and used her family to find out where she was and who she was with.

Prosecutor Nick Addlington said the couple had split several times, but Winward persuaded her to take him back by bombarding her with calls and messages.

He said Winward reacted aggressively when she spoke to other men and followed her when she went out with friends.

“They split up for the final time in June 2024, so the final part of the stalking was in the death-throes of the relationship,” he said.

“Following an argument, she asked him to leave but his car conveniently broke down outside her house and he slept in at night.”

Winward, of Hutton Place, Newton Aycliffe, pleaded guilty to stalking between May 19 and September 12, 2024.

Defence barrister Albany Kidd said her client pleaded guilty on the day his trial was due to start.

She said: “He struggled to come to terms with the finish of this relationship.

“On reflection, he does accept that stalking behaviour.”

She said Winward was 21 when the relationship began and the couple were together for more than a decade.

Recorder Andrew Sutcliffe KC sentenced Winward to nine months in prison, suspended for two years, and imposed a ten-year restraining order.

He said: “This is a serious offence of causing your former partner serious alarm or distress which has had a substantial adverse effect on her day-to-day activities.

“You should have known that your course of conduct would cause her alarm or distress.”

A charge of controlling and coercive behaviour was left on the court file.
