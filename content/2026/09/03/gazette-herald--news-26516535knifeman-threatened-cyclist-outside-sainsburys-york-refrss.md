A knifeman threatened to stab a cyclist next to a cash machine in broad daylight on a busy street close to York city centre, a court heard.

Billy Johnson, 29, had been released from jail less than three weeks earlier partway through a 20-month jail term for burgling a couple in their 70s at night and other offences.

Judge Simon Hickey said only the fact that the cyclist had his bicycle with him and was able to use it to protect himself prevented Johnson doing more than threaten the victim.

Amelia Taylor, prosecuting, said the cyclist was about to withdraw money from the cash machine at Sainsbury’s on Bootham close to Bootham Bar at 2.10pm on July 15.

Johnson approached, pulled out his knife and said: “If you don’t get the money out, I will stab you.”

The victim replied “You will have to stab me then” and put his bicycle between himself and Johnson.

CCTV showed that the street was busy at the time with cars and people passing by.

  Sainsburys on Bootham, central York. The cash machine is to the right. 

*(Image: Google Street View)*

The incident happened less than an hour after Johnson stole meat worth £17 from BP Jorvik Service Station in Lawrence Street.

He had been released partway through the 20-month prison sentence on June 25 and has been known to police for nearly 20 years, said Ms Taylor.

He has 44 previous convictions including two for violence.

Johnson, of no fixed address, pleaded guilty to threatening a person with a knife in public, his third conviction for having a knife or offensive weapon in public, and shop theft.

“If you pull a knife out in the middle of the city of York, people could see it and anything could happen,” Judge Simon Hickey told Johnson as he jailed him for 15 months.

Johnson has already been recalled to finish serving the burglary sentence and is now not expected to be released from prison until June next year.

July 15 was during one of this summer’s heatwaves, with daylong bright sunshine and temperatures close to or above 30C daily.

For Johnson, Neal Kutte said the cyclist was a stranger to the defendant.

“He (Johnson) would never have used the knife in anger, he would never have assaulted him (the victim) with the knife,” said the defence solicitor.

“What he did was effectively to frighten. He didn’t follow through the threat, he didn’t try to stab, he would never have done that.”
