When moving to a new area with your family, one of the most important things to look at is how good the schools are for your children and whether they are easy to enrol in.

Data from the Great British Schools Guide has revealed the hardest and easiest primary schools to get into across County Durham.

The hardest ranking has been decided by using “an equal-weighted combination of catchment tightness and fullness relative to recorded capacity”, with the easiest ranking using the same but from the opposite end.

Here are the primary schools around County Durham that are the easiest and hardest to get into.

  Do the lists surprise you at all? 

*(Image: Getty)*

## Hardest primary schools in County Durham to get into

Browney Primary Academy tops the list and is currently 146% full, according to the Great British Schools Guide.

The school was last inspected by Ofsted in November 2022 and received a rating of good.

The school is described as “thriving” and “filled with smiles” with a “unique” Woodland Village playground for children to explore.

The school not only focuses on preparing children for their SATs but also encourages creativity with activities involving building things, making up stories, and writing poems.

The children are able to get more hands-on by playing sports, growing plants, caring for animals, and carrying out experiments.

Top 10 hardest primary schools to get into:

1. Browney Primary Academy - 146%
2. Acre Rigg Infant School - 137%
3. Silver Tree Primary School and Nursery - 100%
4. Ribbon School - 107%
5. Hartside Primary Academy - 99%
6. St Stephen’s Church of England Primary School - 109%
7. Rosa Street Primary and Nursery Academy - 111%
8. Bullion Lane Primary School - 95%
9. St Anne’s Church of England Primary School - 96%
10. Byerley Park Primary School - 99%


## Easiest primary schools in County Durham to get into

Topping the list of the easiest schools to get into around County Durham is the Bishop Ian Ramsey CofE Primary School, which is currently 55% full.

The school was last inspected in March 2024 by OFSTED and came out with an overall rating of good and achieved a rating of outstanding in both the leadership and management and behaviour and attitudes categories.

The school is “at the heart of the community” and features a modern building, extensive grounds, and its own woodland area.

The school’s website reads: “Each child at Bishop Ian Ramsey Church of England Primary School is valued as an individual and their talents and strengths are recognised, celebrated and developed further.

“Learning opportunities are rich and exciting with children given every opportunity to be the best that they can be.”
