The sale of the site of a former York library has been approved, paving the way for new homes there.

A decision to sell the former Tang Hall Library site, in Fifth Avenue, for an undisclosed sum to build nine homes offering supported living has been approved by City of York Council.

Council officials stated the project aimed to provide homes for young adults to live as independently as possible as their parents or carers grow old and become less able to look after them.

The council is working with developer Kendal House Properties Ltd and registered provider YMCA North Tyneside on the scheme.

It comes after a planning application for the site was lodged in March after the council approved the release of £51,933 in Government funding to support the project last year.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/former-tang-hall-library-york-gsv.webp)

Plans would see eight self-contained flats and one for staff accommodation built on the site, along with communal areas and parking.

The former library closed after it was replaced by the Tang Hall Explore site in Mossdale Avenue in 2018.

The library, which was built in the 1960s, has since been demolished.

A report on the decision to sell the site stated the council had been working with a group of parents on the scheme since 2020.

The Cosy Home project aims to allow young adults to move out of the family home so they can live as independently as possible, according to the report.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/assisted-living-flats-tang-hall-library-site-fifth-avenue-york-pd.webp)

It stated: “The project has been developed over a number of years by families seeking a sustainable, long-term housing solution as parents and carers grow older and less able to care for their loved ones.

“In addition, the development will also deliver a small number of additional homes for other individuals in housing need, increasing the supply of specialist supported accommodation within the local authority area, mitigating the likelihood that those in need of supported housing would need to move outside of the York area.”
