A COUNCILLOR has announced his resignation after eight years.

Peter Carlill, a Labour member for Calverley and Farsley, said he was stepping down in a statement issued on social media.

Mr Carlill was elected in 2018 and served as the party’s executive member for transport and planning.

He said: “Although I have kept it private, for some time I have struggled with health issues, both physical and mental, which make the job more difficult for me to do justice.

“This is a fantastic job – one I have often described as the best job I have ever had – allowing me to make a positive difference to people’s lives and the area I love every day.

“Yet it can also be a challenging job, jumping non-stop from one thing to the next, being available around the clock, and being under continuous scrutiny – some positive, constructive and deserved, some otherwise.”

Mr Carlill said he had written to the council’s chief executive and Labour leader, James Lewis, to confirm he was stepping down.

During his time as a councillor Mr Carlill worked to improve public services as part of the West Yorkshire Combined Authority’s transport committee.

In a separate post, West Yorkshire mayor Tracy Brabin said: “Councillor Carlill will be much missed in his role at Leeds City Council.

“As deputy chair of our transport committee, he brought a real depth of knowledge, enthusiasm, and a cooperative spirit to his brief, helping us to bring buses back under public control and improve transport across West Yorkshire.

“I wish him all the best for the future and know he will continue to serve his part of the city well.”
