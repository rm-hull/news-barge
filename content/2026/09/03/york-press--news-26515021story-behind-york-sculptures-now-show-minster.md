## The story behind the sculptures across York now on show in Minster

The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. *(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

Over the summer, an icon of York Minster has come down from the walls and joined the rest of the city on the streets below.

From June 29 to August 31 there have been 12 sculptures replicating the character, humour, and mystery of the gargoyles on the Minster keeping watch across the city.

Senior events manager at Make It York, Lauren Frost, said: “This is the first time we have delivered a Watchers trail.

"We feel compared to previous sculpture trails, logistically this one was a lot smoother. We reduced the physical size for transportation.

  The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. 

*(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

"We feel that residents and visitors seek out trails in the city. It's a great way to explore York and have some fun along the way.”

Whilst each sculpture adheres to a set form, the variety of design is one of the most exciting elements of the trail according to Ms Frost.

  The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. 

*(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

She said: “All the sculptures are different, each representing a different guardianship of the city.

"The designs are so vastly different. It's hard to pick any particular one.

"That's part of the beauty of this trail. There's much variety and therefore appeal.”

And the beauty of York set a unique challenge for the organisers of the trail as they sought to find the right balance between placing the art and adhering to the style of the environment.

  The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. 

*(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

She continued: “There's a practical consideration on siting sculptures. They are in situ for a prolonged duration so public exposure and sculpture safety is key.

"We want sculptures to blend but not too much. They are supposed to be odd in their locations.

"We want people to ask 'why is that here?'

"In terms of where each sculpture goes, there's a nod from the sculpture design to the location.”

  The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. 

*(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

The artists behind this year’s array of designs are from across the UK.

The selection process is specific, however, opportunities are provided to younger artists as well as charitable groups.

This year's trail includes pieces by York and Scarborough Hospitals Charity and York Against Cancer, with the former’s sculpture being designed by users of the Leveson Centre, and led by resident artist Sue Clayton.

  The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. 

*(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

A spokesperson for Visit York said: "Through symbolic imagery and colour, the piece reflects the strength, creativity, and compassion shown by those affected by cancer and the people who support them.

“We run a national call out for artists to submit applications.

"There are typically offshoots of this for emerging artists and students to create opportunities for them to be involved.

"We look for quality designs to create a varied portfolio of artwork, designs that respond to the theme and deliver high quality, attractive artwork.”

  The sculptures have been on show in the streets of York during the summer and are now on display at York Minster in September. 

*(Image: Alex Dyett/Make It York)*

Whilst the artists hail from across the country, the production team behind the delivery and installation of the work are Yorkshire-based.

Norse Sky have been responsible for implementing this year's sculptures and have, according to Visit York, also worked with “York Dungeon, York's Chocolate Story and The Potions Cauldron to design and build unique and engaging creations".

While the 12 sculptures are no longer dotted around the city, they will now congregate together at the Minster where visitors can enjoy the unique experience of seeing the group together, in their original home amongst the beauty of the Minster from September 3–13.
