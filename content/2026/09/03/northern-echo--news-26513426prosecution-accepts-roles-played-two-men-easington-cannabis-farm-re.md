Two men caught tending a large, potentially lucrative cannabis farm, will be sentenced for their part in the operation, next week.

Tay Phan and Haong Le have admitted production of a class B drug arising from the police search of a vacant County Durham property in June.

Officers recovered a total of 814 plants, in different stages of growth, from the premises in Bede Street, Easington Colliery, on June 19.

*(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

The defendants, who were both present when police carried out the search, pleaded guilty at Durham Crown Court last month.

  Some of the cannabis plants recovered by police in raid on property in Easington Colliery, in June 

*(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

They have each submitted a basis of plea as to their respective role in the operation.

Both appeared back at the court via video link from Durham Prison, where they are detained on remand, assisted by a Vietnamese interpreter.

Paul Cleasby, prosecuting, said the respective basis of plea will not be contested by the Crown.

Judge Richard Clews told the pair: “The only sentence the court can consider in this case is one of imprisonment.

“How long that will be, will be decided on the day of sentence at the end of next week (September 10).”

In the meantime, both 41-year-old Phan and Haong Le, 36, will remain remanded in custody.
