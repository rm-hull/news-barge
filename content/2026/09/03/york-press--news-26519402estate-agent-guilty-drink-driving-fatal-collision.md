## Man who drove over teen and left him fatally injured was 'grossly irresponsible'

Bailey Chadwick died in a fatal crash on his way home from work late at night 

A MAN who drove over a teenager and left him fatally injured on a road in North Yorkshire is not responsible for his death, a court heard.

But Joseph Henry Stott's decision to drive after hours of drinking was "grossly irresponsible", district judge Mark Daley said.

He heard how the 25-year-old estate agent and his partner had spent the evening drinking mostly wine, but also shots and beer in two pubs before driving back to his North Yorkshire home.

Curtis Dunkley, prosecuting, said Stott's route took him along the B6265 near Pateley Bridge where Bailey Chadwick, 19, was lying in the road.

Stott drove over him in his Ford B-max at 2.57am on July 20, 2025, and kept driving.

The defence disputed how much Stott had drunk and how it had affected him.

Defence barrister Phil Morris said: “It has always been his (Stott’s) case he had no idea he had passed over the body of Bailey. He had no knowledge subsequent to the accident the next day.”

District judge Mark Daley said: “How can someone run over an adult human and not realise it if you are not in an unfit state. That is troubling me.”

Banning him from the roads for 20 months and giving him a community order, the district judge said Stott had known he had been drinking.

"It was a grossly irresponsible decision to drive at all," he said. "The consequences are plain to see."

Stott, formerly of Dairy Lane, Darley, Harrogate, and now living at an undisclosed address in West Yorkshire, changed his plea to guilty to driving while unfit to drive through drink on the day he was due to stand trial at Harrogate Magistrates' Court.

He had previously admitted driving without insurance. The prosecution offered no evidence on charges of failure to stop after a collision and failure to report a collision.

He was given a 12-month community order with 80 days' alcohol abstinence enforced by an ankle tag, 10 days' rehabilitative activities and 135 hours' unpaid work. He must pay £400 prosecution costs and a £114 statutory surcharge.

Mr Dunkley said a member of the public who found Bailey's body between Glasshouses and Pateley Bridge at 3.10am alerted police. Bailey had been walking home to Pateley Bridge after work at a local pub.

A police collision expert later concluded that “even a careful and competent driver would have hit Bailey,” said Mr Dunkley. “In fact the collision was unavoidable.”

Mr Morris said that because it was dark, Bailey was on the ground and wearing dark clothing, he would only have been visible in the headlights for 55 metres or two seconds at 60mph, the national speed limit for the road, and Stott would not have had time to react to it.

Stott was himself a parent. “There is recognition as a parent to the anguish caused to this family and his wider friends at the passing of Bailey,” said Mr Morris.

The prosecution and defence agreed there was no explanation as to why Bailey was lying prone on the road in the early hours of July 20, 2025.

The prosecution showed video of Stott in the Box Bar and another pub in Headingley, Leeds, buying drinks in pairs for himself and his girlfriend.

It also showed CCTV of him pouring his partner’s drink into his own glass and drinking it, and footage from a mobile phone and from an all-night garage which Mr Dunkley said demonstrated he was slurring his words and unsteady on his feet.

The defence accepted Stott was slurring his words but disputed he was unsteady on his feet.

Mr Morris said Stott had not intended to drive home but had done so after an argument. He had driven for more than 30 minutes without incident before the collision.
