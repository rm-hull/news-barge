A cycling club has responded to criticism after the cancellation of a community event.

Bradford Council called off Wyke Cinema Day due to a scheduling conflict with a national cycling event hosted by East Bradford Cycling Club.

The National Youth Circuit series was held at Wyke Community Sports Village on August 22.

### 'I think the cycling club has been vilified'

Mandy Parker, head coach and development officer at the club, said the group has faced a wave of negativity online since the cancellation was announced.

Ms Parker said: "I feel really sorry for the organisers of Wyke Cinema Day as it is a great event, and I understand why people are upset.

"However, when they started posting videos online, that’s when we started getting all these comments about the club.

"I think we have been vilified and it’s so unfair that people who live in this community are being treated like this.

"It’s upsetting for us as a local club.

"We are very much embedded in the local community of Wyke."

She noted that the event, organised by British Cycling, is bound by the national calendar and cannot be rescheduled.

### Emergency exit would have been blocked

In an open letter to Bradford Council, Wyke Cinema Day organiser Jodie Cottrell questioned why both events could not have taken place on the same day, but Ms Parker said this was not possible.

She said the main issue was health and safety and explained that the large truck and screen used for the cinema day would have blocked essential emergency access to the cycling circuit.

Ms Parker said: "Lots of people are saying both events could have happened together, but it’s about the health and safety of the attendees.

"It wouldn’t have been safe.

"The cinema day has a massive truck and massive screen, so it blocks the emergency exit.

"There is no way that they could have had both events."

### Parking charge concerns addressed

The open letter also raised concerns about car parking charges.

Ms Parker clarified that the money collected from parking goes directly to the club and helps fund new equipment such as bikes.

She said: "The club is run entirely by volunteers.

"Nobody is paid."

### Event drew in hundreds of people

Despite the controversy, Ms Parker noted that the cycling event drew between 600 and 700 people, with the largest race featuring 62 riders.

She said: "The event is praised by British Cycling so we have a good reputation.

"It’s designed to give our kids that are really going places and will hopefully become professional cyclists the opportunity to race at their home circuit.

"At the end of the day, the track was built for cycling and football.

"It’s a sports centre for community use."
