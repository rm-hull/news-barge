MORE than 60 free events will be held across the Bradford district during this year’s Heritage Open Days.

The annual festival will run from September 11 to September 20, bringing museums, archives, churches, artists, volunteers, community groups and heritage organisations together.

For the first time, a single district-wide programme has been created.

## Events across the district

It brings events from Bradford, Keighley, Shipley, Bingley, Ilkley, Thornton, Baildon, Burley in Wharfedale, Wyke and surrounding communities together in one guide.

Organisers hope the new approach will make events easier to find and encourage people to explore heritage beyond their own area.

The programme has been supported by Arts Council England funding awarded to Bradford Heritage Connection CIC.

It follows the second Bradford Heritage Festival, which attracted thousands of visitors interested in the people, places and stories that have shaped the district.

## What is going on?

Activities will include guided walks, archive sessions, exhibitions, performances and visits to historic buildings that are not usually open to the public.

Walks will explore Bradford’s suffragette history, literary connections and the story of Bradford Beck.

Visitors will also be able to visit historic churches, houses and community landmarks.

Exhibitions and archive events will cover Bradford’s textile heritage, colour science and local community histories. Family activities will be held in parks, museums and community venues.

All events will be free, which organisers say is central to the festival’s aim of making heritage accessible to everyone.

## What the organisers have said

Executive director Vicky Clifton said: "This brilliant shared Heritage Open Days programme is a real milestone for Bradford.

"It reflects the trust, collaboration and ambition of partners across the district, all working together to make heritage more visible, more accessible and more connected."

She said: "With support from Arts Council England, Bradford Heritage Connection has been able to coordinate over 60 free events that showcase the depth and breadth of our stories."

The programme is expected to attract residents and visitors from across the region.

It will provide opportunities to discover well-known landmarks and lesser-known stories from communities across the district.

Ms Clifton said: "After the success of the Bradford Heritage Festival, this programme shows what's possible when Bradford works as one.

"And as we move beyond our City of Culture year, it's a powerful reminder that the legacy lives on in the partnerships, pride and shared purpose shaping Bradford's future."

The full programme of events and booking information is available through the Bradford Heritage Connection website.
