Maternity services at York and Scarborough hospitals have been rated ‘good’ by the Care Quality Commission (CQC) following inspections which found significant improvements in the care provided to women and babies.

It’s a major step forward after the service was previously ranked as “inadequate“.

The CQC inspected maternity services at York Hospital on 14 and 15 April and at Scarborough Hospital on 16 and 17 April 2026 to assess progress since its previous inspections.

Both maternity services are now rated good overall, with all five areas assessed by the CQC – safe, effective, caring, responsive and well-led – rated as good.

The trust’s maternity teams support around 4,000 births each year across the two hospitals.

Inspectors praised the kindness and dedication of staff and found strong evidence of teamwork, learning and collaboration. Women consistently told inspectors that staff were kind, supportive and listened to their concerns.

The reports also highlight improvements in incident management, safer and more consistent triage, fire safety arrangements, maternity equipment, baby tagging and staffing arrangements, alongside stronger governance and oversight.

The CQC also praised the trust’s personalised maternity care plan, developed with the Maternity and Neonatal Voices Partnership to ensure women and families are at the heart of their care.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/york-hospital-march-2022.webp)

**Director of Midwifery at York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, Sascha Wells-Munro**, said: “This is a proud moment for our maternity services and, most importantly, for the women and families we care for across York and Scarborough, and the multi-professional teams who work together every day.

“Our colleagues have been on a significant journey. They have listened, learned and worked incredibly hard to make the changes that were needed, while continuing to care for and support thousands of women, babies and families.

“I have seen first-hand the determination, compassion and commitment they have shown throughout.

“That’s why it means so much to see inspectors recognise not only the improvements we have made, but the culture within our services – the teamwork, the learning and, above all, the kindness women experience from our colleagues.

“Behind this rating are hundreds of people who have supported one another and remained absolutely focused on providing safe, high-quality and compassionate care.”

**Chief Nurse at York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, Joe Hague,** added: “Moving from inadequate to good across both of our maternity services is a significant achievement and reflects the considerable work that has taken place across the Trust.

“Since joining the Trust, I have seen first-hand the commitment of our maternity colleagues and the strength of the leadership and teamwork behind these improvements.

“The findings from the CQC, alongside the positive experiences shared by women using our services, are a real testament to their hard work and determination.

“This rating provides important independent recognition of the progress we have made in the quality and safety of care. It’s an important milestone, but our responsibility now is to sustain these improvements and continue getting better.

“We will take confidence from this report while remaining focused on providing consistently safe, high-quality and compassionate maternity care for every woman, baby and family who relies on us.”

The report will be published on CQC’s website in the coming days.
