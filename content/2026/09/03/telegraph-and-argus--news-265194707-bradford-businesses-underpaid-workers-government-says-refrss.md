SEVEN Bradford businesses have been named and shamed by the Government for failing to pay the National Minimum Wage.

In total, the firms owed 90 employees more than £10,000.

The employers attribute the breaches to issues including staff uniform policies, accommodation arrangements and payroll calculation errors.

## Government named and shamed

The firms were included in the latest Department for Business and Trade naming round, which identified nearly 660 employers across the UK that underpaid workers.

According to the Government, around £4 million has since been repaid to more than 27,000 workers, while employers were issued with a total of £7 million in penalties.

Among those named were seven Bradford district businesses, with underpayments ranging from £747.86 to £2,500.80.

## 7 Bradford businesses named

The Bradford employers listed were:

- City Glass Works Limited, BD4, which failed to pay £2,500.80 to one worker.
- IBI Healthcare Ltd, trading as Lister Pharmacy, BD8, which failed to pay £1,971.59 to one worker.
- Crown Foods (Bradford) Ltd, BD8, which failed to pay £1,495.82 to 17 workers.
- Creative Child Ltd, trading as Bamboo Kids, BD4, which failed to pay £1,377.26 to 18 workers.
- Megaplay (Bradford) Ltd, BD8, which failed to pay £1,218.66 to 14 workers.
- Fashion Wize Ltd, trading as Lister Schoolwear, BD9, which failed to pay £1,003.55 to 28 workers.
- PB2 Ltd, BD7, which failed to pay £747.86 to 11 workers.

Lister Pharmacy declined to comment when contacted by the Telegraph & Argus.

PB2 Ltd and Fashion Wize Ltd have not responded at the time of publication.

## Uniform policies behind some underpayments

Two Bradford employers said the findings related to the way staff uniform requirements were treated under minimum wage regulations.

A spokesperson for Creative Child Ltd said the issue related to a previous policy requiring staff to wear black trousers.

A spokesperson said: "We genuinely didn't realise that specifying the colour of the workwear affected the wage calculations, but they said we should have been making sure that employees having to buy black trousers didn't take us under the National Minimum Wage."

The spokesperson also said all affected staff were repaid in full, additional payments were made, and the company's uniform policy was revised.

Megaplay (Bradford) Ltd gave a similar explanation, stating: "The underpayment identified by HMRC arose from the way our staff uniform requirements were treated when calculating National Minimum Wage.

"At the time, we were not aware that the costs associated with our uniform requirements could have an impact on National Minimum Wage calculations."

The company said the issue was a "genuine misunderstanding of the regulations" and that all affected employees were repaid immediately after it was brought to its attention.

## Accommodation arrangement led to breach

Speaking to the T&A, City Glass Works Ltd said its case involved a worker renting accommodation provided by the business.

A company spokesperson said the employee had been paid at the applicable wage rate, but National Minimum Wage accommodation offset rules created a calculated shortfall.

They said: "This was not a deliberate attempt to underpay the employee, and the issue arose from our misunderstanding of how the separate rental arrangement affected the minimum wage calculation."

The company said the worker had been repaid in full and that additional compliance checks had since been introduced.

## 
  **Crown Foods points to payroll error**

A spokesperson for Crown Foods (Bradford) Ltd said the underpayment arose from an error in calculations rather than a deliberate decision to underpay staff.

The company said the money was repaid shortly after HMRC raised the issue.

The spokesperson said: "As soon as we were alerted, we made the payment.

"It wasn't deliberate. It was a mistake in working things out.

"We reviewed the system and made changes to prevent it happening again."

## Government: Employers who underpay staff will be held accountable

Announcing the naming round, Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds said: "Short-changing your staff isn't a shortcut to success and we are determined to stamp it out."

Future of Work Minister Kate Dearden added: "Underpaying your staff is illegal, and we will not let workers foot the bill for their boss failing to follow the rules."

The naming round is the first since the launch of the Fair Work Agency, which will oversee enforcement of workers' rights.

The Government said employers should review their payroll arrangements and ensure workers receive every penny they are legally entitled to receive.
