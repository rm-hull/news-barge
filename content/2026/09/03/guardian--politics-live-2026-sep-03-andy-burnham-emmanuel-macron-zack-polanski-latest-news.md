Badenoch condemns Reform UK's 'authoritarian' press bans, saying Farage shouldn't be MP if he can't handle scrutiny

Q: [From Emilio Casalicchio from Politico] Reform UK have banned seven reporters from their conference, including the one who revealed Nigel Farage got a £5m donation he did not declare. What do you think of that in terms of free speech? And would you do something like that?

Badenoch replied:

Absolutely not … Conservatives believe in free speech, where people are welcome to criticise us as much as they like.

If you look at Nigel Farage’s attacks on the press, some of them very personal … you look at the things that Zia Yusuf says about journalists constantly, you know what kind of government they would be – authoritarian and using state power to pursue opponents. [That is] what they’ve effectively been threatening.

We do not do that. It’s absolutely not something that we believe in. We believe in free speech. We believe in a free press. And yes, of course, that might be painful for us as politicians, but public scrutiny is part of the gig.

If Nigel Farage can’t handle difficult questions about his dodgy £5m, then he shouldn’t be a politician.

But that comes with, working as an MP and standing to be leader of the country.

Badenoch has not always been comfortable with press scrutiny herself. As a minister in the last government, she was criticised for her aggressive response to journalists reporting on her. But as Conservative leader she has been more tolerant of criticism, and while the Tories don’t exactly welcome negative press coverage, they are more pro-media than Reform UK.

Badenoch claims Tories don't give peerages to people based on donations

Q: Nigel Farage said in a BBC interview yesterday that he would not give peerages to people donating to Reform UK. Is that a policy that you will follow?

Badenoch replied:

We don’t give people peerages based on donations, or government contracts. I am someone who believes in meritocracy.

We give people peerages because we think they’re going to do a good job.

You look at the peerages that I have given: Gen Sir Patrick Sanders, the former head of the British Army, he has joined our benches. We have people that are serious and that are credible joining conservative benches.

Who did Keir Starmer put in? That Matthew Doyle guy and another person whose credentials turned out to be out to be completely fake.

So I’m not worried about the people who I’m putting in the House of Lords.

As Badenoch delivered this answer, some journalists reacted with astonishment. While Badenoch has tended to favour her ideological allies when nominating people for the Lords, there is a very long history of major donors to the Conservative party getting peerages. Labour and the Liberal Democrats have also given plenty of peerages in the past to major donors.

Badenoch says UK must prepare for war if it wants to ensure peace

Back to what was said at Kemi Badenoch’s press conference.

Q: [From Jonathan Walker from the Express] Are you ready to lead the country into war if necessary?

Badenoch replied:

If you want a peace, you have to prepare for war. And I believe that strong countries don’t get attacked. It’s countries that look weak that get attacked.

And I look at what Poland is doing. Why are they increasing their army to 300,000? Why are they withdrawing from all these treaties that would stop them from fighting a war?

I was in Rome last week speaking to Giorgia Meloni about defence. I spoke to Prime Minister Mitsotakis in Greece about defence. Everybody is worried.

So why are we pretending that nothing’s happening? That’s what should concern us, that we have a government that’s talking about things that are not to do with national security, just their pet projects and hobby horses.

Yes, we all want cheaper bus fares, but if anything happens to a Nato country, so much damage will be done we’re not going to be getting any growth.

Q: [From Christopher Hope from GB News] The Conservatives failed to cut welfare when they were in power. Why should GB News viewers believe that you will do it now.

Badenoch say Hope is wrong. The Tories did cut welfare; that is what happened when Iain Duncan Smith introduced universal credit, she says.

Welfare is a safety net. It is not a lifestyle. Our welfare bill is running out of control. So I want GB news viewers to be very reassured. We have done this before and we can do it again.

Q: [From the BBC’s Damian Grammaticas]The target of raising defence spending to 3% of GDP was announced by Liz Truss. But it was dropped by Rishi Sunak. You did not object. And you were also in government when welfare spending went up. So isn’t it the case you have no credibility on this?

Badenoch says the last government had to deal with Covid. And she says she is leader now. She is talking about what she would do now. She has not got a time machine, and can’t ask people about what happened in the past.

At the Tory event Kemi Badenoch has finished her speech, in which she set out her plan to raise defence spending by £10bn a year. She is now taking questions.

Q: Why did you not do this when you were in government?

Badenoch replied:

When I was in government, defence spending went up when I joined the cabinet in 2022.

But we have to be honest, defence spending went down every year from 1989 until 2022. We were spending the peace dividend from the Cold War era. That era is now over.

Journalist banned from Reform UK's conference accuses Farage of going back on free speech pledge to Congress

Helena Horton

Helena Horton is a Guardian reporter.

The investigative journalist Sam Bright, who has contributed to the Guardian and focuses his reporting on climate denial and the hard-right, has also been banned from Reform conference.

Bright, who writes for Desmog as well as his own newsletter, was informed he was banned from the conference shortly after reporting that fossil fuel lobbyists and anti-abortion campaigners would be hosting events at the conference, which kicked off today.

Nigel Farage insisted in Congress last year, during a session about free speech, that he never bans journalists from his events. His appearance in front of US lawmakers was orchestrated by an anti-abortion group, the Alliance Defending Freedom.

It’s therefore deeply ironic, on many levels, that Reform banned me from attending its conference just hours after I reported that three anti-abortion groups are due to speak at this year’s event. Farage should practice what he preaches on free speech and stop running away from scrutiny.

Other journalists who have been banned from the Reform UK conference include the Guardian’s City editor, Anna Isaac, the investigative reporter Peter Geoghegan and the broadcaster and biographer Michael Crick (who published a book about Nigel Farage praised for its fairness).

Kemi Badenoch is about to give her speech on defence spending. These are from Peter Walker, who is at the venue.

I’m in the magnificent if slightly bonkers Scotland House London to hear Kemi Badenoch set out how she would pay for more defence spending - with a lot of the money coming from cuts to housing benefit. You’d think that might bring some knock-on cost repercussions, but let’s see what she says.
