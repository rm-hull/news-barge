Britain's largest rare and antiquarian book fair is set to return.

The York National Book Fair will take place over two days, returning to the city on Friday, September 11, and Saturday, September 12.

The fair will showcase books, maps, ephemera, and prints, attracting visitors and sellers from the UK and abroad.

The York National Book Fair is known for offering a variety of items, from rare and second-hand books to maps, prints, and photographs.

Some items will be priced at just a few pounds, while others will cost tens of thousands.

There will be more than 100,000 books for sale, according to the fair organisers.

For book lovers, collectors, and the curious, the fair offers an opportunity to browse, enjoy, and even purchase a range of rarities.

For collectors, the fair is a chance to meet new dealers and expand their collections.

This year, the fair will see some newcomers.

Roland Belgrave, a specialist photography dealer based in London, and M J Books from California, specialising in unique and beautiful items relating to naturalist pursuits and environmental concerns, will exhibit in York for the first time.

Some highlights can be previewed on the York Book Fair website.

There, potential visitors can get a taste of what will be on offer, from miniature to magnificent volumes, early editions to first editions.

Among the items are two relating to George Orwell’s classic book 1984.

Grimes Hill Books has a first edition in a red dust wrapper, and Ashton Rare Books has one of the limited-edition prospectuses for a modern version of the same book produced in 2024 by the printer and publisher James Freemantle.

The fair will also have items of local interest.

York-based Ken Spelman will offer a ticket for the Sex Pistols’ last ever concert, which was held in Yorkshire.

The pink ticket, with a handwritten number on the back, is expected to attract interest from counter-culture collectors.

Topical items will also be on sale, such as copies of Homer’s translations, following the recent release of the film of The Odyssey.

Rooke Books will bring a version of T.E. Lawrence's translation, bound in full black leather by W. H. Smith & Son Bindery.

The fair will be held at the York Racecourse, which is easy to get to, with free parking and a free shuttle bus running from the Memorial Gardens on Railway Street every 30 minutes.

Entry to the event costs £2 on the door, and tickets can be used throughout the weekend.

Advance free tickets can be downloaded from yorkbookfair.com.

The York National Book Fair is organised by the Provincial Booksellers Fairs Association.
