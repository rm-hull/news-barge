A notorious thief pointed a knife at a young man at a cash machine in a busy York street and told him: “If you don’t get the money, I’ll stab you.”

Billy Johnson, 29, who was wearing a hoodie, brandished the knife in broad daylight on Bootham as pedestrians and vehicles passed by, York Crown Court heard.

Johnson is the same man who left an elderly York couple “quaking in their bed” following a burglary at their home in October last year.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/sainsburys-local-bootham-york-2024-gsv.webp)

Prosecutor Amelia Taylor said the knife incident occurred at about 2.15pm on 15 July when the victim was about to withdraw cash from a machine outside the Sainsbury’s store in plain sight of passers-by.

Johnson held out the knife and told the victim: “If you don’t get money out, I’ll stab you.”

The victim replied: “You’ll have to stab me then.”

He then used his bicycle to put distance between himself and Johnson. At this point, a security guard came out of the store after hearing a commotion outside, whereupon Johnson scurried away towards High Petergate.

“Despite a police area search, he was not located,” said Ms Taylor.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/bp-lawrence-street-york-2024-gsv.webp)

About an hour earlier, Johnson had walked into the Marks & Spencer shop at the BP Jorvik fuel station in Lawrence Street and evaded the security officer – because he was dealing with a robbery by another individual.

As the guard was distracted by the other incident, Johnson walked out of the store with two packs of steak concealed inside his jacket. He was later identified as the shop thief from in-store video footage.

Ms Taylor said that CCTV footage of the knife incident showed people walking past as it unfolded in the middle of the afternoon.

A police constable who reviewed the footage was able to identify Johnson because he had known him “for almost 20 years” due to the York man’s extensive criminal past.

He was arrested in early August, brought in for questioning but refused to answer police questions.

The knife was never found, but Johnson was charged with threatening a person with an offensive weapon in a public place, as well as the shop theft.

Johnson, of no fixed address, ultimately admitted both offences and appeared for sentence via video link today after being remanded in custody.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/york-crown-court-sign.webp)

Ms Taylor said that Johnson had 44 previous offences on his record including numerous thefts, fraud and violence including assaulting an emergency worker.

Last year he received a relatively short prison sentence for carrying an offensive weapon, namely a baseball bat.

In December 2025, he was jailed for 20 months for burgling a shed at St Peter’s School in Clifton and breaking into the homes of an elderly couple in York, leaving them “quaking in their beds”.

Johnson and another named man broke into the couple’s house at about 3am on 12 October 12 and searched the downstairs part of the property before taking their car.

He was released partway through that sentence under early-release legislation and went on to commit the knife offence and the shop theft, which was in breach of his prison licence, resulting in him being recalled to prison to serve the remainder of that sentence until June next year.

His solicitor Neil Cutte said that Johnson had insisted that he would “never have used the knife in anger” nor would he have stabbed the victim and just wanted to “frighten” him.

Judge Simon Hickey told Johnson: “You were holding the knife in clear view, in broad daylight, here in our city. There are motor cars passing by; people passing by, and there is you confronting (the victim) in a hoodie, on Bootham, a busy street.”

Johnson was jailed for 15 months for threatening with a knife but was told it may not affect his due release date of June next year for his existing sentence.
