A BRADFORD MP has described plans that could see around 80 jobs cut at Morrisons' Bradford headquarters as "concerning".

The comments come after the supermarket chain confirmed the roles had been placed at risk of redundancy as part of proposals to streamline the company's central operations.

Affected colleagues have been informed and a consultation process is now underway.

Responding to the announcement, Bradford East MP Imran Hussain (Labour) said: "It is concerning that 80 jobs at Morrisons' Bradford headquarters have been placed at risk."

## MP urges meaningful consultation

Mr Hussain said he had recently met with Morrisons and stressed the importance of safeguarding jobs in the city.

He said: "I recently met with Morrisons and emphasised the importance of protecting jobs in Bradford and the company's long-term commitment to the city and its workforce.

"There must now be a full and meaningful consultation with affected staff and unions, with every effort made to protect jobs."

The MP also called on Morrisons to support staff whose positions may ultimately be lost as a result of the restructuring.

He added: "Where roles cannot be retained, I would urge Morrisons to offer suitable alternative employment wherever possible, on the same pay and conditions."

## 
  **Morrisons says changes affect head office only**

The supermarket giant said the proposals affect roles across its central functions but do not impact store staff or other operational teams.

A Morrisons spokesperson said: “While we are making good progress with our plan to renew and modernise Morrisons - growing sales and our market share and improving the shopping experience - market conditions remain challenging and we are focusing hard on delivering the best value for customers.

They added: “In order to maintain our momentum, we’re continuing to review how we operate and have identified some opportunities to streamline our central structures - to reduce complexity, help us work faster and more effectively and empower colleagues.

“As a result, we’re proposing some changes across our head office functions to ensure they are better placed to serve our stores and improve service for our customers.

“Consequently, the proposed changes will result in a number of colleague roles being placed at risk of redundancy.

"We will be offering these colleagues our full support, including helping them to find alternative roles elsewhere in the business wherever we can.

“A consultation process has now commenced.”

## Turnaround plan continues amid fierce competition

Last month, it was revealed Morrisons cut almost 5,000 jobs last year as it sought to improve its finances amid a major turnaround programme.

Newly published accounts show the retailer's average monthly workforce fell from 101,144 employees to 96,232 during the year to October 2025, a reduction of 4,912 roles.

The cuts affected staff across the business, including more than 4,200 store positions, alongside jobs in food manufacturing and distribution.

Morrisons said it remains focused on improving the shopping experience for customers while delivering better value and increasing efficiency across the business.

Despite the challenges, the company reported revenues of £15.7 billion in its most recent accounts, an increase of 2.8 per cent on the previous year.

## What Morrisons is doing?

Morrisons recently promised customers it will not be beaten on price by its biggest supermarket rivals.

The pledge covers more than 500 essential items and forms part of the retailer’s latest strategy to win back shoppers in the highly competitive UK grocery market.

The supermarket chain, which operates around 500 stores and 1,700 convenience outlets, has seen its market share decline in recent years.

In the 12 weeks to August, Morrisons held an 8.5% market share, according to Worldpanel, placing it behind Tesco, Sainsbury’s, Asda, Aldi, and Lidl.

Morrisons has since launched its new “unbeatable prices” promise, ensuring that customers will not pay more for selected items than they would at any of its five main competitors.

Alex Paver, customer and marketing director at Morrisons, said: "Customers shouldn’t have to choose between great prices and great quality – and at Morrisons, they don’t have to.

## 
  **'Morrisons has deep roots in Bradford'**

For now, the consultation process remains ongoing, with Morrisons saying it will work with affected colleagues and seek alternative opportunities within the business wherever possible.

Mr Hussain also said he would continue engaging with the company as the consultation progresses.

He said: "Morrisons has deep roots in Bradford and is one of our major employers.

"I will continue to liaise closely with the company throughout this process and press them to protect as many Bradford jobs as possible."
