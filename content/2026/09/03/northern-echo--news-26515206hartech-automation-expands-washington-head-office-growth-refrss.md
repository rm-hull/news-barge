AN engineering firm has expanded its North East head office after securing new contracts and entering global markets.

Hartech Automation, based at Washington Business Centre, has increased its office space to support its growth in sectors including renewable energy, oil and gas, and defence.

Mark Hardy, managing director of Hartech Automation, said: "The company has grown significantly over recent years, and this expansion will be key to ensuring that continues.

"It will provide us with the space required to continue bringing in new staff, which is something we’re keen to press on with this year, and will provide us with professional conferencing rooms for meeting with our clients and teams.

"It is a key part of our growth plans."

The company delivers bespoke engineering solutions to manufacturers worldwide and has expanded its team and contract portfolio over the past year.

While traditionally serving markets such as automotive, food and drink, and pharmaceuticals, Hartech is now targeting a broader range of industries.

Mr Hardy said: "The rapid pace at which we’re seeing technology advance means businesses can ill-afford to stand still and this is certainly the case when it comes to automation."

Hartech Automation specialises in the supply, implementation, and management of control systems and automation solutions.

The company also modernises existing machinery, helping businesses update or upgrade old equipment.

The business has operated from Washington Business Centre—which is managed by the North East BIC on behalf of Sunderland City Council—for seven years.

It began in a 515sq ft office and has now expanded by a further 863sq ft, bringing its total space to 2,403sq ft.

Mr Hardy said: "We moved to the centre six or seven years ago and this is the third time we’ve expanded our presence here.

"It’s been a great home to us."

The decision to increase its footprint was influenced by the site's proximity to key manufacturing hubs and transport links.

Hartech Automation has received support from Sunderland City Council’s business investment team since its launch.

Councillor James Jackson, cabinet member for business, housing and regeneration at Sunderland City Council, said: "Sunderland has a rich history when it comes to driving industrial innovation, so it is fantastic to see businesses such as Hartech Automation continuing to invest in the city and fly the flag for the region across the world.

"The additional space will support the creation of yet more high skilled jobs for local people while providing the company with the capacity required to continue winning new orders and competing on the global stage."
