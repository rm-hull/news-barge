Questions have been raised over how a new Bradford retailer can advertise 'Premium Snus & Vapes' despite snus being banned in the UK for more than three decades.

The issue emerged after signage appeared outside NIC Central Premium Snus & Vapes, a new business at 151 New Line, Greengates, prompting criticism from local councillors and concern among some residents.

However, West Yorkshire Trading Standards has now explained that the answer may lie in the growing use of the word "snus" to describe products that are legal to sell.

## Two complaints received

Trading Standards confirmed it has received two complaints or enquiries relating to the premises.

The authority said the concerns had been recorded for intelligence purposes but stopped short of suggesting the shop was breaking any rules.

The response comes after councillors questioned whether the branding was promoting a product that cannot legally be sold in the UK.

## The key distinction between snus and nicotine pouches

While tobacco snus remains prohibited, Trading Standards said the term "snus" is now often used by consumers when referring to nicotine pouches.

Unlike traditional snus, nicotine pouches do not contain tobacco and can legally be sold in the UK.

Officials pointed to photographs published by the Telegraph & Argus showing branding for Snü, a company known for producing tobacco-free nicotine pouches.

As a result, Trading Standards said the use of the term on the shop frontage does not automatically indicate that illegal products are being advertised or sold.

Perhaps most significantly, the authority said there are currently no restrictions governing the use of the name in the circumstances described.

## Why has the shop attracted attention?

The controversy began after councillors criticised the arrival of the business in a newly refurbished former bank building.

Local representatives said residents had hoped the development would bring a community-focused café rather than another vape retailer.

Concerns were also raised because the shop branding prominently features the word "snus", a term commonly associated with the banned oral tobacco product.

The UK Government has repeatedly stated that tobacco snus remains illegal to sell and that it has no plans to reverse the ban.

## Advertising rules set to change

Although Trading Standards did not identify any current issue with the shop name, the authority highlighted upcoming changes under the Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026.

The legislation includes a ban on the advertising of tobacco products, vaping products and nicotine products, with those measures expected to come into force from June 1, 2027.

Those changes could significantly alter how nicotine-related products are promoted in future.

## No visits carried out

Trading Standards confirmed that no compliance visits have been conducted at the premises.

The authority declined to comment on any future enforcement activity or operational planning.

However, it said it has a range of powers available where retailers are found to be selling prohibited products, including inspections, seizures of goods, legal action and prosecutions.

For now, the authority's position appears clear: while tobacco snus remains banned, the use of the word "snus" in shop branding does not necessarily mean a business is selling illegal products, particularly where it may be referring to legal tobacco-free nicotine pouches instead.

## FULL statement from West Yorkshire Trading Standards

Although Trading Standards sent through answers to the questions that the T&A asked, this is a full quote of all of their completed answers.

A spokesperson said: "We have received 2 complaints/enquiries in relation to this premises to date, recorded for intelligence purposes."

"Tobacco snus has been banned for sale in the UK for some time. However people are known to often refer to nicotine pouches as ‘snus’ also, even though they do not contain tobacco and can be legally sold in the UK. Images on the recent news article in the Telegraph & Argus which displays an image on the shop sign for the brand Snü in addition to other vaping brands. Snü are known to produce nicotine pouches which are tobacco free. There are no restrictions governing the name as described."

"The Tobacco and Vape Act 2026 has introduced a ban on the advertising of tobacco products, vaping products and nicotine products which is expected to come into effect from 1 June 2027."

"No visits have been conducted. Unfortunately we cannot disclose details of any operational planning.

"The legislation governing tobacco and vapes, as with other consumer protection legislation, provides trading standards with a range of powers relating to the illegal sale of goods including inspection, seizure, legal proceedings including prosecution, forfeiture of illegal goods etc. We are not able to provide a statement other than the points above."
