## 'She’s the most amazing person' - tribute to North Yorkshire woman who died at 26

Becca and Bonnie *(Image: Provided)*

THE family of a North Yorkshire woman who passed away in her 20s has spoken about their ‘amazing’ daughter ahead of the publication of her book.

Rebecca Alton, who died at the age of 26 in her family home in Alne, is set to publish a book posthumously on November 25 that will tell the story of her battle against illness in a way that aims to bring hope to people in similar circumstances.

Her father Simon described her as "the most amazing person I’ve ever met in my life" and said the book, which will also raise money for several charities, will continue his daughter’s legacy and help those who may be struggling.

Becca was first diagnosed with Acute Myeloid Leukaemia at five years old and received treatment at the Children's Cancer Hospital in Leeds, which was successful at the time.

She relapsed at the age of ten – receiving a bone marrow transplant from her brother Matthew – and then again at 12 when a second unrelated donor found by charity Anthony Nolan was able to donate further marrow.

Throughout this time, Becca and her family raised money for charity – raising £75,000 and visiting Downing Street, winning awards, and speaking to hundreds at charity events.

In 2017, Becca reached five years clear – moving out to attend York St John University, where she graduated with a first, and starting work as a biomedical scientist, eventually going on to work at AstraZeneca, developing cancer drugs in order to “give something back”.

  Becca 

*(Image: Provided)*

Tragically, she then discovered that as a result of chemotherapy and radiotherapy as a child, her lung function was deteriorating and by 2023, she became dependant on medical oxygen.

Simon said: “When Becca got to five years clear, we thought that’s brilliant, then, just before Covid, she realised that she was getting out of breath even going upstairs. Her doctors discovered irreparable damage to her lungs and put her on medication.

“She was only tiny to begin with, so, with the side effects, she dropped down to six stone. There was just nothing to her. We were pushing the hospital that something’s not right, and at end of October 2023, she was put on oxygen 24/7.”

After meeting with specialists, Becca was told that she needed a double lung transplant but was unlikely survive the operation and, if she did, it was probable she would spend the rest of her life in hospital.

  Becca and Bonnie on the beach 

*(Image: Provided)*

Instead, she made the decision to move back in with her parents in North Yorkshire – designing a custom-built annex and fulfilling her dream of adopting a dog - a golden retriever named Bonnie – to enjoy the rest of her time.

Michelle, Becca's mum, said: “Bec just accepted that ‘this is my life and I’m going to get on with it’ – she didn’t sulk about it or anything. She just wasn’t going to let anything hold her back and she set her stall out to do everything she wanted and that’s what she did.

“She always told us that she wanted to get her own golden retriever, so after she was told she couldn’t have the transplant, we got back to her house and she said, ‘right stop crying guys - I’m going to move home and get a dog’.

“It’s such a comfort having Bonnie. Bec knew she wouldn’t be able to have children, so it was like ‘ok this is my baby’. It’s like Bonnie understood from being a puppy that she needed to look after this girl and they had such an incredible bond.

  Becca with Bonnie 

*(Image: Provided)*

“She’s been such a comfort to us – it feels like she was brought to our family for a reason and she was so gentle with Becca, even from being a puppy, and would trot alongside the wheelchair.”

After moving to the family home, Becca exceeded all medical expectations and fought for more than two years – with the family adding that they later discovered that doctors believed she would die within months of being put on oxygen.

She died at home on November 25, 2025, alongside her parents and Bonnie.

Simon said: “She did so much in her short life – she died when she was 26. She’s just the most amazing person I’ve ever met in my life.

  Becca out and about 

*(Image: Provided)*

“Becca was seeing a homeopathic healer to help her before the end. She decided on the Thursday that she couldn’t do this anymore and it was a process through until the Tuesday morning when she passed away.

“She ticked the boxes, did the stuff on her bucket list, all her friends from home and uni came to say goodbye, and you could tell she was at peace.

“Bec was worried she might have to go into hospital or a hospice, but she was in her own home with her mum right by her side, her dog nearby, and me on the sofa in the other room.”

In preparation for her death, Becca took the time to prepare her own eulogy to be played at her funeral, wrote a speech for her sister Charlotte's wedding (where she was due to be maid of honour), and also prepared her book - which now is set to be published on the first anniversary of her death.

  Becca's pictures at her sister Charlotte's wedding 

*(Image: Provided)*

Simon said: “When Becca was first in hospital, she started keeping a diary and always said she’d write a book one day. After she moved back home and started seeing the healer, she spoke to Bec about a book and that made her think she really had to get it written.

“Her plan was always to write it in a way that’s not all doom and gloom - that people will have a smile on their face and she might be able to help them. Obviously, there’s lots of serious bits of the book, but there’s also funny bits. It’s all about trying to help people.

“Becca’s illness made up 21 years of our lives – we had the happy times, but it was always at the back of our minds ‘what if she gets sick again’, so the message in the book is to give families hope that you can still do things.

“Crack on with your life - this is it. This book is Becca’s legacy.”

Becca’s book is set to be published on November 25 with the money raised set to be split between Candlelighters, Anthony Nolan, Blood Cancer, and Bemorefab.
