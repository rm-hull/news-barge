*Regional mayor DAVID SKAITH wants your views on York and North Yorkshire transport plan*

ANOTHER summer draws to a close, and I hope that York Press readers have been able to enjoy a break and make the most of the warm weather in our beautiful county.

A personal highlight for me this summer has been hearing how many children and young people have enjoyed the temporary play park on Parliament Street.

When I was chair of the High Street Forum in York, I thought that the best way to make our high street a better place for people, particularly locals, not just to shop but to spend time in and enjoy, was to increase the number of free amenities. Top of that list for me has always been a playground.

So, the trial by York BID, which my High Street Fund supported, has been great. Every time I walked down Parliament Street, it was busy and being enjoyed by loads of kids.

York and North Yorkshire is a year-round draw for people from across the country. As we leave a warm summer behind and move towards one of York’s busiest times of year, the challenges of moving all those people into and out of our city are at the front of my mind.

Transport is something I’ve written about many times in my York Press column.

While there is a lot to be proud of, such as the largest all-electric Park and Ride network in the UK here in York (which we’ve supported to provide longer operating hours over the past year), or Britain’s most scenic bus service between York and Whitby, it’s clear that we have a long way to go to build the public transport network that we all want, need and deserve.

To do that, we need to set out our long-term plan for transport by creating the first Mayor’s Local Transport Plan for York and North Yorkshire.

While a Local Transport Plan, or LTP, has a big focus on the things you’d expect to be in there, buses and trains, it also touches on other ways people move around the area: private vehicles, freight and logistics, walking, wheeling, cycling, taxis, car sharing, community transport, e-bike and scooter hire.

A good LTP sets out a vision for each of these and, more importantly, how we’ll seek to better integrate those different ways of getting around.

That’s fairly easy to do in a place like Manchester, where the Bee Network has benefited from a collection of neighbouring dense urban areas. Here in York and North Yorkshire, it is obviously more challenging.

That doesn’t mean we don’t have the same ambition, and that’s what we want the LTP to set out.

Buses are something that I know many people care deeply about. Since 2010, the bus network has been systematically cut and reduced. Between 2010 and 2024, North Yorkshire lost 50 per cent of its bus-driven miles, the single largest reduction anywhere in the north of England.

That’s vital context as to why we are in the situation we are. That is because of political choices nationally to slash local government funding, and local leaders who have not had the ambition or drive to make public transport work for the public.

That culture ends with me. No longer will we accept the managed decline of our bus services. York and North Yorkshire will be a place where buses are given the importance and prioritisation that they have not had for too long.

Making those improvements won’t be quick, it won’t be easy, and it won’t be cheap. But they are essential.

I’m never going to shy away from the scale of the challenge, and I’m never going to pretend that there are any quick fixes.

We need a clear plan, which the LTP will provide, and support from the Government to kick-start that journey.

The new Prime Minister, Andy Burnham, has made a good start by bringing back the £2 bus fare. His mission to drive more power and responsibility to communities and the Mayors that represent them is welcome.

I’m clear that, for this to work in York and North Yorkshire, we need to be given the resources to transform how we deliver public transport across a huge, largely rural geography, with dispersed cities, towns and villages.

Giving people more choices about how they move around our county will be the overarching aim of the Mayor’s Local Transport Plan.

I’ve always thought that consultations must mean something, which is why I am really clear that, as we make the final plan, we’ll look closely at what residents, communities, local organisations and businesses have said. This is your opportunity to tell me what you want to see from our long-term plan for how we get around York and North Yorkshire.

To take part in the Mayor’s Local Transport Plan consultation, visit www.yorknorthyorks-ca.gov.uk/local-transport-plan-consultation.
