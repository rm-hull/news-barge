FAMILY and friends of Pride of Britain winner Luke Mortimer are taking a much-deserved rest after completing a massive 131 mile car push challenge.

A ten-strong team, including Luke's dad, Adam, pushed the Peugeot 207 all the way from Sheffield's Children's Hospital, where Luke, a quadruple amputee, will receive treatment until he is 21, to Silverstone Circuit, near Buckinghamshire, for the UK's 'largest family fundraiser festival' CarFest.

  Luke joins driver, team member, Lee Towers at the start of the big push 

*(Image: Malcolm Stoney)*

The push - with some pushing, and some pulling - took six days and with very early starts and late finishes, to avoid busy times on the roads.

The pushing team was supported with a back-up crew of seven, and apart from one night spent in a hotel - for much-needed showers - they slept in tents and the support van in pub car parks, and at a garden centre.

So far, the car push has raised around £10,000 for CarFest, which was set up by Chris Evans to raise money for children's charities, including BBC Children in Need, Teenage Cancer Trust and Starlight Children's Foundation.

The Mortimer family, who live in Embsay, near Skipton, came up with the idea of the car push fundraiser while heading home from last year's big fundraiser when a team carried out a never-done-before tri-national challenge, involving climbing mountains and rowing across a Scottish loch.

Luke, who had to have parts of both legs and arms amputated after contracting meningitis in 2019, received the Child of Courage award at last year's Pride of Britain Awards when he met Dwayne Johnson.

His dad, Adam Mortimer, said they had initially planned to get a car and push it to CarFest all the way from Skipton for their latest fundraiser, but in the end, decided to start from Sheffield.

  The team arriving at Silverstone Circuit 

*(Image: Malcolm Stoney)*

"It would have taken another two days to start from Skipton and a lot of the team had taken a week off work to do it," he said, "so, in the end we started from Sheffield."

The push took an enormous amount of planning, and involved informing all the police forces along the route to let them know the slow-moving team was coming through.

They also had to make sure the pushing was safe, so had some pulling and some pushing - essential for some of the hills they tackled along the way.

The car itself, a Peugeot 207 which had failed its MOT, was donated and was specially adapted by the mechanics team at Craven College in Skipton.

"They took the engine and gearbox out and specially adapted it for pushing, we are very grateful for what they did," said Adam.

The team's route took them from Sheffield to Bollsover Castle, Newark-on-Trent, Kettleby, Market Harborough, Milton Malsor, and finally Silverstone.

"We were getting up at 4am for a 5am start, pushing until 7.30am and stopping for breakfast for the rush hour, starting again at 9am and pushing again until 3.30pm and stopping again for rush hour. We'd then start again at 5pm or 6pm and push until 8pm. We ended up staying every night in pub car parks, apart from on the Wednesday, when some of us decided we needed a shower," said Adam.

And, on the whole, everyone was very supportive, including the pubs along the way.

The 131 mile journey started on August 23 and finished on Friday, in time for the three-day festival, with a line-up of top acts, including Jessie J, Tom Jones, Nile Rodgers and Rick Astley and where Luke and Adam joined Chris Evans on the stage.

  Luke, Adam and the team at Silverstone 

*(Image: Malcolm Stoney)*

"A huge thank you to everyone involved in the planning and execution. Another huge thank you to everyone who supported us. Another huge thank you to everyone who donated, be it money, food, encouragement or hugs. We couldn't have done this without you," said the team.
