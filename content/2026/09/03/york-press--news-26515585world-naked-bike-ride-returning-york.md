**World Naked Bike Ride: why it is returning to York**

CYCLISTS in and around York are invited to York's most distinctive annual cycling event, the World Naked Bike Ride.

This is a protest primarily about cyclist safety, focusing on how vulnerable we are on fast and busy car-infested roads.

However it is also a celebration of our diverse bodies which power our machines with zero carbon emissions.

This year's ride is seven miles, and starts at 4pm at the Millennium Bridge on Saturday, September 12. All cyclists are welcome, whether clothed, in fancy dress or in the buff.

Safe cycle routes mean more people ride bikes which means fewer cars stuck in traffic jams and less pollution.

  *John Cossham,*

  *Hull Road,*

  *York*

  **More signs to keep tourists off tower mound**

IN recent years I have watched the progress of Clifford's Tower for the benefit of our visitors to York. I have also noticed the increasing detrimental use of the mound on which it sits.

In earlier years there was less observable climbing but now it appears that it is carried out as a right. The signage forbidding climbing has never been 'noticeable'.

I once observed a coach that parked on the road behind the official bus stops full of tourists who disembarked, began taking photographs and all then set about climbing up the mound to take selfies.

The signs to prevent climbing are too small and only in English. I'm sure that many who climb, do so without noticing a sign as they are much too far apart.

Usage of the mound as a form of entertainment detracts from the value of its historical significance and as such should be protected and respected. There are other more suitable green spaces in York for children and others to use for play areas and picnic sights.

I would suggest that the base be surrounded in low fencing as an obvious first deterrent and that the signs be much larger, more noticeable and clear with No Climbing written in English and other languages.

People travel from all points in the world to visit our historic city. We can only maintain that interest if we preserve what they want to see.

  *Rae Mould*

  *Member of the Voluntary Guides Association to the City of York*
