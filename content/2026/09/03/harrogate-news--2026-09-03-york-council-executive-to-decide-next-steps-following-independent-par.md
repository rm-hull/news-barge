City of York Council’s Executive will meet on Wednesday 9 September 2026 to consider the recommendations of an independent review into community car parks and on-street parking charges, following sustained pressure from local traders and residents.

The review was commissioned under the Traffic Management Act 2004 “Right to challenge parking policies” following petitions submitted by community members in four distinct areas: Bishopthorpe Road, East Parade (Heworth), Micklegate, and The Groves. The petitions were launched in direct response to controversial changes to car parking charges implemented by the council in 2025.

## Proposed changes to charges and resident badges

The upcoming report invites the Executive to review the findings of the independent assessment alongside subsequent feedback from the Place Scrutiny Committee meeting in June 2026.

Key recommendations put forward for Executive approval include:

- Alterations to the cost of the Minster Badge, which is available to all York residents.
- Specific reductions in parking charges for residents who hold a Minster Badge within the four reviewed locations.
- The introduction of an additional, non-statutory consultation process before any significant changes to parking charges are made in the future.
- The publication of a revised annual parking report to provide clearer transparency on how parking revenue is collected and spent.

The report also outlines the council’s broader intention to develop a comprehensive parking strategy for the city, as identified in the Local Transport Strategy and its long-term Implementation Plan.


### The Specific Parking Charge Changes Proposed:

The independent review report outlines concrete structural changes to the city’s current parking tariff framework, specifically targeting resident permit costs and localized hourly rates:

- **The Minster Badge Price Cut:** The report recommends reducing the annual cost of the resident Minster Badge down to a flat fee of**£15 per year** .
- **50% Community Parking Discount:** Residents holding a valid Minster Badge will receive a**50% discount** on standard hourly parking fees when using car parks and on-street bays within the four heavily impacted petition zones (Bishopthorpe Road, East Parade in Heworth, Micklegate, and The Groves). This effectively drops their parking rate from £3.00 down to**£1.50 per hour** .

### Proposals Formally Rejected by the Review:

While local traders and residents lobbied heavily for broader systemic changes to boost high street footfall, the independent review formally recommended **against** implementing the following measures due to council revenue impacts:

- **No Short-Stay Free Windows:** The council has rejected requests to introduce a 15-minute or 30-minute free parking interval for rapid “dash-in” high street shopping.
- **No Half-Hour Pricing Tiers:** Proposals to allow drivers to purchase half-hour parking increments were turned down; the standard minimum charging period will remain a full hour.

### New Policy and Transparency Frameworks:

To avoid similar community backlashes in the future, the Executive is being asked to approve a new administrative protocol:

- **Mandatory Consultations:** A new, non-statutory public consultation phase must be launched prior to enacting any significant parking charge changes moving forward.
- **Financial Ring-Fencing Transparency:** The council will publish an overhauled annual parking report explicitly detailing exactly how much parking revenue is collected and precisely which local road and transport budgets that money is spent on.


## Council leadership response

The final decision on whether to accept, reject, or modify these recommendations sits with the council’s executive leadership team next week.


**Councillor Kate Ravilious, Executive Member for Transport:**

This is the final stage of the review.

We will be looking carefully at the recommendations from the independent report, as well as those from Place Scrutiny Committee, before deciding on the way forward.

Thank you to all involved in the review, particularly residents and traders who gave their views.



The Executive will debate the report and finalise the council’s parking approach for the affected community hubs on 9 September.
