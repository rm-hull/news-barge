A police chief in a northern Mississippi city has been fired following a fresh wave of controversy, months after officers shot and killed a one-year-old Black child named Kohen Wiley during a shoplifting call.

Harold Vanderford, the police chief of Senatobia, tendered his resignation on Tuesday. However, the city’s board of aldermen rejected his departure and opted to unanimously vote to sack him instead, the city clerk confirmed without offering further detail.

The dismissal comes after joint reporting by The New York Times, Mississippi Today and Verite News alleged Vanderford used racial slurs in private text exchanges with a colleague, alongside ongoing fallout from the fatal shooting.

Young Kohen died and a woman was wounded on 14 June when an officer fired into a vehicle outside a Walmart. The boy’s mother, Vellesiya Wiley, denied that she or a friend had stolen anything, and no charges have been filed.

The officer who shot Wiley and the driver has been placed on administrative leave while the Mississippi Bureau of Investigation conducts an enquiry.

Kohen Wiley was Black, as is his mother. The death ignited anger in a small city where tensions between Black residents and the police have boiled over in recent years. Senatobia, with a population of more than 8,000, is about 40 miles (65 kilometers) south of Memphis.

"I believe the Board has a responsibility to protect the integrity and trust of the Senatobia Police Department," Alderman Demetrius Garrett wrote in a Facebook post. "I still believe in unity. But unity does not mean ignoring serious issues it means standing together for what is right."

The city had previously addressed the reporting about Vanderford's texts, saying in a statement on Aug. 25 that he had appeared before the mayor and board to answer questions and that he would continue to serve as police chief.

Senatobia Alderman Chris McConnell called for an independent investigation of the police department.

"We need answers about departmental leadership, internal culture, the handling of complaints and misconduct, financial practices, and whether the Board has been receiving the information necessary to provide proper oversight," McConnell said in a statement. "Our citizens deserve a police department they can trust."

National civil rights attorney Ben Crump, representing Kohen Wiley's family, called the chief's firing the "right decision."

"The racist language that cost Chief Vanderford his job is exactly why this family cannot simply be told to trust the process," Crump said in a statement calling for the Mississippi Bureau of Investigations to release body-camera footage of the shooting. "Give this family the facts they have been owed since the day Kohen died."

The Senatobia Police Department declined to comment on the firing, and Vanderford could not be immediately located for a response Wednesday.

## Kohen Wiley's shooting death was one controversy among others

Vanderford was appointed police chief on Sept. 29, 2025, after the board of aldermen declined to reappoint the previous chief, Richard Chandler, in July of that year.

![Harold Vanderford](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/02/19/39/Police-Chief-Harold-Vanderford.webp)

Chandler led the police department through several controversies. They included the 2023 arrest of a 10-year-old Black boy for urinating in public and an incident in which an officer threatened a woman with a Taser and pulled her from her car during a dispute over a handicapped parking space outside the same Walmart where the child was shot.

"You removed Richard Chandler," said Senatobia community activist Patrick Lumumba. "But you did not remove the culture of racism, and that's what Vanderford harbors."

Following the child's death, Lumumba formed the Senatobia Committee for Accountability and Transparency, a community watchdog group that is demanding answers about the June shooting and advocating for better policing.

He voiced confidence in Lt. Angelica Maze, who will serve as the department's interim police chief, calling her a"good person."
