A DIFFERENT kind of exhibition is opening at Bradford’s 1in12 Club this September, featuring 25 cartoons drawn live at gigs.

Artist John Davison has drawn more than 600 bands. He talks about his work.

I became interested in cartoons as a child. I drew a lot on car journeys, and on holiday I would read comics like The Dandy and The Beano, and loved the intricacies of things like Asterix and Viz.

I remember an RE teacher getting us to draw scenes from stories - I enjoyed the challenge of drawing crowds.

I first drew at a gig in 2011. A friend of mine, Harry, was stepping down from a hugely popular open mic night in Leeds, Lyrically Justified. His last night was busy and although he couldn’t put me on stage, I stayed around and drew an A4 picture of him with the regular performers.

I gave him the artwork and also published it in a community magazine I was editing at the time - it was really appreciated by readers.

Generally, I would work for more than three hours on a piece, which is great, but I became inspired to do something faster after I became involved with zines (a zine is a small booklet or magazine created independently by an individual or a small group).

Drawing each band on smaller sheets, really quickly, getting into the crowd to capture the action, I published these in zines I called Cartoons from the Pit.

It’s a habit now and I draw nearly every time I watch live music. I’ve probably drawn more than 600 bands.

I work in a fine point Biro pen on an A6 notepad in landscape. I used to work entirely on offcuts of photocopier paper, but now prefer using RNLI charity notepads on a standard size. The smallness of the pad makes it less conspicuous in a crowd.

People seem to like the drawings because of the energy in them. I’m particularly interested in the feedback between the audience and performers: how each is bringing energy to each other.

People are fun to draw when they dance. I like the diversity of a crowd and depicting what can happen in a moment. I love when people lose their inhibitions to share joy with each other, or a catharsis in relation to what the musicians are giving out. My people are wobbly and imperfect. I’m not trying to flatter people, but represent them. If I see something like a long neck, crooked teeth or whatever, I’ll make sure it’s seen, but I’m not trying to throw any custard pies.

It’s the imperfections that make people beautiful. I don’t draw things like mobile phones, or people looking sad.

Some people have compared my work to Quentin Blake, Lowry, or a punk version of Where’s Wally. I’m flattered by anyone who likes it enough to come talk to me.

For me, Quentin Blake is for art what punk rock is for music - it can be quick to get something across and doesn’t need to be too technical. Three chords, play a song kind of thing. If I succeed, each member of the crowd gets their own little narrative and is going on their own journey. We’re all together in a moment of joy.

These days I prioritise fiction writing over zines - I’m currently getting my second novel ready for publication. I teach English for my day job and have spent the last six months editing a short story anthology for my students which we’ve just published. Soon I’ll get back to my book.

I’m so happy to be working with the 1in12 Club to host this exhibition. All profits will go to the club and we’re also making the exhibition interactive by having each band donate a track you can hear when you look at the drawing.

You can buy the compilation, prints, or a zine featuring the artwork. We’re collaborating with two other artists: Yaya, a street artist, illustrator and activist who is creating a bigger piece on the opening night which people can add to during the exhibit, and Cass of Go Mug Yourself, who will help people create ceramic caricatures.

We’re running workshops and an art afternoon at the club from 1pm to 4pm on Saturday October 10.

*Cartoons from the 1in12 Club opens on Friday September 11 and runs until Sunday November 22. The opening night features performances from; Rabies Babies, Blue Orchid Reaction, Character Actors and Lewis Pugh.
