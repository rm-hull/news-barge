The owners of a Barnard Castle business are seeking to keep its striking purple shopfront after a complaint that it had been painted without consent.

Sanders Cove, a crystal, mineral and fossil shop on the Market Place, opened in its new premises less than two months ago by husband-and-wife team Ian and Holly Sanderson.

The business, which occupies a Grade II* listed building within the Barnard Castle Conservation Area, installed new purple signage and painted the front of the building to match brand colouring

The couple now plan to apply for retrospective permission to retain the purple frontage and new signage, after a complaint was made to Durham County Council.

Holly Sanderson, 35, said the colour had drawn a positive response from people in the town.

She said: “There’s an amount of people who are walking past and say they love the colour and asking to sign a petition to keep it going.”

  Holly Sanderson outside her shop in Barnard Castle. 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

The couple said they had discussed plans for the frontage with their landlord before carrying out the work and had been told the premises had been painted in several different colours during the past three decades.

They met council officers on Monday, August 31, to discuss the issue.

### ‘We’ve poured so much into it’

Ian Sanderson, 36, said the couple had invested a substantial sum in transforming the premises and installing signage designed around the purple exterior.

He said: “It’s particularly upsetting as we’ve only just had our new signage installed, designed around the purple shopfront.

“Barnard Castle has such a lovely variety of colourful shopfronts - pinks, teals, yellows and more - and before we arrived, ours was green.

"Since changing it to purple, every comment we’ve received has been about how lovely it looks and how much brighter the shop is.

“We’re just a husband-and-wife team trying to build a small family business and support our family.

"Taking the leap into a much larger premises was a huge — but very exciting — step for us, and we’ve poured so much into making the new shop something we can be proud of.

“We really just want to be left to trade, grow our little business and hopefully bring something positive to Barnard Castle.

"As a small business, things are already hard enough without unexpected hurdles like this.”

Stephen Reed, Durham County Council’s planning manager, said: “Having received a report about a Grade II* listed building being painted without consent, we visited the property to discuss the works.

“We have offered our advice and support to the business owners and will continue to seek a solution that balances their needs with the building’s listed status and its location within the Barnard Castle Conservation Area.”
