A centuries-old tradition has been revived at a Durham pub as punters are welcomed back after a £1 million revamp.

  The Court, a restaurant and bar in the former Court Inn on Court Lane, __has been relaunched six years after last orders were called for the final time.__ 

North East entrepreneurs Richard Nellis, Terry Haley and Imad Ali have fused their experience across hospitality, construction, and development to breathe new life into the site.

And to mark the occasion, Dr Arnab Basu MBE DL, High Sheriff of the County Palatine of Durham, poured himself a pint as part of a long-standing tradition.

Centuries ago, the High Sheriff, as the King's representative for law and order in the county, was responsible for checking the quality, strength and price of ale sold by local publicans.

It made sure customers received a fair measure and value for money.

And Dr Basu was able to continue that tradition at the new-look venue, which had been a pub for around 150 years before its closure.

The Court is home to a bar and restaurant alongside a series of individual seating and dining areas, featuring dark hardwood panelling, leather seats, marble, and polished brass.

The design of the space has been influenced by traditional Durham and classic London venues.

Its menu combines elevated pub classics with bistro-style and à la carte dishes, while the interior has been deliberately designed to retain the welcoming character of a traditional pub.

The opening follows a further £2.3m investment in The Sanctuary Knocker, the luxury boutique aparthotel created on the upper floors of the same historic building.

Dr Basu said: “It is a great pleasure to officially open The Court and particularly fitting to mark the occasion by pulling and raising a pint.

“Centuries ago, one of the responsibilities associated with the office of High Sheriff was ensuring the ale being served to local people was of the right quality, strength and measure, so there is a wonderful historical connection.

“Fortunately, my role today is a rather more ceremonial one!

“I also have my own connection with The Court Inn from my student days in Durham, so it is particularly special to return as High Sheriff and see this historic venue given a new lease of life.

“What Richard, Terry and Imad have created is an impressive example of entrepreneurs investing in Durham and having the confidence and ambition to bring an important building back into use.

“The attention to detail is evident throughout The Court and it is wonderful to see a venue with such a long association with the city beginning a new chapter.”

Richard Nellis said: “There has been a huge amount of love, thought and attention to detail put into every part of The Court.

“We could have refurbished the building, opened the doors and had a perfectly good pub, but that was never what we wanted to do.

“We wanted to create something with real longevity. We've used real hardwood, marble, brass and quality materials throughout and we've looked at everything, from the way the rooms flow to the lighting, furniture and artwork.

“That same commitment to quality runs through the kitchen. We want to serve really good food, made with good ingredients and done properly.

“The food, service and surroundings all need to work together to create the experience we want people to have at The Court.”

Terry Haley said: “We set out to create one of Durham's destination venues, somewhere that feels special from the moment you walk through the door but is equally somewhere you can come back to time and again.

"We've always talked about creating a destination venue. Somewhere you can come for a drink after work, have dinner with friends, celebrate something special or settle in and spend the whole evening.

“The Court Inn was part of Durham for generations and there was a real desire to see it return, but if we were going to bring it back, we wanted to do it properly.

“A lot of passion, time and thought has gone into creating somewhere that respects that history while offering something new for the city.”
