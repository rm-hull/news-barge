A CONSERVATION group has raised concerns after felled trees were left blocking a public walkway.

It is believed the trees - including alder, elm, and sycamore - were cut down more than six weeks ago along North Beck in Keighley and remain obstructing Beck Side path, a popular riverside route.

The obstruction has drawn criticism from River Worth Friends, a volunteer group that regularly carries out clean-up and conservation work in the area.

The Environment Agency said the felled trees are not linked to any ongoing work by them.

They are now investigating the matter.

  One of the felled trees in North Beck, Keighley 

*(Image: Sue Patchett)*

## 'A staggering lack of care or competence'

Sue Patchett, a trustee for River Worth Friends, said: "When I first heard about the trees being cut down and saw them for myself, I was pretty angry that someone had done that and, frankly, that they'd had the cheek to do it.

"The way the work has been carried out shows a staggering lack of care or competence.

"You don't need to be an expert in tree management to look at the aftermath and realise that this isn't exactly a textbook example of how it should be done."

## Group now struggles to clean up the area

The group says the felled trees are not only unsightly but are also making their volunteer work more difficult.

Ms Patchett said: "When we visit the area to do clean-ups, we usually enter the river near where the trees are and work our way down the channel.

"That's normally the safest and easiest option for us, but now we have to work around the trees by entering and leaving the channel at three separate points.

"That involves climbing up and down a wall and makes our job considerably harder."

  The trees block access for the group 

*(Image: Sue Patchett)*

She also stressed the ecological value of the lost trees.

Ms Patchett said: "It’s upsetting to see established riverside trees simply cut down by someone who doesn't appear to have given any thought to what happens afterwards."

Ms Patchett added that leaving the trees across the beck for so long is unacceptable.

She said: "They've now been there for more than six weeks, creating an obstruction that should have been dealt with much sooner.

"Having the trees left lying across the beck is an eyesore, and it's a pretty depressing scene to see in the middle of town."

## Worries over antisocial behaviour

One of the felled trees is partially blocking access along Beck Side and has created a visual screen, which Ms Patchett fears could worsen existing problems.

She said: "The area has always attracted antisocial behaviour, so I don't want to claim the trees are responsible for that, but the additional screening can't help."

Ms Patchett wants the trees removed urgently and the site properly cleared.

She said: "The trees need to be removed from the channel as soon as possible and the obstruction to Beck Side dealt with properly.

"I'd also expect the cut timber to be dealt with in whatever way is appropriate for the site, following the relevant advice and guidance."

She also raised concerns about the remaining stumps, some of which are already regrowing.

Ms Patchett said: "Some are around waist height and are already producing regrowth, and at the moment they just add to the eyesore.

  One of the felled trees in North Beck, Keighley 

*(Image: Sue Patchett)*

"If the site is being properly cleared up, I'd like the stumps to be dealt with appropriately too.

"And in an ideal world, I'd like the people responsible for the felling to be identified and, if they have carried out the work unlawfully, held accountable."

River Worth Friends continue to campaign for their removal.

Ms Patchett said: "I don't think it's unreasonable to expect something to happen after six weeks.

"I'd like to see the trees removed and the situation resolved, but if they're still there I'll continue asking questions about why they haven't been dealt with and what is being done about them."

## Environment Agency response

A spokesperson for the Environment Agency said: "We can confirm that the felled trees are not linked to any ongoing work by the Environment Agency.

"Officers went down to the site today and we are investigating who is responsible."
