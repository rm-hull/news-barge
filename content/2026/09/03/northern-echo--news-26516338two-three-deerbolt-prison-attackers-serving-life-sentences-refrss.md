Two of three inmates who have admitted involvement in a group attack on another prisoner at a County Durham jail are potentially both facing a second life sentence.

Shawn Spence, 20, and 21-year-olds Kai Howitt and James Morton were originally charged with unlawfully and maliciously wounding the other inmate with intent, in the assault at HMP Deerbolt, near Barnard Castle, on April 27, last year.

Their victim, a 20-year-old inmate from Durham, was said to have been stabbed in the head, neck and back, but his injuries were not described as life threatening.

At different stages in proceedings, Spence and Howitt have now admitted the original charge, while Morton appeared at Durham Crown Court in late June, when he pleaded guilty to an alternative count of affray, which was “accepted” by the prosecution.

All three appeared via video link to Teesside Crown Court from Holme House Prison, Stockton, where they are now detained, on Wednesday (September 2), for a scheduled sentencing hearing.

But Judge Jo Kidd said she needs more information about their previous crimes with a probation input as to how they are responding to their current sentences, before she passes sentence.

“In light of serious offences all three have been previously convicted of and the circumstances of the present offences it’s important for any sentencing judge to assess dangerousness.”

She said both Spence and Howitt have previously received life sentences or the youth equivalent, so the latest attack, now admitted in both cases, could be seen as a second qualifying offence.

But she said any sentencing judge would be “hampered” by a lack of information about the circumstances of their previous offending.

She said Morton, however, is in a slightly different position, as he is not serving a life sentence and has admitted the slightly lesser offence of affray rather than wounding with intent.

Judge Kidd told Spence: “The court needs to know more about the circumstances and background of your previous offending.

“I’ll need to assess in the cases of you and Mr Howitt if I should pass another life sentence.

“I need to have information to assess the current risk you present.

“It wouldn’t be right if I, as the sentencing judge, don’t have all the relevant information, including from your offender managers.”

She said she wanted all the relevant information by September 30, with a view to passing sentence at Durham Crown Court on October 15.

All three defendants, who are listed to be either of 'no fixed abode', or, ‘care-of HMP Holme House’, were remanded to remain in custody until the new sentencing date.
