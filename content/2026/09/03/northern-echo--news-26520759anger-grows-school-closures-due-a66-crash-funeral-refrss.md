Anger is growing after schools “felt they had no option but to close early” due to fears of disruption from an A66 crash funeral.

  The funeral of Jakub Matusiak - one of the __five people in the Volkswagen Passat who died__ in the crash on August 22 - is set to take place in South Bank tomorrow (September 4). 

  The incident, which involved a marked Cleveland Police vehicle and the Passat, which was travelling the wrong direction, also took the lives of __PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough.__ 

  Pc Matthew Blades, 37, and Pc Tom Clough, 38 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE)*

Several schools, as well as Normanby Medical Centre on Normanby Road, have announced that they will be closing early due to the disruption expected by the funeral, which is taking place at St John the Evangelist Church on Normanby Road.

And now, concerns are growing about how schools felt they had no option but to shut due to fears about road disruption or potentially dangerous behaviour.

Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council leader Alec Brown said it is “awful” that children’s education is being disrupted and said any dangerous riding, reckless driving, or antisocial behaviour is “not acceptable”.

  Alec Brown, leader of Redcar and Cleveland Council 

*(Image: Alec Brown/Facebook.)*

A spokesperson for the council said the decision to close schools early followed discussions between local headteachers and the authority over anticipated traffic and transport disruption.

They said the decision was made after "careful consideration of the potential impact on home-to-school transport and the departure of pupils, staff and families at the end of the school day".

South Bank Primary School said it would be closing at 12pm on Friday “to ensure the safety and wellbeing of our pupils”.

In a message posted on social media, head teacher Tammy Cooper said: A funeral is being held locally at St John’s church on Normanby Road that day and we are expecting large numbers of people in the area which will inevitably cause congestion and transport disruption in the immediate area.

“This decision has been reached after careful consideration of the potential impact on the departure of pupils, staff and families.

“We recognise the inconvenience this may cause and thank our families for their understanding and co-operation.”

Ms Cooper said the school also recognises “some pupils may feel upset or anxious about the funeral taking place locally” and the pastoral team would be available for support.

In a letter to parents, Mark Robinson, principal of Outwood Academy in Normanby, said the school would also close from midday after discussions between local headteachers and the council.

Cllr Brown said he “fully understands that funerals are deeply personal occasions, and I respect the right of families to grieve” but is “deeply concerned over the school closures.

“Our children should be able to attend school safely and without their education being affected by events happening elsewhere,” he said.

“Parents should not be put in the position of having to make alternative arrangements for their children because schools are worried about road disruption or potentially dangerous behaviour.
