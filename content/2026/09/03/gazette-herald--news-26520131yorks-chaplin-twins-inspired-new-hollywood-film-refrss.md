*A Hollywood film inspired by York's most famous twins puts the fascinating lives of sisters Greta and Freda Chaplin in the spotlight again*

YORK'S world-famous Chaplin twins are the inspiration for a new Hollywood film which has premiered at a prestigious international festival.

Top actors Rooney Mara and her sister Kate star in the film which is based on the lives of Freda and Greta Chaplin, twin sisters from Tang Hall who shot to fame in the early 1980s following a sensational court case in York – turning them into tabloid sensations.

The movie – titled Bucking Fastard – also stars A-listers Orlando Bloom and Domhnall Gleeson and has premiered at the 81st Venice International Film Festival.

It is due to be shown in cinemas this autumn.


The film's release will bring the world's attention once more to its source story – that of the intriguing relationship of York twins Freda and Greta Chaplin.

The twins were prosecuted at York Magistrates' Court for breach of the peace for harassing a local truck driver, Ken Iveson.

Their court appearance was the first time the strange lives of the Chaplin twins came to the attention of the wider public – and their story was reported in newspapers across the world: journalists from Brazil, Sweden and Minnesota covered the case.

The twins were bizarre mirror images of each other. They dressed identically, spoke in unison and even walked in step.

They were inseparable and at the trial would shout out together to plead their innocence.

Press reports at the time record them saying: "No, no, no, no, no. It's not true. It's not true. We haven't been near him, we haven't been near there, it is a bitter, bitter lie" and the magistrates ordering them to be quiet.

They were jailed for one month.

Media interest continued and two documentaries were made of their lives – A Pair of One by Julia Darling, filmed in 1987, and The Twins in 1994 by Journeyman Pictures.

Greta died in 2007, followed by Freda in 2020. They are buried together at Fulford Cemetery.

But now their unusual life story has received the Hollywood treatment.

In the movie, Rooney Mara (famous for her roles in The Girl With The Dragon Tattoo and Carol) and her real-life sister Kate (who starred in Brokeback Mountain and Fantastic Four) play inseparable sisters Jean and Joan Holbrooke, modelled on the lives of Freda and Greta.

The title of the film, Bucking Fastard, is based on a verbal slip the twins made simultaneously during their court case.

The Mara sisters are considered Hollywood 'royalty' – Rooney is the partner of Oscar-winner Joaquin Phoenix while Kate is married to Billy Elliot star Jamie Bell.

  Rooney Mara, left, and her sister Kate, right, play sisters in a new Hollywood film based on the lives of York twins York twins Freda and Greta Chaplin


The movie has been written and directed by award-winning, veteran German filmmaker Werner Herzog, who has long been fascinated by the story of the York twins.


In his memoir, Every Man for Himself and God Against All, Herzog recalls meeting the Chaplin twins and being struck by their uniqueness, particularly the way they walked and talked.

He recalled their meeting: "We know that twins sometimes develop their own secret language when they are all alone by which they can exclude the rest of the world, but Freda and Greta spoke the same words at the same time.

  Exterior of the former Chaplin family home in Tang Hall, York, which went on the market following Freda's death in 2020


"They wore exactly the same clothes, hairstyles, shoes. Their handbags and umbrellas were identical.

"When they walked, they didn’t walk in step like soldiers, left-right, left-right, but they had their inside feet together and kept time with their outside feet.

"You could have folded a picture of them, and the two halves would have matched."

The film is scheduled to be screened at several international film festivals before a general release later in the autumn.
