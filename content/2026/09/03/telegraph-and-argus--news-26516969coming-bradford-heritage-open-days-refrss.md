WHAT was the ‘accident waiting to happen’ that devastated a community? What led a parish priest to become an activist? What went on in the old blacksmiths towpath forge? And who were the heroes of Bradford concert hall?

Find out all this, and more, when Bradford opens its doors next week for the district’s “biggest, most connected” Heritage Open Days. From September 11-20, more than 60 free events will explore our rich, diverse and surprising heritage.

The programme brings together museums, churches, community groups, artists, volunteers and heritage organisations - a collaborative approach made possible by Arts Council England funding awarded to Bradford Heritage Connection CIC.

Step inside historic buildings, try hands-on activities, delve into archives, from colour science to community records, discover rare documents and photos revealing Bradford’s industrial, social and creative past, and explore it on foot, from riverside heritage to literary landmarks.

Bradford Heritage Connection CIC was founded in 2024 to amplify the district’s history and heritage. Executive Director Vicky Clifton says it is helping more people connect with Bradford’s past “in meaningful, joyful and accessible ways”.

Adds Vicky: “This shared Heritage Open Days programme is a milestone for Bradford. It reflects the collaboration and ambition of partners across the district, working together to make heritage more visible, accessible and connected. After the success of this year’s Bradford Heritage Festival, this programme shows what’s possible when Bradford works as one. As we move beyond our City of Culture year, it’s a powerful reminder that the legacy lives on in the partnerships, pride and shared purpose shaping Bradford’s future.”

Here are some highlights of the Heritage Open Days programme:

l Bradford’s Beck Plaque Walk: Discover the city’s underground river with a city centre plaque trail, and hear tales of medieval mills, breweries, tanners, strikes, floods, bridges and buildings.

  The parlour at the Brontë Birthplace. Image: Matt Gibbons


l Brontë Birthplace Butcher’s Shop Story: Explore the Thornton house where the famous literary siblings were born - and find out who lived there after they left.

l Bradford Unitarian Church Open Weekends: See original stained glass and memorials, and learn of Rational Dissent and the birth of radical Bradford.

  An exhibition of archive photos and programmes explores the story of Bradford Festival Choral Society. Pic: BFCS


l Music, People and a Changing City: Uncover 170 years of Bradford Festival Choral Society through photographs, programmes and stories from the historic choir’s remarkable archive, at Bradford Mechanics Institute. Explore Victorian Bradford, garden parties, balls and choir life. Visitors can also share their own memories and memorabilia.

l The Ten Hours Parson: Find out what led the priest at St John The Evangelist, Bierley, to participate in a national movement that changed the lives of mill-workers in Bradford and beyond. Rev George Stringer Bull, known as Parson Bull, was Minister of the church from 1826-1839. Shocked at living and working conditions in the parish, he became an active reformer.

l Heritage games at Darley Street Market: Step up and have a go at carnival games such as Roll‑a‑Coin, Ball‑in‑a‑Bucket and Table Quoits.

l Explore Saltaire’s Victoria Hall, from the 19th century to its role in 2025 film The Choral. Enjoy Wurlitzer organ concerts, meet historians and find out more about this remarkable building.

  Tour guide Joanne Crowther, dressed as Bingley suffragette Nell Kenney, who will be leading a walk from Saltaire to Shipley Glen - site of a major 1908 suffragette rally. Image: Paul Brown


l Suffragette walking tour: Bingley suffragette Nell Kenney leads a walk from Saltaire to Shipley Glen, site of the Monster Suffragette Rally of 1908. Includes a ride on the Shipley Glen Tramway.

l Low Moor 1916: Performance by the Low Moor Comical Company about the munitions factory explosion. Through drama, music and song, six performers explore events leading to the tragedy. Low Moor Local History Group also leads a walk of locations near the blast.

l Everyday Histories Sports and Pastimes: Uncover the district’s sporting heritage and clubs, games and hobbies that brought communities together. The event, at Cliffe Castle, includes a display of Keighley Cougars memorabilia.

  All aboard on the Northcliff Miniature Railway.


l Bradford Model Engineering Society: Family day at Northcliff Miniature Railway, Shipley, with a woodland ride on the miniature line.

l Manningham Walk: ‘Samuel Lister’ explores buildings and people past and present.

l Everyday Heroes of St George’s Hall: Tour celebrating those who’ve kept the venue running, including stonemasons, technical crews, front-of-house and backstage teams and cleaners.

l Dobson Locks Blacksmiths Forge: Demonstrations at historic workshop on the Leeds and Liverpool Canal.

l Bradford Archives open day: Pop-up celebrations of work, play, shopping, transport and daily lives of Bradfordians, from Victorian times to present.

  Pinny featuring plaques honouring Bradford women.


l Pinny Piece: At Mechanics Institute discover an appliquéd cotton apron by artist Caro Blount-Shah featuring 12 blue plaques honouring pioneering Bradford women and the Women’s Humanity League.

l 4,000 Miles to Home. A Tale from Punjab to Yorkshire: Artist talk on Akbal Singh Kang’s journey through photographs documenting migration, family, work and community life in Bradford.

l 150 Years of Hanson School: Stories of pupils, teachers, wartime histories, suffragette staff and educational reform.

l Bradford’s Cycling Heritage: Darley Street Market hosts displays on pioneering clubs, record-breaking riders, bicycle frame making, a film about Pennine Cycles, historic bikes, family activities and local cycling organisations.

l Origins of Football Culture in Bradford: Sports historian John Dewhirst explores rugby and football, early clubs, Park Avenue glory and huge crowds.
