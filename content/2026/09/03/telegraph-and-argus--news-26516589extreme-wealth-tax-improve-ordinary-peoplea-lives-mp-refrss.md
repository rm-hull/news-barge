**This column is written by Bradford East MP Imran Hussain**

The cost-of-living crisis has not gone away. For too many families across Bradford, making ends meet remains a daily struggle.

Whether it is the weekly food shop, energy bills, rent or mortgage payments, or simply the cost of getting to work, people are still feeling squeezed. Too many are working hard, doing everything that is asked of them, yet finding there is simply not enough money left at the end of the month.

That is why I welcome the clear focus the new Government has placed on tackling the cost of living.

The decision to cut VAT on domestic electricity bills will provide some help with energy costs. Bringing the bus fare cap down to £2 will make everyday journeys more affordable. It’s also good to see disabled people getting 24-hour free bus travel. Free bus travel for children this summer has also helped families at a time when the cost of keeping children entertained during the school holidays can quickly mount up.

There have also been welcome announcements to cut business rates for pubs, social clubs and live music venues and action to tackle subscription traps and misleading discounts that can leave consumers out of pocket.

These are positive steps and represent a welcome change of direction.

But the Prime Minister himself has acknowledged that they are not enough. He is right.

The scale of the cost-of-living crisis means the Government must go much further if we are going to give families the financial security they desperately need.

That is particularly true here in Bradford.

For years, I have highlighted the unacceptable levels of poverty in my constituency. Families were struggling before the recent cost-of-living crisis began, and successive economic shocks have only made their situation harder.

We cannot accept an economy where people go to work and still cannot afford the essentials. Nor should we accept a society where having a secure home, heating it properly or putting decent food on the table becomes increasingly difficult for ordinary working families.

Government can and must do something about this.

Housing has to be part of the answer. Rents have risen dramatically and housing costs now swallow an enormous proportion of many household budgets. We desperately need far more genuinely affordable and social housing, alongside action to ensure private rents do not continue rising beyond what ordinary people can afford.

Energy is another area where much more fundamental reform is needed. Cutting VAT provides immediate help, but ultimately we need to address why an essential service has become so expensive in the first place.

I welcome the Prime Minister’s recognition that stronger public control of essential services such as energy, water and transport can provide a more substantial answer to the cost-of-living crisis.

For decades, too many essential services have been organised around private profit rather than public need. Families have paid ever-increasing bills while shareholders and executives have benefited. We should have the confidence to challenge that failed model.

Public transport is a good example of what can be achieved. The £2 bus fare cap is welcome because affordable transport is not a luxury. It determines whether somebody can afford to travel to work, attend a hospital appointment, visit family or simply get into town.

But tackling the cost of living cannot just be about bringing individual bills down. We also need to ensure people have enough money to live on.

That means protecting and strengthening workers’ rights, driving up wages and tackling insecure employment. It also means ensuring our social security system provides a genuine safety net for people when they need it.

And we need to have an honest debate about who pays.

At a time when families are struggling with the basics, enormous wealth remains concentrated in the hands of a tiny number of people. I have long argued that those with the broadest shoulders should bear the greatest burden.

That is why I continue to support a 2% tax on extreme wealth above £10 million. Such a measure could raise billions that could be invested in our public services and used to improve the lives of ordinary people.

This is not about punishing success. It is about creating a fairer economy in which wealth contributes its fair share and working people are no longer repeatedly asked to carry the greatest burden.

The forthcoming Budget provides the Government with an important opportunity.

The measures announced over recent weeks are a good start. They show that government does not simply have to shrug its shoulders when people say everyday life has become unaffordable. Political choices can make a difference.

Now we need to build on them.

People in Bradford do not expect every problem to disappear overnight. But they rightly expect their Government to be on their side and to understand the pressures they face.

We need a sustained programme to bring down the cost of life’s essentials, build affordable and social homes, strengthen workers’ rights, improve wages and public services, and create a fairer tax system.

Families in Bradford have endured years of financial pressure. They deserve more than simply getting by from one month to the next.

They deserve some breathing space, financial security and the chance to enjoy life rather than constantly worrying about how they will pay the next bill.

The Government has made a welcome start. Now it must go further.
