Major high street names have been named and shamed in a government list for failing to pay some of their staff the minimum wage.

DIY retailer B&Q and fast food chain Five Guys are among 658 employers across the UK found to have underpaid workers, according to the Department for Business and Trade.

The businesses, which include shops, restaurants, nurseries, social care providers, and NHS trusts, were ordered to repay a total of £4 million in unpaid wages and have been fined £7 million.

A B&Q spokesperson said: "The shortfalls in payments were unintentional.

"They relate to calculations involving geographical allowances which are paid in addition to minimum hourly rates.

"All affected colleagues were quickly paid in full in July 2025."

B&Q underpaid 4,530 workers a total of more than £456,000, while Five Guys owed more than £54,000 to 3,699 staff.

Five Guys said the underpayments resulted from "technical differences in how payroll regulations were applied" and were identified during a review by HMRC.

The company said: "We worked closely and transparently with HMRC throughout the process and have made all required payments to affected current and former employees."

The government did not specify the time period over which the underpayments occurred.

Several nursing homes and multiple childcare providers were also named.

Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds said the government was committed to ending the practice of "short-changing your staff."

Mr Reynolds said: "The best businesses know that looking after your workers isn't just the right thing to do, it's the smart thing to do."

Kate Dearden, minister for the future of work, said: "Underpaying your staff is illegal, and we will not let workers foot the bill for their boss failing to follow the rules.

"Every employer should check their payroll now and reach out to Acas if they need further support."

The national minimum wage is currently £12.71 per hour for workers aged 21 and over, £10.85 for those aged 18 to 20, and £8 for under 18s and apprentices.
