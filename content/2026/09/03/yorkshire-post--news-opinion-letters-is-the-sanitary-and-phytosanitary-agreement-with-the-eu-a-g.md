# Is the sanitary and phytosanitary agreement with the EU a good deal? - Yorkshire Post Letters

## From: Peter Auty, Great Hatfield.

Is the sanitary and phytosanitary agreement with the EU a good deal or a bad deal? The UK is close to losing sovereignty over farming if the deal is agreed.

This government appears to be going for full alignment whereby farmers will have to abide by all EU agricultural rules and have no voice to change anything.

It appears that the government seems to have already agreed in principle as farmers are already being asked to follow the new rules, even though full negotiations have not been completed, this implies there are things they don't want the farmers to know until they have signed on the dotted line.

![A farmer sprays crops in the field. PIC: James Hardisty](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjE0MDBlY2U2LTJiNzYtNDVhMS05MzE1LWI0MzNkNTY0Mjk2MTphMzZjNjdiZC0yZTBiLTQ0ZDUtYTk3Yi00Mzc2NzVmNDVmMTE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



The UK badly needs a stand alone farming minister with a farming background, someone who understands rural and agricultural life (maybe a minister without portfolio), not career politicians without any hands on experience of the agricultural way of life, who when the going gets tough move on to another ministry leaving the usual mess behind.

Most farmers I have spoken with are, mostly, in the dark as to what they can actually do as some of the new rules already approved seem to be vague.

Can they use this fertiliser or that pesticide as using the wrong one could deem the crops unsaleable as the new rules take effect in the middle of 2027?

It's time everything was out in the open so that farmers can agree with the new rules and red tape, or not as they are the ones having to abide by them.
