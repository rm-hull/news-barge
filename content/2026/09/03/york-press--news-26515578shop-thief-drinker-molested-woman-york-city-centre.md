A once successful business owner is today a sex offender, thief and street drinker, York magistrates heard.

Damen Metcalfe, 37, sexually assaulted a woman outside a York city centre pub, and when staff took her into the bar, told them he was a “bad man” who had killed and made stabbing threats.

On other separate occasions, he hurled homophobic abuse at a police officer when arrested in Elland Road, Leeds, and pushed one shopworker out of the way and insulted a second during a spate of offending, said Antony Farrell, prosecuting.

Defence solicitor Adam Henry said Metcalfe had previously had a successful flooring business but then had had “some sort of setback in his life” and started drinking. He gave no more details about the “setback”.

“The sexual offence is directly related to alcohol consumption,” said Mr Henry.

Metcalfe now cannot be trusted with his own bank account, lives on benefits and when the money runs out, steals alcohol for himself and his street drinking companions to drink, the defence solicitor said.

Metcalfe, of Rosemary Court, Easingwold, pleaded guilty to sexual assault, using threatening words or behaviour towards a pubworker, two offences of using threatening words or behaviour towards shopworkers and one of using threatening words or behaviour towards a police officer, and theft of alcohol worth £12 from the Co-op on Clarence Street and £16 from Londis on Eastholme Drive, Rawcliffe. The offences were committed between March 19 and July 31.

Magistrates ordered him to abstain from drinking alcohol entirely until the end of the year.

As part of an 18-month community order, they said he must wear a tag for 120 days. It will alert the authorities if he drinks a drop of alcohol.

He must also do 25 days’ rehabilitative activities and pay a £160 fine for the homophobic abuse, £16 compensation to Londis and £12 to the Co-op, a £114 statutory surcharge and £85 prosecution costs.

He will be on the sex offenders’ register for five years.

Mr Farrell said Metcalfe was wobbling and shouting as he approached the woman outside the pub in the early hours last month and appeared to be drunk. He molested her sexually and told her she was “sexy”. His actions made her feel uncomfortable.

The homophobic incident happened in March and the thefts and related threatening words and behaviour offences in July.

Mr Henry said Metcalfe knows he needed help in dealing with his drinking, which had been happening for some years. He had the offer of a job in London, said the defence solicitor.
