MORE than 400 entries were submitted to this year’s Ampleforth Village Show, with organisers celebrating a rise in participation.

The annual event, organised by Ampleforth & District Gardening Club, attracted 306 adult entries on Saturday, September 19 – an increase of 85 on last year.

Children’s entries also rose, with 120 entries, five more than in 2025.

The event brought together villagers, families and competitors, with prizes presented by Reverend James Trowsdale before the show ended with a grand auction of entries offered by exhibitors.

Among the adult winners, Richard Lancaster took the fruit and vegetables prize for his plate of three onions, while Roger Beck won the flowers section with three dahlias.

Kate Beck was named best in photography for Reflections in Byland Pond, while Carolyn Smith won the handicrafts section with her pencil drawing The Green Man.

Lot Doucet took the preserves and drink prize for a jar of plum chutney.

Lucy Dobson was joint first in the gluten-free baking class, winning with both a Victoria sponge and lemon snow slices.

Roger Beck’s three dahlias were judged the best entry across the fruit, vegetable and flower sections.

His son Sam accepted the RHS Banksian Medal on his behalf.

Rob Scott topped the points table for the fruit, vegetable and flower classes with 25 points, earning the Shirley Halliwell Memorial Rose Bowl for one year.

The trophy was presented by Andy Halliwell, husband of the late Shirley Halliwell.

Mr Scott also topped the overall adult points table, scoring 35 points and receiving a £20 prize.

Sue Smith was second with 17 points and Amy Brooks third with 16.

There was plenty of success among the younger entrants too.

Dominic Dobson won the award for the overall best children's entry with his vegetable dragon in the eight-to-11 age group.

Organisers also highlighted the popularity of the animal painting and drawing classes across the three age groups.

The highest scorers were Harriet Hartley in the under-four category with eight points, Owen Dickie in the five-to-seven group with 18, and Matthew Dickie in the eight-to-11 group with eight.

The Gardening Club thanked everyone who entered, attended and helped organise the show, which ended with the traditional grand auction.
