US president Donald Trump has reportedly dismissed an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within a week.

Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi had presented the seven-day plan to American negotiators on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly. It stated that Tehran would reopen the key waterway and resume talks on its nuclear programme but only if the US lifts its naval blockade, waives oil sanctions and observes a ceasefire that includes Lebanon.

Araghchi told reporters at the United Nations: “The choice ‌now rests with the United States.”

## Iran awaits US response over Strait of Hormuz deal

Iran awaited an official US response to its proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end fighting in the wider Middle East war after the *Wall Street Journal* reported that US president Donald Trump had rejected the deal.

Iran raised the diplomatic initiative at the United Nations General Assembly in New York, saying it had been transmitted to the Americans via Qatari mediators and ⁠could reopen the strategic waterway and put an end to hostilities in the region within seven days.

Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araqchi told reporters on Friday the deal would trigger a seven-day countdown to reopening the strait and a pause in regional fighting.

If the US were to agree, ​the ⁠warring parties could start wider talks "on mutually agreed subjects," Araqchi said.
