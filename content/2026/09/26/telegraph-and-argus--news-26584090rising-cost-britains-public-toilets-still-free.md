A trip to the loo can cost as much as £1 in some parts of Britain, according to data from the Great British Public Toilet Map, with seaside resort Eastbourne topping the rankings for the nation's priciest public conveniences.

While the average charge for paid public toilets across the UK is 31p, people in Eastbourne face an average charge of £1 per visit, more than three times the national average.

Other towns with some of the highest toilet charges include Newbury (75p), Hackney and Norwich (60p) and Stornoway (40p).

![]()Public unisex toilet with green, blue and yellow doors and a sign in Braille for the blind and visually impaired at the seafront at Weymouth Seafront in Weymouth, Dorset, England, UK. *(Image: Getty Images)*

The data also showed:

* Penzance is Britain's ultimate pay-to-pee town, with its only mapped public toilet charging users.
* Blackpool has one of the highest proportions of paid facilities, with 74% of public toilets requiring payment.
* Boston (65%) and Lytham St Annes (65%) also rely heavily on charged toilets.
* Nationally, roughly one in seven public toilets (14.7%) now charge users.
* Some of the UK's most visitor-heavy destinations, including Westminster, Eastbourne, Scarborough and Norwich, rank among the most expensive places to answer nature's call.
