IN GEORGIAN Yorkshire, bearing even one child outside marriage could bring lifelong shame, so for Elizabeth Brown, who bore five or six illegitimate children, there must have been a lifetime of pointing fingers and wagging tongues.

When she died, aged 70 in 1836, Elizabeth had had enough. From beyond the grave she issued a defiant blast against the gossips and the rumour-mongers whose words had wounded her when she had been among them.

![]()Elizabeth Brown's weathering headstone in Kirkby Fleetham churchyard bears her withering message to her critics

Her headstone still stands in St Mary’s churchyard at Kirkby Fleetham, between Scorton and Northallerton, carrying an extraordinary rhyming denunciation of the critics of her lifestyle:

*Farewell vain world I’ve had enough of thee And now am careless what thou sayest of me
Thy smiles I court not nor thy frowns I fear
My cares are past and my head lies quiet here.
What faults you saw in me take care to shun
And look at home enough there’s to be done.
Where’er I lived or dy’d it matters not
To whom related or by whom begot
You now cannot ask no more of me
Tis all I am and all that you shall be.*

In Georgian Yorkshire there may not have been as many prudish moralisers expressing their dismay at her behaviour as there would have been later during Victorian times. Indeed, in a village like Kirkby Fleetham, where everyone knew everyone and everyone’s affairs, everyone probably had a shrewd idea who the father, or fathers, of Elizabeth’s fatherless children were.

![]()Elizabeth Brown is buried on the right hand side of St Mary's Church as you look through the gate

There may have been an understanding why Elizabeth had never married – perhaps the father was himself already married; perhaps the class gap between them was so great he could never bring himself to marry her.

And if, by working hard, she had managed to bring her children up without becoming an expense to ratepayers by resorting to the workhouse, there may have been grudging respect in the village about what she had achieved.

![]()St Mary's Church at Kirkby Fleetham, with Kirkby Fleetham Hall on the right

Yet her headstone proves that throughout her life she was followed by nudges and winks and questions about who was the father.

“It matters not, To whom related or by whom begot,” she says in her amazingly personal message. As well as bringing up six kids on her own did she study poetry by candlelight in her spare time?

This remarkable woman finishes by pointing out the universal truth that no matter how lowly an opinion her critics on the moral high ground had of her, they too would one day end up on the same level as her, buried six feet underground: “Tis all I am and all that you shall be.”
