*Meet the mum who’s the perfect fit to manage a new campaign to support disadvantaged areas of Darlington.* * PETER BARRON**reports*

IT’S Saturday morning in Red Hall Community Centre, and Sarah Small is busy doing what she loves most – volunteering with the grass roots dance company she set up more than 20 years ago.

The Red Hall Dance Company plays a vital role on the Darlington estate, and Sarah's surname is an incongruity because of the big impact she has on the local community.

As she tirelessly puts the children through their paces on the centre’s wooden floor, the song that’s playing happens to be You’ve Got A Friend In Me.

It fits the bill perfectly because Sarah is a role model for scores of families.

“All the kids love her – she’s so lovely with them. Her support is really appreciated,” says Amy Bowman, whose daughter, Emily, 13, has been attending dance classes since she was five.

![]()Sarah puts the children through their paces *(Image: Peter Barron)*

Stacey Hammond, who's run the company's tuck-shop as a fundraiser for the past eight years, is another who has no doubt about the dance company's value.

"Sarah's brilliant – she never stops," says Stacey, whose own commitment is underlined by the fact that she's at her table, selling sweets and drinks, straight from a night shift at a care home.

"If it wasn't for Sarah, all these children wouldn't be dancing today. It gives them something to do, and helps build their confidence."

Stacey's daughters, Lily-Rose, 13, and Heidi, 11, started dance lessons as soon as they could walk. "I can relax here and be myself," says Lily-Rose.

It's not only the dance school that represents Sarah's commitment to community life, but the 30 years she's devoted to girl guiding in Cockerton.

And it’s those local connections which make her the perfect fit to manage the Pride in Place initiative. Officially launched yesterday, it's a campaign to invest up to £20m of Government funding in the five most disadvantaged areas of Darlington: Skerne Park, Bank Top, Northgate, Branksome and Red Hall.

Sarah grew up in Cockerton, and having enjoyed being a brownie, she was a Young Leader in St Mary’s Guides by the time she was 16.

“From a young age, I just always wanted to help people," recalls Sarah, who is now Guider in Charge of the 3rd St Mary’s Rainbows and Brownies.

It's hard to think of anyone more embedded in the Darlington community. Like generations of Darlington folk, Sarah was a 'Greenbank baby' – born at the old Greenbank Maternity Hospital – and went to St Mary's Primary School and Hummersknott Comprehensive.

After studying Child Psychology With Science at Durham University, she got a job in a private nursery before becoming a play co-ordinator for the Red Hall Partnership in 2003.

Dancing had been part of her life since she was a tot, and launching the Red Hall Dance Company was a natural extension of her job.

![]()Sarah Small has run the Red Hall Dance Company for more than 20 years *(Image: Peter Barron)*

Today, 23 years later, the dance company has 104 members, ranging in age from three to 30.

It's not about competitions or qualifications – the focus is on fun, and practising for their next show at Hummersknott Academy on November 27 and 28.

Membership costs just £2 a week, with all the money being invested back into running costs.

"Dancing can be expensive, so it's about giving the children of Red Hall an opportunity to do what they love without a financial barrier," explains Sarah, who is also treasurer of the Darlington Dance Festival Association.

"They genuinely love it. It's so much more than just a dance school, it's the social side too – not just for the children but the parents.

"Over the years, so many friendships have been forged among people who probably wouldn't have met without the dance company."

On top of her job as the borough council's Darlington Partnerships Programme Manager, Sarah spends most of her Saturday's teaching six dance classes, plus another two on Tuesday evenings.

On Thursday evenings, she's looking after the rainbows and brownies at St Mary's Church Hall, in Cockerton.

Inevitably, her nine-year-old daughter, Ava, is a keen brownie and dance company enthusiast.

Sarah has also managed to fit in being a governor at Red Hall Primary School for the past eight years – four of them as chair.

Her CV also includes 10 years working as a play co-ordinator for Groundwork, plus a consultancy role for the council in which she focused on development work in Red Hall, Skerne Park, Branksome, and Northgate.

That led to her current role, and the responsibility for managing Pride in Place.

"I always wanted a job where you can see you're making a difference, and Pride in Place is a fantastic opportunity for us to do that here in Darlington," she says.

"In the world we live in, it's so important to support people to be the best version of themselves, and help them function.

"I'm often struck by how much people can achieve with very limited resources, and Red Hall is a great example of this. Having worked there for more than 20 years, I've had the privilege of knowing some truly remarkable individuals who are real assets to the community.

"When I first started working in Red Hall, there was a great community spirit. Everyone knew each other, but there's been a massive shift, noted by the residents themselves, and I'm hoping Pride in Place will bring back that sense of community."

Sarah cites the example of one estate where a woman organised bingo and coffee mornings, but it all stopped when she passed away because community life so often revolves around key individuals.

Branksome is given as another illustration. The estate used to have a thriving community centre but "it just fizzled out".

"Buildings have to be sustainable, and it's the people who do that," adds Sarah.

"We have to encourage more people to get involved and it''s vital that we listen to what local residents think, rather than impose ideas on them.

"We have to make sure every penny is spent in the right way, and that means listening to what local people feel is important to them.

"I want local residents to see change happening as a direct result of their input."

So, what does she hope Pride in Place will achieve over the next decade?

"It would be amazing if we could have community spaces run by committed volunteers, so that Pride in Place has a legacy way beyond it's 10-year lifespan.," she replies.

"From the very beginning, I've been supported by a group of dedicated volunteers whose commitment to the dance company has never wavered. Their passion, kindness and willingness to give their time have been invaluable, and the company simply wouldn't be what it is today without them.

"This is our chance to give local people a little bit of glue to make their ideas stick, come to life, and build a better future."
