Fiber is a critical component of our daily diet – but experts say 95 percent of Americans just aren’t getting enough.

The plant-based complex carbohydrate can help people lower cholesterol levels and reduce the risk of life-threatening heart attacks and strokes.

Experts say adults typically need between 25-30 grams daily for optimal health, though most adults currently consume just around 15 grams.

And with the rising cost of groceries, it may seem challenging and expensive to up your intake of fiber or fiber-rich products.

Fortunately, you don’t need to break the bank to add more fiber to your diet.

![Fiber is a crucial part of any diet. There are several affordable ways to get more without spending too much](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/25/12/36/GettyImages-2291383930-(1).jpeg)

## **Can it**

One tried and true method to get fiber and spend less is to buy canned goods.

Beans pack a quick nutritious punch, and are high in both protein and fiber.

Navy beans are the most fiber-rich, with over 20 grams per can, and a can of black beans has about 15 grams, according to *The Independent’s* review of several online products.

The cans often cost under $2.

“Like most legumes, black beans are versatile and inexpensive,” Cleveland Clinic registered dietitian Natalie Romito said in a statement.

“They shine in many types of dishes, and they’re great hot or cold.”

## **Stocked shelves**

Products that can last for awhile on a shelf can make for a quick fiber boost at morning, noon or night.

Steel-cut oatmeal can be served for a sweet or savory breakfast with fruit or eggs and veggies.

One packet of prepackaged oats has around four grams of fiber, according to the University of Rochester Medicine.

A box can cost under $6, depending on what kind you buy.

![A package of popcorn for movie night has around five grams of fiber](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/25/12/03/GettyImages-1438753646.jpeg)

Popcorn is a favorite fiber-rich snack — especially on movie night.

There’s around five grams of the snackable treat in a microwaveable package and a box costs less than $9.

## **Fruity fiber**

Some of the cheapest fruits at the grocery store are also the most fiber-rich.

One medium-sized banana has three grams of fiber, one medium-sized apple has 4.5 grams, a pear has 5.5 grams and a cup of raspberries has 8 grams, according to the Mayo Clinic.

None of these fruits are more than $1 individually.

Mixing together a fruit salad is a fun and delicious way to get more fiber fast, and adding slices as a pancake-topper or on ice cream can give a sweet treat a healthy upgrade.
