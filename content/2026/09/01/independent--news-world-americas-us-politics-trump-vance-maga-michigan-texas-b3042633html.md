A campaign event in Michigan featuring Vice President JD Vance on Monday was financed by MAGA Inc., marking a rare expenditure in a battleground state from a dominant political action committee that has largely stayed on the sidelines this year.

With just over two months remaining before the midterm elections, President Donald Trump continues to hold onto his massive campaign chest, opting to expand his reserves rather than deploy them.

Although the president and his team maintain that funding will be released soon, impatience is rising among Republicans striving to hold onto Congress.

The party faces significant hurdles, some tied to Trump himself; while the president remains a potent draw for core supporters, his approval numbers are historically low, driven by dissatisfaction with his handling of the economy and the conflict in Iran, which a majority of Americans consider unnecessary.

The declining time frame severely restricts options for party strategists, as television advertising becomes scarcer and far more expensive as Election Day approaches.

"Hope springs eternal," said Republican donor Dan Eberhart, echoing the sentiments of many party figures uncertain about the president’s intentions.

Texas has become a major concern. Trump endorsed state Attorney General Ken Paxton in his primary victory over incumbent Sen. John Cornyn, turning what was expected to be a secure Republican seat into a competitive battleground.

Democratic candidate James Talarico has dramatically outpaced Paxton in fundraising, securing over $68.5 million compared to Paxton’s $9 million. At the end of June, Talarico held more than $21.5 million in available cash, whereas Paxton had $1.75 million.

"We need help from the president," Sen. John Kennedy, a Louisiana Republican, said during an appearance on Fox News. "I’m hoping that the president is going to spend $100 or $200 million in Texas, but he hasn’t committed to it yet."

Campaign finance filings show MAGA Inc. held more than $403 million at the close of July, having raised an additional $3 million during the month while spending almost nothing. That leaves the committee with more cash on hand than any major party organization.

Its only notable expenditure thus far was in South Carolina, where the group directed nearly $830,000 toward a late voter turnout effort to help Sen. Darline Graham secure the Republican nomination following the death of her brother, Lindsey Graham.

That funding included approximately $774,000 for phone outreach and $53,500 for text messages.

Meanwhile, Trump has maintained an active fundraising calendar. He headlined a Republican National Committee event in Texas last Thursday, followed by MAGA Inc. meetings and a dinner at his Virginia golf club on Saturday.

The political action committee announced last week that it intends to host rallies across several swing states, kicking off with Vance's appearance in Michigan on Monday.

An operative familiar with the group’s plans, speaking anonymously to discuss internal strategy, confirmed that MAGA Inc. will spend funds in competitive contests.

That spending is expected to include commercials highlighting the president and his policies, an emphasis Trump has personally requested despite his low approval ratings, The Atlantic reported.

However, the delay introduces distinct challenges. Leaders at MAGA Inc. instructed vendors—including ad buyers, direct mail operations, and field organizers—to be ready in August, but sources familiar with private discussions say no comprehensive campaign blueprint has been shared.

These delays carry financial costs. Rates for broadcast spots are significantly higher now than in the spring, when competing organizations locked in airtime.

Strategists familiar with the ongoing planning indicate it is likely too late for independent committees to purchase television time in major Texas media markets, where Senate Republican leadership urged MAGA Inc. to intervene.

MAGA Inc. has test-run advertisements supporting Paxton, including a spot targeting Talarico over rural hospital policy, but the ad has not aired on television. Sources familiar with conversations indicated the content might run digitally or online instead.

Beyond traditional broadcast advertising, the group is considering alternative avenues. Following the brief primary runoff, team members focused on direct voter contact targeted at Trump loyalists who rarely participate in non-presidential elections.

According to sources aware of internal talks, a similar strategy is under consideration for Texas, focusing on direct outreach like door-to-door canvassing, text messaging, and direct mail targeting low-turnout voters. Operatives note that such field programs could be effective provided MAGA Inc. allocates substantial capital.

The president and his team insist that financial support is forthcoming.

"Of course," Trump told Punchbowl News when questioned about campaign funding. "I’m going to help Republicans."

James Blair, the former White House deputy chief of staff now managing Trump’s midterm operation, reiterated that message during a June appearance on "The Sean Spicer Show."

"The president is going to expend substantial resources to win the midterms," Blair said. "He cares deeply about the party winning, and anything he can do to support that he’s going to do and is already doing."

Still, Trump has clear reasons to preserve his treasury. As a president barred from seeking another term, keeping a dominant cash reserve allows him to maintain political leverage over the party. He has shown he can defeat primary opponents, and future candidates will likely keep traveling to Mar-a-Lago seeking his endorsement.

As Trump told Punchbowl News about his cash reserves: "I can spend it on pretty much anything I want."
