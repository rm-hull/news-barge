A YORK councillor has called for urgent cross-party action to tackle the issues around sewage pollution in the city's rivers - which are among the worst in the country.

Cllr Christian Vassie, who represents Wheldrake for Liberal Democrats, has said that national and local government must act together to create a plan that will stop sewage overflows in the city's rivers.

He said: "We all know what’s wrong. We kind of strut around parading our indignation, but what does it actually deliver?

"We could scrap bonuses for Yorkshire Water tomorrow, but would that stop the single gram of sewage going in a river? I don't think so - it's all the difficult stuff has yet to be done."

He pointed to a 2010 government act on flood and water management that remains non-compulsory in England - in contrast to Wales, where it has been enforced for several years.

Cllr Vassie said: "I think it was six years ago that the act was made compulsory in Wales, so they’re beginning to see the fruits of change in the way they build, the way they develop land, the way they manage water, but here we’re still just talk."

He described a planning meeting last year where he raised concerns about separating surface water from sewage at a new 700-home development in Haxby.

The councillor was told it was "too complicated" and "too late to do anything" - something he said is concerning with a number of new developments, including 3000 home Langwith village, on the horizon.

He added: "These are the moments that really matter for York. Now, it has to be right from the start - if we get that wrong, that's a whole load of new water just pouring into our drains and then into our rivers."

He believes City of York Council could adopt a more robust sustainable urban drainage (SuDS) design guide, similar to the one used in Cambridge, which he believes would push developers into more sustainable action - as well as lobbying central government to make the act compulsory in England too.

Cllr Vassie said: "It'll educate people as to how things are done rather than give people excuses for not doing anything here in York.

"It's so frustrating, watching my fellow councillors queuing up to wave placards and the posters when as politicians, we have the power to change things and set the rules.

"As councillors and the administration can do is to bring this into our planning framework - we can't change the law nationally, we can do this. York should also be using our MP's to call on the government to make the flood water management tax compulsory."

Cllr Vassie also highlighted recent weather warnings from the Met Office, which predict heavier rainfall later this year due to El Niño - something which he says could lead to even more frequent sewage overflows if action isn’t taken - and shared images of a fatberg that he spotted in the Foss.

He said: "We know now what El Nino is going to do - we're going to have our rivers flooded with more sewage.

"As the rain pours onto our roads, the urgent of doing this is now. I'm happy to work with anyone to do this and I think all the parties should work together.

"It's going to impact on the whole country more and more, so we need to do something together that people in 50 years will be proud of."
