A YORK woman ditched her Krispy Kreme habit to lose 13 stone on Mounjaro and has saved £4,000 in a year by cutting out Uber Eats.

Rebecca Miller, 37, weighed 26st 8lb at her heaviest and snacked in the evening on large pasta bowls followed by sugary treats.

She'd previously tried various diets but decided to give Mounjaro a go in June 2025 - after seeing her family have success with the jabs.

She was able to cut out her favourite Krispy Kreme doughnuts and she started swimming three times a week and eating more protein alongside the jabs to shred 12st 11lbs.

Now a size 14 and 13st 11lbs, she's also been able to save £4,000 a year from cutting out snacks and cutting back on delivery services.

Rebecca, who works in human relations, from York, said: “I tried diets before, I've gone low carb, I've gone calorie controlled. You name it. I'll lose a stone then I will put on two stone, it was always backwards and forwards, backwards and forwards.

  Rebecca Miller, before and after she lost weight. Picture: SWNS


"I’ve saved over £4,000 since I started my weight loss journey. I am using that money to take a trip with my mum and sister next year to Boston and Salem in the USA to celebrate my sister’s 40th birthday.

"This shows that although Mounjaro is expensive, it’s still less expensive than what I used to spend on food.

"A lot of money went on snacks previously and on delivery services if I couldn’t be bothered to cook or if I wanted a weekend treat.

"I still do treat myself from time to time, but they are actual treats now rather than a weekly or daily occurrence."

  


Rebecca knew her weight was an issue but couldn't escape her cycle.

She said: "My old diet was mostly made up of carbs and sugar.

"Cereal, donuts, sweets, chocolate, pizza etc. I would usually only eat in the evening and would have a big meals and then graze on sweet treats. I had a special fondness for Krispy Kreme donuts.

"It wasn’t everyday, but I could easily eat half a dozen in one sitting."

  Rebecca Miller, after she lost weight. Picture: SWNS


Rebecca believed she was spending around £200 a month on food delivery services.

She was scared to step on the scales but when she decided to start Mounjaro she had to weigh herself. She decided to go on the jabs after her mum and sister saw "good results" on them.

She told Sell Us Your Story: "I knew it was bad and I hadn't wanted to weigh myself, but when I decided to start Mounjaro last June I had to weigh myself.

“Embarrassingly, I knew it was going to be bad but I didn't realise how bad it was going to be.

“I was way bigger than what I thought I was, horrified, and I knew at that moment, I didn't have any health problems or anything, I had no high blood pressure, no diabetes, no nothing.

"But I knew that in another 10 years, I probably would have.

"My sister and mum were using them and had good results. I also knew that I was getting older and, although I didn’t currently have any health issues, I knew I would very soon if I didn’t do something about my weight."

Rebecca pays £275 every four weeks for a private prescription - and started noticing a difference after sticking with the jabs and a new exercise regime.

  Rebecca Miller, before she lost weight. Picture: SWNS 

*(Image: Rebecca Miller / SWNS)*

She said: “So I started taking Mounjaro and I did exactly what they tell you to do. I focused on protein, focused on hydration and I just really became aware of what I was eating."

Rebecca said despite taking the jabs, it was hard work to keep up her healthy habits and now she eats a lot less beige food and has cut out carbs and sugar.

She said: “I have worked really, really hard but I think there is this misconception that people have about Mounjaro in that it loses the weight for you.

“It doesn't do that. I am still hungry all the time. I want to eat all the time, but I've made the choice that actually being healthier is what I want to do more.

“I go swimming three times a week and I have started training for the couch to 5K now.

“I can cross my legs and tie my shoelaces, all these things that I couldn't do before. It doesn't make those healthy choices for you. It doesn't do the exercise for you.

“It does not deal with the warped vision of your body for you and it doesn't improve your, self-confidence for you, all of that has to come from you.”

Rebecca said that when people have noticed her weight loss they have a negative reaction after she explains she is on Mounjaro.

She said: "There is almost a taboo. People obviously have noticed that I've lost some weight and then when I say I am on Mounjaro they go, oh, right. Oh, okay, then.

"As if I've just skirted around all the hard work and it's not true."

Rebecca said she is currently on a 10mg dose and still has around two stone to go to reach her goal weight.

She said: "I will consider using a maintenance dose once I reach my goal to help maintain my weight loss if needed."
