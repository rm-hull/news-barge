Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has accused Democrats of using rhetoric to unfairly demonize Immigration and Customs Enforcement and undermine President Donald Trump’s immigration agenda.

Speaking at a press conference in New York on Tuesday to announce the results of “Operation Rotten Apple” — a month-long statewide sweep targeting non-citizens with criminal records — Mullin argued that public backlash against federal immigration authorities was being driven primarily by political opponents rather than the agency’s actions.

“The Democrats throw out all these fearmongels to try to make ICE and President Trump’s administration look as worse as possible,” Mullin said. “Unfortunately, we have the left media that buys into that. I was being very, very clear when I said ICE isn’t there to patrol the polling places.

“We’re there to enforce specific threats. The only reason why we would be at polling places if there is a threat to that polling place or we’re serving a warrant on someone that we have been actively tracking down.”

The remarks come amid heightened scrutiny of ICE following a series of contentious aggressive tactics, workplace raids and mass detainment operations executed under the Trump administration.

![Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin accused Democrats of leveraging political rhetoric to vilify Immigration and Customs Enforcement and sabotage President Donald Trump’s immigration policies](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/01/19/2026-09-01T161213Z_53057502_RC2EANADS0HT_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-MIGRATION.JPG)

Civil rights organizations and Democratic lawmakers have repeatedly criticized the agency over allegations of due process violations, family separations and the execution of arrest warrants without proper judicial oversight.

“ICE’s job is immigration custom enforcement,” Mullin said. “That’s their job. And so, if we’re doing something, we’re doing that specifically for immigration custom enforcement.”

The agency’s operational conduct has faced intense national focus following two separate fatal shootings by federal agents in Minneapolis earlier this year. Renée Good, 37, was shot and killed inside her vehicle by an ICE agent on January 7, while Alex Pretti, a 37-year-old ICU nurse, was shot multiple times and killed weeks later on January 24 by Customs and Border Protection officers deployed alongside immigration enforcement teams.

Separately, opponents have voiced concern over the conditions within federal detention facilities, pointing to reports of overcrowding, inadequate medical care and restricted access to legal representation for detainees.

During his confirmation proceedings earlier this year, Mullin faced intense questioning from Senate Democrats over previous statements defending controversial ICE operations, as well as proposals that critics argue infringe on local authority in sanctuary cities.

Despite the growing pushback from advocacy groups, DHS leadership has maintained a hardline stance.

Mullin’s comments reflect the administration’s ongoing defense of ICE, casting public criticism of the agency as a calculated political tactic by Democrats rather than a response to its enforcement practices.

Critics pushed back on the secretary’s comments.

“It’s the truth,” social media user Christina Fleming wrote in response on X. “ICE has murdered a couple of people.”

Another user, @kered4321, argued that “ICE and the Trump Administration don’t need Democrats’ help to look as worse as possible.”

Others cited reports of recent violence involving federal agents, with account Fozon Capital pointing to fatal shootings by federal immigration enforcement officers.

“ICE literally gunned down an unarmed woman,” the account posted on X. “Then did the same to a legal concealed carry man with his hands up and weapon holstered before being shot 10 times in the back and the back of his head. Go back to hiding behind your fake leather chair.”
