Former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer has announced his resignation as an MP in a shock move, setting his successor Andy Burnham up for a fresh by-election.

Sir Keir’s decision to stand down from his role as MP for Holborn and St Pancras will trigger a by-election in the constituency just months after he stood down as prime minister.

His announcement comes ahead of Mr Burnham’s first address as prime minister in the House of Commons as Parliament returns from recess.

The speech will focus on the cost-of-living crisis as the Labour leader makes a case for bringing more essential services under public control, according to reports in *The Guardian*. 

Mr Burnham has announced a range of policies over the summer that have largely gone uncontested but will now face scrutiny by MPs.

Chancellor John Healey has also promised to hike UK defence spending after Donald Trump said he was reviewing the United States’ position on the British sovereignty over the Falkland islands.

The US president is reportedly willing to oppose British rule of the overseas territory as an incentive to encourage Mr Burnham to increase defence spending.

## Count Binface hints at another by-election run after Starmer resigns as MP

Count Binface has hinted he could run in a by-election in Holborn and St Pancras after Sir Keir Starmer announced he will step down as MP.

Minutes after news broke of Sir Keir’s exit from the House of Commons, the self-styled “intergalactic space warrior” took to social media where he posted “Can't get a moment's peace on this bloody planet” in apparent response to news of the incoming by-election.

It would be the novelty candidate’s third by-election of the year after he made waves in the Clacton-on-Sea standoff against Nigel Farage where he stole 26.9 per cent of the vote from the Reform UK leader.

He also stood against Andy Burnham in Makerfield in June, where he gained just 95 votes in the landslide victory for the current prime minister.

## Fiscal discipline 'bedrock' of economy's stability, Downing Street insists as borrowing soars

No 10 said fiscal discipline was the “bedrock” of the economy’s stability after the government’s long-term borrowing costs soared to their highest levels for 28 years.

The prime minister’s official spokesman said: “We don’t comment on markets. More generally, I would just say that fiscal discipline is the bedrock of economic stability and national security.

“The chancellor and the prime minister are in lockstep that the government will meet the fiscal rules with a buffer against uncertainty and we’re cutting the deficit faster than any other G7 economy to the lowest level in six years.”

He would not say what size buffer the prime minister was looking to secure.

## Starmer 'left the country in a better place than he found it,' former minister says

Former minister Nick Thomas-Symonds paid tribute to Sir Keir Starmer after he announced he was quitting the Commons.

Mr Thomas-Symonds, who served as EU relations minister and paymaster general in Sir Keir’s government, said on X: “Keir led Labour from its worst defeat since 1935 to a landslide victory in 2024.

“His work as prime minister left the country in a better place than he found it.

“Thank you, Keir, for all your service & all best wishes to you & your family for the future.”

## Burnham thanked Cabinet for 'strong sense of momentum' in first meeting

Andy Burnham kicked off Tuesday morning’s Cabinet meeting by thanking his colleagues for establishing “a strong sense of momentum” in his first few weeks as prime minister.

In a Downing Street readout of the meeting, his official spokesman said: “The prime minister opened cabinet by thanking colleagues for establishing a strong sense of momentum in his first weeks in office.

“He said the country have noticed a change of pace with the government setting the agenda and starting the process of giving local areas the levers they need to create good growth.

“He said that during his summer tour, it was clear that life was hard for many people, that they wanted to have hope and hear a positive outlook for the country.”

He thanked Justice Secretary Alex Norris for his work reviewing the prisoner early release scheme and Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood for her work on small boat crossings.

## Green leader Zack Polanski has not ruled out contesting in upcoming by-election

Zack Polanski has not ruled out contesting the Holborn and St Pancras seat following Sir Keir Starmer’s resignation, the Green Party has said.

The Green leader is currently a member of the London Assembly.

Asked by Sky News if Mr Polanski will contest the Holborn and St Pancras seat, a Green Party spokesperson did not rule it out.

They said: "Zack has always made it clear he would like to be a London MP.

"London has been his home for over 20 years."
