## Disabled people face fares at some York stops despite free travel extension

York disability rights activist Flick Willams on a bus *(Image: Flick Williams)*

DISABLED people boarding buses at some York stops face having to pay fares despite a pledge to make travel free all day.

A concessionary fare of £1.40 is charged to pass holders if they board a bus at one of York’s Park & Ride sites but they can travel for free from any other stop.

Disability rights activist Flick Williams said concessionary pass holders unaware of the charges but whose closest bus stop was a Park & Ride were being caught out.

A spokesperson for City of York Council, which runs the city’s Park & Rides, said concessionary fares were charged there to cover the cost of additional facilities such as toilets and waiting areas.

It follows a Government announcement that concessionary bus passes for disabled passengers would be valid all day, every day from April in a move backed by £60 million.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said buses were a lifeline for so many people and it was not fair that passes were only valid at certain times of the day.

Liberal Democrat Harrogate and Knaresborough MP Tom Gordon campaigned for the changes and York councillors also called for them to be made in November.

Passes are currently only valid nationally from 9.30am to 11pm, though morning hours in York were previously extended to 9am.

Council Labour transport spokesperson Cllr Kate Ravilious said disabled people did not live their lives according to bus timetables and had to get to work, hospital and meet other commitments.

Activist Ms Williams said the move was welcome, though her and others feared any savings could be wiped out by future welfare cuts.

But she added the council, which has charged concessionary fares at Park & Rides following law changes in 2011, needed to show it was committed to free travel for disabled people.

Ms Williams told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): “People use the Park & Rides all the time by those who don’t have stops near them.

“If they get on the bus at a Park & Ride they’re charged but they’re going to them on the bus or get on at the next stop along the route from the site it’s free.

“They might be impaired and drive to the Park & Ride before getting on the bus, but if they don’t know about the fares then they might just drive into town.

“I fail to see why there should be any distinction between travelling from a Park & Ride or any other bus stop.”

The council’s spokesperson said they were working with the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority which is set to take over public transport powers.

The combined authority’s remit is set to include managing the changes to bus pass hours.

The council’s spokesperson said: “While the current concessionary fare of £1.40 is charged for the bus journey, it is actually to cover the cost of various additional facilities available at Park & Ride sites.

“Concessionary pass holders can use their pass for free when boarding at any other stops across the city.”

The Government is set to work with local authorities and bus operators ahead of next year’s changes to ensure they understand how and where free travel will apply.
