The early release scheme was launched in September to tackle overcrowding

**The number of available spaces across the male prison estate in England and Wales has fallen to its lowest this year, sources have told the BBC.**

As of 1 September, there were about 1,500 free spaces, just under half the number available at the start of June, which Ministry of Justice (MoJ) figures put at 3,040.

Justice Secretary Alex Norris is due to give more details in the Commons later on changes to the early-release scheme, which aims to free up space in prisons as they near maximum capacity.

It comes as Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced changes to the scheme, which will mean more prisoners, including those convicted of manslaughter, will remain behind bars for longer.

MoJ figures show the number of available male prison spaces has been steadily falling since the middle of June, with the last official figures released on 24 August placing them at 1,665.

This week's numbers have not been officially released due to Monday's bank holiday but prison sources say vacancies have fallen once again.

The government has said it would create more cells in existing prisons in England and Wales as part of its plans to tackle overcrowding.

![A graph showing the number of vacant male prison spaces in England and Wales falling from over 3,000 in June to 1,500 in September.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1280/cpsprodpb/372d/live/524418e0-a613-11f1-b109-879e35c24276.png)

An investment of £110m, which will come from existing budgets, will be used to convert unused office space, laundry rooms and storage space into cells, the MoJ said.

The justice secretary told the BBC prisoners being released early did not mean "freedom" and victims would have a say in restrictions placed on those criminals.

Asked where clothes would be washed if laundry rooms were converted into cells, Norris told BBC Breakfast: "Of course we're going to make sure that the prisons can still function properly.

"This is a creative look, as I say, around our estate and being able to use some space that we can re-prioritise."

Norris said he suspected the number of extra prison spaces created from freeing up existing space within prisons would be "landing somewhere in the middle" of 200 and 900.

But the chairman of the Prison Officers' Association, Mark Fairhurst, said: "You can create as much additional space as you like, and build as many new prisons as you like, but where are the staff coming from to supervise people? Nobody wants to be a prison officer anymore."

Prisons in England and Wales are running at 97% capacity.

Under the early release scheme some 4,500 prisoners are now expected to start being let out of prison in tranches from October.

On Sunday, the prime minister told BBC Breakfast anyone convicted of manslaughter would be excluded from the scheme, following a campaign from PC Andrew Harper's family to block the release of two men convicted over his killing in 2019.

Extra prison space would also be created by deporting more foreign prisoners and releasing some inmates serving indefinite sentences.

Government figures show there were 10,134 foreign nationals held in custody on 30 June, 3,283 of whom were on remand and would not be eligible for deportation.

In the year to June, 5,962 foreign offenders were sent back to their home countries - the highest rate of deportations since 2017 and a 12% increase on the previous year.
