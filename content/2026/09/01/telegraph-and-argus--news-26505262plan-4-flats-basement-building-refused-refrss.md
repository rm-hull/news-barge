PLANS to turn the basement of a Conservation Area building into four flats have been refused, with one officer saying the application includes “a total lack of relevant and accurate information.”

Bradford Council has refused plans to create one bed flats in the basement of 3 – 5 Parkfield Road, a building in the St Pauls Conservation Area of Manningham.

Officers said the proposals, by Aliya Bahadur, provided “an inadequate level of amenity” for future residents.

And they also criticised the fact that a heritage statement included with the plan seemed to refer to a completely different building.

The application acknowledged that while the development would cause harm to the Conservation Area: “This harm is demonstrably outweighed by the public benefits of the proposal, including the provision of new residential accommodation and the enhancement of the long-term viability of the listed building.”

## Building already homes

The building is currently divided into separate homes.

If plans had been approved, the basement would be divided into a further four flats.

  But several different council departments raised serious concerns about the proposals.

  Housing standards officers said: “A key concern with basement flats is the lack of outlook and natural light from windows.

“The proposed plan shows that the majority of rooms would benefit from a window, though their position below ground level means they would not provide adequate levels of light or outlook.

“Although the proposed light wells to the front would help some light enter the kitchen/living areas of flats A and C, this would still not provide sufficient outlook or light to all flats in a manner that would facilitate an acceptable standard of living.

“The use of the basement as four self-contained flats would therefore not be appropriate.”

## More concerns raised

Highways officers said the number of flats proposed for the whole building would require around 10 parking spaces. The existing layout would allow, at a push, five cars to safely park on the grounds.

And Conservation Officers also had concerns.

They pointed out that changes to the property over a number of years had already harmed the Conservation Area, saying: “The property has an open and somewhat unsightly frontage following removal of several mature trees.

“This is in contrast to the generally well screened and consistent gardens along the road with mature trees and scrubs contributing to the character of the conservation area.”

They said: “The heritage statement refers to a different property and is totally irrelevant.

“The plans showing the extent and layout of the proposed lightwells are inadequate. No plans whatsoever are provided of the new exposed basement elevations or the retaining structures.

“There is a total lack of relevant and accurate information to assess the application.”
