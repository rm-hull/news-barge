A furious group of Darlington residents say they're having sleepless nights after being charged nearly £40,000 for urgent repairs to a wall that have yet to be carried out three years later.

To make matters worse, the residents on the Polam Hall Estate say they've now been asked to cough up a further £34,000 for the works, despite saying they have never been shown proof they own, or are legally responsible for, the wall.

The leaseholders also say they've received no revised quote or detailed breakdown of costs for the work from Kingston Property Services, management agent for freeholder Persimmon.

  Residents and the damaged wall. Anja Smith and Glenyce Toole front left. 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Kingston says it is aware of residents' concerns. It says it has held residents' £40,000 in reserve since work stalled in 2024 due to "observations raised regarding responsibility for the wall and the interpretation of the lease" and has only recently had approval from Persimmon to allow works to proceed. Persimmon says it takes concerns "extremely seriously".

Anja Smith, who lives in Chelsea Court, said: "They've taken our money and we have to pay in line with our lease, even though the wall is not in our lease and there is no evidence that the wall is our responsibility.

"I have sleepless nights over it.

"I get so wound up."

The group of residents say that in September 2023, Kingston recovered £39,279.31 for urgent repairs to a boundary wall behind the Lombard Court, Chelsea Court, Dorchester Court, Buckingham Court and Stratton Court apartment blocks.

### The location of the boundary wall


They said KPS insisted they had evidence of leaseholder responsibility but later charged the residents for a surveyor to investigate ownership.

In March 2026, the group of around sixty residents, were told they might need to pay an extra £34,510, bringing the total to almost £74,000 between them.

   

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Anja has created a petition in response to the further charges, which has been signed by 29 leaseholders.

Glenyce Toole says she was forced to move out due to the stress of the situation.

The 70-year-old said: "I became so ill.

"I was absolutely stressed out of my head.

"I had to go to the doctor's and I was put on medication.

"I told my husband I couldn't do it anymore, I couldn't cope with it.

"We'd put a lot of money into the flat and made it our home.

"We're retired, we were never going to move from there and all of a sudden, we're in a position where we're not going to get that much money back for it.

"We ended up living with relatives and we had to buy a holiday lodge, so we spent some of the time there.

  Glenyce Toole, third from left. 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

"I've been left virtually homeless.

"I don't think they realise how much of an impact this has had.

"It's not fair, someone has to stand up to them."

Leaseholders say they have been informed that the proposed works for the wall have now been approved by Persimmon Homes, the freeholder.

However, no further consultation has occurred, and no evidence has been provided showing that leaseholders own or are legally responsible for the boundary wall.

Leaseholders are also worried the wall has not been properly inspected, repaired, or maintained for well over a decade or even longer, letting it deteriorate to the point of being at risk of collapse.

Anja, a business manager, added: "If they prove the wall is our responsibility then fine but they haven't maintained it up until this point.

"Because they haven't maintained it, it's now brittle, and they want us to pick up the bill."

She feels Persimmon Homes and Kingston Property Services have often ignored her emails.

   

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

The 58-year-old said: "I've pushed this matter and I went from pillar to post through Persimmon.

"I went to customer service, I went to senior management and the matter just gets passed around, I don't know what to do anymore.

"I kept asking, what are you going to do with the money you already have? Why have you asked for more money? Where are the reports to prove it is our responsibility?

"But we just get passed onto someone else.

A Kingston spokesperson said: “We are aware of the concerns raised by leaseholders at Polam Hall Estate regarding the boundary wall. Significant work has been undertaken in recent years to investigate the condition of the wall with the aim of progressing repair works on behalf of our client, the freeholder.

“Following the initial Section 20 consultation process, a number of observations were raised regarding responsibility for the wall and the interpretation of the lease. As a result, matters stalled and a chartered land and building surveyor was instructed to provide an independent assessment.

“The surveyor’s findings were subsequently provided to the freeholder in September 2024, and approval was requested to progress the repair works. Approval has only recently been received by the freeholder to allow works to proceed. Initial funds collected towards the proposed works remain held in a reserve fund for the development.

“Due to the time that has elapsed since the original tender exercise, a fresh tender has to now be undertaken, and a new Section 20 consultation carried out before works can proceed.

“As managing agent, our role is to act on behalf of our client and oversee the consultation, procurement and communication processes associated with major works. We are required to seek approval to proceed with works and are unable to do so until this is received. We remain committed to keeping residents informed as the project moves forward.”

A Persimmon Teesside spokesperson said: “We take the concerns raised by residents extremely seriously which is why we have undertaken a detailed review of the matter working with Kingston Property Services who are the management agent for this site.
