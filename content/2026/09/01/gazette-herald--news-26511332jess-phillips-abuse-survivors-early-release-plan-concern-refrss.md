The former safeguarding minister has said it is “utterly unforgiveable” that a North Yorkshire domestic abuse survivor was told her abuser would be released from prison early as he was not jailed for a serious crime.

Helen Duncanson said she was told by the government that her abuser, Michael Boyes, who was jailed for three and a half years, would serve a third of his sentence as it was not a serious crime.

Ms Duncanson told The Press she felt her safety had been “overlooked”, after it was revealed that domestic abusers would be among the offenders released from jail early under the plans to ease overcrowding in prisons.

She urged ministers to reconsider including domestic abusers in the early prison release scheme.

The Ministry of Justice said it is working to fix a prison system it inherited “on the brink of collapse” and shares “the public’s anger that these options are even being considered”.

  Michael Boyes who was jailed on Tuesday (January 13) 

*(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Boyes, of Ruffa Lane, Pickering, was jailed for three and a half years on his 40th birthday in January for abusing Ms Duncanson over a period of one year and eight months

Prosecutor Michael Cahill said Boyes, who has more than 20 previous convictions including for domestic violence, attacked Ms Duncanson and tried to strangle her with her own necklace.

Ms Duncanson said Boyes, her former partner, subjected her to “mental torture” that “was worse than the physical injuries”, but she felt “safe” and has started to rebuild her life after he was jailed.

However, Ms Duncanson said she has since been told by the Probation Service that Boyes is due to be released from jail in September 2027.

  Domestic abuse survivor Helen Duncanson who has urged ministers to rethink proposals to release domestic abusers from jail early 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Speaking on ITV’s Good Morning Britain, Ms Duncanson said she has not been given a release date for Boyes, besides being told he will be released from jail early.

Ms Duncanson said she felt her safety is “all up in the air” and “with this lack of communication” felt Boyes “is going to be released and they’re not going to let me know”.

Boyes was jailed after pleading guilty to assault occasioning actual bodily harm and engaging in controlling or coercive behaviour three weeks before his trial was due to begin.

Ms Duncanson questioned how the case had not been deemed a serious crime, telling Good Morning Britain: “If this isn’t a serious crime, seconds away from death as he is strangling you, I want to know what is.”

  Former safeguarding minister Jess Phillips 

*(Image: Jonathan Brady/PA Wire)*

Jess Phillips, the minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls in Sir Keir Starmer’s government, told the programme it was “utterly unforgiveable” for Ms Duncanson to be told it was not a serious crime.

“The level of seriousness, the level of torture that people undergo I think is grossly underestimated by the system,” Ms Phillips said of domestic abuse cases.

“And for Helen to be told that it wasn’t a serious crime, frankly, is utterly unforgiveable – but does speak a little bit, I think, to the broader problem of it not being taken seriously.

“… Nobody underestimates that we need to be able to put people, like Helen’s vile torturer, into prison in the first place. And so we need prison places and there is a crisis.

“All said, that is not Helen’s responsibility. That is the responsibility of the state and of the organisations in the criminal justice system to ensure that when we have to do these things, Helen is at the absolute heart of it.

“… There are so many points throughout this when we do not place the victim at the heart of it, but place the perpetrator above that of the victim.”

Ms Phillips said Probation Service systems have been “woefully degraded” and the government must tackle overcrowding in prisons.

She said she called for domestic abusers whose cases involve assault occasioning actual bodily harm or grievous bodily harm to be flagged while in government when being considered for early prison release, but that "has not happened".

The former minister said “that is what has to happen now. And I am 100 per cent behind what Helen is trying to do.”

## 'It may be release, but it isn't freedom' - justice secretary

Justice secretary Alex Norris sought to reassure survivors whose perpetrators are still due for early release by saying restrictions such as tagging and restriction zones will be designed around victims’ lives.

He told Good Morning Britain: "It may be release, but it isn't freedom.

"These are the most significant controls [being put on offenders after they are released from jail], and we are committed to working with victims in getting that right."

Mr Norris added: "I accept that victims, survivors in the community generally will not be happy to hear people coming out a minute sooner.

"I would have to be clear here, we are living in a permacrisis in prison places that has existed for many years now and that we are determined to break.

"And this sentencing, as well as our building programme, which is the biggest since the Victorian era, is our way of solving that.

"But I appreciate that will leave a degree of dissatisfaction."

A Ministry of Justice spokesperson said: “We have always stated we wanted to go further, and we are fast-tracking a plan to keep more of the most dangerous offenders behind bars. That’s on top of murderers and those serving sentences for rape, serious child sexual abuse and grooming offences who are already blocked from any changes.

“We share the public’s anger that these options are even being considered, after we inherited a prison system on the brink of collapse. We are fixing this by building 14,000 new prison places, expanding electronic tagging on an unprecedented scale, and investing in probation services at record levels.

“We are also exploring how we can go further, including faster deportation of foreign criminals and reviewing the unjust IPP sentences.”
