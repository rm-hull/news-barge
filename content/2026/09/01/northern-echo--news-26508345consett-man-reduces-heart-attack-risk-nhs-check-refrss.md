A County Durham man was left shocked after being told that he had a one in five chance of having a heart attack or stroke in the next decade, despite thinking he was 'broadly healthy'.

Ben Aitken, from Consett, was 42 when he attended an NHS health check at his GP in February 2022.

He expected to be told he was broadly healthy, but instead learned his risk of suffering a heart attack or stroke in the following 10 years was more than 18 per cent.

Mr Aitken said: “When I was told of my risk of a heart attack or stroke, my greatest fear was that I would die.

  Ben Aitken, from Consett 

*(Image: Joe Hall/BHF)*

“It was so much of a shock that I can’t even remember walking out of the doctor’s surgery. I went to my car, and I burst into tears.

“Everything rushed through my head. I thought about my husband, David, who is deaf and relies on me for a lot of things, like telephone calls, doctor’s appointments and hospital checks. How would he manage if I was ill, or died?”

The warning prompted Ben, who is now 46, to make immediate changes to his health.

He had smoked around 20 cigarettes a day since his early twenties, but began using nicotine patches and vaping before attending a stop-smoking clinic and quitting completely.

  Ben Aitken, from Consett 

*(Image: Joe Hall/BHF)*

He added: “I had never had much self-control, but the reality-check I got at the doctor’s changed everything. It gave me the will to stop.”

He was also prescribed the weight-loss medication Orlistat for two months and followed a low-fat diet, losing around two stone, or 13kg, during that period.

After stopping the medication, he joined a weight-loss group and continued changing what he ate and cooked.

He said: “I absolutely love cooking, it's one of my big passions, but I changed what I was buying and cooking.

“I enjoyed it and saw it as a challenge. Rather than seeing it as having to give up things, I was enjoying the changes I was making in my life.”

  Ben Aitken, from Consett 

*(Image: Joe Hall/BHF)*

In the 13 months after his health check, Ben lost a total of 38kg.

A follow-up appointment found his estimated 10-year risk of heart attack or stroke had fallen from more than 18 per cent to 4.3 per cent in just 10 months.

Almost four years later, Ben continues to follow his healthier lifestyle and takes statins to help lower his cholesterol.

He said: “If I'm honest, I spent years trying not to think about my own health. When you're overweight or know there are things you should be addressing, it's easy to bury your head in the sand and tell yourself that nothing bad is going to happen.

“Thank goodness I went to my health check. I dread to think what could have happened if I hadn't gone. It's far easier to prevent a heart attack or stroke than recover from one.

“I'd encourage anyone who's offered an NHS health check to take it. Getting checked can feel daunting, but knowing where you stand is far better than not knowing at all, and it could make a real difference to your future health.”

His story comes as the British Heart Foundation urges people not to ignore invitations for NHS health checks.

NHS health checks are offered every five years to eligible people in England aged between 40 and 74 and assess factors including blood pressure, weight, cholesterol and cardiovascular disease risk.

Professor Bryan Williams, chief scientific and medical officer at the British Heart Foundation, said: “NHS health checks play a vital role in helping people understand and reduce their risk of serious health problems before these develop.

“Many people in their forties are juggling busy lives, while others may wrongly assume that heart disease is something that only affects older people.”
