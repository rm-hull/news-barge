A DRAFT Neighbourhood Development Plan is now open for public consultation.

The plan, which will shape Baildon’s growth and development up to 2040, has been published by Bradford Council for feedback from residents and interested parties.

Neighbourhood plans set out local planning policies and priorities, reflecting what the community wants future development to look like and influencing planning decisions made by the local authority.

Councillor Gill Dixon, chair of the Baildon Neighbourhood Plan Working Group, said: "It has taken years of detailed work to reach this stage but the Baildon Neighbourhood Plan has now been submitted to Bradford Council who will progress it through the final stages in its preparation.

"This will include a local referendum.

"Bradford Council is now undertaking a further formal public consultation on the Plan, which runs until September 30, 2026.

"We hope local people engage in the process and use this opportunity to give Bradford Council their feedback on this important Plan before it goes forward to the final stages."

Led by Baildon Town Council, the plan has been in the works since 2015, involving extensive research and community engagement.

A formal consultation on an earlier version of the plan took place between November 2025 and January 2026.

The current consultation, required by law, runs until September 30, 2026.

Once the consultation closes, the plan will undergo independent examination to ensure it meets legal requirements and the Basic Conditions set out in law.

Depending on the examiner’s findings, Bradford Council could then hold a local referendum, giving residents the final say on whether the plan should be adopted.

If approved by a majority vote, it will become part of the statutory development plan for the Bradford district, meaning it must be considered in all planning decisions within the Baildon Town Council area.

Councillor Andrew Judson, portfolio holder for economy, regeneration, and planning at Bradford Council, said: "I’d like to thank Baildon Town Council for the work they have undertaken on their Neighbourhood Development Plan, as well as those who have engaged with the process so far.

"As chair of the Keighley Neighbourhood Development Plan steering group, I understand what it takes for a Town Council to deliver such a document.

"This is an extremely important piece of legislation, and I’d encourage members of the local community to have a look at the plans and take part in this latest consultation."

The draft plan and supporting documents are available online at bradford.oc2.uk.
