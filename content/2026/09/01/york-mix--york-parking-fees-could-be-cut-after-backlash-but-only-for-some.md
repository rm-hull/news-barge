Proposals to cut some parking charges in York have been unveiled after a review of the highly controversial price hikes.

It follows new figures that suggest that city centre businesses have taken a hit since the car park cost increases came in.

Under the City of York Council recommendations, the price of a York Minster Badge will be reduced from £45 for two years to £15 annually.

And Minster Badge holders parking in community areas – including Bishopthorpe Road, Micklegate, Heworth, and The Groves – would see their discount increased from 30% to 50%.

The move would effectively halve hourly rates for residents in those suburban hubs from £3 to £1.50 per hour.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/york-stonegate-shoppers-vy.webp)

Reduced rates for Minster Badge holders would not apply in the council’s city centre car parks.

Council highways lead Helene Vergereau said, if approved, the changes would be made in the weeks following the executive meeting once council parking systems have been updated.

Both city centre spending and footfall dropped in 2025 and 2026 compared to previous years, according to York Business Improvement District (BID) data.

But the council report said national cost-of-living pressures, high inflation and shifts in consumer habits have also heavily impacted high streets nationwide, making it difficult to ascertain how much blame to assign to parking charges.

Council figures also showed there has been an increase in walking, cycling and the use of local buses and Park & Ride services.

## Lost revenue

A council report stated the changes would cost an estimated £18,000 in lost parking revenue but that could be at least partly offset if more people buy Minister Badges.

It added car park use would likely increase if the changes are made, resulting in more cars on York’s roads though officials expect the rise to be limited.

The forecast comes despite the council’s aim to cut car journeys by a fifth by 2030, which was one of the main reasons why charges were hiked in 2025.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/parking-sign-micklegate-york-sept-2025-1.webp)

Hikes in 2025 provoked the outcry from residents, traders and opposition councillors which ultimately led to the independent review into their impact in Bishopthorpe Road, Heworth, Micklegate and The Groves.

Charges in the Bishopthorpe Road car park and for on-street parking in Micklegate were later lowered following the backlash.

Parking charges at central council-run car parks and on-street within the inner ring road currently stand at £5-an-hour for the first two from Sunday to Thursdays.

A 10 per cent premium applies on Fridays, Saturdays and during events when charges are £5.50-an-hour for the first two.

Consultants Arup found changes made in 2025 followed due process and were broadly in line with similar moves elsewhere in the UK.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/york-council-kate-ravilious.webp)

But they found the way the hikes were handled did not follow best practice and ultimately fed into the backlash that sparked the legal challenge which led to the review.

The council would carry out informal consultations before any future significant increases to parking charges and publish car parking fee and income figures yearly if the changes go ahead.

## Valuable lessons

Labour transport lead Cllr Kate Ravilious said she hoped the proposals would allow the authority and those who objected to the higher parking rates move forward and work more closely together.

But she added other measures, such as free half-hour stays, had been ruled out.

Cllr Ravilious said the review had been valuable and the council had learnt the lessons from the fallout from the 2025 hikes.

“It’s been a long and difficult process for all of us,” she said.

“This is about trying to find a balance and accepting that some felt like they needed that balance to be different from what it was.

“It’s always difficult because some people live outside York but consider it their city but we had to draw the boundary somewhere.”

People who still have a valid Minster Badge bought at the existing rate would not be offered a refund.

## Changing transport use

The impact on car park usage has been clear. Official figures included in the council report reveal a slide in parking transactions between 2024/25 and 2025/26:

- **Community car parks** saw transactions drop by**31.7%** (from 83,932 down to 57,300)
- **On-street pay-to-park bays** saw a**36.3%** drop in usage (falling from 153,121 to 97,607)
- **Short-stay central car parks** fell by**8.5%**, while** long-stay central car parks**dropped by** 12.0%**
- Overall, council-managed parking transactions fell by **14.6%** in a single year –representing a loss of more than 205,000 parking stays.

Meanwhile, there were gains in sustainable travel.

- Park & Ride passenger journeys rose to **3.88 million** in 2025/26 (up from 3.78m)
- Local bus passenger trips reached **10.16 million**
- Area-wide cycling flows climbed to **103%** of the baseline
- And pedestrian walking counts into the city centre rose to **130%** of 2009 levels.
