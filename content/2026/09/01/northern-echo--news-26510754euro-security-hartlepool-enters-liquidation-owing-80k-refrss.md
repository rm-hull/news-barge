A Hartlepool security company has entered voluntary liquidation after building up debts of more than £80,000.

Euro Security Limited, which traded as Euro Security and Euro Fire & Security, filed its statement of affairs after entering creditors’ voluntary liquidation on Wednesday, August 26, revealing debts of more than £80,000.

The liquidation came less than two months after the company challenged plans to strike it off the Companies House register.

Euro Security received a First Gazette notice for compulsory strike-off on June 9 after failing to file its 2026 confirmation statement.

The action was suspended after an appeal on June 24 and formally discontinued on July 1.

By the end of August, the company had gone into voluntary liquidation.

HM Revenue and Customs was the company’s biggest creditor, owed more than £49,000 in corporation tax, VAT and PAYE.

Other creditors include HSBC, owed almost £17,000; security-products supplier ADI Global, owed more than £10,000; and Middlesbrough-based Dynamic CCTV, owed more than £3,500.

Founded in 1999, Euro Security announced new management in February 2023, opened a base at Hartlepool’s HUB ONE Innovation Centre and appointed Jonathan Whitfield as company secretary.

Mr Whitfield signed the company’s statement of affairs on August 4, weeks before it went into voluntary liquidation.

The company had £200 of stock, £150 of furniture and equipment, and a £17,000 overdrawn director’s loan account, though the amount likely to be recovered from those assets is uncertain.

The filing put the company’s total liabilities, including debts and shareholder capital, at more than £80,000.

The Companies House will carry out the creditors' voluntary liquidation under the Insolvency Act 1986.

Euro Security Limited could not be contacted after entering liquidation.
