Nato fighter jets were scrambled early on Tuesday as at least four people were killed in a sixth consecutive night of Russian aerial attacks on Ukraine.

Nato's Baltic air policing mission said it was responding and Finland and Latvia restricted air and maritime traffic close to Russia as the attack on Ukraine unfolded.

Border crossings into Romania were also affected as Russia targeted Ukraine's Izmail region.

Russia's latest aerial attack involved both missiles and drones, wounding over a dozen people in the war-hit nation.

Ukraine responded with its own attacks, with Moscow mayor Sergei Sobyanin saying more than a dozen drones were shot down as they headed for the Russian capital.

In Russia's northwestern Leningrad region, home to major export ports, officials said Russian air forces were shooting at drones.

This is despite Volodymyr Zelensky’s message last night that said he held a “detailed and constructive” call with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner to agree on dates for their visit to Kyiv for peace talks.

## US irks Europe and G20 by inviting Russia to finance meeting

The United States' move to welcome Russia back to a meeting of G20 countries while denying some journalists access to the gathering dismayed some finance leaders present, deflecting from its bid to focus talks on global economic growth.

As US treasury secretary Scott Bessent opened the meeting, some ministers were surprised and dismayed to see Russian finance minister Anton Siluanov sitting at the G20 table.

This is the first time Russia has attended the forum in person since Vladimir Putin invaded Ukraine in 2022.

Polish finance minister Andrzej Domanski told Reuters he was unhappy to see Russia represented, although he recognised the right of G20 hosts to invite guests.

“We do not trust Russia. They lie constantly and you need to be really, really cautious while discussing with them,” he told Reuters in an interview, stressing that Russia was the aggressor in its conflict with Ukraine.

"So for me it would be very difficult to have any kind of conversation with Russia,” he said.

German finance minister Lars Klingbeil said Europe was preparing a further package of sanctions against Russia, but Siluanov's presence at the Asheville meeting sent a "quite troubling" signal for US cooperation with the effort.

“I would have wanted greater clarity from the American side that he should not be received here as a normal guest,” Klingbeil said.

Siluanov also held a bilateral meeting with Bessent that covered financial cooperation within the G20 framework, Russia's finance ministry said.

## Drones hit Novokuibyshevsk in Russia's Samara region

Drones hit the town of Novokuibyshevsk in Russia's Samara region, blowing out windows in an apartment building, regional governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev said.

Novokuibyshevsk and the surrounding area on the Volga River are home to a number of Russia's major oil refineries run by the state oil company Rosneft.

## Putin and Xi discuss Ukraine at Kyrgyzstan meeting

Vladimir Putin has discussed the war in Ukraine with Xi Jinping during a meeting in Kyrgyzstan on Monday.

The Kremlin issued a statement on Monday to say the Russian and Chinese leader touched upon the conflict in Ukraine, but added that it was not the main theme of their meeting.

The leaders met in Bishkek alongside their Indian counterpart Narendra Modi and Iranian president Masoud Pezeshkian, for the annual two day Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Summit.

## Estonia says Nato ready to protect allied territory

Nato is reacting swiftly and exactly as planned and the alliance is “vigilant and ready” to protect its territory, Estonian foreign minister Margus Tsahkna said on X, after Nato scrambled fighter jets following alerts.

The Nato Baltic air policing mission briefly activated jet fighters in Latvia, and Finland has restricted air and maritime traffic close to Russia, armed forces of both countries said separately on X.

## Bessent told Russian minister no economic relief until Ukraine war ends

US treasury secretary Scott Bessent has made clear to Russian finance minister Anton Siluanov that there will be no economic relief for Russia or agreements on other issues until its war in Ukraine ends, a source familiar with the ministers' bilateral meeting told Reuters.

Bessent met bilaterally with Siluanov on the sidelines of a G20 finance leaders meeting in Asheville, North Carolina.

The Russian finance minister's first in-person G20 appearance since Russia invaded Ukraine has irritated European officials who are working on more sanctions against Russia.

Earlier, a US official told Reuters the focus of the Bessent-Siluanov meeting was president Donald Trump's peace plan for Ukraine.

Russia's finance ministry said the two officials discussed financial cooperation within the G20 framework.

When Siluanov tried to discuss areas of mutual interest, the source said Bessent interrupted him and said "nothing is possible until the war is over."

The US imposed sanctions on Russia's oil majors in October 2025, but after the US-Israeli war against Iran cut off energy supplies through the Strait of Hormuz, Bessent issued licences allowing the sale of seaborne Russian oil stored in tankers to ease supplies, sending some funds to Moscow.

## Drones shot down in Moscow

Russia's air force has shot down over a dozen drones heading towards Moscow, mayor Sergei Sobyanin said on Telegram.

Sobyanin’s preliminary update did not mention how many total drones have been fired in the Russian capital’s direction.

In Russia's Samara region, home to a number of major oil refineries some 800km (500 miles) southeast of Moscow, an air attack damaged civilian facilities, the local governor said on Telegram.

## Nato activates fighter jets in Latvia and Finland amid Russia's major aerial barrage

The Nato Baltic air policing mission has activated jet fighters in Latvia, and Finland has restricted air and maritime traffic close to Russia, armed forces of both countries said separately on X.

Massive strikes from Russia have already killed four in Ukraine in the early hours today and left over a dozen injured.

The aerial activity came as Russia's northwestern Leningrad region, home to major export ports, was shooting at drones.

## Four killed and over a dozen injured as Russia strikes Kyiv region for sixth successive day

At least four people were killed and over a dozen injured in Russian air attacks on the Ukrainian capital of Kyiv and the surrounding region in the early hours this morning, local authorities said, in what was the sixth day in a row of strikes on the area.

Explosions were heard in Kyiv, a Reuters witness said.

At least three people were killed and another five wounded, and a fire broke out in a non-residential area of the capital, city authorities said this morning.

In Boryspil in the Kyiv region, one person was killed and another nine, including a child, were injured after missiles and drones damaged an apartment building and a business facility, governor Tymur Tkachenko said on Telegram.

The attack marks the sixth day of attacks on Kyiv and the surrounding region, disrupting the daily lives of millions of people.

Air raid alerts covered most of Ukraine's territory in the early hours of Tuesday.
