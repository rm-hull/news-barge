A paedophile who sent sexual Whatsapp messages to a 'girl' and asked her for a photo wanted to book an Airbnb to meet her for sex.

James Morgan sent a direct message on to a chat site believing the recipient to be an “Olivia”, but it was actually a decoy profile posted by an undercover police officer seeking to catch online paedophiles.

Durham Crown Court was told that Morgan sent the message in both his name and that of his partner at the time, and implied she was party to the exchanges.

Martin Towers, prosecuting, said “Olivia” made it clear from an early stage that she was aged 12.

The chat moved on to WhatsApp and Morgan made sexualised comments and asked “Olivia” to send him a photograph.

Mr Towers said an image of a girl was sent to Morgan, who replied that she was “pretty” and then said that he and his partner wanted to have sexual contact with her.

He told the girl they wanted to come to Manchester “on Friday” and that they would book into an Airbnb, but his partner was working that day and would not be able to join him.

Mr Towers said the messaging, confined to February 6, 2024, abruptly ended there.

Six days later police attended at Morgan’s County Durham home, but he was not there as he was in London, so arrangements were made for him to attend a police station in the capital.

Mr Towers said Morgan’s partner at the time was arrested and although she was soon ruled out of the investigation, she did spend several hours in police custody.

Morgan, himself, voluntarily went to Brixton Police Station, where he was arrested and a mobile phone was seized from him.

It contained the messages which were exchanged with the police decoy.

He denied responsibility in two police interviews and claimed he must have been the victim of a malicious attack.

Morgan, 49, formerly of Laburnum Grove, St Helen Auckland, but now of Twickenham, in London, maintained his denials, but shortly before a scheduled trial in June he changed plea and admitted a single count of attempted sexual communication with a child.

Dr Chris Wood, for the defendant, said as with many defendants in such cases, he “effectively, buried his head in the sand”.

But Dr Wood said that having eventually pleaded guilty, albeit late in proceedings, the defendant has now demonstrated an insight, realising the distress he caused his former partner and the impact his actions have had, on her.

He said it understandably resulted in her ending their relationship and the defendant has recently been living with friends, who do not have children, in London.

Recorder Mark McKone told Morgan the two serious aspects of the case were the fact that the defendant knew from an early stage the supposed young age of the “girl” he was messaging and, in “sinister” manner, he cast suspicion on his former partner, leading to her arrest and detention for several hours.

The Recorder said despite seeking a liaison with “Olivia”, “mercifully there could be no meeting as she was not real”, but he described it as “very good policing, designed to protect children.”

He said the defendant, who is self-employed, has some health issues and is “effectively of previous good character”.

Imposing a 13-month prison sentence the Recorder said he could suspend it for 18 months so the defendant can work with the Probation Service to address his sexual interest in children and reduce the risk of harm he poses.

He ordered the defendant to attend 25 rehabilitation activity days overseen by the Probation Service and complete 160-hours’ unpaid work.

He must also pay £1,600 towards the prosecution costs in the case.

Morgan was also made subject of registration as a sex offender and a Sexual Harm Prevention Order, prohibiting unsupervised contact with children and regulating his future internet use, both for ten years.
