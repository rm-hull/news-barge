The famous landmark was removed on Tuesday morning

**Whitby's landmark Whale Bone Arch has been taken down from its clifftop home, the council has confirmed.** 

The latest pair of bones, which were installed in 2002, have been photographed by legions of visitors after being gifted to Whitby by Alaskan twin town Barrow.

The removal on Tuesday morning came after years of exposure to the weather led to a deterioration in the arch's condition.

An ethical bronze replica of the structure is set to be installed early next year. It is hoped the new creation will be more durable than bone.

Mark Crane, from North Yorkshire Council, said: "Importantly, the bronze replicas provide an ethical alternative to the use of whale bones, allowing this iconic landmark and its connection to Whitby's maritime heritage to be preserved in a way that reflects modern conservation values.

"We look forward to seeing the new arch in situ. It will stand for decades to come, ensuring this iconic feature continues to inspire residents and visitors long into the future."

![Workers in high‑visibility clothing handle a large padded arch made from curved bones at a coastal landmark, while two other people observe nearby with the sea, pier and lighthouse in the background.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/2048/cpsprodpb/f865/live/1d5a7c50-a5e1-11f1-ae66-53604efd186f.jpg)

The bronze replica will cost £70,000, according to the council

According to the council, bronze was chosen as the material for the replacement due to its strength, longevity and ability to withstand the challenging conditions on Whitby's West Cliff.

The 19ft (5.8m) arch will be recreated through a £70,000 process of moulding the existing bones and manufacturing an internal framework.

Whitby Town Mayor Sandra Turner, added: "The whale bone arch means a great deal to residents and visitors alike.

"While it is sad to see the current bones reach the end of their life, the new bronze arch will ensure this landmark continues to celebrate Whitby's history and provide a focal point on West Cliff for future generations."
