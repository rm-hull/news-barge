One of Whitby’s most recognisable landmarks has been removed after more than two decades overlooking the town’s famous seafront.

The whale bone arch was taken down today, Tuesday, September 1, because years of exposure to the coastal weather have caused significant deterioration.

The Original Whale jaw bone arch was erected some time after 1853 and is now displayed in the Whitby Archives & Heritage Centre.

The new landmark was then installed in 2002 after being gifted to Whitby by its twin town of Barrow in Alaska.

But the bones have now reached the end of their life and will be replaced with ethical bronze replicas.

  The Whitby whale bone arch on West Cliff being removed 

*(Image: NORTH YORKSHIRE COUNCIL)*

Following a detailed appraisal of options, bronze was selected as the preferred material for the new arch.

The replacement, costing £70,000, is expected to be installed early next year.

It will replicate the appearance and scale of the existing arch while being designed to withstand the challenging conditions on Whitby’s West Cliff.

  North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for open to business, Cllr Mark Crane (pictured left), and coastal infrastructure engineer, John Woodhead, were on site for the removal 

*(Image: NORTH YORKSHIRE COUNCIL)*

North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for open to business, Cllr Mark Crane, whose responsibilities include harbours, said: "The whale bone arch is one of Whitby's most treasured landmarks and an important part of the town's maritime heritage.

“The removal of the arch is a landmark day in the town’s recent history, as visitors have flocked to see and photograph the bones for over two decades.

"Importantly, the bronze replicas provide an ethical alternative to the use of whale bones, allowing this iconic landmark and its connection to Whitby's maritime heritage to be preserved in a way that reflects modern conservation values.

“We look forward to seeing the new arch in situ. It will stand for decades to come, ensuring this iconic feature continues to inspire residents and visitors long into the future.”

  The Whitby whale bone arch on West Cliff being removed 

*(Image: NORTH YORKSHIRE COUNCIL)*

The bronze will develop a natural patina over time while retaining the distinctive shape which has become synonymous with Whitby’s seafront.

Feedback from residents and visitors showed bronze was the most fitting option for reflecting the town’s maritime heritage and the importance of the internationally recognised landmark.

The arch frames views towards Whitby Abbey and attracts thousands of visitors every year.

Creating the new structure will be a complex process, involving moulds made from the existing bones and a bespoke internal framework.

Whitby Town Mayor, Cllr Sandra Turner, said: "The whale bone arch means a great deal to residents and visitors alike.

“While it is sad to see the current bones reach the end of their life, the new bronze arch will ensure this landmark continues to celebrate Whitby's history and provide a focal point on West Cliff for future generations.”
