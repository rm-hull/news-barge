Work is planned to regenerate the South Walmgate area of York with 36 new Council homes and new landscaping.

A Neighbourhood Improvement Plan (NIP) has been taking shape following a series of consultations involving local residents, and is being presented to the council Executive for approval in early September.

Work to enhance the local high street for shoppers and businesses, and to deliver wider estate regeneration is also being proposed as part of the Council’s NIP, and will run alongside creating new Council homes on the former Willow House care home site.

The area regeneration is being funded by nearly £1 million: £500,000 from the council’s Local Transport Grant; £250,000 from City of York Council’s Council Housing budget; and £217,000 from the Vibrant and Sustainable High Streets Fund, part of David Skaith’s Mayoral Challenge Fund through York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

These improvements are informed by resident engagement.

They include proposals to improve security in the adjacent estate, develop a communally-managed garden scheme, make junctions more accessible for pedestrians and cyclists, improve the shopping parade, and set up a heritage trail in this historic area.

Alongside those plans, residents have been helping shape the 36 new social rent and shared ownership homes planned for the Willow House site.

They form the next stage of the Council’s ambitious Housing Delivery Programme, which includes Ordnance Lane and other Council-owned sites across the city.

The Council has recently appointed a new contractor to demolish the former Willow House care home ahead of building work. As a member of the considerate contractor’s scheme, they will inform residents of progress and keep disruption to a minimum.

Work is also underway to secure ownership of a small number of brick stores and garages required to deliver the Willow House redevelopment safely and efficiently.

Executive will be asked to approve the preparatory work needed to support the potential use of compulsory purchase powers, should they be required.

Any decision to make a Compulsory Purchase Order would be considered separately by the Executive later.

Find out more about the Executive on 9 September at 4:30pm and the Combined Executive Committee Meeting on 8 September at 10am.
