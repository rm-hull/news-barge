*Free creative workshops for all ages launch at York Explore*

THIS October, Explore launches the fourth year of its National Portfolio Organisation (NPO) programme, supported by Arts Council England, with a new season of free creative activities at York Explore Library and Archive.

Following successful projects at Tang Hall, Acomb and Clifton Explore Libraries, this year's programme moves to York Explore, the city's flagship library and home of the Archives.

Inspired by the nationally significant collections held by Explore York Archives, the programme offers opportunities for people of all ages to discover local history, develop creative skills and connect with others.

The programme includes three strands: History Misbehaves, Creative Cafés and the Explore Labs Digital Making Youth Club.

History Misbehaves invites residents to work with director Kate Valentine of Digital Drama and playwright Paul Birch of Riding Lights Theatre Company on a new creative project inspired by York's rich printing heritage. Participants will explore archive collections, uncover stories from York's printing past and work together to create an original audio drama.

For adults looking to try something new, Creative Cafés offers free monthly workshops led by professional artists Katie Matthews and Gracie Richmond. Using inspiration from local history and archive collections, participants will experiment with different art forms in a relaxed and welcoming environment. The sessions are designed to support creativity, wellbeing and social connection, with no previous artistic experience required.

  Katie Matthews and Gracie Richmond with artwork created by participants in the Creative Cafés project at Clifton Explore. Photo supplied


Young people aged 10 to 15 can take part in the Explore Labs Digital Making Youth Club, delivered by award-winning York-based company HYEN. Through hands-on activities, participants will explore coding, electronics, digital maker tools and creative technology while developing skills and confidence.

Over the past three years, Explore's NPO programme has engaged residents across Tang Hall, Acomb and Clifton through community storytelling, visual arts, exhibitions, performances and creative workshops inspired by local heritage. Participants have worked alongside professional artists to uncover stories from York's past, create original artwork, develop new skills and build connections within their communities.

Taking place at York Explore, participants will be closer than ever to the archive collections that inspire the creative activities.

From York's printing heritage to local historical records and collections, the programme encourages people to discover new perspectives on the city's past while creating something new of their own.

Dave Fleming, creative producer at Explore York Libraries and Archives said: "Over the past three years, we've worked with communities across Tang Hall, Acomb and Clifton to uncover local stories, explore creativity and create something special together.

  Participant recording audio for History Misbehaves at JORVIK Radio. Photo supplied


"We're excited to bring the fourth year of the programme to York Explore, where even more people will be able to discover the archives and be part of these welcoming creative projects."

One participant from last year's Creative Cafés project said: "Everything was brilliant. Warm, friendly and welcoming. It's encouraged me to be more creative at home and look for creative art courses in the future."

Thomas Mason, founder of HYEN said: “HYEN delivers hands-on STEAM sessions across the North of England, and Explore York Libraries is where we most enjoy doing it.

"Digi Club gives young people a creative space to make, build, explore and, most importantly, have fun. The library sits at the heart of the community, and together we can give young people the chance to try new tools and build real skills.”

All activities are free and open to beginners, with no prior experience necessary.

For more information about the activities, visit exploreyork.org.uk.

To join History Misbehaves, email [arts@exploreyork.org.uk.](mailto:arts@exploreyork.org.uk.)

Places for Creative Cafés and the Explore Labs Digital Making Youth Club can be booked via Explore's Ticket Tailor page.

Explore's National Portfolio Organisation programme is supported by Arts Council England and funded thanks to the National Lottery.
