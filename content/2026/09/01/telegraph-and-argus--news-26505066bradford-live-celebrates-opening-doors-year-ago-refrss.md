BRADFORD Live welcomed thousands of visitors in its first year as a city centre entertainment venue - and a bright future is in store with bosses saying "we're only getting started".

Acts such as Kaiser Chiefs, Bill Bailey and Gorillaz have graced the stage since its opening during Bradford’s UK City of Culture year.

People also enjoyed watching England's recent World Cup journey after the building became home to the city's FanZone.

Venue director Darren Moore celebrated what Bradford Live has already achieved and teased that there is plenty more to come.

He said: “We can't quite believe we're celebrating our first birthday already.”

He said: “What a year it has been for Bradford Live.

"Since opening during Bradford's City of Culture celebrations, we've welcomed thousands of people through our doors and established ourselves at the heart of the city's cultural and live entertainment scene.

## How we got here

The anniversary follows a turbulent period before the venue opened.

Birmingham-based NEC Group, named as the original operator in 2017, withdrew from the scheme in September 2024.

In a joint statement from the NEC Group and Bradford Council at the time, they said: “This follows a decision by the NEC Group that the venue will be better served by an alternative operator.”

“Following a strategic review of its business, the NEC Group will focus on its existing operations in the Birmingham area.”

Bradford Council confirmed to the Telegraph & Argus in July 2025 that it received £5,371,000 from NEC Group under a settlement agreed after its withdrawal.

The council said no public money was paid to NEC Group.

The T&A sought the information for several months before taking the matter to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

The ICO then confirmed it had ordered the council to disclose the details.

## New operator takes it on

The wider Bradford Live project cost £50.5 million, with £43.75 million provided by Bradford Council through grants and loans.

The council said Trafalgar Entertainment had signed a 25-year lease to operate Bradford Live, which is council-owned.

It expects total income of “up to around £17 million” from rent and a share of profits over the lease period.

The authority also said the venue could generate £580 million for the local economy over the contract’s lifetime.

Trafalgar Entertainment invested £3 million in fitting out the building.

## Bradford Live history

Bradford Live’s building has a long history in the city.

The building was constructed in the 1930s as a cinema and later became the Gaumont Theatre.

It hosted acts including the Beatles and the Rolling Stones.

It later became an Odeon cinema, with the original auditorium split into two screens and a bingo hall.

The building closed in 2000 but was saved from demolition after public campaigns.

It has since been restored as a live music venue and entertainment hub.

Its first anniversary marks the latest chapter in the building’s long history.
