The way people search for information is changing. Increasingly, they’re turning to AI tools such as Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Claude instead of traditional search engines. That changes what businesses need to optimize for.

Content quality, authority and trust still matter enormously. But they're no longer enough by themselves. Even the best content won’t do much if AI struggles to find it or understand it.

Senior Director of Engineering at Cloudways by DigitalOcean.

The CMA's decision to give publishers more control over how their content appears in Google’s AI search is another reminder that AI is changing how people find information online. That's creating new opportunities, particularly for smaller organizations. AI doesn't necessarily favor the biggest brands; it favors sources it can understand and trust.

## Why aren’t search rankings enough anymore?

Search rankings alone aren’t enough anymore because many people now receive answers directly from AI rather than clicking through search results. Traditional SEO has always been about helping people find your website.

You create useful content, build authority, improve the technical health of your site and compete for the search terms that matter most. The difference is that users don't always follow the same journey anymore.

Someone researching providers might never look beyond an AI-generated summary. Instead of opening multiple browser tabs and comparing businesses themselves, they ask a question and receive a recommendation in seconds.

For example, the best independent café within walking distance that's open now, clothes stores nearby running a 50% sale, or where to find a family-friendly Italian restaurant with good reviews. The AI does the searching, filtering and comparing before presenting a shortlist.

That changes what businesses need to optimize for. Success is no longer measured purely by where your website ranks—it's also influenced by whether AI systems recognize your content as accurate, trustworthy and useful enough to reference.

AI doesn't create answers in isolation. It still relies on information published across the web. Websites are the source AI uses to understand businesses, compare different services and then create a recommendation. If anything, they're becoming more important because they now serve two audiences: the people visiting them and the AI systems interpreting them.

That's why treating AI visibility as purely a marketing challenge misses part of the picture. From an engineering perspective, the quality of the underlying website matters just as much as the quality of the content published on it.

## Why does website performance matter to AI search?

Website performance matters because AI systems can only recommend content they can easily find, access and understand. Before AI can reference a website, it needs to crawl it, collect information and understand the content before it can include that information in a response.

Sites that are too difficult to navigate, slow to load or poorly structured make that process more difficult. Important information that’s buried behind scripts or inconsistent page structures also makes it harder for AI to interpret, meaning they’re less likely to use it as a source.

That's why technical performance influences visibility in ways many businesses haven't considered before. Website speed, uptime, structured data, accessibility and clear information architecture have traditionally been viewed as operational concerns. They're becoming visibility signals too.

Performance has always mattered because users expect fast, reliable websites. AI search gives businesses another reason to invest in those fundamentals.

## What should businesses do differently?

The first step is to look at the parts of your website that are often overlooked. Does it still perform well when traffic suddenly spikes? Are pages loading quickly for real users? Can people, and AI systems, find the information they're looking for easily?

Good hosting and reliable infrastructure play a much bigger role than many businesses realize. A website that's fast, stable and able to handle busy periods gives customers a better experience and makes it easier for AI systems to access and understand your content.

If your website struggles when demand increases, it's not just visitors who notice. AI-powered monitoring tools can also help identify unusual traffic patterns and potential issues before they begin affecting users or AI systems trying to access your site.

It's also worth looking beyond performance scores alone. A website might perform well in a controlled speed test but still struggle during a seasonal sale, a product launch or a viral social media campaign. Those are often the moments when visibility matters most, and when businesses need confidence that their infrastructure will scale without compromising the user experience.

One of the simplest things businesses can do is make their website easier for AI to understand. That means keeping pages well organized, using structured data where it makes sense and making sure important information isn’t buried or difficult to find. Paired with reliable hosting and solid infrastructure, businesses can give both customers and AI a much better experience.
