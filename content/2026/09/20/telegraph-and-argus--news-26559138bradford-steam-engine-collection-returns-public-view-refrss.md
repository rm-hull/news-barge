A long-lost collection of model steam engines has returned to public display.

The William H Smith collection, once a central feature of Bingley’s industrial heritage, was stored away for years after the closure of the old Bingley Library.

It has now found a new home at Damart’s historic mill building thanks to the efforts of community members and organisations.

## The man behind the models

The models were crafted by Harry Booth, a respected model engineer from the area who began building engines at the age of 14.

![]()The models on display *(Image: Newsquest)*

Jane Hall, Mr Booth’s great-granddaughter, said: "The vast majority of his models were based on engines that powered local industries.

"A lot of them were used in the woollen mills.

"He would visit the mills, look at the engines, visualise them.

"Then he would go and make an exact replica of that engine, and they would actually work."

Ms Hall described her memories of Mr Booth and his models.

She said: "his house was like treasure trove, he had these beautiful models in beautiful glass cases."

### Bringing the collection back to Bingley

Ms Hall said: "We hadn't seen them for years.

"It took us a long time to find them and quite a lot of persistence."

Their return to public view was made possible through a collaboration involving Damart, heritage volunteers, Bradford Museums and Galleries, Councillor Susan Fricker, and long-time advocate David Sturdy.

Councillor Susan Fricker, Labour representative for Bingley East, said: "These models are beautiful and inspiring, it is wonderful that they are out of the wooden creates where they were stored for too many years, and to see them back here in Bingley in one of our great and historic mill buildings."

![]()The models on display *(Image: Newsquest)*

Councillor Fricker first learned about the collection in summer 2023 when Mr Sturdy attended one of her community meetings.

She said: "David has known these models for most of his lifetime and has doggedly worked for many years to bring them back to Bingley, it has been a pleasure to work with David over the past 3 years to achieve this."

She then contacted Elizabeth Llabres, collections manager at Bradford Council Museums and Galleries, who arranged a visit to see the models in storage.

### A new home at Damart Mill

After considering several options, support from Ben Northrop and the Damart team proved crucial in securing a new home for the collection.

![]()Damart Mill in Bingley *(Image: Newsquest)*

Councillor Fricker said: "I am immensely grateful to David Sturdy for telling me about these wonderful models and for his persistence to see them returned to Bingley, to Elizabeth Llabres and our Museums and Galleries team for their readiness and support to achieve this, and to Ben Northrop and the team at Damart for ensuring that this long held wish has been finally realised."

Ms Hall said: "As a family, we're absolutely delighted that, the models are back on display.

"Children will be able to appreciate and see the wonder of these amazing models"

The collection is now open to the public, schools, and visitors.
