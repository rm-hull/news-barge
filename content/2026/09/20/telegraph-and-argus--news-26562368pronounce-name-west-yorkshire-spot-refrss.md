The English language can be incredibly confusing even to people who have lived here their whole lives.

With many names of towns and villages being influenced by Roman, Celtic, Norman, and Viking languages, a lot of them can be quite difficult to pronounce.

Yorkshire is known for having strange place names that are not said how they are spelt out.

Mytholmroyd is a village in West Yorkshire that is surrounded by the Calder Valley and often has visitors left wondering how to pronounce its name.

## How do you pronounce Mytholmroyd?

If you are taking a trip to West Yorkshire and want to impress the locals with your pronunciation of its many strange towns and villages, here is a guide to help you.

The village’s name might look simple to pronounce but people have a few different ways of saying it.

You might think to split it up and say My-tholm-royd or even say something like Mal-thum-royd.

But both of these ways are incorrect according to the village’s locals.

The village is actually pronounced Mith-uhm-royd with its name being rooted in Old Norse.

Mythom denotes a confluence of rivers and royd means a clearing in the woods.

However, there is still some debate around whether this is the only way to say the village’s name, with people from West Yorkshire debating it.

![]()The village is a good place to stop during a long walk. *(Image: Tripadvisor)*

A TikTok content creator, Bewildered Yorkshire, posted a video about commonly mispronounced place names in Yorkshire after he said Mytholmroyd incorrectly.

Many people took to the comments to discuss the strange pronunciation of the village.

One person said: “I’m from Halifax and never heard Mytholmroyd pronounced any different to how you did?”

Another person said: “I’m 20 mins from Mytholmroyd and have always pronounced it ‘Mal-thum-royd’ no idea if correct, nor do I care.”

### **What can you do in Mytholmroyd?**

Mytholmroyd is a great place to stop for people on a hike through the area thanks to its connection to many walking trails.

The village is close to Calder Weir, which is a picturesque spot along the River Calder, and the Cragg Vale Heritage Trail, and is worth visiting after a long walk.

The village has plenty of places to stop for some food including the highly rated Blue Teapot that has a 4.8 out of 5 rating on Tripadvisor.

There is also the Dusty Miller, a historic building with a pub and a few rooms in case you want to extend your stay in the area.

Barbarys offers take-out beers, plenty of outdoor space, pies, and lots of live music to enjoy.
