A North Yorkshire theatre has unveiled a packed autumn and winter season of drama, stand-up, live music and festive favourites.

The Georgian Theatre Royal in Richmond will open the season with drama, comedy, music and immersive storytelling.

Jo Mawhinney, chief executive of the Georgian Theatre Royal, said: "We're delighted to unveil another exciting and varied season that celebrates everything audiences love about live theatre.

"From outstanding drama and brilliant comedy to live music, fascinating talks, heritage events and our much-loved pantomime, there's something for everyone.

"We look forward to welcoming both loyal supporters and first-time visitors through our doors for what promises to be a fantastic season."

The programme opens on September 4 with 10CCLO performing classic hits by 10cc and the Electric Light Orchestra.

Our Star Theatre brings The Three Musketeers to the stage on September 5, promising "chaos, comedy and swashbuckling silliness."

Dot Productions will present Oscar Wilde’s Lady Windermere’s Fan on September 12, followed by The Phantom Raspberry Blower of Old London Town on September 18, bringing Spike Milligan and Ronnie Barker’s surreal humour to life.

The theatre will host three literary events as part of the Richmond Walking and Book Festival.

Hindpal Singh Bhui of HM Inspectorate of Prisons will discuss What Are Prisons For? on September 19.

Lady Brenda Hale, former president of the UK Supreme Court, will discuss her book With the Law on Our Side on September 25.

Terry Deary, creator of Horrible Histories, will introduce his latest murder mystery, Actually, I’m a Corpse, on September 27.

The West Yorkshire Savoyards return with Gilbert and Sullivan’s The Pirates of Penzance on September 26.

The Loves of Others arrives on October 8, billed as the North’s answer to Abigail’s Party.

The Mikron Theatre Company returns on October 16 with Wensleydale Whey: The Grate Cheese Quest, a comic history of Wensleydale cheese.

Stand-up comedians Charlie Baker and Daniel Foxx perform on September 8 and October 23 respectively.

Adrian Bramley and Gregan Davis return on October 15 with An Unexpected Item, featuring songs and monologues inspired by Flanders and Swann, Tom Lehrer, Marriott Edgar and Jake Thackray.

John Godber’s The Highwayman, set in 18th-century Yorkshire, returns on October 30 and 31 after proving popular.

Alchemy Live, a tribute to Dire Straits, comes to the theatre on October 24.

Spectres and Spotlights: Stage Fright runs on October 28 and 29, combining ghost stories with the theatre’s history on an immersive evening tour.

The Richmond Amateur Dramatic Society presents Jean Anouilh’s The Cavern from November 5 to 7.

The season ends with the theatre’s annual pantomime, The Pantomime Adventures of The Little Mermaid, running from December 4 to January 9, 2027.

Ms Mawhinney said the season spans drama, comedy, music and storytelling.

Full programme details are available in the theatre’s brochure, from the box office or at www.georgiantheatreroyal.co.uk.
