## Before Primark and Sports Direct – 9 'lost' York shops we want back

C&A in York's Coppergate centre

*Before Primark and Sports Direct: 9 York shops we miss, by Maxine Gordon*

MANY much-loved York shops have long since closed, but they certainly have not been forgotten.

Names such as Woolies, BHS, C&A, Scotts the butchers, and Leak and Thorp, are etched into the memories of York people – and most folk would love to see them back on our city streets today.

We asked members of our Why We Love York – Memories Facebook group which shops from the city's past they missed most and, if they had the chance, which they would bring back.

The answers prompted a trip down memory lane, with department stores, fashion shops, butchers, bookshops, toy shops and some much smaller independent businesses all earning a mention.

But two names stood above the rest.

Woolworths and British Home Stores (BHS) were the joint favourites, each receiving eight mentions from readers.

![]()Woolworths in Coney Street

Andree Gotel simply nominated: "Woolworths everywhere", while Carol Gateley picked both of the most popular names, saying: "Woolworths and BHS."

Gordon Kent-Smith also chose Woolworths, while Wendy Blanchard's choice was British Home Stores.

![]()BHS in Coney Street, when it opened in 1968.

Jean Macnaughton remembered both, alongside several other old favourites, while Mary Ann Dearlove picked "British Home Stores, Precious (toy shop), Woollies, Droopy and Brown's."

Jean Robinson Powell was another reader longing for the big high street names of the past, choosing "Woolworths, C&A, BHS, Leak and Thorp".

Margaret Cawthorne completed a similar trio with Woolworths, C&A and British Home Stores.

![]()C&A in York's Coppergate centre

Huxtables came next in our readers' ranking, with six separate mentions. Huxtables, in Fossgate, was one of the most popular unisex boutiques in York.

For Tim Bruce, the shop stirred memories of the fashions of his younger days.

He said: "Huxtables – used to get Wrangler and Brutus jeans (that totally covered your shoes!) from there back in the day."

![]()Huxtable in Fossgate in 1986, with Wild Poppy visible to the right

Keith Tyreman remembered "Huxtables in Fossgate", while Alex Meehan, Simon Fried and Kevin Odonnell also nominated the shop.

Bruce Wray picked Huxtables too, along with another York favourite, Scotts.

Scotts butchers was mentioned four times, and one former employee had particularly fond memories.

Adele Mortimer said: "Scotts butchers... I'd have never given up my job, absolutely loved it."

Roland Dobson also picked Woolworths and Scotts, while Dan York voted for "Sarah Coggles and Scotts the pork butcher."

![]()The Sarah Coggles store in Low Petergate, York..

C&A came next with three mentions, while Sarah Coggles, Droopy and Brown's, Leak and Thorp and Precious toy shop each appeared twice.

But many of the one-off nominations showed just how varied York's shopping scene is in readers' memories.

![]()Precious toy shop in Petergate, York. Photo by Ann Pearson

They included Burdekin's the stationers, Unit 5 in Acomb, Bead Machine, Track Records, Boyes on Ouse Bridge, Renaissance, the Scotch wool shop on Coney Street, Farmers Butchers on Bridge Street and the original Christmas shop on Petergate.

There were evocative memories too.

Kevin Holland recalled Rieveley's pet shop on Colliergate, saying it "always had a wonderful smell", as well as Precious toy shop on Low Petergate and Ken Spellman's bookshop on Micklegate.

He particularly remembered the bookshop "in the winter when the coal fire was on in the back room".

Dick Sefton reeled off another collection of lost favourites: Stubbs hardware in Fossgate, Ernest Roy's in Patrick Pool, Hunter & Smallpage in Goodramgate and Bibi's Italian restaurant on Micklegate, finishing wistfully with: "Sigh!"

![]()The Droopy and Browns shop in Stonegate.

Valerie Wynne's memories stretched even further back. She recalled the Barbican book shop "when it was on top of Walmgate Bar in the 60s".

Other nominations included the Danish Kitchen, Sound Effects, Bakers, Bubbles, and Gillygate fish and chips cafe.

And Linda Metcalfe perhaps summed up the mood of the discussion best.

Asked which old York shops she missed, her answer was simple: "All the golden oldies."
