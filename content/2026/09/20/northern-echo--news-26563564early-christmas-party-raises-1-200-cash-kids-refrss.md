**Alfie Joey gets on the Christmas bandwagon early – but for very good reason**

HOW soon is too soon?

Are you one of those people whose hackles go up – whatever hackles are – when Easter Eggs appear in the shops before Lent has even begun? Do you get spooked when Halloween decorations go on sale at the tail end of summer?

And what about Christmas parties… in September?

**READ MORE: Great North Run charity runners raise around £25m each year for vital causes**

Well, dear reader, I’ve been to two Christmas parties this month already!!

The first was at the top of Hadrian’s Tower, the tallest building in Newcastle. The views are breathtaking and the genius ‘3Sixty’ event manager Kyrie launched the venue’s Christmas offerings to local businesses, with brilliant Geordie singer Jennifer (‘Give her a panto!’) Hastings belting out Mariah Carey’s, ‘All I want for Christmas is you!’

Sounds mad but everyone joined in. Surreal and seasonal, despite the September sunshine pouring into the restaurant.

The next day was my second Christmas party in as many days.

This one I attended in costume at Newcastle’s Pitcher and Piano – dressed as Scrooge!

And I wasn’t alone. There was a Grinch, a fire-breathing Snow Queen, an elf on stilts and even a snow machine. Santa was meant to be there too, but his sleigh must have got stuck in a drift.

Before I hear you bark “Humbug!”, it was all for a very good cause.

Here’s the background. There are countless creative mini-business get-togethers taking place all over the North East every week. Many happen at ungodly hours. There is one at a pub near me at 7am every fortnight. No beer is pulled – just coffee, conversation and medium-to-small-sized businesses and sole traders trying to help each other along the way.

One of the people doing more than most to bring them together is the brilliant David Thompson, whom I call the ‘Knight of Networking’. One of his flagship gatherings is held at Lumley Castle, but he also runs satellite ‘North East Network’ events at South Shields Football Club, the Great North Air Ambulance base in Stockton, two meetings in Gateshead, the Grand Hotel in Gosforth and one in Sunderland.

The glad tidings aspect to all of this? All of these groups and meetings have a charitable commitment or a good cause to support. Beneficiaries have included the Red Sky Foundation, Louie’s, the Sick Children’s Trust, Business Beats Cancer North East and the Great North Air Ambulance. This network raises up to £20,000 for charities like these every year!

And this matters. Small businesses and self-employed people are sometimes portrayed as miserly Ebeneezers, constantly chasing the next pound, but as I now know, many are also quietly doing their bit for their communities, while facing plenty of pressure themselves – rising costs, uncertain trading and cash flow fears.

At the same time, North East charities are finding it tougher and tougher with the continuing cost of living crisis. So when a room full of local businesses gets together, buys a raffle ticket, donates a prize or simply puts a few bob into a collection, it really is making a difference.

Last September, David came up with a crackpot idea: an Autumnal Christmas party at the Pitcher & Piano on Newcastle’s Quayside.

It was such a success, and raised money for charity, a crazy Christmas tradition was born and last week we met again.

I was dressed as Scrooge – partly to test my costumes for my upcoming tour of ‘A Christmas Carol’’ but mainly because it was on brand and added to the daftness. And, the best bit, ‘Cash for Kids North East’ received £1,200.

In the SME world, you have to start Christmas early for a very good reason: if you snooze, you lose! For example, fabulous photographer Phil Benton and awesome artist Darren Mundy have both released their excellent 2027 calendars last week because doing it in December would mean they’ve missed all of the Christmas fairs. This self employed journey takes so much thinking ahead, while still taking the time to act generously.

So, ‘God rest ye ladies and gentlemen, and nothing you dismay. ‘For Christmas in September is now the time to sleigh!’

Or something like that?!
