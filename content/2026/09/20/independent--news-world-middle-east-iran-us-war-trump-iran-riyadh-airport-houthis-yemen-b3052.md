US president Donald Trump has cut short his weekend stay at Camp David and returned to the White House earlier than expected, as tensions escalate across the Middle East.

Trump had been scheduled to remain at the presidential retreat until Sunday, but the White House gave no reason for the change in plans.

His return came after the US State Department issued a security alert warning Americans in the Middle East that hostilities between Saudi Arabia and the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen had the “potential to escalate rapidly”.

The development came as flames and a large plume of black smoke were seen near Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport on Saturday. Saudi Arabia sent alerts overnight warning of potential danger around the capital and the city of Al-Kharj, before issuing an all-clear on Saturday morning.

Meanwhile, Iran has laid out to Trump its conditions for re-engaging in negotiations aimed at ending the war with the US, security chief Mohsen Rezaei said.

The conditions include ending the war on all fronts, unfreezing Iranian funds and ending the naval blockade, Rezaei said in an interview according to Al Jazeera.

## Turkey says it could help meet Saudi military needs under defence pact

Turkish foreign minister Hakan Fidan said on Saturday that Saudi Arabia may have military needs due to the ⁠ongoing attacks by Yemen's Houthis and that Turkey was ready to help under a trilateral defence pact that also includes Pakistan.

"We see there are very serious attacks on Saudi Arabia's territorial integrity, sovereignty. We have an alliance agreement in our pact. We are in serious solidarity on this point... They may have some military needs, especially on ⁠some technical issues. We would have no issues in meeting ​these," Fidan ⁠told broadcaster NTV.

The joint defence agreement, dubbed the "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement", signed last month stipulates that an armed attack on any one of the three countries will be regarded ⁠as an attack on all of them.

The Houthis, who control the most populous parts of Yemen, have ​been launching ⁠attacks on Saudi Arabia since declaring ‌a naval blockade against Riyadh in July, a new front in the wider war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in late February.

Fidan said that it was unacceptable for Saudi Arabia to be made a part of the US-Iran war as it had no desire to join the conflict. He added, without elaborating, that proposals have been conveyed to all sides to end the fighting.

"There are many initiatives, but the sides need to accept these," he said. "There are new proposals, I hope they accept them. Otherwise, the crisis was tolerated until a certain point, but the economic reflections have started to exceed the ‌point of being tolerable."

Turkey, which has NATO's second-largest army, has said the pact with nuclear-armed ​Pakistan and top oil exporter Saudi Arabia was open to expansion and did not seek ‌to replace any existing alliances.

Pakistan has said that ⁠there were no discussions about a military reaction from Islamabad under the Mecca agreement in response ⁠to the Houthis' attacks on Saudi Arabia, but added that Pakistan would act when the "time comes".

## US renews blanket Mideast travel alert

The US State Department on Saturday renewed a blanket travel alert for Americans in or traveling to the Middle East, warning that the conflict with Iran "has the potential to escalate rapidly."

Security alerts for Bahrain, Oman, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Qatar, Lebanon, Kuwait and Jordan warned the regional security environment “remains complex with the potential for unforeseen escalation.”

Meanwhile, at least 11 US embassies in the region issued a warning that travel disruptions are possible.

## Iran conveys its conditions for ending war with the US

The head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, Mohsen Rezaei, told Al Jazeera in an interview that Iran has conveyed its conditions for ending the war to the US via Qatar and it is waiting for President Donald Trump's response.

It was not clear when the conditions were shared.

Rezaei said they include ending the war on all fronts, releasing assets seized from Iran and ending the US naval blockade of Iranian ports. Earlier US-Iran negotiations fell apart.

He also said Iran wants the fighting involving Yemen and Saudi Arabia to end.

## Trump cuts shorts Camp David stay to return to White House

President Donald Trump cut short his stay at Camp David and returned to the White House on Saturday evening, a day earlier than scheduled, as his administration weighs its next steps in the war with Iran.

Trump arrived at Camp David late Friday and had been expected to remain there until Sunday. The White House did not give a reason for his early return.

His return comes as tensions continue to rise across the Middle East, with the administration weighing its next moves in the conflict with Iran.

US diplomatic missions across the region have also issued security alerts warning Americans of the potential for a rapid escalation.

Trump is also preparing to travel to New York for the UN General Assembly, where the war with Iran and efforts to bring the conflict to an end are expected to feature prominently.

## Saudi Arabia says Houthis tried to attack its capital

Saudi Arabia on Saturday said that Yemen's Houthi rebels tried to attack Riyadh with a ballistic missile, the first time the Saudi capital has been targeted since the escalation in fighting with the **Iran**-backed rebels opened a new front in the** Iran** war.

The statement said the attack was intercepted. Some residents in Riyadh heard an explosion, and later some saw a plume of smoke near the airport.

There were no reports of casualties or damage.

The statement also said the Houthis tried to attack civilian infrastructure in the Red Sea port city of Yanbu — where a key Saudi pipeline ends — and in Taif, Baysh and Farasan but they were thwarted, giving no further details.

The Houthis earlier claimed, without providing evidence, that they had targeted "sensitive sites" in Riyadh and facilities of Saudi oil giant Aramco in Yanbu, causing "massive fires." Aramco, the world's largest oil company, did not respond to a request for comment.

## Trump brags 95 per cent approval among Republicans in bizarre post

US president Donald Trump claimed on Friday that his approval rating among Republicans had reached 95 per cent, calling it a record.

“TRUMP Approval Rating in the Republican Party is now 95%, A RECORD. Second place is Ronald Reagan at 86%,” Trump wrote on Truth Social.

It was not immediately clear which survey Trump was referring to.

A recent Reuters/Ipsos poll found Trump’s overall approval rating among Americans at 35 per cent, up slightly from a record low of 33 per cent in the previous poll.

The 11-14 September survey of 1,143 Americans also found that 63 per cent disapproved of Trump’s proposal to give Americans a $5,000 dividend if Republicans retain control of Congress.

## UN Security Council condemns Houthi missile attacks against Saudi Arabia and commercial vessels

The UN's most powerful body said late Friday that the Houthis' military escalation, attacks and threats "risk further expanding the conflict, threatening maritime security and international trade" while undermining regional and international efforts to secure peace in Yemen.

It was warned that continued escalation would exacerbate the already dire humanitarian situation in the country.

The council's 15 members called for "practical cooperation" to prevent the Iran-backed Houthis from acquiring arms, military training, financing and other military-related assistance needed to carry out their attacks.

They stressed that all countries must enforce the UN arms embargo against the rebel group.

## 300,000 Iranians attend biggest pro-regime rally since war began as Tehran recruits new ‘volunteer’ forces

Hundreds of thousands of Iranians took to the streets of Tehran on Friday in a massive government-organized display of defiance as they pledged their readiness to take up arms in the largest demonstration since the war began in February.

Tehran is seeking to project national unity as its economy struggles under the weight of a US naval blockade and fresh sanctions. The Iranian government simultaneously reported launching a new attack on a commercial vessel in the strategic Strait of Hormuz, a vital maritime passage it has attempted to control since the war began.

Varying reports emerged regarding the headcount of volunteers enlisting for limited military instruction to safeguard the nation in an initiative dubbed "Janfaday-e Iran," or the "ones who sacrifice their lives for Iran." Dressed in combat uniforms and waving flags, participants marched through central Tehran as onlookers cheered.

## JP Morgan says it is struggling to predict oil prices amid US-Iran war

Investment bank JP Morgan has admitted it is struggling to model how oil prices will respond to the US-Iran war, saying: “we simply don't know how to model the endgame”.

The bank said it had initially expected economic pressures – including oil above $100 a barrel, inflation at 4 per cent and 10-year US borrowing costs at 5 per cent – would push the Trump administration towards a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

“The market is on edge,” its analysts said.

Oil has since risen above $100 a barrel and 10-year US government bond yields have crossed 5 per cent, while inflation and petrol prices remain below the thresholds JP Morgan had identified.

"Six months later [since the war began], many of those lines have been crossed, yet the exit strategy is less clear, not more," said the commodities research team at JP Morgan in note.

"For the first time since the start of the Iran conflict, we don't have a baseline view. We simply don't know how to model the endgame."

## Yemen is not Iran’s bargaining chip, foreign minister says

Yemen is not an Iranian bargaining chip and its future must be decided by Yemenis through their own political process, Yemen’s foreign minister Afrah al-Zouba said in an interview published on Friday.

“Yemen is not compensation for anyone’s losses in another arena, nor an arena for settling scores, nor a bargaining chip to improve the regional negotiating position of any regional power,” al-Zouba said.

She said restoring the Yemeni state’s authority over its coast, ports and islands was essential to regional security and freedom of navigation, particularly in the Red Sea and Bab el-Mandeb, where Houthi advances along the western coast posed a serious threat to shipping.

“We reject Iranian support that strengthens Houthi capabilities and uses Yemen’s geography to threaten neighboring countries and maritime routes,” she said.

Al-Zouba said Yemen’s crisis predated the US-Iran war but that the wider regional conflict had made the situation more complicated.
