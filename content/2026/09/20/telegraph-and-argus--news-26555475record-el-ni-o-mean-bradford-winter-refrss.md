BRADFORD residents are being advised to prepare for possible disruption as experts warn a record-breaking El Niño weather event could bring wetter and stormier conditions to the UK this winter.

West Yorkshire Prepared, the region's Local Resilience Forum, has issued guidance to help residents prepare for severe weather and other emergencies.

The warning comes after Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle said households should consider keeping supplies at home to help them cope during extreme weather events.

Speaking to The Guardian earlier this month, Dame Eagle said the UK was "about to experience probably the largest El Niño that has ever been seen", which she said would lead to "wetter winters, with more extreme storms in this country".

She encouraged people to become more resilient and suggested keeping emergency supplies available at home.

![]()Environment Secretary Dame Angela Eagle arriving for a Cabinet meeting in Downing Street *(Image: Stefan Rousseau/PA Wire)*

## What is El Niño?

According to the Met Office, the El Niño event currently developing over the Pacific Ocean is forecast to become the strongest since records began, with sea temperatures expected to rise more than 3C above average.

El Niño is a climate phenomenon in the Pacific Ocean that can influence weather patterns worldwide, including in the UK.

Forecasters say the phenomenon increases the likelihood of wetter and stormier conditions across north-west Europe during autumn and early winter.

## What is being done locally?

In West Yorkshire, emergency responders and local authorities are already planning for the potential impacts of severe weather.

West Yorkshire Prepared brings together emergency services, councils, health organisations and other agencies responsible for preparing for, responding to and recovering from major incidents.

Dave Walton, co-chair of West Yorkshire Prepared and Deputy Fire Officer at West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, said: "While major emergencies are relatively rare, being prepared can make a big difference."

![]()A woman's umbrella falls victim to the wind in Bradford city centre *(Image: Chris Booth)*

Mr Walton said the organisation's website contains practical advice designed to help residents prepare for a wide range of emergencies, including severe weather, pandemics and power outages.

He said: "There are many actions households and individuals can take to help themselves.

"Our dedicated website contains practical advice and guidance to help residents prepare for a range of risks, including severe weather, health pandemics and power outages."

## Residents should prepare an emergency 'grab bag'

Mr Walton said one of the most important things people can do is create a household emergency plan, so everyone knows what to do if an incident occurs.

He also encouraged residents to prepare an emergency "grab bag" containing essentials such as medication, important documents, a mobile phone portable charger, a torch, spare batteries, cash, bottled water, snacks, warm clothing and basic toiletries.

Mr Walton said: "Preparing for emergencies is not just about individual households.

"Communities that understand their local risks, support one another and plan ahead are often better able to respond and recover when incidents occur."

West Yorkshire Prepared also works with community groups to develop local response plans aimed at strengthening resilience during emergencies.

## Agencies monitor weather risks year-round

Addressing concerns about the developing El Niño event, Mr Walton said agencies across West Yorkshire were already monitoring weather risks throughout the year.

He said: "Partner agencies across West Yorkshire plan and prepare year-round for severe weather and the disruption it can cause."

Mr Walton said organisations work together through a dedicated severe weather group to coordinate planning for storms, flooding and periods of extreme heat or cold.

![]() *(Image: Mike Simmonds)*

"This includes overseeing the regional Severe Weather Plan, which sets out local resources, roles and responsibilities," he said.

"Partners meet regularly to review current and future risks and share information.

"Forecasts are monitored closely and, where severe weather is expected, partners will get together to assess risks and determine whether any further action is required."

## Practical measures to take

With the Met Office warning that El Niño increases the chances of a wetter and stormier autumn and winter, Mr Walton said residents should consider taking a number of practical precautions.

He said: "Though it's difficult to say with any certainty exactly how weather patterns will develop, it makes sense for residents to take a few simple steps to ensure they are prepared."

These include checking whether they live in an area at risk of flooding, signing up for flood alerts and Met Office weather warnings, preparing for potential power cuts and registering vulnerable household members with utility providers' Priority Services Registers.

Mr Walton added: "Emergencies can happen with little warning, but being informed, planning ahead and knowing where to find trusted information can help people respond with confidence."
