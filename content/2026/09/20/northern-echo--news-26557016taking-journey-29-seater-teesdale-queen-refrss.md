"THERE are not many who could say that their father drove the Queen, but I could – and I was teased about it at school," says Ted Hoyle.

It was the Teesdale Queen that Ted’s father, Harry, drove, which was a mid-sized bus garaged at Harwood at the top of the dale beyond even High Force, where the lanes and farm gates could not accommodate anything bigger.

![]()Joe Wilson, the original operator of the Teesdale Queen service, beside his bus which bears the name "Fawn Lea" *(Image: Ted Hoyle)*

The service had been started in Mickleton in the 1930s by Joe Wilson who called it "Fawn Lea", probably after the name of his house.

After Joe died in the late 1940s, Harry bought his old Commer bus and the routes, and he soon updated to a 29-seater Bedford Duplex.

![]()One of the Teesdale Queen's small buses for tackling the narrow lanes of the dale *(Image: Ted Hoyle)*

"My father operated several services from Harwood to Darlington twice a month on the first and third Mondays for the market, Barnard Castle on a Wednesday, and on a Tuesday, Holwick to Middleton-in-Teesdale," says Harry.

"He also ran mystery trips and excursions to Morecambe Bay, Blackpool, the Lakes and Dumfries which were very popular as very few people owned a car."

Saturday was the busiest night of the week, particularly when the Cosy Cinema in Middleton shut and last orders were being called in the pubs.

![]()A postcard showing buses at Middleton-in-Teesdale battling through the snow *(Image: Ted Hoyle)*

The record for the number of people crammed on the 10pm 29-seater Teesdale Queen out of Middleton, going through hamlets like Newbiggin, Bowlees, Forest and Langdon Beck, was 90!

"They were hanging out of the windows," says Ted.

![]()One of the Teesdale Queen's small buses in Middleton-in-Teesdale *(Image: Ted Hoyle)*

The Teesdale Queen had other sidelines beyond ferrying people. For 3d and 6d, Harry delivered parcels, and for 6d he’d take people’s wireless accumulators to the Cosy Cinema to be charged – careful not to put them on the bus floor so the acid didn’t eat into the seat covers.

![]()One of the Teesdale Queen's small buses for tackling the narrow lanes of the dale *(Image: Ted Hoyle)*

"When the Tees Valley Railway from Barnard Castle to Middleton-in-Teesdale was closed, he won the contract to transport pupils to school, and we had to get bigger buses," says Harry.

"When I came out of the army in 1964, I passed my PSV licence and drove for my father. I met my wife, Dorothy, on the school bus – I drove and she was a pupil at Teesdale school."

![]()One of the Teesdale Queen's small buses for tackling the narrow lanes of the dale *(Image: Ted Hoyle)*

When they married, Dorothy became a deputy headteacher at several schools in Darlington while Ted worked as a chef at Greenbank maternity hospital and then in security at the Rothmans factory at Lingfield Point.

"My father retired in 1967 and we sold out to Fred and Colin Jewitt," says Harry. "There were a few other coach firms at that time, Percival’s, Maude’s, Howson’s, Wright’s from Alston and Bond Brothers of Willington.

"They were all striving to provide a service and to make a living, but with the coming of the motor car, it was the end of an era."
