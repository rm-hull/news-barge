A 70-year-old family tradition of service came to an end when a North Yorkshire farmer and firefighter retired.

Crew manager Stuart Tindall followed the footsteps of his father Bob when he joined Danby Fire Station 38 years and three months ago.

Bob Tindall had served at the same station for 32 years and between them they spent 70 years protecting their community.

Station Manager Chris Watson said: “Stuart has brought his practical agricultural skills to the Fire and Rescue Service and has regularly passed these valuable skills on to younger firefighters.

"His impact on the local area is immeasurable.

“Through attending incidents, he has helped so many people, businesses and communities on their worst days, always with a calm and level-headed approach.

"His commitment and enthusiasm for the Fire and Rescue Service will be greatly missed by everyone at Danby Fire Station and across the Whitby District.”

Dairy farmer Stuart committed to being on call for more than 160 hours a week – which excluded milking hours.

When the alert sounded and he had to down tools and race to the fire station at no notice, his family, especially his wife Linda, covered for him at the farm.

Linda also sent food parcels to him so that he could eat when he was attending longer incidents.

A post on North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service’s Facebook page says: “Everyone at North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service would like to wish Stuart a long, healthy and happy retirement.

Thank you Stuart for you dedication and exemplary service!”
