Firefighters are urging e-bike owners to take extra care after a battery sparked a house fire in Teesside.

Cleveland Fire Brigade has issued a fresh warning after firefighters were called to a house fire caused by an e-bike battery charging inside a property.

The service says lithium-ion batteries used in e-bikes and e-scooters can catch fire if damaged, faulty or charged incorrectly.

Fire chiefs are now reminding residents to follow a number of safety precautions to reduce the risk of a fire in their home.

Among the advice issued is to only buy e-bikes, batteries and chargers from trusted retailers and to always use the correct charger for the device.

Batteries should be charged on a hard, flat surface away from anything flammable and need to be unplugged as soon as they are fully charged.

Another key warning is never to leave batteries on charge while unattended, including overnight or while nobody is at home.

In a previous case, an e-bike left charging overnight caused a house fire in Washington, leaving the resident lucky to be alive and without his home.

Cleveland Fire also said hallways, staircases and exits must stay clear, and batteries should never be charged where they could block a fire escape route.

Old batteries should be disposed of using designated recycling points and never thrown into household bins, where they can also start fires.

As e-bikes become more popular across the UK, fire services are warning about the dangers of damaged or poorly maintained lithium-ion batteries.

Firefighters say anyone looking to buy a bike, battery or charger should be warned against purchasing unusually products, which may fail to meet safety standards and could cause fires.

Anyone who currently owns an e-bike is encouraged to regularly review the safety guidance and make sure their batteries are charged and stored correctly.
