A "disgraceful" cocaine-fuelled driver caused a head-on crash before fleeing, leaving another with life-threatening injuries, a court heard.

Brandon William Turnbull crossed double white lines to overtake on the A183 between Chester-le-Street and Bournmoor at 8.15pm on Sunday, December 28, last year.

When he saw a car approaching, he accelerated to complete the manoeuvre but still crashed head-on into the Mazda.

The Mazda driver, a carer returning from work, tried to swerve but had no time to avoid the crash, Durham Crown Court heard.

A witness behind the Mazda, who had to brake sharply to avoid the crash, said the Mercedes driver should have abandoned the overtake but instead accelerated to about 70mph.

![]()Brandon William Turnbull, jailed for three years for causing serious injury road accident and then fleeing scene, leaving other driver suffering life-threatening injuries

Prosecutor Charlie Thompson said the witness heard a “huge bang” as both cars’ front ends lifted into the air.

They were each were badly damaged and written off.

The witness found both drivers outside their cars and confirmed neither had a passenger.

Mr Thompson said the Mazda driver clutched his stomach, screamed in pain and bled from the mouth as the other driver hobbled down an embankment towards Chester-le-Street.

The badly injured driver shouted for someone to stop him leaving the scene.

Mr Thompson said the Mercedes driver made no attempt to check on the other driver, who was clearly badly injured.

Police searched the area with dogs and a helicopter, but found no sign of him.

Turnbull’s father was the registered keeper of the Mercedes, but the defendant was not at the address.

But he later appeared at University Hospital of North Durham’s accident and emergency department wearing shorts, with cuts and scratches to the legs suggesting he had walked through foliage.

Mr Thompson said Turnbull told someone in the waiting area he expected police to come looking for him after he crashed into a wall and fled, “through a swamp and over a golf course”.

After hearing hospital staff call out the name ‘Brandon Turnbull’, the man went outside and rang and report it to police, describing him as “loud” and “excitable”.

Two officers found Turnbull outside the A&E department in shorts, with mud covering his trainers.

He tested positive for alcohol and drugs but claimed, despite being barely able to walk, having gone to a pub after the crash where he bought cocaine.

Mr Thompson said the victim suffered life-threatening internal injuries and needed emergency surgery to save his life.

Doctors said he would probably have died without swift treatment.

He spent 40 days in hospital, then returned for another 15 days after complications developed.

Still suffering stomach pain and headaches, he returned to work in April because of financial hardship.

In his victim statement, he described flashbacks, PTSD and a fear of walking beside roads.

Turnbull admitted the dangerous overtake and fleeing the scene.

The 27-year-old, of Grasmere Terrace, Chester-le-Street, admitted causing serious injury by dangerous driving, failing to stop after a crash and driving while over the drink and drug-driving limits.

Caoimhe Bicknell said Turnbull accepted blame for the crash and fleeing the scene, withdrew his claim that he bought cocaine at a pub afterwards and admitted drinking alcohol and taking drugs before driving.

She said he was “genuinely remorseful and ashamed about his behaviour” and described his actions that night as “disgraceful”.

Miss Bicknell said Turnbull is a self-employed plumber with a previously clean driving licence and no past convictions.

She told the court Turnbull had suffered a significant family bereavement since the crash and was the main carer for his mother, who has motor neurone disease.

Judge Tom Mitchell said Turnbull was “highly impaired” by alcohol and cocaine when he attempted a “highly dangerous, aggressive driving manoeuvre.”

The judge called it “a moment of madness” which caused “grave injury”, leaving the victim abandoned in the road suffering lasting physical and psychological consequences.

He jailed Turnbull for three years, saying the offences were too serious for anything but immediate imprisonment despite his family difficulties.

Judge Mitchell also banned Turnbull from driving for 54 months and ordered him to pass an extended test before driving again.
