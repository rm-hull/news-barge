York is the next major city to join Yorkshire Water’s smart meter programme as the company nears 500,000 installations across the region.

The five-year scheme began in Sheffield in January 2025 and aims to replace 1.4 million water meters with smart technology by 2030.

The upgrades show customers how much water they use and help Yorkshire Water find and fix leaks faster.

![]()Smart meter installation *(Image: Supplied)*

James Wilson, manager of smart metering delivery at Yorkshire Water, said: "Smart meters are making a big difference by helping customers save money, helping us find and fix leaks faster, and helping us all to save water.

"York is the next destination to make the move to smart technology.

"Working with our partners, Netmore and MGroup, we are gathering pace towards exchanging 1,000 meters per day."

![]()Smart meter installation *(Image: Supplied)*

Yorkshire Water has installed 461,000 smart meters, including more than 325,000 replacements across Sheffield, Rotherham, Barnsley, Northallerton, Wakefield and Doncaster, plus 136,000 in new properties or conversions.

The programme has detected 16,300 possible leaks on customers’ properties since it began.

Customers were alerted through their smart meter data and 13,100 of those leaks have been repaired.

Yorkshire Water estimates the programme saves 16.5 million litres of water a day—more than 206,000 bathtubs’ worth.

Mr Wilson said: "Customers have really embraced the importance of using water wisely, understanding their own water use, and helping us to spot and fix leaks quickly, particularly over the very dry summer and numerous heatwaves.

"Smart meters send over 20 readings per day, providing accurate billing, and this increased visibility is helping customers understand their own water usage and make changes to cut down their usage where possible."

Yorkshire Water aims to halve leakage across its network by 2050, with smart meters playing a central role.

The meters provide hourly readings, helping detect leaks before they become bursts and cut disruption and costs.

Replacing a meter takes about 20 minutes.

Customers do not need to be at home if their existing meter is outside, but engineers will notify them before work starts.

For indoor meters, customers will get a text with a reference number and a link to book an appointment.

The water will be off briefly while the meter is replaced.

Mr Wilson said: “Even if customers are not due for a smart meter exchange just yet, we would recommend that they register for an online account as soon as possible, and it is straightforward to do."

Yorkshire Water is offering free water-saving kits, which customers can order from its website.

Find out more about the smart meter programme at yorkshirewater.com/smart-meters.
