The sudden death of her mother inspired a Yarm woman to take a chance on her passion and launch a jewellery business.

Kate Seow, 42, spent years balancing family life and a career teaching chemistry while making jewellery in her spare time.

But after her mother died unexpectedly in 2017, she found herself re-evaluating what she wanted from life.

Today, she runs Kate Seow Jewellery from a workshop in her home in Yarm and is preparing to showcase her work alongside some of the country's finest jewellers and silversmiths at a prestigious exhibition in North Yorkshire.

![]()Kate Seow Jewellery *(Image: Kate Seow)*

Kate said: "My mum died really suddenly and unexpectedly. It was one of those moments where you stop and think, 'What am I doing?'

"At the time I was teaching part-time and looking after my son, while my husband was working crazy hours as a junior doctor. I was finding everything quite stressful and then, with the grief of losing my mum on top of that, I just felt overwhelmed.

"It became one of those life re-evaluation moments. It brought into focus that we only have one life. I decided to stop teaching and focus on looking after my son and my family, but also see if I could turn the jewellery into a business.

"I wanted something that was mine, something I could take pride in and something that allowed me to keep my own identity."

![]()Kate Seow Jewellery *(Image: Kate Seow)*

Kate's love of jewellery began long before she launched her business.

As a child, she spent hours making jewellery from bead kits and selling handmade earrings at school charity events.

She said: "I've always been interested in jewellery. Even as a child I was making things. I think someone bought me a beading kit for a birthday and I started making little earrings and taking them to school charity events.

"I've always had those magpie tendencies. I love shiny things and I love making things. I can't sit still. If I'm not making jewellery, it'll be knitting or embroidery or something else creative."

Years later, after having her son, she rediscovered the hobby while on maternity leave.

![]()Kate Seow Jewellery *(Image: Kate Seow)*

"I wandered into Hobbycraft and saw a bracelet-making kit," she said.

"I thought, 'I haven't done this in a while.' It gave me something that was my own. When you have a young baby, your life revolves around looking after them and it was nice to have something creative that belonged to me."

After launching her business in 2018, Kate continued to develop her skills, eventually completing both a jewellery diploma and an advanced jewellery diploma.

![]()Kate Seow Jewellery *(Image: Kate Seow)*

She now creates pieces using precious metals and gemstones, specialising in silver and gold jewellery.

Kate said: "I really enjoyed the soldering side of things and the stone setting.

"The diploma suited me brilliantly because I could do it from home around family life. I'd send work off to my tutor, get it marked and then carry on improving.

"I absolutely loved it and my work has evolved so much over the years.

![]()Kate Seow Jewellery *(Image: Kate Seow)*

"Now I work predominantly with silver, gold and gemstones. I love creating rings and pieces with lots of colour where I can get creative with how the stones are arranged."

Alongside building her business, Kate has also focused on using responsibly sourced materials.

She said: "I've always been aware there can be some not very pleasant practices in parts of the jewellery industry.

"When I started working with precious metals and gemstones, I really wanted to think about where those materials came from.

"I've prioritised responsible sourcing from the start because I want my work to have a positive impact on the lives of everyone involved in its creation."

![]()Kate Seow Jewellery *(Image: Kate Seow)*

Kate's jewellery can be found in galleries and exhibitions, including PeaceGold in Bishop Auckland and Saltbox Gallery in Helmsley.

Kate says turning her passion into a business was the right decision.

She said: "I never imagined jewellery would become my career. It started as a hobby and something I loved doing.

"But losing my mum made me realise that life is short and if there is something you really want to do, you should give it a go.

"It was a difficult time in my life, but it pushed me to make a change. I'm glad I did."
