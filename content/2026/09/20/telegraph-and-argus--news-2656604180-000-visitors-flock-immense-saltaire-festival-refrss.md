Crowds flocked to the final day of the Bradford festival on Sunday.

Saltaire Festival chairman Ian Swailes said crowds had been “immense” this year.

Mr Swailes said: “We had 80,000 visitors two years ago and we will have done that again this year."

![]()A few of Saltaire Festival's team of volunteers

“The village is packed too, and we’ve had more traders than ever this year.”

Saltaire Festival trustee Hawarun Hussain said: “The festival is an opportunity for Saltaire to celebrate its heritage.”

“The people love it and it’s great for the businesses because they get a lot of trade.

![]()Salts Mill in the background of the field in Roberts Park packed with Saltaire Festival-goers

“Whatever you normally sell as a local business, during the festival you can get ten times as much.

“Shipley College is also an important partner of ours as many of their students volunteer to help us.”

Young musician Aaron Dulay volunteered this year and hopes his band, Trigger Thumb, can play at next year’s festival.

He said: “You can’t measure the cultural impact of Saltaire Festival amid the chaos of life.”

Sunday is the final day of the nine-day festival, featuring activities, stalls and live music.

![]()Ronnie Stone, 7, holds a White-faced Scopse owl at the Angel Falconry bird of prey display

Angel Falconry hosted a bird-of-prey display visited by Baildon’s Matt Stone and his seven-year-old son, Ronnie.

“Saltaire Festival is brilliant," said Mr Stone. "We come most years because it’s so family friendly.”

Young Ronnie bravely held a tiny white-faced scops owl from South Africa, which its handler said can “transform” as a defence mechanism.

![]()Crowds pack into and enjoy the delights of the Continental Market at Saltaire Festival

After Ronnie handed Gizmo back, the tiny owl ‘transformed’ before our eyes: its face changed, its eyes widened and its ‘horns’ rose when it spotted a dog on the path.

Roberts Park’s main field was packed on Sunday afternoon, with visitors listening to the main-stage band. In the Caroline Street car park, festival-goers sat at picnic tables as solo artist Indie Andrews performed covers of Dolly Parton’s ‘Jolene’, the Cranberries’ ‘Zombie’ and other hits.

Stallholders in the Continental Market, the main field and upper Roberts Park were doing brisk business.

David Stalbow, owner of The Cookie Pie Man, said: “There has been a lot of footfall and there are a lot of people.”

He said trade had been “very good”, with the festival’s second weekend busier than the first.

“This is my third year at the festival,” added Mr Stalbow.

Organisers said the Saturday before the final day was the festival's busiest day since 2019, before the Covid pandemic.
