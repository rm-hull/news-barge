A music charity near Darlington has been left facing a £10,000 shortfall after a wave of gig cancellations.

The Specialized Project CIC, based in High Coniscliffe, has spent the past 15 years raising funds for charities through music, including Teenage Cancer Trust as well as projects helping sick children and vulnerable families in the UK and abroad.

However, founder Paul Williams says a series of cancelled fundraising events has left the organisation struggling to meet costs and forced it to put new charitable plans on hold.

![]()Paul Williams CEO of Specialized Project CIC, music company Picture: SARAH CALDECOTT *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The 59-year-old believes the cost-of-living crisis has played a major part, with audiences increasingly unable to commit to buying tickets in advance.

He said: "This is the first time in 15 years that we've faced something like this. We've always done the same format and it's always worked. We have satellite events around the country and they are the lifeblood of the project.

"The money raised from those gigs goes straight back into the funding circle and helps us support projects here in the UK and abroad. This year we've lost the majority of those events and with them more than £10,000.

"It isn't just us. There are tours and shows being cancelled all over the country because people simply don't have the spare money they used to have. People are having to pick and choose what they can afford to go to."

The Specialized Project began after Mr Williams, a lifelong fan of The Specials, organised a fundraising tribute album inspired by the band's own charity work.

What was intended to be a one-off fundraiser quickly snowballed into a national project, with music releases, festivals and events attracting supporters from across the UK and overseas.

![]()Paul Williams CEO of Specialized Project CIC, music company Picture: SARAH CALDECOTT *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Since then, the organisation has built a dedicated community of volunteers, musicians and supporters.

Mr Williams said: "We've spent 15 years building this. People have met lifelong friends through our events. Some have even met their partners there.

"We bring together people from every background imaginable. We've had international bands come over, we've created a real community and that's what makes this so difficult.

"It doesn't just affect me. We've got volunteers who put in countless hours after finishing work. They give their time because they believe in what we're doing."

The project is currently trying to raise £5,000 through fundraising page to help cover essential costs and keep future plans alive.

Among those plans was a new hospital transport fund designed to help families whose children are receiving treatment away from home.

Mr Williams said: "We were on the verge of launching a hospital and carer transportation fund. The idea was to help families whose children are seriously ill and who suddenly find themselves travelling to different cities for treatment.

![]()Paul Williams CEO of Specialized Project CIC, music company Picture: SARAH CALDECOTT *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"People don't always think about the extra costs. You've got fuel, parking charges, hotels and food. It all adds up and there aren't enough places to get support from.

"We've had to put those plans on hold for now, which is heart-breaking because we know how much of a difference it could have made."

Despite the setback, Mr Williams said he is determined to keep the project going.

The organisation is preparing to celebrate its 15th anniversary with The Big One, a major festival in November and hopes a successful event can help secure its future.

He said: "I don't want this to end. I've put my heart and soul into it for 15 years.

"It would restore my faith in people because it would show that people still care about what we're trying to do.
