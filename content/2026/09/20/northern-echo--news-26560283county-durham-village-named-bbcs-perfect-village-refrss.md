A County Durham village once judged to be “perfect” by the BBC continues to stand out for its historic buildings, traditional village green and rural setting.

Heighington, around six miles from Darlington and close to Newton Aycliffe, was given the accolade in 2006 during a BBC programme presented by architect Ptolemy Dean.

The programme looked at the characteristics that make up the ideal English village, with Heighington ultimately judged to be a “perfect village”.

Heighington Parish Council says historical factors were among those considered, including the settlement's defensible layout, steep approach roads and narrow entrances.

![]()Heighington Village, County Durham *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

The village is centred around a green, with St Michael's Church forming one of its most recognisable landmarks.

The Grade I-listed church has origins stretching back to before the Norman Conquest, with later additions made over several centuries.

Historic England records that parts of its tower, nave and chancel are pre-Conquest, while the chancel was rebuilt during the 12th century.

Its tower was later altered and extended, while further additions were made during the medieval period.

Today, the church continues to overlook the centre of Heighington, where traditional properties surround the green.

The village also has a shop, village hall and pubs, while its position puts it within relatively easy reach of Darlington and Newton Aycliffe.

Heighington's appearance and setting have continued to make it a sought-after place to live.

![]()Heighington Village Hall *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Savills has previously described it as a “most desirable village”, highlighting the attractive green as well as its facilities and accessibility.

The estate agent also noted its links to the A1(M) and A66, making the village accessible for people travelling across the wider North East.

Heighington's history extends well beyond its picturesque centre.

The village is closely associated with the pioneering Stockton and Darlington Railway, which opened in 1825.

Around a mile and a half east of the village stands the former Heighington and Aycliffe railway station building.

The Grade II\*-listed structure was built in 1826-27 and is regarded by Historic England as an early prototype railway station.

![]()Heighington Village, County Durham *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Historic England describes it as possibly the world's first railway station and records that George Stephenson's Locomotion No.1 was first placed on the rails at the site in 1825.

The railway connection forms another important part of Heighington's heritage, alongside buildings around the historic village centre.

Darlington Council's conservation area appraisal says the settlement's layout changed relatively little from the medieval period through to the 19th century.

It also notes that a grammar school was founded beside the church in 1601, while buildings including the Manor House and Bay Horse date from the late 17th century.

Many other properties around the village date from the 18th century, when growing prosperity led to the construction and rebuilding of larger homes.

Despite later expansion, the historic green and surrounding buildings remain at the heart of Heighington.

Two decades after the BBC accolade, the combination of its history, architecture and traditional village setting helps explain why Heighington was once judged to be a “perfect village”.
