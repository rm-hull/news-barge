## Samsung R95H review: Two-minute review

Samsung’s Micro RGB lineup is its first foray into the RGB TV market, and the Samsung R95H is the flagship model in that lineup. It's an impressive TV, and a very strong performer in key areas such as movies and gaming; and, while I don’t think it quite beats the best OLED TVs, it's a good showcase for how RGB models are shaping up against the best TVs in general.

The R95H delivers great overall picture quality, with vibrant colors which also look accurate in most scenes, as well as strong contrast and refined detail. While its black levels don’t quite match those of OLED, they’re still very rich for a backlit TV. It’s not all smooth sailing, as the R95H can get overly bright, especially in Movie mode, and it’s not immune to blooming in very dark scenes. Still, its overall picture quality is of a high standard.

Sound quality on the R95H is solid for the most part, with good accuracy and detail, as well as commendable bass and solid mid-range tones. Speech is clear in most scenes I watched, and there is some sense of surround. However, the soundstage could be wider, and the volume does need to be pushed sometimes, so I’d recommend one of the best soundbars for those wanting a more of a home-theater experience.

The R95H is up there with the best gaming TVs, offering four fully-specced HDMI 2.1 ports along with a low 11.7ms input lag time for smooth gaming performance. There are tons of cloud gaming options available too, and Samsung's Gaming Hub is still an excellent feature. While its Game picture mode could be more accurate, this is still an excellent gaming TV.

Tizen is a good smart TV platform, giving you access to all the major streaming apps, as well as a plethora of AI tools for picture, sound and general usage. Its menus are generally well laid out and easy to navigate, and, while there are some frustrations, such as autoplaying Samsung TV Plus and large banner ads on the home screen, these don’t detract too much from what is a solid smart TV interface.

The R95H is a premium TV, with the 65-inch model priced at $2,799 / £2,899 / AU$4,999. This puts it at a similar price level to flagship OLED TVs, and while I don’t think it *quite* tops OLED, it shows how rapidly RGB tech is advancing.

A quick note on the images in this review: the TV may appear to have a red tint, but this is caused by the camera reacting to the anti-glare screen, and it not something that's discernible when viewing content on the TV.

## Samsung R95H review: Features

![Samsung R95H ports](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/bESQ4rEHi2cA549SSsFpq7.jpg)

* **RGB backlight**
* **Glare-free screen**
* **Samsung Vision AI and Tizen smart TV platform**

The R95H uses Samsung’s ‘Micro RGB’ backlight technology, which uses LEDs that are smaller than mini-LEDs, and which consist of red, green and blue LEDs (hence RGB) rather than the traditional white in order to deliver bolder colors and brightness.

It comes equipped with the Samsung Micro RGB AI Engine Pro processor, which has features such as Micro RGB Precision Color, 4K AI Upscaling Pro and Color Booster Pro. The TV itself also has a Glare Free screen, an anti-reflection matte screen that has featured in several of Samsung's TVs, including the Samsung S95H / S99H.

In terms of HDR support, the R95H supports HDR10+, but as usual, there is no support for Dolby Vision.

For gaming, the R95H has four HDMI 2.1 ports, which support 4K 165Hz, full VRR including Freesync and G-Sync, ALLM and HDR10+ gaming. It also supports cloud gaming apps from the likes of Xbox and Nvidia GeForce Now. The R95H is also compatible with the Wireless One Connect Box, which can be used in tandem with the TV’s built-in ports, meaning there are a potential eight HDMI 2.1 ports on offer.

The R95H has a 4.2.2 channel speaker array, totaling 70W of power. Supported sound features include Object Tracking Sound+ (OTS+), Active Voice Amplifier Pro, Adaptive Sound Pro and AI Sound Controller Pro. The R95H supports Dolby Atmos, but not DTS:X.

The R95H’s smart TV platform is Samsung’s own One UI Tizen, which supports major streaming apps including Netflix and Prime Video, and for those in the UK it supports all major apps including BBC iPlayer and ITVX. There's also support for Samsung Vision AI, which enables AI features such as AI Soccer Mode Pro and Generative Wallpaper.

* **Feature score: 5 / 5**

## Samsung R95H review: Picture quality

![Samsung R95H with shot of Peach looking at Lumas from The Super Mario Galaxy Movie on screen. Colors are vivid and bright](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DZJp5tHcAxdAKWrURgv8a8-1200-80.jpg)
![Samsung R95H with a shot of the Racer family kitchen from Speed Racer on screen. The R95H&#039;s colors are bold and vibrant](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/FcbnBZ9LYGvSK8v43Nptq8-1200-80.jpg)
![Samsung R95H with shot of Peach looking at Lumas from The Super Mario Galaxy Movie on screen. Colors are vivid and bright](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/DZJp5tHcAxdAKWrURgv8a8-1280-80.jpg)
![Samsung R95H with a shot of the Racer family kitchen from Speed Racer on screen. The R95H&#039;s colors are bold and vibrant](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/FcbnBZ9LYGvSK8v43Nptq8-1280-80.jpg)

* **Strong contrast and detail**
* **Bold colors**
* **Effective anti-glare**

Starting with some brightness measurements, the R95H registered 1,977 nits and 2,066 nits peak HDR brightness in Filmmaker mode and Standard mode respectively. These are solid brightness results, but surprisingly lower than those of Samsung’s flagship OLED, the S95H/S99H, which registered 2,739 nits and 2,831 nits in the same modes respectively.

Where the R95H has an advantage over OLED is in its fullscreen HDR brightness, which hit 612 nits and 600 nits in the same Filmmaker and Standard picture modes, roughly 150 nits higher than the S95H/S99H OLED. While these numbers are solid, they're lower than those of rival RGB TVs such as the Hisense UR8, which hit over 750 nits in both picture modes, and even the Sony Bravia 7 II, which hit over 800 nits in its own Filmmaker mode.

The R95H showcases good upscaling when watching lower-resolution and SDR content. Watching various channels on Samsung TV Plus, from America’s Got Talent re-runs to news channels, the picture looked more detailed than anticipated, showing the R95H’s effective upscaling.

Moving on to the reference 4K Blu-rays I use for testing, the R95H demonstrated very good picture quality. RGB’s selling point is its color reproduction, and the R95H succeeded here. Throughout *The Super Mario Galaxy Movie*, colors were bold and vibrant, shown in particular whenever the Lumas were on screen. Colors also appeared accurate throughout, with the red of Mario’s overalls looking true to life. I did notice that while watching in Movie mode, there was some clipping of bright objects, and color luminance could look a little distorted at times. Dropping the brightness down to 40 improved this.

In the live-action movie, *Speed Racer*, I was impressed at how the R95H rendered the gaudy, saturated color scheme of the Racer household, with its vibrant greens and oranges. It captured the oversaturated look, while still delivering realistic colors. While I did prefer the Sony Bravia 7 II’s colors in my Bravia 7 II vs R95H comparison, in isolated viewing, the R95H’s colors were impressive. The R95H did, however, reproduce accurate and realistic skin tones throughout my viewing.

Measuring the R95H’s color gamut coverage, it registered a staggering 148% of the DCI-P3 color gamut, as well as a 94.1% of the BT.2020 color space. These are incredible results, rivaling the best projectors, and the BT.2020 result rivals QD-OLED, found in Samsung's flagship S95H/S99H TVs.

![Samsung R95H with shot of Batman standing on subway platform from The Batman on screen. R95H shows excellent detail and contrast](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/uXgPR5JpycEVtVzHti5uo8-1200-80.jpg)
![Samsung R95H with shot of Bruce and Alfred in the Batcave from The Batman. R95H shows strong contrast](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/g33c3BsBrk5hv3ugK95yD8-1280-80.jpg)
![Samsung R95H with shot of Batman standing on subway platform from The Batman on screen. R95H shows excellent detail and contrast](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/uXgPR5JpycEVtVzHti5uo8-1280-80.jpg)

Switching to *The Batman* for higher-contrast and darker scenes, the R95H demonstrated strong contrast in almost every scene, with a great balance between light and dark tones. This was most evident in the crime scene at Mayor Mitchell’s house, and in the Batcave. The R95H also delivered excellent shadow detail, with objects like paintings on the wall of Mitchell’s house still legible even in the most shadowed areas.

I did find that while I could watch *The Batman* in bright conditions, thanks to the R95H’s effective glare-free screen, it was best watched in Filmmaker mode in more ambient/dimmer conditions. In Movie mode,*The Batman* had a tendency to look over-sharp and over-bright in places.

While its black tones weren’t quite OLED-level, the R95H did deliver some seriously rich, deep blacks in *Alien: Romulus*. Shots of space looked as inky as I could have hoped for, and were impressive for a backlit TV.

The R95H also demonstrated strong viewing angles, and while there was some blooming present in some scenes in *The Batman* and*Alien; Romulus* when I viewed the TV off-center, its backlight control was far more effective than you’ll see from standard LED and mini-LED TVs.

![Samsung R95H with shot of space from Aline Romulus on screen. R95H delivers deep black tones](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/huQrC23fb4V7yRBMok4w48.jpg)

Finally, for motion, the R95H is capable of handling fast-paced scenes from movies like *Speed Racer*, as cars weave around the track. It showed some judder as commentators panned across the screen, but I’ve found every TV has struggled with this. For the best motion, I found setting blur and judder reduction to 3 looked smooth without venturing into soap opera-effect territory.

For sports, I watched some Major League Soccer highlights via Apple TV, and the R95H not only handled the action well but produced a very natural picture in Standard mode (my preferred picture mode for sports) that I found pleasing to watch.

* **Picture quality score: 4.5 / 5**

## Samsung R95H review: Sound quality

![Samsung R95H with shot of Speed during a race from Speed Racer on screen](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/XDzkEw6ZG3kvYVxYZRge29.jpg)

* **Effective OTS+ results in great sound positioning**
* **Decent bass and mid range**
* **Sound can feel contained to rear of the screen**

The R95H’s built-in speaker array is a 4.2.2 channel, 70W system. It supports Dolby Atmos and has two preset sound modes: Standard and Amplify. There are AI sound features such as Adaptive Sound Pro available to use.

Watching the Batmobile chase scene from *The Batman* and the various race scenes in*Speed Racer*, with the TV set to its Amplify sound mode, I was unsurprised to hear that the R95H delivered accurate sound that was well mapped with the action on the screen, something I’ve found with a lot of Samsung TVs, thanks to its OTS+ feature. Whether it was a spray of bullets hitting the Batmobile in* The Batman*or the sound of the Mach 5 grinding a rail in* Speed Racer*, the sound accuracy was impressive.

The speakers deliver good mid-range tones throughout, as well as some punchy bass that accurately captures the rumble of the Batmobile’s engine and the roar of the race cars in *Speed Racer*. There were some impressive surround effects in both reference movies too.

While the speakers are generally decent, I did have to push the volume to higher levels (70+ out of 100) to get a more cinematic experience. The bass also felt flat at times, and sound did still feel contained to the rear of the screen. Turning on Adaptive Sound in the AI settings added more width, but at the cost of bass. While its sound is decent, those wanting a more cinematic experience would benefit from a soundbar.

* **Sound quality: 4 / 5**

* **One UI Tizen smart TV platform**
* **Hubs including Home and Game**
* **Good range of settings**

As with all Samsung TVs, the R95H uses Samsung’s own Tizen OS smart TV platform, now known as One UI Tizen. Tizen has several different hubs, including the Home hub, where apps and TV shows and movie recommendations are presented, and Game hub, where cloud gaming apps are housed. There are also Daily and Art hubs, for lifestyle and art-themed apps respectively.

Tizen supports a wide array of apps, including major streaming services such as Netflix, Prime Video and Disney. In the UK, streaming services such as BBC iPlayer and ITVX are supported. It should be noted that Tizen doesn’t use Freely or Freeview for live TV, instead using its own Samsung TV Plus system.

Various AI features are supported, including the Samsung Vision AI Companion, which can be used to find specific content, or answer more general questions that you ask the AI. Also included are other AI picture and sound features, as discussed in the Features section above.

Tizen is good at offering recommendations, and the Home hub is well laid out, but as with all smart TV platforms nowadays, there's a large banner ad at the top of the screen that can be intrusive when you're trying to navigate.

Menus are well laid out and easy to navigate, and there are a good number of settings, enabling you to tailor the picture to your preferences. Tizen is fairly straightforward to use, although I did find that Samsung TV Plus would play if I lingered on the Home menu, which could be frustrating.

* **Smart TV & menus score: 4.5 / 5**

## Samsung R95H review: Gaming

![Samsung R95H with Battlefield V and game menu on screen](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/gKR2FruPyJ45GCPbQibtt8.jpg)

* **Four HDMI 2.1 ports**
* **11.7ms input lag time**
* **4K 165Hz, FreeSync and G-Sync supported**

The R95H has four HDMI 2.1 ports, all of which are fully equipped for gaming. It supports 4K 165Hz, full VRR including Freesync and G-sync, HDR10+ gaming and ALLM. There's a dedicated Game menu where you can adjust game-specific settings, and the Game Hub, which houses cloud gaming apps such as Xbox, Nvidia GeForce Now, and Luna.

When I measured the R95H’s input lag, it registered an 11.7ms (4K 60Hz) time. This is below the 15ms input lag time we look for, but is higher than other Samsung TVs I’ve tested, which all tend to register sub-10ms times.

Actual gameplay on the R95H is smooth, even during fast-paced action. Playing *Battlefield V* on Xbox Series X, the R95H easily handled the chaotic gunfights as I had to snap from target to target, showing no signs of judder or stutter. The Game picture mode rendered crisp textures and accurate colors for the most part, though I did find it favored red tones in one desert mission I played. Overall, though, the R95H is a superb gaming TV.

* **Gaming score: 5 / 5**

## Samsung R95H review: Value

![Samsung R95H SolarCell remote](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/HPcmuk6LaEiQQjCic2TBk7.jpg)

* **Premium priced TV**
* **Similar price to OLEDs**
* **Good performance and features**

The R95H is a premium TV, with the 65-inch model I tested available for around $2,799 / £2,899 / AU$4,795 at the time of writing. This puts it in a similar price bracket to flagship OLEDs, such as Samsung’s S95H/S99H, which costs $2,799 / £2,899 / AU$5,295, and the LG G6, which costs $2,699 / £2,999 / AU$4,995 for a 65-inch. Compared to other RGB TVs I’ve tested, it’s one of the pricier models, but is cheaper than the rival flagship Sony Bravia 9 II, the 65-inch model of which costs $3,599 / £3,499 / AU$5,995.

The R95H commands a premium price, but does deliver premium performance and features similar to those rival OLEDs and RGB TV mentioned above. Since its release, prices for the R95H have dropped, and I anticipate they’ll continue to drop from now on, improving its value proposition. For now, I don’t think it can quite outshine rival OLEDs for the money, but it's close, and this is still a very good TV.

* **Value score: 4 / 5**

## Buy it if...

**You want brilliant picture quality**  
The R95H's picture is vibrant, colorful and detailed. Add in strong contrast, rich black tones and a glare-free screen, and the R95H's really impresses.

**You want a TV for gaming**  
The R95H is packed with all the gaming features you could need, and delivers terrific performance to match.

**You want to watch in a bright room**  
The R95H's glare-free matte screen makes watching in bright rooms easy, doing a great job at eliminating reflections.

## Don't buy it if...

**You're on a budget**  
The R95H is a flagship TV and carries a premium price. While it has excellent features and performance, cheaper RGB TVs and OLEDs with similar features are available.

**You want Dolby Vision HDR**  
The R95H, like all Samsung TVs, doesn't support Dolby Vision HDR.

**You want the very best picture quality**  
While the R95H boasts very good picture quality, it still doesn't quite beat the best OLEDs for me.

## How I tested the Samsung R95H

![Samsung R95H with white window for brightness testing on screen](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/gQnpkejTNq8PpqC42deLN8.jpg)

* **Tested over a few weeks**
* **Tested in varying light conditions in our testing lab**
* **Measurements taken using Portrait Displays Calman software**

The first step of my testing was casual viewing to establish the best pictures modes. For sports and daytime TV, Standard worked best, and for movies and TV shows, I switched between Movie and Filmmaker depending on the content.

After this, I did my critical viewing using reference 4K Blu-ray scenes to test color, contrast, motion and detail. I also used SDR broadcast TV to test upscaling, as well as sports to test the R95H's sports viewing.

Next, I tested the R95H using specialized measuring equipment, including a Murideo Six G 8K metal test pattern generator, Klein K-10A colorimeter and Jeti Spectral 15VA spectroradiometer and Portrait Displays' Calman color calibration software.

To test brightness, I took SDR and HDR brightness measurements using white window patterns in sizes 1-100%, focusing on peak (10%) and fullscreen (100%) measurements.

After this, I tested color and grayscale accuracy in SDR, using Delta-E values (the margin for error between the test pattern signal source and what is displayed on screen.)

Next, I tested HDR color gamut coverage, using the BT.2020 and UHDA-P3 color spaces. The closer to 100%, the better.

Finally, I measured the R95H's input lag using a Leo Bodnar 4K HDMI Input Lag Tester.

We also do two new tests as part of our testing. The first is the HDR EOTF, which shows the accuracy of a TV's brightness across its range, using 1,000, 4,000 and 10,000 nits targets. Then, there is the Spectral Power Distribution test, which measures how a TV recreates colors.
