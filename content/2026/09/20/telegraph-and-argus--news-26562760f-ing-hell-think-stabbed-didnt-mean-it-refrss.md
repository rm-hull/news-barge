A man accused of fatally stabbing a stranger during a commotion outside his girlfriend’s flat has said what happened was an accident.

Giving evidence on the fifth day of a murder trial at Bradford Crown Court, John Robinson said he was “scared and panicking” when he confronted two men who were shouting outside his girlfriend’s flat in Burley-in-Wharfedale at around 9.50am on March 1 this year.

Moments later, Mason Jones staggered away holding his abdomen, having been stabbed in the belly by Robinson, who was holding a chef’s knife.

Mr Jones, who was 30, was pronounced dead in hospital at 1pm.

Speaking from the witness box, Robinson said Mr Jones and Will Clarke were banging loudly on the door of the flat where his girlfriend Annabel Ramsay lived.

The two friends had travelled to Burley-in-Wharfedale from Bury and had been invited into the flat but were later thrown out following an argument.

They returned hoping to charge their mobile phones.

In the meantime, Miss Ramsay had called Robinson, asking him for help because they would not leave.

He arrived in a taxi that she paid for.

He took with him an Asda bag containing various items including a chef’s knife, which had been bought for him as a Christmas gift by Miss Ramsay when Robinson had been working as a chef.

He left the bag by the door to the flat.

During questioning by defence barrister Ian Henderson KC, he outlined what happened in the moments before the stabbing.

He said he was looking down on the scene from an upstairs window.

He said: “They shouted up aggressively, ‘Come down here now, you Pa\*i bastard. I’m going to smash your head in’.”

“I’m saying nothing, just ‘go away’ once.”

“I notice the front door open. It was Annabel. Mason then pushed Annabel.”

Robinson said he hadn’t noticed Miss Ramsay go downstairs and open the door.

He added: “I saw Mason try and push past Annabel to get into the flat. Annabel pushed him back.”

“I left the window in a hurry and went downstairs. I was going down to help her because she was getting attacked.”

“They [Annabel and Mason] were both shouting at each other. He [Mason] was trying to push her, basically, while I’m at the window.”

At this point, Robinson, who was dressed in t-shirt, tracksuit bottoms, and socks, said he decided to go downstairs.

He said: “I was rushing quite quickly. I nearly fell down the stairs, to be honest.

“I look out and I see Mason with his hands around Annabel’s throat, and Annabel against the wall.”

“Will [Clarke] is at the side of Mason. He was waving his arms about. I couldn’t tell if he was waving to Annabel or if he was trying to break them up.

“I was panicking. I looked down. See my knife. Pick it up with my left hand. Just grabbed it and left the property.”

Asked why he didn’t just rush out, he replied: “I was scared, panicked. Literally panicked.”

He added: “I was scared that Annabel could potentially die, and they could end up beating me up.”

He said he could see the knife in the bag and grabbed hold of the handle.

He said he wasn’t aware that the knife had slid out of its leather sheath or that its blade protectors were not in place.

Asked what he was intending to do, Robinson said: “Scare them away.”

He told the jury: “I shouted, ‘Let go! Get off her!’ from the door.”

“I took two, three steps towards Annabel and Mason. I was near enough there, tried to stop and slipped and ending up at Mason.

“Will was facing me and then turned around, facing into the alley. He was behind Mason.

“Mason was facing me at the time.”

“The knife was out in front of me, slightly forward in my left hand.”

Mr Henderson asked: “When you came down, was it a deliberate act? Did you move your hand forward to stab Mason?”

Robinson replied: “No.”

Henderson: “At the point that you came together with Mason, what was going on with Annabel?”

Robinson: “Quite loud. Trying to get him off her. Fighting back.”

Henderson: “Were you aware that your knife had penetrated Mason?”

Robinson: “No. I didn’t know exactly what had happened. I knew that I hit him. I knew that we collided but I didn’t know exactly where or what.

“I took the knife out. I pulled back. I could tell that I had hit something. When I pulled back it did feel like I was pulling away from something.

“Mason and Will then leave. I was in shock. I just heard Annabel screaming, ‘F\*\*\* off!’”

“Annabel came to the side of me. I said, ‘F\*\*\* ing hell, I think I’ve stabbed him. I didn’t mean to do it.’”

The couple then went back inside the flat where Robinson put on a jumper and jacket.

They decided to leave in a taxi as Robinson said he didn’t feel safe.

He put the chef’s knife back in its sheath in the Asda bag.

He told the jury: “I just saw feathers [from Mason’s puffer jacket on the knife]. I didn’t see any blood.”

“The police were going to be called inevitably. I didn’t want to do anything with [the knife]. I just wanted to put it back and leave it alone and see what happens.”

He said he only found out Mason Jones had died from his injury later, when in police custody having been arrested from his home in Derry Hill, Menston, at 11.29am.

Asked by Mr Henderson where the stabbing occurred, Robinson said: “I knew it was in the midriff somewhere. I wasn’t 100 per cent sure, but I thought it was in his belly.”

“I was scared. I was in shock. I couldn’t believe what had happened.”

Asked if he was running away, Robinson said he went home to tell his mum and say goodbye to his grandma, for whom he was a carer, and “potentially to get arrested”.

Mr Henderson asked: “Did you deliberately stab Mason Jones?”

Robinson replied: “No.”

Mr Henderson asked: “Were you angry or seeking revenge?”

Robinson replied: “No.”

Mr Henderson asked: “Were you trying to hurt him?”

Robinson replied: “No.”

He added that after being arrested and being told of Mr Jones’s death, he “just cannot believe it. I just did not mean to hurt anybody. I’m sorry it happened.”

Robinson, 30, denies murder, manslaughter, and having a knife in public.

The trial continues.
