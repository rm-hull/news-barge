A TEAM of dedicated volunteers is helping to preserve Bradford's heritage by restoring historic graves at a cemetery, ensuring hundreds of stories from the city's past are not lost to time.

Grade II\* listed Undercliffe Cemetery opened in 1854 and spans 26 acres.

From elaborate monuments of industrialists to unmarked graves, the cemetery contains around 23,000 burial sites, offering a unique insight into Victorian and early 20th century Bradford.

For the past 17 years, volunteer Graham Swain has spent his weekends repairing and reinstating fallen headstones across the cemetery.

## Graham and the team at work

Graham said what began as a desire to correct neglected and damaged memorials had grown into a major heritage preservation effort.

He said: "From day one we started lifting headstones up, crosses up, anything we thought was not right."

"Now we're a bit more involved. We do bigger projects."

The work is carried out entirely by volunteers, with no dedicated funding available for restoration work.

The project is supported by Graham's team, which includes Bradford Through the Lens, run by photographer Riaz Ahmed, who has documented the group's efforts for the past four years.

## T&A visits restorations

Last weekend, I visited the cemetery to see Graham and the team in action.

Arriving at 9:30am, Graham and Riaz, as well as volunteers John Bokalo and Craig Magson, had been hard at work for nearly two hours.

![]()Graham restoring a grave *(Image: T&A)*

The team had just completed work on the grave of Joseph Bentley, who served as Bradford's town crier for 20 years before dying at just 38 years old.

Graham said: "Also in the grave is his wife who died in her 70s, he's got his daughter who also died in her 70s, and he's also got his son in law and his son in law's wife in the grave."

Graham said this reflected Victorian funerary practices where multiple relatives were often buried in the same grave.

The volunteers also restored the grave of Henry Pullen, a theatre proprietor whose venue once stood above Rawson Market.

## Graves are 'part of our history'

Graham said the aim was not only to preserve Bradford's history but also to raise awareness that Undercliffe remains both a historic and active cemetery.

He believes decades of neglect allowed nature to reclaim large parts of the site, leaving many memorials hidden beneath overgrowth or toppled by time and the elements.

![]()Graham and the team at Undercliffe Cemetery *(Image: T&A)*

"What's happened at Undercliffe is that nature has actually taken over and for over 30 years it's been neglected," he said.

"At the end of the day, we have people in these graves, and they are part of our history of Bradford."

## 5,000 graves still require maintenance

Graham said preserving graves helps keep the stories of former Bradford residents alive for future generations.

"I need to leave a legacy for my grandchildren and my children," he said.

![]()Graham and the team at Undercliffe Cemetery *(Image: T&A)*

"If we all stand about waiting for everybody to do something else, nothing ever gets done."

He estimates the team has repaired between 300 and 400 graves so far, but says thousands more still require attention.

"We've got another 5,000 headstones to lift up. It's a big, big job," he said.

"If we didn't come here on a Saturday morning and do what we do, these would never get done.

"The district would lose the heritage."

![]()L-R Riaz Ahmed, Graham Swain, John Bokalo. *(Image: T&A)*

## Memorable stories

Among the most memorable projects undertaken by the volunteers was the restoration of the Atchison family memorial.

The grave commemorates brothers Ralph Graham Atchison and Harold Vivian Atchison, who were killed during the First World War.

Although neither is buried at Undercliffe, the memorial prompted volunteers to trace their stories to war cemeteries in Belgium, where they visited and located both men's graves.

In 2023, the volunteers commissioned a film documenting the brothers' stories, helping connect a Bradford memorial with events that unfolded on the battlefields of Europe more than a century ago.

"They're just some of the highlights," Graham said.

![]()Graham and the team at Undercliffe Cemetery *(Image: T&A)*

## Appeal for volunteers

For Graham, the work remains as important as ever.

"This to Bradford is a jewel in the crown," he said.

"This is history in stone."

The group is now appealing for more volunteers to help continue the restoration work, with weekly volunteer sessions held on Tuesdays and regular weekend projects.

More information on how to volunteer can be found here.

On plans for the future, Graham said: "There's no goal, there's that much to do here."

He continued: "Cemeteries need volunteers.

"We've got plenty of cemeteries in Bradford that need renovating. If you want to get involved, you'll be welcomed."
