A Durham County councillor has spoken out after being left in hospital after an attack.

Social Democratic Party (SDP) councillor Jack McGlenen, who represents Dalton and Dawdon, has issued a new statement after he was assaulted during a "cowardly" attack in Seaham on Wednesday (September 17), in which the suspect struck him in the face from behind.

He thanked Durham Constabulary officers who attended the attack and staff at Sunderland Royal Hospital who treated his injuries.

He also thanked residents for their support after an attack, vowing to keep serving his community.

![]()Councillor Jack McGlenen was attacked in Seaham on Wednesday (September 17). *(Image: Cllr Jack McGlenen)*

Cllr McGlenen said: "First of all, I would like to say a huge thank you to Durham Constabulary for coming straight out and supporting me after I was attacked.

"I'm extremely grateful for the help and support they provided during what was a very difficult time.

"I would also like to thank all the NHS staff at Sunderland Hospital who looked after me, treated my injuries and were so supportive throughout," he said.

"Your care and kindness meant a great deal to me."

Police were called to Colling Avenue in Seaham at 1.55pm, where a suspect was identified with investigations ongoing.

The attack left McGlenen with bruising and a cut under his eye, and he said a long-term eye injury meant he would be out of action for a while.

![]()Councillor Jack McGlenen was attacked in Seaham on Wednesday (September 17). *(Image: Cllr Jack McGlenen)*

A spokesperson for Durham Police said: “The man sustained bruising and a cut under his eye as a result of the incident.

"He was taken to hospital to be checked out.

"A suspect has been identified, and arrest attempts are ongoing.”

McGlenen also said residents, friends, family and fellow councillors had overwhelmed him with messages of support since the attack, insisting that the incident would not deter him from serving as a councillor.

"I want to make one thing very clear – I'm not going anywhere," he said.

"Being a councillor for Dalton and Dawdon is a responsibility I take extremely seriously.

"I'm proud to represent the residents of our community, and what has happened will not stop me from doing the job I was elected to do."

The councillor vowed to continue representing local people and carrying out the work voters elected him to do.

"My commitment remains the same: to listen to residents, stand up for our community, raise the issues that matter to local people and work hard to make Dalton and Dawdon, Seaham and the wider area a better place to live," he said.

"There is still plenty of work to do, and I will continue to be out in the community, speaking to residents and representing them to the best of my ability."

Cllr McGlenen was elected as a Reform UK councillor in May 2025 with 876 votes but was **expelled from the party for drunken behaviour at an Armed Forces Day event**.

He **joined the Social Democratic Party** last month.

The councillor said he missed a Police and Communities Together (PACT) meeting after the assault, but thanked everyone who contacted him.

McGlenen ended his message by once again thanking those who had supported him following the attack.

"Your support means more than you know," he added.
