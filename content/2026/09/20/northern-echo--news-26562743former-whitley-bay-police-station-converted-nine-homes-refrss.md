A former North East police station is being turned into homes while preserving the character of the landmark building.

Gosforth housebuilder Bluerock Homes is converting the old Laburnum Avenue police station in Whitley Bay into nine homes.

Original features are being retained wherever possible to preserve the building’s history.

Robbie Peel, co-founder of Bluerock Homes and a chartered building surveyor, said: "We wanted to retain as much of the character of the original police station as possible.

"It isn’t a listed building, but it is certainly a building of heritage interest, and the planning authority was keen to see its original features retained and restored where possible.

"I think we’ve achieved that."

![]()The former police station on Laburnum Avenue has been transformed over the past 18months by Bluerock Homes, headed by co-founder Robbie Peel, pictured here *(Image: Angela Carrington)*

The former police station has been transformed over the past 18 months.

Bluerock Homes restored the main station building and reused salvaged items in the design.

Distinctive Dutch gables facing the street were preserved, while a cell gate and ornate cast-iron ceiling roses were reused in the development.

The cell gate now forms part of the rear pedestrian entrance, while the refurbished ceiling roses are set into the new vehicle gates.

Mr Peel also saved original Northumbria Police signs and carpet bearing the force’s logo, framing them as reminders of the site’s past.

The development includes three- and four-bedroom homes at 1-9 The Old Police Station, Laburnum Avenue.

The new-build homes are three-storey houses with balconies, while the converted sections are mainly two storeys.

One home combines the original building with a new extension and has a large, light-filled studio at the top.

The former magistrates’ court was demolished to make room for a courtyard and parking, while other buildings were removed because of structural and asbestos problems.

The developer built four energy-efficient townhouses using structural insulated panels (SIPs).

Bluerock Homes specialises in small and medium-sized residential developments across the North East.

Its past projects include executive homes in Yarm, student housing in Newcastle and seafront apartments in Whitley Bay.

Mr Peel began his career after studying at Northumbria University, taking a role at a multidisciplinary practice in Hexham.

He later moved into property refurbishment before progressing to full-scale development.

His first major scheme involved converting a community hall in Morpeth into five homes.

The police station project has special meaning for Mr Peel, who shares his name with Sir Robert Peel, widely regarded as the founder of the modern police force.

He said: "The fact that my name is the same as the person who basically invented the police force hasn’t escaped me and that makes it all the more important that I get this development banged to rights."

Cooke & Co in Whitley Bay is marketing the homes.
