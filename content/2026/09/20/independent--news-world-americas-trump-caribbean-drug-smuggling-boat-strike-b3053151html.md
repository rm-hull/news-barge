Four people were killed on Saturday when the U.S. military struck a suspected drug smuggling vessel in the Caribbean Sea, marking the latest action in the Trump administration's ongoing operation against alleged Latin American traffickers.

This strike raises the death toll from American military vessel attacks to at least 231 individuals across 69 operations since the administration began targeting self-described "narcoterrorists" nearly a year ago.

Consistent with previous statements regarding strikes in the Caribbean Sea and eastern Pacific Ocean, U.S. Southern Command indicated it focused on suspected traffickers along established smuggling routes.

No proof was provided by the military to confirm the vessel carried drugs.

Video footage released on X depicted what appeared to be a moving boat followed by a flash.

The Trump administration is attempting to secure agreements with regional partners to expand its offensive onto land.

Speaking in Panama last month, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth stated that Colombia, Guatemala, and Honduras had consented to joint U.S. military actions against criminal networks on their territory.

Guatemala denied making such an agreement, though Ecuador began similar missions alongside the U.S. in March.

Declaring that the U.S. is in an "armed conflict" with Latin American cartels, President Donald Trump has defended the strikes as essential to curbing drug flows and fatal American overdoses.

Yet, his administration has presented little evidence to substantiate claims that those killed were smugglers.

Critics of the strikes have questioned both their legality and their effectiveness.

Part of the argument has been that the fentanyl behind many fatal U.S. drug overdoses is typically trafficked over land from Mexico, where it is produced with chemicals imported from China and India.
