Earl Spencer has accused the King of gaslighting him over claims in his bombshell memoir that Charles said “we’ll forget her soon enough” after Princess Diana died.

The earl has made a series of damning claims about the King in a new memoir chronicling the life of his sister Diana that prompted the response from Buckingham Palace, which said “the pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason”.

Now, the earl has accused the King of gaslighting him and called the response a “rather poor imitation of the ‘recollections may vary’ quote” – the late Queen’s response to the Duke and Duchess of Sussex’s allegations in their Oprah Winfrey interview, *The Mail on Sunday* reports.

“It is not every day you wake up to find you’ve been gaslit by the King,” Earl Spencer told the newspaper.

“It was astonishingly provocative and below the belt because it was essentially trying to say I’d lied without the King being able to deny what I’d said.

Buckingham Palace is bracing for a new TV interview with Earl Spencer set to be aired by CBS News on Sunday.

## When is Earl Spencer's book due to be released?

Earl Spencer’s book titled *Swan Song: Diana, My Sister*, is set to be released globally on Tuesday.

However, the pages have been serialised by the Daily Mail, leaking some of the most explosive allegations ahead of publication.

An interview with Diana’s brother and BBC broadcaster Laura Kuenssberg will also be released on Sunday evening, in which more revelations are expected.

## Prince Charles ‘had no place at his his ex’s funeral’

Earl Spencer recalled his sister’s funeral as a “a form of mental torture,” stating that he believed neither Prince Charles, or Princes William and Harry should have been in attendance.

Sharing his feelings on the funeral procession, one passage read: “What was Prince Charles actually doing there? I thought at one moment.

“This ex-husband, from a marriage that had collapsed so bitterly four years before and finally ended in divorce a year earlier, surely had no place at his ex’s funeral.

“Supporting his sons was the reason given for his prominent place, but what were the boys doing there, anyway? It all seemed mad to me.

“I was anxious that I could not look sideways to check how Harry was doing. But at one point I found us marching briefly out of sight, under a massive arch. Relieved that nobody could see us here, I quickly leant across to Harry, and gave him an encouraging hug. ‘Well done, Harry!’ I said. ‘You’re doing bloody brilliantly!’”

## King Charles 'saddened that old sorrows are being dragged back up', says friend

The row between Buckingham Palace and Earl Spencer, whose bombshell claims about King Charles’ conduct before and after Princess Diana’s death have sent shockwaves through the royal family, is rapidly deepening.

One aide told the outlet: “The King was rather more concerned about the future of mankind than the unkind claims of one man picking at the scars of the past.”

Another friend, speaking of Spencer’s claim that he had written his memoir to “to tell the truth about Diana”, responded: “That’s like an arsonist trying to sell you a fire hose and about as convincing as all those others close to Diana who’ve cashed in on her confidences over the years.

They added to the Times: “It is an irony lost on no one except perhaps the author that a man who once accused the media of hounding Diana to the grave has now peddled in the public bazaar what little remained of her privacy.”
