The wife of top Trump administration official Stephen Miller says she fears looming legal bills if Democratic wins in the crucial midterm elections lead to congressional subpoenas for the president's inner circle.

”After the first Trump administration, my family spent money we did not have on lawyers because of multiple partisan Democrat led investigations into President Trump,” Katie Miller said Saturday on social media. “I watched as many of my friends also had no idea how to pay expensive legal bills because they simply served their country.”

She added: “You can’t convince me Democrats won’t do this again.”

Democratic Rep. Robert Garcia of California, ranking member of the House Oversight Committee, told Bloomberg Government in April that Stephen Miller was among several people the committee would subpoena if Democrats take control after the midterms.

Garcia also mentioned former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, her former associate Corey Lewandowski, FBI Director Kash Patel and now-Attorney General Todd Blanche.

“They would all be in front of us pretty consistently,” Garcia said at the time.

Katie Miller’s comments came in response to a Bloomberg podcast in which New York Times reporter Maggie Haberman put the likelihood of billionaire Elon Musk being subpoenaed at “100 percent.”

“People have privately told me that’s what they plan to do. I think that they will,” said Haberman, co-author of the best-selling book *Regime Change: Inside the Imperial Presidency of Donald Trump*.

Katie Miller served as an adviser to Trump's ad hoc, Musk-led Department of Government Efficiency before leaving the administration in May 2025 to work for Musk, whose estimated $946.5 billion net worth makes him the world's richest person.

Three months later, she told *Axios* that she'd left that job to launch a podcast for conservative women. It now has about 73,000 YouTube subscribers.

In April 2022, Stephen Miller — a two-term Trump aide who's now his deputy chief of staff for policy and homeland security adviser — testified by video for more than eight hours after receiving a subpoena from the Democrat-led House January 6 select committee.

He also testified in April 2023 before the federal grand jury that later indicted Trump for allegedly conspiring to prevent Congress from certifying his 2020 electoral loss to Joe Biden.

Special counsel Jack Smith dropped that case, and another in which Trump was accused of illegally hoarding classified documents after leaving the White House, following Trump's 2024 victory over Biden's vice president, Kamala Harris.

Katie Miller, who was an aide to Vice President Mike Pence during Trump’s administration, avoided having to testify about the January 6, 2021, attack on the Capitol by Trump supporters because she was on maternity leave at the time.

Republicans hold a narrow, 219-214 House majority with two vacancies, and the GOP also controls the Senate, 53-47.

Polls show Democrats leading in the so-called generic congressional race, 50.4 percent to 42.3 percent, according to Real Clear Polling, but the *Cook Political Report* rates future control of both chambers as a toss-up.
