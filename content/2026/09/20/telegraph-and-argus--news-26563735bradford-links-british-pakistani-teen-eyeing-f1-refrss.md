Haarni Sadiq is a 16-year-old British Pakistani racer with huge dreams.

He is aspiring to become the first Formula 1 driver in history to represent Pakistan.

He has strong connections to Bradford, with his father brought up there and Haarni himself spending lots of time in the city with extended family.

He will be telling his story at an industry event held at the Silverstone Museum this coming Friday, while we spoke to the teen and his family for a Q&A to learn all about his racing career and background.

**Has Haarni always had the ultimate dream of being an F1 racing driver?**

Not really, because Haarni only started karting when he was 13.

It was something he took to very quickly, and once he started racing competitively and realised he was good at it, his ambitions began to grow.

As he progressed through karting and then into single-seaters, Formula 1 became the ultimate goal.

What started as something he enjoyed quickly became something he was completely focused on and wanted to pursue seriously.

In some ways, the fact that he started later than many drivers has made him even more determined.

He knows he has had to learn quickly and make the most of every opportunity, and that has become a big part of his mindset.

**Just how has Haarni managed to progress so quickly over the last two years?**

I think it has been a combination of natural ability, a huge amount of hard work and being willing to learn very quickly.

Because Haarni started karting later than a lot of drivers, he has always known that he has to make the most of every session and every opportunity.

This has been combined with intense work alongside experts such as Steve Deeks, Chief Race Instructor at Silverstone Circuit.

He listens closely to his coaches, studies where he can improve and is very honest with himself when something hasn’t gone well.

He has also been fortunate to have experienced people around him who have helped guide his development.

The biggest thing is probably his determination.

He has gone from starting karting at 13 to racing in British F4 in a relatively short space of time, and that progression has only been possible because he has treated it seriously from the moment he realised this was something he wanted to pursue.

**We've seen the lack of black F1 drivers, aside from Lewis Hamilton, so in a similar vein, would that be an extra special achievement for Haarni to make it, as someone with British-Pakistani heritage?**

Haarni is incredibly proud of his British-Pakistani heritage, and if he were to reach Formula 1 and represent Pakistan, it would be a hugely significant achievement for him personally and for the communities he represents.

Motorsport has made progress in becoming more diverse, but it can still feel difficult to access for many young people, particularly those who do not traditionally see people from similar backgrounds represented within the sport.

Haarni would love to use his own journey to help change that perception.

Lewis Hamilton has shown the impact a driver can have far beyond the racetrack, inspiring people through both his achievements and his work away from racing.

Haarni hopes that, in his own way, he can encourage more young people from British-Pakistani and other under-represented backgrounds to believe that motorsport can be a place for them too.

And that does not just mean becoming a racing driver.

Motorsport offers an enormous range of careers in engineering, technology, data, design, media and many other areas.

If Haarni’s story can help introduce young people to those opportunities, that would be something he would be extremely proud of.

**Can Haarni tell us a little about his links to Bradford?**

Bradford has always been a big part of my life because so much of my extended family is there.

Growing up, I spent a lot of time in the city visiting family, so I’ve always felt a real connection to it.

Some of my best memories are around family gatherings, weddings and social events.

There was always lots of food, music and people coming together, and I loved the energy and sense of community.

Food was definitely a big part of it too – being surrounded by Pakistani food and culture from a young age made those visits really special.

Although I didn’t live in Bradford full-time, spending so much of my childhood there helped me feel connected to my Pakistani heritage and to a much wider family and community.

Those experiences have stayed with me as I’ve grown up and are an important part of who I am today.

That’s one of the reasons I’m so excited about the event as there’s lots of people coming to join us from the Bradford area.

**And we know that Bradford has a large British-Pakistani diaspora, just how good would it be to act as an inspiration to young boys of a similar age from our city?**

It would mean a huge amount to me.

Bradford has always felt like part of my story because of my family and the time I spent there growing up, so if my journey can inspire other young British-Pakistani's from the city, that would be really special.

Motorsport can sometimes feel like a world that is very difficult to get into, especially if you don’t see many people from a similar background doing it.

I’d like young people in Bradford to look at what I’m doing and think, “Maybe I could do something in Motorsport too.”

For me, it’s bigger than just racing.

If my journey encourages even one young person to believe that their background doesn’t have to limit their ambitions, whether that’s in motorsport, engineering or anything else, I’d be really proud of that.

**How honoured is Haarni to be hosting next Friday’s event at such a prestigious venue?**

Haarni is incredibly honoured to be hosting the event at Silverstone.

The idea for the evening actually came from conversations between Haarni and Steve Deeks, with Haarni wanting to create something that could open young people’s eyes to the opportunities that exist within motorsport and the wider industry.

The invitation was aimed at young people from diverse, under-represented and lower-income backgrounds, and the response has been far beyond what he could have hoped for.

The level of interest has made the event feel even more meaningful, because it shows there is a real appetite for young people to be given access, encouragement and the chance to see what might be possible for them.

To then be able to hold the first event in this programme at Silverstone, the home of British motorsport, really is the icing on the cake.

For Haarni, it brings together everything the evening is about – motorsport, opportunity and inspiration – and he could not be more proud to be part of it.

**And what does that say about Haarni's confidence, that he is able to deliver this talk in front of all these prominent figures etc at the age of just 16?**

Whilst Haarni is most comfortable at speed behind his helmet, he has a natural charisma in front of the camera as seen on his growing social media channels.

It shows how much confidence Haarni has developed through motorsport.

At 16, to stand up in front of successful business leaders, industry figures and guests and speak openly about his journey takes a lot of maturity.

Racing has helped him become comfortable under pressure.

He is used to being questioned about his performance, having to make quick decisions and dealing with both good and difficult results, so speaking in front of people is another challenge he has learned to embrace.

What is probably most impressive is that he is still very young and still learning, but he is confident enough to share his ambitions and his experiences honestly.

Hopefully that makes him relatable to other young people too, because they can see someone their own age having the confidence to step forward and give something a go.
