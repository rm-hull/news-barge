A North East beach has been ranked among the country’s worst, but visitors have rushed to defend the secluded spot.

Hendon Beach in Sunderland ranked eighth from the bottom in a new UK-wide table based on Google reviews.

The beach, south of Sunderland Docks, scored an average 4.21 stars from about 170 reviews, according to the study.

Despite its lowly position, more than half of reviewers gave Hendon Beach five stars, praising its peaceful atmosphere, sea views and dog walks.

The ranking was produced by family holiday specialist Lapland Famille, which analysed Google Maps ratings for more than 2,000 UK beach listings.

Coastal beaches with at least 100 reviews were ranked by their average score from all star ratings.

Lower-scoring reviews focus on Hendon’s lack of facilities and the difficulty of reaching it without a car.

A visitor who left a two-star review last year said there was “nothing there”, called the beach hard to reach and complained of poor public transport.

However, that appears to be exactly what appeals to some of Hendon's biggest fans.

A five-star reviewer called it their “go-to place for a short walk”, saying it might not be the area’s best-known beach but was still “home” and a place they loved returning to.

Another recent visitor said it was “always nice to come by the seaside”, even on a wet and windy day.

Others have praised Hendon for being quiet, dog-friendly and a good place for walking and fishing.

One reviewer called it a “lovely beach” which was “so peaceful and quiet”, while another described it as a “great spot for walks” with dogs.

Another five-star review praised Hendon as a year-round place to relax, highlighting its sunrise and sunset views and plentiful parking.

Another visitor summed up Hendon's appeal by describing it as a “nice little secluded beach” which was particularly good for a walk along the shoreline.

Online reviews also describe Hendon as clean and peaceful, and a good place to eat fish and chips while looking out to sea.

The beach itself is a mixture of sand and shingle, with grassy coastal paths stretching towards Ryhope Cliffs.

Unlike the North East’s better-known seaside resorts, it has few traditional attractions and facilities.

Reviewers repeatedly praise its quieter character, citing walks, fishing, picnics and sunrise views as reasons to return.

The table measures Google review scores, not bathing water quality, scenery, cleanliness or facilities, while Hendon’s average remains above four stars.

Lapland Famille’s table named Splott Beach in Cardiff the UK’s lowest-rated beach, with an average score of 2.99 stars.
