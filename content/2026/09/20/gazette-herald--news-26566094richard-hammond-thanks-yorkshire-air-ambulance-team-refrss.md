TV star Richard Hammond has paid tribute to the staff of the Yorkshire Air Ambulance – exactly 20 years after they saved his life.

In an Instagram post to mark the anniversary of the day the former Top Gear presenter crashed on Elvington airfield at 288mph, he described how he was convinced he was about to die.

He was filming for the programme by driving a jet-powered dragster called Vampire.

![]()Richard Hammond relives his near-fatal crash *(Image: Ross Tomkins/Alamy/PA)*

“I am only here today to note this anniversary and write these words because of the work of the many people involved in rescuing me from the shattered car, flying me to hospital and treating me there,” he wrote in an Instagram post today.

He said when a tyre burst, he had pulled the lever to release the parachute and done his best to steer the car from disaster.

“But disaster was, it seemed, my fate and having done everything I could do avert it, I gave myself over to that fate.”

Hammond went on to say that he “didn’t scream” during the incident and said it “wasn’t frightening”, adding that “it simply occurred to me that the next thing on my to do list was to die”.

He suffered serious head injuries and was flown by the air ambulance to Leeds General Infirmary.

He named the members of the Yorkshire Air Ambulance team and aftercare teams who helped him saying: “I have had so many lucky career breaks, so many amazing adventures around the world and am father to the two happiest, kindest, most wonderfully talented and loving daughters.

“But I would have seen none of them if these people hadn’t turned up to work that day and done their job.

“Thank you all from the bottom of my heart.”

![]()One of Yorkshire Air Ambulance's two ambulances. *(Image: Yorkshire Air Ambulance)*

Yorkshire Air Ambulance said in a Facebook post: “On that day, our crew responded to his crash at Elvington Airfield, providing critical care at the scene before he was flown to hospital.

“We are so pleased we were able to have helped when needed – and grateful to Richard for remembering our crew and the work of YAA.”

Following the crash, his wife Mindy told his supporters not to send him flowers in hospital but instead give money to Yorkshire Air Ambulance, a charity that does not receive funding from either the Government or the NHS.

Donations flooded in and in October 2007, just over a year later, it was able to expand by acquiring a second helicopter.
