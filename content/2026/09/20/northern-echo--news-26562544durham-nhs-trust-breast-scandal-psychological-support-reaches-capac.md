Psychological services for women affected by failings in County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust’s breast service are now full.

The trust opened a dedicated psychology clinic in autumn 2025 for patients suffering psychological harm after its breast-services review.

But new board papers, released ahead of an extraordinary meeting on September 24, show both the trust’s consultant psychologist-led service and its externally commissioned psychotherapy provision are full.

The papers come as health bosses consider widening the breast-surgery review to thousands more historical cases, potentially dating back to 2015.

![]()County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

The trust says every patient referred to its dedicated psychology clinic recorded scores showing “clinically significant levels of anxiety and/or depression”.

Some patients have also displayed symptoms consistent with post-traumatic stress disorder.

The papers say the “psychological impact on some patients and families has been considerable” and warn that demand will grow as more reviews are completed.

The clinic assesses patients identified by the review as having suffered psychological harm.

Patients can then be signposted or referred elsewhere, or receive psychological treatment from the trust.

![]()Darlington Memorial Hospital *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Board papers prepared for an extraordinary public meeting on September 24 say psychological care must “move away from a reactive approach”.

They call for a “proactive”, “substantively funded” service with dedicated resources and enough capacity for future demand.

The trust says staff carrying out the review also need support because of the “emotionally difficult nature of reviewing cases” involving avoidable harm.

The warnings come as concern grows over the number of women suffering physical and psychological harm after treatment.

So far, 20 women have been identified as having unnecessary mastectomies.

One is 51-year-old Denise Howarth from Consett, who was diagnosed with grade one cancer in her left breast in February 2023.

She had a lumpectomy and lymph nodes removed, then was told at a follow-up appointment that cancer remained and she needed a mastectomy.

She underwent the operation days later.

![]()Denise Howarth *(Image: SUBMIT)*

But Ms Howarth was contacted last year and told the mastectomy had not been necessary after all.

She said the news left her feeling “betrayed, hurt and angry” and badly damaged her confidence.

The trust’s latest figures show that 315 of 514 reviewed patients suffered some level of harm.

Of those, 77 suffered significant harm, and one patient is known to have died.

The review found missed or delayed cancer diagnoses and patients undergoing more extensive surgery than necessary.

The trust board is considering extending the review beyond its current January 2023 to February 2025 timeframe.

The broadest proposals could mean about 4,500 cases are reviewed over five years.

The board papers warn that any extension would “increase demand for psychological support”.

The trust says it remains “deeply sorry” to patients and families harmed or let down by failings in its breast services, and that significant changes have since been made.
