The investigation into failures in County Durham and Darlington’s breast services cost more than half a million pounds in the last financial year alone, new documents reveal.

County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust spent £523,830 on its breast surgery lookback during 2025/26, with the largest share going to the dedicated team carrying out and coordinating the review.

The figures are contained in papers prepared for an extraordinary public board meeting on September 24, when the Trust will decide how far back the breast services lookback should extend.

The papers show £258,653 was spent on the lookback team, made up of three full-time nursing staff and one full-time administrative assistant.

![]()County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

A further £177,153 was spent on sessional reviewers, while £83,024 went on psychology support and £45,000 on legal services.

The Trust says the eventual cost of the programme will depend heavily on how large the review becomes and how long it continues.

Its board paper says: “Future costs will be influenced by the scope and duration of the programme and the number and complexity of cases requiring review.”

It adds that the financial estimates are “provided for information only”, stressing that decisions over the future of the lookback will be driven by patient safety, the need to identify and address harm, and the Trust’s responsibilities to patients and families.

The current lookback focuses mainly on patients treated between January 2023 and February 2025.

![]()Chief executive of the County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust, Steve Russell *(Image: Chris Booth)*

However, the Trust is considering extending the review further into the past, potentially towards 2015.

The two widest options being considered could involve around 4,500 reviews and take approximately five years to complete at the current sustainable rate of around 80 reviews a month.

Its board papers say each case can require "detailed scrutiny of clinical notes, imaging, correspondence and diagnostic reports, while some patients need input from more than one expert."

The Trust also warns that looking further back could "significantly increase the workload" because historic records from before October 2022 were "largely paper-based and have since been scanned into electronic systems."

A dedicated lookback team was established in September 2025, while a specialist psychology clinic was set up later that year to support patients identified as having suffered psychological harm.

The Trust says both its internal psychology provision and externally commissioned psychotherapy support have since reached full capacity.

Board members will decide the future scope and timeframe of the lookback at the meeting on September 24.
