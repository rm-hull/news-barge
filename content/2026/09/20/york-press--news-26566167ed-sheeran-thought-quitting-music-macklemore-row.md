Ed Sheeran has said he questioned whether he wanted to continue as an artist.

The singer made the comments during his first performance since controversy erupted over the removal of US rapper Macklemore from his world tour, following Macklemore's public comments on the conflict in Gaza.

During the show at Lincoln Financial Field in Philadelphia, Sheeran told the audience it had been a difficult week.

He said: "I really didn’t know whether this tour would be carrying on.

"I really didn’t know whether I’d be even getting on stage again, whether I even wanted to be an artist at all.

"In these situations, you really find out who your friends are, and it’s been a weird week.

"I just feel like it happened very quickly."

Macklemore was removed from the tour after speaking out about the conflict in Gaza.

Following his removal, several support acts and Sheeran’s backing band withdrew from the remaining tour dates.

Sheeran opened the Philadelphia show by acknowledging he had made “mistakes” and publicly condemned the violence in Gaza as “catastrophic” and “unjustifiable.”

He said: "I cannot hide how I feel about it anymore.

"What happened in Israel and at the Nova music festival on October 7 was horrific and compounded centuries of Jewish pain.

"What is happening in Gaza is catastrophic and unjustifiable and disproportionate.

"My heart has been broken by the scale of devastation and loss of civilians’ lives, children’s lives.

"And the systemic injustice we are seeing unfold in the West Bank cannot be overlooked."

He closed the concert with a message of hope, despite the turmoil.

Sheeran said: "The world is a scary place right now.

"America is a scary place right now.

"This gives hope."

Fans at the show expressed their support for the singer.

James Webber IV from Scranton, Pennsylvania said: "I stand with Ed.

"I think there’s a time for politics and a music concert is not one of those places."

Kassandra Rivera from Philadelphia said: "Everyone has their own opinion and it’s fine.

"I came here to have a good time and watch Ed Sheeran.

"I’ve been waiting a whole year to come and see him, so I’m excited to see the show."

Dwayne Smith from Canada said: "We’re just here for Ed Sheeran.

"All politics aside, we just like the music.

"We are here to support him.

"It’s not about Palestine or Israel tonight, it’s just about the music for us."

Outside the venue, a small pro-Palestine protest took place near the stadium’s subway station.

Acts who withdrew from Sheeran’s tour include Irish singer Aaron Rowe, Grammy award-winning musician Finneas, Danish artist Lukas Graham, and Sheeran’s backing band Beoga.

On Monday, Macklemore claimed on Instagram that Sheeran and his team had removed him from the tour.

Sheeran later clarified on Instagram that the decision had been made by the tour promoter, not by him personally.

He said: "Upcoming tour dates had communicated that they would pull the shows if he was still supporting," and identified Robert Kraft, owner of Gillette Stadium, as one of those who raised concerns.

Sheeran said he was "appalled by the conflict between Israel and Palestine" and expressed respect for Macklemore "as an artist."

The singer’s world tour began in Auckland, New Zealand, in January and is due to conclude in Mexico City in December.

Sixteen shows remain, with stops across North and South America.
