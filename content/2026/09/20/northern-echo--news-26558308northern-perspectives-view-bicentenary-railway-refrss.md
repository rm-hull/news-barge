AS the year since the bicentenary of the Stockton & Darlington Railway was celebrated has flown by, we asked our regular photographic contributor, Northern Perspectives, whose drone flew alongside the replica of Locomotion No 1, to look back – or, more accurately, look down – on the recreation of the inaugural journey.

It is estimated that 100,000 people over three days, and from all over the world, lined the track from Shildon to Stockton.

The centre piece of the weekend was Saturday, September 26, when the replica engine travelled across the Skerne bridge, recreating the famous painting and the view that was on the back of the £5 note during the 1990s.

About 6,000 beneath the bridge cheered so loudly at the engine that, like a rock star, it did several encores.

![]()Dave Wilkin with the Northern Perspectives' drone *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

“It was a great weekend, and I took thousands of pictures and videos which got more than 2.5m views on my Facebook page,” says Dave Wilkin, the man at the controls of the Northern Perspectives drone.

“It is really nice to be able to share them again.”

The 201st anniversary celebrations next weekend are obviously not on such a scale, but at Hopetown in Darlington, there will be rides behind the replica Locomotion No 1 and the Tornado mainline engine will be on display.

In the carriageworks, the Memory of a Journey exhibition of 9,250 travelling peg dolls is well worth a look before it comes to an end.

Next Sunday, from 10am to 4pm, the Friends of the S&DR are holding a free festival at Heighington station, the world's first railway station, on the edge of Newton Aycliffe.

There will be everything from live music to archaeology to children’s readings to pizzas, beer, coffee and cupcakes, plus a chance to see inside the derelict station building and hear about the plans for its revival.

![]()This is as near to 1825 as it is possible to get in 2025: Locomotion No 1 trundling through the undeveloped south Durham countryside. “It was very difficult to get access to the line, but I knew the farmer who said I could go on his land,” says Dave. This was taken at Old Towns Quarry between Shildon and Newton Aycliffe. It was the original site of the settlement of Middridge but about 1,000 years ago, the villagers abandoned their “old town” and restarted their community nearly a mile to the north. Their movement could have been caused by the plague although some sources say it was because they were burned out by William the Conqueror’s Harrying of the North in 1069. *(Image: Northern Perspectives)*

![]()This is Northern Perspectives’ classic view of the moment Locomotion No 1 stopped on the Skerne bridge. It is the wrong angle to get the 2025 recreation of John Dobbin’s famous 1825 painting – but it is the right picture to capture the bicentenary celebrations. “There were 6,000 people there all taking pictures and 5,999 of them are probably better than me, so it’s about thinking about doing something that’s different,” says Dave. *(Image: Northern Perspectives)*

![]()The difficulty with recreating the inaugural journey was that the replica Locomotion No 1 had to somehow cross the East Coast Main Line. This required the mainline to be powered down, and so it could only be done late at night. It was planned to be shortly after midnight, but delays elsewhere on the mainline meant that it wasn’t well after 1am that it was able to set off from Hopetown in Darlington and begin its nocturnal journey by crossing the Skerne bridge. The delay and persistent rain meant that only the diehards were there to capture the moment. “What I like about the picture is that it was the complete opposite of the day time when there had been 6,000 people there,” says Dave. “In the early hours, there were fewer than 100 – and mine was the only drone. And it had stopped raining.” *(Image: Northern Perspectives)*

Locomotion, though, had to continue on its way to Ferryhill where it could be removed from the tracks. Thousands of people turned out at Rochester Road in Stockton to congratulate it on its successful recreation of the day in 1825 that changed the world.
