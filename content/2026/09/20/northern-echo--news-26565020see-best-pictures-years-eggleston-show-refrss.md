Thousands of visitors gathered in Teesdale this weekend as the historic Eggleston Show returned for another celebration of farming, food and countryside life.

Held at Hayberries Farm near Mickleton, the annual show drew livestock exhibitors, horse competitors, local businesses and families from County Durham and beyond.

The show dates back to 1864, with this year's event continuing a tradition of more than 160 years.

Cattle, sheep and horse classes drew competitors from across the region before the Best in Show contest and traditional Grand Parade closed the day.

Judging took place across a packed programme of livestock classes, including Swaledale, Suffolk, Texel and Blue Faced Leicester sheep alongside beef cattle, young handlers and horse competitions.

Visitors also watched sheepdog demonstrations and dog shows, admired vintage tractors and browsed stalls selling local products and services.

The show also celebrates traditional rural skills and local talent.

Marquees showcased horticulture, flowers, floral art, baking, preserves, handicrafts, photography, art and children's competitions, with exhibitors of all ages displaying their work.

Food and drink were another big draw, with local caterers, a licensed bar and a food and craft marquee keeping visitors refreshed.

The Eggleston Agricultural Society runs the show with help from volunteers and supporters who work throughout the year to make it a success.

Organisers describe it as a showcase not only of agriculture but of the wider Teesdale community and the people who help keep rural traditions alive.

Prize-winning livestock, champion horses, craft exhibits, vintage machinery and family attractions gave this year's show something for everyone.
