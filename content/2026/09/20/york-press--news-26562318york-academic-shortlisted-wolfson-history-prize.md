A York academic has been shortlisted for a prestigious history prize for his book about Liverpool’s turbulent post-war transformation and resilient communities.

Dr Sam Wetherell, a University of York senior lecturer, is shortlisted for the 2026 Wolfson History Prize with Liverpool: A Story of Britain.

The book follows Liverpool’s post-war recovery, the decline of its docklands and its people’s struggle through deindustrialisation, ending with the Toxteth riots.

Dr Wetherell said: "I feel absolutely delighted to be shortlisted.

"With this book, I wanted to tell a very different type of story about the history of modern Britain, and I wanted this story to resonate with readers outside as well as inside of academia.

"Historical writing has always been extremely important to me and one of the reasons I love being a historian is that it is a craft where writing and communication are taken seriously.

"The Wolfson Prize does wonderful work in this regard, and it is the highest honour for someone like me who cares deeply about prose, storytelling and about disseminating complex ideas as widely as possible."

Now in its 54th year, the Wolfson History Prize is the UK’s most valuable history writing award, recognising outstanding historical non-fiction.

The winner will be announced on December 1.

Other shortlisted titles include The Boundless Deep: Young Tennyson, Science and the Crisis of Belief by Richard Holmes and Vanished: An Unnatural History of Extinction by Sadiah Qureshi.

Sir David Cannadine, chair of the Wolfson History Prize judges, said: "These remarkable six books remind us of the power of history writing, not just to uncover the past but to bring new perspectives and nuance to our understanding of the present."
