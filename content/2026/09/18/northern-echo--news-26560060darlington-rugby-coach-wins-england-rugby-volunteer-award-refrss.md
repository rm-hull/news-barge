A Darlington rugby coach who created a competitive pathway for young female players has won a prestigious England Rugby volunteer award.

Jon Hawkins, coach of Darlington RFC’s under-18 girls, won England Rugby’s Keeping Players in the Game Award for developing the North East and Cumbria Under-16 Girls League.

He received the award at a ceremony at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham.

Mr Hawkins said: "I am obviously extremely proud.

"I do not do any of this (coaching) for the rewards.

"However, I am proud that the North East, Durham, and the Darlington Rugby Club, are being recognised for the work that we are doing and what we're trying to do for the women and girls' game."

All four of Hawkins’s children—two boys and two girls—play at Darlington Rugby Club, making the sport a family affair.

He said: "It's a family club.

"Further to that, I am committed to trying to help girls in the North East get the recognition they deserve.

"The nearest women’s premiership team is Sale, and that's in the North, but it is still three hours from our girls.

"We have some amazing talent across the North East, and we are trying to build something that supports them."

England Rugby praised Hawkins’s contribution to the game.

A spokesperson said: “Jon has driven the development of the North East & Cumbria U16 Girls League.”

“He brought together volunteers to form the league committee and developed a regular fixture schedule, with teams from 21 clubs across four counties now reporting increased player registrations.”

Mr Gunnell said: “Darlington Rugby Football Club is delighted that Jon Hawkins has been recognised for his work.”

“While undoubtedly a great personal honour, the award recognises Jon and the dedicated coaches building girls’ age-grade rugby across the region.”
