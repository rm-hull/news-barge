Dogs Trust has launched a Darlington service helping struggling owners rehome their dogs without sending them to kennels.

Its Homestay scheme lets dogs stay with their owners while the charity finds them new families.

The scheme aims to spare dogs the stress of kennels while they are being rehomed.

Jake Flatman, head of rehoming at Dogs Trust, said: "We know that giving up a dog is an incredibly difficult decision, but we want people to call us the minute they need help, rather than when they reach the point of crisis.

"The earlier people get in touch, the more we can do to understand their situation and help find the most suitable support.

  Nala, at her new home 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"If behaviour challenges are contributing to the decision, our behaviour services – like our Behaviour Support Line – may be able to help dogs and their owners stay together.

"But, if giving up a dog is the right choice, we’ll support them every step of the way and find them the best option to rehome their dog."

Homestay is for owners who need urgent rehoming help but can care for their dogs until a new home is found.

The UK’s largest dog welfare charity has received more than 28,000 rehoming enquiries so far this year.

The service eases pressure on full kennels and foster homes while offering an alternative to desperate measures such as giving dogs away or selling them online.

Homestay keeps dogs out of kennels and gives Dogs Trust a clearer picture of their personalities, routines and needs, helping it find the right adopters.

Dogs rehomed through Homestay wait an average of 14 days for a new home, compared with 58.5 days in a rehoming centre.

More than 700 dogs have already been helped through Homestay this year.

Dogs Trust is urging owners considering giving up their dogs to get in touch early so it can assess their situation and offer the right support.

Owners struggling with their dogs’ behaviour can also get help from the charity to keep them together where possible.

Nala, a Bernese Mountain Dog-Collie cross, was reserved through Homestay after just three days.

Nala struggled to be left alone and showed signs of stress in kennels, so the Darlington team recommended Homestay as a calmer option.

After a few meetings, Nala settled into her new home and has thrived ever since.

Her new family said her confidence is growing every day and they feel "incredibly lucky" to have her.

They even threw her a second birthday party, complete with a special dog-friendly cake.

Nala’s story shows how Homestay can help dogs likely to find kennels overwhelming.

It also frees kennel space for dogs with nowhere else to go.

Dogs Trust is urging anyone who may no longer be able to care for their dog to get in touch early.

If Homestay is not suitable, the charity can discuss behavioural support or rehoming through its centre.

Asking for help early is not giving up—it gives your dog the best chance of a better future.

For help, call Dogs Trust on 0303 003 0000 or visit dogstrust.org.uk/homestay.
