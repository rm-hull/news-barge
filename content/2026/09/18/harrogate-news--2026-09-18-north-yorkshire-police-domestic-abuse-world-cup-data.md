**NORTH YORKSHIRE**— Newly published police statistics have revealed a significant escalation in local domestic abuse crimes recorded during key England match dates throughout the recent World Cup tournament.Data obtained from North Yorkshire Police via a Freedom of Information (FOI) request by researchers at 101 Great Goals shows that recorded domestic offences surged by 46 per cent on Tuesday, 23 June 2026, when England fought out a 0-0 group-stage draw against Ghana.The force logged 26 separate domestic abuse crimes during the fixture window, compared to a baseline average of fewer than 18 offences across the preceding four Tuesdays.

While it is true that football itself does not cause domestic abuse, it is a well-established sociological and policing reality that high-stakes tournaments act as a significant aggravating factor. Spikes in recorded incidents are frequently driven by a combination of increased alcohol consumption, extended licensing hours, and heightened emotional stress.

The operational logs demonstrate that crime levels exceeded the standard four-week weekday baseline during three of England’s eight tournament match days. In addition to the spike witnessed during the Ghana fixture, offences rose by 27 per cent on Saturday, 27 June, as England secured a 2-0 victory over Panama, with 28 crimes recorded against a weekend baseline average of 22.


**Jon Fisher, the researcher who led the statutory data request, stated:**

Football should bring people together. Whatever happens on the pitch, nobody should ever have to fear what might happen at home when the final whistle blows.

Domestic abuse is complex and these figures alone cannot tell us what was behind every incident, but no football result can ever excuse abuse.



A further 29 domestic offences were documented on Saturday, 11 July, during England’s narrow 2-1 extra-time win against Norway, representing a 13 per cent inflation above typical seasonal baselines. Conversely, recorded crime levels dropped below the corresponding four-week averages during the national team’s five remaining fixture dates.

In total, North Yorkshire Police processed 178 domestic abuse crimes across the eight specific match dates. Hourly tracking data identified 8pm as the peak period for emergency calls and deployments, accounting for 19 recorded incidents across the series. Police commanders noted that the figures categorise crimes based on whether an arrest was logged in connection with the occurrence, though the underlying arrests may not exclusively relate to the domestic abuse offence itself.
