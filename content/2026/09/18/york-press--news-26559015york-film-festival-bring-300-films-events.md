A MAJOR film festival is set to return to York this November.

The Aesthetica Film Festival will run from November 4 to 8, bringing more than 300 films, premieres, masterclasses, live music, and immersive art experiences to the city.

Now in its 16th year, the BAFTA-qualifying event promises its largest programme to date, drawing creative professionals from more than 60 countries.

Cherie Federico, director of the Aesthetica Film Festival, said: "Aesthetica has always believed that the arts should be part of everyday life, not something people have to feel they need permission to enter.

"This isn’t a regional version of something that belongs in London.

"It is an international event with York at its centre."

The festival’s reach extends well beyond film, with events spanning television, art, games, sound, and immersive media.

Industry leaders from the BBC, Film4, ITV, Aardman, Framestore, Neal Street Productions, and Ubisoft will take part in the programme, alongside established and emerging talent from around the globe.

A central feature of this year’s festival is the Immersive Media Arts Trail, developed in partnership with Audible.

The trail will transform parts of York’s city centre into an open-air gallery featuring sound, light and media art.

At York Theatre Royal, environmental artist Steve Messam will present Fruere Silentio, a large-scale inflatable structure accompanied by a vocal score recorded in the city’s medieval churches and York Minster.

Other installations include Henge: Echoes Through Time in King’s Square by Gemma Wood of Being Now Here, and The Sound of Coming Back to Life in Exhibition Square by Squidsoup, which blends volumetric light and sound.

The festival also includes a dedicated Games Lab showcasing 50 titles across platforms, a VR Lab exploring immersive cinema, a New Music Stage, and a Podcasting Lounge for emerging audio creators.

Workshops and events will support creative businesses and students, including Creative Business Workshops delivered in partnership with York Business School.

The programme highlights York’s status as the UK’s only UNESCO City of Media Arts and includes the UNESCO City of Media Arts EXPO, which will spotlight the city’s media arts community and global creative identity.

There is also a significant programme for York families to get involved with.

The organisers say they want this festival to be for everyone in the city, extending the experience beyond industry professionals and established cultural audiences.
