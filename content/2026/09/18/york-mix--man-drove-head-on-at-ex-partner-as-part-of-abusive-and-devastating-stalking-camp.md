A North Yorkshire man has been jailed for a prolonged and abusive stalking campaign against his ex partner.

At one point Max Midgley drove head-on at her car, forcing her to take emergency evasive action to avoid a crash.

The court heard that the victim had been in a relationship with Midgley for approximately two and a half years.

During their relationship his controlling and abusive behaviour caused the victim significant fear and distress.

Midgley was reported to have strangled the victim while making threats to kill her.

He attempted to push her down the stairs, forced entry into a bathroom where she had sought refuge, and pushed her against tiles causing her head to strike the wall.

On other occasions, he spat at the victim and grabbed her hand so hard he caused a suspected broken finger.

In a separate assault, he threw a freshly made cup of hot tea into her face.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/york-magistrates-court-march-2026.webp)

After the breakdown of the relationship, Midgley’s behaviour escalated. When the victim returned to collect her belongings and leave the address, she later encountered Midgley driving towards her vehicle on a country road.

On a bend, Midgley crossed the carriageway and drove directly towards the victim, forcing her to brake heavily and swerve to avoid a collision.

The court heard that Midgley’s actions formed part of a wider course of conduct amounting to stalking and harassment and left the victim fearing for her safety.

## Sent to prison

At York Magistrates’ Court today (Friday), Midgley, 38, of Hustwaite Road, Coxwold, was sentenced to 40 months in prison, and given an indefinite restraining order.

Police Staff Investigator Anne Yates from North Yorkshire Police said: “This was a prolonged campaign of abusive, controlling and intimidating behaviour which left the victim fearing for her safety. Nobody should have to experience this kind of conduct.

“Midgley’s actions had a devastating impact on the victim and demonstrate how domestic abuse can continue long after a relationship has ended. His behaviour was persistent, frightening and completely unacceptable.

“I would like to commend the victim for the courage and resilience she has shown in reporting these offences and supporting the investigation throughout the court process.

“North Yorkshire Police will not tolerate domestic abuse, stalking or harassment in any form. We are committed to safeguarding victims, pursuing offenders and bringing them before the courts.

“If you are experiencing abuse, stalking, controlling behaviour or harassment, please know that you are not alone and support is available. We urge anyone going through a similar situation to come forward and report it.

“We will listen, we will investigate and we will do everything we can to protect you.”
