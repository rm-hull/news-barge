Twenty women have undergone unnecessary mastectomies, and more than 300 patients have been harmed while under the care of an NHS trust's breast cancer services.

Women affected by failures at County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust have described feeling “butchered” and “mutilated” after discovering surgery they underwent was not needed.

The scale and impact of the failings are examined in a new BBC investigation, Breast Cancer Scandal: Exposing the Harm, which airs on BBC One in the North East and Cumbria and is available on iPlayer from September 18.

The Trust has confirmed that, of 514 cases examined so far, 315 patients were found to have suffered harm, including 77 who were significantly harmed.

One patient is known to have died.

Some patients have also been identified whose cancer diagnoses were missed or delayed, in some cases allowing the disease to spread.

The Trust is currently reviewing the care of patients treated between January 2023 and February 2025, alongside former patients who have contacted a dedicated helpline.

More than 640 people have now contacted the service.

Trust chief executive Steve Russell, who took up the role in September 2025, told the BBC that thousands more patient files could ultimately be reopened.

A public board meeting on September 24 will consider whether the lookback should be extended further into the past, potentially as far back as 2015.

The BBC investigation follows a series of reviews and investigations into breast services at the Trust.

Amir Bhatti was clinical lead for breast services at County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust from 2013 to 2024.

His clinical practice has since been restricted while an internal Trust investigation continues.

In a statement to the BBC, Mr Bhatti said: “I am grateful for the opportunity to respond, but I remain committed to the ongoing trust investigation, and it would be inappropriate for me to discuss this while proceedings are ongoing.”

Last year, the BBC reported that almost £6 million had been paid by the Trust over a six-year period to privately run breast cancer diagnostic clinics and surgery companies connected to Mr Bhatti.

An independent governance review carried out by Mary Aubrey later raised concerns about the arrangements.

  Amir Bhatti


The review said outsourcing appointments on payment-per-patient contracts created financial incentives which were potentially a risk to clinical standards.

A separate Royal College of Surgeons review also raised concerns about whether breast cancer cases had been properly discussed at multidisciplinary team meetings.

The National Crime Agency and Durham Police are now investigating to establish whether any criminal offences were committed.

The trust is cooperating with the investigation.

Mr Russell told the BBC he did not personally know why the unnecessary mastectomies had taken place.

He said: “There are no words that I could use to convey how personally distressed I am and my colleagues are for those outcomes and the harm that those women have experienced.”

In a separate statement, Mr Russell said: “We are deeply sorry to the women and families who have been harmed and let down by failings in our breast services.

“We recognise the profound impact this has had and have been clear that there were opportunities when, as a trust, we should have listened more carefully and acted sooner.

“Significant changes have since been made to the breast service and are bringing improvements for patients.”

  University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City. CEO of the County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust, Steve Russell


He said the ongoing lookback would continue examining past care, identifying patients who may have suffered harm and ensuring they received appropriate information and support.

Mr Russell added: “On September 24, our trust board will meet in public to consider the future scope and timeframe of the look-back.

“No final decision has been made and it would not be right to pre-empt the board’s discussion.”

The Department of Health and Social Care said its thoughts were with the women and families affected by the “unacceptable” failings.

It said the trust was working with NHS England, the Care Quality Commission and independent experts to address what happened and make sure future patients receive safe care.

Rachel O’Connor, of Hudgell Solicitors’ medical negligence team, has called for a public inquiry.

She said the firm was representing a number of women who had undergone surgery which they had subsequently been told was unnecessary.

She said: “Breast cancer services at hospitals should be places where patients have the reassurance of exceptional care and support, from clinics and scans to surgeries and treatments.

“Sadly, at County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust, this has not been the case for many years, as deep-rooted, systematic failures have led to women suffering serious harm.”
