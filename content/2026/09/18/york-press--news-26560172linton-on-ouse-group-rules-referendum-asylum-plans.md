A group opposing plans to house 1,200 male asylum seekers at a former RAF base near York has ruled out holding a Piddington-style “referendum” over the proposals.

Linton Action Group, which is opposing the asylum centre plans at RAF Linton-on-Ouse, said it “recognises the work of Piddington and other groups in raising attention to this issue nationally”, but it would not like to see the North Yorkshire village leave the UK.

It comes after residents in Piddington voted overwhelmingly to become an independent state in the symbolic protest vote, organised in response to government plans to house 1,256 asylum seekers at a former military base near the Oxfordshire village.

Asked about the result of the vote, prime minister Andy Burnham said he understood the “strength of feeling” across the country about migration.

“It’s difficult,” Mr Burnham said, “and obviously I will look at the issues being raised by the community and in the referendum, but we have to ensure that we have a fair plan, as well as getting the numbers down, and the numbers are coming down significantly now.”

*(Image: Ryan McDougall/PA Wire)*

A Linton Action Group spokesperson said that although Linton-on-Ouse is in a similar position to Piddington due to the proposals, residents of the village have “faith in British democracy and plan to very much remain in the UK”.

They added that local politicians from across the political divide, including the county’s Labour mayor, have also expressed concern over the asylum centre proposals.

“The prime minister has talked about growth in every postcode and the power of devolved decision making," they said. "He has said he is going to listen to local mayors on issues affecting their community.

“Local Labour mayor David Skaith has made clear his opposition to the Home Office plans and proposed positive growth in Linton-on-Ouse.

“We plan to remain part of the UK and hold the prime minister and his government accountable to their own standards.”

The spokesperson said the Linton Action Group’s invitation to Home Office minister Anna Turley to visit RAF Linton-on-Ouse to speak to villagers about the proposals also remained on the table. Ms Turley has not yet responded to the invitation.

“Every official who has visited the site has been clear it is unsafe and inappropriate for this plan,” the Linton Action Group spokesperson said.

“We have faith that if Anna Turley, the asylum minister, took up our repeated invites to visit, she would inevitably come to the same conclusion, as would the officials completing the feasibility study, if it is conducted in a fair and appropriate manner.”

  RAF Linton-On-Ouse in North Yorkshire which the Home Office is planning to use to house asylum seekers in its quest to shut more hotels

A Home Office spokesperson said: "The concerns of communities are central to our immigration reforms.

"That is why we are closing every asylum hotel and moving asylum seekers into alternative accommodation, including former military sites.

"We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, engaging with local authorities to reduce the impact on communities."
