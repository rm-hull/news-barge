More than 100 workers at a County Durham factory have been told they will not be paid with the firm set to enter administration.

Staff at Clayton Glass, based on the Harelaw Industrial Estate in Stanley, were told on Friday (September 18) that the company had filed a notice of intention to appoint an administrator.

In an email to staff seen by The Northern Echo, workers were told they would go without payment after the company had filed for administration.

It said: "Regrettably, due to our current lack of liquid funds and restrictions placed upon us during this process, we cannot process or issue due and upcoming payments.

"You will not receive your regular salary or wages on the upcoming payday.

"We know this news is devastating. We are deeply sorry for the immense stress and hardship this causes you and your families".

"Once an administrator is officially appointed, they will take control of operations and communicate directly with all staff regarding claims for unpaid wages and entitlements."

Staff were told the move would provide temporary protection while advisers explored possible options for the business.

Clayton Glass has been approached for comment.

A staff member who asked not to be named said around 160 employees had been told to attend the factory for a meeting at 8am on Friday, regardless of their shift pattern. They said colleagues gathered at the site but no managers or directors attended to address staff.

The company manufactures insulated glass units, but pictures shared from inside the factory show carts and production lines empty.

A spokesperson for Durham County Council said: “Durham County Council was not informed of this in advance by the company.

“We are concerned if the situation is as it appears and we are making efforts to contact Clayton Glass directly.

“The council is here to support any affected residents and their families in whatever way we can.”

Councillor Darren Grimes, who represents the Annfield Plain and Tanfield ward, said he had spoken to affected families.

   

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

He said: “I am deeply concerned by this news. Clayton Glass is one of the last remaining energy intensive manufacturers in our area offering good jobs that we need, good jobs that our community can't afford to lose.

“I've spoken to families today who have not been paid and have no idea when or if they will be.

"These are good people whose lives are stressful enough without wondering whether a pay packet is going to turn up.
