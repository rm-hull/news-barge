The firm behind a County Durham factory has been branded "heartless" after staff learnt they would not be paid and the firm was going into administration when they turned up for work.

In an email seen by The Northern Echo, staff were told they would not receive their wages because of a lack of available funds.

The message stated: "Regrettably, due to our current lack of liquid funds and restrictions placed upon us during this process, we cannot process or issue due and upcoming payments.

"You will not receive your regular salary or wages on the upcoming payday.

"We know this news is devastating. We are deeply sorry for the immense stress and hardship this causes you and your families."

  The factory in Hare Law, Stanley, employs around 160 people. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

It added that, once appointed, administrators would take control of the business and communicate with staff about unpaid wages and other entitlements.

Clayton Glass has been approached for comment.

Photographs taken inside the factory show empty production lines, vacant carts and warehouse areas stripped of stock.

   

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

## 'Nobody's been paid'

One employee, who asked not to be named, said workers arrived at the factory on Friday (September 18) expecting answers ,but were left frustrated and uncertain about their future.

He said: "We got told by email this morning that we basically weren't getting paid and that administrators were coming in.

"Payday's today. We've just never got paid."

The worker said the mood inside the factory was "horrible".

"Everybody's frustrated," he said.

"They feel hard done to.

"People have families, they've got bills to pay, and some people work Friday to Friday. Not everybody's got savings.

"It's going to be a hard time for them."

Employees are still waiting to learn whether the site will continue operating under administrators or whether redundancies will follow.

"We're still waiting for the administrators to turn up," the worker said.

"None of us know what's going to happen.

  Clayton Glass employees were told by email that they would not be paid. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"We took a seven per cent pay cut, and office staff took a 10 per cent cut, to help keep the business open.

"This is how they've been treated and it's not fair on them."

He added that many long-serving employees were now facing an uncertain future in a job market that has changed significantly since they last looked for work.

"Some of these guys have been doing this job for 20 years," he said.

"They don't know how to go and get interviews."

## Politicians condemn treatment of workforce

Annfield Plain and Tanfield councillor Darren Grimes said workers had been treated in an "absolutely disgusting" manner.

"To come in, be told you're not going to get paid on payday itself, and you have to go away and wonder how you're going to actually feed your family, that's how serious it is," he said.

"I think the way in which they've gone about it is truly anti-human and really quite sinister.

  Councillors Darren Grimes and Karen Allison said treatment of staff was 'heartless'. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"You turn up expecting to put in your shift only to be told it's going into administration and you're not going to get paid.

"I can't get over the heartless nature of the way in which it's been conducted."

The company posted on LinkedIn that it had hosted an 'open day' at its Swindon site on Thursday, saying a hog roast "went down an absolute storm."

Cllr Grimes hit back, saying: "Other parts of the business have been celebrating this week with an [open day] and more food than Henry Tudor would have found recognisable."

  A post on LinkedIn shows the company having a well-stocked open day at one of their other sites in Swindon a day before the decision. 

*(Image: LINKEDIN)*

Follow councillor Karen Allison said the collapse would have implications far beyond the factory gates.

"Obviously our thoughts go out to the families," she said.

"It doesn't just affect those employees, it also affects the supply chain. You probably have companies now that are going to be struggling because of what's happened here.

"It is really sad and it's going to have a devastating effect on the area as a whole."

North Durham MP Luke Akehurst described the situation as "badly mismanaged".

He said: "I am disgusted on behalf of the hard-working staff at Clayton Glass that their employers have put them in this position.

"While I am very appreciative of the pressures businesses are under, for example due to energy costs, there is no excuse for springing this kind of announcement on staff.

"This has been badly mismanaged, and I will support any constituent that reaches out to me."

## Support available

Durham County Council said it had not been notified of the company's difficulties before Friday.

Mark Stamper, the council's head of place, planning and growth, said support could be available for workers facing immediate financial hardship through the authority's Crisis and Resilience Fund.
