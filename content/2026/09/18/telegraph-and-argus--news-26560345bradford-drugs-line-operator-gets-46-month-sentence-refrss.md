A street dealer who worked as the “night shift operator” for a Bradford-based “ring and bring” drugs line peddling heroin and crack cocaine has been locked up for almost four years.

Police spotted Mohammed Butt at the wheel of a VW Golf on July 23 this year despite knowing he was a disqualified driver.

An hour later he was seen next to the open boot of a different car near Velvet Mill and was arrested by officers from Operation Phoenix, which investigates county lines drug-dealing.

Prosecuting barrister Nicola Hoskins told Bradford Crown Court how Butt, 31, of Waverley Avenue, Little Horton, was arrested at the scene as he was on officers’ radar.

He was searched and found with two phones as well as £799 in cash.

His car contained cannabis and around £50 in loose change which, said Miss Hoskins, appeared to be “a float”.

An investigation of telecommunications data showed that the unbranded line linked to the defendant was in regular contact with Class A drug users and appeared to be used as the “night shift.”

Data showed it diverted to another number during the day but the subscriber details matched Butt’s personal number and email address.

Butt was also seen topping up the pay-as-you-go phone on three occasions.

Miss Hoskins said: “The court will be well aware that drugs lines do not simply supply drugs to customers; they also bring exploitation of vulnerable individuals and a risk of serious violence and harm.”

Miss Hoskins said the drugs line being run by Butt was “well-established and trusted” in the local area.

Butt pleaded guilty to being concerned in the supply of both heroin and crack cocaine between March 23 and July 23, possession of cannabis, driving whilst disqualified, and having no insurance.

He said the cash was not from criminal proceedings but was a loan from his father, and that the cannabis was for personal use.

The court heard that he had previous convictions for like offences dating back to 2015.

Mitigating, John Hobley said he bitterly regretted and was remorseful for what he had done.

And in a letter of apology that he read personally to the judge via video link from HMP Hull, Butt said: “I feel I have let my family down massively. I know the impact that the long-term effects of drugs have on people and the community.”

Sentencing Butt, Mr Recorder Tom Little KC said: “You were in control of, for at least part of the day, a significant drugs line.

“You appeared to be the night shift holder of that line. As it often the case with it being a 24-hour line, people are not able to work 24 hours a day.

“Messages obtained revealed a significant amount of Class A drug-dealing at that time. It was an established line and it was plainly linked to you.”

He handed Butt 42 months’ imprisonment for being concerned in the supply of heroin and crack, one month concurrent for possession of cannabis, and one month consecutive for driving whilst disqualified.

There was no separate penalty for having no insurance.

He also activated three months of a six-month suspended sentence order imposed for harassment in March this year, to run consecutively, and revoked the previous order.

Butt’s total sentence was 46 months.

He was also banned from driving for 54 months.

Recorder Little ordered the forfeiture of the drugs and paraphernalia, and the £799 in cash.
