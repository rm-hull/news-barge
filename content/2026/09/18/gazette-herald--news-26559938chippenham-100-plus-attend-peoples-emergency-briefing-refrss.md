More than 100 people gathered for a film screening followed by a discussion about the climate crisis.

The People's Emergency Briefing was shown at Hardenhuish School, Chippenham, on September 5,

The film is part of a national initiative encouraging communities to engage with environmental issues, and is backed by naturalist Chris Packham.

He said: "I'd encourage people everywhere to attend a screening of The People's Emergency Briefing.

"This new film creates exactly the kind of honest local conversation we now urgently need, both about the threat from climate and nature breakdown, but also about what we can do, together with our politicians, to build a better future."

  Screening of the People’s Emergency Briefing at Hardenhuish School 

*(Image: Zero Chippenham)*

The discussion following the screening touched upon various topics, with a focus on the summer's extreme heat.

Attendees generally agreed that the climate crisis is already affecting daily life in the UK.

Concerns were voiced about year-round weather extremes, from winter flooding and heating costs, to summer heat impacting jobs, health, and daily life.

The need for energy and money to cool buildings to safer levels was also highlighted.

Participants shared positive ways to help, from personal actions at home and locally, to backing policies and projects for wider "system" change.

The campaign aims to build support and understanding for urgent government action by broadcasting a national televised briefing about the risks.

Chippenham's MP, Sarah Gibson, was one of the 145 MPs who signed a cross-party parliamentary call supporting the campaign.

She attended the event, emphasising the importance of public support for the campaign.

When asked who would write to her to show their support, a sea of hands were raised.

Dr Nick Murry, from Zero Chippenham, who organised the event, said: "It was truly inspiring to see so many people turn out for this event, to talk about the issues together honestly and openly, and to see their concern turn into momentum for action.

"Whilst there were plenty of questions about how we get there, there was clear consensus that things need to change, and that a televised briefing would help build that change."

Zero Chippenham closed the event by offering copies of the new Chippenham Eco Map.

The map, illustrated by local artist Kelly Wariner, showcases 21 projects in Chippenham that help reduce greenhouse gas emissions, cut waste, protect natural spaces, and provide co-benefits like easing the cost of living and improving health and wellbeing.

The map will be available on Zero Chippenham's website, and they encourage anyone interested in local involvement to reach out.
