A Bishop Auckland mother ran the Great North Run in memory of her husband and was met at the finish line by their toddler son, who will never know his father.

Charlotte Iveson ran the half-marathon to raise money and awareness for Sarcoma UK after her husband David died from a rare, aggressive cancer at the end of January 2025.

Their son Archie was waiting at the finish line in tribute to the father he will never know.

  Charlotte and Archie at the Great North Run 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Iveson said: "He was so full of life, confident, funny and kind.

"He had time for everybody."

David, known as ‘Ivo’, was a well-loved figure in Bishop Auckland who worked as a team leader at a student loans company.

He was friends with television personality Scarlett Moffatt, and they used to share a car.

His cancer journey began in May 2024 with a mild ache at the top of his leg.

  Charlotte, Archie and David 

*(Image: Supplied)*

After his GP referred him for an ultrasound, a lump formed and he began to limp.

He went to A&E before the scan, where doctors suspected cancer.

Tests showed he had a rare and aggressive sarcoma called a peripheral nerve sheath tumour.

By August, the tumour had grown to the size of a small football.

  w 

*(Image: Supplied)*

David underwent a 12-hour operation to remove the tumour, including a nerve in his leg.

He had to relearn how to walk.

Despite the ordeal, he stayed upbeat, joking that he was so close to the surgical team they would invite him to their Christmas party.

A second lump soon appeared on his other leg, and scans confirmed the cancer had spread.

The couple welcomed their son Archie in September, during David’s illness.

  David in hospital 

*(Image: Supplied)*

David began chemotherapy in November, but his condition soon deteriorated.

Dangerously high calcium levels left him bedridden and forced doctors to pause treatment.

He was admitted to hospital in December but returned home for Christmas.

By January, doctors discovered that swelling in his foot, initially thought to be a blood clot, was the tumour cutting off blood flow to his leg.

  David 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Doctors told the family there was nothing more they could do.

David died at the end of January 2025.

David documented his illness on Instagram, raising awareness of sarcoma, a disease little known to the public.

Since his death, friends and family have raised £11,000 for Sarcoma UK through boxing matches, runs and walks in his memory.

  Friends of David 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Iveson added to the total by completing the Great North Run.

Kerry Reeves-Kneip, director of fundraising and communications at Sarcoma UK, said: "David's story is heartbreaking, a young dad, taken from his family within months of his son being born.

"But what Charlotte and everyone who has fundraised in his memory are doing is making sure his life means something bigger than his loss.

  w 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"Every pound raised brings us closer to better treatments, so fewer families have to go through what David and Charlotte went through."

Ms Iveson said Archie “keeps me going” as she copes with her grief.

She said Archie already recognises his father in photographs, but one day she will have to explain what happened to him.

Sarcoma UK is the only UK charity dedicated to every type of sarcoma, funding research, supporting families and campaigning for better treatment.

The charity has invested £8 million in 98 research projects to date.
