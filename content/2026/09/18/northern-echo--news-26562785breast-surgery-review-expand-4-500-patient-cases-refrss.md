Concerns about breast cancer care at County Durham's NHS trust had been raised for more than a decade before a scandal in its service emerged, new papers reveal.

Warnings over surgical practices, delayed diagnoses and patient care were flagged repeatedly, the documents detail.

  The County **Durham** and**Darlington** NHS Foundation Trust has accepted there were opportunities to act sooner.

It comes as the trust is reviewing thousands of patient records after a review of cases found hundreds of patients came to harm during treatment in the breast unit

The trust has already spent more than £500,000 on the review, while its own board papers, released ahead of a meeting next week, say there are “likely more cases of harm” outside the January 2023 to February 2025 period being examined.

Its board will decide next week whether to extend the review further back, potentially as far as 2015. The widest option would mean reviewing around 4,500 cases and could take five years.

Of 514 cases reviewed, 315 patients were found to have suffered harm, including 77 who suffered significant harm.

One patient is known to have died.

The documents show concerns about the service stretch back years before the current scandal emerged.

### 
  **Years of concerns**

The trust has published a detailed timeline of problems in its breast services, dating back to training issues in 2012 and 2015.

In 2012, the vascular surgery services were centralised at University Hospital of North Durham, with a vascular and breast surgery trainee post at Darlington Memorial Hospital lost.

In 2015, a trainee reported "limited opportunities to gain operative experience at UHND" and said the supervising surgeon performed most procedures while the trainee watched.

In 2018, the Northern Cancer Alliance called the ta “regional and national outlier for high re-excision and benign excision rates”.

That year, the Care Quality Commission raised concerns about delayed diagnoses, serious incidents, reporting delays, complaints and audit findings from the previous 18 months. The trust responded, and the CQC took no further action at that point.

In 2019, NHS England’s 'Getting It Right First Time' programme called the trust a “negative outlier for high mastectomy and re-excision rates and low immediate reconstruction rates”.

Further concerns followed in 2021, when external oncoplastic breast surgeons raised concerns about the high rate of benign operations.

National Cancer Patient Experience Survey results published in 2023 and 2024 identified the trust as an outlier in several areas.

National Cancer Patient Experience Survey results published in 2023 and 2024 also found County Durham and Darlington to be "an outlier across several areas of patient experience."

  By May 2024, escalating concerns prompted another **external review by the Northern Cancer Alliance** of breast surgery carried out in 2023.

The review graded cases red, amber or green according to concerns about departures from evidence-based practice.

Around one-third fell into the red, highest-concern category.

The review found a 30 per cent re-excision rate at the Trust – double the 15 per cent regional average.

The report was issued to the Trust in June 2024.

The Royal College of Surgeons was asked to review the service. Its review found “significant problems” in breast surgery and recommended immediate external support.

The Trust has admitted it could have acted sooner.

The board papers state: “We recognise and acknowledge that there were opportunities to consider and commence duty of candour processes earlier than happened in 2025.”

In February 2025, a patient safety incident prompted the trust to revisit earlier reports and legal advice.

The board papers say legal advice in late 2024 focused mainly on litigation risk, not the trust’s duty of Candour responsibilities.

The trust sought fresh advice and decided to review patients treated in 2023.

The review was later widened to include patients treated in 2024 and up to February 21, 2025.

### 
  **Review could be extended**

The trust's board is considering four options over whether the review should go further.

The first would keep the review within its current January 2023 to February 2025 timeframe.

The second would keep the same timeframe but leave the patient helpline open for two years after the reviews end.

The Trust’s preferred option is to finish the current review and then work back towards 2015 one year at a time.

Experts would assess each year’s findings before deciding whether to go further back.

The fourth option, favoured by the North East and North Cumbria Integrated Care Board (ICB), would extend the review straight back to 2015.

The board papers say “more cases of harm are likely to be found” and already has been found in patients treated before 2023.

283 patients already identified for review would not have been found through the trust’s data or criteria, and were found via the patient helpline, complaints, reports from other hospitals and concerns raised by the current breast team.

The Trust’s papers admit it "must identify and contact affected individuals rather than relying on patients to come forward," saying: "Responsibility should not fall on patients.”

Of the 240 patients reviewed by September 2, 19 met the threshold for the statutory duty of candour.

By September 2, the dedicated helpline had received 642 calls and emails.

### 
  **Findings so far**

The number of patients found to have suffered harm has continued to rise.

The latest figures show that 315 of 514 patients reviewed suffered harm, including 77 who were significantly harmed.

At least 20 women underwent unnecessary mastectomies.

Other cases involved delayed or missed cancer diagnoses and unnecessarily extensive surgery.

  Denise Howarth 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Among those affected is Denise Howarth, 51, from Consett.

Diagnosed with Grade 1 breast cancer in 2023, she initially had a lumpectomy and lymph nodes removed.

She was later told she needed a mastectomy.

Last year, the trust told her the mastectomy had not been necessary.

She was also told she could have had a second wide local excision instead.

Denise said: “I’ve lost so much confidence. I feel robbed of the person I was before.”

Catriona Dunbar, 41, from Stanley, received an apology after the trust admitted failings including a lack of informed consent, poor wound dressings, a three-month delay in test results and unacceptable communication.

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

The trust said her care “fell significantly short of the standards expected”.

Catriona described her experience as “continued torment”.

### 
  **MPs demand action**

Meanwhile, MPs have called for a complete reform at the NHS Trust and want the government involved in a review.

  Mary Kelly Foy, MP for Durham City 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Mary Kelly Foy, City of Durham MP, said: "The latest revelations that 20 women have now been confirmed as undergoing unnecessary mastectomies further highlight the serious patient-safety failings at the trust.

"Independent reviews identified serious weaknesses in clinical oversight, multidisciplinary decision-making, leadership and governance, and the trust itself accepts there were opportunities when it should have listened, asked questions and acted sooner.

"I have met with many of the women who were impacted by this scandal; their stories are heartbreaking, with many of their lives changed forever by the professionals they placed their trust in during some of their most vulnerable moments.

"Their bravery in speaking out and challenging their care should be commended.

"The priority now has to be getting every affected woman the answers and support she deserves, establishing the full scale of what happened, and demonstrating that the changes being made genuinely prevent this from happening again."

Luke Akehurst, North Durham MP, added: "The more we hear about this dreadful scandal that has affected so many local women, the more horrified I am.

"I will do everything I can to secure complete transparency and redress and ensure the failings in the CDDFT breast services are never repeated."

### 
  **Impact of scandal**

The trust set up a dedicated psychology clinic for victims in autumn 2025, but its service and external psychotherapy provision are now full.

The papers say every patient referred to the clinic showed “clinically significant levels of anxiety and/or depression”, while some showed “symptoms consistent with post-traumatic stress disorder”.

Meanwhile, the trust spent £523,830 on the review programme in the 2025/26 financial year.

That included £258,653 for the dedicated lookback team, £177,153 for sessional reviewers, £83,024 for psychology support and £45,000 for legal services.

Future costs will depend on how far the review goes and how complex the cases are.

It has not given a projected cost for extending the review by five years.

### 
  **Investigations ongoing**

Durham Police, with help from the National Crime Agency, is investigating the failures to establish whether any criminal offences were committed.

Amir Bhatti, clinical lead for breast services from 2013 to 2024, has restricted clinical practice while an internal trust investigation continues.

  Amir Bhatti, former breast surgeon lead at the Trust 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

It has previously been reported that almost £6 million was paid over six years to privately run breast diagnostic clinics and surgery companies connected with Mr Bhatti.

An independent governance review raised concerns about payment-per-patient arrangements, saying they created a “clear financial incentive” to treat more patients.

The Trust has issued another apology ahead of its September 24 meeting.
