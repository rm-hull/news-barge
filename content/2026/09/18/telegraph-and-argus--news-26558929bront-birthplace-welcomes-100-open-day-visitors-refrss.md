The Brontë Birthplace has attracted fresh interest thanks to a new exhibition exploring its lesser-known history.

Located in Thornton, Bradford, the birthplace of the famous literary family welcomed more than 100 visitors during a special open day held on Sunday, September 13, as part of a scheme of free heritage open days.

### Exploring the building's history

The exhibition, titled From Butcher's Shop to Birthplace: The Story of the Brontë Birthplace, focused on the building’s transformation after the Brontë family left Thornton.

  Visitors exploring the exhibition 

*(Image: Evie Lauren Photography)*

Thomas Haigh, vice chair of the Brontë Birthplace, said: "Visitors loved the exhibition, and it was really nice to see the Brontë Birthplace attracting new audiences who aren't necessarily Brontë fans.

"One visitor said the exhibition was so informative, and it was a wonderful experience to learn about the butcher’s shop as well as the Brontës history.

### 'the birthplace of the famous Brontë authors.'

"The building is so iconic because it is the birthplace of the famous Brontë authors."

The Brontës lived in Thornton for five years before moving to Haworth.

The exhibition traced the building’s later life, including its time as a butcher’s shop run by Reg Lovette for more than 40 years.

  Barbara (R. Lovette's Daughter) and husband Alan at the Exhibition 

*(Image: Evie Lauren Photography)*

Mr Haigh said the highlight of the day was their VIP guests Barbara, the daughter of Mr Lovette and her husband Alan.

"Barbara was there throughout the day sharing more memories and greeting visitors as they came to learn more about one of Thornton's Master Butchers."

### Support of the local community

The exhibition featured photographs, plans, and stories from the building’s time as a butcher’s shop, as well as its restoration and later uses as a visitor attraction and café.

Mr Haigh said: "The local community has responded to the exhibition with so much love and support.

"It has helped educate people and show what life was like in a different time.

"We had a wide range of visitors of all ages admire and explore the exhibition, as well as the rest of the Birthplace, and for most of them it was their first time visiting."

Among the visitors were people with personal ties to the building. Mr Haigh said one former resident, who lived there before it became a single house, used the maid’s staircase to Nancy’s bedroom as a pantry cupboard. “Everyone loves hearing that story,” he said.

He described the biggest success as being able to share Mr Lovette’s story with the community.

Mr Haigh said: "Seeing the joy in Barbara's face when she saw how I designed and presented the exhibition.

The events continue with a walking tour on Saturday, September 19, which invites visitors to explore Thornton's local history and connections to the Brontë family.
