Campaigners are calling for a County Durham leisure centre to be saved after the charity running it said it could no longer afford to keep it open.

Trustees put Crook Community Leisure Centre and the surrounding land up for sale after soaring energy bills, maintenance costs and other pressures made it unsustainable.

The announcement has sparked fears for Crook, with politicians urging organisations to unite to save the centre.

  Crook Leisure Centre 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Crook councillor Robbie Rodiss said: “This is a shock and another setback for Crook and the residents who, with this terrible news, have seen the loss of all their community-based resources over the last decade.

  Cllr Robbie Rodiss 

*(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

“As a member of Crook Community Leisure (CCL), I know how disappointed other members will be.

“CCL also hosted several residents' clubs that combatted isolation, and it will be terrible should they not be able to continue.

“It is very important that a new organisation is found to ensure CCL continues to provide community-based activities for Crook, and I will be working with the other ward councillors to find a solution that not only ensures the centre stays open but that it actually becomes sustainable.”

  Sam Rushworth, MP for Bishop Auckland 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Bishop Auckland MP Sam Rushworth added: “It's disappointing to see Crook Community Leisure in this position, finding it no longer viable to continue.

“I’ve met with the trustees, and I know how much they care about the facility and the local community it serves.

“It's obviously a massive setback to be losing a leisure centre just when I've secured £2 million a year for a decade from central government to improve the area.”

  Dorothy Winter at the leisure facility 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Mr Rushworth said local access remained vital despite the rise of health and fitness businesses and high street gym chains.

He feared some residents would travel elsewhere to exercise while others might stop using leisure facilities altogether.

“Crook is a town of 10,000 people, and we should have our leisure centre,” he said.

  Dorothy Winter at the leisure facility 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

“This is important to the future of Crook, and I'll do all I can to bring people together and see what can be done to retain leisure provision for our community.”

Crook Community Leisure put the site up for sale after saying it could no longer responsibly use charity funds to subsidise the centre.

Trustees blamed Covid, soaring energy and utility bills, higher National Living Wage and National Insurance costs, and the expense of maintaining the ageing site.

Staff have been told about the sale, with trustees saying they will support them through the transition and seek to protect their jobs under a new operator.

The charity had previously explored extending the site with extra facilities, but planning difficulties brought those plans to an end.

Barnard Castle estate agent Jonathan Wallis is marketing the property, including the leisure centre, sports facilities, running track, football pitch and adjoining land, for offers over £187,500.

Trustees hope a new organisation or developer will invest in the site and save it.

The centre is expected to remain open while the sale continues.

The current facility was developed after a 2013 public vote secured £500,000 from a community fund and opened in 2017.
