NHS investment will keep a vital rural health service running in North Yorkshire after a long-serving doctor retired, allowing patients to see a GP closer to home.

University Hospitals Tees has secured a five-year lease for Reeth Medical Centre to keep healthcare running in the Dales.

The deal means Central Dales Practice can continue providing GP services to more than 1,600 people in Swaledale and Arkengarthdale.

  University Hospitals Tees staff members at Reeth Medical Centre 

*(Image: Other)*

James Dunbar, consultant in infection and general medicine at UHT, said: "Dr Mike Brookes and Marie Brookes have delivered an outstanding service for the people of Reeth and the surrounding Dales.

"Their dedication to rural healthcare has created a strong foundation on which we are proud to build.

"While no one can truly replace the contribution they have made to the community, we share their commitment to ensuring that local people continue to receive high-quality, accessible care close to home."

  University Hospitals Tees staff members sharing a joke at Reeth Medical Centre 

*(Image: Other)*

Dr Brookes’s retirement raised fears that the medical centre could close.

The new lease means the centre will stay open.

Mr Dunbar said UHT now plans to expand community and specialist services across the Dales.

He said: "Our immediate priority has been to keep primary care running in Reeth by supporting Central Dales Practice at Reeth Medical Centre.

"At the same time, we are working to expand local healthcare, including more home visits for frailer patients, better access to specialist hospital care in the community and extra support for people who struggle to travel."

Vicky Ewan, a consultant in older people’s medicine, said the plans support wider efforts to provide joined-up care across the region.

She said: "We are delighted to be working with Heartbeat Primary Care CIC, ICB, the local authority and our GP partners to provide integrated neighbourhood clinics for frailer patients in Richmondshire in the next few weeks, based at Reeth Surgery.

"By bringing services closer to patients wherever possible, we hope to support the NHS 10-Year Health Plan and secure sustainable healthcare across Richmondshire for years to come."

The plan brings together NHS, council, voluntary-sector and mental health services to provide more joined-up, patient-focused care.
