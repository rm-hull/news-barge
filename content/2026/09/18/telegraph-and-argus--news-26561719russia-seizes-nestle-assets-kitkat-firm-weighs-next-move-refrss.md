Russia has seized Nestle’s business, leaving the KitKat maker considering its next move.

President Vladimir Putin signed a decree late on Thursday placing Nestle’s Russian assets into temporary administration.

The company makes millions of KitKats at the former Rowntree's site in York.

The order also covers the Russian assets of French chain Auchan.

It is thought to be the Kremlin’s largest takeover of foreign corporate assets since at least 2024.

Swiss food giant Nestle, which makes KitKat, Nescafe and Cheerios, said on Friday it was “assessing the situation and its options”.

Nestle said: “Nestle is committed to taking all necessary steps to protect its rights and ensure continuity of business operations in the interests of all stakeholders, particularly its employees.”

The takeover comes amid tensions between Russia and Europe.

More than 1,000 firms left Russia after its invasion of Ukraine in 2022, although some companies stayed, including Cadbury owner Mondelez and PepsiCo.

Russian law was changed to allow the state to take control of shareholdings owned by firms classed as “unfriendly”.

That power has already been used against Carlsberg, Danone and AB InBev, the brewer of Stella Artois and Budweiser.

Nestle has operated in Russia since 1995, but has reduced its operations there since the war began.

The company has six manufacturing sites and employs around 7,000 people in the country.

Its Russian business is thought to generate around two billion Swiss francs (£1.81 billion) in yearly revenue, equal to about 2% of the group’s global annual sales.
