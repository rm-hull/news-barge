A GLOBAL campaign is set to return, promoting fair treatment and better pay for farmers and workers.

Fairtrade Fortnight, running from September 21 to October 4, will highlight the importance of ethical trade and climate justice in communities across the world—including in the Bradford district, which has been a Fairtrade Zone since 2006.

This year’s campaign is themed People. Planet. Pay. Do it fair, focusing on safe working conditions, environmental protection, and fair earnings for a sustainable future.

Councillor Chris Herd, Lord Mayor of Bradford, said: "We are proud to continue supporting Fairtrade in our district.

"Buying Fairtrade helps farmers get the support they need and empowers them to face the challenges of the changing climate."

The campaign will be marked with events across the Bradford district, designed to raise awareness and encourage action.

Ilkley, which celebrates 20 years as a Fairtrade Town in 2026, will host a quiz trail, school activities, and a community coffee morning.

The Fairtrade Fortnight town quiz trail will run throughout the fortnight, with entry forms available from Oxfam and the Christchurch foyer on The Grove.

The winner will receive a hamper of Fairtrade products donated by the Co-op.

On October 2, Stanbury Primary School will host a Peru Day, exploring Fairtrade farming in the Machu Picchu region through music, dance, and cultural activities.

A coffee morning will be held at Christchurch on October 10, from 10.30am to 12 noon, to celebrate Ilkley’s Fairtrade journey.

Entry is free and all are welcome.

The Ilkley Fairtrade Group has partnered with Ilkley Business Improvement District (BID) to launch a new Ilkley Fairtrade Directory, which will be available on the Discover Ilkley website during Fairtrade Fortnight.

Fairtrade is embedded in Bradford’s Climate Action Plan and Food Strategy.

The district is home to several Fairtrade Villages and Towns, with ongoing support from Bradford Cathedral and the University of Bradford.

By choosing Fairtrade products such as tea, coffee, bananas, and chocolate, consumers help ensure that farmers receive a Fairtrade Minimum Price.

This enables them to invest in their farms, support their families, and plan for the future.

The Fairtrade movement also contributes to climate action through environmental standards and training in adaptation and sustainable farming practices.

To mark the fortnight, the Fairtrade flag will be flown in Centenary Square in Bradford.

City Hall, the Mirror Pool, and Darley Street Market will also be illuminated in Fairtrade’s blue and green colours.
