A new £1.5 million scheme will help innovative firms develop projects and boost Tees Valley’s digital and creative sectors.

The Tees Valley Investment Zone Innovation Voucher is part of the £160 million zone and will fund businesses testing, building, piloting and scaling new ideas over the next eight months.

The scheme has already attracted interest from 155 businesses.

Ben Houchen, Tees Valley Mayor, said: "We’ve got brilliant digital and creative businesses right across Teesside, Darlington, and Hartlepool – and the demand for these vouchers shows just how much ambition is out there.

"The Investment Zone needs to be backing growing firms, helping them turn good ideas into real products and services, and making sure more high-quality jobs and supply chain opportunities are created here.

"We already have real strength in digital, tech, and creative industries, and this funding is about building on that brilliance so more companies can grow to compete nationally and internationally."

Teesside surgical technology firm Surghive is among the programme’s first beneficiaries.

Surghive has received a £40,000 Innovation Voucher to develop the next phase of its digital surgical training platform.

Surghive co-founder and CEO Dr Rachael Coates said: "The voucher allows us to accelerate development of a critical part of the Surghive ecosystem.

"We have already developed immersive robotic surgery training, but the app creates the digital layer around that training by connecting surgeons with learning, competency requirements, relevant opportunities, and, ultimately, personalised recommendations.

"For an early-stage technology company, the support enables us to bring forward development significantly and, importantly, build the right technical foundations now so the platform can scale as our national and international activity grows."

The company is working with surgeons, training organisations, universities, NHS stakeholders and robotic surgery manufacturers ahead of wider pilots.

Middlesbrough firm Unasys Ltd has secured a £112,000 Innovation Acceleration Voucher.

The funding will help the engineering firm develop and commercialise new AI tools for its digital asset management platform.

Unasys director Michael Flynn said: "This will help Unasys improve productivity, reduce project delivery costs, and create new recurring revenues through software licensing and SaaS (Software as a Service)."

Middlesbrough firm Many Cats Studio is also set to benefit from the programme.

Chris Goodyear, CEO of Many Cats Studio, said: "Being selected to receive the funding offers a major boost in the ways Many Cats Studios can show our expertise – and shows the whole industry why accessibility is important for their projects.

"The funding provided a unique opportunity to develop innovation from within the team, something which would have taken us longer without the support."

Teesside University leads the Innovation Voucher programme, which helps Tees Valley digital and creative businesses develop and accelerate ideas for growth.

Professor Stephen Cummings, Pro Vice-Chancellor (Research and Knowledge Exchange) at Teesside University, said: "We are delighted to support this first cohort of businesses through the Tees Valley Investment Zone Innovation Voucher programme.

"We look forward to working with ambitious founders on a wide range of exciting innovation projects, helping them develop new digital and creative products and services that have the potential to drive growth, create opportunities, and strengthen the region’s innovation ecosystem."

The programme supports feasibility studies, prototypes, pilots, market development and commercial launches.

Chris Cooke, Mayor of Middlesbrough and TVCA Cabinet Member for Finance, Organisational Improvement, and Business Growth, said: "Middlesbrough has a huge role to play in this, with Teesside University, our businesses, and our town’s digital and creative talent helping to drive new ideas forward.

"We want to make it easier for local firms to get the support, expertise, and confidence they need to grow.

"That means backing our strengths, connecting businesses with research and skills, and making sure the benefits are felt by people and communities across the whole Tees Valley."

The wider Tees Valley Investment Zone aims to create more than 2,000 jobs and attract up to £175 million in investment over the next decade.
