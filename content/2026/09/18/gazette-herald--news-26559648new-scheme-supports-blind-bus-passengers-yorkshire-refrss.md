Blind and partially sighted people across Yorkshire could find bus travel easier after a new scheme trained drivers to understand sight loss and passengers’ needs.

Thomas Pocklington Trust (TPT), its Sight Loss Councils and Guide Dogs have launched a Bus Driver Vision Awareness Training programme to help drivers support passengers with sight loss.

The first session was held at Arriva Yorkshire’s Dewsbury depot, with 16 drivers taking part in practical and classroom activities.

  Thomas Pocklington Trust, its Sight Loss Councils and Guide Dogs have launched a Bus Driver Vision Awareness Training programme across Yorkshire to help drivers better support blind and partially sighted passengers and make bus travel more accessible 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Kelle McParland, sighted guide training officer for Guide Dogs in the North of England, said: "For many people with sight loss, catching the bus can feel overwhelming.

"By working alongside Thomas Pocklington Trust and Arriva, we're helping drivers build the skills and confidence to support blind and partially sighted passengers, creating journeys that are more accessible, more welcoming and more inclusive for everyone."

  Arriva Yorkshire’s Dewsbury depot 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The training builds on TPT’s work with Arriva, which has already seen parts of its vision impairment course added to the company’s Driver Certificate of Professional Competence (DCPC) programme.

Sessions cover common eye conditions, clear communication and sighted guiding techniques.

Drivers also board a bus blindfolded while a colleague guides them.
