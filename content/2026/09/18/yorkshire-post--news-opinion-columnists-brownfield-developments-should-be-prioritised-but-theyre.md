# Brownfield developments should be prioritised but they’re not the panacea

## It is a given that Britain needs to build more houses and the mistakes of the past few decades, where not enough homes have been built, cannot be repeated.

Labour’s zeal for building, though, has alienated many communities that feel development is not suitable on their doorstep.

A dismissive attitude towards these people, labelling them Nimbys, and trying to railroad through development is not only damaging politically but also to the drive to build more homes.

New research from the Campaign to Protect Rural England (CPRE) provides food for thought. It shows that there is now enough brownfield land in England for more than 1.54 million new homes, enough to exceed the government's entire 1.5 million target.

![New houses being constructed. PIC: Gareth Fuller/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjljNTc5NzljLTQyNDMtNDU4OC05ZTg2LWZjN2U2OTcyNTE1NDo3YmFmY2E4OS0wZmI5LTQxYWItODdjOS1lZGFlOTM2YTg3MWU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Brownfield development must always be prioritised before allowing developers to start ripping up greenfield sites.

However, building on brownfield sites is not the panacea. There are myriad issues that need to be addressed if the Government is to succeed when it comes to ensuring there is enough stock for everyone to have a roof over their heads.

The first place to start is get existing housing stock updated and fit for purpose. There are still many areas of the country where houses, sometimes entire streets, stand deserted.

Empty town centres should be reimagined for the purposes of housing. There is a readymade mixed use development opportunity there.

It is too simplistic to say build houses here or there. Rather it needs to be about building and expanding communities. To do this, the infrastructure needs to back the housing.
