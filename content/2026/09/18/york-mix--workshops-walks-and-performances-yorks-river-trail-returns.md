Discover a new way to explore York’s rivers in returning trail.

Since launching earlier this summer, the York rivers trail has reached more than 6,000 people to help introduce new audiences to stories, places and connections along York’s rivers.

Now, as the trail enters its next chapter, residents and visitors are being invited to discover, create and explore through a new programme of activities taking place as part of York Walking Festival, York Environment Festival and the York 50+ Festival.

The York rivers trail offers a new way to explore York’s rivers through parks, gardens, historic spaces, exhibitions, digital experiences and community stories.

Councillor Jenny Kent, executive member for environment and climate emergency at City of York Council, said: “York’s rivers have always shaped our city, and the York rivers trail helps bring that relationship to life in new and engaging ways. It shows how water connects communities, landscapes and everyday life.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Following-the-Foss.webp)

“This autumn programme builds on the trail’s successful launch by creating opportunities for people to explore, learn, reflect and share ideas about the future of York’s rivers.

“Through walks, workshops and exhibitions, people can discover how nature, water quality, climate resilience and community action can help create healthier rivers and a more sustainable future for York.”

Mark Henderson, flood risk manager at City of York Council, said: “Whether we are talking about water quality, wildlife, flood risk or the way people use our rivers, it is important to understand that everything is connected.

“As we move into autumn and river conditions begin to change, the York rivers trail provides an opportunity for people to better understand how York lives with water. It helps make those connections visible, linking upstream landscapes, local action and the river through York into one shared story.

“The autumn programme offers opportunities to explore those connections through heritage, nature, creativity and practical learning.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Exhibition.webp)

Following a successful first summer season, the focus now turns to participation, creativity and reflection, inviting people of all ages to explore York’s rivers in new ways and help shape what comes next.

Highlight events include a creative storytelling and performance workshop, a guided exploration of the River Foss, and a special exhibition that explores the relationship between people, rivers, nature and place through photography, stories and creative responses developed with communities across York.
