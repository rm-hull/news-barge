De Goede's Canada side were beaten 33-13 by England in the 2025 World Cup final

**It is nearly a year sinceCanada lost the Women's Rugby World Cup final to England- but time has not been a healer for everyone.**

"Four to five nights a week I can't fall asleep because I'm still thinking about the final," says Canada captain Sophie de Goede, who is preparing to take on the world champions this Saturday at Sandy Park in Exeter.

So, is this match revenge for the side ranked third in the world?

Canada head coach Kevin Rouet is keen to play this down: "Revenge is a good brand but they [England] won the final of the World Cup - that was the most important game to win, the next important games will be in the World Cup 2029."

After Canada, England play New Zealand at Allianz Stadium, before two more tests against Canada in Toronto and Ottawa. In recent history, England's women have never faced such tough successive opposition.

Red Roses head coach John Mitchell says the games are an "audition" for the 2029 World Cup.

"You look at the geography that we're going to cover, we're not always going to be in our training base. We're going to be going to different facilities. It's a challenge," he said.

"It's going to be really cool to see how they handle pressure as well. I think the Red Roses are very good around pressure because I think it reveals how much they care".

## Canadians not PWR spies

There continues to be misconceptions around the status of the Canadian women: they are professional athletes but contracts, as with most other nations, do not yet compare to England's women.

The Canadian programme involves a clever strategy of using England's domestic PWR league to develop its players.

There are 25 Canadians in PWR and the current Canada coaching staff includes Kevin Rouet and Alex Austerberry, who both coach Saracens Women, and Nathan Smith from Loughborough Lightning.

"It's not like we're coming in to spy," joked De Goede. "We just don't have the quality league [in Canada]."

Canada are not the only country to do this - PWR is littered with top internationals from across the globe.

Mitchell has told BBC Sport that he and Charlie Hayter, head of women's performance at England Rugby, have lobbied to increase PWR game time for English Qualified Players (EQP) from next season.

Mitchell has frequently pushed his theory that players who can reach 1,000 PWR playing minutes a season "gives them a higher probability" of playing for the Red Roses.

"I haven't enjoyed seeing young English players between 21 and 23 not getting enough minutes," he said.

"I think that number [1,000] is very meaningful."

The RFU confirmed to BBC Sport the current requirements around 13 EQP players on average across matchday squads will remain. However, the lure of signing foreign talent will be dampened by the removal of club injury credit, which helps cover the cost of replacing an injured player.

From the 2027-28 season, only players who sustain an injury while on international duty for one of England's representative teams will be eligible.

Besides this, if any EQP player is captured by another union, they will only be able to hold their EQP status until the end of that season.
