A chef has reached the final of one of the UK’s most prestigious culinary competitions.

Tom Heywood, head chef and owner of Pignut & The Hare in Scawton, is among the final 10 contenders for the National Chef of the Year title after impressing judges throughout the semi-final stage of the competition.

The contest, organised by the Craft Guild of Chefs and judged by some of the most respected names in the industry, is considered one of the highest honours a chef can achieve in their career.

David Mulcahy, competition director, said: "Reaching the National Chef of the Year final is an outstanding achievement.

"Every chef who has made it this far has demonstrated exceptional skill, commitment and professionalism throughout the competition.

"Year after year, the standard continues to impress the judges, and we look forward to seeing how Tom rises to the challenge on the day."

Mr Heywood secured his place in the final following a rigorous judging process that tested creativity, technical skill, flavour, presentation and the ability to perform under pressure.

Chair of judges and chef patron of Moor Hall, Mark Birchall, said: "I've been incredibly impressed by the commitment and effort that's gone into the competition so far.

"I know how much pressure chefs put themselves under, but also how much they grow and learn from the experience.

"My advice to Tom is to keep it simple and focus on creating delicious, brilliantly executed dishes rather than trying to overcomplicate things."

The final will take place at Le Cordon Bleu London on Tuesday, October 6, where the 10 finalists will cook against the clock in front of an esteemed panel of judges.

The winner will be announced later that evening at a prestigious awards ceremony attended by leading figures from across the hospitality industry.

A spokesperson for the Craft Guild of Chefs said: "Organised by the Craft Guild of Chefs and judged by some of the industry's most respected chefs, reaching the final of National Chef of the Year is widely regarded as one of the greatest achievements in a professional chef's career.

"Each chef impressed the judges throughout multiple stages of the competition, earning their place in what promises to be a fiercely contested final."

The Craft Guild of Chefs was established in 1885 under its former name of the Cookery and Food Association. It has developed into the leading Chefs’ association in the UK and has many members worldwide.

Craft Guild of Chefs members come from all aspects of the foodservice and hospitality sectors working in a wide variety of positions from students and trainees to top management.

The organisation is supported by a royal patron, HRH The Duchess of Edinburgh KCVO CD, and its current president is Vice Admiral Sir Tony Johnstone-Burt, KCVO, CB, OBE, DL.

He will now compete for the chance to join an elite group of past winners and make his mark on the UK’s hospitality industry.
