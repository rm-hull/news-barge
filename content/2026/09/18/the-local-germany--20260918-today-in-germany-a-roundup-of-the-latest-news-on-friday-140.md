**Friday's top story:CDU promises fuel price relief ahead of key elections**

Germany’s governing CDU has for the first time outlined the scale of fuel price relief promised by Chancellor Friedrich Merz, with party Secretary General Franziska Hoppermann suggesting motorists could see prices fall by between 21 and 25 cents per litre from early October.

Speaking on ZDF’s *Maybrit Illner*, Hoppermann said discussions with Germany’s federal states would now focus on how to implement the measure. She firmly rejected calls from Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil of the SPD for a windfall tax on oil companies, arguing that such a levy would do nothing to bring down prices currently approaching €2.50 to €3 per litre.

Hoppermann also moved to quell speculation over Merz’s future, insisting the chancellor would remain in office despite ongoing political debate.

The comments come as the CDU faces a difficult electoral test. Polling ahead of elections in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania places the party on just six percent, raising the prospect of failing to cross the five per cent threshold for the first time in a federal state.

In Berlin, the CDU is polling at 20 percent, narrowly behind the opposition Left Party.

Germany has officially inaugurated its largest offshore wind farm to date, marking a significant milestone in the country’s transition towards renewable energy and greater energy independence.

The He Dreiht wind farm, located in the North Sea around 85 kilometres north-west of Borkum, features 64 turbines with a combined capacity of 960 megawatts. According to operator EnBW, the facility will generate enough electricity to supply approximately 1.1 million households once fully operational.

Speaking at the launch ceremony in Hamburg, Environment Minister Carsten Schneider described the project as a boost for Germany’s economy and energy security.

He said that renewable energy is not only the most climate-friendly option but also the cheapest and safest form of electricity generation, reducing the country’s reliance on costly fossil fuel imports.

The €2.4 billion project was built without government subsidies, with EnBW securing long-term power purchase agreements for the electricity produced. The company said some turbines are already feeding power into the grid, while others will be connected in stages over the coming months.

A series of explosions outside businesses in Frankfurt has intensified concerns over a growing wave of organised crime-related violence in Germany, with police arresting a 17-year-old Dutch suspect following the latest attacks.

Two explosive devices detonated in the early hours of Thursday outside a café in the Nordend district and a shisha bar on the Zeil shopping street.

The blasts caused extensive damage to both premises, shattered windows in nearby buildings and damaged parked vehicles, although no injuries were reported.

Investigators are examining links between the two attacks and three similar incidents in Frankfurt and neighbouring Offenbach in recent days. A separate 15-year-old suspect is already in custody in connection with an earlier explosion.

Prosecutors believe the attacks may be part of a “Violence as a Service” network, in which young people from the Netherlands are allegedly recruited to carry out bombings on behalf of criminal groups.

Hesse’s Interior Minister Roman Poseck described the incidents as evidence of a “new dimension” in organised crime, while authorities pledged close cooperation with Dutch investigators to identify those directing the attacks.

**Third death reported in Frankfurt Airport malaria outbreak**

A third person has died in a rare malaria outbreak linked to Frankfurt Airport, as local health authorities confirmed two additional cases among residents living near the airport.

The latest infections bring the total number of confirmed cases to eight since late August, including three fatalities. Health officials believe the victims were infected by an Anopheles mosquito that arrived on an aircraft, a phenomenon known as “airport malaria”.

Unlike the six airport employees infected during the initial outbreak, the two most recent patients had neither travelled to a malaria-endemic region nor worked at the airport. Both live in Frankfurt’s Schwanheim district, close to the airport.

Authorities said no further cases had been reported since the start of the week but urged doctors to consider malaria as a possible diagnosis in patients with unexplained fever, particularly those living or working near the airport.

**German factory orders hit record high as economic outlook improves**

Germany’s manufacturing sector has reached a new record level of outstanding orders, driven by strong demand for aircraft, ships, trains and military vehicles, according to the Federal Statistics Office.

The industrial order backlog rose by 1.5 percent in July compared with the previous month and was 10.9 percent higher than a year earlier. Officials said the backlog has now reached an all-time high, largely due to the lengthy production cycles associated with transport and defence-related manufacturing.

The duration of the order backlog, which measures how long companies could remain busy fulfilling existing orders without receiving new business, increased to a record nine months from 8.9 months in June.

Mechanical engineering also recorded growth in outstanding orders, while the automotive sector saw a decline amid mounting competitive pressures.
