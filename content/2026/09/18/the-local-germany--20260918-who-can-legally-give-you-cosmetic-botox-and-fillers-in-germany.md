Germany's booming cosmetic-injections market has also created opportunities for illegal providers. In recent months, courts in Berlin have dealt with multiple cases involving beauticians who allegedly injected Botox and hyaluronic-acid fillers despite lacking the necessary legal authorisation.

In one instance, a client reportedly suffered facial paralysis and required hospital treatment, while other patients needed corrective procedures after treatment.

If you're considering treatment in Germany, understanding who is legally allowed to inject what can help you avoid unqualified providers and reduce the risk of complications.

**Botox and other fillers are big business in Germany**

According to figures compiled from plastic surgeons in Germany, botulinum toxin, better known as Botox, was the country's most common non-surgical cosmetic procedure in 2024, with around 361,000 treatments performed.

Hyaluronic-acid fillers ranked second, with more than 220,000 procedures.

That popularity has created a large market for providers, but Germany's legal framework draws some clear boundaries around who can offer these treatments.

Botulinum toxin is a prescription-only medicine in Germany. As a result, Botox treatments are generally restricted to licensed physicians. This means that beauticians and cosmetic practitioners cannot legally administer Botox injections.

When it comes to Botox, this prohibition also extends to Germany’s alternative practitioners (*Heilpraktiker*).

Dentists occupy a limited middle ground. They may use botulinum toxin within the scope of dentistry, for example in treating conditions such as teeth grinding. But purely cosmetic facial Botox treatments generally fall outside the scope of dentistry.

**What about hyaluronic-acid fillers?**

The situation in Germany is a little more complicated when it comes to hyaluronic-acid fillers, which are not prescription medicines here.

Broadly speaking, licensed doctors can administer fillers legally and beauticians cannot. The more disputed question is whether and under what circumstances *Heilpraktiker* may perform filler treatments.

For anyone unfamiliar with the term, *Heilpraktiker* are state-authorised alternative practitioners who can provide certain healthcare services despite not being medical doctors.

Legal experts disagree about how far that authority extends when it comes to cosmetic fillers. Physician and lecturer Dr Sophia Wilk-Vollmann describes the legal position as "controversial" and says there is "no blanket authorisation" for *Heilpraktiker* to inject hyaluronic-acid fillers.

By contrast, lawyer Thomas Bruggmann argues that licensed *Heilpraktiker* are "generally permitted" to administer hyaluronic-acid fillers because they are not prescription-only products.

**Do doctors need a special Botox licence?**

Doctors in Germany don’t need a specific Botox certification from the state before administering treatment. In legal terms, a German medical licence is generally sufficient.

But there’s an important distinction between being legally allowed to perform a treatment and knowing what you're doing. Patients considering treatment are advised to ask about training, experience and how frequently the practitioner performs the procedure.

**How can you check whether your provider is authorised?**

Germany does not have a single national register dedicated specifically to Botox providers. Instead, the first step is usually to verify whether the person is actually a licensed doctor.

In Germany, titles such as *Facharzt für Plastische und Ästhetische Chirurgie* (Board-Certified Specialist in Plastic and Aesthetic Surgery) are protected qualifications that require extensive specialist training.

By contrast, marketing terms such as *Schönheitschirurg* (Cosmetic Surgeon) are not protected specialist titles. On their own, they don’t necessarily tell you anything about a person's formal qualifications.

That's why checking official registration and specialist credentials is often more useful than relying on how somebody describes themselves on Instagram, TikTok or a clinic website.

Each federal state has its own medical chamber (*Landesärztekammer*), and many maintain public physician-search databases. The German Medical Association (* Bundesärztekammer*) can also direct patients to the relevant regional doctor-search services.

Specialist societies can also be useful. The DGPRÄC, Germany's main professional body for plastic surgeons, and organisations such as the DGÄPC and VDÄPC all maintain searchable directories of members.

**What should you check before booking?**

If you're thinking about receiving Botox or fillers in Germany, start by finding out exactly who will perform the injection.

Check whether they're a licensed doctor, verify any specialist qualifications they claim to hold and ask about their experience with the specific treatment you want.

You should also be cautious of injections being offered in beauty salons, private apartments or other non-medical settings. Professional-looking social-media accounts, training certificates and online reviews are not substitutes for legal authorisation.
