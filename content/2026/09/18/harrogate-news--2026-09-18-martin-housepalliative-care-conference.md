**BOSTON SPA**— More than 70 healthcare professionals gathered at Martin House Children’s Hospice this week to participate in the charity’s first dedicated conference focused on advancing paediatric palliative care standards across Yorkshire.

Held on Tuesday, 15 September 2026, the event—titled *Martin House Children’s Hospice Conference 2026: Inspiring Excellence*—unfolded within the organisation’s newly redeveloped complex. The session served as an educational framework for regional practitioners to exchange specialist clinical insights, analyse real-world case studies, and review practical care models for young people living with life-shortening conditions.

Practice Educators Mandy Thorpe and Jen Knell initiated the agenda with an operational brief on the hospice’s community and site-based support networks. Following the opening overview, Chief Medical Officer and Consultant in Paediatric Palliative Medicine Dr Michelle Hills joined Clinical Nurse Specialist Helen Storton to lead an expert panel discussion navigating complex clinical scenarios.

The core morning curriculum also featured a research briefing delivered by Dr Naomi Taylor and Sinead McBryde, detailing current academic initiatives and field trials anchored at the facility.


**Charlotte Walker, Deputy Director of Clinical Services at Martin House, stated:** Children’s palliative care is about helping babies, children and young people live as fully and comfortably as possible, while supporting their whole family. Children with life-shortening conditions may receive care in many different places, not only in a hospice. By sharing the expertise developed at Martin House, we can help more professionals feel confident supporting children and families wherever they encounter them.



The afternoon segment transitioned into practical training streams, including specialised simulation modules structured to build practitioner confidence around care protocols following a patient death, alongside updated administrative frameworks for when and how to refer complex cases to hospice networks.

Delegates were also guided through technical tours of the redeveloped estate, inspecting specialised therapy installations including the newly updated hydrotherapy pool infrastructure. During the mid-day session, Dr Hills conducted an author signing event for her newly published text, *The Wrong Order of Things*.

Operating across West, North, and East Yorkshire, Martin House delivers comprehensive, family-led clinical intervention services free of charge to more than 550 children and young families annually, deploying teams directly into residential homes, regional hospitals, and dedicated hospice beds. Practitioners seeking further information regarding upcoming educational intakes, curriculum schedules, or organisational resources can access the data ledger via the verified Martin House Children’s Hospice Webspace.
