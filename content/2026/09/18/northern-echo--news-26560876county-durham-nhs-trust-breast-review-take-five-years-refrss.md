Thousands more breast surgery cases could be examined in a growing investigation into failures at a North East NHS trust, which warns the work could take five years.

County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust is reviewing breast patients’ treatment amid concerns about clinical practice, governance and oversight.

The current review covers patients treated between January 2023 and February 2025.

At an extraordinary meeting on Thursday, September 24, the trust board will consider extending the review as far back as 2015.

Board papers estimate that extending the review to 2015 could bring about 4,500 cases into scope.

At its current maximum rate of 80 reviews a month, the trust estimates the work could take five years.

Reviews of treatment before electronic records were introduced in 2022 are likely to take longer because older records are harder to examine.

Four options will be put before board members.

The first would keep the January 2023 to February 2025 timeframe. The second would do the same but keep the patient helpline open for two years after the reviews finish.

The trust’s preferred third option would complete the current review before moving back one year at a time towards 2015.

Experts would assess the findings after each year before deciding whether to move further back.

The fourth and widest option would immediately extend the review to 2015, when the trust says evidence of systemic failures emerged.

The trust estimates both wider options could involve about 4,500 reviews.

The board paper says the work is "highly specialised" and "reliant on a limited national pool of suitably qualified independent breast experts."

Cases can involve detailed checks of clinical notes, scans, pathology results, correspondence and records from different healthcare organisations.

About 20 per cent of cases need opinions from at least two experts, while one in four must be discussed by the Trust’s Clinical Reference Group.

  University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City 

*(Image: Chris Booth)*

The trust says its current resources allow a maximum of 80 reviews a month.

It added that going further back also "presents difficulties because records relating to treatment before October 2022 are predominantly historic paper records which have subsequently been scanned".

Reviewers may have to search hundreds of pages for clinic letters, scans, pathology results, multidisciplinary meeting notes, consent forms and operation notes.

The board papers call this "one of the most significant practical constraints" on extending the investigation further back.

  University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City. CEO of the County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust, Steve Russell 

*(Image: Chris Booth)*

The trust acknowledges evidence of prolonged governance failures in its breast service and says patients and the public want transparency and reassurance that nobody harmed has been overlooked.

The decision comes as concern grows over the scale of the problems already uncovered.

Reports say 20 women underwent mastectomies they did not need.

Of 514 cases reviewed, 315 patients suffered harm, including 77 who suffered significant harm, according to the Trust’s latest figures.

One patient is known to have died.

The review has identified delayed cancer diagnoses, treatment below best practice and patients undergoing more extensive surgery than necessary.

The trust board will decide the review’s future scope at its extraordinary public meeting on September 24.

Chief executive Steve Russell has apologised to women and families affected by the service’s failures and said significant changes have been made.

The Trust says it has not yet decided how far back the investigation will go.
