An army veteran who used a private browser in the early hours has been jailed for breaching a court order.

Steven Lancaster was given a sexual harm prevention order in 2022 after being convicted of possessing child abuse images and attempted sexual communication with a child.

The 40-year-old received a suspended prison sentence last October for breaching the same order, Newton Aycliffe Magistrates’ Court heard on Thursday (September 17).

Prosecutor Jade Houston said officers found Lancaster had been using a private browser during a routine check of his devices on May 17 this year.

Lancaster, formerly from the Bishop Auckland area, admitted breaching his sexual harm prevention order on May 17 this year.

His solicitor, Ben Pegman, called it a ‘momentary lapse’ and urged magistrates to avoid sending him to prison.

He said Lancaster was working as a lorry driver and sleeping in his cab when he logged on to the private browser without realising.

“It was a momentary lapse,” he said. “He wasn’t really thinking and was a bit groggy from waking up during the night.

“When he has realised, he has thrown the mobile phone in his bag of dirty washing as he didn’t want to go down that rabbit hole at all.”

Mr Pegman said the father-of-three had served in the army for about 15 years before his 2022 conviction.

Magistrates’ chairman Thomas Reay activated Lancaster’s ten-week suspended sentence and added 34 weeks for his latest breach of the SHPO.

Lancaster was also ordered to pay £270 in costs.

The 2022 conviction followed more than a month of online messages with a profile purporting to belong to a 14-year-old girl, to whom Lancaster sent sexual images.
