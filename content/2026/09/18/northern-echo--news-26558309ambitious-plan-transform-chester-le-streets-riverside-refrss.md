Durham County Council has backed a 'once-in-a-generation' report which could turn Chester-le-Street's Riverside site into a major sport, health and leisure hub.

Cabinet members approved an early vision for Riverside Sports Complex and Riverside Park, but no final redevelopment decisions have been made.

Under the plans, the authority hopes to secure outside investment to improve facilities and reduce long-term costs to the council in what is being called a "once-in-a-generation opportunity" to make Chester-le-Street a regional sports and recreation centre.

The council will now assess the plans, consult residents and draw up a business case before seeking approval.

The ideas include a community sports hub, improvements to Riverside Park, bigger cricket facilities and a possible new home for Durham Women FC.


Cllr Joe Quinn called the proposals "an exciting opportunity" for the area.

He said: "This report is an exciting opportunity to understand investment in sport, leisure, health, through a single vision of the Riverside area."

"It sets out a strong vision that builds on the town's strong sporting heritage while creating opportunities for future generations."

  Cllr Joe Quinn, cabinet member for planning, investment and assets. 

*(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

He said the plans could widen access to sport, improve parking, boost health and wellbeing, and raise Chester-le-Street's profile as a sporting destination.

The council says schools, clubs and community groups rely heavily on the Riverside complex, but ageing facilities—especially the pavilion—are becoming costly to maintain.

Talks are continuing with Durham Women FC, which has chosen Riverside as its preferred site for a new Women's Super League-standard stadium.

Council papers estimate such a development could cost around £30 million and would need to be funded entirely through private investment.

Cllr Kyle Genner said: "This is a sensible and proportionate proposal that allows the Council to explore what could be a significant opportunity for Riverside and Chester-le-Street whilst ensuring that any future decisions are informed by detailed evidence, consultation and planning."
