PLANS for a holiday let next to an historic York building have been blocked.

An application to convert former offices joined onto Cumberland House, in Cumberland Street, into holiday accommodation has been refused by York Council.

Plans from developers Helmsley Group stated the conversion would see the vacant building significantly improved.

But council planning officers stated there was a serious risk of flooding which could be dangerous to those staying there.

Cumberland Villa, which joins onto the Grade I-listed Cumberland House overlooking King's Staith and the River Ouse, would have been converted as part of the plans.

The building was constructed in the 1970s.

The application proposed converting the upper floors of the building to house accommodation with a kitchen and living area and three bedrooms across two levels.

Plans stated the accommodation would be close to many of York's restaurants, bars, shops and tourist attractions.

They added physical limitations meant it could no longer function as an office.

Plans stated: "The proposal is considered and sensitive and seeks a new viable use for the existing building whilst making a positive improvement to the building's facade and relationship with the adjacent listed building.

"The proposals represent a significant improvement to the existing building which in its current condition does not offer a positive contribution to the streetscape, and is currently in a vacant state and no longer lettable as office space.

"The alterations would improve what is seen as an average quality building into a more sensitive and contemporary design.

"They provide a positive front for Cumberland Street whilst preserving the significance of the historic character and unique Grade I-listed Cumberland House."

Council planning officers stated the proposals were unacceptable.

Officers stated: "It is unacceptable due to the combination of the transient nature of the future occupants of the holiday accommodation and the serious residual flood risk to the building and its occupants in an undefended one in 100 year plus 30 per cent climate change event which could achieve 1.7m depths.

"These depths would be serious should occupants have not evacuated the building prior to flooding occurring.

"The reuse of a vacant listed building, in a sustainable location, does not outweigh this serious potential risk to life or serious harm for future occupants of the short-term holiday let."

Historic England’s listing entry for Cumberland House stated it was built in around 1710 for William Cornwall, who served as York’s lord mayor and sheriff.

It takes its name from Prince William, the Duke of Cumberland, who is said to have stayed there after winning the Battle of Culloden in 1746.
