A RETROSPECTIVE application to retain a cafe that was built without planning permission has been withdrawn.

The application for Tea O Clock - based on the grounds of Chacha’s Plaza on Ingleby Road – was withdrawn on Monday, just two days before the plans were due to go before a planning committee.

No reason has been given for the withdrawal, but it came days after planning officers wrote a report saying the café was “of a poor design and constructed in poor quality materials.”

The report urged members of the Bradford Planning Panel to refuse the plans.

## Planning history of the site

Work on the café was completed in late 2024, but planning permission was never submitted, and this summer a retrospective application to retain the business was submitted by Anwar Bashir.

Chacha’s Plaza is the former Westfield House – a building that was once home to the Grattan catalogue shop.

In 2021, a planning application to convert the building into a “charity founded community hall, games room, classrooms and community canteen” was approved by Bradford Council.

The building has since been rebranded as Chacha’s Plaza, a venue that describes itself as having “a diverse range of retail units and food outlets” and attracting “a significant number of customers.”

The cafe is located in front of the building.

## What did the application say?

The application said: “Whilst the development has already been completed, this application provides the Local Planning Authority with the opportunity to fully assess the proposal against current planning policy.

“Rather than operating as an isolated destination, the café complements the existing commercial uses and enhances the overall attractiveness of Chacha Plaza.”

## Objections and councillor support

12 people had objected to the plans, raising concerns ranging from poor parking by customers of the café to its poor appearance.

There had been one letter of support, from a councillor – not named in the planning officer’s report.

Their letter said: “Please consider the money invested in this business within last few years and financial repercussions of a refusal for the owner.”

## Why would it have been refused?

The planning officers report suggesting the application be refused said: “The unit has formed a visual obtrusive feature within the site and adjacent to the main plaza building.

“The use of uPVC cladding and poor quality glazed frontage gives the appearance of a temporary rather than a permanent building.

“The café building that has been constructed within the site is considered to be of a poor design and constructed in poor quality materials resulting in a visually incongruous feature in this prominent location next to Ingleby Road causing significant harm to the character and appearance of this site and the wider streetscene.

“The proposal could result in vehicles parking on the adjacent highway which will obstruct the free movement of traffic on Ingleby Road which forms part of Bradford’s busy Outer Ring Road.

“It is noted that there is another unsightly structure being used as a café within the site, but this is also unauthorised and subject of ongoing enforcement action.”
