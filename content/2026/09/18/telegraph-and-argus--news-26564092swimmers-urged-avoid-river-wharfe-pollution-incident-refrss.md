Residents have been advised not to swim in part of the River Wharfe after a pollution incident in Ilkley.

Bradford Council said it had been notified by the Environment Agency of an incident at Beanlands Parade which impacted the river on Friday.

According to the council, the incident began at around 3pm and was brought under control by 4:54pm.

As a precaution, the Environment Agency has advised people not to swim in the River Wharfe at Cromwheel, Ilkley, while investigations continue and repairs are completed.

The warning has been published through the agency's website and will remain in place until officials receive confirmation that an effective repair has been carried out, the council said.

In a statement shared on social media, Bradford Council said: "We have been notified by the Environment Agency of a pollution incident at Beanlands Parade, Ilkley, which has impacted the River Wharfe."

## Where is the warning in place?

The warning affects the designated bathing water at Wharfe at Cromwheel, Ilkley, a stretch of the River Wharfe popular with swimmers and paddlers.

The Environment Agency's bathing water profile for the Ilkley site provides water quality information and alerts for river users.

No details have yet been released about the cause of the pollution or the extent of its impact on the river.

The affected stretch of river was designated as a bathing water in 2021 and is monitored by the Environment Agency during the bathing season.

The site's most recent classification was rated "Poor" under Environment Agency assessments.
