A Newcastle data centre has generated more than £140 million for the UK economy and could add another £1.2 billion over the next decade, according to a report.

The National Innovation Centre for Data (NICD), based at The Catalyst in Newcastle’s Helix district, was set up nine years ago to help businesses use data and artificial intelligence.

It has helped organisations across the UK boost productivity and attract investment through data innovation.

Professor Paul Watson, director of the National Innovation Centre for Data, said: "This new economic analysis highlights the significant positive contribution that the National Innovation Centre for Data has made to both the North East and the wider UK through developing workforce skills, creating jobs, and attracting inwards investment.

"Since its formation, NICD has enabled businesses and public organisations – from multinational companies and start-ups to local authorities and charities – to innovate, improve productivity and build their data science capabilities.

"Our collaborative model is designed to leave organisations with the skills and capacity to continue developing and applying AI and data science long after a project has ended.

"As NICD's reach and reputation continues to grow, we want to build on this impact and help even more organisations realise the value of their data."

Since launching, the centre has delivered more than 180 projects for small firms, major businesses and public bodies across the UK, including Sage, NHS England, Procter & Gamble and Newcastle Building Society.

It offers practical support, joint projects, training, technical seminars and events to help organisations improve their data and AI skills.

Ortus Economic Research says NICD generated £141 million in net additional gross value added for the UK economy between 2018 and 2026, directly creating 570 jobs.

The report forecasts another £1.2 billion for the UK economy by 2036, including £1.1 billion in the North East.

Much of that money is expected to come from inward investment in the region.

NICD said it was set up with £30 million from the UK Government and Newcastle University and generated £27.20 in net additional GVA for every £1 of direct public funding.

That is about four times the UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) average.

NICD has directly boosted Newcastle’s regional economy.

Ortus estimates NICD’s joint projects have generated £116 million in net additional GVA across the North East.

Its impact goes beyond the organisations it supports, strengthening the region’s data and AI network and attracting investors to the North East.

Ortus estimates NICD has directly created 467 regional jobs, with inward investment in digital and data science creating another 1,584.

NICD has also helped drive research growth at Newcastle University.

The report says NICD helped the university secure more than £102 million in public research funding by contributing to bids.

Professor Chris Day, vice-chancellor and president of Newcastle University, said: "Newcastle University is proud to be the home of the UK’s National Innovation Centre for Data.

"Its work reflects the University’s commitment to ensuring that world-class research and expertise are translated into practical solutions that respond to the technological demands of the modern world.

"The success of NICD demonstrates the important role universities can play in turning academic expertise into real-world outcomes."

NICD combines research, skills transfer and long-term partnerships to help organisations boost productivity, attract investment and create high-value jobs.

As its economic impact grows, NICD offers a model for universities using specialist expertise to drive regional and national growth.

A spokesperson for Newcastle University said: "By combining research, skills transfer and long-term partnership, NICD is helping organisations improve productivity while strengthening the conditions for investment and high-value job creation.

"With its impact projected to grow over the next ten years, NICD provides a model for how universities can use their specialist strengths to support regional and national economic growth."
