Community services for north Halifax residents will be assured of equal weight with the rest of Calderdale now it is being brought “in-house”, says a senior councillor.

Calderdale Council’s Cabinet approved proposals to reconfigure the borough’s neighbourhood teams, whose role includes keeping communities safe.

The service includes staff who liaise with partner services including the police and deal with issues ranging from dealing with issues ranging from illegal fly-tipping to anti-social behaviour.

It also includes staff who liaise with community groups on litter picks and community clean-ups, for example.

They also offer housing support and advice on issues including employment, benefits, health and debt management.

For most of Calderdale this is done in-house already, but arrangements for four wards – Mixenden, Ovenden, Warley and Northowram and Shelf, which is on the Bradford border – have been different.

North Halifax Partnership has been commissioned to deliver the service there and has done a good job – there is no criticism of them, said the council’s Cabinet member for Public Services, Coun Peter Hunt.

But it meant there were differences in the way it was operated and this should be done in same way across the whole borough – it was n imbalance that existed for a long time, he said.

Coun Hunt (Reform UK, Elland) said: “The report openly acknowledges disparity in service delivery and provision not driven in the same way as everybody else.”

Continuing with the same model would continue the same disparity.

“Residents raising concerns about community safety and so on are entitled to ask why north Halifax did so differently for so long.

“The council should never have allowed north Halifax to be an outlier in service provision,” said Coun Hunt.

Cabinet councillors agreed to take the £169,510 annual arrangement back in-house.

Bringing it in-house will bring that budget into an overall £801,300 pot for the services across the borough and will tighten a consistent approach, bringing it under the scope of a full service review to ensure alignment with available resources and focus on delivery of new corporate priorities, Cabinet members were told.

They were warned care must be taken to ensure that the North Halifax wards do not miss out – impacts identified by officers included, noting some of the wards have higher levels of deprivation compared to the rest of the borough, that by moving the Neighbourhood Team out of a local community organisation which is based at a local community asset, Mixenden Activity Centre, “we could be moving services further away from a disadvantaged community.”

Coun Hunt said there would be renewed investment and strong accountability through the change.

Efforts will be made to retain a physical presence of the Neighbourhood Team within the North Halifax team ward areas, council officers said in the briefing paper.

North Halifax Partnership is a community-led charity that brings people and organisations together to improve health, well-being and opportunities.
