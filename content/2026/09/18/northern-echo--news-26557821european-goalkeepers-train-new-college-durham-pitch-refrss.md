A multi-million-pound sports facility in County Durham is attracting professional goalkeepers from across Europe while benefiting elite athletes, students and the wider community.

Professional goalkeepers from Sweden, Portugal and Poland recently trained at the £4.5 million all-weather Multi-Use Games Area (MUGA) at New College Durham’s Framwellgate Moor campus.

Mark Robinson of Modern-Day GK said: "We’re always looking for venues where we can train and, ultimately, we want to be on a full-size football pitch that meets the professional standards we need.

"When I heard how good the facility was here, I had to see it for myself, and the pitch is amazing.

"We had three professional goalkeepers here at New College Durham’s MUGA, and when you reach their level, you want to coach on a professionally maintained pitch, and this definitely is."

MDGK was founded a decade ago by brothers Lee, Shaun and Mark Robinson and offers specialist training beyond traditional shot-stopping drills.

Its programme builds players’ mindset and resilience through decision-making, game analysis and learning from mistakes in a supportive environment.

The organisation has helped eight goalkeepers move from outside the professional game to securing full-time contracts in recent years.

New College Durham’s MUGA opened in 2025, meets FA and Sport England specifications and is already used regularly by student programmes.

The facility is part of the college’s wider £34 million investment in facilities and advanced equipment over the past five years.

A further £6 million is being spent on sports and music facilities, with work due to finish by 2027.

Barry Poskett, curriculum manager for sport at New College Durham, said: "This is much more than just a pitch.

"Here at New College Durham, we actively pursue and achieve the ideal of being a catalyst for positive change.

"It is great to see our facilities being used by organisations across the whole spectrum of ability.

"Whether starting out as an under-6 team member or attracting interest from players in Europe seeking to benefit from the coaching skills and facilities in the North East is a fantastic achievement for everyone involved.

The college’s Football Development Centre and weekday student programmes already use the MUGA, while the college is exploring ways to open it up to more local clubs and leagues.

The MUGA is fast becoming a County Durham sporting hub, offering players at every level a high-quality place to train and compete.
