## Halal and kosher meat ban call in York branded 'racist attack'

Cllr Mark Warters called for the council to stop buying halal and kosher meat 

A CALL for support in York to ban forms of religious animal slaughter has been branded a thinly-veiled racist attack.

Restore Britain's Cllr Mark Waters said legal religious exemptions for halal and kosher meat slaughter effectively sanctioned the torture of animals.

Rabbi Elisheva Salamo said animal welfare violations reported in some slaughterhouses were abhorrent to the Jewish community and the practice of kosher was carefully supervised.

Green Party activist Joe Nasson said the call singled out halal and kosher slaughter but ignored the cruel and inhumane treatment of animals in factory farms.

Cllr Warters made the call in a motion tabled at City of York Council's full council meeting on Thursday, September 17.

The Osbaldwick and Derwent ward councillor was unable to secure a seconder for the motion so it fell without being put to a vote.

Cllr Warters' motion would have required the council to call on the Government to remove religious exemptions to prevent slaughter without full, unrecoverable stunning.

It also called for the council to stop buying non and recoverable stun ritually-slaughtered meat and ban the sale and consumption of it on its premises.

Council catering contracts would be drafted to exclude the use of the meat and the authority would have been required to inform hospitality businesses of the authority's stance on it.

Cllr Warters told Thursday's meeting such practices should not be tolerated in modern society.

He added his motion followed one from former Labour councillor Paul Blanchard passed in 2007 banning the procurement and sale of foie gras on council premises.

The Restore Britain councillor said: "The abhorrent and unnecessary cruelty involved in halal and kosher slaughter is on a vastly different scale than the number of animals involved in foi gras which the council was so rightfully concerned about.

"It's disgusting and thankfully a growing number of progressive countries have recognised this and it's banned in those countries.

"We all have a moral duty to ensure animals meet their end in the most humane way, none of us should accept a religious exemption for the effective torture of animals in the 21st Century."

Rabbi Dr Salamo, of York's Liberal Jewish Community, told councillors exemptions to the rule where violations had been committed were not a reason to ban the practice outright.

The rabbi said: "Part of the point of kosher is the treatment of the animals, 88 per cent of halal meat slaughtered in the UK is stun killed beforehand so it's very difficult to determine the source of the meat and how it's dispatched.

"The problem for myself and co-religionists is that we believe strongly that there is a method to what we eat which is part of the holiness in our lives and our the centuries that method's been made as humane as possible.

"Many of the kosher laws are about the good treatment of animals."

York Green Party Co-Chair Mr Nasson said Cllr Warters should go further and he was wrong to think that the non-ritual killing of animals was any more humane.

The activist said: "Cllr Warters will tell you how he thinks that slitting an animal's throat is intolerably cruel, painful and utterly abhorrent in this country, not everywhere seemingly, just in the UK.

"However, speaking to you as a brown, vegan, I have to say let's not kid ourselves that Cllr Warters' very thinly-veiled call for animal welfare is anything but a racist anti-Muslim and anti-Jewish attack.

"Singling out halal and kosher practices and ignoring our very own home grown intolerably cruel, painful and utterly abhorrent practices just proves that."
