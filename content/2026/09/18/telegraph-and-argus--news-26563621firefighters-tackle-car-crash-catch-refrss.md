FIREFIGHTERS tackled a multi-vehicle crash - but it was only for training purposes.

The exercise of a realistic scenario tested Spen Valley Blue Watch crews on stabilising vehicles, reaching casualties, cutting them free and using specialist rescue equipment.

Photos show several overturned and badly damaged cars scattered across rocky ground covered in loose stones and mud.

Quite naturally, teamwork and scene management were also key elements of the exercise.

## What the fire service had to say

A spokesperson for West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue (WYFRS) said: "Spen Valley Blue Watch recently took part in a challenging multi-vehicle road traffic collision training exercise, providing crews with an excellent opportunity to put their skills into practice in a realistic setting.

"The scenario tested a range of RTC response techniques, including vehicle stabilisation, casualty access and extrication, the use of specialist rescue equipment, teamwork, and effective scene management.

"The complexity of the incident challenged decision making under pressure and provided valuable learning opportunities throughout.

"Training like this helps ensure our crews remain prepared to respond safely and effectively when real incidents occur."

## Why are these exercises so vital?

Multi-vehicle collision exercises are regularly carried out by fire crews to ensure they are prepared for the complex challenges often presented by serious road traffic incidents.

Such incidents can involve multiple casualties, heavily damaged vehicles and difficult rescue conditions, requiring crews to make rapid decisions while working alongside other emergency services.

Training scenarios are designed to replicate the pressures of real emergencies, allowing firefighters to practise using hydraulic cutting equipment, vehicle stabilisation systems and specialist rescue tools in a safe but realistic environment.

The exercises also help crews familiarise themselves with evolving vehicle technology, including electric and hybrid vehicles, which can present additional hazards during rescue operations.

By recreating realistic incidents, fire services are able to assess operational procedures, reinforce safety measures and identify areas for improvement.

The training ensures firefighters can maintain the skills needed to rescue casualties quickly and safely when attending real-life collisions on the region's roads.
