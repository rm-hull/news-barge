A SECOND Bradford primary school has been forced to shut in an area that has no water supply.

Hoyle Court Primary School, on Fyfe Grove in Baildon, announced a full closure after the building lost its water supply, with families informed shortly after the issue emerged.

The school caters for 301 pupils aged between five and 11.

## No water supply to school

In a notice published on Bradford Council's emergency school closures page, Hoyle Court Primary School said it had no option but to shut after water supplies to the building stopped.

The school said: "Unfortunately we have had to close school due to there being no water supply in the area.

"Water supply stopped to the building at around 8.20am and families were advised of the closure immediately following this."

The closure notice was last updated at 10.34am.

## Yorkshire Water working to restore supplies

The closure comes as another school, Baildon Church of England Primary School, also remained closed due to a lack of water, prompting concerns that wider supply issues may be affecting parts of the district.

A spokesperson for Yorkshire Water said the company was aware of the issue within the Baildon area and expected repairs to take several hours.

They said: "We're aware of the issue and would like to apologise for the inconvenience caused.

"We aim to get to site and would expect supplies to be restored within four to six hours.

"You may have no water or low water pressure at the moment.

"It may be cloudy or discoloured for a while when it comes back, but you can clear this by running your tap for a few minutes."

## 
  **Yorkshire Water says supplies should return soon**

A spokesperson for Yorkshire Water said the disruption was caused by a fault with a pressure relief valve and apologised to affected residents.

They said: “We’re aware that a number of properties in Baildon have been without water supply this morning, and we apologise for the inconvenience. This was the result of a fault with a pressure relief valve, which we are currently working to fix. In the meantime, we have a clean water tanker on site, and have rezoned the network, so all properties should be back on supply soon.”
