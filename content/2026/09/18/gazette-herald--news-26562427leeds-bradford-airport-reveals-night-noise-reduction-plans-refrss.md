Leeds Bradford Airport has revealed plans to reduce noise from night-time flights and condense its overnight hours.

The airport’s CEO, Vincent Hodder, said the proposals aim to make Yorkshire better connected and are “not the ‘thousands more night flights’ some would claim”.

Leeds Bradford Airport has applied to introduce a modern cap system, incentivising quieter, more environmentally friendly planes at the airport.

The airport has been limited to 2,920 aircraft movements between 11pm and 7am but is now applying to condense its night-time hours.

Leeds Bradford said condensing its night-time hours to 11.30pm to 5.59am would put it in line with other UK airports.

The airport has also announced plans to introduce a noise insulation scheme, saying this would “support the local community”.

Grants would be offered for glazing, ventilation improvements and funding for bedroom air conditioning in eligible properties, Leeds Bradford Airport said.

A spokesperson for the airport said it believes the “combination of maintaining a fixed overall noise limit while introducing targeted community support represents a balanced approach that benefits both local residents and the wider regional economy”.

They added that the proposals to update operating conditions dating back more than 30 years will ensure Leeds Bradford Airport “remains competitive, sustainable and capable of supporting Yorkshire's future ambitions”.

Mr Hodder said: “Modernising operating conditions that were introduced more than three decades ago is an important part of that journey.

"It enables us to encourage quieter, cleaner aircraft while maintaining robust environmental controls and providing meaningful support for our neighbours.

"This is about creating a stronger, more sustainable and better-connected Yorkshire for the decades ahead, this is not the ‘thousands more night flights’ some would claim.”

## Chamber of commerce backs plans

The West and North Yorkshire Chamber of Commerce is one of the organisations supporting Leeds Bradford Airport’s plans.

"Leeds Bradford Airport is not only a major employer in its own right, but also supports a broader ecosystem of jobs, apprenticeships and skills development across the region,” the chamber’s chief executive, James Mason, said.

"Its continued growth and investment create valuable opportunities for local people to build careers in aviation, engineering, hospitality and customer service.

"This exciting next phase of modernisation sends a clear signal of confidence in the region’s potential and will further enhance its appeal as a place to do business, invest and visit.”

Steve Heapy, CEO of Jet2, added: “This modernisation is welcome news, both for the regional economy and for local holidaymakers.

"We have been proudly operating from Leeds Bradford Airport since 2003 and our longstanding commitment has never been stronger.

"We look forward to continuing to work in partnership with the airport on the next exciting phase of development.”
