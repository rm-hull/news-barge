Six trees at a Todmorden care home can be removed because they are in a poor state, Calderdale planners have agreed.

The trees at Pennine Lodge Care Home at Burnley Road are subject to a group tree preservation order (TPO) and permission must be given before they can be removed.

Associated Wellbeing Limited made the application, to which Calderdale Council planning officers have now given consent.

A report from arboricultural and ecological consultants JCA Limited, said removal of three of the trees should be a “high priority.”

In all, the report covers maintenance and management of 74 trees on the site of the care home.

All six of the trees subject to the application are marked by the arboriculturist as unsuitable for retention due to their poor condition.

“These trees are in such a condition that they cannot be realistically retained as living trees in the context of the current land use for longer than 10 years,” said their report.

The work should be carried out within two years and the consent does not override the legal protection afforded to nesting birds, bats or other protected species.

It is the responsibility of the applicant to ensure compliance with all relevant wildlife legislation prior to undertaking the works.

The decision does not remove the requirement to obtain any other permissions or consents that may be necessary, said the planners.
