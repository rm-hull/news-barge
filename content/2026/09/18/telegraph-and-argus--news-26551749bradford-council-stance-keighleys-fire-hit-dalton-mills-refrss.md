Bradford taxpayers could be left exposed to significant liabilities and costs if the Council were to make an ill-judged intervention in the long-running saga over a fire-ravaged mill, it is feared.

Due to the complex ownership history of Dalton Mills in Keighley, the Council's Reform UK leadership has warned that any action it takes must be "carefully considered" to avoid future financial and legal consequences for the public purse.

When the bankrupt company which previously owned Dalton Mills was dissolved via a compulsory strike-off in 2021. the 19th century complex became subject to an arcane law called escheat - meaning it effectively became ownerless - and it was given up to the Crown Estate.

Bradford Council has come under pressure to take swift and decisive action amid fears for the site's future - but the local authority has warned that any intervention would not be straightforward.

Councillor Andrew Judson, Deputy Leader of Bradford Council, said: "Dalton Mills is a complex legal issue due to a lack of identified owner.

"Any action taken on site must be carefully considered to avoid exposing the Council, and ultimately local taxpayers, to future legal liabilities or costs."

Coun Judson, who is also a representative for the Keighley West ward, said: "Some measures were put in place by the Council last year to restrict unauthorised access, but despite these efforts some individuals remain determined to target this historically significant building and put residents, businesses and the public at risk.

"We remain committed to supporting efforts to bring Dalton Mills back into use and to securing a viable future for the site under private ownership."

Coun Judson was speaking after Councillor Caroline Firth (Labour, Keighley) accused Bradford Council of failing to act after another major fire at the derelict complex.

  Councillor Caroline Firth has spoken out against the inaction by Bradford Council regarding the Dalton Mills blaze 

*(Image: Newsquest / Bradford Council)*

The latest blaze broke out at the historic site on Saturday, prompting renewed calls for action.

Coun Firth has written to the Council's chief executive Lorraine O’Donnell, describing the council’s response as lacking urgency and leadership.

### 'Devastation, panic and fear' at the scene

In her letter, Cllr Firth described visiting the scene and witnessing devastation, panic and fear.

She said: "Large chunks of debris were raining down on people and homes.

"The fire crews were fantastic, dampening down roofs before the embers took hold."

She said the fire nearly ignited the roof of one resident’s home, while another person’s hair caught fire.

Some residents were reportedly too frightened to return home, with one telling Cllr Firth they would not go back until they were sure they would not be burnt alive in their home.

### Residents feel 'ignored, powerless and last on the list'

Cllr Firth was highly critical of Bradford Council’s response, arguing that officers were failing to provide leadership and that the authority was already being blamed by the public for not taking action.

She said: "The council has a leadership role in the community that it is completely abdicating."

She also said residents in Keighley felt ignored, powerless and last on the list of priorities for the council.

The councillor believes a lack of urgency and joined-up working within Bradford Council contributed to the situation.

### Support for private ownership

Cllr Firth praised Tim Rogers of Future Transformations, who has been trying to bring Dalton Mills into private ownership.

She said Mr Rogers had been asking the council for more than a year to use legal powers to create a new title for the land, which would remove a historic charge.

Cllr Firth believes that transferring the site into private ownership offers the best hope of securing its future.

She said: "If Dalton Mills stays in the Crown Estate we know that the only outcome is the building will eventually burn down.

"This latest fire, which has destroyed the only building left worth saving, is something I’ve warned of and essentially begged for action on for well over a year."

### 'Please take action'

She ended the letter by urging the council’s chief executive to step in, saying she could no longer keep her frustrations private.

She wrote: "My plea: Please take action.

"I am and have done all I can on this issue.

"All of my emails have been private and I have kept my frustrations private.

"I can no longer do this - I need my residents to know I have been and am fighting for them and our place, and will continue to do so."
