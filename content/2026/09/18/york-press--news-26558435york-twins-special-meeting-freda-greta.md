## York identical twins say Freda and Greta understood what others never could

Growing up in the 90s, Victoria and Charlotte would frequent a coffee shop in Coppergate after school, which is where they met Freda and Greta *(Image: Supplied)*

TWIN sisters from York have spoken of their special connection with the city’s renowned identical twins Freda and Greta.

Victoria and Charlotte (née Beal) say they understand first-hand what it is like to be treated differently as identical twin sisters.

Growing up in the 90s, the pair, now 48, met Freda and Greta Chaplin, who they describe as “welcoming and incredibly friendly”.

The inseparable Chaplin sisters, who grew up in Tang Hall and became internationally known following a sensational court case in the 1980s, are the inspiration behind Bucking Fastard, a new film starring sisters Rooney and Kate Mara.

  Freda and Greta Chaplin - screen shot taken from A Pair of One, documentary by Juliet Darling 

*(Image: Supplied)*

  


The sisters, who were about 14 or 15 at the time, often stopped for tea after school at a café above a bookshop in Coppergate when they bumped into the sisters.

  The pair say there are many misconceptions about the iconic York twins that people who don't have an identical twin would 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Charlotte said they immediately felt a sense of kinship with Freda and Greta because, as identical twins themselves, they recognised some of the challenges the sisters faced.

"People thought they were scary and freaky, but they made us feel most welcome, like we were part of their club," she said.

"They were completely different from what we expected them to be and chatted to us.”

The Beal sisters said they frequently saw Freda and Greta around York city centre and noticed how members of the public would often stare.

  Victoria and Charlotte described their identical looks as a "gift" 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"People were curious about them, naturally drawn to them," said Victoria.

"People used to stare at them, and at us too. We found it funny."

Like Freda and Greta, the sisters enjoyed leaning into the novelty of being identical.

They developed what they called the "T-game", deliberately copying each other's mannerisms and behaviour to confuse people.

"We used to behave in exactly the same way to throw people off balance," Victoria said.

## 'It's often as if you know exactly what the other is thinking'

The pair admit they regularly left teachers flustered while attending All Saints School in York.

One memorable incident saw Charlotte and Victoria swap places during lessons and exams.

Charlotte attended Victoria's French exam while Victoria turned up to Charlotte's history class, only to discover there was an unexpected test.

"I got something awful like 15 per cent," Victoria laughed.

"Charlotte went to the teacher crying and explaining what had happened, but he told her she had to keep the bad score to learn a lesson."

  Victoria and Charlotte moved to Amsterdam after school. Charlotte continues to live there today and Victoria has returned to York 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The sisters also remember being inseparable outside school, even going on a date with identical twin brothers, who were in on the joke and wore the same clothes, making everyone at the pub do a double take.

After school Victoria joined Charlotte in Amsterdam for work.

Their uncanny similarities continued into adulthood. Ahead of one New Year's Eve party, Charlotte bought a new outfit, only for Victoria to unknowingly purchase exactly the same clothes from the same shop a few hours later.

Victoria said: “It's often as if you know exactly what the other is thinking, especially when you’re young.

“You have a gift that few people can relate to.

“I think this comes across when people speak about Freda and Greta and how in-sync they were – people just don’t understand the bond you have with your identical twin.”

  The pair followed each other to Amsterdam, where Charlotte continues to live 

*(Image: Supplied)*

As for wearing the same clothes, Victoria said: “You have the same body type, so naturally you buy something that suits you.

“Inevitably you end up looking and acting the same way, intuitively.”

The family resemblance has continued into the next generation too, with Charlotte giving birth to identical twin boys in 2009.

While identical twins occur randomly and are not inherited, the odds of the phenomenon striking the Beal family twice, given it occurs in just three to four per 1,000 births, did not go unnoticed.

  Charlotte said when she and Victoria would go places with her twin boys people would stare at them like they were "party attractions" 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Charlotte said: “When my boys were born and Charlotte and I would be outside people would stare at us like we were a party attraction.”

As The Press reported, more than five years after Freda's death, the extraordinary lives of her and Greta, from Tang Hall, are set to reach a new audience.

Their story has inspired a major Hollywood film, Bucking Fastard, starring Rooney Mara (famous for her roles in The Girl With The Dragon Tattoo and Carol) and her real-life sister Kate (who starred in Brokeback Mountain and Fantastic Four), who will play inseparable sisters Jean and Joan Holbrooke, modelled on the lives of Freda and Greta.
