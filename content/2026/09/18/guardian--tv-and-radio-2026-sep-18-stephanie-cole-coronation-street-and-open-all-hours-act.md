The actor Stephanie Cole, best known for roles in Coronation Street, Waiting for God and Open All Hours, has died aged 84.

She also appeared in the prisoner-of-war drama Tenko, A Bit of a Do and Doc Martin.

A statement from her agent, John Grant, on behalf of her family, said: “It is with great sadness that we announce the passing of Stephanie Cole, OBE.

“She passed away this morning at the age of 84. Stephanie had been living with Alzheimer’s disease for some years.

“Stephanie was an extraordinary talent whose long and distinguished career included many iconic performances in theatre and television. She brought joy to millions through her many much-loved roles in sitcom and drama.

“She will also be remembered for her tireless work supporting mental health charities. During this incredibly difficult time, the family kindly requests privacy.”

![Cole sitting at a kitchen table reading a magazine, alongside her fictional son Roy Cropper and his wife, Hayley Cropper](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/6cdbfc6bcfdd61b73df97390f22578717a61ec99/37_0_1959_1339/master/1959.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Cole appeared on Coronation Street between 2011 and 2013 as Sylvia Goodwin, the mother of Roy Cropper. She won the British Soap award for best comedy performance for the role.

Her other notable performances included roles in Still Open All Hours, in which she reprised her Open All Hours character Mrs Featherstone, The Cleaner, Gentleman Jack and Talking Heads: Soldiering On.

She won the British Comedy award for best actress for the 1990s sitcom Waiting for God, about residents of a retirement home.

Cole also had an extensive career in the theatre, with roles in King Lear, A Passionate Woman and Noises Off.
