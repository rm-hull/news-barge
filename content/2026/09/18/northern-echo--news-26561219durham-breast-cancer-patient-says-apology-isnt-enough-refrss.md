A County Durham breast cancer patient has said an NHS apology cannot make up for years of “continued torment” after the trust admitted her care fell below expected standards.

Catriona Dunbar, 41, from Stanley, was diagnosed with breast cancer in June 2023 and says she was treated “appallingly” from her first appointment through surgery, follow-up treatment and subsequent checks.

The County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust has now apologised for shortcomings including a lack of informed consent, insufficient dressings following surgery, a three-month delay in providing test results and repeated examples of unacceptable communication and behaviour by staff.

Catriona said the written apology had left her feeling angrier in some respects because she believes there has still been no proper accountability for what happened.

She said: “The letter is full of apologies, but there is no accountability.

“It says things shouldn’t have happened, but there are no admissions as to why, or as to who was at fault, so in some ways it’s made me angrier, because accountability is what we need here.”

Catriona underwent a lumpectomy at the University Hospital of North Durham after being diagnosed with cancer.

She has since questioned whether the tumour should instead have been removed as part of a breast reduction procedure and has raised concerns about the stitching and dressing of the wound after surgery.

  Catriona and her husband Paul 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

She later developed an infection which caused her breast to swell.

The incision subsequently burst open while she was out shopping, an experience she said left her feeling “completely humiliated and without dignity”.

The infection delayed the start of her chemotherapy.

Catriona says that when she later returned for tests to check whether her cancer had come back, she encountered staff who were “abrupt, rude and dismissive”.

She claims she was told on one occasion that she was “wasting their time” and then had to wait three months for results despite repeatedly contacting the hospital while preparing for her wedding.

She was eventually reassured that she remained cancer-free.

  Catriona and husband Paul 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

However, dissatisfied with her treatment, Catriona made formal complaints to the trust and sought legal advice.

She has also obtained a second opinion privately and has been interviewed by Durham Police as part of its investigation into whether any criminal offences occurred within the trust’s breast cancer services.

The trust’s deputy chief executive Kelly Angus has since written to Catriona apologising for the “significant distress and upset” caused during her treatment.

The letter said her care “fell significantly short of the standards expected” both in her treatment and when she sought answers about what had happened.

  Catriona Dunbar 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

The trust acknowledged a lack of informed consent around her surgery, inadequate dressings and the delay in confirming results when Catriona was waiting to learn whether her cancer had returned.

Ms Angus said the delay would have caused “unimaginable distress”.

The letter also acknowledged “repeated instances of unacceptable communication and language, poor behaviour and unsatisfactory conduct” by staff.

But Catriona said the cancer itself had not been the most traumatic element of her experience.

She said: “For me, the cancer has not been the hardest part of all this. Having cancer was probably the least traumatic part of this experience.

“It has been continued torment.

  Catriona Dunbar 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

“I was treated appallingly and given no dignity throughout my treatment. I was never consulted with; I was just told what was happening."

She added: “I shouldn’t be where I am today, three-and-a-half years after my diagnosis, having had to seek second opinions from private specialists and undergo more surgery, engaging with lawyers and speaking with the police.

“The whole thing has been completely inhumane.”

Catriona said she had become increasingly determined to speak publicly after learning that other women had also suffered harm.

She said: “I went from being really sad at how awful my own experience was to being entirely determined to ensure this can’t happen to more people.

“There is a massive issue within the trust which needs to be resolved and fixed, and I want to be a part of making sure that happens.”

  Catriona Dunbar 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

She added: “We cannot be here in another 20 years’ time in the same position. Lessons have to be learned not only in this trust, but across the country.”

Hudgell Solicitors are representing a large group of patients, including Catriona, and calling for a public inquiry.

Her comments come as the trust has issued a fresh apology in board papers prepared for an extraordinary public meeting on September 24.

The County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust said: “We acknowledge that, during the period under review, the standards our patients had a right to expect were not consistently met and that failures in care, governance and oversight caused distress, uncertainty and harm for some patients and their families.

“We offer our sincere apologies to everyone affected and remain deeply sorry for the impact this has had.

“We recognise their lasting impact and remain committed to learning from the past, building on the significant improvements already made, and ensuring patients receive the safe, compassionate and high-quality care they deserve.”

Her case comes amid a major independent review of breast services at County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust.

The review currently covers 1,472 patients treated between January 2023 and February 2025.

According to figures contained in the material supplied, 514 cases had been reviewed, with 315 patients found to have suffered harm, including 77 who suffered significant harm. One patient is known to have died.

The Trust is also considering extending its lookback further into the past.
