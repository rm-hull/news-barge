Residents fear Hamsterley will become 'cut off' after Durham County Council agreed to permanently close a road into the village from the A68.

Crakehill Bank has been closed since 2022 when part of the embankment gave way.

On Wednesday, September 16, the council’s cabinet agreed to seek a magistrates’ order to permanently close the road after repair costs topped £10 million.

The council said worsening ground conditions mean the route cannot realistically reopen safely and repairs would not guarantee issues would be resolved.

But villagers say the closure will lengthen journeys, pile pressure on alternative roads and leave emergency-access fears unresolved.

Jan Wilkinson, 73, who has lived in Hamsterley for 33 years, said many residents feared the road would never reopen after it closed.

She said: "It's a wonder there hasn't been a death on the diversion road.

"The council really do have to listen. We've got one road out.

  Crakehill Bank has been closed since a landslide in 2022.


"We've had ambulances stuck, we've had the fire brigade stuck, cars going on footpaths and the air ambulance was in again last Sunday."

Mrs Wilkinson said the closure was lengthening journeys and making Hamsterley less attractive to younger families.

"The young ones don't want to move into the village because they can see the objections to living here," she said.

"It could add anywhere from 10 to 20 minutes on your journey."

  The road was condemned by Durham County Council on Wednesday (September 16).


Retired farmer Brian Hurworth, 67, said Crakehill Bank was a vital link between Hamsterley and the A68.

"The village is cut off a little bit by not having that road," he said.

"It was a major route out of Hamsterley.

"I think there should have been more done to keep it open."

Mr Hurworth said the closure had also sent more traffic on to alternative roads and bridges.

"They're worried about the bridge starting to crumble at the sides," he said.

"If that road had to close, we're buggered. Simple as."

  Brian Hurworth said the village could become 'cut off'.


Councillors insisted the decision had not been taken lightly.

A report to the cabinet said the embankment was still deteriorating and warned that even a multimillion-pound repair would not guarantee the road’s long-term stability.

Councillor Tim McGuinness, cabinet member for rural communities, highways and transport, said: "This has been a difficult decision and one that has not been made lightly, but it is not possible to reopen Crakehill Bank, guarantee public safety, or ensure the road will not slip further again."

Carer Emma Atkinson, 51, called the decision “a travesty” and questioned whether the council had properly explored alternatives.

She said: "We've only been managing because we thought it would be reopened at some point and it's looking like it won't be.

"I think it's a travesty."

Ms Atkinson said the diversion was causing delivery problems and warned that alternative roads were struggling to cope.

She said: "A lot of takeaway places are charging £10 and above to deliver here because that road is closed."

  Hamsterley villagers have had to deal with lengthy diversions for years.


She also raised concerns about safety on the alternative route used by walkers, horse riders and families.
