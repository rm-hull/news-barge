Concerns about St Peter's Church were raised at a Bishop Auckland community meeting days after a major fire reignited scrutiny of the troubled building.

The Grade II listed church on Princes Street, which is over 130 years old, suffered extensive damage in a blaze on Sunday, September 13.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service sent a major response to the incident.

Firefighters spent several hours at the scene as flames spread through parts of the building, while thick smoke was visible across Bishop Auckland.

Images taken after the fire showed significant damage to the roof and inside the church.

The blaze has renewed questions about the future of the town-centre landmark.


However, concerns about the vacant building had been raised regularly by residents before the incident.

On Wednesday night (September 16), at a PACT meeting, residents asked about the church and said they were unsure whether problems at the site should be reported to police or Durham County Council.

Durham Police outlined steps taken before the fire to address issues at the property.

A police representative said: “The main door has been screwed down and windows boarded up.

"We are aware of the issue with the church and the owner has been contacted multiple times.”

Officers also said that they had searched the building several times while regional fire services had entered the church on a number of occasions before Sunday’s blaze.

The discussion focused on which organisation should be contacted when concerns arise and what action had already been taken by the authorities.

Residents’ questions reflected long-standing worries about the building’s condition, security and future, while highlighting the wider disconnect between emergency services and the community.

Sunday’s fire has brought those concerns into sharper focus, with people now anxious about what will happen to the prominent landmark.

Durham County Council said separately that it remained committed to working with the building’s owners.

Graham Wood, the council’s economic development manager, said: “In the case of St Peter's Church, we will continue to work with the owners of the Grade II listed building and use all powers at our disposal to make sure vital work is carried out to bring the building back into use.”

The council’s statement said work to secure the church's future would continue.

For now, the church remains damaged, and its long-term future is uncertain.
