Open earbuds are the big new thing in headphone land. Unlike in-ear variants, open earbuds are like tiny speakers that rest on the ear and let outside sound in naturally, so you can fully hear the world around you. Some use a hook style that wraps around the back of your ear, while others are clip-ons that look more like statement-piece earrings. Style aside, they are excellent for anyone who finds traditional earbuds uncomfortable or wants full environmental awareness for activities like running, biking, or even just doing chores around the house.

I've tested dozens of models since 2022, and while I still recommend having a pair of noise-canceling earbuds, I also love open earbuds for specific use cases.

## Best Open Earbuds Overall

The Shokz OpenDots 2 are everything I want open earbuds to be. These are truly take-everywhere, do-anything headphones. That's partly thanks to extremely long battery life—10 hours on a single charge, and 40 hours total with the charging case. They also have an IP57 dust and waterproof rating, the same as premium, flagship earbuds like Apple AirPods Pro 3 and Samsung Galaxy Buds4 Pro. I've worn them in the rain, on the beach, and fully submerged them in water multiple times without issue.

They're clip-ons that wrap around the earlobe, with the speaker end resting at the ear canal opening and the other end behind the ear. Importantly, the top of your ear is free for glasses, sunglasses, or hats. Like nearly all open earbuds, they let in sound from your surroundings, so don't expect them to be a great companion for long-haul flights or daily gym sessions.

They are a bit pricey, though. Shokz recently launched the lower-priced Shokz OpenDots Air and Shokz OpenFit Air 2—I may update this recommendation depending on how my testing goes.

The Bose Ultra Open Earbuds were a big deal when they launched in 2024—a brand-new design from Bose and one of the biggest swings from a major brand in clip-on open earbuds. The Shokz OpenRun Pro 2 are more headphones than earbuds—they have a wraparound headband styling, long battery life, great sound quality (for open headphones), and no charging case. **EarFun Clip for $70:** For around $70, these are a solid pick for anyone on a budget. **Suunto Spark for $179**: The Suunto Spark has a similar story to the Suunto Wing 2: with athletes in mind, it has features that sync with Suunto fitness watches, but it is outclassed in the areas that matter (comfort, sound quality, battery) by the similarly priced Shokz Openfit 2.

**What Are Open Earbuds and How Do They Sound?**

At a glance, open earbuds seem a lot like typical wireless earbuds. The big difference is that, instead of going inside your ear canal, they rest somewhere on the outside of your ear, leaving it open so you can easily hear the world around you. I use them all the time for outdoor exercise like running or biking, where I want my music but also need to hear other runners, bikers, and cars to stay safe.

Because they leave your ears open and the sound comes from farther away (outside your ear rather than inside), audio performance isn't as clear, intimate, or bassy as regular earbuds. They also typically don't have noise-canceling features, so they are not a great fit for the office or air travel.

**When or Where Would I Use Open Earbuds?**

Open earbuds are perfect for outdoor activities, or when you want to talk to other people without taking off your headphones. When I run or go for a bike ride, I only use open earbuds so I can hear my surroundings and stay safe. I also like using them around the house when I am cooking or doing laundry.

**What About Semi-Open Earbuds?**

Semi-open earbuds, like Apple's AirPods, sit inside the ear but don't have eartips that create a sound seal. They can offer better audio quality than other open earbuds because the speakers and microphones are closer to your ear and mouth. Semi-open earbuds are comfortable, but they're usually pretty loose—they can fall out easily and may be a poor option for fitness activities.

**How Do I Test Open Earbuds?**

I test open earbuds like I test any pair of headphones: in the real world, in my daily life. I wear each pair for at least a few weeks to test comfort, battery life, ease of use, and onboard controls. I listen to podcasts, watch TV shows, jam out to my favorite playlists, and call friends.

With open earbuds in particular, I also wear them on jogs, bike rides, and errand runs to get a better feel for how stable they are on my ears. I do not expect them to cancel any noise, so I don't make a point of testing them on airplanes or loud commutes—they aren't really made for that. My main audio source is Spotify Lossless, but, when possible, I test other formats and codecs as well.
