A ROYAL florist will headline a special event in the Yorkshire Dales for a national movement in sustainable church flowers.

Shane Connolly, who created the floral displays for the coronation of King Charles III and Queen Camilla, will appear at Bolton Priory on Friday, September 25, to share his expertise in eco-friendly floral design using British-grown flowers and no floral foam.

His visit coincides with the start of the preparations for Bolton Priory’s Harvest Festival All Ages service on October 4.

Rev Nicholas Merchant, rector at Bolton Priory, said: "Stewardship of God’s creation is one of the two marks of mission at Bolton Priory and we are delighted to host and support the Sustainable Flower Initiative."

The Sustainable Church Flowers movement began as a grassroots effort in Harpley, Norfolk, and has since become a growing national network promoting environmentally conscious church floristry.

Alongside the Sustainable Church Flowers movement is the growing number of local British flower growers, many of whom are part of the Flowers From the Farm collective who believe in celebrating flowers that reflect the seasons, the land they were grown on, the care with which they were cultivated, and the communities they support.

Liz Clayton, a member of the priory’s 20-strong volunteer flower arranging team, said: "Our next job is to persuade wedding planners that it’s much better to choose flowers and colours that reflect the seasons."

The team has been looking at ways to work more sustainably including compiling a list of local flower growers to supply seasonal flowers.

The upcoming conference will feature presentations from industry experts, including Zanna Hoskins of Spindle, a British grower and agro florist who runs courses with a focus on growing foliage for flower arranging, and Dr Jill Timms, who will explain the science behind sustainable flowers.

The conference is supported by the Bolton Abbey Estate, which is pleased to welcome everyone and warmly invite attendees to stay and enjoy the estate afterwards.

The Priory Church is open daily from dawn until dusk for visitors who would like to see examples of Shane Connolly’s work.

The Harvest Festival All Ages service will take place at 10.30am on Sunday, October 4.

A limited number of free tickets remain for the conference and can be booked via Eventbrite by searching for SCF Northern Conference Tickets.
