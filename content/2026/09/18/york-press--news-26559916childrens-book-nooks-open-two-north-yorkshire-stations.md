A new reading space aimed at inspiring a love of books among young rail passengers has been unveiled in North Yorkshire.

The children’s Book Nook was officially opened at TransPennine Express’ (TPE) Thirsk station by author Amelia Tait, who grew up in the area.

It is one of two reading areas donated by publisher Hachette UK to TPE as part of its Raising Readers initiative.

  Author Amelia Tait with Mike Drewery and Thirsk station team 

*(Image: TransPennine)*

A second Book Nook is located at Malton station.

Mike Drewery, area customer experience manager for TPE, said: "We want our stations to be welcoming places for everyone, and the new Book Nooks are a great way to help keep our younger customers entertained while encouraging a love of reading.

"We’re delighted Amelia could join us at Thirsk to officially open the new space."

Each nook features comfortable seating and a selection of children’s books for young passengers to enjoy while waiting for their train.

  Author Amelia Tait at Thirsk station 

*(Image: TransPennine)*

The books are for use at the station only and are not part of a book swap or to be taken away.

Amelia Tait visited Thirsk to celebrate the launch and mark the success of her debut children’s novel, Lily Tripp: Diary of an Accidental Time Traveller, which was recently selected as a national Waterstones Children’s Book of the Month.

Ms Tait said: "Thanks so much to TransPennine Express for inviting me to Thirsk station to open their brand-new Book Nook.

"Having grown up in North Yorkshire, I’m especially thrilled to see their partnership with The Reading Agency and Hachette’s Raising Readers initiative, helping make books more accessible to local families in community spaces."

Hachette UK was introduced to TPE through its partnership with The Reading Agency for the National Year of Reading.

Following TPE’s support for the Summer Reading Challenge, the Book Nooks are another way the train operator is helping to encourage children to discover and enjoy reading.

The station team has already received positive feedback from parents.

Many have said the books helped keep their children entertained while waiting for their train.
