There was a “clear financial incentive” for a private company run by a breast surgeon who performed mastectomies to see more patients, according to an NHS-commissioned review.

Cathedral Surgical Services Limited (CSS Ltd) had an arrangement with County Durham and Darlington Foundation Trust (CDDFT) based on “individual payments per patient”.

Concerns were also raised about how the “dual role” of its directors may be “influenced by personal interests rather than solely by patient or trust priorities”.

Durham Surgical Services Ltd LLP (DSS LLP) was set up to help manage demand for breast surgery services at the trust.

The weekly Saturday operating list was managed through CSS Ltd.

The 2025 report, carried out by governance specialist Mary Aubrey, said the “dual role” of the company’s directors “raises important governance and conflict of interest considerations”, with decisions potentially “influenced by personal interests rather than solely by patient or trust priorities”.

The review highlighted that NHS theatre lists include two to three breast surgery cases per session, though these “are often complex procedures that require more time”.

“Notably, the same consultant was able to perform between ten and 12 procedures, typically five in the morning list and five in the afternoon, during a full-day Saturday theatre session as a director under the CSS Ltd arrangement,” it said.

The contract between CSS and CDDFT allowed ten cases per list for a Saturday, but the figure was “often higher” with between ten and 12 procedures.

These were “generally minor” such as “lumps, bumps and hernias”, the report said.

It also highlighted that the arrangement between CSS and CDDFT “was based on individual payments per patient, creating a clear financial incentive”.

The report also raised concerns about the amount of time patients had at diagnostic appointments at the clinic.

She said the consultant in charge was reportedly able to see a “significantly higher number of patients per session”, the review found, ranging from 18 to 20 in a four-hour Friday evening clinic and 30 to 42 patients on a Sunday.

The sessions were designed as one-stop diagnostic breast clinics, with patients given 10-minute appointments rather than the typical 15 to 20 minutes.

The report said: “For example, Saturday clinics at University Hospital of North Durham were allocated 42 slots, each scheduled for 10-minute appointments from 0900 to 1640, often overrunning until 1830.

“One-stop diagnostic breast clinics typically require 15 to 20 minutes for new patient consultations, plus additional time for imaging, biopsy, or follow-up care.

“The scheduling model described raises governance concerns and appears inconsistent with the principles of safe, sustainable clinical practice as outlined in emerging guidance, which emphasises adequate time for diagnostic accuracy, patient safety and appropriate clinical decision-making.”

Amir Bhatti was the trust’s clinical lead for breast services from 2013 to 2024.

His clinical practice is currently restricted while an internal investigation is carried out, with CDDT also co-operating with Durham Police, who are looking into what happened at the trust.

Last year, the BBC reported that nearly £6 million had been paid by the trust over six years to breast cancer diagnostic clinics and surgery companies run privately by Mr Bhatti.
