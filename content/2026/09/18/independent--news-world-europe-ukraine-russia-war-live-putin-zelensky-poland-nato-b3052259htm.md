Polish prime minister Donald Tusk says Russia is planning hybrid strikes with drones or missiles targeting Nato countries supporting Ukraine, potentially including Poland.

The warning came as “a massive attack on the western part of Ukraine – very close to the Polish border,” took place, with Poland repeatedly forced to scramble its aircraft.

While Poland’s airspace was not breached, Tusk warned that Russia was planning to strike Nato territory and then pretend the attack was "accidental".

This, he told the parliament, would be in a bid to "paralyse or at least weaken the motivation of Nato countries" to rush to the defence of the member state coming under attack.

Earlier on Thursday, Russian forces carried a barrage of attacks on Ukrainian cities, including a strike on an industrial park west of Kyiv that killed one person, a local official said.

And in Russia, a Ukrainian drone attack damaged an oil refinery in the city of Yaroslavl, Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed.

## 'Results are easy to rig,' Russian voters admit

Some voters in Moscow have cast doubt about the outcome of Russia’s three-day State Duma election that kicked off this morning.

One man, who declined to give his name, said he believed the results were easy to rig and raised doubts about an electronic voting system being used in some parts of the country.

The electoral commission has rejected such suggestions as false.

Meanwhile, Anatoliy Simonenko, 76, said he was positive about what might happen after the election amid speculation that a new wave of mobilisation may be announced.

"There are some very grim predictions, and others that are more optimistic,” he said.

“I expect the outcome will likely fall somewhere in the middle. War is war. It entails expenses, defence spending, and cuts to various social benefits. Well, it’s necessary; there’s no getting around it.”

The State Duma election, the first since Russia launched its full-scale war in Ukraine, is widely expected to see the ruling pro-Putin United Russia party retain its dominance.

The result is likely to be held up by the Kremlin as evidence of public support for Putin's policies.

## Kremlin officials 'secretly urging loved ones to flee' amid mobilisation fears

![Russian reservists depart for military bases during mobilisation of troops, in Sevastopol](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/01/15/12/40/TOPSHOT-CRIMEA-RUSSIA-UKRAINE-CONFLICT-MOBILISATION-g7gimh30.jpeg)

Kremlin officials have been secretly telling friends and relatives to flee Russia amid fears of a looming mass mobilisation, it has been reported.

Documents seen by Bloomberg show that officials have been looking to open foreign bank accounts and move assets abroad, while encouraging loved ones to leave.

It is believed that Vladimir Putin plans to draft an additional 300,000 men into the army after the parliamentary elections, amid mounting losses on the frontline.

While the Russian president has previously denied such plans, Russians are taking no chances and leaving for neighbouring countries like Georgia.

Volodymyr Zelensky warned last month: “We believe it will not take place in the central cities. But I don't think they will be able to hide it very well.”

Ukrainian intelligence said Russia plans to call up 300,000 men by the end of 2026 and another 300,000 in 2027.

## France to convene meeting a meeting on 'rapid deterioration' of international order

![Emmanuel Macron welcomes candidates for the 2027 presidential election and party leaders at the presidential Elysee Palace in Paris on September 18, 2026](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/18/11/2026-09-18T095955Z_1815131365_RC2LLNAT33BE_RTRMADP_3_FRANCE-BUDGET-FUEL.JPG)

Emmanuel Macron will hold an emergency meeting on the consequences of the wars in the Middle East and Ukraine on France.

Party leaders and heads of intelligence agencies have been summoned to discuss the “rapid deterioration in the international situation.”

The meeting, to be held at 10.30am today, will focus on the country’s security and the rising energy prices.

"Given the rapid deterioration of the international situation in recent days , the meeting, scheduled for 10.30am [on Friday], will focus on the conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine and their consequences for France in terms of security and energy," the President's office announced yesterday.

Eager to demonstrate his proactive approach, Macron held talks with Iraq’s prime minister and the Crown prince of Saudi Arabia and the Emir of Qatar on restoring the freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz.

“When oil is under pressure, pump prices climb,” he said in a statement on X. “I know what that means for the French.

“What happens thousands of miles from our shores always ends up weighing on our daily lives. So I'm taking action to reduce tensions, secure our supplies, and ease the pressure on prices.”

## PM Meloni says Europe 'should not respond to provocations' by Russia

Italian prime minister Giorgia Meloni has advised Europe to refrain from responding to provocations from Moscow in comments on Thursday amid rising fears of a Russian strike on a Nato country following a spate of hybrid attacks.

“I believe these provocations from Moscow are multiplying because Putin needs to shift attention by creating an escalation with Europe,” she said.

“This comes from a situation on the battlefield in Ukraine that is not going as Putin had predicted, declared and promised to his people.

“I think we should not respond to these provocations. Responding to these provocations would be more in the interest of Moscow than of Europe.”

## Family of five injured in Russian drone strike on Kyiv

A family of five, including three children, has been injured in a Russian drone attack on the Kyiv region in the early hours of this morning.

Kyiv Regional Military Administration head Tymur Tkachenko said: “The children sustained acoustic trauma and acute stress reactions; all three have been hospitalised.

“A woman suffered an acute stress reaction, while a man sustained thermal burns, shrapnel wounds, and a laceration to his foot. He was also hospitalised.”

One house was destroyed and a second damaged in the strike, Tkachenko added.
