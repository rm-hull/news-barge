A former gift shop is set to become a deli and sandwich business in one of Halifax’s historic buildings dating to the town centre’s Victorian shopping past.

Calderdale Council planners have agreed Mr Zubair Mubarik can make the change at the Market Street premises housed in Palatine Chambers.

The chambers, which was built in 1894, was designed by W. H. D. Horsfall, originally as an office building with five shops, and it is Grade II-listed.

This means any change required listed building consent, which the council has now given.

It is also in Halifax Town Centre Conservation Area.

Palatine Chambers’ significance derives from its architectural and historic interest as a late Victorian commercial building, reflecting Halifax’s prosperity during the late 19th century.

Its design and detailing contribute strongly to the town’s historic streetscape, said agents for the application, Lacuna Designs.

It is close to other historic buildings including the Grade II*-listed Halifax Town Hall, Grade II-listed Halifax Borough Market and Grade I-listed Piece Hall.

Lacuna say the conversion to a take-away deli represents an appropriate reuse of the property which will contribute to the vitality of Market Street – one of Halifax’s main shopping thoroughfares.

“The scheme has been carefully designed to respect the building’s character while meeting operational requirements,” they said in supporting statements with the application.

The ground floor will be reconfigured to create food preparation and serving areas, and new signs for the deli will use the existing fascia.

“The design respects the building’s traditional character through sympathetic shop front design,” said the agents.

Changes will maintain the existing building line and proportions, materials and finishes appropriate to the conservation area will be used.

“The works are internal, reversible, and sympathetic to the building’s character,” said the agents

It’s outside appearance will be unchanged, they added.

The application also sought permission, now given to install an extract fan inside for ventilation – an additional unit only to a window already including other fans which will not alter the window further in any way, said the agents.
