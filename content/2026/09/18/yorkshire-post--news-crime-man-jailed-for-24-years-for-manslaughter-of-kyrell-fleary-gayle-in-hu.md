# Man jailed for 24 years for manslaughter of Kyrell Fleary-Gayle in Huddersfield

## A criminal who fatally stabbed a man in Huddersfield has been jailed for more than 20 years.

Luke Belbin, 25, of Mitre Street, Huddersfield was today sentenced at Leeds Crown Court to 24 years in prison for the manslaughter of Kyrell Fleary-Gayle in January 2026.

The 25-year-old had been found guilty of manslaughter and possession of an offensive weapon by a jury at Leeds Crown Court on August 6 following a two-week trial.

During the trial jurors heard Belbin had fatally stabbed 20-year-old Kyrell after they came into contact with each other outside an address on Greenwood Street, Huddersfield, on January 20 this year.

![Luke Belbin](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI5YzI5OTc3LWIwNzYtNGNkOS04NTQ1LWE1OTI3M2ExM2JlYzozYjBiNmJlNC03YTEwLTQ0ZGQtYWU4ZC03ZDYyNTIzMWM1NTE=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Belbin had fled in the aftermath of the stabbing and was picked up on Bell Street by a man driving a Nissan X Trail car.

Belbin later handed himself into police four days afterwards.

The X Trail driver, Cameron Boylan, 20, of Fanny Moor Crescent, Huddersfield was later arrested and charged.

He was given a two year suspended sentence for assisting an offender at today’s court hearing.

![Kyrell Fleary-Gayle](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjM3ZjkxYzkyLTBhOWYtNGZhZS1hMTg0LWFlNjIxNzkxN2QxMjpjYmM5ZWM2ZC04ZTUyLTRkZDgtOWMwNC0wY2EyMTRjYzJmOTc=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



Detective Chief Inspector Stacey Atkinson of the Homicide and Major Enquiry Team, who led the investigation, said: “We welcome the sentencing of these men today following the dreadful events of 20 January this year which resulted in a young man losing his life and robbed a family of a son. “Luke Belbin had armed himself and, judging by the injuries he inflicted on Kyrell, was willing to use a lethal level of violence on others. We hope Kyrell’s family can take some comfort from seeing him jailed.”

“Kyrell’s manslaughter is another devastating example of the tragic consequences of knife crime in our communities. Significant work remains ongoing across West Yorkshire with the Violence Reduction Partnership to reduce knife crime, including continuing education programmes to deter young people from carrying knives. Specific targeted initiatives also continue at local levels in areas where particular issues have been identified. “We continue to urge anyone who has information about persons carrying illegal weapons on our streets to contact us, or make contact with the independent Crimestoppers charity anonymously on 0800 555 111.

“Reports to West Yorkshire Police can be made to your local neighbourhood policing team or online at [https://www.westyorkshire.police.uk/livechat](https://www.westyorkshire.police.uk/livechat) “
