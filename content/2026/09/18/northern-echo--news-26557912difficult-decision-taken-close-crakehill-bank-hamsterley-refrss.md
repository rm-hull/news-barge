A County Durham road that has been closed for nearly four years after a major landslip will stay shut permanently following a council decision.

Durham County Council's cabinet agreed on Wednesday (September 16) to apply to magistrates for an order to permanently close Crakehill Bank in Hamsterley after worsening ground conditions pushed estimated repair costs to more than £10 million.

The road has been closed since 2022 when part of the embankment gave way and initial estimates suggested repairs could cost around £1.7m.

But further investigations found the slope remained unstable and continued to move and council officers concluded there was no realistic way of reopening the road safely, despite exploring a range of options.

Speaking at a cabinet meeting in Durham on Wednesday, councillors said the decision had not been taken lightly.

Cllr Tim McGuinness, Durham County Council's cabinet member for rural communities, highways and transport, said: "This has been a difficult decision and one that has not been made lightly, but it is not possible to reopen Crakehill Bank, guarantee public safety, or ensure the road will not slip further again."

   

*(Image: DURHAM COUNTY COUNCIL)*

Cllr Joe Quinn said the authority had inherited a longstanding problem and could not continue delaying a decision.

He said: "This is not a decision that has been taken lightly, nor an easy decision. However, we have inherited a problem that has already been kicked down the road and we won't as an administration kick the can down the road any longer.

"Officers have reviewed all options available to keep this section of road open. However, due to the continued subsidence, this has not been possible."

A report presented to cabinet said the embankment had continued to deteriorate, with the nearby river and complex groundwater conditions making any repair scheme highly challenging.

The report warned that even if the council invested millions of pounds in repair works, there would be no guarantee the road would remain stable in the long term because of the continuing risk of further movement.

Cabinet members approved plans to seek a magistrates' order stopping up the highway, paving the way for the road to be permanently closed.
