PLANS to build homes on a Green Belt area of Wilsden have attracted 22 objections.

The application for land next to 3 Shay Lane is for “permission in principle” – with the developer seeking confirmation from Bradford Council that the site is suitable for housing.

The nature of the application means that there are no firm details of what the development would look like – but it seeks permission for up to seven homes on the site.

It was submitted by Fiza Din earlier this month, and claims changing planning policies mean the site is suitable for development – despite sitting within the Green Belt.

The application says that in past discussions about the possibility of housing on the site, planners were “not expressly supportive.”

But it adds: “Changes to national planning policy since this advice was issued, the (previous discussions) are largely disregarded in the consideration of this application.”

It suggests new Government planning policies that introduced the concept of “grey belt” should weigh in favour of the scheme.

That policy allows developers to build on Green Belt sites that “do not strongly contribute” to the purposes of the Green Belt.

Referring to this site, the application says: “The area of Green Belt around Bradford comprises of around 23,880 ha of land.

“The site measures 0.2 ha in size and would therefore represent 0.0008 per cent of all Green Belt land within the District, the loss of which would be immaterial to the remaining Green Belt.

“It can therefore be concluded that the site comprises grey belt land and its change of use for the purposes of residential development will not fundamentally undermine the purposes of the remaining Green Belt.”

It adds: “There is an evidenced unmet need for housing within the District.

“The benefits of the proposed development in addressing an unmet need for housing are considered to outweigh any adverse effects of the proposed development.”

22 people have objected to the plans since they were submitted earlier this month.

A decision on the application is expected next month.

Since the introduction of grey belt policy a number of housing developments in the District have been approved for Green Belt land.

Last month an application to build 100 homes on Green Belt land off Harden Lane in Wilsden was approved by Bradford Council’s Regulatory and Appeals Committee.

Members were told that due to the national policy changes, the committee had little choice but to approve the development.
