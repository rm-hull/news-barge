Reform UK would fall behind both Labour and the Conservatives if a general election were held today, new polling suggests, in a major blow to Nigel Farage.

In yet more bad news for Reform UK, YouGov’s latest large-scale seat poll predicted the party would secure just 123 seats as they slide down from top spot to third place.

It marks a stark difference from last year’s polling, which suggested Reform UK would form the largest party in parliament, followed by Labour and the Conservatives.

In good news for Andy Burnham, Labour would secure 241 seats if a general election was held today. The Tories would remain in second place with 130 seats.

But the MRP poll suggests the UK is heading for a hung parliament, with Labour likely having to secure support from the Liberal Democrats and the Green Party in order to govern after losing a predicted 162 seats.

![YouGov’s latest large-scale seat poll predicted the party would secure just 123 seats as they slide down from top spot to third place](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/22/11/01M31V9KWHMXYBNBA36J4PBQSE.jpg)

The 2025 data predicted Reform would secure 311 seats, beating Labour on 144 seats and the Tories with just 45 seats.

In more bad news for Reform UK, the poll predicts Tory defectors Robert Jenrick and Suella Braverman would lose their seats to their old party.

Patrick English, head of elections at YouGov, told The Times there “just simply isn’t” a way for Reform and the Tories to form a coalition to build a majority.

“YouGov’s latest projections put Labour clearly ahead of both Reform and the Conservatives in terms of seats, while being only a fraction ahead of each on vote share,” he said.

“While Labour alone remain well short of a majority, there are a wide range of possible ‘coalition of the left’ options open to them to build a majority in a way where there just simply isn’t for Reform and the Conservatives on the right.

“While the British political left is more fragmented than the right in terms of the number of different parties, their collective vote share is bigger and translates efficiently into seat wins.”

It comes after Reform UK recorded its worst result in YouGov polling for two years, following months of turbulence for the party.

![Prime Minister Andy Burnham (Toby Melville/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/23/22/64603d4c4d03d9c80e04c6e109dfaafeY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzkwMjYzMzk4-2.85939187.jpg)

The new poll released earlier this week put their support down by two points at 21 per cent – neck-and-neck with the Tories in second place. Both parties are two points behind Labour in first at 23 per cent.

In third place is the Green Party, up three points with 14 per cent support. The Liberal Democrats, meanwhile, are on 12 points, down by one.

The latest polling has returned the lowest share YouGov has recorded for Reform UK since the pollster restarted its voting intention tracker in January 2025. It comes just days after Mr Farage‘s individual popularity reached a record low.

Mr Farage now has a net favourability rating of -45, identical to Sir Keir Starmer’s score just before his resignation as prime minister. This is down from a rating of -31 this time last year, with Britons now nearly three times as likely to see him negatively than positively
