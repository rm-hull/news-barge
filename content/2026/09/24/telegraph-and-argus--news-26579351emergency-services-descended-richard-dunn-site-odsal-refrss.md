A major emergency services presence at the Richard Dunn Sports Centre caught attention across south Bradford today.

Seven fire engines were spotted in the former sports centre's car park, with additional quick response vehicles surrounding them.

The vehicles could be seen outside the long-disused facility in Odsal, which has been closed to the public since November 2019.

### Why was West Yorkshire Fire Service at Richard Dunns?

A spokesperson for West Yorkshire Fire Service said: "We have several crews there today for a pre-planned training exercise."

The space was previously used for immersive training back in February of this year, and a recent report to the West Yorkshire Fire Authority described it as "an excellent training venue for firefighters."

![]()Fire presence at Richard Dunns *(Image: Newsquest)*

The site has hosted a variety of realistic rescue exercises, including line rescue scenarios with the Technical Rescue Team from Spen Valley, breathing apparatus drills, and mass casualty exercises with St John Ambulance.

Students from Bradford College acted as "live" casualties, helping provide "realism" for emergency crews.

The report noted that the exercises have helped crews develop and test their skills in realistic conditions.

The fire service has also conducted training at other sites in Bradford, including high-rise exercises at Margaret McMillan Towers inspired by lessons learned from the Grenfell Tower fire.

Crews were described as "more engaged" and "more confident" as a result of the scenario-based approach.
