Solution to pollution and traffic gridlock

In response to Terry Greene’s letter (Gazette & Herald Sept 16th).

To my knowledge I have never heard that the reasoning behind changing the priority at the Church Street/County Bridge junction in Norton was because motorists were getting stuck on the railway crossing. If they were it would be their fault for not following the basic rules of the road and entering a ‘yellow box’ when their exit was not clear of traffic.

North Yorkshire Council gave no justification for changing the road priorities and simply changed them overnight in 2016 with no consultation or information distributed to anyone. Even the town council was left in the dark and the first I heard of it as Mayor of Norton on Derwent was when my phone started ringing off the hook the following day with complaints from confused motorists. In the following days there were numerous ‘near misses’ at the junction, and although local residents have got used to the junction it still confuses visitors to the town.

The traffic lights Mr Greene mentions were indeed proposed by the Malton & Norton Infrastructure and Connectivity Plan in 2018, along with a number of other suggestions to improve traffic flow and reduce air pollution. Of these proposals the only ones delivered in eight years so far have been the introduction of intelligent traffic lights at Butcher Corner, the long awaited and much trialled one way system on Norton Road and the recent pedestrian crossing – still incomplete – on Railway Street.

At Norton town councils September meeting a representative from North Yorkshire Council could offer no commitment to local residents as to when the promised traffic lights on the County Bridge, Welham Road, Church Street and Welham Road junction might be trialled, let alone installed, so the solution Mr Greene believes in may well take a further eight or more years to materialise – if it does at all.

Whilst the air pollution zone in Castlegate has now been removed due to the improvement in air quality, the pollution levels on St Nicholas Street are appalling – residents complain of being able to taste the pollution in the air – and this on a road that is daily used by children and young people attending both primary and secondary schools in Norton.

Add to this the daily Russian roulette played by motorists both turning right to exit St Nicholas Street towards the railway crossing or those exiting Lidl to cross to the road towards the same street, and worst of all, those travelling down Welham Road who aren’t willing to wait while the railway crossing is closed and who drive on the wrong side of the road to turn right into the same street and encountering other motorists turning left off Church Street head on.

If Mr Greene is concerned about pedestrian safety – this is the area where pedestrians are regularly put in danger crossing the road and at least one cyclist has been seriously injured.

Residents who live on St Nicholas Street have come up with an alternative – a much simpler and cheaper solution - to the daily chaos and gridlock everyone faces on the street, which we believe is easier to deliver than the traffic lights Mr Greene hopes for. A one way system from Welham Road to Langton Road, a 20 mph zone and changing the Church Street junction back to its original priorities would at a stroke put an end to back to back congestion on a residential street and make the whole area a cleaner and safer place to live again.

Di Keal

Norton
