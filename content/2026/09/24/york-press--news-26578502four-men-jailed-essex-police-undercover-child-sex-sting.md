## North Yorkshire restauranteer caught in police undercover child sex sting

Nurettin Onay, who owns restaurants in Selby and Chelmsford, said he tried to book an adult, was offered a child and only showed up to investigate *(Image: Essex Police)*

A North Yorkshire restaurant owner is among four men jailed after undercover Essex Police officers “entrapped” them into booking sex with fictitious children.

Nurettin Onay, 63, who owns a kebab shop in Selby and a Turkish restaurant in Chelmsford, a policeman, a second restauranteur and a care worker were each sentenced to more than three years in prison after making bookings at fake brothels in Southend and Chelmsford.

Each was trying to book an appointment with an adult escort but were told by an officer posing as a madam that only underage girls were available.

Anyone who made an appointment and showed up at the bogus brothels was swooped on and arrested, Basildon Crown Court heard.

“It appears that in each case their need for sexual gratification overcame any initial reticence, common sense or common decency,” said Judge Shane Collery KC.

“It was plainly and obviously the wrong thing to do, both morally and legally… All they had to do was to put down the phone in each case, and they did not do that.”

Onay was jailed for 43 months and will be on the sex offenders register for life..

All four including Onay denied attempting to arrange sex with a child but were convicted by a jury earlier this year.

![]()Avanos Kebab and Pizza Shop in Selby *(Image: Google Street View)*

The child sex sting, Operation Excalibur, saw officers post an advert on an adult services website called Viva Street, featuring photographs of two women in lingerie.

The models were in their 20s and 30s but when men called to make bookings, they were told the women they were looking at were actually only 14.

All four men sentenced this week proceeded with their appointments and were arrested in June 2022.

Onay, owner of Avanos Kebab shop on Micklegate, Selby, according to Companies House, claimed to the jury he only went to the fictitious brothel to investigate, not to fulfil the arrangement.

He too had queried how it was possible that the brothel could be offering two 14-year-old girls.

“They know what they are doing so you won’t be disappointed,” the undercover officer told him.

“The crime was induced by police,” said his defence barrister Joshua Kern.

He told Judge Collery where stings “entailed police inducement” or “there was encouragement by undercover officers”, case law said the sentence should be reduced.

![]()Clockwise from top left: Gavin Hawkins, Tadislavs Buinickis, Nurettin Onay and Fakhir Mahmood were ensnared by a fake child brothel concocted by Essex Police *(Image: Essex Police)*

Leigh on Sea chef and restauranteur Gavin Hawkins, was arrested when he arrived at the fake brothel with £200 on his person.

He had been told both girls were “young, slim and fresh”.

“It’s a bit strange isn’t it, their age?” he asked, but was told lots of clients liked younger girls.

“I’m a f\*\*\* ing idiot!” exclaimed the now 50-year-old, of Highbank Close, as the officers pounced.

He maintained at trial and after his conviction that he had no intention of following through and only showed up as arranged to investigate what was going on.

Former Latvian police officer Tadislavs Buinickis, moved to the UK 20 years ago.

“Mr Buinickis irregularly used the legitimate website Viva Street in respect of the legal services of women in their 30s and 40s,” said his defence barrister Chris Martin.

“Girls under the age of 18 had never featured. It’s not unrealistic to suggest never would they have, had the operation not taken place.”

All four men’s phones and computers were seized and analysed by Essex Police, yielding no evidence of any prior sexual interest in children.

Buinickis,46, formerly of Southchurch Avenue, had not renewed his lease in June and had been living ever since in his car, although continuing to work full-time.

When told in 2022 that the girls were 14, he queried whether the madam meant 40.

When she told him she really meant 14, he replied: “But this is so young!”

“Yes, they are young, but I’ve got plenty of clients who enjoy that,” the officer replied, promising he would “not be disappointed”.

He told police he did not believe the girls in the photos were actually 14, so proceeded with the booking.

Former care driver Fakhir Mahmood, 49, of St Annes Road, Southend, was also sentenced.

“He accessed a legal advert for legal services online,” said defence barrister Molly Dyas.

“The advert said the girls were 18. They were in fact adults that were in the photographs.”

Once told the girls were 14, said Mrs Dyas, Mahmood repeatedly queried the legality of the arrangement in both text messages and phone calls.

“He is somebody who in fact seems to be keen not to break the law,” she said.

In his last phone conversation with the undercover officer, moments before he was arrested, Mahmood seemed to believe he was talking to the escort and was asking to speak to the madam.

He had said he wanted to see the girls in person and assess the truth of the situation, offering to pay upfront and let the madam keep the money even if he decided to walk away.

Prosecutor Stephen Rose KC said the lawfulness of the men’s original intent did not alter the fact that “the commission of these offences in each case has revealed a sexual interest in children.

“In a short period of time, each defendant is reacting – unexpectedly, they would say – to an offer they weren’t looking for, but nonetheless prepared to do so.”

Judge Collery said there would be reductions in sentence for the fact that no real child was ever in danger and for the more than four years which had elapsed between arrest and sentence.

“The court also does not ignore the fact of the issue of entrapment,” he said. “There is an issue in that regard, even if it does not amount to a defence.”

Hawkins was jailed for 40 months, Mahmood and Buinickis for 45 months each.

All four will be on the sex offenders register for life and a hearing will take place in October to decide whether they will also be made subject to Sexual Harm Prevention Orders.
