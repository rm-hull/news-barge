There has been a “significant development” over a West Yorkshire site subject to planning controversy taking in a mystery structure and “deep burial” of material some believe to be Qurans.

It relates to Forestry Commission intervention over removal of some trees at the site at Wood Nook Lane, Sowerby Bridge.

Mystery has shrouded activity and erection of a monument and pillars at the former allotments site.

A Planning Inspectorate appointed inspector will consider an appeal by appellants calling themselves Private Community Gardeners Group – Calderdale Council and Planning Inspectorate documentation says the appellants are Rest Gardens Ltd – against council enforcement action over activity at the site at Wood Nook Lane.

July saw councillors receive a petition with more than 1,800 signatures about the site, leading to cross-party action from Sowerby Bridge ward councillors, with Coun Adam Wilkinson (Lab) and Coun Mike Payne (Reform UK), who referred it to the council’s planning enforcement team and the Environment Agency respectively.

![]() *(Image: SWNS)*

The petition said people had seen substantial and rapid changes to the site over months leading up to the council’s decision to enforce.

It has been reported that residents, after initial concern about burial, now believe the structure is the centre piece of a burial site for old Qurans, the text sacred to Muslims, called Rest Gardens.

They have also reported TikTok videos posted on the landowner’s account, now deleted, appearing to show men using diggers to bury “thousands of Qurans.”

In Islam, burial is a preferred method to dispose of worn out Qurans, which still remain sacred.

This week, Coun Wilkinson reported that the Forestry Commission has called for remedial measures to be taken over tree felling.

In a social media post he said: “There has been a significant development regarding the tree felling at the former Britannia allotment site.

“The Forestry Commission has now served the occupier of the site with a Restocking Notice.”

Coun Wilkinson explains that following an inspection and review of the information available, the Forestry Commission says it appears that an offence was committed when growing trees were felled in December last year without the required felling licence.

The Restocking Notice requires the site to be replanted with trees by June 30, 2027, with the new trees then maintained for 10 years, he said.

“The occupier does have a right of appeal against the notice, and the Forestry Commission has made clear that the notice is being issued because agreement could not be reached.

“This is an important step in ensuring that the loss of trees at the site is properly addressed,” said Coun Wilkinson.

In July, the petitioners said: “While the site has been described as an allotment, the extensive destruction of mature vegetation, major excavation, and the importation and deep burial of materials from outside the local area point to a significant material change of use…for which proper planning permission has not been sought.”

People can comment about the issue to the Planning Inspectorate until October 5 – comments can be made by logging on to [https://appeal-planning-decision.service.gov.uk/comment-planning-appeal/appeals](https://appeal-planning-decision.service.gov.uk/comment-planning-appeal/appeals) and entering the reference number 6014342.

The enforcement notice relates to the raised plinth, marble slab structure and around ten columns built without planning permission.

The appellants have argued their case in documents submitted in support of their appeal – the documents can be viewed on Calderdale Council’s Planning Portal under the “Appeals” tab, under number 26/00051/ENF.

They argue there has been no breach of planning control arguing the flag poles are flying the national flag, the Union Jack, and do not constitute development while the marble slab and plinth are “communal garden furniture” – seating, work surfaces and gathering areas with no roof, enclosed space or walls.

A photograph proves, they argue “the primary land use remains allotment gardening…there has been no material change of use.”
