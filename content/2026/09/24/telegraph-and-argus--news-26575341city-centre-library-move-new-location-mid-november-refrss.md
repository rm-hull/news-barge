BRADFORD city centre’s library will finally move to its “wonderful” new home in November – councillors have been told.

In late 2024, Bradford Council announced that it would be moving City Library from its current location in City Park to the ground floor of Britannia House.

The move into the Council owned building would mean the Council would no longer have to pay the £420,000 a year rent it current pays for the current building.

When the announcement was first made by the Council’s then Labour Executive, the aim was for the move to take place in early 2026.

Although work on the ground floor of the Grade II listed building began earlier this year, no official date for the move had been announced.

![]() *(Image: Merienda Coffee)*

At a meeting of Bradford Council’s Regeneration and Environment Scrutiny Committee on Tuesday, members were given an update on the district’s library service.

During the presentation, Chair of the committee Councillor Susan Fricker (Lab, Bingley East) asked what the status of City Library’s move was.

Members were told that the move to Britannia House was due to take place in mid-November.

Cllr Fricker said: “It looks like a lot of investment is going into this.”

Dionne Hood, head of the library service, said: “We don’t know the exact budget for it, but it does look wonderful.

“It will have more flexibility, more meeting space, more event space. Much more space than we have at the moment.”

The move is expected to save the council around £1m.

The large ground floor space in the listed building had, until recently, been used by the Council’s customer services.

That service has since moved to City Hall.

The move will put the library at the heart of the huge refurbishment of the city centre that has taken place in recent years.

Streets around the Victorian building have been pedestrianised as part of an award-winning scheme to make the centre more attractive to visitors.

At the time of the announcement, the Council said: “The library will be much more visible through an increased pedestrian level presence with large windows giving great views over City Hall and St George’s Hall, Norfolk Gardens, and creating new pedestrian access via Broadway and Hall Ings.”

City Library moved to its current location in City Park from Margaret McMillan tower in 2013.

The high-rise building was condemned as a fire risk in 2011 and was dealt a further blow two years later when asbestos was found throughout the building.

When it moved into the City Park building it was on a 10-year deal to pay £420,000 in rent a year.
