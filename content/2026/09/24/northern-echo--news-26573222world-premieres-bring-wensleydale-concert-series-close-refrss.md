A Yorkshire Dales concert series has concluded with the world premieres of two new works, bringing together internationally-acclaimed composers and emerging musical talent for a "memorable finale".

The Wensleydale Concert Series ended at Aysgarth Church with world premieres by British composers David Blake and Cheryl Frances-Hoad, performed by young London musicians.

Cellist Inez Karlsson inspired Blake’s new composition and performed both pieces with pianist Ayaka Shigeno.

Moray Welsh, former principal cellist of the London Symphony Orchestra, said: "About two years ago, when Inez was barely 16, I went to a lesson that she was having with Melissa Phelps in London and I thought, 'my goodness this is something special.'

"So, because I had moved up to Yorkshire about six years ago and I had got to know Carol [Haynes] and Liz [Sowter] and the wonderful series that they put on, I thought how great it would be if they could give her one of her first important concerts."

Welsh also spoke about his long friendship with composer David Blake, whose 90th birthday fell close to the concert.

He said: "It's wonderful for me, now 60 years since I became a student of David Blake, that the relationship still continues.

"So when we realised that David's 90th birthday almost coincided with this concert I approached him and I said, 'Come on David - write another piece for the cello. You have done two, do another one.'

"Luckily he agreed and then, in addition to that, of course, it turned out that Carol already had a piece from Cheryl Frances-Hoad which had never been performed.

"So amazing that these two pieces have come together like this."

Blake’s new piece, Envoi, was composed especially for the concert.

He said: "Long ago, I discovered Ezra Pound’s Personae, a collection of his shorter poems, one of which is Envoi, denoting the poet’s farewell to his readers.

"This short piece, written earlier this year, is a series of rather enigmatic, improvisatory ideas, not entirely tuneless."

Alan George, a recently retired conductor, violist and founding member of the Fitzwilliam String Quartet, praised Mr Blake’s work.

He said: "David, as you have heard tonight, is a composer of great expressive means and the love that he puts into his own music is in a sense a reflection of all the music that he has learnt and played himself over many years."

Cheryl Frances-Hoad’s work, Three Supplications, was commissioned in 2019 by series organiser Carol Haynes and takes inspiration from J.S. Bach.

She said: "The work unfolds in three connected movements, each prayer-like in character.

"Each takes loose inspiration from short phrases in Bach’s music."

Ms Karlsson and Ms Shigeno also performed Boccherini’s Cello Sonata in A major, G 4, Frank Bridge’s Elégie, Debussy’s Sonata for cello and piano and Brahms’s Sonata in F major.

Mr Welsh said: "What I really want to say is how important it is for young players, like you, to be able to have this relationship with composers, just like I did with David, because we need them as much as they need us."

The Wensleydale Concert Series will return next year, beginning on May 15 with pianist Tom Hicks, followed by a concert on June 19 featuring tenor Thomas Guthrie and pianist Daniel Grimwood.

The full programme is available at www.wensleydaleconcertseries.co.uk.
