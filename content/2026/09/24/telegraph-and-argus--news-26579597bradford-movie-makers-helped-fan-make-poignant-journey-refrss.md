BRADFORD’S amateur film-making club went from obscurity to global fame when it was the subject of a multi award-winning documentary.

Now the historic club has helped one fan, from the Netherlands, to fulfil a poignant wish.

Ron Hoek, from Rotterdam, contacted the Bradford Movie Makers asking if members would help him make the journey to Top Withens to scatter his beloved wife’s ashes.

Ron came across A Bunch of Amateurs when it was broadcast on Dutch television. The film is about the Bradford Movie Makers - one of the world’s oldest amateur film clubs - and its fight to survive.

When Ron and his wife, Nanda, watched the documentary they fell for the quirky charms of the amateur film club, as many other fans around the world have done.

Ron emailed the Movie Makers’ website, revealing that he and Nanda had travelled by car through England for several trips over a period of about 10 years, from Land’s End to John O’Groats.

“My wife had a great love of British literature, so we visited various places associated with writers, including Daphne du Maurier in Cornwall, Dylan Thomas in Wales and Lewis Carroll in Whitby,” wrote Ron.

Nandy passed away in March 2025, after a short illness. Ron told the Movie Makers that “to give her a final resting place that she would have cherished” he planned to travel to England by ferry to Hull, then by train to Bradford.

“From there, I hope to travel to Ponden Kirk or Top Withens to scatter her ashes,” he wrote.

“Ron said he and his wife loved our film. He asked if we would help him get to Top Withens,” says Dave Marshall from the Movie Makers. “Of course, I said yes.”

When Ron arrived in Bradford, Dave took him to the Movie Makers club in Little Horton, where veteran member Harry Nicolls put on an impromptu magic show for him. “Naturally, we filmed it,” says Dave.

“Unfortunately, my health means that I am no longer able to manage a walk of the Top Withins distance. Fellow members Andrew Cockerill and Joe Ogden therefore volunteered to accompany Ron on the journey.

![]()Andrew Cockerill and Joe Ogden from the club.

“Ron himself is not in the best of health either and struggles to walk even 500 yards, so I lent him a crutch. With great determination and a large amount of fortitude the three of them set off from Back Lane in Stanbury, bound for Top Withens.

![]()Ron contacted the film-making club to ask if they would help him with a poignant request. Photos: Ron Hoek

“Four hours later, they returned. Ron had been able to fulfil his wife’s wish and give her the final resting place he had hoped for.

“Afterwards, we headed to the Jacobs Well pub in Bradford for pie and peas and a pint. The next day Ron returned to Rotterdam, with his mission accomplished. He has since very generously donated £100 to the club.”

The Movie Makers have kept in touch with Ron. Says Dave: “During his visit, we were delighted to make Ron an Honorary Member of Bradford Movie Makers - joining a very select few who have received that honour.”

Now the club plans to edit together the footage of Ron’s journey to Top Withens, along with Harry’s magic show, and send the finished film to Ron in Rotterdam.

“It was a privilege to help Ron fulfil such a special and deeply personal wish,” says Dave.

“This is exactly the sort of thing our club loves to do - not just making films, but making memories, helping people, and being there when it matters.”

* The Bradford Movie Makers, formed 94 years ago, is a group of film enthusiasts who make films, take part in competitions and restore archive footage.

The club’s films, from family trips to pre-war Bradford, document the district’s social history. Members are digitising the archive to preserve it, and are often handed home videos from people.

“We have 2,000-plus films on many formats,” Dave Marshall told the T&A. “We’ve copied 16mm films, 57 9.5mm films, 250 Mini DV tapes and dozens of VHS and SVHS tapes.”

Founded in 1932 as Bradford Cine Circle, membership grew in the 1960s with cheaper Standard 8, then Super 8 colour film. Analogue video cameras appeared in the 1980s then came camcorders. Embracing new technology, the group became Bradford Cine and Video Club, then Bradford Movie Makers in 2010.

Based in Little Horton, in premises that includes a little cinema, the club shot to fame in 2022 film A Bunch of Amateurs, a charming documentary which had five-star reviews and was nominated for a Bafta.

![]()A Bunch of Amateurs was an international hit.

The film was directed and produced by Kim Hopkins under Labor of Love Films, with co-founder Margareta Szabo and The BFI Doc Society Fund, and Glusburn-born Oscar-winner Simon Beaufoy as executive producer.

Billed as an affectionate tribute to a group who “cling to their dreams, fuelled by endless cups of tea”, it’s a gently comedic, profoundly moving portrait of shared creative enthusiasm and friendship.

Last week the T&A reported that Colin Egglestone, the club’s longest-serving member and much-loved by fans of A Bunch of Amateurs, recently died, aged 93.
