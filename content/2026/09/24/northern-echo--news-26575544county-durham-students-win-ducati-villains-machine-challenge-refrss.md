Students from County Durham have been crowned winners of a national challenge set by motorcycle manufacturer Ducati UK after creating their vision of the ultimate "villain's machine".

The competition formed part of the 2026 Build the Villain's Machine challenge, organised by the North East STEM Foundation (NESF) and Ducati UK to inspire young people through engineering, design and creativity.

Sedgefield Community College’s winning team was announced at NESF’s annual Black Tie Dinner at Dalton Old Pump House in Seaham, where a Ducati took centre stage.

Tania Cooper MBE, Chair of NESF, said: "The competition was designed to show young people that engineering is creative, exciting and full of possibilities, and Sedgefield Community College’s winning entry really captured that, combining engineering understanding with imagination and creativity."

![]()Tania Cooper MBE (back left) and Will Hiftle (front left), of Vertu Ducati Sunderland, join staff from Sedgefield Community College as the winning students receive their ‘Villain’s Machine’ trophy and certificates. *(Image: Supplied)*

The students will receive a Ducati and Silverstone Performance Experience, including a behind-the-scenes visit to Ducati UK’s Silverstone headquarters, Silverstone Circuit and its museum.

The challenge invited North East students aged 11 to 18 to choose a Ducati model and reimagine it as a “villain’s machine”.

Teams from Polam Hall School in Darlington and Whitworth Park Academy in Spennymoor finished as runners-up.

Will Hiftle, general sales manager at Vertu Ducati Sunderland, said: "Engineering, design and innovation are at the heart of Ducati, so we’re passionate about encouraging young people to explore these areas and discover where their own interests could take them.

"It has been fantastic to see the creativity and enthusiasm shown by all the students who took part."

Katie Lennox is head of design technology at Sedgefield Community College.

Ms Lennox said: "Our students really embraced the challenge and put a huge amount of thought and effort into their ideas.

"Experiences like this bring the subject to life and show young people the opportunities STEM subjects can open up."

The announcement was a highlight of NESF’s annual fundraising dinner, where Ms Cooper revealed that NESF programmes and activities have reached 10,488 young people over the past year.

The total includes students on the Future Makers programme, which encourages seven- to 18-year-olds to explore STEM subjects.

Participation in the programme has grown from 956 children in 2022 to 2,593 this year.
