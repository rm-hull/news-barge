A major East Cleveland regeneration project is beginning to take shape, with new shops emerging inside a landmark development set to transform part of the town centre.

The Lookout - being developed on the Redcar seafront - will bring together family entertainment, cultural spaces, food and drink venues, a modern library and interactive experiences under one roof.

The seafront attraction will offer family entertainment, cultural spaces, food and drink venues, a modern library and interactive experiences.

![]()Artist's impression showing the proposed look of The Lookout development in Redcar. *(Image: Redcar & Cleveland Borough Council)*

Fresh footage from Tees Valley Combined Authority shows commercial units taking shape inside the building as developers prepare to bring in new businesses.

The Lookout forms part of Redcar's wider Town Deal programme, which secured £25 million of Government funding to support major regeneration projects across the town centre.

The scheme follows extensive public consultation and will include flexible indoor and outdoor event spaces, alongside digital technology and learning areas.

Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council say the development is designed to bring people into Redcar throughout the year, boosting footfall and supporting surrounding shops, cafés and businesses.

Plans include a modern library, flexible family entertainment, food and drink, augmented-reality games and other digital experiences.

The council will also host a range of new events designed to increase foot traffic in Redcar throughout the year.

The scheme’s commercial spaces are now being marketed to potential operators, marking another project milestone.

Councillor Lynn Pallister, the council’s cabinet member for growth and enterprise, called the marketing of the commercial space "an exciting milestone".

Backed by Tees Valley Combined Authority funding, The Lookout aims to become a year-round seafront destination, strengthening Redcar’s visitor economy and supporting nearby businesses.

The transformation of one of Redcar’s best-known empty buildings is now clear, with retail and leisure space emerging inside the former department store.
