Plans have been lodged for Home Bargains to take over a former Post Office unit in a bid to add a café to its new Spennymoor store.

The discount chain re-opened its bigger store in Spennymoor last Saturday (September 19) having taken over the former Iceland unit.

The frozen food chain relocated to the former Original Factory Shop unit on Festival Walk in March, freeing up an extra almost 1,000 square metres of floor space for Home Bargains.

The wider building was also home to the town’s Post Office until recently, with the branch shutting on August 17 before moving to the former Lloyds Bank on Cheapside.

Now, new plans have revealed that Home Bargains want to knock into the old Post Office so it can introduce a customer café.

Home Bargains would operate the café, which would be accessed from within the store.

The discount retailer said it needs to carry out some work to the shopfront to facilitate the plan, which will ensure there is no empty floor space in the building.

The “modest” work would include a new front with security shutters, the existing ATM removed and bricked up, slit windows removed and filled, and a general refurbishment.

Existing paving and bollards would also be removed, with two customer parking spaces added, boosting the total number from 56 to 58 spaces.

The application is yet to be decided upon by Durham County Council.

Plans for Home Bargains to extend in Spennymoor were first announced last August.

This was despite Iceland, whose lease was due to expire last September, arguing the approval would effectively “force” it out of the town.

Iceland said at the time that it had no desire to close, leave the area, or relocate - and its closure would have a “detrimental impact” on the town.

However, the refurb went ahead and Iceland moved, meaning the internal wall could be knocked through and old Iceland entrance bricked up.
