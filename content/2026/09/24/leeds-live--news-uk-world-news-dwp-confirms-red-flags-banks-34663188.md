# DWP confirms the red flags banks are checking benefit claimant accounts

## The final Code of Practice sets out how the DWP will be able to require banks to check accounts

The Department for Work and Pensions has revealed the warning signs that banks will be monitoring in the accounts of benefits recipients.

Under the new Eligibility Verification Measure, the DWP will have the authority to request that banks and financial institutions scrutinise accounts linked to Universal Credit, Pension Credit and Employment and Support Allowance (ESA).

Banks will receive specific eligibility criteria and must flag accounts that meet these requirements before sharing particular details with the DWP.

Crucially, these regulations don't grant the DWP direct access to individuals' bank accounts, nor do they permit officials to track how claimants are using their benefits.

Financial institutions are expressly forbidden from disclosing transaction details when responding to an Eligibility Verification Notice (EVN), reports Birmingham Live.

The finalised Code of Practice overseeing these new powers has been released following public consultation, clearing the path for the DWP to implement the measure.

The system works by the DWP issuing an EVN to a bank or financial institution, outlining the eligibility markers against which accounts should be assessed.

Significantly, the DWP is prohibited from using an EVN to supply banks with personal information about individual claimants and request data about them.

Rather, financial institutions will rely on information already in their possession to pinpoint accounts that satisfy the conditions specified in the notice. Once an account has been flagged, specific details can be forwarded to the DWP to assist officials in establishing whether a benefit payment may have been made in error.

The DWP has stated that the measure is intended to pinpoint potentially incorrect payments, rather than to determine an individual's eligibility for benefits.

As such, a flagged account will not automatically indicate that someone's benefit award is incorrect, nor that their payments will be halted.

The Child Poverty Action Group (CPAG) has noted that once a claimant has been identified through this measure, the DWP will utilise its existing procedures to decide whether further action is necessary. This could ultimately result in a benefit decision being revised or payments being suspended.
