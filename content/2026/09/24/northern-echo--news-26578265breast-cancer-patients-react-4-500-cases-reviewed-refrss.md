Breast cancer patients have reacted with shock and anger at the scale of serious failings in care after hospital bosses revealed up to 4,500 women could have their treatment reviewed.

Campaigners said they were stunned by the scandal’s potential scale, while others demanded a public inquiry and warned women must not wait years for answers.

On Thursday (September 24) the County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust board agreed to continue the review, covering cases from January 2023 to February 2025.

![]()Nadeen Lister outside Prospect House in Durham *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

The review will then move back year by year from 2022 to 2015, with findings assessed before the board decides whether to examine the previous year.

After the meeting, Nadeen Lister, who runs the 150-strong Pink Justice Network support group of women who have had breast cancer treatment from the trust alongside Amelia Green, questioned the timetable for the review.

The board was told that about 4,500 women’s treatment would be reviewed if the exercise reached back to 2015.

Board papers suggested the review could take five years, meaning women treated in 2015 might not have their cases examined until 2031.

![]()Nadeen Lister outside Prospect House in Durham *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Nadine, who was twice told not to worry about a lump in her breast back in 2017 before learning it was cancerous, said: "Personally, I'm not happy about the decision today. It's good that the lookback has been extended, but people who were impacted during the timeframe they are proposing may not have their investigation resolved until 2031.

"When I found out that it could be 4,500 women, I did get quite emotional, and I did cry, because the scale of this scandal is never-ending."

Despite the trust’s decision to review cases year by year, Mrs Lister questioned why it would not go back to when former lead breast surgeon Amir Bhatti was first employed there in 2005..

"I would have extended it all the way back until he was employed; he is mentioned by so many patients and members of our group."

![]()Catriona Dunbar outside Prospect House in Durham *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Fellow patient Cartriona Dunbar had mixed feelings about Thursday’s meeting but was relieved the trust rejected a ‘limited approach’ to the review.

But she called for a deeper investigation into how the trust’s senior bosses were overseen at the time.

"It's a relief that the Trust didn't pick a limited approach to the lookback, but there's already knowledge about cases stretching back before 2008, so the scope of this lookback still doesn't go far enough," she said.

"While Amir Bhatti has been named multiple times, there are surely plenty more people at the Trust at the time that need to be investigated for signing off all of this."

Jackie Holmes also attended the meeting. Her daughter Amelia Green was diagnosed with breast cancer in 2019, ten years after her other daughter Michaela Holmes died aged 35 in 2009 after chemotherapy following a breast cancer diagnosis.

![]()Jackie Holmes with a photo of her daughters Michaela and Amelia outside Prospect House in Durham *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Visibly upset after the meeting, 71-year-old Mrs Holmes called for a public inquiry instead of a trust-led case review.

She said: “It would be better if the Government said they would hold a public inquiry.

“It would be better for the trust as well.

“It’s alright for the trust to say they’re going to do this and they’re going to do that, but they have admitted this morning that they don’t have enough staff to do what they’re doing.

![]()From left, Nedeen Lister and Jackie Holmes outside Prospect House *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Gill Hunt, the trust’s lead nurse, said it would recruit more specialists to review cases if possible.

The trust has offered patients psychological support.

Women who were not offered reconstructive surgery in the past will now be given the option.

Some patients chose not to have their cases reviewed because it could revive traumatic experiences.

Patients worried about their past treatment have been urged to call a hotline on 0191 333 2126 rather than wait to hear from the trust.

Chief Executive of the NHS Trust Steve Russell said: “We have heard again today about the profound and lasting impact that failings in our breast services have had on women and their families.

"We remain deeply sorry for the harm and distress caused, and for the times when, as a Trust, we should have listened more carefully, asked more questions and acted sooner."
