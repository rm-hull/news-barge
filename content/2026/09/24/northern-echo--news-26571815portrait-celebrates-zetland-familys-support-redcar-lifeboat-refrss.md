A new portrait celebrating a prominent family’s long-standing connection to one of the world’s most historic lifeboats has been unveiled in Redcar.

The Marquess of Zetland revealed the artwork during a visit to the Zetland Lifeboat Museum and Redcar Heritage Centre.

Created by photographer Philip Chisholm, also known as Pip of Redcar, the work is the third in a series honouring three generations of the Zetland family and their close ties to the lifeboat.

The Zetland, built in 1802 by Henry Greathead in South Shields, is the world’s oldest surviving lifeboat.

![]()Marquess of Zetland unveils artwork at the Redcar Heritage Centre *(Image: Philip Chisholm)*

She served the Redcar community for 78 years and saved more than 500 documented lives.

The lifeboat was later named Zetland in tribute to the family’s support in bringing her to Redcar.

In 1864, when the RNLI declared the boat unfit for service, the people of Redcar refused to let her be scrapped.

They preserved the vessel and, in 1907, housed her in the former Emma lifeboat house on the Esplanade, where she still resides today.

![]()New portrait celebrates Zetland family's lifeboat legacy in Redcar *(Image: Philip Chisholm)*

The museum has operated in its current form since 1980 and is operated by a dedicated team of volunteers.

Visitors can explore Redcar’s maritime history through lifeboat artefacts, historic photographs, and displays dedicated to former crews and coxswains.

A replica early 20th-century fisherman’s cottage is also part of the exhibition.

The museum is open Monday to Friday from 11am to 4pm, and on weekends from 11am to 5pm.

Admission is free, and donations are welcome.

It is also dog-friendly.

For more information, visitors can contact the museum on 01642 494311, email [zetlandmuseum@hotmail.com](mailto:zetlandmuseum@hotmail.com), or visit www.zetlandlifeboat.co.uk.
