A popular beer sold UK supermarkets, including Tesco, has been recalled because it may explode.

Cans of Lucky Saint Hazy IPA have been recalled as some cans may become over-pressurised and rupture unexpectedly.

The affected products are sold in four packs of 330ml cans and have a best before date of August 2027.

A spokesman for the Food Standards Agency (FSA) said: “Lucky Saint is recalling the above product.

>
> — Food Standards Agency (@foodgov) September 23, 2026

"Point of sale notices will be displayed in all retail stores that are selling this product. These notices explain to customers why the products are being recalled and tell them what to do if they have bought the product.

“If you have bought any of the above product do not drink it. Instead, follow the guidance set down in the Point of Sale Notice (POSN) below. Do not return the item to store.”

A Lucky Saint spokesman added: “We are recalling Lucky Saint 0.5% Hazy IPA 4 x 330ml because some cans may become over-pressurised. This can cause cans to swell, leak or rupture unexpectedly, presenting a risk of physical injury.

“Check if you have bought the affected best before date of the Lucky Saint 0.5% Hazy IPA 330ml.

“Share proof of purchase with the store for a full refund. Please safely dispose of the product. Do not return cans to this store.

“To safely dispose of the product; handle the cans as little as possible, place them carefully, upright, in a sealed bag and dispose of them with your household waste.

“We’re sorry for the inconvenience, and thank you for your understanding.”

## **What is a product recall?**

If there is a problem with a food product that means it should not be sold, then it might be 'withdrawn' (taken off the shelves) or 'recalled' (when customers are asked to return the product).

The FSA issues Product Withdrawal Information Notices and Product Recall Information Notices to let consumers and local authorities know about problems associated with food.

In some cases, a 'Food Alert for Action' is issued.

This provides local authorities with details of specific actions to be taken on behalf of consumers.
