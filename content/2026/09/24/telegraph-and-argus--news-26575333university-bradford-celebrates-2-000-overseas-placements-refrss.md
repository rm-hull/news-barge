MORE than 2,000 overseas student placements have been delivered through an international study scheme.

The University of Bradford reached the milestone through the Turing Scheme, launched in 2021 to support students in gaining overseas experience.

The university has secured more than £3.6 million in grants since the programme began. The Turing Scheme enters its final year as the UK prepares to rejoin the EU-backed Erasmus+ programme in 2027.

Carlos Santos e Sousa, international operations and partnerships manager at the University of Bradford, said: "Between 2021/22 and 2025/26, the University of Bradford students taking part in the Turing Scheme gained valuable overseas experiences that supported their personal, academic and professional growth.

"An impressive 92% (979 students) were very satisfied with the Team's support. Through their overseas experiences, 766 students (64%) reported greater confidence in their abilities, while 652 (54%) believed their chances of securing a new or better job had increased."

He added: "Behind these figures is something even more important: the opportunity for more University of Bradford students to develop confidence, skills, networks and global perspectives through international experiences.

"This achievement also reflects the tremendous collaboration that makes these opportunities possible – from building partnerships and engaging students to delivering Turing projects effectively and efficiently."

In its final year, the university was awarded £693,097 to support 395 overseas placements.

Placements have taken students across Asia, Africa, Europe, and the United States.

Gabriel Bailey, 24, a BEng civil and structural engineering graduate, completed placements in Germany, China, Vietnam, and the US through the scheme.

He said: "I chose Bradford because it was ranked 7th nationally for civil engineering.

"The opportunities I have had here have helped me gain confidence and develop both in my field and culturally.

"My first trip abroad was daunting, because I didn't know anyone.

"Now, I have friends around the world, from Australia to the Czech Republic.

"I cannot recommend this type of experience enough.

"It has changed my life and I'm grateful to the University for the opportunities it has made possible."

![]()Gabriel Bailey, BEng Civil and Structural Engineering University of Bradford graduate on a study abroad scheme in the US *(Image: University of Bradford)*

The university will join the Erasmus+ Programme from the 2027/28 academic year, marking a return to the EU-sponsored mobility scheme.

The Turing Scheme has funded placements in countries including China, South Korea, Vietnam, Egypt, Jordan, Tanzania, Japan, Rwanda, Kenya, and Turkey.
