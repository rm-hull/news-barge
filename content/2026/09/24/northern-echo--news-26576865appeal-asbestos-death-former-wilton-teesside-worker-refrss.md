The widow of an engineer who died from asbestos-related cancer is appealing for his former colleagues to come forward.

Stanley Rogers, who spent much of his career working at Sellafield Nuclear Power Station in Cumbria and at ICI Wilton in Teesside, died in May 2025, just months after being diagnosed with mesothelioma.

Mesothelioma is an aggressive cancer caused by exposure to asbestos, often decades before symptoms appear.

Stanley's family, with help from Slater and Gordon, are trying to establish how he was exposed to asbestos at work.

Stanley, a father of two, joined Sellafield as a teenage maintenance-engineering apprentice before qualifying as an instrument technician.

He installed, repaired and maintained equipment in reactor buildings, turbine halls, water-treatment plants and chlorine plants.

![]()Young Stanley Rogers, who passed away from asbestos-related cancer. *(Image: Slater and Gordon)*

His family believe the work regularly brought him into contact with asbestos insulation on lagged pipes and equipment.

After leaving Sellafield, he joined ICI Wilton as an instrument technician and carried out similar maintenance work around industrial plant and steam pipes.

Slater and Gordon are appealing for anyone who worked with Stanley at Sellafield’s Calder Hall site between 1970 and 1978, or at ICI Wilton between 1978 and 1981, to come forward.

The family are particularly keen to hear from anyone who remembers maintenance work, pipe repairs or asbestos lagging being removed at either site.

Stanley had no idea he had been exposed to asbestos until he began experiencing lung problems in early 2025.

His wife, Joanne Rogers, said: "It wasn't even six months before Stanley died that he was diagnosed. He started having problems with his lungs in early 2025 and once he was diagnosed, he just went downhill so quickly.

"It was his chest. He was struggling to breathe and coughing all the time. One day he called an ambulance because he didn't know what was wrong with him. That wasn't like Stan at all as he never complained about anything."

She added: "We lost Stan far earlier than expected and I'm angry that he died from something that wasn't his fault.

"Stan was the kind of person who always followed the rules. He was conscientious in everything he did and took enormous pride in doing his job properly.

"It's cruel that this happened to such a nice man and that something from work all those years ago has taken him away from us."

![]()Stanley Rogers, who passed away from asbestos-related cancer. *(Image: Slater and Gordon)*

Joanne and Stanley had been married for 36 years and had two children and two grandchildren.

Joanne, who has the rare neurological condition hereditary spastic paraplegia and uses a wheelchair, said Stanley had been her main carer for many years.

"He helped me dress, bathe, drove me everywhere and looked after all the jobs around the home," she said.

"Since losing him, it's incredibly difficult and I'm struggling without him."

The family’s legal team are appealing for witnesses who remember asbestos being present or disturbed at either workplace.

Simon Matthews, a senior associate solicitor specialising in asbestos and industrial disease claims at Slater and Gordon, said: "Mesothelioma can take decades to develop after exposure to asbestos and, sadly, Stanley passed away before evidence could be obtained from him regarding the circumstances of how he came into contact with asbestos.

"This makes witness evidence particularly important in helping his family uncover what happened.

"Even information that may seem minor or insignificant could prove vital in helping us establish the conditions workers were exposed to, and in securing the answers his family desperately needs."

Anyone who worked with Stanley at Sellafield between 1970 and 1978 or at ICI Wilton between 1978 and 1981 is asked to contact Slater and Gordon.
