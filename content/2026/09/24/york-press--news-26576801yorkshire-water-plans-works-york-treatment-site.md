MAJOR infrastructure in York is set for critical works under new plans.

An application lodged with City of York Council proposes upgrades to Yorkshire Water's Naburn Wastewater Treatment Works.

Plans from the utility company stated the improvements would help improve the treatment process, compliance with regulations on phosphorus removal and enhance the long-term resilience of the site.

It comes after Yorkshire Water announced in August it was investing £35m in improving the Naburn facility.

The company stated efforts to reduce the amount of phosphorus present in wastewater which is returned to rivers was the most significant part of the project.

Phosphorus is present in shampoos and liquid detergents and is a normal part of domestic sewage, according to Yorkshire Water.

A small amount is harmless but it can damage the health of animals and humans if it is left unmanaged.

Plans for Naburn are among 85 schemes Yorkshire Water is set to undertake to remove more phosphorus from wastewater over the next five years in a £350m programme.

Yorkshire Water Project Manager Lucie Arger said there were also plans to invest in reducing discharges from storm overflows into the River Ouse in the coming years.

The project manager said: “The health of Yorkshire's rivers is incredibly important to us and to our customers, and reducing the levels of phosphorous in our treated wastewater is one way in which we can do our bit to improve it.”

![]()Works are planned at Yorkshire Water's Naburn Wastewater Treatment Works (file photo) *(Image: T&A)*

Yorkshire Water's planning application proposes the installation of a number of 'kiosks' and storage tanks across the Naburn Lane site.

Plans unveiled by the company in August said the works were also set for upgraded equipment and pipework to improve its efficiency.

The application stated the works would not have an unacceptable impact on the environment, road safety or nearby residents.

Plans stated: “The development is appropriately located within an existing operational wastewater treatment works and has been designed to minimise impacts on the surrounding environment whilst delivering critical wastewater infrastructure improvements.

“Appropriate drainage measures have been incorporated into the design, and the proposed assets have been located and designed having regard to identified flood risk constraints.

“The scale, form and appearance of the proposed elements and infrastructure are all appropriate to their operational function and will be viewed in the context of the existing wastewater treatment works, minimising the impact on the landscape character and the openness of this part of the green belt.”

Yorkshire Water's £350 million programme covering 85 schemes follows a £500 million investment to remove phosphorus from wastewater.

The company stated that programme saw phosphorus levels in treated wastewater fall by 68 per cent.
