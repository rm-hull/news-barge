Music legend Paul Jones surprised a North Yorkshire festival organiser with a special tribute to mark its 21st anniversary.

Paul Jones, Manfred Mann’s original frontman and a current member of The Manfreds, presented promoter Chris Wood with a 21st-anniversary cake at Whitby Pavilion.

For more than two decades, the festival has brought 1960s music to the North Yorkshire coast.

![]()Another full house for the 21 year Whitby 60s Festival at the Whitby Pavilion at the weekend *(Image: Supplied by Goffy Media)*

Mr Jones said: "I’ve loved performing at Whitby 60s Festival a number of times over the years, but this weekend felt different.

"There was an even greater attachment to the occasion, knowing we were celebrating 21 years and everything Chris and his team have built here.

"It was a wonderful weekend of celebration and I’m delighted to have been part of it."

The Manfreds headlined the anniversary event with hits including Do Wah Diddy Diddy, 5-4-3-2-1, Pretty Flamingo and Sha La La.

The surprise tribute visibly moved organiser Chris Wood.

He said: "I got hugely emotional when Paul called my name and then everyone stood up to applaud me.

"It was a very special moment."

The Whitby 60s Festival is now held twice a year, in June and September, drawing fans from across the UK and beyond.

But Mr Wood said one of the weekend’s most touching moments came from an attendee’s personal story.

He said: "He told me he had lost his wife five years ago and that they had both loved coming to these shows.

"Before she passed away, she told him to make sure he kept attending.

"He has done that and, through continuing to come along, has made some wonderful friendships and is now loving life and with a partner that he met right here with us at a previous festival.

"It really moved me that these events had touched someone’s life in such a strong way and proved to be a real game changer."

Stories like this show the festival is about more than music, Mr Wood said, creating community, connection and lasting memories.

He also credited his wife, Christine, for supporting the festival throughout its 21-year history.

He said: "Christine has been a huge support over the years and a calming influence during some of the more stressful moments that inevitably come with organising events of this size.

"Her encouragement, understanding and support have helped me more than she will ever know."

The festival also brings major economic benefits to Whitby and the surrounding area.

During each three-day event, visitors fill hotels and guest houses and spend money in Whitby’s restaurants, bars, cafes and shops.

Many stay for the full weekend, giving the town’s economy a further boost.

Boom Radio has also promoted the festival nationally, with presenters Simon Bates and David Hamilton raising its profile across the UK.

But Chris Wood said the festival will not slow down after reaching its 21-year milestone.
