A North East domestic abuse charity has won national recognition for helping victims and survivors access specialist support through their GP surgeries.

Middlesbrough charity My Sisters Place has won Gold-standard accreditation for delivering the IRIS (Identification and Referral to Improve Safety) programme.

The award recognises the charity’s work supporting domestic abuse survivors and improving how primary care responds to abuse.

![]() *(Image: Supplied)*

Claire Moore, domestic abuse strategic lead at Middlesbrough Council, said: "This achievement recognises the expertise, hard work and passion of the staff working to deliver IRIS in Middlesbrough.

"Their commitment to improving outcomes for those affected by domestic abuse has been instrumental in My Sisters Place achieving this prestigious standard.

"We would also like to thank GP practice teams across Middlesbrough for their ongoing support and commitment to IRIS and ensuring they participate in training and identify and refer their patients who are affected by or experiencing domestic abuse."

IRIS is a national, evidence-based scheme that helps GP surgeries respond to domestic abuse.

It aims to improve patient outcomes, make surgeries safer and raise awareness among primary care staff.

Middlesbrough Council has supported My Sisters Place in rolling out IRIS across the town’s GP services since 2021.

The scheme trains clinical and non-clinical staff to spot early signs of domestic abuse and offer prompt support.

Each GP practice gets a dedicated domestic abuse advocate and ongoing specialist training from My Sisters Place.

More than 450 patients have been referred through the programme and received tailored support.

Middlesbrough is one of 40 IRIS sites across the UK and the only one in the North East to have successfully implemented the programme.

The work is a key part of Middlesbrough’s Preventing Domestic Abuse Strategy, launched in January.

A recent Domestic Homicide Review found gaps in health professionals’ ability to recognise and respond consistently to abuse.

Ms Moore said the training had given GPs and practice staff the “skills and confidence” to spot abuse earlier, assess immediate risks and refer patients for support.

She said it had also helped identify “hidden” victims who might struggle to seek help themselves.

Ms Moore said: "Many of the patients who have accessed support via Middlesbrough IRIS report feeling safer, more able to cope and needing to visit their GP less frequently as a result."

Lynsey Gibson, chief executive of My Sisters Place, said: “We are delighted with the Gold standard accreditation, which is testament to the hard work and dedication of advocate educator Leanne Smith and clinical lead Jennifer Standen.

“It is recognition of the importance of the IRISi programme in helping bridge the gap between health and domestic abuse support.”

Mel Goodway, IRISi’s director of programmes and innovation, said the award recognised the “high-quality, best-practice” delivery of the programme in Middlesbrough.

Ms Goodway said: “Achieving a Gold-standard award reflects the hard work and collaboration of everyone delivering IRIS locally.

“Ultimately, this work is about more than meeting a set of standards.
