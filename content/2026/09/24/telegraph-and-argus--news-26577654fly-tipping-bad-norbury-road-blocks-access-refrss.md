A resident has contacted the T&A to complain about fly-tipping on a residential street, warning it could put public safety at risk.

The resident, who has lived near the fly-tipping hotspot on Norbury Road in Bradford’s Eccleshill ward for years, wants to remain anonymous but had plenty to say about the daily blight.

He said: "It's like this all the time.

"Fly-tipping has been a massive problem for as long as I can remember, and the council never seems to have found a way of properly tackling it."

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

## But what's the solution?

The Eccleshill resident believes prevention is better than cure.

He claims that he has had "numerous conversations" with Environmental Health regarding installing cameras, but adds, "unfortunately, they don’t seem particularly interested."

He said: "This rubbish is being dumped on a residential street, not even a back alley, so I’m also not entirely sure why Highways don’t seem to be involved."

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

The council told the T&A it had no record of a CCTV request for the site but would assess it and explore installing cameras.

They also said the mess was reported on Wednesday, September 23, and would be cleared on Friday, September 24.

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

A Bradford Council spokesperson said: "The land surrounding Norbury Road is owned and maintained by Incommunities.

"Historically, Bradford Council and Incommunities have worked together to address waste accumulations in this area, including a significant joint clean-up operation last year which took place over several weeks and involved skips being provided to remove substantial quantities of waste."

## 'It’s only a matter of time'

The eyesore is not the resident’s only concern: he fears someone could be injured by the rubbish.

He continued: "It’s only a matter of time before someone seriously hurts themselves.

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

"I’ve reported an abandoned caravan more times than I care to remember, warning that if it wasn’t removed it would probably end up being set on fire.

"Four months later, it was still there – and, unfortunately, someone did exactly that."

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

## Money wasted clearing up after others

The resident questioned whether recent investment would tackle fly-tipping or simply "just end up covering the cost of removing rubbish."

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

The resident explained: "I remember reading back in July about the £20 million investment into Ravenscliffe, but I can’t help thinking that a large chunk of that money will probably just end up covering the cost of removing rubbish.

"You can’t keep spending money cleaning up the same problem without actually addressing why it’s happening in the first place.

"Prevention has to be part of the solution."

![]()Fly-tipping on Norbury Road

In response, a Bradford Council spokesperson said: “Unfortunately, fly-tipping can be difficult to prevent without evidence identifying those responsible.

"Where evidence is available, we will investigate and take appropriate enforcement action.

"We would always encourage residents to continue reporting incidents so that they can be assessed and appropriate action taken.”

Incommunities have been contacted for a comment.
