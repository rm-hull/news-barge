# Met Office forecasts Saturday thunder risk in parts of three UK areas

## The Met Office forecasts heavy downpours with a chance of thunder

Autumn is set to grip the nation this weekend, with three regions bracing for thunderstorms on Saturday as torrential rain sweeps across the country.

Following a predominantly settled and dry week, the Met Office is cautioning that an Atlantic trough will merge with a weakening frontal system, delivering a substantial band of precipitation, with the most severely affected areas potentially receiving up to 100mm of rainfall.

Heavy showers, accompanied by the possibility of thunder, are anticipated across portions of southwest England, Wales and the West Midlands, preceding a broader band of rain. During a thunderstorm, the Met Office recommends Brits remain mindful of metal objects that can conduct or attract lightning, refrain from using the landline unless it's an emergency, and if outdoors, avoid using the landline unless absolutely necessary.

Motorists caught in a thunderstorm should close their windows and remain inside their vehicle, reports the Express. The forecaster indicates that on Saturday, the rainfall will arrive in the northwest initially before extending across Scotland and Northern Ireland throughout Sunday.

Northern Ireland and western Scotland could experience up to 100mm of precipitation from late Saturday through to Sunday, with certain locations facing sustained spells of heavy rainfall. The Met Office notes there are developing indications of "increasingly wet and windy weather next week".

A change in the jet stream is anticipated to fuel the formation of low-pressure systems early next week. Britain could be hit by a bout of wet and windy conditions from late Monday through to Tuesday, with western regions expected to bear the brunt of heavy rainfall and powerful gusts.

Eastern areas may avoid the worst of the unsettled weather, with drier spells more probable. The spell of wet and windy conditions could persist into Wednesday, bringing the possibility of coastal disruption across western and southwestern regions.

Met Office deputy chief forecaster Dan Holley said: "While details are naturally uncertain at this range, a particularly deep area of low pressure could pass close to the UK during the first half of next week.

"This may bring some wet and windy weather for some, which coupled with high spring tides could bring impacts to some coastal areas in the west."
