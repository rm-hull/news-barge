A North Yorkshire teenager who had to learn to walk and talk again after suffering a devastating stroke has completed his first Great North Run, raising money for the hospital that helped save his life.

Tom Davies, 17, from Catterick, finished the half marathon in two hours and 43 seconds, more than two years after suffering an intracerebral haemorrhage during a swimming session at age 14.

Tom said: "I was just aiming just to complete it but was over the moon to achieve that time.

![]()Tom in hospital *(Image: Supplied)*

"I may have to do it again to get under two hours now!"

The stroke was caused by a ruptured arteriovenous malformation (AVM).

Tom spent four months at the Great North Children’s Hospital in Newcastle learning to walk and talk again.

He raised £1,202 for the hospital through the run.

His mother, Rachel Davies, said: "We are so proud of Tom. It was an emotional moment when he finished the run."
