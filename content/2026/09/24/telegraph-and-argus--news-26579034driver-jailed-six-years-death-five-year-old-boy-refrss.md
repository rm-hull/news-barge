A man has been jailed for dangerous driving after causing the death of five-year-old Abdullah Ebrahim.

Colin Agyei, 32, of Pear Tree Grove, Knottingley, was sentenced to six years in prison at Leeds Crown Court after being found guilty of causing Abdullah's death by dangerous driving.

The incident took place on July 7, 2022, on Back Camberley Street in Leeds, a narrow road not typically used by vehicles.

Agyei drove his Nissan Note past a no entry sign and struck Abdullah as the child ran across the road.

Abdullah died in hospital shortly after the collision.

### Mother's grief - 'It is something I carry every single day.'

His mother, Salma Begum, spoke of her ongoing grief and the pain of reliving the incident throughout the trial.

Ms Begum said: "What I cannot understand, and what I will never forget, is that he failed to stop and left my child there.

"I will never forget the image of Abdullah lying on the floor on his own.

"That image has stayed with me for four years.

"It is something I carry every single day."

She also expressed frustration with Agyei’s changing accounts during the trial.

Ms Begum said: "I attended the trial and had to sit and listen to the defendant say that he did not see my son.

"I heard his account change several times, and I felt that he was not telling the truth about what happened.

"Four years have passed, but my grief has not passed.

"I still miss my son.

"I still think about him every day."

### 'Completely avoidable'

Detective Sergeant Steve Suggitt, from West Yorkshire Police’s Major Collision Enquiry Team, described the incident as "completely avoidable."

He said: "This was a truly tragic incident that resulted in the death of a young boy and robbed a family of a lifetime of shared memories.

"This collision was completely avoidable had Agyei complied with the no entry sign and not driven into Back Camberley Street.

"Young Abdullah would not have been expecting a vehicle to be driving up the road and did not have time to react.

"While we estimate that Agyei was driving at a relatively low speed of around 15mph, during our investigation we thoroughly tested the back street and found that because of the nature of the road you cannot drive safely on it at speeds of over 7mph.

"Agyei admitted a lesser careless driving offence but was found guilty of causing death by dangerous driving.

"While he has been given a custodial sentence, I know that no sentence can ever in any way equate to the lifetime of grief that Abdullah’s family face."

The court heard that Agyei failed to stop at the scene of the collision.
