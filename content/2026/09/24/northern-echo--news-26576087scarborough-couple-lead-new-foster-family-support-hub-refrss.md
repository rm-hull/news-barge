A North Yorkshire couple who call fostering one of the most rewarding experiences of their lives are taking on a new role supporting other foster families.

Scarborough couple Jayne and Tom Dockerty have fostered with North Yorkshire Council for more than five years and will run the county’s fifth Mockingbird hub.

The Scarborough hub will give foster families across the area practical support and guidance, making it the town’s third.

Mrs Dockerty said: "The Mockingbird hub offers that extended family feeling.

"Children are able to build relationships with familiar families through activities, outings, sleepovers and social events.

"It creates a sense of belonging and helps young people find their voice and build confidence.

"We are true believers in a buddy system for people who are new to fostering or thinking about becoming foster carers.

"It can feel daunting at first, and having someone to talk to, a shoulder to lean on, or a reassuring phone call can make a huge difference."

The couple began fostering during the Covid-19 pandemic and have since cared for six children, from a two-day-old baby to a 16-year-old.

They have provided long- and short-term care, including sleepovers and day care for other families.

They have helped a teenager move into semi-independent living and supported a young boy through adoption.

One of their most meaningful experiences was caring for a baby who later returned home to their family.

The bond was so strong that Mrs Dockerty was asked to become the child’s godmother.

Mr Dockerty said: "We love making a difference in children’s lives, but the truth is that they make such a rewarding and loving difference in ours."

The Mockingbird Family Model, which began in America, uses a centralised “hub” to support foster carers and has been adopted in North Yorkshire.

Fostering North Yorkshire, part of North Yorkshire Council, joined The Fostering Network’s Mockingbird Family Model programme in 2019.

The scheme aims to keep placements stable and strengthen links between foster carers, children, fostering services and birth families.

The new Scarborough hub expands the programme’s reach, adding to existing hubs in Scarborough, Thirsk and Selby.

Councillor Janet Sanderson, North Yorkshire Council’s executive member for children and families, said: "Dedicated foster parents like Jayne and Tom play a vital role in transforming the lives of children and young people in our care.

"Their dedication, compassion and commitment over the past five years have helped provide stability, encouragement and opportunities for children at some of the most challenging times in their lives."

The Dockertys are calling for more people to become sleepover and respite carers for teenagers and their foster families.

Mrs Dockerty said: "There is a real shortage of sleepover opportunities for teenagers and their carers.

"These short breaks can provide valuable support for everyone involved.
