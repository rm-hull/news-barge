An arsonist who set fire to her own home during a mental health episode has been given a chance to turn her life around.

Kayleigh Gallon used lighter fuel to start the fire, then called emergency services and left the property, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The 37-year-old caused extensive smoke damage when she started the blaze at the terraced house on December 9, 2024.

Prosecutor Emma Atkinson said Gallon’s former partner and another man entered the burning house on Herbert Street, Middlesbrough, to check nobody was inside.

“Two men kicked down the door, entered the house and tried to find Gallon,” she said.

“At that time, Gallon was in an alleyway.”

The court heard she initially claimed she had fallen asleep with a cigarette and accidentally started the fire before accepting responsibility.

Miss Atkinson said one of the men went to hospital with smoke inhalation.

Gallon, of Eleventh Street, Horden, County Durham, pleaded guilty to reckless arson.

Her lawyer, Michele Turner, said the mother-of-11 had endured a difficult childhood and was involved in numerous toxic relationships.

She said Gallon had been in care as a child and became pregnant almost every 18 months, leading to spiralling alcohol addiction.

“In her head, that’s what she thinks she deserves and that is very sad to hear,” she said.

Ms Turner said Gallon was struggling with her mental health when she committed the arson.

Judge Richard Bennett gave her a three-year community order with several conditions.

“You must understand that this is a direct alternative to you going to prison,” he said.

“If you breach this order, and I make it clear to you that almost everybody who appears before me for setting fire to properties goes to prison.

In 2019, a closure order was secured on a Middlesbrough property where Gallon was a tenant following complaints about drug dealing and antisocial behaviour.
