The North East Mayor has been warned a tourism tax would "send visitors elsewhere" and harm jobs, as four council leaders urged her to rule out the proposal.

Four Reform UK council leaders have written to Kim McGuinness calling on her to abandon plans for a potential overnight visitor levy, arguing it would damage the region's tourism industry and increase costs for families and business travellers.

The letter is signed by Andrew Husband, leader of Durham County Council, Nick Allan of Gateshead Council, Paul Mackings of South Tyneside Council and Chris Eynon of Sunderland City Council.

A Reform UK spokesman said: "According to Labour the answer to getting more people to visit the North East is to charge them for the privilege. Typical.

"Families are already counting every penny, while our hotels, pubs and guesthouses are being hammered by higher taxes, growing costs and red tape. A holiday tax will make our region more expensive and send visitors elsewhere.

"The North East needs more bookings, more investment and more jobs. Not another bright idea from Labour politicians who think every problem can be solved by inventing a new tax."

![]()Reform UK's North East council leaders. Left to right: Andrew Husband, Durham County Council, Paul Mackings, South Tyneside Council, Nick Allan, Gateshead Council, Chris Eynon, Sunderland City Council. *(Image: NORTHERN ECHO / LDRS)*

Under Labour's plans, local leaders will be able to set the charge as a percentage of the cost of accommodation, rather than a flat fee, which ministers argue will protect budget holidays.

Mayors will be able to decide how to spend the revenue, which will apply both to overseas tourists and Britons, rather than the money being funnelled back to central government.

Writing to the Mayor, the council leaders said the proposed levy would make holidays more expensive, discourage visitors and place further pressure on hospitality businesses already facing higher employment costs and rising wage bills.

"Making the North East more expensive at the checkout risks driving visitors, spending and jobs to neighbouring regions," they said.

![]()North East mayor Kim McGuinness. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The leaders said industry estimates suggested the charge could add around £100 to the cost of a two-week holiday for a family of four and cited warnings that visitor levies could threaten thousands of hospitality jobs nationwide.

They also argued that hotels, guesthouses, holiday lets, caravan parks, pubs, restaurants and visitor attractions would ultimately bear the cost, either through reduced bookings or by absorbing some of the charge themselves.

A spokesperson for the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority stressed that no decision has yet been made on introducing a levy.

They said: "No decision has been taken to introduce an Overnight Visitor Levy in the North East and no rate has been set as of yet.

"Our priority is to grow the visitor economy, support jobs and bring more visitors to the North East. Any levy would need to be modest, proportionate and designed with the sector, taking into account the needs of accommodation providers, including rural and coastal businesses.

"Crucially, any revenue raised would stay in the North East and be reinvested into attracting visitors, support the delivery of more major events and strengthening the visitor economy, helping local businesses and communities benefit from increased tourism.

"We are confident that any levy would be used to bring many more visitors to the North East and grow the number of tourism jobs here."
