After accusations of "chaos" at the Reform-led Durham County Council, leader Andrew Husband says his administration is "stronger, more cohesive, and more focused on delivering for local people than ever before" in his latest column.

I write this at a time when terrible things are happening in our country, which really puts things into perspective when politicians become irrelevant as real-life problems grab the headlines.

The Reform smears, both locally and nationally, are boring, boring - as Nigel would say. Most people do too. It is not working. The coordinated coup in Durham has failed; it is business as usual. I won’t waste time responding to unhinged accusations made by former councillors just to get their five minutes of fame.

Meanwhile, Durham County Council has just been chosen to lead a national social care improvement programme – the first North East authority to be selected, a recognition of the high quality of children’s social care services we have in Durham.

In a little over 12 months, we have scrapped stupid net-zero schemes with no business case, found tens of millions in savings, and ended the ridiculous climate emergency in favour of a social care emergency.

We delivered the lowest council tax increase in the United Kingdom (just 1.99 per cent), with no use of reserves, and still managed to invest £264m in new Sen schools providing 200 places, including a £26m investment in the new Villa Real Send school and a further £33m in Send provision generally.

Don’t forget more disability support, with a service that's been improved by AI digital infrastructure to make things even more efficient.

We have vowed to re-industrialise the county and protect farmers.

We have introduced new rules to enable the council to resist new HMOs and support communities. We saved the county tens of millions by re-negotiating a major waste contract and by pulling out of the very expensive repair of Milburngate, which the Lib Dems had committed us to before the election.

We are making a major investment to tackle homelessness by providing new temporary accommodation. We are driving significant improvements in towns and villages, developing local community assets and restoring civic pride. We are investing in facilities to help disabled people live independently in their own homes.

All this while reducing borrowing by £21.3 million.

Finally, our highway improvements have resulted in pothole reports falling by a third, with data showing that we’re delivering higher-quality, larger-scale repairs that last longer.

This week we saved the cockerels! We listened to residents and went against the officer and national recommendations who wanted to ban them from allotments. This is what we were elected to do: to stand up for residents and make a difference.

The people leaving the party now have let their residents down. They have conned them by using Reform colours and betrayed the many members who spent their time and money to help them get elected.

There is no chaos in Reform Durham. That chaos now sits on the opposite side of the chamber. Despite their very best attempts to derail us, we are stronger, more cohesive, and more focused on delivering for local people than ever before.
