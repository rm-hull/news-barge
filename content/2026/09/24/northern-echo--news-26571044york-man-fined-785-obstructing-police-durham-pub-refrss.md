A MAN'S pub trip to watch World Cup football ended with a £785 court bill after he obstructed police.

Police were called to the Newton Grange pub in Newton Hall, Durham, on Wednesday, July 1 after customer Lee Franklin was reported to be behaving in an aggressive manner.

Peterlee magistrates heard Franklin was outside when police arrived, and appeared heavily intoxicated and slurring his words.

Prosecutor Michael Embleton said officers approached Franklin about an unrelated matter, but he initially ran off, insisting he had done nothing wrong.

![]()Lee Franklin left court with a total bill of £785 for his drunken aggression at the Newton Grange pub in Newton Hall, Durham, where he was with his partner watching football

Police eventually pepper-sprayed Franklin and arrested him on suspicion of obstructing an officer in the execution of her duty.

Franklin, 41, of Alcuin Avenue, York, admitted the offence at his first court hearing.

The defendant was said to have 37 convictions for 94 offences and was nearing the end of an 18-month suspended prison sentence for assault imposed at York Crown Court in March last year.

![]()Lee Franklin, 41, admitted obstructing police in incident at The Newton Grange pub in Durham, in July

Franklin was also subject to a community order imposed by Glasgow Sheriff’s Court, under which he was carrying out unpaid work.

Josh Peacock, in mitigation, said Franklin pleaded guilty promptly and accepted there was no excuse for his behaviour in the pub.

“He was in Durham at the pub watching football and had fallen out with his partner.

"He was being spoken to over an unrelated matter.

“He accepts he did obstruct the officers and was pepper sprayed and taken into custody.

“But I don’t believe any officers were injured during the offence.”

Mr Peacock said Franklin’s York suspended sentence has now expired, but he was still carrying out unpaid work under the Glasgow community order.

He said Franklin worked in Scotland during the week and earned a good wage.

Bench chair Sian Allan-Gilbert said the magistrates had dealt with much worse cases, but had to consider that Franklin was on a suspended sentence and a Scottish community payback order when he carried out his latest offence.

“So, we don’t feel we can give you a community order for this, but we’ll issue a fine instead.”

Franklin was fined £500 and ordered to pay £285 in costs and a statutory surcharge.

Mrs Allan-Gilbert told him: “The sooner you can pay it off, the better.”

“I would strongly suggest you don’t do anything that brings you before the court again.

“If you come back again for anything else, I can almost guarantee you’ll be going to prison.”

The Newton Grange was later shortlisted for the Best Marston’s Pub category at the Great British Pub Awards.
