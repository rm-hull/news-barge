Women driving growth in the North East’s space and technology industries have been celebrated at an event in County Durham.

Space North East England organised the event at NETPark in Sedgefield to celebrate women shaping the region’s space and technology sectors.

More than 70 people attended talks, discussions and speeches from leading space and technology professionals.

Lisa Mullen, cluster manager at Space North East England, said: "The North East is home to an incredible community of talented women who are making a real impact across the space, technology and research sectors.

"This event was an opportunity to celebrate their achievements, share their experiences and inspire the next generation to explore the exciting careers our sector has to offer.

"By showcasing role models from across industry and academia, we're helping to build a more diverse and inclusive space ecosystem that reflects the ambition and talent we have here in the region."

Speakers included former engineer Alex Knight, who runs Stemazing, an organisation that encourages more women into STEM careers by training mentors to work with young people.

Marianne Whitfield, vice-president of sales and business development at Newcastle’s DHS IT Solutions, also spoke.

Ms Whitfield has more than 30 years’ technology experience and uses data and AI to improve productivity while championing women in STEM careers.

Other speakers included Christina Munn from Durham University’s Space Research Centre; Dr Kirsty Lindsay, associate professor at Northumbria University’s Physiotherapy, Aerospace Medicine and Rehabilitation Laboratory; and Kirsty Donaldson, senior mechanical engineer at Rapid Fluidics.
