INTEREST in taking on an empty York venue and reopening it has prompted a call for its owner to come to the table.

York's Campaign for Real Ale (CAMRA) branch's pub protection lead Nick Love said Victoria Vaults viably reopening had not been ruled out in a planning appeal ruling.

The campaigner added they had been speaking to a number of credible businesses and operators who were interested in taking the venue on.

But developer Nabeel Hanif, the owner of the Nunnery Lane building, said the ruling which upheld City of York Council's refusal of his plans to convert it would keep it empty.

It follows a Planning Inspectorate hearing on Tuesday, August 25 at the developer's appeal against the council's decision to block plans to convert Victoria Vaults into a café.

Mr Hanif also appealed over the council not deciding on a separate but related application to convert the Nunnery Lane venue into three homes.

The venue, which hosted Bez from The Happy Mondays, Blondie drummer Clem Burke, goth discos, poetry readings and several other rock, pop and reggae acts, closed in December 2024.

Planning documents stated the closure followed considerable losses and a £25,000-a-year lease is currently being marketed by Barry Crux.

The planning inspector ruled the conversion would see an important cultural facility and cultural venue lost without justification.

They also stated they were not persuaded that the venue could not reasonably be made viable.

Mr Love said the inspector's rulings came as people in the music industry had told CAMRA Victoria Vaults could reopen successfully with the right owner and business model.

![]()The Victoria Vaults *(Image: LDRS)*

The campaigner, whose bid to have the venue listed as an Asset of Community Value (ACV) was approved in August, told the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS): "The inspector made a big point of recognising the historical cultural importance of this dedicated grassroots music venue to the York music and arts scene and the harmful impact of its potential loss.

"We have been speaking with credible businesses and operators that are interested in acquiring the property to reopen it as a venue, which reinforces this decision and we hope that the current owner re-evaluates their approach and opens up dialogue with these people.

"Local York residents unequivocally don't want a small number of luxury townhouses to replace a much valued arts and culture venue used by thousands of people every year."

Speaking following the ruling, developer Mr Hanif told LDRS it was not the decision he had wanted.

The developer said: "The Victoria Vaults closed in December 2024 after more than £100,000 of public grants failed to keep it open.

"In the 21 months since, nobody has put a viable, funded proposal on the table to run it as a pub, and the council's own officers recorded in May that no viable interest had emerged.

"Refusing every alternative use put forward does not bring the building back to life, it keeps it empty."
