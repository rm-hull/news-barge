# Film Wakefield: 'I've supplied police props and costumes for TV and film for 30 years, now I'm helping to put Wakefield on the map for screen production'

## ​For 30 years, David Clayton has supplied film and TV companies with police props and costumes. Now he’s helping to put the district on the map for screen production. Laura Reid reports.

If your favourite television show features an on-screen police officer, there’s a good chance their props or costume will have come from Yorkshire. For 30 years now, Wakefield-based Policelot has been supporting UK film and TV production. As well as having more than 9,000 authentic police props and uniform available for hire, the business is able to provide police consultants, supporting actors and sets ranging from station interview rooms to police cells.

Founder and CEO David Clayton and his team have worked with all-manner of high profile shows, including Yorkshire-filmed Adolescence, EastEnders, Ackley Bridge, the James Bond movie Skyfall and, more recently, Trigger Point and Vigil. But, he says, only a few projects have actually been based in his home district.

"I've worked in the industry for 30 years, I’m Wakefield born and bred, but I realised I could count on one hand the amount of productions I’d worked on in Wakefield itself,” he says. “We weren't getting the numbers that say Leeds, Manchester, Liverpool and elsewhere got.”

![Director of Policelot and founder of Film Wakefield, David Clayton.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmRlNTU2YTM2LWIzZGYtNGYzMy1iYzliLWNhNTMwNzg3MmNjZDplNjNlM2QzYS05MjIyLTRhNTMtOGIzMC00NDZiYjIwYjNkNmY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Over the last few years, David has set about to change that, identifying a need for Wakefield’s screen infrastructure, locations and talent to be brought under one banner. Last year, that became a reality, with Film Wakefield officially being established as “a gateway to the region's growing film scene”. Supported by organisations including Wakefield Council, Experience Wakefield and Production Park, it exists to promote Wakefield and the Five Towns as locations for film and TV production, whilst supporting a network connecting filmmakers with local creative talent and businesses.

Its key remits are to attract filming activity to the area, increasing local investment and economic growth while nurturing a screen-sector ecosystem in the district, with training and development for local people. Year one has established those foundations, David says, speaking to The Yorkshire Post ahead of an anniversary event at WX in Wakefield this evening. "What we've achieved in just one year demonstrates the appetite and potential that exists within Wakefield's creative community.

"We've built new relationships, showcased local talent and started important conversations with industry partners. This event is not only about celebrating our first anniversary but also about looking ahead to the next chapter and continuing our mission to put Wakefield firmly on the map as a destination for film and television production."

This first year has seen the establishment of an ambassador programme, with every area of the district now having a representative of Film Wakefield to act as a point of contact for the screen industry. There has been a series of film club and networking events, bringing together creative and industry professionals, emerging artists and students, and a Big Screen, Big Idea initiative, which was aimed at encouraging young people to create their own original scripts, storyboards or trailers. Wakefield Council now also has a dedicated Film Officer in Business Support Manager Sarah Marshall.

She says: “We've got a lot of different areas in the district that we're willing for companies to use as locations. And we are a film friendly council – so we're fully supportive of anybody wanting to come and film here. We will help sort permits and agreements for shooting in the area and try to make that process as smooth as possible as well because often time is one of the most important things to a production company, having things done quickly and effectively.

“Wakefield has that urban grit that you can get in the city centre but then you can have the rolling hills if you just drive a couple of miles down the road. If you want a 1920s building or a 1940s building, we've generally got everything here and sometimes production companies are looking for derelict buildings and rundown places or pieces of land...There's not much we can't find.”

“We aren’t overshot here like some of your bigger cities,” David adds. “A lot of their locations have been used over and over again, whereas we've got some new places to film in and around that haven't been used before. Lots of little hidden gems.”

Those behind Film Wakefield say over the past year, there has been an increase in filming enquiries for Wakefield. Supernatural drama thriller Faith was filmed in the district, whilst The Yorkshire Vet and The Repair Shop are among other productions that have been supported. It is hoped that Film Wakefield helps to attract new investment to the area, raises the district’s profile, and creates opportunities for local businesses and residents.

David and Sarah explain, for example, how productions can benefit the local economy by creating demand for accommodation, catering, transport, trades and other professional services. This can provide opportunities for local businesses and suppliers. "We want to reach out to more people and want more people to be involved,” says David.

“It's getting people to understand that this is for everybody,” Sarah adds. “I think people often think, oh, I don't work in the industry. But actually, have you got an interest in the industry? You could be a dress maker. You could be a painter, a designer. It's for everyone and so many different businesses can get involved.”

"It’s also about giving people the understanding that there is a pathway to screen industry jobs as well,” she continues. “We want to showcase that this is ‘a proper job’ and you can have that job up north.”
