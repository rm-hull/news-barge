# DWP explains when Universal Credit payments may need to be repaid back

## Universal Credit overpayments may need to be repaid

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has outlined circumstances where Universal Credit recipients might be required to return benefit payments. The benefit offers financial support to individuals below State Pension age who have restricted income and/or are unemployed.

The majority of claimants receive monthly payments, with Universal Credit progressively replacing Housing Benefit and income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA). Beyond requiring assistance with living costs, applicants must reside in the UK, hold savings under £16,000, and be below State Pension age.

The DWP states Universal Credit payments might need to be repaid owing to mistakes made by the department or if recipients submit incorrect details, reports Birmingham Live. Repayments could also be necessary if individuals don't swiftly inform authorities of a "change in your circumstances".

Gov.uk guidance confirms you must notify the DWP if there are alterations to your health or caring duties. You're also obliged to report if you have a child or if somebody moves in or out of your household.

Additional changes requiring notification include alterations to your address and if you or your partner will be abroad for any duration. Parents must also refresh their accounts if their child aged 16 to 19 ceases or restarts education or training. Once you've ceased receiving benefits, you'll receive correspondence from the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) informing you that you can access this service.

If you're based in Northern Ireland, the correspondence will arrive from the Department for Communities (DfC). Once you receive the letter, the service can be used to establish or manage a payment plan.

You can log in using the security code found in your letter from DWP or DfC, or alternatively via GOV.UK One Login. If you don't already have a GOV.UK One Login account, you'll be able to register for one.

Those claiming benefits can inform the relevant authorities of any changes either through their Universal Credit online account or by ringing the helpline on 0800 328 5644.
