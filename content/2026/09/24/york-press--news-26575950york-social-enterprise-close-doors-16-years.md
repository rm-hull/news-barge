A York social enterprise will be closing its doors after 16 years in the city.

York Disabled Workers Cooperative will shut its unit in the Raylor Centre, off James Street, within weeks.

Team leader Jerry Nelson said the not-for-profit organisation, run predominantly by disabled volunteers, has yet to set a closing date but plans to vacate the site ahead of its eight-week notice period.

![]()Jerry Nelson, second from left, with the York Disabled Workers Cooperative team *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

![]()Stock being cleared from York Disabled Workers Cooperative at the Raylor Centre, off James Street, ahead of its closure *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

Stock is being cleared from the cooperative, which was set up with help from the GMB and other unions in 2010 after the closure of Remploy York.

Jerry said the York Disabled Workers Cooperative specialised in making garden furniture which was sold to pubs and business across York and the north of England.

But he said there “wasn’t a big enough output to provide a good income”.

“There’s no chance of carrying it on,” Jerry said. “There’s just no viable product that you can turn out in a quantity to make money.

“You can’t survive on selling bat boxes and bird boxes, which is basically all we were doing – there were no big orders.”

![]()Flashback to 2018: York Disabled Workers Cooperative volunteers Christine Beecroft, Mark Riley, Chris Howden and Emma Blunden *(Image: Frank Dwyer)*

![]()Bird boxes made by volunteers at the York Disabled Workers Cooperative *(Image: Dylan Connell)*

The closure leaves the organisation’s nine volunteers, aged from their 20s to 80, looking for roles elsewhere.

Jerry noted that rising costs have dealt a further blow to the cooperative but said: “We can’t lay the blame on the government [for the closure].

“Companies increase rents, electricity [prices] are horrendous – we just can’t make that kind of money [to stay open].”

## 'It’s been good fun'

Jerry, the GMB’s national secretary for the manufacturing sector before retiring, has been involved with York Disabled Workers Cooperative for 18 months following the death of his predecessor, Phil Davies.

“I love it,” Jerry said of the cooperative. “I love the people. It’s been good fun. But it’s been tough – you can never have enough income.

“There’s just not the market for this good quality garden furniture. And we’re a limited production of it.”




He added that the closure is “really sad”. “It’s sad in any circumstance having to part ways with friends that you’ve made in the course of running the business.

“It’s just a sad day for guys to part company. But we’re going to have a good do at the end!”

He said the cooperative’s customers are also “all sad to see it go”.

Many customers have visited to buy firewood ahead of winter and say farewell, Jerry added.

Jonathan Bonner, who has been a customer of York Disabled Workers Cooperative for the past five years, said it was “fantastic" organisation, adding that the closure showed “the cold hard face of business”.
