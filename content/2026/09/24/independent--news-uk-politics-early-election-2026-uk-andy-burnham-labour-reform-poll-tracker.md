A sustained polling boost for the new government is fuelling speculation that Andy Burnham is considering an early general election, as a fall in popularity for Reform UK continues.

The governing party has remained consistently above Nigel Farage’s party for approval since Mr Burnham’s coronation in July, when Labour overtook Reform for the first time since March 2025.

A new poll from YouGov now finds Reform would come third in a general election, below the Conservatives. Mr Farage’s party would win 123 seats, the pollster finds, compared to 130 for the Tories.

However, the polling also puts Labour on 241 seats – a loss of 162 from the current total. This would make for a hung parliament, with Labour likely having to look to the Greens and Liberal Democrats for a coalition deal.

Andy Burnham’s leadership has also affected the ratings of Zack Polanski’s Green Party, which has seen a drop in support over the past few months.

The government has said it does not intend to call an early election. However, Labour sources recently told *The Independent* that the by-election to replace Sir Keir Starmer in Holborn and St Pancras is being seen as a test run for the possibility.

Sir Keir announced that he would be leaving parliament earlier this month, after resigning as prime minister and Labour leader to make way for Mr Burnham. Mr Polanski has confirmed he will stand as the Green candidate for the seat next month.

Opposition parties also appear to be preparing for the country to go to the polls early, with Liberal Democrat leader Ed Davey revealing a manifesto working group has already been formed.

“We don’t normally do it this early,” he told *The Guardian*.

Meanwhile, Reform sources have also suggested Mr Burnham could be convinced to call an early election depending on the outcome of the London by-election.

They told *The i* that if Labour’s candidate beats Mr Polanski “convincingly”, he could be dismissed as a “dud” and convince Labour that the progressive vote was uniting behind them once again.

A Tory source also told the paper: “[Burnham’s] whole thing is to consolidate the left. If he continues to constrict the Green vote then it will tempt him into an early election whilst the right remains split.”

Under Sir Keir’s leadership, polling showed Labour began to lose support from left-leaning voters over issues such as Gaza and welfare spending.

This coincided with a boost to the Greens, who recorded a high of around 14 per cent approval in April this year, based on an average of top pollsters, compared to a Labour low of 19 per cent in the same month.

Pollsters have broadly forecast that Labour will win the Holborn and St Pancras by-election, having taken nearly 50 per cent of the vote in the seat in 2024.

However, the picture has changed since then. The Greens took three London councils from Labour at the local elections earlier this year, and increased its share of seats in Camden – home to Holborn and St Pancras – from one to 11 at the contest, while Labour held overall control.

## Will Labour call an early election?

There are clear upsides for Mr Burnham in calling an early election, particularly if Labour holds the seat of Holborn and St Pancras.

The new prime minister has pledged to make sweeping changes. However, after he secured the role with just 24,000 votes from the people of Makerfield – and ran uncontested to be Labour leader – his opponents have argued that he does not hold a mandate from the nation to pursue his agenda.

A general election victory would inarguably grant this, plus an extra two years to deliver the policies he has started to set out.

Under present rules, the latest the next general election can take place is in 2029.

![A sustained polling boost for the new government is fuelling speculation that Andy Burnham is considering an early general election](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/14/15/d2e8cf7cbb01ea1264861e34cad0e25bY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzg5NDc5MzIy-2.85730188.jpg)

Polling suggests it would also be an inopportune time for two of Labour’s key political opponents – Reform and the Greens – given falling support for both parties.

Reform’s approval now sits at its lowest in 20 months, at an average of 23 points, and the Greens at 10 points, down from 14 in April.

The Conservatives, meanwhile, have begun to plateau at around 20 points, and the Lib Dems at around 11.

However, polling expert Sir John Curtice has cast doubt on Labour optimism in the face of these trends, telling *The Independent* there is still a chance for Reform UK to become the biggest party at a nationwide contest.

“The numbers are just not there,” he said.

Labour's disastrous local election results – where it lost 38 councils – saw it drop support in places where it was previously the strongest party, Sir John explained. This pattern means Reform could “still do perfectly well in terms of seats”.

Mr Burnham has also been “dying [to become PM] for years”, Sir John added.

“Holding an election now means risking three years to gain two,” he said. “It’s a bad deal.”
