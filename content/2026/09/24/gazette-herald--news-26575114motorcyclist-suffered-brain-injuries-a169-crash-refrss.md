A DRIVER'S "momentary lapse of concentration" led to a motorcyclist spending four weeks in a coma and suffering life-changing brain and other injuries, York magistrates decided.

Thomas Metcalfe, 35, was driving a Peugeot SUV on the A169 near Hole of Horcum when he hit the biker, said Kathryn Walters, prosecuting.

The biker spent months in hospital following the crash on the Saturday of the August Bank Holiday weekend in 2024.

For a time, his parents had to give him 24-hour care, alternating 12 hours each day and night and he still needs regular therapy and other treatment.

He suffered collapsed lungs as well as nine bleeds on the brain, and fractures to his neck, spine and ribs.

"I am now a brain injury survivor and have to live with the consequences of someone else's actions," he said in a personal statement.

He added the crash had completely changed his life and that of his family.

Ms Walters said the CPS case was that Metcalfe was driving too close to two motorcyclists, one of whom was the victim, and didn't slow or brake.

Ghaz Iqbal, for Metcalfe, denied that he was tailgating.

Traffic, which had been going at 35mph, had slowed down and "he didn't slow down quickly enough," said Mr Iqbal.

"It was an accident and it was caused by Mr Metcalfe."

The injuries were not caused by the initial crash but the immediate aftermath during which the motorcyclist crashed into the second motorcycle, said the defence solicitor.

Another vehicle was also involved.

After watching dashcam footage from a vehicle travelling the other way and reading the statement of the driver immediately behind Metcalfe, magistrates decided the incident had been caused by a "momentary lapse of concentration" and not tailgating.

Metcalfe, of Holey Green Road, Halifax, pleaded guilty to causing serious injury by careless driving when driving north on the A169.

Magistrates gave him a 12-month community order with 100 hours' unpaid work, banned him from driving for two years and ordered him to take an extended driving test before driving alone again.

He must also pay a £114 statutory surcharge and £85 prosecution costs.

Mr Iqbal said Metcalfe may never drive again.

Minutes after the crash, he had an epileptic fit, possibly caused by the crash, and would have to pass a medical test before getting a new licence.

"No sentence will take away the guilt Mr Metcalfe is living with," he said.

"If he could drive, he wouldn't want to drive. He wants to apologise directly with (the biker) and say 'I am very, very sorry'"

Metcalfe had lost his marriage and his home as a result of the crash.

He had no previous convictions and a clean licence.
