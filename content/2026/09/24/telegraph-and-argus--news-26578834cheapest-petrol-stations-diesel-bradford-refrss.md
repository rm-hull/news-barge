Diesel prices are nearing record highs, renewing calls for a further cut in fuel duty to relieve pressure on Bradford drivers.

The average price of diesel has climbed to 196.1p per litre, just shy of the all-time high of 199.1p recorded in July 2022, while petrol now averages 172.4p per litre.

The AA is urging the Government to extend and increase the current fuel duty cut to support small businesses and rural drivers.

## Small businesses in Bradford under pressure as diesel prices rise

Luke Bosdet, road fuel price spokesman for the AA, said: "Every 6p increase in the pump price of fuel includes an extra penny in VAT for the Treasury.

"Petrol currently averages around 172p a litre, with diesel above 196p.

"This time last year, they averaged 135p and 142p respectively.

"The extra 37p on petrol therefore includes a 6p-a-litre boost to VAT receipts, while diesel adds 9p a litre to the tax haul."

Mr Bosdet argued that diesel users deserve additional relief as prices approach previous record levels.

He said: "There would be a good argument for diesel getting further relief as it nears the July 2022 all-time record of 199.1p a litre.

"While many big businesses pass the extra cost of diesel onto their customers, often via a sliding scale of fuel surcharges, smaller businesses don’t automatically.

"These include taxi drivers, craftspeople, domestic repairs, vets, farriers, and many who rely on vehicles to look after their customers."

He also stressed the impact on rural and lower-income communities.

Mr Bosdet said: "Added to that, diesel is heavily used by rural communities who already face big road fuel bills because of the longer distances they have to drive.

"Often with lower wages, they need all the help they can get with road fuel costs."

The Government currently has a 5p-per-litre reduction in fuel duty, equivalent to 6p per litre at the pump once VAT is included.
