A NEW audio documentary is bringing the story of a Victorian poet back into the spotlight.

The series, produced by the University of Leeds’ Cultural Collections, explores the life and legacy of John Nicholson, a working-class writer from Bingley.

Titled Death of a Poet, the documentary retraces Nicholson’s steps across Bingley, Eldwick, Bradford and Saltaire, and explores how his verses continue to resonate.

Dr RJ Wade, exhibitions curator and presenter, said: "What began as a small exhibition in the university’s Treasures of the Brotherton Gallery, sparked by an intriguing conversation about a plaster bust in the Tony Harrison Archive, has grown into an expansive and emotional sonic expedition connecting two of West Yorkshire’s most irrepressible poets."

Nicholson, who died in 1843 after falling into the River Aire while intoxicated, was known for his spontaneous poetry, working-class background, and colourful life.

A crowd of 1,000 reportedly attended his funeral, and Queen Victoria is said to have supported his widow and children.

The documentary features contributions from poets, artists, actors, historians, and a direct descendant.

Season one is currently being released weekly via the University of Leeds Cultural Collections & Galleries’ Gloves Off podcast platform.
