Calls have been made to demolish two neighbouring buildings blamed for holding up the long-awaited Stack development in Bishop Auckland.

The proposed food, drink and entertainment venue on Newgate Street has been stalled for more than a year because of structural issues involving adjoining properties.

Speaking during a discussion on the future of the project, Bishop Auckland MP Sam Rushworth said compulsory purchase powers could be used to acquire and clear the affected buildings.

He said: "Stack isn't being built right now because the two buildings next door are deemed unsafe.

"For about £150,000, you could probably compulsory purchase those buildings, clear it, either make public realm or give more space to Stack.

It is possible if we had people with the ambition to get together and make it happen."

![]()Sam Rushworth MP. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The comments come as frustration continues to grow over delays to the development, which was approved in 2023 and had been expected to play a key role in efforts to revitalise the town centre.

Work on the Newgate Street scheme was halted in March 2025 because of structural problems involving neighbouring buildings and rising construction costs.

The development is planned for the former WHSmith site and would feature a range of bars and street food vendors centred around an open communal seating area.

![]() *(Image: Stuart Boulton/NORTHERN ECHO)*

However, questions remain over who would pay for any demolition work and whether public money should be used to remove the neighbouring properties.

During the discussion, Cllr John Kinvig said the condition of the buildings presented a challenge but warned there were significant financial and legal considerations.

He said: "The occupants say, well, it hasn't changed in the last six months, therefore it should be okay.

"It might not be okay. So in order to build STACK, we probably need to demolish that.

"Who's demolishing it?

"Whose money are we using to do that?

"What's in it for the council? We have to be accountable for this money.

"And that's on the portfolio holders on the cabinet to work out if it is a viable option for us to do."

Durham County Council said it understood residents were eager to see the scheme completed and confirmed it was continuing to work with Stack and neighbouring property owners to find a solution.

Cllr Joe Quinn, the council's cabinet member for planning, investments and assets, said: "We appreciate that residents in Bishop Auckland are excited for the Stack development to be completed and this is a project we have heavily invested in as part of the multi-million pound regeneration programme being delivered in the town.

"We are working positively with Stack and the owners of the neighbouring properties to ensure any issues are resolved quickly, but we are also prepared to use statutory powers if they are required for the development to progress."
