A FIRST step in the regeneration of Skipton's struggling market is being taken by town councillors.

Councillors agreed to paying consultants to identify funding streams likely to look favourably on a major overhaul of the four-day a week market.

Just ten years ago, it was named 'best small outdoor market' by the National Association of British Market Authorities.

But last week's full council meeting heard from three traders who together have clocked up around 100 years on the market.

All three stressed how the market was on a downward spiral and how its fortunes were intrinsically linked to the prosperity of the town.

Councillors almost unanimously agreed to paying £4,000 to consultants IntoPlaces to develop a 'route map' and to identify likely funders, such as the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

Trader Mark Howard told the meeting held at Craven College: "The expectations of the public have changed, and if we don't change, we will lose them.

"Footfall in the town will go down and it will have an effect on the shops and the cafes.

"We must work together to build a strong and busy town which will benefit all retailers, cafes, pubs and shops."

He added: "What is in front of you tonight is the first practical step to unlock major investment.

"InterPlaces would produce a report that will show us how to unlock money from the Government so we can make it a lot better place and revitalise the town centre.

"The town is struggling and where I stand just down from Skipton Building Society, there is just one shop left at the moment.

"We don't want that to spread all round Skipton.

"If the market improves, the shops will be let, there will be no empty spaces and it should be better for Skipton itself. We need to act now."

Trader Mohammed Saghir said he had been working on the market for 40 years, had been on various council committees over the years, but this was the first time serious steps were being taken to improve the market.

Kimberly Howard, one of the younger traders on the market, said: "For young people like me, Skipton Market should be the best place to start a business.

"Low cost, great footfall, but right now, it feels a little bit stuck. It needs investment, it needs vision so that young people think 'that is where I want to be'.

"Invest in us, and we will invest back in Skipton."

Councillor Peter Madeley, chair of the council's market and events committee, said shopping habits had changed and there was no going back.

He said: "I have been working to improve the market over many years and I have kept on banging the drum for investment.

"We cannot carry on as we have been doing. Over the years, the market has got quieter, it is nobody's fault, its not the council's fault, it is just the way that shopping has changed.

"Our market has not changed with it."

Over the years, he said he had watched it decline.

"It is a shame that we haven't done anything. But, we have a chance now to look at it. This is the first small step," he said.

He added he and the chief officer had visited two markets which had been regenerated as a result of work carried out by InterPlaces.

"We are looking for the survival of this market, to change it to make sure it has a long term future," said Cllr Madeley.

In its proposal for Skipton Market, IntoPlaces says without a clear vision and coordinated approach, there is a 'real risk' that the market will continue to decline.

In a report to the council, it says the decline of the market will have 'wider consequences for the town centre's economic vitality and Skipton's regional appeal'.

However, it adds: "There is a significant opportunity to reposition the market as a modern, vibrant destination that supports the town's wider regeneration ambitions and remains central to its future success."

![]()Skipton Market *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

Initial work carried out by the consultants will establish a 'clear direction of travel and provide the evidence needed to attract further external investment for detailed consultation, business planning and market development'.
