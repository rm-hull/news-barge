Department fined $140,000 after child’s hands caught fire at Questacon

The government department that overseas Questacon has been fined $140,000 for work health and safety failures after a child suffered serious burns from an exhibit at the science centre.

The Department of Industry, Science and Resources (DISR) pleaded guilty in the ACT to a single charge, admitting it exposed visitors to a risk of death or serious injury. Comcare’s investigation into the matter found a nine-year-old child visiting the centre in 2022 had applied alcohol-based hand sanitiser provided by Questacon.

The child then touched a plasma globe in a gallery before their hands caught fire, causing second degree burns to their wrists, hands and fingers.

DISR admitted it had failed to comply with its safety duties under the Work Health and Safety Act and should have taken other measures to eliminate or minimise risk.

Colin Radford, the CEO of Comcare, said in a statement:

This was not an isolated event. The child’s injuries marked the sixth safety incident in five months involving the globes. …

Questacon’s response to these incidents was inadequate. It’s a large, interactive facility with inherent workplace health and safety risks that require a proactive approach and constant vigilance.

11th hour decision to protect endangered birds from SA space project plans welcomed

Conservation groups have welcomed an 11th-hour decision to protect “precious and important habitat” on South Australia’s Eyre peninsula from plans for a rocket launching facility.

The Whalers Way “orbital launch complex”, which could see as many as 42 rockets a year take off from conservation land, had already been approved under federal and state planning and environment laws.

But a recent decision by the state’s independent Native Vegetation Council could scuttle the space project, located about 32km from Port Lincoln.

Eyre Peninsula southern emu-wren. Photograph: Mark Lethlean

The 23-hectare site is protected under a long-standing heritage agreement, a type of covenant on private land designed to protect native wildlife and habitat in perpetuity.

The chief executive of BirdLife Australia, Kate Millar, welcomed the council’s decision to protect “one of the most precious and important habitats in the state”, which was a stronghold for one of Australia’s tiniest birds, the endangered Eyre Peninsula southern emu-wren, and home to 80% of the endangered mallee whipbird population.

Interest rate rise next week only a little less likely after jobless rise

Financial markets are pricing in a greater (but still small) chance the Reserve Bank might leave interest rates on hold after a surprise rise in unemployment.

As we reported earlier, an unexpectedly large number of people started looking for work, pushing up the jobless rate.

The Australian dollar has fallen from US70.36 cents to 70.22, as traders account for the lower chance of an interest rate rise here. Bond markets also dropped a little, predicting lower interest rates in future.

Sharemarkets are up, with the benchmark ASX200 up from 8,656 points to 8,691.3 points, still down from yesterday.

The RBA is still more likely to raise rates than not when its board meets next Tuesday, in part because the labour market is still very strong. Since the end of last year, Australia has added 110,000 full-time jobs and 66,700 more part-time jobs. The workforce now includes a record 14,836,000 people.

The unemployment rate has unexpectedly risen to 4.6%, the highest since November 2021, August data from the Australian Bureau of Statistics shows.

The jobless rate rose both in seasonally adjusted and trend terms, from 4.5% in July.

The rise was mostly due to more people looking for jobs, with an extra 28,000 unemployed Australians in August. An extra 46,000 people took on part-time jobs but 6,000 full-time jobs disappeared.

Markets were expecting a much smaller change in the number of people employed and looking for work, predicting a steady 4.5% unemployment rate.

The higher jobless rate is unlikely to stop the Reserve Bank from raising interest rates next Tuesday. The RBA governor, Michele Bullock, said on Tuesday she thought the jobs market was still tight while unemployment was near 4.5%, adding:

I think between 4.5 and 5 will probably take enough heat out of the labour market that it’ll ease pressure on inflation.

It’s a completely different system, and the two shouldn’t be conflated. It’s a public website. It’s most often used by researchers and academics, who get that aggregated data about benefits statistics, prescribing statistics, to use in their own research.
