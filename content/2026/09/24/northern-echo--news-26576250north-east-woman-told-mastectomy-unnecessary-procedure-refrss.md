A North East woman said she felt "sick to the stomach" after being told she had an unnecessary mastectomy under a scandal-hit NHS trust.

Rachel Gray was 37 when she found a small lump in her left breast and was diagnosed with cancer in January 2022.

She was treated at County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust under Dr Amir Bhatti, then clinical lead for breast surgery, and says she was told a mastectomy was her only option.

Rachel, from Sunderland, had the mastectomy in March 2022 and later underwent breast reconstruction.

After concerns emerged about breast cancer treatment at the trust, she asked for an independent review of her case.

![]()Rachel Gray *(Image: SUBMIT)*

The review found she had not been fully advised of her options and should have been offered therapeutic mammoplasty, which removes the cancer while reshaping the breast.

Rachel, now 41, has received an apology from the trust admitting she was not told about all her treatment options and was wrongly told a mastectomy was required.

She said: "I felt sick to the stomach when the trust told me I'd had my breast removed when it wasn't needed. It's absolutely appalling and unforgivable.

"I wasn't given any choice at all. I was simply told I needed a mastectomy.

"Never once was any other option presented to me.

"Losing a breast is completely life-changing. I hated my body and how I looked after my surgery.

![]()Rachel Gray *(Image: SUBMIT)*

"It was devastating. I had to wear a fake breast and I just lost all of my confidence."

Her case has emerged amid a major investigation into breast cancer services at CDDFT.

The trust said last week that harm had been identified in 321 of 539 breast cancer cases reviewed since January 2023.

One patient is known to have died and 20 women have so far been identified as having undergone an unnecessary mastectomy.

The trust board is considering extending the review back to 2015, when an independent review said systemic failings first emerged.

Around 4,500 patient cases could then be examined.

Rachel joined the patient support group Pink Justice Network after concerns about the trust became public and now wants the review extended.

![]()Rachel Gray *(Image: SUBMIT)*

She said: "I think every woman's treatment should be investigated now.

"Everybody deserves answers. There may be many women who have since passed away who were badly let down and harmed. Their loved ones deserve to know."

Solicitor Hayley Collinson of Hudgell Solicitors, who represents Rachel and other affected patients, said the scale of harm meant the review should go further.

She said: "Rachel was given the impression that she'd benefitted from the exact treatment she needed to make her cancer-free, yet she was in fact harmed to the extent that she lost a breast which could have been saved.

"Given the scale of harm revealed so far, we can't see how the trust board can come to any other decision than to extend this lookback investigation."

![]()Rachel Gray *(Image: SUBMIT)*

An independent governance review published last year found "systemic failings" in the trust’s breast surgery service and concerns about oversight.

Dr Bhatti was restricted from performing breast cancer surgery by the trust last year.

The Pink Justice Network is calling for a public inquiry into breast cancer treatment at the trust.

Her comments come as the trust has issued a fresh apology in board papers prepared for an extraordinary public meeting today (September 24).

The County Durham and Darlington NHS Foundation Trust said: “We acknowledge that, during the period under review, the standards our patients had a right to expect were not consistently met and that failures in care, governance and oversight caused distress, uncertainty and harm for some patients and their families.

“We offer our sincere apologies to everyone affected and remain deeply sorry for the impact this has had.

“We recognise their lasting impact and remain committed to learning from the past, building on the significant improvements already made, and ensuring patients receive the safe, compassionate and high-quality care they deserve.”
