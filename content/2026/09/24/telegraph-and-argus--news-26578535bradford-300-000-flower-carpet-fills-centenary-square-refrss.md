VOLUNTEERS are hard at work creating a giant flower carpet in the heart of Bradford.

The UK debut of the internationally acclaimed Flower Carpet project is now on display in Centenary Square until September 27, transforming the public space into a living artwork made from more than 300,000 dahlia flower heads.

Teams started the pain-staking process at 6am this morning after the flowers arrived in a refrigerated truck from Belgium.

Measuring 29 metres across, the floral installation has been designed by Bradford artist Shiraaz Ali and draws inspiration from the city’s textile heritage and diverse communities.

The event marks the first time the Flower Carpet has been staged in the UK, thanks to a partnership with Tapis de Fleurs de Bruxelles, the team behind the world-famous displays in Brussels.

![]()Bradford's flower carpet is made up of 300,000 individual blooms *(Image: Anna Dyson-Clarke)*

Visitors can watch the carpet being assembled and enjoy four days of free public viewing.

Each evening, the carpet will be illuminated with a bespoke sound and light show.

Family-friendly flower-themed activities will also be available throughout the weekend.

The display is part of West Yorkshire’s Nature Season and is expected to be one of Bradford’s most eye-catching attractions of 2026.

![]()Volunteers working on the flower carpet today *(Image: Anna Dyson-Clarke)*

Once the exhibition ends, all flower heads will be composted locally.

The event aims to create a lasting and environmentally friendly legacy for the city.

Set against the backdrop of Bradford City Hall, the Flower Carpet offers a rare opportunity to enjoy a world-class art experience in Yorkshire.

![]()Volunteers working on the flower carpet today *(Image: Anna Dyson-Clarke)*

First popularised in Belgium in the 1970s, Flower Carpets have become major international attractions.

This inaugural UK edition celebrates Bradford’s industrial history and contemporary identity through vibrant colour and design.

The display will naturally evolve over its short lifespan as the flowers respond to the weather.
