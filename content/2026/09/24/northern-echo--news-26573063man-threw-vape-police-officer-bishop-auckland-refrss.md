A man threw a vape at a police officer who tried to arrest him after a domestic incident.

Police went to a property in Bishop Auckland after a report of a domestic dispute, on Wednesday, July 29.

Peterlee magistrates were told an officer approached Michael Leslie Dougall and said he was under arrest.

The defendant turned and ran, throwing an object towards the pursuing officer as he did so.

![]()Man who threw vape at police officer given 18-month conditional discharge and not ordered to pay compensation, as the impromptu missile missed the constable, in question

Prosecutor Michael Embleton said the officer did not know what had been thrown but confirmed it narrowly missed him.

The 42-year-old, of no fixed abode, but formerly of The Oval, St Helen Auckland, admitted assaulting a constable in the execution of their duty.

Elizabeth Dunbar, in mitigation, told the court the item thrown at the officer was a vape.

“There was an incident for which he wasn’t charged and he was asked to stay where he was.

“But as he began to run away, he simply threw what was in his hand towards the officer.

“He had been drinking prior to this, but not at the time.”

Mrs Dunbar said Dougall has been addressing the “underlying issue” since the incident because he, “did not think straight or behave particularly well in drink”.

She said Dougall had been referred to Alcoholics Anonymous and attended several meetings to tackle his drinking.

Mrs Dunbar said the incident was an impulsive reaction to the break-up of a relationship and Dougall has since received help from family and friends.

“He’s someone who recognised he has a problem and has pleaded guilty at the earliest opportunity.

“He’s not only recognised he has a problem but has taken steps to address it.”

Mrs Dunbar said the former labourer hopes to recover and return to work.

She added that he has been out of trouble with the courts since 2019.

Magistrates’ chair Sian Allan-Gilbert told Dougall he had pleaded guilty early and appears to be taking active steps to address his problems.

The Bench, therefore, imposed an 18-month conditional discharge and Dougall was told if he stays out of trouble, “that will be the end of it.”

Mrs Allan-Gilbert added: “Hopefully, that will help to keep you on the straight and narrow.”

Dougall was ordered to pay £111 in costs and a court surcharge, but magistrates awarded no compensation because the vape missed the officer.
