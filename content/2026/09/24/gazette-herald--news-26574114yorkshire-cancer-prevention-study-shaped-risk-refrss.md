A GROUND breaking cancer prevention study is being shaped by the voices of those most at risk.

More than 1,300 people in Yorkshire with inherited cancer risk will take part in the research, which focuses on BRCA1, BRCA2 and PALB2 gene variants that increase the likelihood of developing cancers such as breast, ovarian, prostate and pancreatic.

The study explores whether PARP inhibitors could help reduce cancer risk in people with certain genetic variants.

Beth Elias, 37, from Bradford, who carries the BRCA2 variant, said: "We’d all gone through the process together, but when we opened the letters, both my sisters tested negative and I tested positive for the BRCA2 variant.

"It was a huge shock.

"Suddenly it felt like it was just me."

She admitted the knowledge gives her some control, but the decision-making process around treatment options is overwhelming.

She said: "Surgery is a massive decision.

"You’re talking about weeks where you can't drive, can't work and can't pick up your children from school."

Dr Stephanie Archer, associate professor at the University of Cambridge, said the study would provide crucial insights to shape future trials.

She admitted: "Those insights will be crucial for designing any future prevention trials."
