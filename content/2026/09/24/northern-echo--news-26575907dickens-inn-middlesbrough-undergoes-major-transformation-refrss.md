A legendary Teesside bar that hosted Middlesbrough FC promotion parties and generations of big nights has had a dramatic makeover.

The Dickens Inn on Southfield Road, Middlesbrough, has been transformed inside and out.

Inspired by the Dickens Inn near London’s Tower Bridge, the bar opened in 1993 and quickly became a Teesside nightlife fixture.

Over the years, the venue has also become closely linked with Middlesbrough FC.

![]()The Dickens Inn, Middlesbrough. *(Image: MICHAEL LAZENBY)*

After Middlesbrough’s 1995 promotion to the Premier League, striker John Hendrie famously phoned ahead to say the squad would visit after training, with celebrations lasting into the evening.

More than two decades later, another promotion party was held at the bar after Aitor Karanka's side secured promotion to the top flight in 2016, with defender Ben Gibson reportedly leading the players there to celebrate.

Now under the stewardship of Jack Spensley, the venue has undergone what he describes as its most extensive makeover in decades.

Working with Newcastle-based Dakota House of Design, the team replaced the main bar and revamped the entrance, cocktail and coffee bars, kitchen, toilets and stairways.

Sports fans will also notice the return of Sky Sports, with four large screens, a projector and around 30 televisions installed throughout the venue, compared with just three previously.

A new games room with darts and pool has joined a bigger live-music programme and expanded food menu.

Chef Alberto’s new pizza menu is already proving popular with customers.

![]()The Dickens Inn, Middlesbrough, before renovations.

Outside, freelance street artist Glen Malpass has replaced the plain white walls with a striking mural celebrating Teesside landmarks including the Transporter Bridge, Roseberry Topping and the former Middlesbrough Town Hall.

The mural also features Guinness references after the brewer agreed to make The Dickens one of its flagship bars. The remodelled games room now has a self-pour Guinness machine.

Jack said: "From front door to back, we've ripped everything out and given the place a whole new look.

"I've been here 10 years and we've never done anything to this extent.

"But the industry has changed so much and if you don't adapt and change with it, you get left behind.

"I see people walking past with their gaze locked onto the building.

"It's become a focal point, whereas before the white building probably got a bit lost in this university campus. Now it stands out."

The refurbishment has also created more jobs, with the business taking on 10 additional members of staff and increasing its workforce to around 40 people.

"Internally, it's effectively a new place," Jack said.

"Sometimes I'll be sat with a group of friends and I hear people come in and say 'this looks fantastic'.

"Previously, people might come in, have a drink and move on. Now, people come and stay because there's so much going on and the place looks so good."
