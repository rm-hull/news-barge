## Police confiscate £13k worth of illegal cigarettes from York shop

Operation Super Tornado resulted in 13,000 illegal cigarettes being confiscated by police at the 4th Avenue Store *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Thousands of illegal cigarettes have been confiscated after a shop in a York suburb was forced to close by North Yorkshire Police.

The 4th Avenue Store, in Fourth Avenue, was closed after 13,000 illegal cigarettes were taken taken by police on Wednesday afternoon (September 23).

It comes before police concluded its largest operation in the force's history - Operation Super Tornado - which last 24-hours and saw officers deployed across York and North Yorkshire make 64 arrests and seize over a million pound's worth of drugs.

The operation involved officers, PCSOs, specialist units, partner agencies, and neighbouring police forces.




Together, they executed 14 warrants, located four wanted individuals, and seized six weapons.

Officers also recovered 14 vehicles and £3,750 in cash.

A major drugs discovery was made in Northallerton, where a cannabis farm estimated to be worth around £1 million was found.

The operation also included action against organised immigration crime.

![]()Police from numerous units were out in force during Operation Super Tornado on September 23 *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Officers and partners visited 12 high street businesses, resulting in the closure of the 4th Avenue Store.

During these checks, £1,280 was seized from a nail bar as suspected proceeds of crime.

Community engagement formed a key part of the operation.

Officers delivered educational sessions to more than 380 children and engaged the public in conversations about fraud prevention, rural crime, exploitation, and road safety.

In a first for the force, live facial recognition technology was deployed in York.

The system generated 12 positive matches from the watchlist.

This led to one arrest, eight checks on individuals subject to Sexual Harm Prevention Orders, and engagement with two others connected to intelligence alerts.

Officers also took time to explain the technology to members of the public, answering questions and addressing concerns.

Chief Constable Forber said: "Super Tornado Two allows us to bring those efforts together on a much larger scale, increasing our visibility and maximising our impact.

"To those who believe they can bring drugs, violence and criminality into our communities, our message is clear.

"We will come after you, we will disrupt your activities and we will use every tactic available to us to make North Yorkshire a hostile place for criminals.

"And to our communities, thank you.

"The information you provide us is invaluable.

"It helps us identify offenders, safeguard vulnerable people and direct our resources to where they are needed most."
