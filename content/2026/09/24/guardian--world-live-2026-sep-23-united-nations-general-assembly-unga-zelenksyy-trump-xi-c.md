Venezuela’s acting president has told the UN her country is coming back strong after “setbacks” this year and thanked Donald Trump for being willing to resume diplomatic relations after the US military spirited her predecessor out of the country.

“Today, Venezuela is being reborn,” Delcy Rodríguez said in addressing the general assembly for the first time since she came to power.

Rodríguez took over in Venezuela after the US military’s raid in January that captured Nicolás Maduro and brought him to the US to face drug trafficking charges.

This is the first trip to the US by a Venezuelan head of state since 2018, when Maduro travelled to New York to take part in the UN general assembly, the Associated Press reports. He is currently being held not far from the organisation’s headquarters as he awaits trial.

“I do not promise you an easy way forward,” Rodríguez said, saying she was addressing her people.

Rebuilding our national soul will be the priority.

More now on XiJinping saying China and the US should be “partners rather than rivals”. He also said: “The Chinese and US peoples have enjoyed long-standing friendly exchanges, and our interests are deeply intertwined.”

Xi’s written comments – published by state news agency Xinhua – came hours after he was greeting by Donald Trump on arrival in the US ahead their high-stakes talks. Trade, artificial intelligence and the Iran war – including Beijing’s support for Tehran – are expected to be prominent.

The Chinese leader said he looked forward to “in-depth exchanges” with Trump on “major issues concerning our bilateral relations and the global situation”, Xinhua reported.

The countries should “work towards each other to foster a stable relationship characterised by cooperation as the mainstay, competition within bounds, manageable differences and peaceful prospects”, Xi said.

In case you missed this, Delcy Rodríguez earlier celebrated what she called a “historic” meeting with Donald Trump in New York, posing for a smiley photo with him less than nine months after he ordered the abduction of her boss, Nicolás Maduro.

The former foes met at a five-star hotel a few blocks from Trump Tower in Manhattan late on Tuesday on the sidelines of the UN general assembly, Tom Phillips reports. The hotel is about 11km (7 miles) from the Brooklyn prison where Maduro has been held since he was snatched during a special forces raid on Caracas in January.

On the eve of Tuesday’s encounter, a reporter asked Rodríguez if she would be visiting Maduro – under whom she served as vice-president – during her stay in New York. She did not reply.

But she did find time to meet Trump at the luxury Lotte New York Palace hotel, with the get-together seen as a key part of Rodríguez’s push for international legitimacy after years of Venezuela’s diplomatic isolation under Maduro’s dictatorship.

Returning to Venezuela for a moment, interim leader Delcy Rodríguez also told the UN her country would hold elections.

“I want to state this very clearly before this general assembly: there will be elections in Venezuela,” she said, while giving no timeline.

Many Venezuelans have been hopeful that the ongoing US-backed political transition talks between the interim government and parts of the opposition would lead to a democratic election, as AFP reports. But those hopes were dented early this month when Donald Trump – speaking shortly after striking a huge oil deal with Caracas – brushed off the notion of a vote in the near future.

Rodríguez echoed those comments at the time.

Rodríguez replaced president Nicolas Maduro after his ouster by US forces in January, and she has served as a pliant ally to Washington since then.

As mentioned earlier, while addressing the UN general assembly she thanked the Trump administration for its “willingness to resume the path of diplomatic relations and cooperation with Venezuela”.

More on Scott Bessent saying the US and China had agreed to a two-month extension of their trade war truce, which had been due to expire on 10 November.

The deal came after marathon talks between the US treasury secretary and his Chinese counterpart, vice-premier He Lifeng, in New York on Sunday and in Washington on Wednesday.

The announcement came just as Chinese leader Xi Jinping arrived in the US on Thursday evening for a high-stakes summit with Donald Trump.

“We have agreed today that we will extend what we call the Busan agreement, the economic detente between the two countries that was scheduled to end on November 10th,” Bessent told Fox News.

That is going to be extended until January 10th to give us more time to see what we can do on the economic front.

Chinese vice-premier He Lifeng (left) and US treasury secretary Scott Bessent arriving for their meeting in New York City on Sunday. Photograph: Vincent Alban/Reuters

The truce, which took effect last year, came after Trump struck a deal with Xi. The agreement saw both sides agree to reduce tit-for-tat tariffs and for the US to extend certain tariff exclusions.

Washington had also agreed at the time to suspend responsive actions after an investigation into China’s shipbuilding sector, as AFP reports.

Bessent on Wednesday also flagged the possibility that both sides could reduce tariffs on $30bn of non-sensitive goods.
