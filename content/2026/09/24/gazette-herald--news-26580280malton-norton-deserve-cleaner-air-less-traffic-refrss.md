At Butcher Corner, Malton, in 2025, the concentration of nitrogen dioxide (NO₂) averaged approximately 24 µg/m³ (micrograms per cubic metre of air) more than twice the World Health Organisation’s 2021 target of 10 µg/m³.

North Yorkshire Council can point to the UK legal limit of 40 µg/m³ and declare Malton’s air legally compliant. But does legal mean safe?

Giving evidence to Parliament’s Environmental Audit Committee (on Monday 14 September 2026), England’s Chief Medical Officer, Professor Sir Chris Whitty, warned that air pollution affects people throughout life, from premature births and severe childhood asthma to cardiovascular disease, stroke, dementia, cancers and long-term lung disease.

He added: “The targets are useful as a prompt, but even if we met the UK targets, we should keep going in the areas where we can, because of the very large impact on chronic diseases, acute diseases and child development.”

Professor Anna Hansell, giving evidence to the Committee on 22 April 2026, stated: “We have not been able to find a threshold level beyond which there are no effects of air pollution.”

The question, therefore, is not simply what is legally acceptable, but what is harmful. The evidence is clear: meeting the UK legal limit does not mean the breathable air in Malton and Norton is harmless. Professor Whitty’s words apply directly to North Yorkshire Council: “we should keep going in the areas where we can.” You should go further because you can, not stop because the law lets you dodge the bullet. Yours is a moral choice and, I suggest, you have a moral obligation to protect our children from dangerous air pollution.

You can provide new slip roads on/off the A64 at the B1257 Broughton Road. You can build the long-awaited A64 roundabout at Musley Bank. You can properly enforce the 7.5-tonne HGV restriction over Norton level crossing (a no-brainer).

Members, you have another means at your disposal: your vote. You can vote to reduce air pollution and through-traffic in Malton and Norton and, because you can, you should.

The choice is clear: either you invest in the highways infrastructure required to remove HGVs and through-traffic from our towns, or accept responsibility for the harm caused to our children by forcing them to breathe dangerously polluted air.

Cleaner air, less traffic and thriving town centres are not competing ambitions. Malton and Norton deserve all three.

Simon Thackray

Malton
