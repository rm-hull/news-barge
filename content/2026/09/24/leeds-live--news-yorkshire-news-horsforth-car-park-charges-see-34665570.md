# Horsforth car park charges see cars 'now parked in residential streets'

## Horsforth Town Council has urged Leeds City Council to scrap parking charges at Fink Hill car park

Local businesses are struggling after trade took a hit following the introduction of controversial parking charges at a town centre car park, Leeds City Council has been told.

Leeds City Council is facing calls to abolish fees brought in nearly a year ago at Fink Hill car park in Horsforth. Town councillors expressed concern that charges of up to £3.65 a day were putting people off visiting the area.

They also suggested that vehicles were spilling out onto neighbouring residential streets as motorists looked to dodge the fees.

Horsforth Town Council chairman Simon Dowling said: "A lot of cars that would normally be parked in this car park today are now parked in the local residential streets.

"From listening to local businesses, they are also saying that footfall into those areas has drastically decreased since the parking charges have been introduced."

Aiden Goulden, who chairs the town council's planning licensing and traffic committee, said he appreciated that the city council needed to generate revenue from the charges.

He said: "But we feel the impact it's having on the local economy is disproportionate."

In July, the city council's executive board opted to proceed with a review of charges that had been rolled out in Wetherby, Horsforth, Rothwell, Garforth and Guiseley.

A public consultation was subsequently launched, with the findings due to be examined by senior councillors. A Leeds City Council spokesperson said: "Our public consultation has now closed, and we will consider all responses received as part of the review.

"The findings and recommendations will be presented to the council's executive board in due course."

The council additionally committed to examining parking fees in Otley following pressure from Liberal Democrat councillors in the area.

However, no review will be conducted into charges implemented at Roundhay, Temple Newsam, Golden Acre and Middleton parks, as well as Otley Chevin.

The council stated that those fees were funding improved facilities, including enhanced disabled access at Golden Acre.
