WEST Yorkshire is piloting a major new approach to help people with health conditions stay in work.

The region's combined authority is one of more than 200 organisations taking part in the development of Britain's first Workplace Health System.

Under the national Keep Britain Working programme, the system aims to reduce health-related economic inactivity and promote long-term employment.

Sir Charlie Mayfield, who leads the programme, said: "Britain has spent too long treating economic inactivity as a welfare problem.

"It is not.

"Instead, it’s one of the largest and most attainable growth opportunities this country has."

In West Yorkshire, 106,600 people are currently out of work due to long-term sickness.

The new system seeks to join up employers, the NHS, and support services to intervene earlier and prevent unnecessary job loss.

Tracy Brabin, Mayor of West Yorkshire, said: "Too many people are being written off from good work when the right support could help them thrive.

"Supporting people with health conditions and disabilities to access good work isn’t just the right thing to do – it’s key to unlocking talent and building a healthier, wealthier West Yorkshire."

The government is backing the programme with a £3.5 billion employment support package.
