A teenager who punched a taxi driver in the face before robbing him of £30 has narrowly avoided an immediate custodial sentence.

Brandon Moloney booked a taxi in Middlesbrough, then carried out the “unplanned” robbery, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The 18-year-old was arrested on unrelated charges before the taxi driver identified him as the attacker.

Moloney stole £30 from the taxi before fleeing along Holyrood Court in Middlesbrough, the court heard.

In a victim impact statement, the taxi driver said: “This incident has left me feeling extremely upset that I went to work to earn a living and support my family and this male has prevented me from doing so by using violence and stealing money.

“He caused me to feel intimidated and left in a vulnerable position.”

Moloney, of Evenwood near Bishop Auckland, admitted the robbery on June 11.

Moloney has since moved away from Middlesbrough, the court heard.

His lawyer, Gary Wood, said Moloney was a care leaver who was still immature.

He said: “The incident was short-lived and there was the use of a single blow.

“This was not a pre-planned incident.”

Passing sentence, Judge Anthony Dunne said: “People who rob taxi drivers nearly always go to prison and that could easily have happened to you.

The judge gave Moloney a 22-month sentence, suspended for two years.
