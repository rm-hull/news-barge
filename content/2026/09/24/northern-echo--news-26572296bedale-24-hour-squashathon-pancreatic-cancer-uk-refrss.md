Friends of a long-standing North Yorkshire squash player battling pancreatic cancer will take to the court for a 24-hour charity challenge.

Bedale Squash Club will stage a 24-hour squashathon from 7pm on Friday, September 25, to 7pm on Saturday, September 26, in support of member Paul Bell.

Players will rotate on and off court to keep one squash match going for 24 hours.

The event at Bedale Squash Courts will celebrate Paul, a familiar face at the club for years, while raising money and awareness for Pancreatic Cancer UK.

Organisers hope club members, Paul's friends and people across Bedale will take part.

![]()Paul Bell *(Image: SUBMIT/CHARITY)*

Friends describe Paul as a "lover of life, squash, motorcycles, music, friends and family".

A message on the fundraising page set up in his name said: "Paul has always been a quiet man who probably doesn't realise how well liked he is.

"Since his diagnosis, so many people have reached out to me asking how he is and offering support for him and his family.

"Paul's involvement in so many things meant he inevitably had a very wide circle of friends.

"Having known him for many years, I, like many others, have enjoyed motorcycle riding, squash, walking and music with him.

"Always with a smile on his face and always willing to help anyone. Paul truly is one of a kind."

Organisers said Paul's illness showed how quickly pancreatic cancer can affect someone fit and healthy.

They added: "The speed at which this disease takes hold is incredibly scary.

"Paul was a fit man, extremely healthy, and knowing this can happen to him is a scary thought for everyone."

Paul helped plan the squashathon and is pleased it will raise awareness of pancreatic cancer while promoting the squash club he loves.

Organisers said: "After speaking with Paul, he is very happy that not only the cancer charity but also his beloved squash club is getting some exposure too.

![]()Bedale Squash and Racket Club *(Image: SUBMIT)*

"Paul is sure to be hoping we can do it and that our efforts help raise awareness and vital funds."

People are being urged to sign up for a slot on the rota so dozens of players can keep the match going for 24 hours.

A bake sale will run during the event, so people can support it without picking up a racket.

The club said: "Even if you're not a squash player, come along and support the event by having a brew and a bit of cake."

The fundraiser has already raised £839 towards its £1,500 target.

The squashathon will take place at Bedale Squash Courts, DL8 1HA, from 7pm on Friday, September 25, to 7pm the following evening.
