Politico, CNN, MS Now reporters blocked from White House despite judge's ruling

Yesterday evening, after briefly taking the case under consideration, a federal judge ordered the White House to temporarily restore access to CNN, MS NOW, and Politico, the three outlets the president banned from the building via a social media post last week.

White House security may not have gotten the memo, according to Thursday morning reports from the three news outlets involved. All three reported that they had journalists turned away from the White House early in the morning.

Politico wrote this morning that a reporter showing up for work was blocked from entering the White House on Thursday and had his press credentials seized. The alleged denial of entry came after a federal judge instructed the Trump administration last night to allow the news outlets their usual access while their lawsuit against the president over the ban moves forward.

CNN reported that some its staff – including reporter Betsy Klein and a photojournalist – were barred from entering the White House early on Thursday around 6a.m., without receiving a reason for being denied entry. However, the channel said one of its producers gained entry to the building using their pass.

Some CNN staff, however, were unable to gain access to the White House early Thursday morning. CNN reporter Betsy Klein and a photojournalist were denied access to the White House grounds in the 6:00 a.m. hour.

The Associated Press also reported that a CNN journalist told the outlet she was denied access to the White House on Thursday morning.

CNN said would it continue trying to gain access to the building as the day goes on.

MS Now also reported on Thursday morning that one of its journalists had been barred entry.

MS Now White House correspondent Laura Barrón-López said that a member of the secret service informed her that her press credentials were “still coming up as disabled” when scanned. “Then he confiscated my badge, and I had to leave the premises,” she said.

Barrón-López said it was unclear whether the refusal of entry was in deliberate defiance of the court order or “incompetence or a lag in the system.”

Israeli, Palestinian leaders set to address the UN, with Netanyahu expected to attack Mamdani

Taz Ali

The third day of the UN General Assembly’s annual gathering of world leaders is about to kick off in New York City today, with speeches from leaders on both sides of the Israeli-Palestinian conflict.

Benjamin Netanyahu, the prime minister of Israel, is expected to devote at least part of his address to attacking Zohran Mamdani, New York City’s progressive mayor who has labeled Netanyahu a war criminal and threatened to arrest him for his country’s killing of civilians in Gaza.

Michael Oren, the former Israeli ambassador, said he expected Netanyahu’s UN address to resemble a “campaign speech,” leveraging his feud with the mayor of the largest Jewish city outside of Israel to boost his political position at home.

“Maybe he’s thinking, ‘if I can attack Mamdani and attack the Iranians, somebody who was wavering between ‌voting for me and voting for (Gadi) Eisenkot may decide to vote for me’,” Oren told Reuters, referring to Netanyahu’s main political rival in next month’s Israeli general election.

In a video statement earlier this week, Netanyahu falsely accused Mamdani, New York City’s first Muslim mayor, of supporting Hamas and inciting riots against the city’s Jewish population.

Mamdani said earlier this year that he was looking into whether his office could arrest Netanyahu, who is wanted by the International Criminal Court (ICC) over alleged war crimes in Gaza, if he visited New York. But he later conceded that he did not have the legal authority to do so. The US is not a party to the ICC and does not recognize its authority.

Despite his criticism of the Israeli government and staunch support for Palestinian rights, Mamdani is reportedly not taking part in the protests against Netanyahu scheduled to take place in New York. The New York Times reported that he will instead be visiting an LGBTQ+ centre with the prime minister of the Netherlands.

The president of the Palestinian Authority, Mahmoud Abbas, will also be addressing the UN General Assembly via video. Like last year, Abbas was banned from entering the US by the Trump administration, with the State Department saying in a statement that the Palestinian Authority and the Palestine Liberation Organisation (PLO) had “failed to live up to their commitments.”

The news has been trickling in this morning from the White House reporters showing back up for work, but now it’s confirmed that all three outlets targeted in Trump’s White House press ban are saying they’re still being denied access to the building, after a judge’s order to let them back in.

Ahead of talks with Xi, Trump points to AI as major subject

President Trump teased in a social media post this morning that artificial intelligence would be a major part of his talks today with Chinese leader Xi Jinping during his state visit to Washington.

He also suggested that he would avoid entertaining any kind of agreement between the two nations limiting AI development.

“A big day with President Xi of China,” Trump wrote in the post to his personal social media platform. “Super Intelligence (SI) will be a big topic of discussion, but I want to leave it exactly where it is. That is China’s position also. Our guardrail is the DOJ! President DJT.”

(Super intelligence is the term Trump has been pushing to refer to artificial intelligence. He pitched the language earlier this week in a tangent during his speech to the world leaders at the United Nations. It’s unclear at this point if anybody is going to take him up on it.)

After an unusually warm red carpet welcome ceremony on the tarmac yesterday, Trump is scheduled to receive Xi and his wife, Peng Liyuan, at the White House South Portico. The pair will deliver remarks, exchange gifts, and watch a military review before a busy day of talks and diplomatic pageantry.

Donald Trump has been raging about a story first reported by MS Now on Wednesday that he is seeking to rename the Ford Theatre in Washington DC, a site infamous for the assassination of president Abraham Lincoln, after himself.

Last night he posted on Truth Social about the news item, blaming “Dumocrats” for the “ridiculous lie.”

This morning, hours after the late night ruling from the judge that temporarily reinstated CNN, Politico, and MS Now, Trump (again) said:

The Dumocrats make up lie after lie. The new one is that I want Ford’s Theater named after me. That’s where Abraham Lincoln was assassinated. Who would want that? I don’t! It is FAKE NEWS started by MSDNC AND CNN.

This is his first mention of the day of his lesser-favored media outlets. He has not acknowledged the judge’s ruling to restore them access to the White House – yet. We’ll bring you more lines as they come in.

Some more background information on this ongoing situation between the Trump administration and the White House press correspondents:

The three outlets sued Trump and other members of his administration in federal court in Washington on Monday, saying the ban violated the US constitution’s first amendment protections for free speech and a free press, as well as their due process rights. They requested a temporary restraining order that would immediately reinstate their White House access while their legal challenge plays out.

Trump had said on social media that the three outlets “shouldn’t be ⁠able to constantly write or ​report ​FICTION and LIES,” ​and justice department lawyers ​had ‌sought to ​justify ​the ban on national security grounds.

A Justice Department lawyer told the judge the Trump administration provided a sufficient explanation for the revocation of the ​press passes in letters sent to the news outlets ​on Tuesday. But the judge signaled skepticism of that argument, ​noting that the letters were sent after the revocation occurred and the lawsuit was filed.

In a legal filing on Tuesday, the ​Justice Department argued that access to the ‌White House is a ​privilege, not an entitlement, and that the ​president has the authority to suspend access by news organizations.
