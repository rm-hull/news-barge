Sixty-four people have been arrested in a North Yorkshire Police operation said to be one of the biggest in the force’s history.

Weapons and cash were also seized during Operation Super Tornado Two, 24-hours of action across York and North Yorkshire.

And for the first time the force deployed controversial Live Facial Recognition technology in York, which resulted in one arrest.

One major part of the operation centred on Fourth Avenue, as ***YorkMix*** reported yesterday.

Dozens of officers descended on the street, and they closed the Fourth Avenue Shop.

A North Yorkshire Police spokesperson said: “As part of activity targeting organised immigration crime, officers and partners visited 12 high street premises, with the activity resulting in the closure of one premises in York linked to the sale of illegal tobacco.

“During these visits £1,280 was seized as suspected Proceeds of Crime from a nail bar. And 13,000 illegal cigarettes were also seized.”

Police described the operation as “a day of action which saw teams deployed across York and North Yorkshire to target serious and organised crime and those causing the greatest harm to our communities”.

They say:

* 64 arrests were made
* 14 warrants were executed
* four wanted people were located
* six weapons were seized
* and 14 vehicles and £3,750 in cash recovered.

“Significant quantities” of drugs were also seized, including a cannabis cultivation in Northallerton with an estimated street value of around £1 million.

## Facial recognition

For the first time, the controversial Live Facial Recognition Cameras were deployed by the force, on Whip-ma-whop-ma-gate and Davygate.

The technology, which scans people’s faces and compares them to a police database, will help to identify wanted criminals, the force has said.

But human rights charity Liberty told us the technology is “deeply intrusive” and treats “everyone as a potential suspect”.

North Yorkshire Police said: “The system generated 12 positive matches to the individuals on the watchlist.

“As a result, officers made one arrest, stopped and checked eight individuals subject to Sexual Harm Prevention Orders, and engaged with two people linked to intelligence-related alerts.

“Officers also spoke with members of the public throughout the operation, explaining how the technology works, why it was being used, and answering questions from those passing by.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/north-yorkshire-police-operation-super-tornado-two-23-sept-2026-nyp-2.webp)

**Chief Constable Tim Forber from North Yorkshire Police said:** “Operation Super Tornado Two is about taking the fight directly to those who cause the most harm in our communities.

“For 24 hours, our officers and staff worked around the clock across York and North Yorkshire, making arrests, executing warrants, seizing weapons, drugs and cash, targeting wanted offenders and working alongside our partners to protect vulnerable people.

“Importantly, this operation bolsters the work our officers, staff and partners carry out every single day.”

He added: “To those who believe they can bring drugs, violence and criminality into our communities, our message is clear.

“We will come after you, we will disrupt your activities and we will use every tactic available to us to make North Yorkshire a hostile place for criminals.”
