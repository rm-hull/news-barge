Nearly 90 percent of U.S. citizens now believe government corruption is widespread in Washington, D.C., according to a new poll.

The figure revealed by Gallup’s latest survey, which questioned 1,000 people between May and June, represents a 10-percentage point rise year-on-year and a two decade high. The number had largely held steady between 72 percent and 79 percent from 2010 to 2025.

There was a slight difference between political affiliations, with 83 percent of Republicans saying they considered the federal government corrupt, compared to 90 percent of independents and 91 percent of Democrats.

The perception has spiked most dramatically among the latter group, according to Gallup, which found that only 57 percent of Democrats said the same when Joe Biden was in power in 2024. The figure climbed abruptly to 76 percent under President Donald Trump last year and then again this year.

The U.S. total was higher than that of any of the other 37 countries in the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development.

While former Soviet satellite states once routinely scored above 90 percent, only four other nations have recorded bigger totals since 2023 – Nigeria and Ghana both returned 90 percent in 2024 and Lebanon and Peru chalked up 92 percent in 2024 and 2025 respectively.

The survey also showed 71 percent of respondents consider the U.S. business sector corrupt, an 18-point hike on last year but still 18 points below the low opinion of life in D.C.

“There is a broad base consensus for the fact that something is broken in Washington,” David Szakonyi, a political science professor at George Washington University, told *The Washington Post*, noting a rare point of agreement in a landscape riven by partisan division. 

Democrats have already begun capitalizing on the issue in midterms campaigning, pushing anti-corruption messaging, accusing Trump of “self-dealing” and using the presidency to enrich himself or allies.

![President Donald Trump has been accused of using his office to enrich himself and reward his friends](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/01/14/2026-08-31T194453Z_987284569_RC2V9NAXAD4H_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG)

Trump caused outrage in May when his Department of Justice proposed opening a $1.8 billion compensation scheme for victims of alleged political persecution under Biden, only for the idea to be scrapped amid complaints that it amounted to a “slush fund” designed to reward Trump’s aggrieved supporters.

Politicians on both sides of the aisle have played their part in building up the perception of alleged corruption.

Former New Jersey Democratic senator Bob Menendez, for instance, was sentenced to 11 years in jail in 2024 after being found guilty of 16 felonies including bribery, extortion, wire fraud, obstruction of justice and acting as a foreign agent.

Concern has also been raised about members of Congress playing the stock market to feather their nests.
