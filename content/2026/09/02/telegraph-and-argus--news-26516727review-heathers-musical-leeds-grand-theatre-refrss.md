IF it isn’t already, Heathers The Musical is following in the wake of Richard O’Brien’s The Rocky Horror Show, by rapidly becoming one of theatre land’s handful of ‘cult’ shows.

The merchandise queue ahead of curtain up snaked all the way up to the dress circle and beyond, as groups of teens, dressed in identical costumes to their stage heroines - Heather Chandler, Heather Duke and Heather McNamara, aka ‘The Heathers’ - had cards at the ready.

And yet, for all I loved this very clever show (again!), Kevin Murphy and Laurence O’Keefe’s music and lyrics remained largely unmemorable from five years ago when I first saw it at Leeds Grand Theatre, and I am not convinced that little will change because I'm still struggling to hum a tune in the car on the way home!

As far as the libretto goes, this show is beautifully dark and cocks a snook at all things politically correct, but, somehow, in a way that everyone laughs.

The show is inspired by the 1989 Daniel Waters film, Heathers, starring Winona Ryder, and I remember saying in 2021 that it was a breath of fresh air that would leave society’s ‘snowflakes’ gasping for air.

And, whilst that irreverence is delightfully intact, something has changed: screaming teens in the audience, dressing up and excessive love of the product in front of them. As I said to my guest: “This feels like the show teens are nuts for until they get to uni then they shift to Rocky Horror!”

Westerberg High’s Veronica Sawyer (Gerardine Sacdalan) is a school nobody, desperate to be popular so, when she gets adopted by the glamorous but impossibly cruel trio, The Heathers - Heather Chandler (Liberty Stottor), Heather McNamara (Lou Henry) and Heather Duke (Jessica Ibadin) - she is ecstatic...even though they only want her for her ability to forge signatures and handwriting!

  Liberty Stottor, Gerardine Sacdalan, Lou Henry and Jessica Ibadin 

*(Image: Pamela Raith)*

It satirises everything in its path, including sorority sex, wrapping its near-the-edge naughtiness in a high-octane, black comedy veneer, pushed along by a powerful, energetic rock score.

Liberty Stottor was the nasty piece of work and chief ringleader, Heather Chandler, and, once ‘out of the way’ she returns as a ghost, dishevelled, less beautiful and a permanent conscience on the shoulder of her co-murderer, Veronica Sawyer, a little powerhouse of exuberance and talent.

And, as if they had been watching Keanu Reeves and Alex Winter in Bill and Ted’s Excellent Adventure, the two high school jocks of the piece, Kurt and Ram, were played brilliantly by Markus Sodergren and Beau Jackson.

  Beau Jackson and Markus Sodergren 

*(Image: Pamela Raith)*

It features a hard-working, energetic, young cast, brimming with talent who wring every ounce of musicality from the score and every one liner from the script.

Even resident serial killer, Jason JD Dean (Louis Hearsey), has time to deliver lines that leave you either laughing or wondering whether he is one Hannibal Lecter’s apprentice success stories!

  Gerardine Sacdalan and Louis Hearsey in the lively show 

*(Image: Pamela Raith)*

This is a clever musical - curtain down around 10.10pm so not the shortest offering on the circuit - but, if you’ve got your listening ears on, you can really dig into a clever script and intelligent lyrics.

Well worth an outing but get there early if you want to join the merchandising queue!

* Heathers The Musical runs at Leeds Grand Theatre until Saturday, September 5.
