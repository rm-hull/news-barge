The family of man killed outside a County Durham pub on Christmas Eve by his own son have spoken of their heartbreak.

Steven Shevels suffered a cardiac arrest after he smashed his head on concrete when he was pushed over by Martin Greensmith.

The 61-year-old never regained consciousness and was pronounced dead in the beer garden of the Queen's Head in Willington despite the best efforts of paramedics.

During Greensmith's trial for manslaughter, Teesside Crown Court heard how Mr Shevels had been annoying members of his son’s family, including his mother and his nanna, after sitting next to them.

CCTV cameras inside and outside the pub captured a series of interactions between the pair before the fatal push resulted in Mr Shevels untimely death.

Speaking after the guilty verdict, Steven’s sister Angela said: “My brother Steven was the victim of an unprovoked act of violence that took his life far too soon.

“His passing has left a hole in all our hearts, and a future forever changed for all of his family, friends, and everyone who knew him.”

Greensmith was one of several people who went to help Steven, but he left the pub shortly before emergency services arrived.

He was arrested on Christmas Day after returning to his home in Willington.

Examination of his mobile phone revealed he had spent an hour searching online for solicitors and looking at a page about involuntary manslaughter.
