A Middlesbrough dad says his daughter’s determination to overcome hearing loss has inspired him in life and on the running track.

Ben Taylorson, 45, from Nunthorpe, is preparing for this year’s Great North Run to raise money for a cause close to his heart, because of his daughter Phoebe.

Now 21, Phoebe was born deaf in one ear and with partial hearing loss in the other, and the effects of her hearing loss were often misunderstood when she was growing up.

Ben said: "She would get categorised as naughty quite a lot at school because she would interrupt the teacher - she didn't have the social prompts that the rest of us have.

"At home we took all the practical steps we could to accommodate her deafness. For example, we had the house fitted with the right types of smoke alarms, including a vibrating pad in her bed that could wake her in an emergency."

*(Image: Hearing Dogs for Deaf People)*

He added: "We live in a three-storey house and we very quickly became aware that she couldn't tell where sounds were coming from, so we would always try to make it obvious to her where we were in the house.

"If we were calling her down for a tea for example, we would make a point of telling her we were in the kitchen."

Ben says Phoebe’s hearing loss has never defined her.

"She's a very determined young woman," he said. "She's now living in Australia, working as a groundskeeper for a golf club, and I'm very proud of her."

Ben's admiration for Phoebe is one of the reasons he has become such a passionate supporter of Hearing Dogs for Deaf People - a charity that supports deaf people using trained assistance dogs and other specialist services to help with every day life.

  Hearing Dogs for Deaf People. 

*(Image: Paul Wilkinson)*

Hearing dogs are trained to alert deaf people to sounds including smoke alarms, alarm clocks and intruder alarms, while also providing companionship and emotional support.

This year’s Great North Run will be Ben’s eighth and his fourth fundraiser for the charity.

Running has been a huge part of Ben’s life since he took up the sport 16 years ago to lose weight - becoming an output for him after facing his own health issues with cancer, as well as a serious back injury.

Ben has been training three times a week for the Great North Run, often heading out before his work as a librarian at Durham University.

Ben is looking forward to a particular reward when he completes the 13.1-mile route.

"Picking up that medal at the end and stroking the hearing dog in the tent at the end," he said.

Ben plans to celebrate with a takeaway alongside Phoebe and the rest of the family, perhaps with a full-fat Coca-Cola too.
