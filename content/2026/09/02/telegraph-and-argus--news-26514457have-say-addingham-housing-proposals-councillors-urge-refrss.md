THREE ward councillors are urging residents to comment on plans to build 55 homes on land in the Bradford district.

A planning application seeks full planning permission for the Addingham development, together with a new access from Main Street, internal roads, parking, landscaping, public open space and drainage works.

The proposed development would be on Green Belt land adjoining Addingham Cricket Club. The entire site is also within Addingham Conservation Area.

Councillors Jane Sellers, Richard Downey and Andrew Loy are encouraging residents to examine the plans and submit their views to Bradford Council before the consultation closes on Wednesday, September 16.

They say residents may particularly wish to consider the potential impact on the conservation area, the cricket ground and its setting, important local views, traffic, drainage, wildlife and local infrastructure.

Comments should explain clearly which aspects of the application residents support or oppose and the planning reasons for their views.

Cllr Jane Sellers said: “Building 55 houses on these open fields would fundamentally change the eastern approach to Addingham and the rural setting of the village, and I will strongly oppose this application.

“Bradford Council’s own Conservation Area Assessment says that ‘the cricket ground and fields to the south of Main Street are designated as Green Belt and incremental development should be resisted. The views across this area towards Moorside, Ilkley Moor and into the village are important and enhance the rural setting.’ The cricket ground is a cherished part of community life in Addingham, and I do not believe this is the right place for a large housing estate.”

Cllr Richard Downey said: “I have very serious concerns about the scale and location of this proposal. This is not a small infill development - it would place 55 houses on open land within the conservation area and immediately beside the cricket ground.

“The Addingham Neighbourhood Plan identifies the view from the cricket ground across these fields as important, with the old stone barn acting as a focal point. I encourage residents to examine the application and ensure that Bradford Council understands the nature and extent of local concerns.”

Residents can view and comment on the proposals through Bradford Council’s online planning portal by searching for application 26/02592/MAF.

The consultation deadline is Wednesday, September 16, 2026.
