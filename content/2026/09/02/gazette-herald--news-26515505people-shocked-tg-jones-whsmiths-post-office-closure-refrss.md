PEOPLE in York have shared their shock after a long-running shop and the main Post Office for the city revealed that they will be closing their doors next month.

TG Jones, formerly WHSmith, has announced that it will be shutting its Coney Street shop on October 19 – with banners for a sale appearing in the windows of the city centre location.

As reported by The Press, this follows news that the brand has secured court approval for a major restructuring, which is expected to lead to the closure of around 150 stores nationwide and affect hundreds of jobs – including York.

Representatives for the Post Office added that they 'want to open a replacement branch as soon as possible', but that shoppers may be waiting up to six months for the re-opening.

One shopper - Annie Donaghy – who visited TG Jones on the morning of the announcement, said that she was disappointed to see the shop closing, but not surprised after the rebrand from the WHSmiths name.

She said: “We'd always come here in September before schools started, but they’ve changed the name of it and since then, it’s all been a bit confusing. I wasn’t surprised though – everything is shutting now.

“The Post Office closing is really bad though, we were talking about it this morning, because I think a lot of the older community of York really rely on it as well.

“It’s also going to affect the businesses down this street, I used to work in a shop down the road, so I would come in and do all the cash on the night - it's not great for the high street.”

  Linda said that she feels the closure reflects badly on the state of the city centre 

*(Image: Alice Kavanagh)*

Also concerned about the state of Coney Street was Linda Storey – adding that the closure, combined with the empty former TK Maxx shop opposite (which is currently under renovation), reflected badly on the state of the city going forward.

She said: “What message does this send to people? It definitely isn’t a good one. Everything has been taken out of York and the city centre is getting shoddier and shoddier.

“I live in the city centre – nothing here is improving and everything is going downhill. I think the council should think about the residents who live here and not just the tourists as we’re so reliant on them.

“Everyone pays a large price to live here, but we don’t get return on the money."

  Andrew said he's concerned the unit could become more flats 

*(Image: Alice Kavanagh)*

Multiple people visiting the shop on Wednesday morning (September 2) added that they were shocked to see the retail outlet, which has been a fixture on the high street for decades, go – especially with the loss of the Post Office sbranch as well.

Shopper Andrew said: “I’m very shocked to see it go and I think it’s sad – it’s on the main thoroughfare and it’s got a good footfall.

“The Post Office going is another thing that shouldn’t happen, we won’t have a post office now that it moved in here from Lendal. I don’t know what’s going to happen.

“I’m worried it’ll become luxury flats – which we don’t want, we want affordable housing. I think the whole thing is disgraceful.”

  Signs on the glass doors at one end of the shop 

*(Image: Alice Kavanagh)*

Gareth, another shopper, added: “It’s a triumph of Tory privatisation.”

Only one shopper, Pauline from Poppleton, said that the closure was expected – adding that she had ‘seen it coming in a way’, having recently visited Monmouth and its branch of TG Jones as it prepared to shut.

She said: “It’s sad, but since WHSmiths changed its name, people don’t seem to use it. I don’t use this Post Office regularly as we’ve got one in the village, but I can see how it’s so useful.

"When I saw the shop in Wales close, I knew we had to make use of them. The only bright side of the closure is that I’m an artist, so the sale has been good to get lots of new paper – which is nice.”
