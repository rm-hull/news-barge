Plans for a new beer garden in the heart of York have been criticised by another bar operator.

As ***YorkMix*** revealed in July, the owners of Viking-themed pub Valhalla want to turn the courtyard behind the St Sampson’s Centre into an al fresco drinking space.

It’s opposite Valhalla on Patrick Pool, and the proposal is a partnership between that pub and the charity that runs St Sampson’s.

Now a licensing application for the courtyard is set to go before York councillors for a decision.

Valhalla says it would see the beautiful, spacious but under-utilised courtyard put to use to raise income for St Sampson’s Centre which owns the space.

But a licensed premises opposite Valhalla in front of the courtyard has objected, saying its fire escape exited onto the courtyard and claimed it could be an operational nightmare.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/st-sampsons-york-beer-garden-5.webp)

Pivni is in front of the courtyard and the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS) tried to contact them to confirm whether they were behind the objection but they did not respond.

It comes ahead of a City of York Council licensing hearing on the application on Monday (7 September) after it was lodged in July.

Signs put up a the entrance to the courtyard stated its opening times were 12pm to 11pm on Friday and Saturday and until 10pm on Sunday.

The area would be licensed for alcohol sales from 11am to 11pm every day if councillors approve the application.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/valhalla-beer-garden-entrance-york-ldrs.webp)

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/valhalla-beer-garden-entrance-signs-york-.webp)

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/valhalla-beer-garden-entrance-pivni-york-.webp)

Valhalla’s application stated it was a family-friendly venue which had been run to high standards since it opened in 2017.

It added the beer garden would help support the work of St Sampson’s Centre which offers help and activities to elderly people and others facing loneliness and isolation.

The application says: “As neighbours Valhalla and St Sampson’s Centre have been in discussion for some considerable time about how the two venues can work together and support each other.

“The area shall operate with a fully self-contained outdoor bar including toilets which will be operated during the busy times of the week and year.

“During these times the area shall be constantly staffed and all drinks will be served from the self-contained bar within the licenced area.”

Co-owner of Valhalla, Vincent Roberts, told ***YorkMix*** it would be a “fantastic” new space.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/st-sampsons-york-beer-garden-6.webp)

North Yorkshire Police and the council’s public protection team did not object to the bar’s application.

But a redacted objection from the premises which said it had operated from the Grade II*- listed building in front of the courtyard stated the plans could cause several problems.

It added potential issues included noise from customers shouting and singing, people congregating in the small street between the bars, overcrowding and smoking and vaping affecting neighbouring properties.

The objector stated: “Operationally I am seriously concerned regarding the interaction and friction between our staff and customers and the customers of Valhalla.

“This will become a nightmare for our staff and undoubtedly cause friction between customers.

“Valhalla customers will undoubtedly enter into our building unknowing that we are a separate business, our staff will have to manage this.”
