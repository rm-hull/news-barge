Proposals to cut some parking charges in York have been unveiled following a review of hikes which provoked a backlash.

Plans drawn up by York Council would see the cost of Minster Badges for residents reduced to £15-a-year and discounts for parking in community areas increased to 50 per cent.

Council Labour transport spokesperson Cllr Kate Ravilious said she hoped the proposals would allow the authority and those who objected to parking rates move forward and work more closely together.

But she added a recommendation to explore measures for some car parks such as limited periods of free parking and half-hour tariffs had been ruled out.

It comes as a council report stated city centre footfall and spending was lower in 2025 and 2026 than previous years, according to York Business Improvement District (BID) data.

But it added it was impossible to say how much responsibility could be borne by parking charges versus the effects of national and international factors which have dampened consumer confidence.

Council figures also showed there has been an increase in walking, cycling and the use of local buses and Park & Ride services.

Proposals for the Minster Badge are set to go before the council’s executive for a decision on Wednesday, September 9.

The changes would see the cost of the residents’ permit reduced from the current £45 for two years to £15-a-year.

People who still have a valid badge bought at the existing rate would not be offered a refund.

Discounts for the Bishopthorpe Road, East Parade and Rowntree Park car parks and on-street spaces including in Micklegate and The Groves would rise to 50 per cent from the current 30 per cent.

It would equate to parking charges for Minster Badge holders being cut from £3-an-hour to £1.50-an-hour.

Reduced rates for Minster Badge holders would not apply in city centre car parks run by the council.

Council highways lead Helene Vergereau said the changes would be made in the weeks following the executive meeting once council parking systems have been updated.

A council report stated the changes would cost an estimated £18,000 in lost parking revenue but that could be at least partly offset if more people buy Minister Badges.

It added car park use would likely increase if the changes are made, resulting in more cars on York’s roads though officials expect the rise to be limited.

The forecast comes despite the council’s aim to cut car journeys by a fifth by 2030, which was one of the main reasons why charges were hiked in 2025.

Hikes in 2025 provoked the outcry from residents, traders and opposition councillors which ultimately led to the independent review into their impact in Bishopthorpe Road, Heworth, Micklegate and The Groves.

Charges in the Bishopthorpe Road car park and for on-street parking in Micklegate were later lowered following the backlash.

Parking charges at central council-run car parks and on-street within the inner ring road currently stand at £5-an-hour for the first two from Sunday to Thursdays.

A 10 per cent premium applies Fridays, Saturdays and during events when charges are £5.50-an-hour for the first two.

Consultants Arup found changes made in 2025 followed due process and were broadly in line with similar moves elsewhere in the UK.

But they found the way the hikes were handled did not follow best practice and ultimately fed into the backlash that sparked the legal challenge which led to the review.

The council would carry out informal consultations before any future significant increases to parking charges and publish car parking fee and income figures yearly if the changes go ahead.

Transport Executive Member Cllr Ravilious said the review had been valuable and the council had learnt the lessons from the fallout from the 2025 hikes.

Cllr Ravilious said: “It’s been a long and difficult process for all of us.

“This is about trying to find a balance and accepting that some felt like they needed that balance to be different from what it was.

“It’s always difficult because some people live outside York but consider it their city but we had to draw the boundary somewhere.”
