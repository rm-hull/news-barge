HMRC and other creditors look set to go empty handed following the administration of a well-known North Yorkshire brewery for the second time in less than four years.

A report published on the Companies House website in recent days also says HMRC and creditors are set to lose millions.

As the Press previously reported, the Great British Drinks Company bought Masham-based Black Sheep Brewery as part of a £6.5million deal in January after its parent company, Keystone Brewing Group, filed several notices of intention to appoint administrators towards the end of last year.

The deal saved 145 jobs, the brewery and its brands, including sister companies such Purity Brewing Co. and others.

The Great British Drinks Company is backed by the entrepreneurs behind the Paramount Retail Group, which also owns the Saltaire Brewery.

In April, a report by the administrators FRP Advisory, known as a Statement of Affairs, said the overall Keystone Brewing Group had a total deficiency of more than £20million of outstanding debt.

The progress report now breaks down some details down to the constituent parts of the Keystone Group.

The report says HMRC has a secondary preferential claim of £1,011,074 from Black Sheep for outstanding VAT and PAYE.

However, the report said it is “currently anticipated that there will be insufficient funds to distribute to secondary preferential creditors”, such as HMRC.

The administrators added that trade creditors are owed £2,225,083 from Black Sheep with sister companies owing trade creditors more than £700,000 between them.

Again, the report says it is estimated that there will be insufficient funds for a distribution of any money to unsecured creditors- those who are paid last.

However, the largest creditor of The Keystone Group, the Secure Trust Bank, has so far received £3.3million (including £2,802,000 from Black Sheep) of the £8.6million it was owed. More is to be paid back but it was ‘uncertain’ how much this might be, the report said.

Black Sheep Brewery was founded in 1992 by Paul Theakston, a member of Masham’s Theakston brewing family.

He left his family's business, T&R Theakston, to launch his own independent brewery. It made him the “black sheep” of the family, which gave Paul’s business its name.

However, as previously reported, the Black Sheep Brewery first entered administration in May 2023 before it was sold to London-based Breal Capital- which later became The Keystone Group- for £5million in another pre-pack administration sale.
