A York man’s new book about the “strange death of football” has been praised by a well-known sports writer.

John Wheatcroft’s new book, The Strange Death of Football, contains 11 short stories in which football is the subject or the starting point.

Sports writer and broadcaster Jonathan Wilson said the book is “wise and moving”, describing it as a “beautiful album of short stories that performs the rare feat of using football successfully as a stage, a structuring device and a metaphor”.

Sports broadcaster Gary Imlach said the book is “a heartfelt narrative that's as much about family as football”.

  The Strange Death of Football by John Wheatcroft 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

John, a former Press journalist, said he set out to write a volume of stories using football "to explore universal themes such as love, friendship, family and loss".

It is John’s third book, following novels Here in the Cull Valley (2016) and Rocket Boy (2019), but his first collection of short stories.

“I've enjoyed the discipline of trying to write as economically as possible with a necessarily narrower range of focus,” he said.

John said the book “offers a picture” of what football was like during different stages of its development, from war-time to the modern era.

“The sting in the tail, which explains the title, is a rather gloomy view of football's (and the world's) future although it should perhaps be read as a warning rather than a prediction,” he said. “In any case, many stories are quite upbeat and the protagonists predominantly likeable.”
