There is a "high risk" of domestic abusers reoffending and "harm escalating" if they are released from prison early, a York charity has warned.

IDAS (Independent Domestic Abuse Services) joined domestic abuse survivor Helen Duncanson, from North Yorkshire, in urging ministers to rethink including domestic abusers in the government's early prison release scheme to ease overcrowding in jails.

It comes after Ms Duncanson told The Press she was “deeply concerned” about the plans which could see her abuser, Michael Boyes, 40, of Ruffa Lane, Pickering, released almost halfway through his three-and-a-half-year jail sentence.

  Domestic abuse survivor Helen Duncanson who has urged ministers to rethink proposals to release domestic abusers from jail early 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Former safeguarding minister Jess Phillips voiced support for Ms Duncanson and called for domestic abusers convicted of assault occasioning actual bodily harm, as was the case for Ms Duncanson’s abuser, or grievous bodily harm not to be released from jail early.

The Ministry of Justice said it is working to fix a prison system it inherited “on the brink of collapse” and shares “the public’s anger that these options are even being considered”.

An IDAS spokesperson told The Press that the charity has joined organisations across the sector in signing a letter to the prime minister calling for “greater protection for victims and survivors as part of the government’s early release scheme”.

“We believe the safety of victims and survivors must be at the heart of decisions about releasing perpetrators, with robust risk assessment, effective information sharing, appropriate monitoring and timely notification for those at risk,” they said.

Ms Duncanson said she felt her safety had been "overlooked", after it was revealed that domestic abusers would be among the offenders released from jail early under the plans.

  Domestic abuser Michael Boyes who was jailed in January 

*(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Boyes, Ms Duncanson’s former partner, was jailed for three and a half years in January for abusing Ms Duncanson over a period of one year and eight months.

But Ms Duncanson said she has been told that Boyes, who has more than 20 previous convictions including for domestic violence, is due to be released from jail in September 2027.

Prosecutor Michael Cahill, during the sentencing in January, said Boyes attacked Ms Duncanson and tried to strangle her with her own necklace.

Ms Duncanson said Boyes subjected her to “mental torture” that “was worse than the physical injuries”, but she felt “safe” and has started to rebuild her life after he was jailed.

  Former safeguarding minister Jess Phillips 

*(Image: Jonathan Brady/PA Wire)*

However, speaking on ITV’s Good Morning Britain, Ms Duncanson said she felt her safety is “all up in the air” following news that Boyes is due to be released early.

Ms Phillips, the minister for safeguarding and violence against women and girls in Sir Keir Starmer’s government, told the programme she was “100 per cent behind what Helen is trying to do” in speaking out about the early release plans for domestic abusers.

## Domestic abuse 'does not end when a perpetrator is sentenced or released from prison' - charity

IDAS’ spokesperson said that being subjected to domestic abuse “does not end when a perpetrator is sentenced or released from prison”.

“As Ms Duncanson has explained, a perpetrator’s release can bring back fear and anxiety and can create very real concerns about safety, the safety of children and what might happen next,” they said.

“This can be particularly difficult where there is a history of repeated or serial abuse.

“Domestic abuse is often part of a pattern of behaviour that extends beyond specific incidents and can continue with different intimate partners.

“This makes domestic abuse perpetrators at particularly high risk of reoffending and harm escalating.”

They added: “It takes great courage to speak out about domestic abuse, particularly where there are ongoing safety concerns. We would like to thank Ms Duncanson for raising this important issue.

“We are here to support anyone impacted by domestic abuse. We can provide practical and emotional support, help survivors understand their options, safety plan around a perpetrator’s release and work alongside other agencies to help manage risk. No one should have to navigate this situation alone.”

The spokesperson continued: “We also want anyone reading this who is worried about the release of a perpetrator to know that support is available. Whether you need help with practical safety concerns, emotional support or simply someone who understands what you are going through, you can contact IDAS. You do not have to wait until something happens before asking for help.”

  Justice secretary Alex Norris 

*(Image: Jordan Pettitt/PA Wire)*

Justice secretary Alex Norris sought to reassure survivors whose perpetrators are still due for early release by saying restrictions such as tagging and restriction zones will be designed around victims’ lives.

He told Good Morning Britain: "It may be release, but it isn't freedom.

"These are the most significant controls [being put on offenders after they are released from jail], and we are committed to working with victims in getting that right."

Mr Norris added: "I accept that victims, survivors in the community generally will not be happy to hear people coming out a minute sooner.

"I would have to be clear here, we are living in a permacrisis in prison places that has existed for many years now and that we are determined to break.

"And this sentencing, as well as our building programme, which is the biggest since the Victorian era, is our way of solving that.

"But I appreciate that will leave a degree of dissatisfaction."

A Ministry of Justice spokesperson said: “We have always stated we wanted to go further, and we are fast-tracking a plan to keep more of the most dangerous offenders behind bars. That’s on top of murderers and those serving sentences for rape, serious child sexual abuse and grooming offences who are already blocked from any changes.

“We share the public’s anger that these options are even being considered, after we inherited a prison system on the brink of collapse. We are fixing this by building 14,000 new prison places, expanding electronic tagging on an unprecedented scale, and investing in probation services at record levels.

“We are also exploring how we can go further, including faster deportation of foreign criminals and reviewing the unjust IPP sentences.”
