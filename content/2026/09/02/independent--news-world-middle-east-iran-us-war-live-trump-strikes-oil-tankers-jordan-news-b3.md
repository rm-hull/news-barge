Iranian hackers have targeted critical American infrastructure with cyberattacks in recent weeks, a new report has claimed, as fresh strikes threatened to plunge Washington and Tehran back into all our war.

Four sources with access to government and industry information about cyberthreats told NBC News that hackers are believed to have targeted US telecommunications, energy and other infrastructure, bringing the war ever closer to the American public.

The attempts have focused on automated systems connected to the internet but have not been successful so far, according to the report, noting that new threats against energy, water and telecommunications were still emerging on Telegram over the weekend.

The FBI said at the end of July that at least seven states have been targeted in a growing wave of cyberattacks on critical water infrastructure across the country. Officials were said to be investigating Iran as the likely agent behind the attacks, per PBS.

The latest threats emerged as the US and Iran exchanged attacks across the Middle East, with US forces striking Iran's southern coast and Iran firing at American bases across the region.

The United States threatened more devastating strikes. Iran accused Washington of hitting a wedding party, killing five people and wounding dozens.

## Car hits pro-government gathering in Iran, killing four

A car struck a gathering of supporters of Iran's government and armed forces in the northeastern city of Mashhad overnight, killing four people and injuring more than 10, several Iranian media reported on Wednesday.

A car travelling at high speed first collided with another vehicle, lost control and then veered into the crowd after hitting safety barriers and traffic signs, Noor News reported, citing Mohsen Mousa-Abadi, head of Razavi Khorasan Province's traffic police.

Initial checks showed the driver, who was arrested, was not in a normal state, Mousa-Abadi said, without giving details on his condition.

There was no immediate indication of a political motive, and authorities described the incident as a traffic accident.

The evening gathering was one of a series of recurring events attended by supporters of the Islamic Republic and its armed forces since the war with the United States began in late February, according to Mehr news agency.

## The latest: Trump has no interest in ‘worthless’ deal with Iran as both sides launch new strikes

US president Donald Trump has insisted he “couldn’t care less” if Iran signs a “worthless” deal to end the war, as both sides launched fresh strikes in the spiralling conflict.

The conflict in the Middle East saw a dramatic re-escalation on Wednesday as Washington and Tehran traded attacks in the biggest exchange of fire in weeks, with US forces attacking Iran’s southern coast and Iran attacking American bases across the region.

“I’m not trying to force Iran to the bargaining table,” the Trump wrote on Truth Social overnight, responding to speculation after the US struck Iran for the first time since July overnight into Monday. “I couldn’t care less if they sign a worthless, to them, agreement.”

## How Iran could stop more even oil leaving the Middle East

Mohsen Rezaei, the former Revolutionary Guards chief and secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, has threatened to shut down oil exports.

He has warned the US that if the economic war continues, not a single drop of oil will be exported through the Strait of Hormuz or anywhere in the Persian Gulf.,

Iranian attacks and threats to shipping have already stopped most traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, largely shutting down a waterway that carried around a fifth of global energy before the conflict.

Although some oil tankers have been moving through the strait in recent weeks, volumes remain very low.

Missile, drone or speedboat attacks on tankers trying to leave the Gulf have repeatedly pushed up oil prices during the six months of the conflict.

Meanwhile, Tehran's Houthi allies have restricted Red Sea shipping with attacks and threats to blockade all Saudi Arabian shipping, which carries crude to Asia through the Bab el-Mandeb strait past the group's Yemeni stronghold.

While the attacks initially only stopped some vessels, with more than half still getting through in mid-August, the situation is difficult for shippers with Chinese shipping companies now rerouting away from Bab el-Mandeb, according to industry sources.

Further attacks, such as the one that targeted a vessel off the main Saudi Red Sea oil terminal at Yanbu last week or a drone strike near the Suez Canal in early August, could raise oil market anxiety levels and crude oil prices.

## Trump's aides push to prevent Iran war from escalating - Reuters

Senior aides to Donald Trump are pushing to prevent the Iran war from escalating, after the US ramped up the intensity of its attacks on Wednesday.

The aides want to prevent the war escalating before November's midterm elections to hinder Republican electoral losses, four sources told the Reuters news agency.

Officials will consider once again ramping up military action after the vote in November, but a return to the full-scale conflict is not a fiven, they said.

They said that for now, the administration is focused on tightening the economic pressure on Iran in an effort to extract concessions in any future negotiation that months of military action failed to secure.

"We are keeping the pressure on Iran," one White House official said. "But November is a priority."

Keeping the war mostly contained until then would help prevent the unpopular conflict from dominating the news as Trump's Republicans campaign to defend thin majorities in the Senate and House of Representatives, said the sources, three of whom are White House officials.

## Exhausted and demoralised? How US sailors are really coping after six months at sea

While President Trump and his self-styled secretary of state for “War”, Pete Hegseth, dismissed it as ‘fake news’, by all accounts, life for the 5,000 men and women on board the USS Abraham Lincoln had become a living nightmare after more than 260 days at sea without respite.

The nuclear-powered warship left San Diego in November and has, until recently, been stationed in the Middle East to support the U.S. war against Iran. While the Lincoln left the Middle East on August 22 on its way home, with a planned Thailand stop, she has spent a record amount of time at sea without a port visit. Stories abound about poor food, bad hygiene, blocked toilets, intermittent showers, plummeting morale and shredded mental health. Reports in the Military Times and the Stars and Stripes claimed that sailors even tried to jump overboard as the deployment kept going.

## Oil tankers hit sea mines while transiting Strait of Hormuz, says Tehran

Two oil tankers hit sea mines and were disabled while attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Wednesday.

In a statement shared by state media, the guards said the tankers had been forced to disembark their crews and were placed in the hands of US operatives to pass through what it called an "illegal route" in the strategic waterway.

It came despite earlier warnings from the Guards' navy about the dangers of crossing the mined passage, the statement added.

## Two killed in security incident in Strait of Hormuz, says Saudi shipping company

Two Filipino seafarers have been killed in a security incident in the Strait of Hormuz, Saudi Arabia's national shipping company, Bahri, said on Wednesday.

The incident involved the company’s vessel "SIDR" while it was transiting the Strait of Hormuz on August 31, it said.
