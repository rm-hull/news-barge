# Imprisonment for Public Protection orders: Andy Burnham pledges to end 'injustice' of IPPs

## Shipley’s Labour MP Anna Dixon, who has long been campaigning against IPPs, said this was “extremely welcome”.

Andy Burnham has pledged to end the “injustice” of Imprisonment for Public Protection (IPP) orders to free up prison places.

IPPs, which were abolished in 2012, were designed to hand out indefinite sentences to criminals convicted of minor offences who were deemed to be a threat to the public.

However, these were highly controversial as people were being imprisoned with little prospect of release for crimes they did not actually commit.

Despite the sentence being abolished, there are still hundreds of people effectively imprisoned indefinitely.

Even Lord David Blunkett, the former Sheffield Hillsborough MP who was the home secretary when they were set up, declared IPPs his “biggest regret”.

In his first speech in the Commons as Prime Minister, Mr Burnham pledged to finally grasp the “nettle on an issue this house has ignored for too long”.

He said when he left Parliament 10 years ago to become the mayor for Greater Manchester “I was worried about this issue and it’s not resolved”.

The Prime Minister said: “I believe the public will support me in recognising the clear difference between those who have served their time and more and those who have not.

“We will bring forward legislation soon, but we can find a solution to end this injustice which balances public safety and basic fairness, and I urge colleagues on all sides of the House to help us do that.”

His official spokesman told reporters: “We’ll set up a cross-party oversight group to look at how this is done.

“It will take time to get right. It will require legislation.

“We recognise there are a lot of offenders in prison with an IPP who have complex needs.

“This will require consideration, it will require a thoughtful, cross-party approach centred around fairness and public protection and that’s why it’s not going to be rushed.”

Shipley’s Labour MP, Anna Dixon, who has long been campaigning against IPPs, said this was “extremely welcome”.

Ms Dixon has been supporting her constituents who have been calling for the release of their autistic son, who has spent almost two decades in prison on an indefinite order for stealing a car, despite being sentenced to two-and-a-half years.

Jimmy was handed a 30-month sentence in 2007, when he was in his early 20s, for robbery, with the judge also granting an IPP

Now in his 40s, Jimmy has been locked up for more than 18 years, currently serving in HMP Wakefield alongside Jeremy Bamber and Pembrokeshire Murders serial killer John Cooper, despite his doctors saying “he is safe and well for discharge”.

Ms Dixon said: “After almost 20 years of battling the prison system, I hope Jimmy’s family can now see the light at the end of the tunnel.

“I am delighted for the families in my constituency and across the country who have been affected by IPPs, and I thank the new Prime Minister for taking a decisive and positive step towards justice.”
