Ed Markey projected to win Democratic nomination for his US Senate seat in November

Senator Ed Markey won the Democratic primary in Massachusetts on Tuesday, beating back a challenge from Seth Moulton, a congressman who tried to unseat him by arguing to voters his opponent was too old to be effective at his job.

Massachusetts is heavily Democratic, making the senator favored to win a third full term in the November general election, where he faces the Republican nominee John Deaton.

The 80-year-old incumbent, according to the Associated Press, easily defeated his 47-year-old rival, at a moment when Democratic voters agonize over the age of some lawmakers, a concern that was amplified by Joe Biden’s disastrous re-election bid in 2024, and Kamala Harris’s subsequent failure to keep Donald Trump from returning to the White House.

The Trump administration seized on House Democrats voting against a resolution denouncing socialism, condemning the lawmakers as “sick [and] dangerous”.

But there was more in the resolution than language critical of socialism “in all its forms”. The Associated Press reports that it called for the enactment of Trump’s SAVE America Act, a strict voting bill that would ban mail-in ballots and require voters to prove their citizenship.

The measure was approved approved 220-192 with a handful of Democrats joining with Republicans in support of the resolution. Democrats described the resolution as a trap ahead of the US midterm elections.

“This isn’t a serious bill,” Congressman Josh Gottheimer told Axios. The New Jersey representative is a moderate who has criticized Democratic Socialism.

“If you actually want to take on socialism instead of playing political games, you don’t slip in the SAVE [America] Act as a poison pill that you know no Democrat will vote for.”

House votes 413-2 to censure Republican congressman after bipartisan panel found he 'engaged in sexual harassment'

Chris Stein

The US House of Representative has voted to censure Chuck Edwards, a Republican congressman who the ethics committee found violated rules against sexual harassment in his conduct towards two young female staffers.

The vote was 413-2, with 207 Republicans joining 205 Democrats.

The largely ceremonial punishment saw Edwards stand in the well of the House chamber and be read the resolution. First elected in 2022 to represent a western North Carolina district, Edwards dropped his bid for re-election following the release of the bipartisan panel’s determination that he “may have created or fostered a hostile work environment and engaged in sexual harassment” by giving gifts and lavishing attention on the two women.

“A member of Congress should not single out a staffer and isolate them from other staff, make frequent comments regarding that person’s appearance and weight, send messages in the middle of the night, text staff deeply personal and seemingly romantic thoughts, intrude on personal occasions without invitation, pay for personal vacations, and spend thousands of dollars on gifts for staffers,” the report read.

Chuck Edwards talks to reporters as he arrives for a House Republican conference in the basement of the US Capitol on 1 September. Photograph: Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images

In a speech on the House floor before the vote, Edwards defended himself, noting that the committee found he did not engage in sexual activity with the staffers or suggested it. He argued the ethics committee overreached in its conclusions, while apologizing both to the unnamed staffers and his wife.

“Regret is not an admission of sexual harassment, I can acknowledge poor judgment, regret the discomfort that some of my actions may have caused, and apologize to those who were hurt by this episode, while still disputing a conclusion that the evidence just simply does not support,” Edwards said.

North Carolina Republicans have replaced him on the ballot with state representative Jennifer Balkcom, who will face the Democratic nominee Jamie Ager in the November midterms.

Kathy Hochul, New York’s governor, criticized Donald Trump for withholding $87m in counterterrorism funds from the state, describing the move as a betrayal of 9/11 victims.

“Don’t undermine our public safety efforts. Don’t defund the police who thwart terror attacks. Don’t play political games with people’s lives,” Hochul said at a press conference on Tuesday alongside Mayor Zohran Mamdani.

The funding is essential to keeping New Yorkers safe, Mamdani said, adding that it funds 12 bomb squads across the city, among other programs.

The US Department of Homeland Security told CBS News Hochul “is manufacturing a funding crisis” and accused Hochul and Mamdani of “[shielding] criminals from the law”.

“New York works with federal law enforcement every day to take dangerous criminals off the streets. So spare us the theatrics,” Hochul said in response. “And while you’re here, cut the $87 million check you owe our cops and counterterrorism officials.”
