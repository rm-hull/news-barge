A WHOLESALE cash and carry in Bradford was told "major improvement" was necessary after it received a one-star food hygiene rating.

Afrogrocers Ltd, trading as Afrogrocers on Caledonia Street in Broomfields, was inspected by Bradford Council’s environmental health food safety team on January 20, 2026.

The business sells African and Caribbean food items such as meat, poultry and frozen products.

Afrogrocers says it has since corrected the issues highlighted and retrained its staff to "ensure that food hygiene processes and procedure are strictly adhered to."

## What does the report say?

Inspectors found dried fish pieces being sold loose in cardboard boxes without appropriate product information or shelf-life details.

This same issue had been raised during a previous inspection.

Other products were also missing necessary information, prompting the officer to contact West Yorkshire Trading Standards and Leeds Council’s food safety team.

Several ambient and frozen foods were found to be unlabelled, despite this concern being noted at an earlier visit.

## Out-of-date corn dough

Additionally, three packages of corn dough were discovered on sale past their use-by dates.

The council instructed the business to carry out regular stock checks to ensure out-of-date items were not available for purchase.

Structural and hygiene problems were also highlighted in the report.

## No hot water in toilet

There was no hot water at the toilet wash-hand basin, which was also leaking.

Inspectors noted the absence of soap and hygienic hand-drying facilities, and reminded staff to wash their hands regularly.

A wall in the toilet was left as bare plaster, making it impossible to clean effectively.

This issue had also been raised previously and was still outstanding.

The ceiling in the main shop had been damaged by an earlier leak and was covered with a black tarpaulin.

Inspectors instructed the business to remove the tarpaulin, repair or replace the ceiling, and fix any other holes.

## A 'large amount' of black mould

A fruit and vegetable chiller was found to contain a "large amount" of black mould.

The business was told to identify the source of the problem, fix it, and thoroughly clean and disinfect the unit.

Food was being stored on the floor, posing a contamination risk, and the storeroom was described as dirty and dusty, with an accumulation of cobwebs.

Shelves in the main shop were also found to have spilled products and dust.

A dirty mop bucket was another concern raised.

## Management issues

The council noted that the business had not provided basic written instructions for staff to follow when the owner or manager was not present.

It recommended using existing instructions from the company’s Leeds branch, the Safer Food Better Business for Retailers pack, or a similar system that included a cleaning schedule.

The officer pointed out that many of the issues identified in January had also been raised during the previous inspection.

## The T&A contacted the owner

When the T&A contacted the store, a spokesperson said: "In regards to the issues raised during the inspection, we have corrected them by the time the inspectors came back for re-inspection.

"In addition to this, members of the staff's have been retrained to ensure that food hygiene processes and procedure are strictly adhered to.

"We are also undertaking structural improvements to ensure all round compliance."
