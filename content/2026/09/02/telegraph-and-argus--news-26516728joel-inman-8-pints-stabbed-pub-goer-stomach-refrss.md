A "DANGEROUS" knifeman who stabbed a pub goer in the stomach during a late-night confrontation in the Bradford district has been jailed.

But Joel Inman, 35, will also be subject to an extended licence period of two years after Bradford Crown Court heard on Wednesday that he already had two previous wounding offences on his criminal record.

Inman, of Cavendish Street, Keighley, will have to serve two-thirds of his 63-month jail term before the Parole Board can consider if it is safe to release him.

## Victim’s jacket 'filling out with blood'

Prosecutor Victoria Barker said that Inman and his victim had been arguing in the Cavendish Hotel and witnesses had tried to discourage the men from going outside to fight.

But when they did go out Inman, who had drunk eight pints, was seen “squaring up” to the complainant before he stabbed him in the stomach with a pen-knife.

A witness reported seeing the victim’s jacket “filling out with blood” as the defendant left the scene.

The injured man was on his way to hospital in a car when it was stopped by police and the complainant was put in the police vehicle and rushed to the A&E department.

## Stomach wound became infected

Inman was arrested later after he had changed his clothing, but he was found to have a pen-knife in his pocket.

Miss Barker said the complainant discharged himself from hospital, but 10 days later he was readmitted suffering from a collection of blood in his abdomen and a partially collapsed lung.

She said the wound to his stomach had also become infected.

The complainant spent a further three days in hospital before being discharged.

## Offence was 'culmination of a number of events'

Inman pleaded guilty to charges of causing grievous bodily harm with intent and possession of a bladed article in a public place.

Barrister Stephen Uttley, for Inman, said his client had shown he could live a normal life when he abstained from alcohol.

He highlighted Inman’s awful childhood and mental health issues and said the offence was the culmination of a number of events.

Mr Uttley said Inman had been assaulted himself and had been carrying the knife for protection.

## 'Dangerous offender'

But Judge Ahmed Nadim said the carrying of knives in public remained a scourge on our communities.

“Members of the public are entitled to use streets, town centres, public houses and leisure venues without the fear of serious violence,” said the judge.

“Knife crime undermines the confidence in the safety of those places and creates fear within the wider community.”

Judge Nadim concluded that Inman was a “dangerous offender” and the extended licence period was necessary due to the high risk of serious harm he posed to the public.
