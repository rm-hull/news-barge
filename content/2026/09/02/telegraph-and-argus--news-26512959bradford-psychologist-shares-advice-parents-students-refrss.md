PARENTS are being reminded to prepare children emotionally as well as practically for the return to school.

Dr Eleanor Bryant, associate professor in eating and health psychology at the University of Bradford, has offered advice to parents on how to support children who may feel anxious about the new term, emphasising the importance of managing expectations rather than aiming for a worry-free start.

Dr Bryant said: "As a psychologist, my advice is simple: don't aim for an anxiety-free first day.

"Feeling nervous about change is normal.

"What matters is helping children believe they can cope with it."

She explained that a child’s needs will vary depending on their age and stage of schooling, from a four-year-old starting school for the first time to an 11-year-old transitioning to secondary.

Dr Bryant recommends making the unfamiliar more familiar for younger children by walking the school route together, discussing the daily routine, and practising tasks like putting on a coat or asking an adult for help.

She advises against dismissing a child’s fears, suggesting parents acknowledge their feelings and reinforce their ability to cope.

Dr Bryant said: "We're not trying to eliminate every anxious feeling.

"We're helping children discover that they can experience those feelings and cope with them."

For those returning after the summer break, she suggests easing gradually into the school routine by shifting sleep and meal times before term begins.

She also encourages parents to help children reconnect with friends before the first day and to focus on positive movement and activity, noting research that shows exercise can reduce anxiety and depression in young people.

For students moving up to secondary school, Dr Bryant recommends practising practical tasks such as the journey to school and learning how to read a timetable.

She stresses the importance of taking friendship worries seriously and offering emotional support, even as children seek greater independence.

Dr Bryant said: "A wobble isn't a failure.

"Learning how to recover from one is part of growing up."

The University of Bradford is set to host an event on September 23 exploring mental health in children and young people as part of its 6 Decades Lecture series.

The event, titled Children and Young People's Mental Health: Why Prevention Matters, will run from 1pm to 2.30pm and feature researchers from the Bradford Mental Health Research Centre.

It will focus on the challenges facing young people, the case for prevention, and the roles families, schools, communities, and policy can play in supporting wellbeing.

The event will take place on campus and online.
