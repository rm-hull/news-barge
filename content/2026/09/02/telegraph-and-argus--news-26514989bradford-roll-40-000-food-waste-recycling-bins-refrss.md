BRADFORD has begun rolling out weekly food waste collections to almost 25,000 households, with 40,000 bins set to be distributed across the district.

The first caddies, liners and outdoor food waste bins have been delivered, with collections due to start this week.

Further deliveries will take place across Bradford over the coming months.

Residents have been told not to contact the council to ask when their equipment will arrive, as all households will receive the items automatically.

The rollout was outlined in a Bradford Council Facebook post.

It included a video of Councillor Ian Walker (Reform, Wyke) visiting Northcliffe Depot to see preparations for the service.

Cllr Walker said: “I’m at the Northcliffe Depot looking at the way we’re going to roll out the food waste recycling.”

He said: “These are the two bins that everyone will get, there are 40,000 of these behind me and these are going to be distributed to various households as we go along.”


## How it will work

Each participating home will receive a small kitchen caddy, a roll of liners and a separate outdoor food waste bin.

The kitchen caddy has a lockable lid intended to reduce odours and stop insects or pests getting inside.

After the supplied liners have been used, residents can use biodegradable bags, standard plastic bags or put food waste directly into the caddy before emptying it into the outdoor bin.

Accepted items include leftovers, cooked and raw food, meat, fish, bread, dairy products, tea bags, coffee grounds, eggshells and uneaten pet food.

Residents must not put packaging, liquids, garden waste or cooking oil in the food waste bin.

Collected waste will be treated through anaerobic digestion.

Microorganisms break down organic material during the process, producing biogas and digestate.

Biogas can be used as renewable energy, while digestate is a nutrient-rich fertiliser for farmland.

## Government scheme

Bradford Council said the service forms part of the Government’s Simpler Recycling legislation.

The law requires councils across the UK to provide separate food waste collections.

Government funding is helping support the programme.

The authority also said processing food waste through recycling is cheaper than sending it to landfill or incineration.

Participation in the service is not compulsory, but the council is encouraging residents to use it.

Officials said recycling even small amounts of food waste could help reduce environmental impacts and support the district’s recycling targets.
