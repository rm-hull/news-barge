More than 3,000 people have died in Congo’s Ebola outbreak from over 6,100 confirmed infections, according to fresh government statistics released on Wednesday.

Transmission of the virus is accelerating at a rate outpacing efforts to trace and contain it.

Figures published by Congo’s Ministry of Health confirm 6,186 infections and 3,007 fatalities since the situation was designated an international health emergency in mid-May. The crisis continues to be the most rapidly expanding Ebola epidemic on record.

Infections have escalated sharply in recent weeks, reaching two additional health zones in North Kivu last week—a province experiencing a significantly higher mortality rate than other impacted areas. In total, the virus has now extended its reach across six provinces.

On Tuesday, an evaluation by the US Centers for Disease Control warned that suppression measures "remained below established response targets". The agency noted that the swift movement of infections across numerous health zones demonstrates an "uncontrolled expansion of the outbreak."

Frontline healthcare staff are operating under difficult conditions and have repeatedly gone on strike demanding their pay.

Multiple factors are driving the swift transmission, including ongoing conflict in the country's eastern region where cases are clustered, targeted violence against medical workers, high mobility among local miners, and deficient healthcare infrastructure.

Concerns have also mounted regarding potential virus transmission inside classrooms following the reopening of schools amid the outbreak.

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), the current epidemic threatens to surpass West Africa’s regional outbreak between 2014 and 2016, which stands as the deadliest on record after claiming 11,000 lives.

The Africa Centers for Disease Control and Prevention stated that the true scale of the crisis could be triple the documented count due to inadequate monitoring and contact tracing. Even so, it is already the most lethal Ebola outbreak Congo has ever faced.

No approved treatments or vaccines currently exist for the rare Bundibugyo virus driving this crisis, although a couple of candidate vaccines are presently being developed to combat the pathogen.

Authorities began rolling out the Ervebo vaccine last week to frontline workers in Kisangani, located in the northeastern Tshopo province. Ervebo helped control earlier Ebola outbreaks, and the WHO noted it may provide a degree of protection.
