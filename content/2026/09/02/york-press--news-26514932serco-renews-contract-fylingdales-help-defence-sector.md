A new defence contract will help protect the UK from missile threats and support space monitoring.

Serco has secured a five-year contract with the Ministry of Defence (MoD) to operate and maintain the Solid State Phased Array Radar (SSPAR) at RAF Fylingdales in North Yorkshire.

The contract includes an option for a further two-year extension.

Anthony Kirby, chief executive of Serco Group, said: "We are immensely proud of our dedicated team at RAF Fylingdales who are entrusted to seamlessly deliver this continuous service, helping to defend the nation and protect our skies."

SSPAR forms part of the UK and US governments’ Upgraded Early Warning Radar (UEWR) programme and is critical to missile detection and tracking.

The radar also plays a key role in monitoring objects in low Earth orbit.

Serco’s team provides 24/7 support throughout the year.

In 2025, the SSPAR completed more than 5.9 million tracks of satellites in low Earth orbit.

Mr Kirby said: "We have a long history over sixty years supporting the UK Space Command, US Space Force and the Royal Air Force at RAF Fylingdales, with our people at the heart of this critical infrastructure asset.

"Our success in securing this contract reflects the strength of our expertise and growing capabilities in defence and builds on our recent strong momentum."

Serco will be responsible for radar hardware and systems operation, maintenance, analytical support, and training.

As part of the contract, a new software tool developed by Serco will be deployed.

The Enhanced Spacetrack Processor (ESP) will analyse large volumes of track data to determine the position, orbit, and size of objects in low Earth orbit.

Developed in collaboration with the RAF and UK Space Command, ESP replaces the long-serving United Kingdom Spacetrack Processor (UKSP), which was in use for more than 30 years.

The contract will also create new training and employment opportunities.

Serco will offer multiple Level 3 apprenticeships for school or college leavers, as well as Level 5 and 6 apprenticeships for current employees.

The company has partnered with Teesside University and Redcar & Cleveland College to develop an RAF Fylingdales-specific apprenticeship scheme.

This programme will equip individuals with essential skills, recognised qualifications, and a pathway to long-term careers in the defence sector.
