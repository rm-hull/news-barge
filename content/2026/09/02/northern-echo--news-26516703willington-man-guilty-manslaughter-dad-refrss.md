A man who killed his ‘dad’ when he pushed him over in a pub beer garden just hours after learning he was his father on Christmas Eve has been found guilty of manslaughter.

Martin Greensmith accepted that his actions resulted in Steven Shevels' death but always maintained that he was acting in self-defence.

The 37-year-old reacted angrily when learning that Mr Shevels was his father when he turned up in the Queen’s Head pub in Willington.

Jurors at Teesside Crown Court heard how 61-year-old Mr Shevels was asked to leave the pub after he became an annoyance to the Greensmith family.

Moments later, 37-year-old Greensmith pushed over his dad in the beer garden after he claimed he tried to block his way back into the pub.

The court heard how Mr Shevels had been annoying members of his son’s family, including his mother Angela, and his nanna, after sitting next to them.

CCTV footage captured a series of interactions between Mr Shevels and the defendant’s mother Angela Greensmith, before Greensmith barged into the victim while he was at the bar.

Greensmith then made an indecent hand gesture towards Mr Shevels before his mother and uncle intervened and moved him away.

Later in the day, cameras captured the defendant push Mr Shevels to the ground in the pub’s beer garden and the 61-year-old cracked his head on the concrete.

Paramedics spent an hour trying to revive Mr Shevels but he was pronounced dead at the scene.

Greensmith, of Rockingham Road, Sunnybrow, Willington, had deniedmanslaughter but was convicted by a majority verdict of 10-2 following almost six hours of deliberation.

Judge Jonathan Carroll remanded the defendant in custody until his sentencing hearing on October 21.

He said: “Looking at the guidelines and likely sentence, I am of the view that it is necessary to remand you in custody.

“I grant you a pre-sentence report which will be prepared in due course.”

Jurors had heard how pathologist Jennifer Bolton said that Mr Shevels suffered a cardiac arrest while unconscious and never recovered.

She said the cause of death was triggered by the fall and not an underlying heart condition he had.
