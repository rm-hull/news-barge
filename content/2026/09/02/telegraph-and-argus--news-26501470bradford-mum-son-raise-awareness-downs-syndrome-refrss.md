A mother and son duo from Bradford have captured the hearts of social media with their honest but uplifting portrayal of a life with Down's syndrome.

37-year-old Shabana Khan, from Bradford, and her 16-year-old son Eisa have built a following of more than 200,000 people across TikTok, Facebook, and Instagram, where they share everyday moments under the handle @shab_eisa.

But it's not your typical 'influencer' content, because their content sheds light on life with Down’s syndrome - a genetic condition Eisa has been diagnosed with.

Sometimes poignant, sometimes funny, they aim to promote inclusion and understanding.

*(Image: T&A)*

## Showing off 'how very cool Eisa is'

Miss Khan told the T&A: "It started off as just sharing our normal life really, the funny moments, the hard days, Eisa being Eisa, which is very cheeky, but over time it’s become so much more than that.

"I use my platforms to raise awareness around Down’s syndrome, SEN families, inclusion, and some of the battles families like ours face, but most of all how very cool Eisa is.

"We’ve built an amazing community online, and I never expected it to become what it has."

She said her main goal has always been to help people see her son for who he truly is.

But she admits Eisa "loves being famous".

She said: "He loves the attention, when he walks in a room you definitely know Eisa is there especially if you're a guy, he will fist bump you and say 'Yes my bro' or 'We are boyos'."

And when she asked Eisa what his thoughts on the social media channel was, he told his mum: “I’m cool and I’m famous too, and I got to meet Zack Knight!”

*(Image: T&A)*

### 'I was terrified, uneducated'

Miss Khan added: "I wanted people to see Eisa for Eisa, not just see Down’s syndrome.

"When people hear a diagnosis, sometimes they already have an idea in their head of what that person’s life is going to look like.

"I'm guilty of it too, when I was told Eisa had Down's syndrome I was terrified, uneducated and Google was my answer, but not the reality."

While their life, like anyone's, can be challenging, it is also full of laughter, love, and possibility - and this is what Eisa's mum wants to portray.

She said: "I never wanted to paint this perfect picture because our life isn’t perfect.

"It’s just our life, and I wouldn’t change it for the world."

## 'I’ve had so many parents message me'

The content is clearly resonating with people. Miss Khan told the T&A that she frequently receives messages from parents and prospective parents to say that her content has helped them feel less alone.

Miss Khan said: "I’ve had so many parents message me saying something we’ve shared has helped them, given them hope, or simply made them feel understood, and that means more to me than views ever could."

## Pinnacle Performance has been life-changing

Their content also highlights inclusive activities and resources in Bradford.

Miss Khan said: "Another huge part of it is showing families what’s actually available to them, especially here in Bradford.

"Eisa and I are always out trying different activities and inclusive places, and because we share those experiences, other parents discover places they might never have known.

For Miss Khan and her son, Pinnacle Performance in Bradford has been life-changing.

She said: "It has honestly been one of the best things to ever happen to Eisa. I’ve watched his confidence, independence and abilities grow there, and now seeing other SEN families finding that same community because we shared our journey is amazing."

## 'I wish people would see the person first'

She describes Eisa as her "biggest inspiration".

Ms Khan said: "He’s funny, cheeky, loving, stubborn, very dramatic and he absolutely knows what he wants.

"I wish people would see the person first instead of seeing the disability first, which is what our pages are teaching.

"Before I shared Eisa's life, people would look at him and not know how to speak to him so it would be just stop and stare, which wasn't nice, but I learned along the way it wasn't being mean, it was just uneducated."

## 'There are sacrifices'

Although their online presence has brought joy, Miss Khan is candid about the challenges of their day-to-day life.

She said: "There are sacrifices, definitely.

"Work, social life, and even just having time to yourself can all come second.

"But I don’t want people to read that and feel sorry for me, because I wouldn’t change Eisa and our life.

"He’s my best friend, my absolute world.

"If he’s happy, so am I."
