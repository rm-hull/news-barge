A MAN in a mobility scheme car hit speeds of 80mph, drove the wrong way round mini-roundabouts, and went down a bus lane to avoid a red light during a police chase in Bradford.

Bradford Crown Court also heard that Aiden Wood-Manders, who was disqualified from driving and uninsured at the time, drove onto a pavement and the 35-minute pursuit only came to an end when officers deployed a “stinger” device to deflate the vehicle’s tyres.

Prosecutor Tom Harris said the Seat Arona then crashed into a police car and a parked vehicle, but Wood-Manders, 31, resisted during his arrest and PAVA spray had to be used.

At the time of the incident in June last year Wood-Manders, of Woodvale Close on the Sutton estate, was still subject to a three-year driving ban imposed March 2024.

## 35-minute police chase

Mr Harris described how the pursuit began at about 9.25pm on the night of June 27 when the Seat was seen speeding on Cemetery Road in Bradford.

Although the car came to a stop on Netherlands Avenue it then accelerated away and Wood-Manders repeatedly reached speeds of up to 80mph.

The court heard that the “stinger” device was deployed in the Halifax Road area and the incident came to an end just before 10pm.

## Man suffers from mobility issues

Wood-Manders, who, the court heard, suffers from mobility issues, pleaded guilty back in December 2025 to offences of dangerous driving, driving while disqualified and driving without insurance, but his sentence hearing was put back earlier this year so that further medical reports could be obtained.

Barrister Kathryn Pitters, for Wood-Manders, urged the court to suspend any prison sentence and said her client’s physical condition had deteriorated since he committed the offences.

She submitted that the impact for her client of an immediate prison sentence would be disproportionately harsh.

## 'A wholly unacceptable risk to innocent members of the public'

But Judge Ahmed Nadim said Wood-Manders had never held a full driving licence and previous record for driving while banned and other driving offences was “deplorable”.

“Time and again the courts have sought to deter you from this sort of behaviour through community penalties and periods of disqualification,” he told Wood-Manders.

“Time and again you have ignored those opportunities.”

The judge said the circumstances of the pursuit were extremely serious and added: ”Your driving created a wholly unacceptable risk to innocent members of the public.

“It is only through good fortune that no pedestrian, motorist or passenger suffered catastrophic injury or death.”

## Man jailed

The judge explained that a prison sentence of 14 months was appropriate, but he had reduced it to 12 months to take account of Wood-Manders’ medical condition.

Wood-Manders was also banned from driving again for two years and he must pass an extended driving test before he can lawfully driving on the roads again.

“Mr Wood-Manders it gives me no pleasure to pass the sentence that I have passed,” said Judge Nadim.

The judge said he hoped that the defendant would reflect on his behaviour and he would not see him before the courts again.
