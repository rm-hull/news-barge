A County Durham working men's club is determined to keep community spirit alive despite officials saying they have been "fighting to keep its doors open".

Grange Villa Working Men's Club, near Chester-le-Street, has been struggling with the rising costs associated with running the venue.

"Sky-high energy bills" are among the financial pressures it faces, alongside what members describe as older financial hurdles inherited by the club.

Despite the challenges, Grange Villa has praised the community for continuing to support it and helping to secure its future.

Rachael Thompson, a member of the club, said: "The support and input from our locals always has been, and always will be, the real driving force behind everything we do."

The club has been working hard to host a range of events for local people, including family fun days, bingo, live entertainment, football and discos.

Recently, those efforts extended beyond its own fundraising needs when it hosted a breast cancer charity fundraiser.

The event raised £795 for the cause.

  Residents came together at Grange Villa Working Men's Club to support a breast cancer charity fundraiser 

*(Image: GRANGE VILLA WORLING MEN'S CLUB)*

Rachael said: "Despite everything we have been facing ourselves, we wanted to give something back.

"Breast cancer is a cause that has touched the lives and hearts of so many people and families, and seeing our community come together to support it was incredibly special."

She said the success of the event reflected the strength of support within the village and highlighted the role the club continues to play in the community.

  A display set up as part of the breast cancer charity fundraiser at Grange Villa Working Men's Club 

*(Image: GRANGE VILLA WORLING MEN'S CLUB)*

Rachael also paid tribute to the volunteers who help keep the club running.

"These wonderful people give up so much of their own free time to keep the club and our events running as smoothly as possible," she said.

"They work so hard behind the scenes just to make sure everyone has the best experience, and we couldn't do it without them."

Grange Villa is not alone in facing financial difficulties.

A 2025 survey by the Centre for Democratic Business found that 83 per cent of social clubs described their financial situation as "poor to fair", while 35 per cent said they had borrowed money.

The survey also found that 73 per cent of clubs believed it was "likely" or "very likely" they would need to borrow or raise additional funds in the coming years.
