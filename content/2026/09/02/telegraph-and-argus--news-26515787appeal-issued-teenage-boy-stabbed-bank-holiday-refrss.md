*(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Police are appealing for witnesses after a 13-year-old boy was stabbed on Bank Holiday Monday.

Officers were called to Roundhay Road, Leeds, at 6.05pm on August 31 following reports that the teenager had been stabbed.

The boy was taken to hospital for treatment to a wound to his abdomen. Police said the injury is not believed to be life-threatening.

## 
  **Investigators identify scene of attack**

Following further enquiries, detectives now believe the stabbing actually took place on Shepherd's Lane, potentially near the junction with Back Hares Avenue.

Enquiries remain ongoing, with officers carrying out house-to-house enquiries and reviewing CCTV footage from the area.

Detective Chief Inspector Scott Hartley said: “This is clearly a concerning incident in which a young teenage boy has suffered a significant stab wound.

“We have extensive enquiries ongoing, including house to house enquiries and reviewing CCTV. We are conscious though that this offence has occurred near to the Leeds West Indian Carnival and there will have been a large number of people in the vicinity who may not necessarily be from the Chapeltown area.

## 
  **Witnesses sought near carnival route**

Police said there were likely to have been a large number of people in the area at the time, as the Leeds West Indian Carnival was taking place nearby.

Detectives are particularly keen to hear from anyone who may have seen a group consisting of a teenage boy and two girls.

The suspect is described as a male aged around 15 to 16 who was wearing a balaclava and was accompanied by two girls.

## 
  **Police believe incident was targeted**

Detectives said their enquiries so far suggest the stabbing was an isolated and targeted incident.

They also stressed that thousands of people attended the Leeds West Indian Carnival safely and without incident over the Bank Holiday weekend.

## 
  **Information appeal**

Anyone with information that could assist the investigation is asked to contact Leeds CID via West Yorkshire Police's website live chat service or by calling 101, quoting crime reference 13260496572.

Information can also be passed anonymously to the independent charity Crimestoppers on 0800 555 111.
