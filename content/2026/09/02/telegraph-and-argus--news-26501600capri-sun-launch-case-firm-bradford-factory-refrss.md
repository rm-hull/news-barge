A major drinks manufacturer with a factory in Bradford is being sued for more than £3 million at the High Court in a contract dispute.

In 2024, the T&A reported how Princes Group had entered a "landmark" deal to produce 250 million Capri-Sun juice pouches every year at its premises in Weaverthorpe Road, off Tong Street, Bradford.

However, Princes Group is now facing legal action from Capri Sun, which claims the company failed to make enough of the soft drink.

Amid the legal dispute, Princess Group told the T&A there was "no disruption to operations or employment at our Bradford facility."

Swiss-based Capri-Sun AG and its UK subsidiary, Capri-Sun UK Limited, filed a claim against Princes Group earlier this year over allegations it breached two contracts agreed in 2024, causing it “loss and damage”.

In court documents, lawyers for Capri-Sun said Princes had committed several breaches of the agreements, including through a “unilateral suspension of production and delivery” in January this year, with the claim worth more than £3 million.

Noel Casey KC, for Capri-Sun, said in court documents that a warehousing and distribution agreement was agreed in January 2024, and a co-manufacturing agreement was agreed two months later.

But he said that in January, Princes suspended production and delivery of Capri-Sun products until Capri-Sun paid £2.583 million.

While production later restarted, Princes said last month that it would stop production again, with Capri-Sun now seeking damages, an injunction requiring Princes to continue manufacturing, and a declaration that the £2.6 million sum was not owed.

The barrister said: “Between on or around February 2 and July 17 2026, Princes has failed to produce and/or deliver substantial quantities of products required under Capri-Sun Group’s orders.

“Consequently, the claimants have been unable to fulfil, in whole or in part, orders received from a significant number of retailers including but not limited to Sainsbury’s, Tesco, Asda, Iceland, Ocado, Morrisons, Farmfoods, Co-op and Amazon.

“This led to shortages of Capri-Sun products at numerous retailers, with certain resellers running out of Capri-Sun products completely.

“During this period, the claimants were unable to fulfil orders for several hundred thousand cases.

“The claimants estimate their lost revenue from this period to be approximately £2,676,778.18 and their lost profits to be approximately £847,202.11.”

### Princes' response

A spokesperson for the Princes Group said: "To clarify, there is no disruption to operations or employment at our Bradford facility – which does manufacture Capri-Sun and continues to run as normal.

"Princes fully disputes the claims made by Capri-Sun and does not accept the allegations as presented.

"Princes considers that Capri-Sun has itself failed to meet certain contractual obligations.

"Princes is preparing its defence and counterclaim, through which it intends to assert its rights and seek recovery of the losses and damages it has suffered.

"As the matter is now before the court, it would not be appropriate to comment further on the specific allegations or ongoing proceedings."

### Capri Sun's response

A spokesperson for Capri Sun said: "As the matter is before the Court, it would not be appropriate to comment on the parties' respective allegations, claims or the ongoing proceedings beyond the factual clarification above.

"Capri-Sun will present its position through the Court process and does not intend to comment further on the matter while the proceedings remain ongoing.

"Our priority remains ensuring a reliable supply of Capri-Sun products to our customers and consumers."
