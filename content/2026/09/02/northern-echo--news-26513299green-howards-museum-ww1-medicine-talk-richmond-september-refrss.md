A talk exploring medical practices during the First World War will take place at the Green Howards Museum in Richmond.

Titled 1914-1918: Medical Expectations, Truths and Progress, the event will be held in North Yorkshire on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, from 6pm to 8pm.

The talk will be given by Andrew Volans, who was a practicing doctor, working in the Emergency Medicine arena, whose family connections to the war have long shaped his interest in the field.

Mr Volans said: "The were family stories that have been with me since childhood and I initially sought the truth of them, before going into the medical history of the skills I have been taught and have taught others."

Four members of Mr Volans’ family served during the war, including his grandfather, who was invalided out during the end of the Passchendaele offensive.

His grandfather later met his grandmother while recovering in hospital; she was one of the nurses who cared for him.

The talk will explore what was expected in 1914 and what was learned in the next 4 years and what happened subsequently.
