Bradford Council has defended its decision to sell a caravan park after facing criticism from Conservative councillors.

The authority was criticised by Bradford Conservatives for deciding to sell Dobrudden Farm Caravan Park in Baildon, which has been managed by the Lawrence family for 70 years.

### Bradford Conservatives criticised the decision

Councillor David Ford, Conservative representative for Baildon, said: "My Ward colleague Debbie Davies and I have both already publicly stated that we recognise that the Council needs to raise funds via the disposal of some of its assets.

"But in this instance we differ with Council Officers as to what constitutes 'best consideration.'

"The family currently renting the site look after it well and have a positive relationship with the tenants and the local community."

Councillor Ford continued: "We can understand why they feel a 'profound sense of unfairness' at the prospect of losing a business they have run for generations and we also understand why the approximately one hundred residents are so stressed out regarding what is unfolding before them."

He said: "We hope that the outcome for tenants on the site results in as little disruption as possible and that they will soon be able to continue to enjoy the stability which they are used to."

### Bradford Council responds

A council spokesperson said: "The property is let on a business lease with protection under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954 to which the tenant has requested a new tenancy, which the Council has not objected to.

"Therefore, the only material change will be that the Council will no longer be the tenant’s landlord once the asset is sold.

"The tenant’s business will still be protected under the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954."

### What happened in the lead-up to the decision

The proprietors first discovered the land, owned by Bradford Council, was going up for sale through an article in the Telegraph & Argus in February 2024.

Despite the family's bid to purchase the freehold, a higher offer from another party meant their offer was rejected, despite having originally been recommended for officer approval.

At a Corporate Overview and Scrutiny Committee on July 30, the decision was made to place the fate of the caravan park back in the hands of council officers.

They have since made the decision to sell to the highest bidder.
