MORRISONS has confirmed that around 80 jobs at its Bradford head office have been placed at risk of redundancy as part of proposals to streamline its central operations.

The supermarket giant said affected colleagues have been informed and a consultation process is now underway.

The proposals affect roles across Morrisons' central functions at its head office, located on Gain Lane, but do not impact store staff or other operational teams.

A spokesperson said the company would seek to find alternative roles within the business for affected employees wherever possible.

## Company says changes will help support stores

Morrisons said the proposed changes form part of its wider plans to modernise the business and improve support for stores and customers.

A Morrisons spokesperson said: “While we are making good progress with our plan to renew and modernise Morrisons - growing sales and our market share, and improving the shopping experience - market conditions remain challenging and we are focusing hard on delivering the best value for customers.

“In order to maintain our momentum, we’re continuing to review how we operate and have identified some opportunities to streamline our central structures - to reduce complexity, help us work faster and more effectively and empower colleagues.

"As a result, we’re proposing some changes across our head office functions to ensure they are better placed to serve our stores and improve service for our customers."

## Consultation process underway

The retailer said the proposed restructuring would result in a number of head office roles being placed at risk of redundancy.

The spokesperson added: "Consequently, the proposed changes will result in a number of colleague roles being placed at risk of redundancy.

"We will be offering these colleagues our full support, including helping them to find alternative roles elsewhere in the business wherever we can.”

“A consultation process has now commenced.”

## Stores not affected

Morrisons stressed that the proposals relate specifically to head office functions and will not affect staff working in stores or other areas of the business.

The company said the changes are designed to improve the ability of its central teams to support stores and deliver for customers, while adapting to ongoing pressures facing the retail sector.
