WHENEVER an MP is elected, they thank their constituents for their votes and say it is the greatest honour of their life to represent this, the greatest constituency in the country, and then they rush off to London to try to climb the greasy pole.

When the time comes and they slide down the greasy pole, you might think they’d like to spend a little time in the greatest constituency in the country, representing the people they owe their careers to, perhaps repaying them by using their name and their status to open a few doors on their behalf.

It is disappointing, then, that Keir Starmer, just 10 weeks after saying he would remain as the MP for Holborn and St Pancras, should choose to resign.

If it was wrong for Nigel Farage to waste taxpayers’ money by calling a by-election to suit his own agenda – and it was very wrong – it is also wrong for Mr Starmer to do the same thing.

It’s almost as if he wished to present his successor, Andy Burnham, with an immediate, and expensive, headache – Labour has held this seat very comfortably since it was created in 1983. It would be a blow to the new PM to lose it.

But it’s also a headache for the Green Party.

In left-leaning London, this is exactly the sort of seat they should win if they are going to be the realistic challenger on Labour’s left flank, and in leader Zach Polanski they have a potential candidate with a high profile.

Will they dare, or will the Burnham bounce allow Labour to bury the last part of the disappointing Starmer years?
