A COMMUNITY-LED campaign is bringing people together to challenge hate and division.

Keighley Together will hold a public meeting at the Civic Centre in Keighley on Thursday, September 10, from 7pm to 9pm, with speakers from Hope Not Hate, Bradford Women Against The Far Right, and the National Education Union.

The group, relaunched in April 2026, aims to address the rise in hate by fostering unity and understanding through open dialogue and community events.

Ian Nichol, secretary of Keighley Together, said: "We are a campaign rooted in local communities and workplaces and at our meeting, which is open to the general public, we will be asking attendees to help to shape our future campaigning activities to successfully challenge the far right narratives that promote hatred and division to undermine social cohesion.

"We owe it to young people to ensure that diversity is celebrated and that communities can live together in harmony and with mutual respect."

Keighley Together will share updates on the campaign’s progress, including feedback gathered from events where residents discussed what they value about Keighley, how the town could improve and ways to strengthen community bonds.

A Politics of Hate workshop delivered to more than 60 students from a local secondary school will also be highlighted, offering insights into young people’s views on Keighley, social media, current affairs and diversity.

The group will also voice its support for the Bradford and District is For Everyone rally and festival against racism, which takes place in Bradford city centre on October 3.

Keighley Together can be contacted by email at [keighleytogether@outlook.com](mailto:keighleytogether@outlook.com) or through their Facebook page.

Hope Not Hate, one of the organisations taking part in the meeting, is known for its work in exposing and opposing far-right extremism.

Bradford Women Against The Far Right will also speak at the meeting, focusing on how far-right groups use women’s safety to promote anti-immigrant and racist agendas.

The National Education Union, which has committed to challenge all forms of racism, fascism and far right extremism, will also be represented.
