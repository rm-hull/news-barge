RESIDENTS are being encouraged to have their say on controversial plans for Hawksworth Quarry after the operator lodged an appeal against the refusal of a planning application by Leeds City Council.

Guiseley councillors Oliver Edwards, Sonia Leighton and Eleanor Thomson are urging local people to submit comments to the Planning Inspectorate before the September 15 deadline regarding proposals to allow the importation and processing of inert waste at the quarry in Odda Lane, Hawksworth.

The appeal follows Leeds City Council's decision to refuse the original application earlier this year, with planners concluding there was insufficient evidence to show the scheme could operate safely without causing significant highway problems.

Councillors say they share residents' concerns about the environmental and traffic impacts of the quarry and have been working with council officers and the Environment Agency to analyse vehicle movements.

They will also be submitting their own formal objections to the Planning Inspectorate.

Councillor Oliver Edwards (Labour, Guiseley and Rawdon) said: "Residents in Hawksworth, Guiseley and Menston have serious concerns regarding the long-term impact of this quarry on our community and we encourage anyone who has suffered from the problems it has created to submit their comments to the Planning Inspectorate.

"We want to extend our sincere thanks to the Hawksworth Quarry Monitoring Group, who have been relentless in pushing for operator accountability.

"Our close partnership with the group and the Hawksworth Village Association remains vital as we fight to protect our local environment."

The original application attracted more than 180 objections from residents, with concerns including increased HGV traffic, road safety, environmental impacts, public health issues and what some viewed as the site's transition from a quarry to a waste-processing operation.

In refusing the application, Leeds City Council said the proposal would have generated a substantial increase in heavy goods vehicle movements compared with levels currently permitted at the site.

A Transport Assessment was not submitted as part of the application, preventing highways officers from fully assessing the impact on local roads.

Alongside efforts to protect the local environment from large-scale waste disposal, councillors also highlighted ongoing Leeds City Council initiatives to tackle localised waste issues like littering and fly-tipping.

Councillor Edwards added: “The appeal from the quarry is disappointing when we have been working hard to tackle waste crime in Guiseley and Rawdon.

"We’ve worked directly with residents to identify the best locations for our community’s share of the council’s 1,000 new litter bins.

"Public vigilance remains crucial, so we urge everyone to report fly-tipping immediately and share any available evidence as there have been several recent successful prosecutions.”

Comments on the Hawksworth Quarry appeal can be submitted online via the Planning Inspectorate portal at: [https://appeal.planning-decision.service.gov.uk/comment-planning-appeal/enter-appeal-reference](https://appeal.planning-decision.service.gov.uk/comment-planning-appeal/enter-appeal-reference) by entering reference 6014331.

Alternatively, written representations quoting the reference number can be posted to: The Planning Inspectorate, Customer Support Team, c/o QUADIENT, 69 Buckingham Avenue, Slough, SL1 4PN.

Comments must be received by September 15.
