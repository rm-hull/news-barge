A FALLEN footballer who became a war hero is the subject of a newly released biography.

Donald Simpson Bell VC, the only English professional footballer to be awarded the Victoria Cross during the First World War, played for Bradford (Park Avenue) and was posthumously honoured for his extraordinary bravery during the Battle of the Somme in 1916.

His story is now told in full for the first time in a new book by Iain McMullen, published by Pen & Sword Military.

The book charts Bell’s rise through the game, revealing the rare blend of discipline, determination and integrity that forged his character long before he set foot on the battlefield.

The biography draws on previously unpublished letters, diaries, and photographs from Bell’s family archive, offering a deeply personal view of the man known as ‘Don.’

The book follows his life from Harrogate to Westminster Training College, his return to Yorkshire as a teacher, and his journey into professional football and military service.

Bell’s time on the pitch began with amateur stints at Newcastle United and Bishop Auckland before making his Football League debut with Bradford (Park Avenue).

His life was cut short in July 1916 during a valiant assault at Horseshoe Trench, for which he was posthumously awarded the Victoria Cross.

He died five days later during a similar act of bravery at Contalmaison.

The book examines the impact of his death on those who knew him and how the football community has continued to honour his memory.

Mr McMullen is a former journalist and long-standing member of the Western Front Association.

He has spent nearly 40 years researching First World War battlefields.

His previous work includes the website footballandthefirstworldwar.org, which explores the experiences of footballers during the conflict.

The book is available now in hardback and is priced £25 (RRP).
