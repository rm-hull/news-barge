A prison officer could be put behind bars herself after having a relationship with an inmate at a County Durham jail.

Lindsay Elizabeth Wilson was told to “expect the worst” and “hope for the best” when she returns to Durham Crown Court to be sentenced next month.

It follows the 46-year-old former officer’s guilty plea to a single charge of misconduct in a public office.

She is said to have been in an improper relationship with a serving prison at HMP Deerbolt, near Barnard Castle, between August 7 and October 26, 2024.

Her counsel, Mairi Clancy, said in admitting the charge, the defendant was offering no basis of plea.

Miss Clancy asked for preparation of a background report on her client by the Probation Service prior to sentence being passed.

Agreeing, Judge Amanda Rippon said the author should prepare the report on the basis that such an offence merits a sentence beyond the custody threshold.

But she told Wilson, “all options will be considered” by the sentencing judge.

“You will be sentenced the next time you are back at court.

“I’m ordering a pre-sentence report.

“It’s an extremely important report.

“Plainly, custody is justified and most people go to custody for this offence, but the sentencing judge will consider representations.”

The judge told Wilson to make arrangement for the potential eventuality of receiving a custodial sentence, adding: “You will have to come back expecting the worst and hoping for the best.”

Judge Rippon bailed Wilson, of Colburn, Catterick Garrison, North Yorkshire, to return for sentence on Friday October 9.
