A selfless cyclist dubbed a "living superhero" by the family of a County Durham toddler battling rare leukaemia has completed a gruelling 180-mile challenge to help change the youngster's life.

Chris Kelk cycled from Skegness to Shildon over two days in honour of three-year-old Maxton Bowman, raising more than £2,400 towards a specialist safety bed.

The mammoth ride on August 29 and 30 saw Chris tackle dirt tracks, riverbanks and steep hills after clocking up more than 1,000 miles in training.

  Chris Kelk arrives in Shildon at the finish of his charity bike ride 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

The autistic, non-verbal toddler has since endured surgery, intensive chemotherapy, lengthy hospital stays and a severe blood infection.

His family say he has now regained control of his legs and is walking again, despite facing a treatment plan expected to last more than two years.

  Chris Kelk at the finish of his charity bike ride with Coralle, Mark and three-year-old Maxton Bowman 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

And after completing the 180-mile route, Maxton's mum Coralle said Chris had become the toddler's "very own living superhero".

She said: "From the first phone call with Chris with his idea to all the hard work in doing all his training to him achieving this goal he had in mind, since Maxton’s diagnosis we have truly seen the kindness in humanity we as a family have always wanted to keep Maxton’s cancer journey a positive one.

"What Chris has done of our little fighter is beyond any amount of thank yous I could say there are just no words but the feeling of pride, love and respect we as a family for Chris his amazing wife and children.

"Chris hasn’t done any of this for thanks, he has done this purely out of the kindness of his heart, Chris is now known as Maxton the brave's very own living superhero"

  Chris Kelk at the finish of his charity bike ride with Coralle, Mark and three-year-old Maxton Bowman and Mayor of Shildon Samantha Townsend 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

The money raised is being used to help Maxton get a specialist enclosed safety bed because of his autism and complex medical needs.

His family say he cannot be left unattended, while the specialist equipment will help prevent him from hurting himself or getting out of bed and wandering.

Chris's fundraising page states: "A safety bed for Maxton is a bed that's made of soft material, a bed that is enclosed so he can not hurt himself or get out and wander."

Chris said the challenge pushed him to his physical limits.

  Chris Kelk arrives in Shildon at the finish of his charity bike ride 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

He said: "After covering over 1,000 miles of training and learning how to fuel my body for such a long ride, I finally completed the extraordinary 180 mile cycling challenge over two days, tackling everything from dirt tracks and riverbanks to steep, unforgiving hills that pushed my body to its absolute limit.

"The demanding route tested endurance, resilience, and determination, all in support of Maxton the Brave."

He was met by a crowd in Shildon after crossing the finishing line.

  Maxton Bowman, three, from Shildon was diagnosed with rare acute lymphoblastic leukaemia last year 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

Chris said: "Throughout the ride, I received an overwhelming wave of encouragement. Donations poured in, messages of support arrived from across the country, and even video messages were sent mid- ride to help power me through the toughest stretches.

"This collective support became a driving force behind every mile and the will to get over the finishing line, where I was welcomed by a roaring crowd."

  'Maxton the Brave', three, from Shildon 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

Chris added: "The donation page will remain open until mid-September, giving anyone who wishes to contribute additional time to do so. Every donation helps continue the vital support for Maxton and his family."

Maxton's dad Mark previously worked with Chris at Stagecoach in Skegness before the family relocated to Shildon.

Coralle said Chris offered to take on the challenge just days after the family told him about Maxton's diagnosis.

She said: "Our jaws dropped to the floor; we couldn't believe it. We have received a lot of kindness during Maxton's journey, but this is wild."

  Maxton Bowman, three, from Shildon 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

The youngster was diagnosed with the rare cancer after his legs stopped working shortly after his second birthday.

His family had taken him to hospital several times after he developed a limp, but his condition deteriorated rapidly before doctors diagnosed him with leukaemia.

Despite the ordeal, Coralle said Maxton has continued to battle through treatment with a smile.

She said: "He has jumped through every hurdle and not broken at all, he is meant to be on this earth. He is such a little fighter and has since regained all control of his legs and is walking again which is just unbelievable."

She added: "He is on a long two-year treatment plan with intensive rounds of chemo, but he hasn't taken that smile off his face, every single day, and that helps us all too. He just keeps coming back bouncing."

The family has thanked everyone who has donated to the appeal, as well as Henfrey’s & Co for helping with the specialist bed and Shildon Railway Institute for providing a venue for their celebration.

Coralle said: "We as a family would like to thank each and every single person who has gone out of there way to donate your kindness is not lost on us at all you all are apart of Maxton’s cancer journey in the most positive way"
