A man given a suspended sentence late last year on charges of possession of child sexual abuse images has now been jailed for similar offending since.

Durham Crown Court was told Kenneth Barnes received a ten-month prison sentence, suspended for 18 months, in November, after admitting possessing indecent images of children in all three categories of severity.

He was also made subject of a Sexual Harm Prevention Order (SHPO) and registration as a sex offender, both for ten years.

Ralf Xhepa, prosecuting, said he was subsequently being managed in the community by police and the Probation Service as, “a high-risk offender”.

*(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

As such, his risk manager and a digital officer attended Barnes’ home on February 17.

They seized his mobile phone, on which, upon examination, he was found to have downloaded more indecent images of children.

He was arrested on April 17 and, when interviewed, only acknowledged that the phone belonged to him.

Mr Xhepa said between his conviction in November and the day his phone was seized, in February, a further 80 indecent images, 13 in the most serious category, were downloaded by Barnes.

Following initial denials, 50-year-old Barnes, of Charles Street, Easington Colliery, changed pleas and admitted three more counts of making indecent images of children, in late July.

Chris Morrison, for Barnes, told the court he completed an overnight home curfew and ten rehabilitation activity sessions with the Probation Service as part of the November sentence.

Mr Morrison said the phone had been seized from Barnes during the previous police investigation but was returned to him.

“He continued to use it , something he’s very remorseful about and, when it was explained to me, the return of the device was, perhaps, ill-advised.”

Mr Morrison said the defendant has struggled with the circumstances of his life, living, “a very solitary and isolated life in Easington.”

He added that he has found it difficult while on remand in prison, since April.

Recorder Mark McKone passed a 15-month prison sentence, including six months activated from November’s suspended jail term.

He warned Barnes that if there is any further similar offending he would be looking at an extended prison sentence.

Barnes remains subject of the SHPO and registration requirements put in place as part of the November sentence.
