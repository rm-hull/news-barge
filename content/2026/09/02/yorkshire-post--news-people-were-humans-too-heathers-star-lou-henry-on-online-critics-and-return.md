# 'We're humans too': Heathers star Lou Henry on online critics and returning to Leeds

## It’s a story defined by isolation, teenage alienation, and navigating the ruthless social hierarchy of high school 'mean girls' - and nearly 40 years on, its bite hasn't dulled. First released in 1988, the cult classic film Heathers is about to turn middle age this year.

Yet the pressure-cooker themes explored in the original movie and its smash-hit stage adaptation, currently at Leeds Grand Theatre, remain every bit as pertinent today as when Winona Ryder and Christian Slater first took to the screen as Veronica Sawyer and J.D.

Where the '80s original reflected the anxieties of a pre-digital generation, today's revival hits a whole new nerve for modern audiences navigating the relentless spotlight of social media.

That delicate balance between '80s nostalgia and modern relevance is something Knaresborough-born actor Lou Henry understands well.

![Lou Henry is performing in Heathers.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjcwMDU4MmUzLTU3NTItNDg2NC05YmZkLThkMzcwOGEwODE1ZTo3MjZhMWMwZS03YzIwLTQ2YzEtYmEzYy0wYTRlOWM2NmQ2NDg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



The North Yorkshire native steps into the yellow blazer of Heather McNamara - marking a major homecoming after previously treading the Leeds Grand boards in SIX The Musical.

For Henry, bringing the tour to West Yorkshire carries a depth that few other stops on the circuit can match.

"I did SIX there a few years ago, which was really, really special. Both my grandparents made it, and it was just so amazing to be at home," Henry reflects. "Bringing something to a home venue is always really special."

Yet stepping into a show with such a fiercely dedicated, hyper-online fanbase brings its own unique set of modern pressures. In an era where theatre commentary unfolds in real time across TikTok and Instagram, actors often find themselves under a digital microscope far beyond the stage lights.

For Henry, navigating that intense scrutiny has required a grounded, surprisingly direct approach.

"With this sort of age of TikTok and Instagram, people are voicing quite brutal opinions sometimes. I find it quite interesting to just reply and kind of make it obvious that we’re humans too,” she says.

"I'm just a normal person who can see what you're writing, and that doesn't mean that I'm immune to it. I'm just a person going about my day, doing my job,” Henry continues.

“I think it's quite fun sometimes to be like, 'Hello, I'm glad you love the show!' just to remind you that we can all see this."

In an era of rising costs and digital streaming, Henry believes that the shared, visceral experience of live drama is something worth protecting at all costs.

"Coming to the theatre, it’s such a sacred thing, and I really want it to remain that way," Henry says. "I think people should come to the auditorium and feel like they’re getting their money’s worth.

"In a world where everything is available on a screen, that live connection is irreplaceable."

Heathers: The Musical plays at Leeds Grand Theatre until September 5.
