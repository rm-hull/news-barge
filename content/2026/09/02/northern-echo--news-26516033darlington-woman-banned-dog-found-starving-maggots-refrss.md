A Darlington woman has been banned from keeping animals for a decade after her “severely underweight” dog was put to sleep when he was found with maggots on his body, including in his eyes and mouth.

Jenna Read, of Rockwell Avenue, Darlington, admitted causing unnecessary suffering to her nine-year-old Bichon Frise, Jim, at Newton Aycliffe Magistrates’ Court.

The court heard that a friend took Jim to a local veterinary clinic on September 26 last year, saying they had “severe concerns” about his welfare.

The friend had Read’s permission for the dog to be put to sleep.

Jim weighed just 2.5kg, or 5.5lb, and was described as skeletal. He had a body condition score of one out of nine, while a healthy dog should normally score between four and five.

The dog was also suffering from muscle wastage and had a mass on his neck. The vet decided euthanasia was the only option to prevent Jim suffering further.

In a witness statement, the vet said a live maggot had been found on Jim’s lips. Larvae were also identified in several locations, including inside his mouth, beneath his left eye and around his anus.

The vet said: “Larvae were concentrated in the oral cavity, indicating a potential dental abscess. There were no visible external wounds identified as the source of the maggots.”

The RSPCA was called to investigate Jim’s condition. An RSPCA inspector was later told there was another Bichon Frise type dog at Read’s property, called Rosy, who was dirty and matted.

  Rosy has since been rehomed. 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

The inspector spoke to Read by telephone and she agreed to sign Rosy over into their care. Rosy has since been rehomed.

Read appeared at Newton Aycliffe Magistrates’ Court on August 20, where she pleaded guilty to causing unnecessary suffering to Jim.

She was banned from keeping animals for 10 years. She was also fined £200 and ordered to pay an £80 victim surcharge and £480 in costs.

The court heard in mitigation that Read had spent time in hospital with mental health issues and that other adults had also been involved in caring for her dogs.

Speaking after the hearing, the RSPCA inspector who investigated said Jim had been in an “extremely poor condition” when he was brought to the veterinary surgery.

They said: “He was severely underweight, his skeletal frame was prominent and there were maggots crawling all over him, even out of his eyes, which was a horrific sight.

“Owners have a responsibility to recognise when their animals are unwell and suffering, and to seek appropriate veterinary advice.

“If an animal’s condition is deteriorating, it is vital that action is taken before it reaches the point where the animal is in as heartbreaking a state as Jim.”

The RSPCA urged people struggling to care for a pet to seek advice and support from an animal welfare charity before a situation reaches crisis point.
