An 18-year-old Teesside man is determined to make the most of every moment after being diagnosed with a rare, aggressive brain tumour.

Ethan Strike, from Billingham, was diagnosed with a Stage 4 Diffuse Midline Glioma (DMG) around six weeks ago after months of unexplained seizures and repeated attempts to find out what was wrong.

As he begins intensive radiotherapy at James Cook University Hospital in Middlesbrough, Ethan is ticking off a bucket list that includes seeing Ed Sheeran live, travelling to Amsterdam, taking a ferry trip and marrying his long-term girlfriend, Lola Moon-Halliday.

Despite his devastating diagnosis, Ethan’s loved ones say he remains the funny, caring person they have always known.

  Ethan and Lola. 

*(Image: Kirstie Halliday)*

Lola’s mum, Kirstie Halliday, described him as “a joker” who is always making people laugh.

She said: "When I first met him, he wasn't like a lot of teenagers. He was so polite.

"I remember getting a shopping delivery and he immediately offered to help carry it in.

"None of Lola's boyfriends had ever done that before. He's just a lovely lad."

Ethan and Lola, both 18, have been together on and off since they were 14, and Kirstie said their relationship has helped them through an incredibly difficult time.

"He's supporting her through this as well," she said. "He's a clever lad. He's Lola's calm. They work together and suit each other so well."

Lola added: "He always makes a bad situation better. If I have a problem, he always comes up with a solution."

  Ethan and Lola. 

*(Image: Kirstie Halliday)*

Just a few months ago, Ethan was looking ahead to the future after completing a trading course at college and recently receiving his exam results.

The teenager first began suffering seizures in June, shortly after celebrating his 18th birthday.

Friends and family initially put the seizures down to illness or nights out, but Ethan repeatedly sought help because he knew something was seriously wrong.

"He kept saying something wasn't right," Kirstie said.

"I saw him having a seizure where he couldn't use his arms and legs properly, and I knew it was something else."

After being told by multiple doctors that nothing was wrong, Ethan's dad decided to join him at an appointment and insist that they run further tests - Ethan was finally sent for an MRI scan that showed a shadowed area in his brain.

A further biopsy at Newcastle’s Royal Victoria Infirmary confirmed an aggressive, inoperable tumour deep in his brain stem, giving him an estimated 12 to 15 months to live.

Since his diagnosis, Ethan has developed pneumonia and now needs a feeding tube because he cannot swallow safely.

Kirstie set up the GoFundMe to help Ethan, his family and friends make the most of life.

A lifelong Middlesbrough FC fan, Ethan has already been offered a VIP matchday experience at the Riverside Stadium, and a fundraising night planned at The Longlands in Middlesbrough on Friday, September 4, will feature a raffle offering prizes including Middlesbrough tickets.

  Ethan's friends and brothers shaved their heads in solidarity. 

*(Image: Kirstie Halliday)*

Several of Ethan's friends and brothers shaved their heads in solidarity after he made the decision to cut off his hair ahead of the biopsy.

The gesture meant a great deal, Kirstie said, particularly because Ethan had always taken pride in his hair.

"He's always been quite vain about it," she joked.

She added: "Lola's studying hairdressing and for years he wouldn't let her touch it. Then one day I walked in and he had rollers in his hair. I couldn't believe it."

"But if anyone can keep smiling through it, it's Ethan."
