A convicted sex offender has been jailed for longer after being found guilty of further offences.

Keith Lockwood, 72, formerly of Park Lane, Keighley, is already serving a 23-year prison sentence for abusing schoolboys. He was given a further 15 months at Leeds Crown Court on September 1 after being convicted of four more indecent assaults.

The offences involved two boys in Kirklees and took place from the late 1970s to the early 1990s.

Detective Constable Gilly Hodds of Kirklees District Police said: "We welcome Lockwood’s latest conviction and sentencing which will ensure he is held accountable for yet more of the horrific offences he committed against children in his care.

"I would like to praise the courage and resilience of both victims, who are now in their 50s and 60s.

"They have lived with the trauma of this abuse for decades and were then forced to relive their experiences during a trial because of Lockwood's refusal to accept responsibility."

One victim travelled from North America to give evidence in person.

In July, Lockwood was convicted of indecently assaulting a victim aged 14 to 15 between May 1979 and May 1981.

He was also convicted of three indecent assaults against a second victim between March 1988 and March 1991. The victim was 12 when the abuse began.

In his victim impact statement, one survivor said: "For 47 years Keith Lockwood ruined my life, he was solely responsible for taking my childhood away.

"I am delighted with the guilty verdict and the fact that the jury saw through his lies."

Lockwood’s 2024 conviction covered offences including gross indecency with a child and sexual assault committed over 30 years in Kirklees.

He remains on the Sex Offenders Register for life.

DC Hodds said: "West Yorkshire Police remain committed to protecting the most vulnerable members of our communities and bringing offenders to justice.

"We encourage anyone who has experienced sexual abuse, whether recent or non-recent, to come forward and report it.

"As this case shows, the passage of time is not a barrier to investigating these offences, and victims will be listened to, supported and treated with dignity throughout the process."

His 2024 conviction followed a unanimous jury verdict on 23 non-recent sexual offences committed across Kirklees during the 1970s, 1980s and 1990s.
