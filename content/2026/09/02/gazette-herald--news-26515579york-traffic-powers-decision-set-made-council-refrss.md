Powers used during York’s Christmas Market which saw Blue Badge holders and others barred from the city centre could become permanent under proposals set for a decision.

City of York Council plans would see a permanent Anti-Terror Traffic Regulation Order (ATTRO) brought in which would be activated during the Christmas Market and other events.

A council report stated the move would streamline the process of bringing restrictions into force to defend against terror attacks from vehicles.

But objections to the plans claimed the powers were draconian and their use could escalate while also raising concerns about their impact on disabled people, small businesses and residents.

The proposals are set to go before the council’s executive for a decision on Wednesday, September 9.

It follows a consultation on the plans launched in March.

A temporary ATTRO was brought in during last year’s Christmas Market, which saw drivers including Blue Badge holders barred from city centre footstreets during the event’s opening hours.

The council approved North Yorkshire Police’s request for the measures after officers said they were needed to deter potential terror attacks.

But disabled people said the measures restricted their access to the city centre in the run up to Christmas.

The Christmas Market is set to trial closing on Tuesdays for a rest day this year as part of efforts to ease the impact of restrictions on those affected.

ATTRO restrictions would typically lie dormant with the council, police and other emergency services deciding when to active them.

Duncombe Place and Deangate would be subject to the restrictions when they are active along with the rest of the footstreet area.

The area around the south side of York Minster is also proposed to be included so powers can be used during New Year’s Eve gatherings.

‘Servicing periods’ outside of events, such as allowing Blue Badge holders and others back into the footstreets during the Christmas Market after 7pm, would generally be provided.

The council’s report on the plans stated the changes would allow officials to quickly process requests to close roads.

It added it would avoid the need to bring in temporary ATTROs which have also been used during royal visits, emergency services events and the funeral of North Yorkshire Police officer PC Rosie Prior.

But the report also stated the powers would sometimes have to be activated without notice if they are required urgently including if the council receives intelligence of a terror threat.

Objectors said traffic bans would affect residents in the area covered by the powers and businesses receiving deliveries.

One said they were concerned about where giving authorities the powers would lead.

  They said: “Coupling such draconian responses with the ability to close down

  the centre of York offers a worrying possibility of over-use and escalation.”

Another said disabled people would pay the price for the restrictions and the measures would not stop terror attacks being committed on foot.

The report stated the council would work with affected businesses when restrictions are in place, if they are approved.

It added they would only be used if there was deemed to be a threat or in response to a specific incident .

Officials also said they aimed to reduce the threat of vehicle attacks as much as possible but officials accepted the risk of others would remain.

The report stated: “The ATTRO would be a proportionate counter terrorism response for regular events, sudden security needs, or emergencies, for the area covered by the order.”
