A history book chronicling Sherburn's doctors over 199 years is set to be released next month.

The book, Sherburn – A History of Village Doctors, will be launched at the weekly coffee morning at Sherburn Village Hall on Thursday, October 8, 2026, from 10am to 12noon.

The book launch is open to everyone, and regular coffee mornings at the Village Hall run on Thursdays from 9am to 11.30am.

A spokesperson for Sherburn Local History Society said: "This exciting event is being jointly organized by the Sherburn Village Hall Coffee Morning Team and the Sherburn Local History Society committee.

"Both organisations are delighted to be supporting Anne who is a popular and well-respected author and historian."

The Sherburn Local History Society, which was founded on September 6, 2021, meets monthly on the first Monday at 7pm.

Updates are posted on the Sherburn Archive and Sherburn History Society Archive Facebook pages.

Sherburn – A History of Village Doctors is the eighth and last in a series of books published since 2000.

The series began with Sherburn – A Walk Down Memory Lane, a compilation of 80 old village photographs by the late Alison J V A Oulton.

The other books were written by Anne Collier and include Sherburn – a 20th Century History (2001), Sherburn in Victorian Times (2003), A History of Sherburn (in Hertfordlythe) Church of England Primary School (2008), Sherburn – A History of St Hilda’s Church (2021), Sherburn – A History of Village Chapels (2025).

The latest book traces the history of medicine and surgery from prehistoric times, documenting the early settlers in Lake Pickering and the formation of the first townships in the Vale of Pickering, including Sherburn.

It details the lives and work of doctors in Sherburn over a period of 199 years from 1823 to the 1980s, highlighting advancements in medical science and documenting pandemics of the times.

The book also lists individuals living and working in Sherburn in alphabetical order, aiding genealogists in their search for ancestors.

It includes histories of local agriculture, the brewing of beer, the building of the railway station, care for the village’s poorest people, and the history of the nearest hospitals.

Other features include the work of the local council, the building of houses, the role of prominent village members, the foundation of the village factory after World War Two, the formation of the fire brigade, some police chronicles, and national historical references.

The total proceeds from the book sales will go towards funds for the Sherburn and Rillington Medical Practice near Malton.
