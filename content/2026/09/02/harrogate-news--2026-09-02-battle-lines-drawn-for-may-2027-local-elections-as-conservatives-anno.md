The local political landscape is heating up ahead of the **North Yorkshire Council** and**Knaresborough Town Council** elections scheduled for**May 2027**, with the Conservative Party officially selecting transport professional Tyler Reeton to contest the Knaresborough East division.

The upcoming May 2027 vote will mark the first full, ordinary all-out election for the unitary North Yorkshire Council since its creation, as well as the first full-term democratic renewal for Knaresborough Town Council following local government reorganisation. Voters across the division will elect a single councillor to represent them on the top-tier unitary authority alongside local town council representatives.

## The candidate landscape

Mr Reeton is seeking to unseat the incumbent Liberal Democrat councillor, **Hannah Gostlow**, who won the Knaresborough East seat during the inaugural unitary elections and currently represents the ward on North Yorkshire Council. While opposition parties have yet to formally finalise their complete candidate rosters for the specific Knaresborough East ward tickets, the battle is expected to be a competitive multi-way race between the area’s primary political forces, including the Liberal Democrats, the Labour Party, and the Green Party.

## “Knaresborough feels forgotten” — Reeton launches campaign

Launching his campaign after speaking to more than 250 residents on the doorstep in Calcutt and across the wider ward, Mr Reeton highlighted a growing local feeling that the historic market town is being overlooked in regional decision-making.

“Across the ward, people say Knaresborough feels forgotten,” Mr Reeton said. “Decisions get made about it, not with it. Walk through the town centre and you see the same story: empty units, shops gone and not replaced, and no real plan to turn it round. Knaresborough is one of the finest market towns in the country. It should not be losing its high street.”

He pledged to challenge the status quo, promising to inject passion, accountability, and transparency back into the town’s public service representation while focusing on keeping local taxpayer costs down.

## Core doorstep issues: Roads and overdevelopment

According to the newly announced Conservative candidate, local doorstep conversations are being heavily dominated by three critical issues: road quality, traffic speeding on key local arteries (including Forest Moor Road, Thistle Hill Road, and York Road), and the pressures of rapid housing growth.

Mr Reeton targeted national housebuilding directives, pledging to stand up against large-scale development that lacks parallel investments in local GP appointments, schools, and infrastructure. He noted that the new Labour Government’s housing targets for North Yorkshire stand at over 4,200 homes a year—more than triple the 1,384 required under the previous Conservative administration.

*Voters will head to the polls across North Yorkshire in May 2027. Prospective candidate Tyler Reeton can be contacted and followed via his official Facebook Campaign Page.*
