Cleveland Police will receive an extra £2 million to tackle serious and organised crime following major incidents in Middlesbrough.

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood announced the funding in the House of Commons on Tuesday, September 1, as MPs paid tribute to two police officers killed in an A66 crash on August 22.

Pc Matthew Blades and Pc Tom Clough died when a Volkswagen Passat travelling the wrong way during a police pursuit in Middlesbrough hit their marked armed response car head-on.

Five young men in the Passat also died in the collision.

  PC Matthew Blades (left) and PC Tom Clough (right) 

*(Image: CLEVELAND POLICE/CARMEL COLLEGE)*

Mahmood said: "Policing is not just a profession it is a calling, answered only by those who are willing to place themselves in harm's way to protect us all.

"Pc Blades and Pc Clough epitomised that courage.

"In light of the situation the force has faced, we will allocate an additional £2 million to Cleveland Police to tackle serious crime in the area."

Following the crash, Cleveland Police have investigated claims that the incident was linked to serious and organised crime in the region.

   

*(Image: OWEN HUMPRHIES/ PA)*

The force has faced heavy pressure, with around 200 officers from elsewhere brought in to support organised crime investigations.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham also paid tribute to the fallen officers.

He said: "I know the whole House will wish to join me in expressing deepest condolences to the family, friends and colleagues of PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough, who were killed in the line of duty on the A66.

"We will do our best to support the Middlesbrough community, which is hurting and grieving right now, and we hear the call from the members of this House representing it for a discussion about investment in policing and police numbers."

   

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Shadow home secretary Chris Philp called the extra funding "wholly inadequate in the circumstances."

He cited Cleveland police and crime commissioner Matt Storey’s warning of a £2.4 million shortfall and demanded confirmation that all the force’s requests for extra support had been met.

Mahmood said: "It is this Government that is righting those wrongs.

"We have increased policing funding by £2 billion since being elected, and all forces will receive a real-terms funding increase this year as part of an overall £18.5 billion package.

"Funding to Cleveland has increased by £18.3 million since 2024, and that's a real-terms increase of 4.4% over two years."

Labour MP Andy McDonald, who represents Middlesbrough and Thornaby East, said Cleveland Police recorded around 123 crimes per 1,000 people - the highest crime rate in England and Wales.

He said: "We must confront the reality of crime honestly.

"Police numbers are not the only factor, but a force facing the highest demand in the country needs the resources to respond."

Mahmood said the Government was committed to tackling serious and organised crime in the region and that the situation reinforced the need for broader policing reform, including directing extra resources to forces facing particularly high levels of crime.

McDonald also said Cleveland had recorded the largest percentage reduction in full-time equivalent officers since 2010, losing around 13 per cent of its officers.
