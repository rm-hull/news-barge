## Before HMV: the York record shops we have loved and lost

Track Records High Ousegate in 1993

  *Before HMV: memories of York's long-lost record shops, by MAXINE GORDON*

NEWS that York's HMV store is set to close temporarily for a major refurbishment has sparked a wave of nostalgia among readers, with many recalling the record shops where they bought their favourite music over the decades.

As reported by The Press, the long-running Coney Street store will close from August 31 until late October while it undergoes a revamp. HMV says it will return with a "reimagined space" designed to improve the shopping experience for customers.

  HMV in Coney Street, York Image: Google Street View


While many readers welcomed the investment in the city centre store, the announcement prompted a trip down memory lane as people shared memories of where they used to buy their records. For some it would be Woolworths, but, for many, York's former independent music retailers were the destination of choice.

  Woolworths on Coney Street.


Several readers immediately recalled Track Records, which once traded in High Ousegate and became a favourite destination for generations of music fans.

They shared their recollections in our Facebook group, Why We Love York – Memories (www.facebook.com/groups/yorknostalgia).

Carl Leng simply posted: "Track Records."

  Track records in Goodramgate. Picture David Harrison..


Paul Wilkinson also remembered the shop, writing: "Track records on High Ousegate, can't figure out which one it was these days tho."

Martyn Jones echoed the sentiment with a brief but enthusiastic: "Track Records."

To jog your memories, Track Records closed its doors for good in 2007, but not after serving three generations with chart hits.

The business began selling jukebox seconds in York's Newgate Market before opening up in Fossgate in 1979. It later moved to the Coppergate Centre in 1985, then High Ousegate in 1992, before finally to its last site in Goodramgate.

Speaking to The Press in 2007 on its closure, Track Records' owner Keith Howe blamed the demise on the rise of music downloading on the internet.

  Keith Howe outside Track Records in 2007. Press photo


Readers also remember another record shop fondly.

Paul Ireson said: "Red Rhino .... The best."

Blank Reg agreed, adding: "Red Rhino on Gillygate."

Tony Kostrzewa set up this shop in 1977 and it was often filled with students and budding musicians eager to get their hands on records and posters. The shop moved from Gillygate to Goodramgate before it closed in 1992.

  Red Rhino record shop in Goodramgate in 1990 Image: Newsquest


Our readers' discussion also revived memories of Sound Effect, another well-known York music retailer.

John Grant nominated "Sound Effect" as his choice, while Blank Reg remembered both the city centre and suburban branches, writing: "Sound Effect, King's Square and the Acomb branch."

For some readers, the memories stretched back decades and were closely connected to family life and growing up in York.

Avril Ellwood recalled: "My son spent his teenage years in Track. He is now in his 50s."

Martin Stubbs remembered another York music retailer. He wrote: "Hugh Robertson - where you could listen to the records in booths before you bought them."

Others highlighted shops that have long since disappeared from the city's retail landscape.

Katie Jayne Cole remembered Riverside Records, while Mike Bristow shared a more unusual recollection.

He said: "Woods Chemist in Osbaldwick had a good stock of Max Bygraves, Perry Como and The Spinners back in the early 70s. Sadly a carpet shop now."

Not all the memories were York-based. Archie Preston fondly recalled Leeds music store Scene and Heard, describing it as "always good".

Linda Metcalfe summed up the mood of many contributors with a simple reflection: "Those were the days."

Although readers reminisced about the city's lost record shops, HMV's planned investment suggests there is still a strong appetite for music retail on the high street.

The company says visitor numbers across its stores have increased this year, with younger customers helping fuel renewed interest in physical music, merchandise and pop culture products.

For York music lovers, the temporary closure may mark the end of one chapter, but many will be hoping the revamped HMV can create fresh memories to rival those made in the city's much-loved record shops of the past.
