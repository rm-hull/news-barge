## New owners plan refurb of popular North Yorkshire pub, which will reopen in 2027

The interior of The Plough Inn at Sleights *(Image: Christie & Co)*

A historic village pub in North Yorkshire has been sold to new owners who plan to reopen it after a refurbishment.

The Plough Inn, a Grade II listed pub in Sleights, near Whitby, has been purchased by David and Katie Cattley, who aim to bring the building back into use in 2027.

They plan to restore and refurbish the property, creating a "warm, welcoming pub" for the community.

Mr and Mrs Cattley said: "We are absolutely thrilled to have secured The Plough Inn.

"As local people, we understand just how important pubs like this are to their communities, and we're passionate about bringing this fantastic building back to life.

"Our vision is to sympathetically restore and refurbish the property, celebrate its heritage, and create a warm, welcoming pub that the village can once again be proud of.

"We look forward to reopening in 2027 and becoming part of the community for many years to come."

Last week, the couple posted online that over the coming months they will personally oversee the refurbishment. But when the pub reopens they won't do the day-to-running themselves.

They plan to recruit an experienced general manager and head chef "who can take real ownership of the operations, build their own teams and help create something special for the longer term."

The Plough Inn, located on Coach Road in Sleights, sits on the main route between Whitby and the North York Moors National Park and offers views across the Esk Valley.

The property includes three letting bedrooms, owners' accommodation, and outdoor trading areas.

The dog-friendly venue also received good ratings on reviews websites.

Google awarded 4.4 stars out of five, based on 631 Google reviews. Tripadvisor awarded 4.1 stars out of five, based on 431 reviews.

The sale was handled by Christie & Co.

Marslie McGregor, senior business agent at Christie & Co, said: "The Plough Inn represented an excellent opportunity for an owner-operator to acquire a characterful village pub with significant potential.

"We are delighted to have completed the sale to David and his partner, whose passion for the hospitality industry and commitment to restoring this much-loved local asset made them the ideal purchasers.

"We wish them every success with their refurbishment plans and look forward to seeing The Plough Inn thriving once again when it reopens."

The pub was sold for an undisclosed sum, off a freehold asking price of £300,000.

However, when the Press reported on the sale in April, the Plough Inn then had a sale price of £375,000.
