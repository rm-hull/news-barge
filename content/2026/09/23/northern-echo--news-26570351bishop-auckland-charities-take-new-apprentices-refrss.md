Two Bishop Auckland charities have taken on apprentices to build skills, create jobs and strengthen their organisations.

The Auckland Project and Angel Trust have hired business administration apprentices with Bishop Auckland College.

The apprentices will support daily services while gaining experience in customer service and administration.

Jane Ruffer, chief executive of The Auckland Project, said: "We believe that investing in people is one of the most powerful ways we can strengthen our community.

"Apprenticeships give local people the opportunity to develop skills, build confidence and establish rewarding careers while making a real difference to the lives of others.

"We're delighted to be working alongside Angel Trust, Bishop Auckland College and New College Durham to create these opportunities and support the next generation of talent in Bishop Auckland."

The Auckland Project has also taken on two finance apprentices.

They will start later this summer, studying for accounting qualifications at New College Durham while working in the charity’s finance team.

The organisations say the appointments show their long-term commitment to developing local talent and creating lasting jobs across County Durham.

The Auckland Project employs more than 200 people, 80 per cent of whom live within 10 miles.

The charity also supports apprenticeships across its operations.

Staff are working towards qualifications from Level 2 Property Maintenance to Level 7 Accountancy, along with Business Administration and Hospitality Supervision.

Ms Ruffer said: "We believe that investing in people is one of the most powerful ways we can strengthen our community.

"Apprenticeships give local people the opportunity to develop skills, build confidence and establish rewarding careers while making a real difference to the lives of others.

"We're delighted to be working alongside Angel Trust, Bishop Auckland College and New College Durham to create these opportunities and support the next generation of talent in Bishop Auckland."

The Auckland Project recently celebrated a trainee management accountant’s success.

He completed his apprenticeship with the charity before taking a new job in Australia, showing how apprenticeships can open doors at home and abroad.

Clair McGregor, chief executive of Angel Trust, said: "Creating opportunities for local people sits at the heart of everything we do.

"Apprenticeships are an important part of that commitment, enabling people to develop valuable skills while contributing to organisations that are working to transform Bishop Auckland.

"We are proud to work alongside The Auckland Project to invest in local talent and help build a stronger future for our town."

Bishop Auckland College helps employers across County Durham recruit and train apprentices.

Shaun Hope, chief executive and principal of Bishop Auckland College Group, said: "Being at the very heart of our community is fundamental to everything we do at Bishop Auckland College.

"Apprenticeships are one of the most powerful tools we have to change lives, providing local people with the skills, confidence and hands-on experience they need to build meaningful careers.

"We are incredibly proud to partner with fantastic local charities like The Auckland Project and Angel Trust.

"Together, we aren't just strengthening the delivery of their essential services, we are investing directly in the future of Bishop Auckland by developing and retaining homegrown talent."

The Auckland Project and Angel Trust work together throughout the year to support residents and strengthen the community.

Surplus produce from Auckland Palace’s Walled Garden is donated to Angel Trust to help families across County Durham.

Together, the organisations offer support, experiences and routes into long-term work to help residents feel proud of Bishop Auckland.
