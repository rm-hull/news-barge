A York residential street plagued by speeding is set for new measures to tackle it amid calls for a rethink.

Humps are set to be installed in Alness Drive and Acomb Wood Drive following surveys which found more than three fifths of vehicles exceeded the 20mph speed limit.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, City of York Council’s Labour transport spokesperson, said she would not wait for a crash to happen before acting, adding the £150,000 scheme would also see roads resurfaced.

But speed campaigner Anna Semlyn said humps were not the answer and called for a wider rollout of 20mph limits across York instead.

Opposition Liberal Democrat transport spokesperson and Dringhouses ward’s Cllr Stephen Fenton said some residents felt resurfacing was a price worth paying for humps they did not want.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/alness-drive-york-speed-bump-plans-cyc.webp)

It comes as the installation of four humps in Alness Drive and two in Acomb Wood Drive was approved at a council Transport Decision Session on Tuesday, September 22.

A council survey in September last year recorded average speeds of 22mph southbound and 23mph northbound.

Speeds reached as high as 26mph southbound and 28mph northbound.

The installation of humps has been proposed after officials decided they would be preferable to speed cushions and raised tables.

Road repairs and resurfacing is also planned as part of the proposals.

Officials stated they hoped the scheme would encourage motorists to drive at more appropriate speeds which would improve safety and reduce the amount of enforcement work required.

But Ms Semlyn, 20 Is Plenty’s campaign manager, said the works planned for the roads did not make sense and expanding signed 20mph zones would make more of a difference.

She added data showed there were no crashes in the roads compared to the 112 seen around York Station in the last 10 years.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/york-councillor-stephen-fenton-lib-dems.webp)

Cllr Fenton said changes between 20mph and 30mph limits in the area meant motorists were driving into parts of them while exceeding restrictions.

He said: “I’ve seen the quite astonishing speeds that some vehicles were travelling first hand.

“But there is confusion and unhappiness about the current proposals, including whether the humps will be maintained and constructed in a robust way.”

Tuesday’s meeting heard the humps would have a smoother incline than speed cushions, meaning they would be less noisy when vehicles drive over them and more comfortable as a result.

Officials said they will also span across the entire width of the road in response to concerns that bigger vehicles could avoid them.

Cllr Ravilious said a lack of crashes and accidents in the roads should not prevent action to stop speeding.

She said: “There’s not just a little bit of speeding here, there’s a lot.

“More enforcement would reduce the need for engineered measures which aren’t always ideal, but without the balance we sometimes have to consider those measures.”
