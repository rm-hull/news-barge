A drunken man who fled a town-centre pub after police warned him about his behaviour, returned 15 minutes later.

Officers went to The Canny Lad in Bede Way, Peterlee, on July 3 after reports of a man behaving in an aggressive and threatening manner.

Peterlee magistrates were told the officers found customer Connor Marsden in a “clearly agitated” condition.

![]()Defendant Connor Marsden was arrested after returning to The Canny Lad pub in Peterlee where police had warned him about his unruly behaviour

Prosecutor Michael Embleton said Marsden wandered around waving his fists threateningly.

Police warned him to control himself or face being pepper-sprayed if he did not “chill out”.

He ran from the pub but returned 15 minutes later.

Mr Embleton said Marsden continued to cause trouble for staff and customers, so police warned him they would arrest him.

After he was detained, Marsden told the officers he was an alcoholic.

The court heard Marsden has three convictions for nine offences, but none involved similar behaviour.

Marsden, 30, of no fixed abode, who was not represented in court, admitted being drunk and disorderly in public.

He apologised to the court and said he would not make excuses for his behaviour.

“It’s inexcusable. I had been going through a lot of problems in my life," he said.

“But I’ve been able to get hold of my drinking and had periods of abstinence.

“I was struggling with withdrawals and how to deal with my mental health.”

Marsden told the magistrates he spent eight or nine hours in police cells after his arrest.

He said he would do whatever he could to avoid returning to drinking and had been “dry” in his latest spell of abstinence for a fortnight.

Magistrates’ chair Sian Allan-Gilbert said the aggravating features were his behaviour and the fact that it upset people who had to witness it.

She said: “You don’t need me to tell you it’s just not acceptable in any circumstances.

“But you are clearly trying to do what you can to deal with your issues.

“Not everyone comes to court and apologises for their actions and that’s in your favour.”

The magistrates imposed a six-month conditional discharge and warned Marsden that any further offence during that time would be dealt with, “at a higher level”.

Mrs Allan-Gilbert told him: “However, if you stay on the right track, nothing more will happen.”

Marsden was also ordered to pay £85 in costs and a £26 statutory surcharge.

Marsden was previously jailed for seven years and four months after admitting causing the death of 10-year-old Melissa Tate by dangerous driving in 2019.
