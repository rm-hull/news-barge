MORE than two in five gamblers in Yorkshire have admitted taking risky actions to continue betting or chase losses.

The findings come from a UK-wide survey by Gamble Mind, which revealed that 41 per cent of gamblers in Yorkshire and the Humber have stayed up all night, missed bills, or skipped meals to keep gambling.

Nationally, nearly half of UK gamblers (49 per cent) reported taking similar actions, with 23 per cent staying up all night to gamble, 13 per cent gambling in secret during work hours, and 12 per cent skipping meals to place a bet.

Paulius Cubera, iGaming expert and chief editor at Gamble Mind, said: "When millions of people are secretly gambling at work, skipping meals, or missing bills just to keep playing, the question isn't how to stop people gambling.

"It’s why so many feel they have to hide it rather than admit it’s got out of hand.

"Gambling is a leisure activity for the vast majority of people, but when the culture around it means someone would rather go without food or lie about where the rent money went than face a loss honestly, the problem isn’t the bet itself.

"It’s that so few people feel able to say out loud when it’s stopped being fun."

The survey gathered responses from 2,001 UK adults who have gambled and explored the behaviours people engage in to continue betting or recover losses.

In Yorkshire, 17 per cent of gamblers said they had stayed up all night gambling, while 11 per cent admitted to gambling during work hours.

Seven per cent had skipped a meal, six per cent had missed paying a bill, and two per cent had used money set aside for a child or dependent.

Nationally, 3 per cent admitted to gambling during a hospital stay or even during the birth of a child.

Mr Cubera said: "Part of the issue is that gambling can be seen as something to be embarrassed about rather than something to be honest about.

"Nobody stays up all night chasing a loss because they’re enjoying themselves, they do it because stopping feels like a defeat."
