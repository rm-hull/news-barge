An international film festival due to land in the North East is looking for filmmakers from across the region.

The Gateshead International Film Festival (GIFF) will take place over three days from July 16 to 18, 2027.

More than 140 film submissions have already been received from over 40 countries.

The festival aims to spotlight regional filmmaking talent alongside independent cinema from around the world.

Steve Wraith, North East actor, writer, and on of the festival's organisers said: "We will be delivering a unique film festival weekend event with the eyes of the world on the North East.

"The focus is on emerging and experienced filmmakers, industry professionals and audiences across our home region for a range of screenings, workshops and community events."

![]()Steve Wraith *(Image: GIFF)*

Steve added: "We want to showcase fresh North East voices and talent alongside international independent cinema.

"The arena is open to everyone."

The festival is the brainchild of ANBOC Ltd (A New Breed of Cinema), a film company born on the banks of the River Tyne.

It has been organised by Mr Wraith, festival programmer and award-winning writer and director Catarina De Cézanne, and producer and filmmaker Neil Jackson.

Screenings will be held at Vue Gateshead, with industry panels and workshops taking place at Gateway Studio CIO on Gateshead High Street.

Mr Wraith said: "Selected filmmakers who attend the festival will also have the opportunity to do a Q&A at the Vue after their screening."

The festival will open with a champagne reception at Hilton Gateshead, and close with an awards ceremony at the historic St. Mary’s Heritage Centre.

Submissions are being accepted through FilmFreeway.

Films from the North East can be submitted free of charge, ensuring that local voices have the chance to be seen alongside international entries.

The festival aims to create opportunities beyond the screen, including industry education, workshops, networking, and audience engagement.

Organiser Catarina De Cézanne, also an independent filmmaker, screenwriter, and script supervisor, said: "As a filmmaker, I understand how important it is to have a space to express and share your art, find your tribe, connect with an audience, and foster meaningful collaborations for future projects.

"I also understand how important it is to make it affordable, open, and accessible for artists of all backgrounds and experiences.

"I envision our festival to operate like a creative hub for artists, and be more than just an entertaining event where we screen good films."

"I want to provide a unique learning and personal experience for people to come back every year, with new surprises beyond our film programme," she added.

GIFF has set out a five-year vision to become one of the North East’s leading annual cultural events.

Organisers hope to expand from the initial three-day event to a week-long international festival.
