M&S, New Look, Sainsbury’s and Oxfam are joining forces to turn unwanted clothes into new materials - and West Yorkshire is set to play a big part.

The project’s first phase will begin in January at Oxfam’s site in Batley, Kirklees.

The retailers and charity are partnering with Circle-8, a start-up that has developed a model bringing together retailers, recovery organisations, recyclers, manufacturers and digital innovators.

Called The Circuit, the initiative will establish routes for old textiles to be sorted and prepared for use as raw materials.

Its aim is to recover, sort and process clothing that cannot be reused, turning it into new or emerging fibres for making clothes.

Partners will explore automated textile sorting at the Batley site. They will also develop reporting systems to improve traceability, identify opportunities for recycled materials and build relationships with recycling companies.

The group will create operating plans to help the system grow and work on a larger scale.

It will also support mechanical and chemical recycling methods to recover materials for textile manufacturing in the UK and overseas.

Oxfam will separate reusable and non-reusable textiles as part of the project, as well as operating the automated sorting equipment, the partners said.

The partnership follows research by waste campaign group Wrap, which found that 1.4 million tonnes of textiles were discarded across the UK in 2024. Half of that amount is estimated to have been made up of textiles that could not be reused.

A report by Parliament’s Environmental Audit Committee estimates that 30% of worn clothes are resold in the UK. The remainder is sent overseas, often ending up in large waste dumps in countries including Ghana and Pakistan.

Rachel Cosgrove-Pearce, retail director at Oxfam, said: “Oxfam has been giving clothes a second life for almost 80 years, and our experience tells us that keeping clothing in use for as long as possible is one of the most effective ways to reduce fashion’s impact on our planet.”

She said: “But we also urgently need better solutions for textiles that can no longer be worn or reused.”

Ms Cosgrove-Pearce said: “Developing the infrastructure to recover valuable fibres from end-of-use textiles is an important part of building a fashion system that uses fewer virgin resources and creates less environmental harm, and The Circuit brings together the expertise and investment needed to move this forward at scale.”

The partners said the project could support the wider UK economy by helping create jobs and manufacturing opportunities.

They also said collaboration across the industry would be needed to develop a more circular fashion industry.

Ben Smith, managing director of Sainsbury’s Tu Clothing, said: “Creating a more circular future for textiles will take collaboration and innovation across the whole industry.”

He said: “The Circuit is an exciting opportunity to work alongside partners from across the value chain to explore how post-consumer textiles generated here in the UK can be given a new life as valuable raw materials.”

Katharine Beacham, head of sustainability and materials in fashion at M&S, said: “(We) believe the most sustainable clothing is clothing that is worn and loved for longer.”

She said: “However, when textiles reach the end of their usable life, we need better solutions to recover and recycle the materials they contain.”

Cyndi Rhoades, chief executive of Circle-8 Textile Ecosystems, said: “The retailers who help build circular textile value chains today will be best positioned to influence and benefit from them tomorrow.”

She said: “Through The Circuit, we’re powering the relationships, infrastructure and commercial frameworks needed to make that possible.”
