Drivers have been slammed for parking on new double yellow lines near a school that were brought in due to safety concerns.

Children being forced to “chicken run” across Green Lane was just one of the issues that sparked a waft of improvements along the busy road near Conyers School.

The pavements have long been used for parents and students to park their cars during pick up and drop off times - but this sometimes “made it impossible” for those walking to pass.

But despite the double yellows recently being installed, Yarm ward Councillor John Coulson said he has been forced to “name and shame” drivers who have already flouted the new rules.

![]()Cars parked on double yellow lines on Green Lane in Yarm *(Image: JOHN COULSON)*

Pictures taken last Thursday show a string of cars parked on double yellows outside the Conyers bus bay, in the lead up to the new traffic lights.

Cllr Coulson said Stockton Borough Council sent enforcement officers last week and dished out penalty notices to those not adhering to the new rules.

“Double yellow lines were put down because of safety issues,” Cllr Coulson said.

![]()The new double yellow lines on Green Lane in Yarm *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

“Pupils and parents were parking on the pavement, sometimes making it impossible for pedestrians, prams, and disabled mobility scooters to actually use the pavement.

“I have witnessed a young mother having to use the road as car(s) were parking on three quarters of the pavement.

“I use (for convenience) a mobility scooter and, twice in one week, I had to use the road as access was impossible.”

![]()The new double yellow lines on Green Lane in Yarm *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Cllr Coulson said the puffin crossing in particular was added due to children crossing the 30mph road: “Pupils on the south side of the Green Lane estates, and from the train station, were literally playing chicken run to cross the road.

“The frustration comes when pupils park irresponsibly and parents and grandparents start to park from 3pm onwards, parking in the north side the pavement from Brittains Garage almost the entire length of Green Lane.

“Enforcement attended last week and even after the double yellow lines were installed people still continued to park on the double yellow lines and the approach to the puffin crossing irresponsibly and were given penalty notices.”

Other works carried out include upgrades to all four bus stops on Green Lane, including new markings and flags, improvements to off-road drainage gullies, and a 32m filter drain.

The remaining footpath works towards Crossways are expected to continue until early October.
