Derry Brabbs found acclaim following the release of James Herriot’s Yorkshire, which shone the spotlight on a landscape that became loved through the Herriot stories. Following its success, he became a prolific writer, publishing books of landscape, walking and heritage.

![]()Derry Brabbs *(Image: Derry Brabbs)*

Derry, who lives in Nidd, near Ripley, was born in 1947 and to mark Yorkshire Life’s 80th, we asked him to choose some of his favourite images of the county. Of his photography, he says: ‘Working outdoors is just like being in a giant studio, albeit with no readily accessible on/off switches! Photographers now have mostly accurate weather forecasts available, which enable them to determine whether or not a long car journey through the night will be worthwhile. Our best images result from unpredictable and surprising light. We need low-angled sunlight to powerfully illuminate the stone walls, buildings and landscape textures. Even though technology now makes it easier to predict, nature still pops up with surprises!’

Derry Brabbs is the photographer who brought the landscape of Yorkshire to the world with his famous book, James Herriot’s Yorkshire, published in 1979. He shares some of his favourite images

![]()Wensleydale *(Image: Derry Brabbs)*

**Wensleydale**

‘The landscape near Hawes and Gayle is covered in the stone barns so crucial to hill farmers and their stock. Although pleasant enough in summer the winter climate soon transformed life and access for vets to attend sick animals was harsh before the creation of four-wheel drive!’

![]()Malham Cove *(Image: Derry Brabbs)*

**Malham**

‘Malham Cove is a huge natural limestone cliff that once comprised a spectacular waterfall but now just has a stream

flowing out of its base. Easily accessed from the village, it has a flight of stone steps up one side enabling walkers to ascent up to the summit. To one side of Malham Cove lies a rugged and uneven limestone pavement. Trees still manage to grow through the harsh terrain and although it’s not the easiest of places to walk across, it is a gloriously picturesque location to visit. One day I got really lucky and was there for a post-sunset pink glow adorning the sky and limestone landscape.’

![]()Swaledale *(Image: Derry Brabbs)*

**Swaledale**

‘Keld is a tiny, remote village in Upper Swaledale within the Yorkshire Dales National Park. Although still isolated, it is now very well known as a welcome resting place for walkers on the Pennine Way Long Distance Path from Derbyshire’s Peak District to the Scottish border and Alfred Wainwright’s Coast to Coast Walk.’

![]()The Dales. *(Image: Derry Brabbs)*

**The Dales**

‘The Yorkshire Dales National Park encompasses Wensleydale and Swaledale, comprising a complex network of stone walling and barns to maintain the safety and security of the sheep population.’.
