This concludes our live coverage of the second Trump administration for the day. Here are the latest developments:

Speaking at the UN general assembly, Donald Trump re-issued his threat to “annihilate” Iran and “drive them into Hell” if the regime didn’t capitulate and make a deal.” He also said he was “working closely with the leaders of Russia and Ukraine” to end the disastrous war, defended his administration’s actions in Latin America – including the midnight raid to arrest former Venezuelan president Nicólas Maduro, defended his drone strikes on boats in the Caribbean and the Pacific, accused Mexico of being effectively controlled by the cartels, and said it was “unacceptable” for the US to share a border with a nation under such conditions, pointed to regime change in Cuba and said he would reject any calls for a “globalist scheme” to control artificial intelligence.

Shortly after his UN address, Trump signed a Greenland deal, which Greenland’s prime minister Jens-Frederick Nielsen said “underlines the importance of the Nato alliance.” In tricky, carefully worded paragraphs, she said the agreement “recognises the US defining historical and ongoing contributions to the security and defence of Greenland,” but it also “respects the sovereignty and the territorial integrity of the Kingdom of Denmark, as well as Greenland’s right to self-determination.”

Trump and New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani appeared friendly after a ‘wide-ranging’ meeting at Gracie Mansion. Trump said he hoped Mamdani would be a “great mayor” for New York, an unusually warm assessment of the democratic socialist leader.

Trump told a CNN reporter at the UN general assembly on Tuesday that he was “surprised” the outlet was covering him, saying it ‘shouldn’t be here’ after he banned the network alongside Politico and MS Now from the White House on Friday. Answering a reporter’s question following her first appearance of the midterm campaign season, Kamala Harris said, “The man is afraid of the voice of the people and that the people understand and know what he’s doing.”

At a joint press conference on Tuesday, Vice President JD Vance and Mehmet Oz, administrator of the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, announced that the federal government was removing 760,000 enrollees from Affordable Care Act insurance marketplaces.

Trump said he is working on a ‘massive’ Belarus, pivoting away from main supplier Canada amid an escalating trade dispute, and moving instead toward an ally of Russia.

Federal immigration officials in Texas prevented US representative Veronica Escobar from fully conducting her congressional oversight duties at a controversial Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detention center, the lawmaker said in an interview with the Guardian.

Senator John Curtis, a Republican from Utah, published a letter calling for a key Senate committee to investigate Trump’s son over his personal and business dealings with foreign governments, as well as his shady ventures in cryptocurrency and predictions markets. Curtis’ letter follows a bombshell report from ProPublica that found a Russian oligarch with close ties to Russian President Vladimir Putin, had helped bankroll Trump Jr.’s lavish Bahamas wedding.

The Republican National Committee has sued top election officials in New Jersey, alleging they failed to produce public voter roll records, including the addresses of noncitizen voters.

The Spanish government has decided to allow the Trump administration’s controversial extradition request to extradite wealthy activist James “Fergie” Chambers to continue in the country’s courts.

Chambers, a US citizen and wealthy donor to leftwing and humanitarian projects, is currently imprisoned in Madrid. It is the first known case of the US seeking to extradite a citizen over alleged support for Hamas, an accusation tied to money Chambers has donated to support Palestinians, including the Sameer Project, in Gaza.

Kamala Harris called Donald Trump’s decision to ban three news organisations from the White House “absolutely abhorrent.”

Answering a reporter’s question following her first appearance of the midterm campaign season, Harris said, “The man is afraid of the voice of the people and that the people understand and know what he’s doing.”

Harris had been speaking at a roundtable on Black maternal health in Michigan alongside Senate-hopeful Abdul El-Sayed.

“He’s trying to stifle the ability of journalists to let the people of America know what’s really going on with their government,” Harris said.

60 Minutes’ blockbuster interview with Patrick Clancy, the ex-husband of Lindsay Clancy, failed to provide a major ratings boost for the Sunday show, according to audience figures released Tuesday.

Sunday night’s episode of the newsmagazine show drew an average of 6.77m total viewers, the third-smallest audience in the past 25 years for the second episode of a season, adding to a disappointing debut for Bari Weiss, who, as CBS editor-in-chief, rewired the show, firing several high-profile correspondents in the run-up to the new season.

The 59th season debuted on 13 September with an audience down sharply from the 58th season debut, drawing 7.94 million viewers compared with 10 million for last season’s first episode.

Angela Paxton – the Texas state senator who is currently divorcing US Senate-hopeful Ken Paxton – encouraged Republican women to vote the party “even if we’re mad at them,” the Texas Tribune reports.

Paxton said she was divorcing her husband on “biblical grounds,” citing allegations of adultery, in 2025, shortly after her husband announced he would challenge longtime Texas senator John Cornyn. She has not commented on her husband’s race since announcing the divorce, including excluding his name from a list of endorsements in statewide primary races.

According to the Tribune, Angela Paxton addressed a meeting of the Texas Federation of Republican Women on Friday. In a speech titled “The Elephant in the Room” she encouraged the attendees to vote for the party even if they disliked an individual candidate.

“We have more control over that seat and the future when we control the seat — when it’s still in the family, even if we’re mad at them,” she said. “But if we lose it to the other team, the nuances that we want to fix with maybe a certain candidate are nothing compared to what it’s going to take to get it back, right?”

Senate minority leader Chuck Schumer says the shooting of a Venezuelan man by a federal immigration officer in Texas on Sunday “is a direct result of Trump ordering ICE agents to continue deadly traffic stops.”

“ICE must provide a full accounting of what happened, why deadly force was used, and why despite billions in funding agents are still roaming around without cameras and accountability,” Schumer said in a social media post.

After the shooting, Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, 28, was taken from a hospital in Austin into Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) detention, even though he apparently still has a bullet lodged near his spine.

The supreme court has refused to get involved with a custody dispute between an Alaska surrogate and a California couple whose child was born with a rare and serious heart defect.

On Tuesday, Justice Elena Kagan denied an emergency request from McKenna West, an Alaska nurse and surrogate who had asked the US’s highest court to halt a California court order recognizing the couple, Omar Ahmed and Nausheen Gilkar, as the boy’s legal parents.

In recent weeks, the case has garnered nationwide attention, sparking broader discussions about surrogacy and abortion.

The Cuban delegation withdrew from a session at the UN general assembly after Donald Trump called the country a “failed state”.

“The US government is unable to accept that Cuba is, and has the right to be, a fully independent and sovereign country,” Cuban foreign affairs minister Bruno Rodríguez wrote in a social media post. “The economic blockade, including the energy siege, only threatens Cuba and the rest of the nations. It is an act of genocide and collective punishment. We are a country of peace and we deserve peace.”

Democratic attorneys general from California and New York have sued the Trump administration over its plans to buy back offshore wind leases as Donald Trump seeks to discourage the expansion of wind energy in favor of fossil fuels.

California attorney general Rob Bonta said he was suing over removal of a project off his state’s coast planned by Chicago-based Invenergy. The Trump administration said in June it was buying back the company’s US offshore wind leases for four wind projects on the east and west coasts.

New York attorney general Letitia James, meanwhile, led seven states in suits seeking to block the Invenergy deal and another proposal with Bluepoint Wind, which agreed in April to end a wind farm under development off the New York and New Jersey coast.

The deals would hand energy companies $1.4bn in taxpayer dollars in exchange for the cancellation of multiple projects. In all, the Trump administration has pledged nearly $4bn to companies to walk away from projects across the country.

Another prominent Florida Republican has publicly distanced himself from Donald Trump, days after María Elvira Salazar called out the US president’s immigration-enforcement policy targeting law-abiding undocumented immigrants.

Byron Donalds, the Republican nominee for governor in November’s US midterm elections, launched an updated campaign website on Tuesday that was stripped of almost every mention of Trump and removed a photograph of the congressman and his wife, Erika, posing with the president, the Miami Herald reported.

Until now, Donalds has proudly touted his close ties with the president, running a Republican primary campaign themed largely on Trump’s endorsement, and displaying a large banner on his website advertising his support.

The move comes as Trump’s overall approval rating fell below 30% for the first time, dragged down by dissatisfaction over his handling of the US economy and the Iran war, and Republicans brace for heavy losses in November, including the possible loss of the party’s majorities in the US House of Representatives and Senate.

Republican National Committee sues New Jersey election officials for refusal to turn over voter records

The Republican National Committee has sued top election officials in New Jersey, alleging they failed to produce public voter roll records, including the addresses of noncitizen voters.

The lawsuit follows New Jersey governor Mikie Sherril’s announcement in July that “a software error” in the state’s motor vehicle registration system added 6,600 noncitizens to the state’s voter registration list beginning in 2023. According to the state, 400 votes were cast by noncitizens – although Donald Trump claimed that “thousands of votes” were cast by noncitizens in the state, in an effort to drum up support for his SAVE America Act.

In 2024, Kamala Harris won New Jersey by more than 252,000 votes – meaning that even if all voters were registered as Democrats, which they were not, their votes would not have swung the presidential election.

In Tuesday’s lawsuit, the RNC states that, New Jersey “objected to or denied” several of its records requests “including the request for records of address confirmation notices”.

In July, assistant attorney general for civil rights Harmeet Dhillon wrote to Sherril requesting the “full names, dates of birth, nationalities, residential addresses, and registration dates and locations” of all 6,600 noncitizen voters.

Sherrill has said she will not provide that information. “We now have certain people that, through no fault of their own, have been registered to vote,” she said at a press conference. “It may imperil their immigration status.”
