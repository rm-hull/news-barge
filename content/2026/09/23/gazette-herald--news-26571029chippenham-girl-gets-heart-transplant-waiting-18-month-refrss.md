A five-year-old girl is thriving at school after receiving a life-saving heart transplant.

Amelia Bolter, from Chippenham, spent 18 months at Great Ormond Street Hospital (GOSH) waiting for a new heart.

Her mother, Jodie, has described the journey as “extraordinary” and is encouraging families to talk about organ donation.

Jodie Bolter said: "She’s like a normal little five-year-old girl. You wouldn’t even think anything happened. She just fits right in, which is so nice."

Amelia was a one-year-old when she was diagnosed with dilated cardiomyopathy, a form of heart failure. She was transferred from Bristol Children’s Hospital to GOSH and placed on a Berlin Heart machine while she waited for a suitable donor.

Jodie, who was seven months pregnant at the time, gave birth to their second daughter, Blossom, during their stay in London.

The family was supported by The Sick Children’s Trust at Guilford Street House, a free ‘Home from Home’ just minutes from Amelia’s ward.

![]()Amelia Bolter pictured with mum Jodie at Great Ormond Street Hospital in 2023 *(Image: The Bolter Family)*

Mrs Bolter said: "It really felt like home to us because of those home comforts. As well as our own room, there was also a lounge with the telly. It was so nice, and the staff are so kind and friendly.

"You'd sit there in front of the telly, have a cup of tea and some biscuits, and you think it feels like home. It just gives you that break from hospital life."

The couple said staying at Guilford Street House helped ease some of the financial pressure of living away from home for so long.

Mr Bolter, a postal worker, was able to transfer his job to London, while Mrs Bolter received maternity pay and support as Amelia’s carer. The family later organised fundraising events to thank the charity for its support.

Mrs Bolter said: "After we left, we worked out how much it cost the charity for us to stay for 18 months, we actually raised the money to pay it back.

"It felt really good to give back after what this amazing charity gave to us, keeping us with Amelia that entire time. It really was the least we could do."

Though Amelia received her transplant and is now doing well, her recovery has not been without complications. She later developed post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder, a form of cancer, and spent five weeks in hospital.

She is now stable and continues to be monitored.

Amelia attends primary school with the support of speech and language therapy and teaching assistants. The family has since settled back in Chippenham.

Blossom is now thriving at nursery, and their youngest child, Ellis, is 17 months old. Jodie and Rich married last year.

Mrs Bolter said: "Amelia is here because of them, and we’ll never, ever forget that. It is the reason why all our kids, even Ellis, are registered donors.

"If the unthinkable happens and one of them isn’t going to make it, it can offer another child their life back. Nobody wants to think about it, but I find that thought quite comforting, a part of my child living through someone else.

"Organ donation can be so difficult to think or talk about, particularly when it involves a child, but it’s so important to have conversations around this."

In the UK, 30 children became organ donors in 2025/26, while 255 children received transplants.

Eight children died while waiting for a transplant during the same period. Anyone of any age can join the NHS Organ Donor Register. In cases where a child was already registered, no families overruled the decision.

More young donors are needed to help save the lives of children still waiting for a transplant.
