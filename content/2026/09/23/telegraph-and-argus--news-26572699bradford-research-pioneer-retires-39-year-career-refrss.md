A RESEARCH leader is retiring after nearly four decades.

Professor Anne Forster has spent nearly 40 years advancing ageing and stroke research in Bradford.

Over her career, she has helped shape national and international stroke rehabilitation.

Professor Forster joined Bradford Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust in 1987, where she and Professor John Young founded the Academic Unit for Ageing and Stroke Research (ASR).

She said: "It has been a privilege to work alongside so many dedicated colleagues over the past 39 years.

"I am incredibly proud of what we have achieved together, from building the Academic Unit into a nationally recognised centre of excellence to conducting research that has made a real difference to patients and their families.

"I want to thank all the patients, carers and clinicians who have worked with us over the years, and I look forward to seeing the unit continue to thrive in the years ahead."

What began as a team of two has since grown into one of the UK’s leading research units, with more than 40 researchers.

Professor Forster, a trained physiotherapist, focused her career on improving outcomes for people affected by stroke and age-related conditions.

Her research led major studies on carer support, information provision, and reducing sedentary behaviour, directly influencing stroke care.

She led the ASR from 2015 to 2023 and also held senior roles at the University of Leeds, including director of research and deputy director at the Leeds Institute of Health Sciences.

In 2017, she was named an NIHR senior investigator, one of the highest honours in patient-focused health research.

Professor Mel Pickup, chief executive of Bradford Teaching Hospitals, said: "We are truly grateful for the legacy of research excellence, leadership and mentorship that Anne leaves behind after an extraordinary career.

"Her impact will continue through the colleagues, researchers and patients whose lives she has influenced."

Andrew Clegg, head of the ASR, also praised her contributions.

He said: "Anne has played a pivotal role in establishing the unit as one of the UK’s leading centres for ageing and stroke research.

"The strong foundations she has created, together with the talented team she has developed, mean the unit is well placed to continue delivering world-leading research that improves the lives of patients."

Professor Forster’s retirement marks the end of a career defined by leadership, innovation, and lasting impact in health research.
