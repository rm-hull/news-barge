**What is an itch, and why are most so easily calmed with a simple touch?**

![Man used the wooden back scratcher for his back itch therapy in small trees background closeup.; Shutterstock ID 2142811281; purchase_order: -; job: -; client: -; other:](https://www.newscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/SEI_302535788.jpg?w=840)

*Mark Dirnhuber*
*Bristol, UK*

Your questioner clearly hasn’t lost many nights’ sleep being driven crazy by, say, a mosquito bite on the back of a toe, seemingly refractory to all treatments other than scratching it until it hurts, which feels better than the itch. Maybe itching has evolved to alert us to attack by blood-sucking arthropods, and if the persistence of the itch leads to a visceral loathing thereof, then surely that too, given dangers like the plague or malaria, will confer an evolutionary survival advantage.
