Two young Teesside entrepreneurs have transformed an empty Saltburn shop into a photography studio - without a photographer.

Snapp’d Studio, at 1a Windsor Road, is set to become the North East’s first "exclusive-hire self-shoot portrait studio" when it opens at the end of September.

Marketer Laura McKeown, 25, and photographer Katy Gettings, 22, founded the business to offer private shoots without a photographer in the room.

Visitors use a professional camera built into a studio mirror and a remote clicker to take as many pictures as they like.

The concept was inspired after Laura, a mum-of-two from Redcar, visited a self-portrait studio in London.

On returning to Teesside, she discovered there was nothing similar anywhere in the North-east and decided to bring the idea home.

The pair snapped up the Windsor Road shop and transformed it in a six-week back-to-brick renovation.

More than 300 people have signed up for updates and early access before the studio has even opened.

Visitors can use the studio for headshots, family portraits, social media content, friendship shoots, celebrations and personal branding.

A changing room and selection of props allow guests to experiment with different looks throughout their session.

“We’re so excited to be bringing a fresh concept to the North-east,” says Laura, who also runs the social media marketing business After Hours Social.

“I grew up near Saltburn and would usually pass Surf Shop on the way to the beach, so for it to be something we’re working on really is a full circle thing for us.

It was the natural next step for our businesses to really come together, and to both see the hype for it already, and work with some great people behind the scenes, is pretty huge.

There is also lots of pressure of course, but I hope everyone welcomes us with open arms and makes some decent use of the space.”

The studio is designed to feel more like a high-end photo booth than a traditional photography session.

Customers enter with a personalised code and are left alone to pose, create and take pictures at their own pace.

Photographer Katy, who also runs Katy Does Media, believes self-shooting solves a common problem for people who feel uncomfortable in front of a camera.

Co-owner Katy, from Skelton, says: “As a photographer who isn’t naturally comfortable being in front of the camera or being told how to pose, the idea behind Snapp’d immediately made sense to me.

When Laura approached me with the concept, there was no hesitation.

There’s no worse feeling than booking a photoshoot, only to get the images back and not like how you look.
