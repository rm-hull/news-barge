Plans to formalise loans linked to the Teesworks regeneration have been approved.

The South Tees Development Corporation has approved plans to formalise its borrowing agreements with the Tees Valley Combined Authority to support Teesworks’ long-term regeneration.

The STDC board unanimously backed the decision on September 10, after the TVCA cabinet considered it on July 31, as the corporation’s lender.

David Smith, chairman of STDC, said: "Formalising these arrangements is an important step in strengthening STDC’s governance and providing clear documentation around the borrowing that has helped enable progress across Teesworks.

"The regeneration of the former steelworks site was always intended to be supported through long-term investment, with borrowing repaid from the economic growth generated on the site. These agreements put that framework on a clear, transparent and robust footing as development continues."

STDC officers will now finalise loan agreements covering repayments, interest, monitoring, information rights and lender protections.

The agreements build on funding that has supported the former SSI steelworks site’s transformation since STDC was created.

The Government-approved business case allowed business rates raised in the Special Economic Area to be kept locally for 25 years to fund upfront borrowing.

With 19 years left on the retention agreement, borrowing remains within the framework originally approved by the Government.

Since its creation, STDC has received TVCA funding to buy, clean up and prepare land, build key infrastructure and attract investment to Teesworks.

The agreements formally document the existing loans while setting clear repayment rules and controls between TVCA as lender and STDC as borrower.

Repayments will come from long-term income generated by Teesworks, including retained business rates, commercial income, project revenue and capital receipts.

That income is backed by Teesworks’ growing occupier base, operating assets, committed investment and long-term contracts.

The approval is another step in redeveloping Teesworks, helping drive future investment, jobs and economic growth across Teesside.
