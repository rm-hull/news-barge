Sam Linfoot finds Dirty Dancing delivers exactly what fans want, combining nostalgia, romance, unforgettable music and a feel-good night out.

There was a definite change in the seasons as I made the drive up the A1 towards Sunderland.

The lighter nights of summer are disappearing, there was a distinctly autumnal feel in the air and, if I'm honest, it felt like exactly the sort of evening when a trip to the theatre was called for.

My destination was the magnificent Sunderland Empire and a show that probably needs very little introduction: Dirty Dancing.

![]()The touring production of Dirty Dancing is at Sunderland Empire this week *(Image: SUNDERLAND EMPIRE)*

Few productions come with quite the same weight of expectation.

Dirty Dancing is one of those films that has become ingrained in popular culture.

Even if you have somehow never watched it from beginning to end, you will know the soundtrack, the dancing, the famous lines and, of course, that lift.

So turning something so familiar into a stage production presents quite a challenge.

We know what is coming.

In fact, much of the audience is sitting there eagerly waiting for those famous moments to arrive.

Yet that actually proves to be part of the fun.

From the opening scenes, you are transported back to the summer of 1963 and Kellerman's holiday resort, where Frances "Baby" Houseman is holidaying with her family.

Baby soon discovers a very different world behind the respectable façade of the resort, particularly among its dance instructors.

And then she meets Johnny Castle.

![]()Dirty Dancing brings the classic story and iconic soundtrack to Sunderland Empire *(Image: SUNDERLAND EMPIRE)*

This touring production stars Joel Benjamin as Johnny, alongside Charlie Varlet as Baby, with Ellie Fergusson taking on the role of Penny Johnson.

The success of any production of Dirty Dancing inevitably rests on Baby and Johnny.

You can have the music, the costumes and the familiar setting.

You can painstakingly recreate the moments everybody remembers from the film.

But if you don't believe in that central relationship, none of it really matters.

There is also the small matter of the dancing.

![]()Charlie Varlet and Joel Benjamin bring Baby and Johnny's iconic love story to life in Dirty Dancing *(Image: SUNDERLAND EMPIRE)*

Dirty Dancing's soundtrack remains a huge part of its appeal, with the stage production featuring the likes of Hungry Eyes, Hey! Baby, Do You Love Me? and the song absolutely everyone is waiting for, (I've Had) The Time of My Life.

This is a show that knows its audience and, importantly, knows exactly what they have come to see.

There is an infectious energy about watching such familiar scenes unfold live on stage, particularly when you're surrounded by an audience who know them just as well as you do.

But what I've always thought makes Dirty Dancing more interesting than people sometimes give it credit for is that underneath the great music, romance and dancing, there is actually a really good coming-of-age story.

![]()The award-winning stage production of Dirty Dancing is at Sunderland Empire until Saturday *(Image: SUNDERLAND EMPIRE)*

Baby starts the summer as the protected daughter who views both her parents and the world around her in a particular way.

By the end of it, both have changed.

Johnny, equally, is much more than simply the impossibly cool dance instructor she first encounters.

That gives the story its heart and prevents the stage version becoming simply a procession of famous songs and scenes.

And then we reach the moment the entire theatre has been waiting for.

Let's be honest, there isn't a person in the building who doesn't know it's coming.

We know the line.

We know the song.

And we most certainly know the dance.

It's one of those rare moments in musical theatre where the anticipation from the audience is almost as much a part of the experience as what's happening on stage.

![]()Cast members perform in Dirty Dancing, now showing at Sunderland Empire *(Image: SUNDERLAND EMPIRE)*

That familiarity should perhaps work against a show like Dirty Dancing.

Instead, it's one of its greatest strengths.

There is something hugely enjoyable about being in a theatre surrounded by hundreds of people who know exactly what's coming and are collectively willing it to happen.

This isn't a production trying to reinvent Dirty Dancing.

And why should it?

People aren't buying a ticket because they want somebody to radically reimagine the story they love.

They want Kellerman's.

They want Baby.

They want Johnny.

They want the music, the romance and the dancing.

And, above everything else, they want to have a great night out.

That's exactly the point.

As I headed back down the A1 afterwards, into that unmistakably autumnal North East night, it struck me that great theatre doesn't always have to challenge you or send you home deep in thought.

Sometimes it can simply make you forget about everything else for a couple of hours.

Sometimes it can make you smile.

Sometimes it can make you want to get up and dance.

And sometimes, if it gets everything right, it really can give you the time of your life.

Dirty Dancing plays at Sunderland Empire until Saturday, September 26.
