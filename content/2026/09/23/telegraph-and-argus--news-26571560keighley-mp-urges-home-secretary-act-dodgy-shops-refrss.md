AN MP has called on the Home Secretary to take action against illegal businesses operating in his constituency.

Robbie Moore, MP for Keighley and Ilkley, raised the issue following recent raids in Keighley, where seven businesses were targeted in a joint operation by West Yorkshire Trading Standards and the Keighley Neighbourhood Policing Team.

The raids resulted in the seizure of £18,000 worth of goods, including tobacco and cigarettes.

### 'Counterfeit goods were taken away, but these shops are still live and active'

Addressing Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood, Mr Moore said: "On Cavendish Street, in my constituency, seven of these dodgy shops were quite rightly raided by the police.

![]()Keighley MP Robbie Moore *(Image: Parliament)*

"Counterfeit goods were taken away, but these shops are still live and active.

"What seems to fail is where there is then not a combative approach by Trading Standards in the relationship with HMRC.

"So, Home Secretary, what are you doing in relation to having these relationships so that when police rightly do their actions, as I’ve experienced in Keighley, the system then doesn’t fail by other services coming in and dealing with the problem?"

### 'Dodgy mini marts' and 'vape shops' being targeted in raids

Ms Mahmood referenced a new high street unit, set on tackling rogue traders by carrying out operational raids.

She said: "The new high street organised crime work is building on a National Crime Agency programme which was shown to have good effect.

"There’s more officers going after high street organised crime, so that’s exactly your dodgy mini marts, your vape shops, your businesses that appear overnight and have blacked out windows and all sorts of nefarious activity happening behind the scenes, which usually the local community has spotted and is well aware of.

"The good thing about the new high streets organised programme is that it’s not just a policing response.

"Through that work, we are funding Trading Standards, HMRC and also taking along immigration enforcement as well."

### Response 'weak and not strong enough'

In a response on social media, Mr Moore said: "Dodgy shops should be shut down for good and their traders locked up.

"They should be replaced with proper independent, local businesses who are not exploiting our town.

"I put these points directly to the Home Secretary.

"Her response - weak and not strong enough. We certainly are not seeing the benefit of her ‘multi agency’ approach in Keighley.

"I am determined to clean up our town."
