Visitors to a North East museum can see a rare Turner watercolour when it returns to public display for the first time in years.

Mustering of the Warrior Angels will go on show at Preston Park Museum in Stockton when the Tiny Things, Treasured Tales exhibition opens on November 14.

Inspired by John Milton’s Poetical Works (1835), the dramatic watercolour by JMW Turner is one of the museum’s most significant and rarely displayed works.

Councillor Nigel Cooke, cabinet member for environment, leisure and culture at StocktonCouncil, said: "This is the latest exciting exhibition to come to The Spence Building and we’re so proud to be partners of Turner on the Tees and The Turner Prize, which is such a prestigious annual award.

"From Turner’s own masterpiece to the tiny treasures on display, which have been co-curated by the Young Producers, people will be able to discover the stories behind each object as well as the personal connections that inspired the young people who selected them.

"We’re looking forward to welcoming returning visitors as well as those people visiting the region for the first time, soaking up all Turner on the Tees has to offer.

"And keep looking out for details of a new immersive, light installation coming soon, opening at the Museum in October."

The exhibition is part of Turner on the Tees, a Tees Valley-wide celebration of creativity and culture staged alongside the 2026 Turner Prize at MIMA in Middlesbrough.

The watercolour was last exhibited in 2018 and donated to the museum through the Edwin Clephan Memorial Bequest.

It depicts heavenly armies preparing for a cosmic battle, with bursts of light cutting through swirling clouds and angelic figures.

Turner was best known for landscapes and seascapes, but works like this also helped build his reputation during his lifetime.

More than 200 years later, his work still captivates audiences around the world.

The exhibition will also feature 14 objects chosen by Preston Park Museum’s Young Producers, who help shape its programme.

Their choices include a medieval gold ring, a clay pipe from Stockton & Darlington Railway excavations and an early 20th-century greetings card featuring Stockton-born actress Ivy Close.

Other highlights include an Elizabethan pint measure and “1000 mph Club” cufflinks worn by Jean Oakes, the first British woman to break the sound barrier in an RAF fighter jet.

The Young Producers worked with museum staff to choose the objects and shape the exhibition’s interpretation.

Tiny Things, Treasured Tales runs from November 14 to February 21, 2027.

Entry is included with a museum day or annual pass, available in advance at prestonparkmuseum.co.uk.

Turner on the Tees is funded by the National Lottery through Arts Council England.

The exhibition is also funded by the Stockton and Darlington Railway 26 Miles Heritage Project, backed by the National Heritage Lottery Fund and the Tees Valley Museum Group.
