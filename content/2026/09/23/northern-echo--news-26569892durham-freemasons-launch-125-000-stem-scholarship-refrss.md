A new scholarship aimed at helping talented young people overcome financial barriers to pursue careers in science and technology is being launched thanks to a major donation from Durham Freemasons.

The £125,000 endowment will support students from the North East to study STEM subjects at Durham University and is funded through the legacy of Norman Jackson, a member of Universities Lodge No. 2352.

The scholarship, named in his honour, will provide £5,000 per year for four years to each recipient.

![]()Clive Roberts discusses the use of the Scholarship with Duncan Maw *(Image: Keith Blundy)*

Alex King of Universities Lodge led the development of the scheme and said: "We wanted to use part of Norman’s legacy to create something permanent, something that would open the door to Durham University for talented young people who might otherwise find the financial barriers difficult to overcome.

"By creating an endowment, the scholarship can continue supporting students for generations.

"It means Norman’s generosity will still be changing lives many years from now."

![]()Clive Roberts discusses the use of the Scholarship with Duncan Maw *(Image: Keith Blundy)*

Born in Sunderland in 1927, Mr Jackson studied metallurgy at Durham and joined Universities Lodge in 1953.

He went on to have an international career in mining and engineering, including work in Zambia and as a consultant to Anglo American in South Africa and Zimbabwe.

Despite living abroad, he remained a committed member of Universities Lodge for 65 years until his death in Zimbabwe in March 2018, aged 91.

![]()Duncan Maw, Phil Twizzell, Clive Roberts, Alex King, Clinton Leeks *(Image: Keith Blundy)*

In his will, Mr Jackson left half of his Spennymoor Trust Fund to "the Master of Universities Lodge to distribute to Masonic charities."

After legal and financial checks, the national Freemasons’ Charity received £943,017.43 on behalf of the lodge in July 2019.

The Norman Jackson Fund was then established and is jointly overseen by Universities Lodge and the Durham Freemasons’ Charity.

![]()Clinton Leeks explains the setup of Durham Freemasons with Clive Roberts *(Image: Keith Blundy)*

The new scholarship will support students from low-income backgrounds and areas with low university participation, with preference given to those from the North East.

The £125,000 endowment will fund a new scholar every four years, with the first expected to begin at Durham in autumn 2027, marking what would have been Mr Jackson’s 100th birthday.

Phil Twizell, who was Treasurer of Universities Lodge when the legacy was received, said: "Norman spent much of his life thousands of miles away, but for 65 years he maintained his membership and his connection with Universities Lodge.

![]()Clinton Leeks, Clive Roberts and Duncan Maw discuss the work of Durham Freemasons Charity *(Image: Keith Blundy)*

"When his remarkable legacy was received, we knew we had been entrusted with something very special.

"To see part of it now returning to the university where Norman studied, and creating opportunities in his name, is a fitting way to remember one of our members."

Professor Clive Roberts, executive dean for science at Durham University, said the scholarship would help students overcome financial challenges and fully participate in university life.

![]()Clinton Leeks, Clive Roberts and Duncan Maw discuss the work of Durham Freemasons Charity *(Image: Keith Blundy)*

He said: "We work hard to encourage talented students from our region to see Durham as a place where they can fulfil their potential.

"For some, financial pressures can still affect their ability to take up their place and participate fully in university life.

"The Norman Jackson Scholarship will help students make the most of the opportunities available across our science disciplines.

"It is particularly fitting that Norman studied metallurgy at Durham and that his legacy will now help a new generation of North East students follow their own path."

Clinton Leeks OBE, Deputy Grand Superintendent of Durham Freemasons, said the scholarship embodied the values of Freemasonry.

He said: "This is what Freemasonry is about.

"One man’s generosity is being turned into opportunities for young people he could never have known, but whose lives could be changed because of what he left behind.

"Durham Freemasons have been making a difference in our communities for generations.

"This scholarship takes that commitment forward, investing in young people who may become the scientists, engineers and innovators of tomorrow."

Duncan Maw, Deputy Chair of the Durham Freemasons Charity, said the fund would create a long-term impact.

He said: "We were entrusted with a remarkable legacy, and our responsibility is to turn that generosity into something that makes a real and lasting difference.
