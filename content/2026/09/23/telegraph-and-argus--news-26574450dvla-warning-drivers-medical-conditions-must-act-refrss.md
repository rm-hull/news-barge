Drivers have been warned they could face £1,000 fines or even lose their driving licence if they do not declare their medical conditions to the DVLA.

According to the government website, there is a long list of conditions drivers must tell the DVLA about when applying for their licence.

Failure to do so could land them in hot water, although it is worth noting that there are exceptions specific to different medical conditions.

The DVLA has issued a further warning to anyone with a medical condition, reminding drivers that they must be report them.

Taking to X, formerly known as Twitter, the DVLA said: “If you’re a car or motorcycle licence holder, you can check and report your medical condition at [https://gov.uk/health-conditions-and-driving/find-condition-online](https://gov.uk/health-conditions-and-driving/find-condition-online)

“You must tell DVLA if a condition affects your ability to drive safely.”

## Which medical conditions need to be reported to the DVLA?

As explained on the gov.uk website, motorists with a driving licence must tell DVLA if:

* you develop a ‘notifiable’ medical condition or disability
* a condition or disability has got worse since you got your licence

Notifiable conditions are described as anything that could affect your ability to drive safely.

This includes conditions such as:

* diabetes or taking insulin
* syncope (fainting)
* heart conditions (including atrial fibrillation and pacemakers)
* sleep apnoea
* epilepsy
* strokes
* glaucoma

To find the full A-Z list, visit the gov.uk website; here you can also report it online using the new service as mentioned above.

If you have a medical condition that affects your driving and do not voluntarily give up your licence, you must inform DVLA.
