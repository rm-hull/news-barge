**23 September 2026** — A Scarborough couple who have transformed the lives of six children through the care system are taking on a leading role to support other local families by launching North Yorkshire’s fifth Mockingbird hub.

**Jayne and Tom Dockerty**, who began fostering during the Covid-19 pandemic, will run the new hub in the seaside town. The launch marks the fifth active Mockingbird hub across North Yorkshire—and the third specifically inside Scarborough—building on established programs running in** Thirsk**and** Selby**.

The innovative model, run under license in the UK by **The Fostering Network**, structures foster support around an extended family framework. A central “hub home” connects a constellation of six to ten satellite foster families, providing peer support, shared social activities, and sleepovers to improve home stability and prevent placement breakdowns.

> **Jayne Dockerty:**
>
> The Mockingbird hub offers that extended family feeling. Children are able to build relationships with familiar families through activities, outings, sleepovers and social events. It creates a sense of belonging and helps young people find their voice and build confidence.

Over their five years with **Fostering North Yorkshire**, the couple have looked after children ranging from a two-day-old baby to a 16-year-old teenager. Their work spans long-term fostering, short-term care, day-care, and sleepover support. Among their key milestones, the couple successfully supported a baby’s return to its birth parents—resulting in Mrs Dockerty being asked to become the child’s godmother—assisted a teenager into semi-independent living, and guided a young boy through to successful adoption.

The couple are utilizing the launch to highlight a critical shortage of sleepover and respite carers specifically for teenagers within the North Yorkshire care framework.

> **Jayne Dockerty:**
>
> There is a real shortage of sleepover opportunities for teenagers and their carers. These short breaks can provide valuable support for everyone involved. It’s a chance to offer reassurance, share advice and remind teenagers and carers that they’re not alone.

North Yorkshire Council joined the Mockingbird programme alongside 22 other national fostering services in 2019. Commenting on the expansion, **Cllr Janet Sanderson**, executive member for children and families, praised the couple’s commitment to providing stability and encouragement to vulnerable local youth during challenging times.

> **Mr Tom Dockerty:**
>
> We love making a difference in children’s lives, But the truth is that they make such a rewarding and loving difference in ours.

For more information on getting involved with the local service, visit the Fostering North Yorkshire portal or read more on The Fostering Network’s Mockingbird Framework.
