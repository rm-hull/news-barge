# Why the Chancellor’s task just got a whole lot tricker ahead of the Autumn Budget

## The high-wire act for the Chancellor just got a whole lot trickier with Government borrowing rising in August to the second-highest level for the month on record.

Ahead of the Autumn Budget, John Healey is going to have to confront the reality that public sector borrowing was £3.5bn more in August than what official Government forecasters had been expecting.

The figure was £2.9bn, or 19 per cent, more than the same month last year. To put this into context, this is the second-highest figure for August borrowing on record, surpassed only by the number in 2020, during the Covid pandemic.

By all accounts this is an alarming figure and puts into doubt Prime Minister Andy Burnham’s pledge to provide support to families facing the cost of living squeeze.

Healey and Prime Minister Andy Burnham face a break or break Budget. They either break down the stubborn resolve of Labour’s backbenchers or they break the economy.

The Government has talked a lot about delivering growth in every postcode but that needs to be underpinned with fiscal probity.

The latest borrowing figures show that there is going to be little headroom for the Chancellor at the upcoming Autumn Budget.

With one-in-five working age people in England and Wales receiving Universal Credit, there is clearly a need to reform the welfare system. That means making difficult decisions that may not sit well with Labour backbenchers.

Shirking difficult decisions is not going to win the confidence of markets nor reassure the electorate.
