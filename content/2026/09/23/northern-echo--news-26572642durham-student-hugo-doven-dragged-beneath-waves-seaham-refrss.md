A "bright" student was seen shouting for help before being dragged under the waves while swimming off the County Durham coast, an inquest heard.

Witnesses heard him shouting for help before he was pulled ashore and airlifted to the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle.

An inquest into his death, held at Crook Coroners Court on September 18, heard that the Durham University student, was enjoying a day at the seaside when he became "distressed in the water."

Despite a rescue operation, the 20-year-old suffered fatal injuries and died days later on May 2 - on his mum's birthday.

As reported, emergency services were called to Seaham Beach shortly after 4.30pm after a member of the public spotted him in difficulty in the water.

Witness Brian Deary, a security guard, described the moment he first spotted what he thought was someone "waving in the water."

He said he could see a white male "waving frantically in the water and looked like he was shouting."

He described the waves as "big at the time" and first thought the male was "showing off."

But after seeing him "dragged under the water a few times", he knew he was in trouble and called 999.

He said after making the call, he saw Hugo go under the water and back to the surface again "three to four times", and then go down again and "seemed to stay under the water."

He said he could see Hugo "struggling" and "getting weaker" as time went on.

Police officer Dylan Middlemiss, who attended the scene, said he arrived at 4.45pm and described seeing a crowd of people on the beach.

He said a member of the public stated there were "belongings at the bottom of the embankment."

The PC said he saw a towel with a bag and clothing laid on it, where he found a wallet and a driving licence with Hugo's details on it.

Detective Sergeant David Airey, from Durham Police, said text messages between friends confirmed Hugo was "solo swimming" after previously spending the afternoon with a friend in Newcastle.

He described the sea at the time as "moderate to rough" and could see how someone "could be swept out quite quickly."

Hugo was airlifted to the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle before later being transferred to the Freeman Heart Unit, where he remained in a serious condition until his death.

A post-mortem examination was carried out by Dr Sam Hoggard at Newcastle Freeman Hospital Mortuary on May 12.

His cause of death was given as a hypoxic-ischaemic brain injury, which occurs when the brain is deprived of adequate oxygen and blood flow, cardiac arrest and drowning.

Coroner Crispin Oliver said there were "no signs to indicate he had taken any substances" at the time of his death.

Hugo was born in Guildford, Surrey, and lived in Farnham.

He lived six hours away in Durham, where he was known by his university friends as the "kindest, funniest, loveliest person they had ever met."

Paying tribute to her son, Louise Dovern said: "Hugo was the youngest of my three sons.

Growing up we regularly spent weekends visiting the coast and he made many friends throughout his life.

"He was a bright, well-rounded child and loved his sport."

She added he was a "fiercely intelligent student" and "talented in sport".

His dad Adam Doven described his son as "adventurous" and that he was a "kind, upbeat young man with a brilliant sense of humour."

He said he was "very reliable" to his family and friends and kept in close contact with his family despite the six hour distance.

He said he was a "keen colder water swimmer", often going two to three times a week.

Hugo had a "really great friend group in Durham", his dad said, and he was "having a ball" at university.

Adam said his son was "very adventurous", with a love of travel, and was "always on the go".

The family were preparing to go away together in Spain in August.

He also had a trip planned with his friend to Ukraine in the summer.

His dad said: "Hugo's passing has left a huge hole in our lives and will be sorely missed."

Coroner Crispin Oliver ruled that Hugo's death was an "accident".

He said the 20-year-old "made a bit of a miscalculation" about the conditions and suffered a "tragic accidental death" after getting into difficulties while swimming.
