## Warning over year-round parking ban at major York shopping centre

The proposals aim to tackle parking problems at the McArthur Glen Designer Outlet *(Image: McArthur Glen Designer Outlet)*

A DECISION to make a seasonal parking ban at a major York shopping centre permanent to tackle congestion has prompted a warning that it could make the situation worse.

Plans to paint double yellow lines on a stretch of Naburn Lane and in St Nicholas Avenue leading to the McArthur Glen Designer Outlet have been approved.

Cllr Kate Ravilious, the council's Labour transport spokesperson, said the measures were needed to stop obstructive parking which has dogged both roads in the run up to Christmas.

But Paul Tyler, the shopping centre's manager, said preventing cars from parking in St Nicholas Avenue as a last resort would fuel congestion.

It comes as proposals for the no waiting at any time restrictions in the roads were approved at the council's Transport Decision Session on Tuesday, September 22.

Traffic cones which are temporarily placed in the roads every year during the Designer Outlet's Winter Wonderland event would no longer be needed as a result.

Restrictions will be in place on Naburn Lane from just after the junction with Fulford Court to just before the Yorkshire Water treatment plant.

Double yellow lines will be painted on both sides of the road, except on one side on the stretch of Naburn Lane in front of homes there.

Restrictions will also be brought in along both the outer sides of St Nicholas Avenue and those closest to the central reservation.

They would be in place from the A19 Fulford Interchange up to and including the roundabout in front of the Designer Outlet's car park.

A City of York Council report stated making restrictions which are temporarily in place in the run-up to Christmas permanent would help tackle parking on verges and footpaths.

![]()The proposals aim to tackle parking problems at the McArthur Glen Designer Outlet *(Image: McArthur Glen Designer Outlet)*

Tuesday's meeting heard parking issues had been reported in St Nicholas Avenue in recent years, with cars being left on the roadside, on verges and in the central reservation.

Officials said traffic cones had been damaged and the year-round restrictions would also help to manage parking during the year if the outlet puts on more events.

Talks between council officials and the shopping centre over the latter covering the cost of putting in the restrictions are set to take place.

Outlet Manager Mr Tyler said problems during the festive period stemmed from too much demand for its 2,700 spaces, 1,000 of which are reserved for the Park & Ride there.

He added the outlet welcomed plans for Naburn Lane but urged the council to look again at those for St Nicholas Avenue.

Mr Tyler said: "I believe this is going to make congestion worse."

"I'm suggesting that you leave the length of St Nicolas Avenue without double yellow lines because that gives us all a last resort for 150 vehicles to park safely."

Cllr Ravilious said she had to take the safety of all road users and the current broader impact on congestion into account.

The executive member, whose Fulford and Heslington ward covers the site, said: "I've seen first hand the issues caused by obstructive parking which is really time-consuming for council officers to deal with."

"We will need to consider the potential for events at the Designer Outlet at other times of the year, it's great that it's so successful but we've got to manage parking and make sure everyone's safe."

"There's a need to balance parking with maintaining traffic flows, I've heard instances of buses missing the stop at the Designer Outlet because the approach was so congested."
