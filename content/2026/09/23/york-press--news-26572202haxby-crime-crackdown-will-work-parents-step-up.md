## 'A small minority are ruining Haxby': Call for parents to take responsibility

I sincerely hope that the recent anti-crime initiatives in Haxby prove successful and that local residents continue to provide the police with the information and intelligence they need to identify offenders and bring them to justice.

The vast majority of people in Haxby take pride in their village and treat others with respect.

Unfortunately, a small minority continue to engage in antisocial and criminal behaviour, showing little regard for the community or its residents.

Tackling this problem requires both effective policing and strong support from the public.

However, the issue also raises important questions about parental responsibility.

Parents and guardians should play a greater role in encouraging respect, accountability and good behaviour.

Early intervention, appropriate supervision and setting clear boundaries can make a significant difference in preventing young people from becoming involved in criminal activity.

Measures such as curfews, electronic tagging and court-imposed penalties may act as effective deterrents and help change the mindset of those who believe that vandalism, intimidation and other forms of mindless crime are acceptable.

Ultimately, creating a safer Haxby will require a combined effort from the police, local authorities, parents and the wider community.

By working together, we can help ensure that the village remains a welcoming and respectful place for everyone.

**John JonesRusset HouseBirch CloseYork**

## Tourist tax could help put York on World Heritage list

Matthew Laverack writes (letters September 19) that York’s streets and public spaces are a disgrace.

He says they are dirty and shabby.

Furthermore, the paving is a mess with tarmac replacing paving stones all over the place which looks dreadful.

The signposts in the city centre feature peeling paint! I agree 100%; central York is a mess.

I also fully support the proposal for a £1 a night, per person, tourist tax which is forecast to yield £26 million p.a. across York and North Yorkshire.

However, I don’t think that I have ever seen any proposals from the mayor David Skaith about how he will allocate this money for expenditure.

I hope that it won’t just be jam-spread to communities on the basis of their population.

That would miss a huge opportunity.

I am looking for the mayor to call on communities and campaigning groups to put forward project proposals designed to make a real difference.

My project suggestions are all York based because I live here.

City of York: Historic Urban Core was put on the UNESCO Tentative List of World Heritage Sites by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport on September 13, 2023.

York clearly ticks a lot of boxes because the entry ends with the words: “York’s Outstanding Universal Value therefore fills a significant gap in World Heritage Site inscriptions.”

Excellent, so what is the hold up?

If it is something at the York end which lacks funding, then that is exactly the sort of thing the mayor should fund; a time limited project bringing a huge benefit.

**Quentin MacdonaldChurch Lane,Nether PoppletonYorkWhat do you think?**
