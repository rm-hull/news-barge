More than 1,000 schoolchildren’s sculptures are being showcased in a North Yorkshire art gallery after taking part in a community project with the acclaimed artist Mackenzie Thorpe.

The sculptures, made from clay, are now on display at Thorpe’s Arthaus Gallery in Richmond as part of the You and I community art project, which involved more than 1,000 pupils from 22 schools across Stockton.

The two-year project was led by Yarm School in collaboration with Thorpe and supported by regional law firm Jacksons.

The young artists took part in workshops with Yarm Prep School art lead Rachel Shepherd and local artist Jessica Jenyns, and also received an inspirational video message from Mr Thorpe.

He said: "It has been fantastic to be part of this project and to see what the children have created over the past two years.

"The idea was to give them the freedom to express themselves and create something that was their own.

"What came back was more than 1,000 completely different characters, each with their own personality.

"Seeing them all together is very powerful, and I wanted people to have the chance to see them here at the gallery.

"The children should be incredibly proud of what they have created."

Each pupil designed and shaped a unique clay figure representing themselves, all of which have been brought together to form a large-scale collaborative artwork.

The sculptures were first displayed at the new Stockton Waterfront Urban Park before being moved to Richmond for the gallery exhibition.

Rachel Shepherd, art lead at Yarm School, said: "It’s been great to see the children’s responses to seeing their artwork in Mackenzie Thorpe’s gallery.

"I’m delighted that their dream has come to fruition."

Dr Huw Williams, headmaster at Yarm School, said: "This project has been two years in the making and has involved an enormous amount of work from everyone involved.
