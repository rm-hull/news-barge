Nigella Lawson's Bake Off debut has been praised by TV critics.

Ms Lawson joined The Great British Bake Off as a new judge, replacing Dame Prue Leith, for the show's latest series.

It comes after she helped boost a York business back in 2018 when she appeared on screen, enjoying a midnight feast, in a dressing gown bought from York boutique Maude & Tommy which resulted in a huge upturn in sales for the local company.

The dressing gown was from best-selling brand One Hundred Stars, and featured a map of Venice in the design.

Not only did she wear it for an episode of At My Table, she tweeted about it afterwards – directing fans to the York boutique on Grape Lane, owned by York businesswoman Anne McCrickard.

Now, her addition to the Channel 4 series of Bake Off has been described as bringing a "new lease of life" by The Telegraph, while Digital Spy lauded her as a "brilliant addition" to the team.

However, the Independent was less impressed, stating the show "wastes Nigella's prodigious charms and misses chance for a true revamp".

The series' return saw Lawson join Paul Hollywood, who has been a staple on the programme since its inception in 2010, along with presenters Alison Hammond and Noel Fielding.

Former Bake Off winner Giuseppe Dell'Anno shared his thoughts on BBC Radio 4's Today, saying Lawson "did produce a step change" to the show and was a "nice fit with the ethos of the programme".

He noted how she balanced Hollywood's technical scrutiny with her ability to capture the 'vibes' and feelings associated with cakes.

Dell'Anno added: "I think, however, that she will get better over time, because my gut feeling yesterday was that she was still in new territory."
