## 'My 70-year love affair with York – in 6 iconic photos'

[1] York Station by William Thackray

*My 70-year love affair with York – in 6 iconic photos – by William Thackray*

MOST of us probably have a favourite teacher from our schooldays – as well as a teacher or two we’d rather forget!

I went to Herringthorpe Junior school in Rotherham in the 1950s and my favourite teacher was definitely Miss Lake.

To be honest I can’t actually remember much about her and I hardly recognised her in a photo I came across on the internet recently.

![]()William Thackray who has created photobooks of York. Image by NQ

But there’s one thing about her I shall never forget – the day she took her class of ten-year-olds on a trip (or “school journey”) to York.

I was smitten and bowled over by York that day. As Baloo said in Jungle Book, “Man, I was solid gone!”

And I’m still solid gone 70 years later.

I’m now a retired vicar living in Northallerton, just north of York, and when my wife and I came here about 17 years ago I whimsically imagined that I’d visit York most weeks to relive, explore and study the places we dashed round all those years ago.

And that’s exactly what I’ve done!

I’ve studied York’s wonderful buildings, the walls, the churches and the snickelways. And every time I complete a project, I put the pictures and information into a “coffee table” book.

Recently Maxine Gordon of the York Press asked me to write one or two articles about my adventures and my books. I’m really thrilled to have a go and so here are just a few pictures from my latest book, The Iconic Buildings of the City of York.

One last thought before we start. I still have the little book I made at school after our 1955 trip to York and in it I outlined our schedule for the day.

![]()William Thackray whose school trip to York inspired his love affair with the city

To this day I can’t imagine how we managed it! What do you think? Leave Herringthorpe School 9am. Arrive York and explore streets near the Minster. Visit the Minster 11.30 am. Walk along Petergate, Pavement and Shambles. Lunch on boat 1–2pm; walk on walls. Pick up bus on Lord Mayor’s Walk. Visit Railway Museum – then Castle Museum. And back to Rotherham by tea-time!

Here are six photos from my book on York's iconic buildings...

**1. York Station**

My 2026 book begins and ends at the station – of course. The present station was designed by Thomas Prosser, the North Eastern Railway’s own company architect, and opened in 1877. Its through and bay platforms, separated by sturdy iron columns, make it look rather like the nave and aisles of the Minster itself. This is one of England’s greatest stations and looks at its best here with “Tangmere” and its carriages on their way to Scarborough.

![]()[1] York Station by William Thackray

**2. St Leonard’s Place**

For several decades I hoped this building would be rescued from its shabby appearance as council offices. And, lo and behold, it was! Now it is as genteel and elegant as York architect George Andrews et al meant it to be back in the 1840s.

![]()[2] St Leonard's Place in York by William Thackray

**3. York walls**

Part of the section of wall behind York Library literally showing layers of history – Roman at the bottom, Danish-cum-Norman in the middle and a medieval top. Where but in York could you see that?

![]()[3] Wall behind York Library - by William Thackray

* **York St John University**

This photo shows its main entrance on Lord Mayor’s Walk, looking at its finest on a lovely September day. I spent three years here in the 1960s when it was a Teacher Training College. What an adventure we once had when we arrived back very late from an outing – and had to find a way into the securely-locked building!

![]()[4] York St John University - by William Thackray

**5. Rowntree Wharf**

Designed by York architect Walter Penty as a flour mill; but from the 1930s to 60s Rowntree’s unloaded cocoa beans here. Now flats and apartments it must still be York’s finest industrial building, mustn’t it?

![]()[5] Rowntree's Wharf - by William Thackray

**6. Holgate Windmill**

The mill always looks so joyous, doesn’t it? Built in 1770 by George Waud, it went to rack and ruin after production stopped in the 1930s. But finally along came the Preservation Society and the job they’ve done is stupendous. Come on Wednesday or Friday morning for a fine bag of Holgate flour!

![]()[6] Holgate Windmill - by William Thackray *(Image: William Thackray)*
