The Kremlin said on Wednesday that British prime minister Andy Burnham was unwise to stir up what they called anti-Russian sentiment, while pouring cold water on hopes of imminent peace talks with Ukraine.

Burnham told the United Nations on Tuesday that London would set up a new National Centre for Information Defence and singled out Moscow for particular criticism when it came to spreading what he called disinformation.

Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov accused Britain of “stoking anti-Russian sentiment and fostering an atmosphere of total Russophobia”, likely in conjunction with France, Germany and other unnamed European states.

“From our point of view, this is an utterly short-sighted policy,” he said. “It in no way brings us any closer to any steps that might, in the long term, lead to a reduction in tensions and de-escalation on the European continent.”

Speaking ahead of a planned meeting in New York between US secretary of state Marco Rubio and Russian foreign minister Sergei Lavrov, Peskov signalled that there were still no plans for high-level talks with either Washington or Kyiv to discuss a potential peace deal.

“Zelensky, if he wishes, may come to Moscow. The necessary security guarantees will be provided to him,” he said, as Putin launched another wave of drone attacks on Kyiv, killing two and injuring at least 22.

## Russia planning major new attack on Ukraine, says Zelensky

Volodymyr Zelensky said on Tuesday that Russia was planning a new major attack on his country.

“There's intelligence right now that Russia is preparing a new massive attack against Ukraine," the Ukrainian president wrote in comments about his meeting with Britain’s Andy Burnham.

He said that there could not possibly be any discussion of easing sanctions against Russia "when Ukrainians live every day under the threat of missile and drone attacks".

Zelensky said Ukraine was in constant communication with its allies on securing new anti-aircraft defences

Russian forces have long unleashed large-scale overnight attacks by drones and missiles but have altered tactics in recent weeks, launching constant barrages throughout the day.

## New UK agency to tackle Russia’s online lies and deepfakes, Burnham tells UN

Andy Burnham has accused Russia of exploiting Brexit to sow division in the UK as he announced a new unit to tackle online disinformation.

The prime minister unveiled plans for a new National Centre for Information Defence to tackle Russian information warfare as he addressed the United Nations General Assembly in New York.

He told world leaders that the UK and its allies had been subjected to an “industrial-scale assault” by hostile states, including Russia, to “prey on people’s fear”.

## Russia attacks port infrastructure in Ukraine's Odesa region

Russia attacked port and civilian infrastructure in Ukraine's southern Black Sea region of Odesa, the regional governor said on Wednesday.

Moscow also hit warehouses used to store medicines, household goods and food, Oleh Kiper said on Telegram.

## Kremlin accuses Burnham of stoking anti-Russian sentiment, says no plans for peace talks with Ukraine

On the heels of Andy Burnham’s comments to the UN general assembly on Tuesday, the Kremlin has accused the British prime minister of stoking ‘anti-Russian sentiment’.

The PM accused Russia of exploiting Brexit to sow division in the UK as he announced a new unit to tackle online disinformation.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said that Burnham's approach, and that of other European countries, was flawed.

"Well, the British Prime Minister said nothing new. That is the first point. The second is that stoking anti-Russian sentiment and fostering an atmosphere of total Russophobia is what Britain is doing. And now, presumably in unison with Britain, the Germans, the French and a number of other European states are doing the same thing," said Peskov.

"From our point of view, this is an utterly short-sighted policy. It in no way brings us any closer to any steps that might, in the long term, lead to a reduction in tensions and de-escalation on the European continent.”

With Volodymyr Zelensky in New York to discuss a potential ceasefire on energy infrastructure with the Trump administration, Peskov also assessed that the groups for wider Ukraine peace talks were still not in place.

He said there were no concrete details on plans to hold any high-level meeting on Ukraine, and that Zelensky would have to come to Moscow for future talks. Zelensky has previously ruled out such a trip.

## Senior Nato commander warns Putin is becoming more cavalier and open to risk as Ukraine war drags on

Russian President Vladimir Putin is becoming “increasingly cavalier, dangerous and risk accepting,” as his war in Ukraine drags on and as he seeks to divide Western allies, Nato’s deputy commander in Europe said Tuesday.

Air Chief Marshal Sir John Stringer spoke at a security conference in Estonia amid warnings from some European leaders that Moscow is planning to test Nato’s collective defense pact with bolder attacks that have already included sabotage and espionage.

Stringer warned that Nato members need to be aware of the “febrile environment” and careful in their response because Putin would like to provoke the alliance into an “overreaction.”

## The UK government wants families to stock up on food. Here’s what to buy

UK households are being urged to stock up on essential items in case of emergencies, according to reports.

A new public information drive is set to encourage people to prepare for extreme weather, cyber attacks and hostile state action.

The national resilience campaign aims to highlight the steps people can take to stay safe in a crisis that may cause power or water supplies to stop or where shops are forced to close.

## Preview: Marco Rubio to meet Russian counterpart on Wednesday

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio will meet Russian foreign minister Sergey Lavrov in New York City on Wednesday morning.

The meeting comes after Ukrainian leader Volodymyr Zelensky met with US president Donald Trump at the UN headquarters on Tuesday.

Zelensky said he discussed efforts to end the war in Ukraine, including a potential bilateral ceasefire on energy-related targets.

Lavrov is now expected to address the UN general assembly on Saturday.

He said last week that Russia will not stop fighting in Ukraine while any potential talks take place.

## NEW: Russian attack on foreign-flagged vessel in Black Sea kills one, Ukraine says

Russia attacked a cargo vessel sailing under the Antigua and Barbuda flag in the Black Sea overnight, killing the captain, Ukraine's seaports authority said on Wednesday.

The attack had caused a fire which was doused and the crew had been evacuated, it said on the Telegram messaging app.

## Azerbaijan pardons Frenchman Martin Ryan after EU lifts sanctions on Russian billionaire Usmanov

Azerbaijani President Ilham Aliyev pardoned a French national imprisoned for espionage on Wednesday, a day after the EU lifted sanctions on Russian billionaire Alisher Usmanov following what diplomats said was pressure from Baku.

The Frenchman, Martin Ryan, had been serving a ten-year prison sentence in Baku following a conviction in March.

Three European diplomats told Reuters that Paris had indicated in private that Baku was using Usmanov to pressure it over the case of two French citizens held in Azerbaijan, an assertion rejected by Baku.
