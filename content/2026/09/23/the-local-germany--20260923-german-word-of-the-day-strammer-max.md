Germany has a long-running love affair with open sandwiches and *Strammer Max* has arguably set the standard for years. But now there’s a newcomer muscling in on his territory.

**What does* Strammer Max*mean?**

*Strammer Max* is a traditional German open-faced sandwich made with buttered rye bread, ham and one or two fried eggs.

Often served with pickles and tomatoes, it's hearty, simple and deeply satisfying.

Grammatically, *Strammer Max* is a noun phrase consisting of the adjective*stramm* and the masculine proper name*Max*. The adjective appears here as* strammer* because there is no article before it.

You may also encounter *der stramme Max*. Here, the definite article* der*changes the adjective ending from*-er* to*-e*, because the article already signals the noun's gender and case.

In German grammar, this is known as the strong masculine nominative ending *-er*.

The adjective *stramm* can mean "tight", "taut", "firm" or "stiff", depending on the context. When describing a person, it can mean "strapping", "robust" or "well-built".

Historically, the sandwich name is said to have carried a double meaning, since early twentieth-century Berlin slang associated *ein strammer Max* with – ahem – male vigour and stamina.

**Why do I need to know* Strammer Max*?**

Sooner or later, if you spend enough time in Germany, you'll spot *Strammer Max* on a menu in a*Kneipe*, bakery café or canteen.

Thanks to its combination of bread, ham, eggs and enough calories to persuade you that life is worth living again, the dish enjoys near-legendary status as a hangover cure.

It's also the perfect emergency meal when you're hungry, short on time and unwilling to engage in anything resembling serious cooking.

The sandwich recently gained fresh competition from a newcomer known as the *Faule Herren-Schnitte* (lazy gentleman's sandwich).

The challenger snack starts with many of the same ingredients – bread, ham and fried egg – but it's topped with cheese and then baked in the oven to create a crispy, melted effect.

**Use it like this:**

*Ich hätte gerne einen Strammen Max und ein kühles Pils, bitte.*

I would like a Strammer Max and a cold Pilsner, please.

*Wenn du großen Hunger hast, kann ich dir schnell einen Strammen Max machen.*

If you're really hungry, I can quickly make you a Strammer Max.

*Ein Strammer Max ist das perfekte Katerfrühstück nach einer langen Nacht.*

A Strammer Max is the perfect hangover breakfast after a long night.

*Heute Abend gibt es bei uns einfach Strammer Max.*

Tonight we're just having Strammer Max.
