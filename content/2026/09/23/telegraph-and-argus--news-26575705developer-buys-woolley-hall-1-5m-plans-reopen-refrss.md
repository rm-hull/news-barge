A Bradford property developer has bought the historic Woolley Hall estate for £1.5 million and hopes to reopen the landmark building as a hotel, spa and wedding venue by 2028.

Mab Hussain, of the Bradford-based Icestone Group, has completed the purchase of the Grade II-listed Jacobean mansion near Wakefield after it was sold at auction.

The historic hall, which stands within around 18 acres of grounds, has been largely unused in recent years following its closure as a conference and wedding venue.

Mr Hussain said the acquisition was among his favourite projects to date because of the building's history and potential.

Speaking to the Telegraph & Argus, he said: “It's amazing and we're really looking forward to it.

“If you read about it online and in newspapers, it's been a long wait for somebody to buy it and make a use of it again after it has been left empty for the last 10 years.”

## **Plans for hotel, spa and apartments**

Mr Hussain said his preferred vision for the site is to restore it as a hotel complete with a spa while preserving its historic character.

The plans could also include luxury apartments within the estate's former stable buildings.

He said: “We want to put it back into a hotel with a spa, carrying on the existing use.”

“It's got around 15,000 square feet of stables and we want to make those into luxury apartments, with a spa connected to the hotel.”

Rather than redeveloping the site and selling it on, Mr Hussain said Icestone Group intends to operate the venue itself.

![]()The present building in Woolley is an example of early Jacobean architecture, and was built about 1635 and added onto at the turn of the 19th Century by the architect Jeffry Wyatville. *(Image: Wendy North / Woolley Hall / CC BY-SA 2.0)*

## **'Custodians rather than owners'**

Mr Hussain said he has already been researching the history of Woolley Hall, including visits to Leeds University and Wentworth Woodhouse, to learn more about the building and its connections to the influential Wentworth family.

He said he sees himself and his company as “custodians rather than owners” of the landmark property.

Mr Hussain added that he wants visitors to feel as though they have stepped back in time when they enter the hall.

He said: “When somebody walks in, they're not walking into a futuristic house. They walk back in time and appreciate what the architects and owners went through to create it.”

## **Targeting a 2028 reopening**

Mr Hussain hopes Woolley Hall can once again become a destination venue for weddings, conferences and visitors.

While any redevelopment will be subject to planning approvals and the restrictions that come with a listed building, he believes the project could be ready to welcome guests by 2028.

Mr Hussain told the T&A: “I feel proud that, away from Bradford, we're buying assets in other towns and cities and using our entrepreneurial skills to bring these buildings back into life.”

Woolley Hall dates back to 1635 and was formerly used as a residential college, conference centre and wedding venue before being put up for sale by Wakefield Council.

The estate was sold at auction for £1.51 million.
