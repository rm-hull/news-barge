A family who spent nearly a year pursuing legal action against a wedding venue say they are finally "relieved" after recovering money awarded by a court following a dispute over a wedding booking for which they paid £11,500.

The family had booked their wedding through The Monal by Jalwa Ltd, but say they learned on the day of the event that the original venue in Cleckheaton was no longer available and the celebration would instead be held elsewhere. While the wedding went ahead at an alternative venue, relatives claim it was unable to comfortably accommodate their 200 guests and did not provide the facilities they had expected when making the booking.

Fozia Aziz, who led the legal action on behalf of her niece and wider family, said relatives accepted they should pay for the catering that was provided, which they valued at around £4,000, but believed they should be refunded the remaining balance. After attempts to resolve the dispute directly failed, the family brought a claim through the courts and were later awarded more than £10,000, including interest and compensation.

Ms Aziz said enforcement agents have now confirmed the judgment debt has been paid in full following months of enforcement action.

However, The Monal by Jalwa Ltd disputes aspects of the family's account and maintains that proceedings remain active. The company says the circumstances surrounding the dispute are "considerably more complex" than the enforcement outcome alone suggests. The family, meanwhile, say no appeal was lodged within the statutory deadline following the original judgment and that a subsequent application challenging the ruling was refused by a judge.

## **'We're relieved'**

Speaking after receiving confirmation that the money had been recovered, Fozia said the family were relieved to finally bring the lengthy ordeal to a close.

She said: "Right now, we're relieved that we've got the money. It's taken nearly a year of going back and forth. We've had confirmation that they've paid in full. The bailiffs hold the money for 14 days before releasing it to us, but the bottom line is we've got it."

Court documents seen by the Telegraph & Argus show an application by the company to set aside a judgment was dismissed at Leeds County Court in July, allowing enforcement action to continue.

## Family refused to give up

Ms Aziz said there were several points during the process when relatives considered abandoning the claim.

Initially, she said, the family were offered a partial refund but decided to pursue the matter through the courts instead.

"My brother-in-law said, 'Let's just leave it'," she recalled.

"My niece didn't want the stress and wanted to move on, but I said no. There were times when I thought it wasn't worth it. There were times when I nearly gave up. But then I thought if I stop, that's exactly what they want."

According to Ms Aziz, the family faced months of paperwork, correspondence and court applications before eventually securing a judgment and later enforcement action.

## Money will go towards couple's first home

Rather than using the recovered money for another celebration, the family say it will now be put towards the couple's future.

Ms Aziz said: "Something good has come out of it. They've bought their first house together as husband and wife, so they'll be putting the money towards their mortgage."

However, she said the experience had left her niece heartbroken and unable to look back on her wedding day fondly.

Ms Aziz explained: "She doesn't want to talk about it. She doesn't want to look at the photos, every time she thinks about her wedding, it upsets her."

## Venue says dispute remains ongoing

In a statement, The Monal by Jalwa Ltd said it did not accept the family's characterisation of the dispute and said the wedding was due to take place on the same day as a serious incident at its premises, which left the building unavailable.

They said: "We must make clear that we do not accept the characterisation of this matter that may be suggested by the current position regarding the judgment or enforcement action.

"The circumstances surrounding this matter are considerably more complex than that. The wedding concerned was scheduled to take place at The Monal on the same day as the serious incident at our premises, which resulted in the building becoming unavailable.

"Despite the exceptional circumstances and the significant disruption caused to our business, our priority remained ensuring that the couple’s wedding could proceed. We took extensive steps to arrange an alternative venue and made the necessary arrangements and provided the services available to us to ensure that the event went ahead.

"Importantly, the event did in fact proceed. We worked to ensure that the wedding could take place despite the circumstances that arose on the day, and subsequently sat down with the family to discuss the situation and seek to work through the issues arising from what had happened.

"Those circumstances, including the arrangements made by us to facilitate the wedding and the subsequent discussions with the family, are relevant to the dispute and will form part of the evidence and our position in the ongoing legal proceedings. They should not be overlooked when considering the matter as a whole.

"There are now ongoing legal proceedings in relation to this matter. The company is represented by barristers, and our full position, together with the relevant evidence and the circumstances surrounding the arrangements made for the wedding, will be placed before the court.

"It is therefore inappropriate, in our view, for the matter to be presented as though the current enforcement position represents the complete or uncontested account of the dispute. Our position remains disputed, and we intend to have our evidence and legal arguments properly considered through the appropriate judicial process.

"We do not intend to conduct ongoing litigation through the press and, given the current legal position, we will not comment further on the substantive issues at this stage.

"For the avoidance of doubt, nothing in this correspondence should be construed as an admission of liability, an acceptance of any allegation or characterisation of events, or a waiver of any rights, arguments or remedies available to The Monal by Jalwa Ltd.

"If you proceed with publication, we ask that you accurately reflect that the matter remains disputed and that The Monal by Jalwa Ltd has not accepted the characterisation of events presented to you.

"We trust that any reporting will be fair, accurate and appropriately contextualised."

## A year-long fight

The dispute began in late 2025 and has only now reached its conclusion nearly 11 months later.

Looking back on the case, Ms Aziz said she hopes the outcome encourages others not to abandon legitimate claims.

She said: "This is why people don't always pursue these kinds of claims because of the hassle involved, but if somebody is in the wrong, they shouldn't be allowed to do the same thing to somebody else, that's why I kept going."

The Monal by Jalwa Ltd said the matter remains disputed and that nothing in its statement should be interpreted as an admission of liability or acceptance of the family's allegations.
