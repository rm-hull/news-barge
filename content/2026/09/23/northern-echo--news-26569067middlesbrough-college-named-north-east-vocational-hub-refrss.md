A North East college will help shape technical education after being chosen as one of nine centres supporting the rollout of new vocational qualifications across England.

The Association of Colleges has named Middlesbrough College the North East’s Technical and Vocational Excellence Hub.

Jessica Barwell, vice principal curriculum at Middlesbrough College, said: "We are proud to have been selected as a TVE Hub and look forward to working with partners across the region to share best practice, strengthen technical education, and support high-quality professional development that benefits both educators and learners."

The hub is part of a Department for Education-funded programme run by the Association of Colleges to help staff introduce new technical and vocational qualifications across England.

Middlesbrough is one of just nine TVE Hubs nationwide.

The college will bring together schools, colleges, training providers and employers to give learners the skills businesses need now and in future.

The programme supports the rollout of T Levels, new V Levels and revised Level 2 qualifications.

It aims to give students routes that match industry standards and workforce needs.

The college will share expertise, provide staff training and strengthen links between education and industry.

The appointment recognises the college’s work in technical and vocational education and its partnerships with employers to prepare students for work.

David Hughes, chief executive of the Association of Colleges, said: "The Technical and Vocational Hubs will sit at the heart of the Technical and Vocational Professional Development Programme, helping to build capacity from within the sector and supporting providers to deliver high-quality technical education.

"We are pleased to welcome Middlesbrough College as one of our regional hub partners.

"The new role is expected to bring together education providers and employers from across the North East, helping to strengthen skills development, support economic growth and ensure the region is well placed to meet future workforce demands."
