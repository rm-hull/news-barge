**23 September 2026** — An 80-year-old grandmother from Hampsthwaite is back on solid ground after completing a daring aerial wingwalk challenge, raising hundreds of pounds for a national sight loss charity close to her heart.

**Janet Mills** braved the top of a moving aircraft as she took to the skies above Leeds East Airport this September. Her high-flying efforts have raised more than £820 for the**Macular Society**, an organisation she has relied upon since being diagnosed with age-related macular degeneration (AMD) nine years ago.

The condition is currently incurable and stands as the biggest cause of sight loss across the UK, affecting nearly 1.5 million people. AMD causes a gradual deterioration of central vision, heavily impacting independent daily tasks such as reading, writing, and recognising faces.

> **Janet Mills:**
>
> I wasn’t at all worried, I was so excited actually. Everyone else was petrified for me and one of my grandchildren called me Mad Nanna, which made me feel even better actually. I have eight grandchildren who were doing much of the screaming on the day but I could hear my daughter’s scream too! For sure the wing walk was one of the best things I’ve ever done.

For the past several years, Janet has attended regular hospital appointments to receive specialised eye injections designed to halt leaking blood vessels at the back of her eyes, keeping severe sight loss at bay. The progressive nature of the disease forced her to give up driving two years ago, requiring her to rely on public transport and her partner to stay mobile.

Despite the challenges, Janet remains highly optimistic about the future of macular treatment and the ongoing research funded by the charity, which also operates vital local support groups across Yorkshire.

> **Janet Mills:**
>
> The middle of my vision is a bit blurry, but it’s not bad. I know one day I will be at the point of being sight impaired but not just yet. I’m just going to go with the flow because what more can I do? I’m very optimistic by the fact that we have these eye injections… They keep sight loss at bay and for that I’m very pleased to have them. I think eventually we’ll find a cure.

The Macular Society warns that around 300 people are diagnosed with macular disease every day in the UK. The charity is actively calling for increased funding into medical research to find a definitive cure and develop new preventative treatments to stop the disease in its tracks.

For further information regarding local support groups, advice, or secondary services provided by the Macular Society, the public can contact their dedicated helpline on **0300 3030 111** or via email at**[help@macularsociety.org](mailto:help@macularsociety.org)**.
