A woman warned prison staff she would drown herself if she got the chance before she was found dead in a prison bath, a watchdog found.

Angela Montgomery, 51, was recalled to HMP Low Newton after allegedly breaching her licence by harassing neighbours.

She died at the County Durham prison on February 25, 2024, after taking tablets and drowning in a bath.

A Prisons and Probation Ombudsman report found that a nurse failed to pass on Montgomery’s warning the day after she returned to prison.

Montgomery told the nurse on December 31: “I would drown myself if I had the chance.”

But the report said that the nurse "did not note this comment in Ms Montgomery’s ACCT or tell her ACCT case manager".

The healthcare provider, Spectrum, and the prison service have since accepted the findings of the report and are "taking action to address the issues identified".

The nurse failed to record the warning on Montgomery’s suicide and self-harm monitoring plan, known as an ACCT.

An ACCT review took place that afternoon, but the staff member leading it was not told about the warning.

The staff member later told investigators she would have explored the comment if she had known about it.

The PPO said healthcare staff had provided “inadequate” input to Montgomery’s ACCT reviews.

It added: “We are very concerned about the clear failures in communication between healthcare and discipline staff throughout Ms Montgomery’s time in Low Newton.”

![]()HMP Low Newton *(Image: Stuart Boulton/The Northern Echo.)*

Montgomery had been released on licence a week earlier after serving a 12-week sentence for harassment, contrary to a restraining order.

On her return to prison, she told reception staff she was having suicidal thoughts, so staff opened an ACCT.

On January 9, Montgomery told a psychological wellbeing practitioner she had previously harmed herself and “often thought that she would be better off dead”.

She said she had no current plans to act on those thoughts.

On January 18, she told an officer she was depressed and suicidal, prompting staff to reopen her ACCT.

After a court hearing on January 30, an officer recorded that Montgomery was crying and said: “I don’t want to be here, I don’t know how much more I can take.”

Staff reopened her ACCT, but closed it the next day after she said she felt safe in prison and hoped to be released at her next court appearance.

The PPO said Montgomery usually told staff during ACCT reviews that she did not want to die.

But it raised serious concerns that staff failed to pass on her warning about drowning.

The report said: “Ms Montgomery made various comments while at Low Newton indicating that she was at potential risk of suicide.

“She specifically said on 31 December that she would drown herself if she had the chance.”

On the morning of February 25, Montgomery asked an officer for permission to take a bath.

Staff allowed her to use the bath alone because they did not believe she posed a specific risk at the time.

CCTV showed her entering the bathroom at 10.07am.

At around 11.55am, officers began locking prisoners in their cells for lunch, but Montgomery was not in hers.

An officer went to the bathroom but did not see her at first. When she returned moments later, she found Montgomery “submerged under water in the bath”.

Staff raised a medical emergency and began CPR.

Paramedics arrived at 12.11pm, but attempts to revive Montgomery stopped at around 12.38pm and she was pronounced dead.

A post-mortem examination found a number of tablets in her stomach.

The PPO said Montgomery had likely accumulated the medication despite receiving it under supervision.

The report stated: “It seems more likely that Ms Montgomery was able to divert the tablets when issued them each day.”

![]()HMP Low Newton *(Image: Stuart Boulton/The Northern Echo.)*

The investigation found “substantial weaknesses in the supervision of medication”.

Low Newton later began using two officers to supervise medication distribution.

The PPO also found failings in Montgomery’s healthcare.

A clinical reviewer said her care was “not of a satisfactory standard and was not equivalent to that which she would have received in the community”.

The report said staff did not fully assess her alcohol-induced epilepsy or create a care plan to manage the risks.

Montgomery also had no mental health care plan.

The PPO recommended that several disciplines take part in ACCT reviews and that healthcare staff properly record their input.

It also called for staff to follow procedures designed to prevent prisoners concealing or diverting supervised medication.

Low Newton later removed baths from standard wings, but prisoners with assessed healthcare needs can still use the healthcare unit’s bath.

An inquest into Montgomery’s death, held between August 24 and 28, 2026, concluded that she died by suicide through drowning.

The jury found that a contributory factor was the failure to share her warning with officers or take action.

Despite the warning and other signs of distress, the PPO report said “there was little to indicate that Ms Montgomery was at imminent risk of suicide when she died”.

A Prison Service spokesperson said: “Our thoughts remain with Angela Montgomery’s loved ones.

“We have fully accepted the Prisons and Probation Ombudsman’s recommendations and are taking action to address the issues identified in the report.

“That includes improving processes for administering medication and ensuring prison and healthcare staff communicate effectively.”

Spectrum Community Health CIC provides healthcare at HMP Low Newton.

A spokesperson from Spectrum Community Health CIC said: “Spectrum Community Health CIC acknowledges your query in relation to the publication of the Prison and Probation Ombudsman’s report relating to the very sad death of a patient at HMP Low Newton.

“We extend our deepest condolences to Ms Montgomery’s family and all those who knew her.

“Spectrum acknowledge the findings of the report and the recommendations outlined throughout have been fully engaged with and implemented.

“Alongside our partners, we remain focussed on continuously improving the care and support provided to patients on entering and throughout their time in the prison system. Together with our dedicated staff and partners, we strive to ensure the highest standards of healthcare and wellbeing in these challenging environments."
