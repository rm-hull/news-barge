A Darlington woman has completed her own triathlon to raise money for a baby hospice.

Angela Robinson, a member at the gym and pool at Middleton Hall Retirement Village in Middleton St George, near Darlington, helped raise £950 for Zoe’s Place Baby Hospice in Middlesbrough.

Ms Robinson took a two-day triathlon involved a 24km cycle, a one-mile swim - 160 lengths of the centre’s pool - and a 10km treadmill run.

![]()A 10km treadmill run was part of Angela Robinson's triathlon challenge *(Image: Supplied)*

Following her efforts at the retirement village, she said: "I wanted to keep my friend company, so I thought I’d give it a go and take on a challenge too.

"I think I might have got the short straw, mine took me two days."

The fundraiser was inspired by her friend Steph, who progressed from Couch to 5K to the Great North Run.

They took on their challenges over the same weekend to raise money for the hospice.

![]()Angela Robinson swam 160 lengths of the Health & Wellbeing Centre pool for her challenge *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Robinson completed the cycle and swim on Saturday, with wellbeing adviser Ellie bringing sweets to keep her going.

She returned to the gym on Sunday to finish the 10km run as Steph competed in the Great North Run.

She said: "It’s a great local cause. She said the training was tough but rewarding. The training has been hard work, and I was enjoying it until life got in the way and it fell away a bit."

![]()Middleton Hall Retirement Village Gym and Poo *(Image: Supplied)*

She joined the health and wellbeing centre more than two years ago after taking a job that involved long hours at a laptop.

She now visits about three times a week and says the training has helped her longstanding knee problem.

Ms Robinson said: "I have a bad knee, but the training has actually helped with it."

She usually goes to the gym with her daughter, who recently had a baby, and hopes they can resume their routine when childcare allows.

She previously managed Middleton Hall’s reception team.

Ms Robinson said: "It’s lovely seeing some of the residents I knew and also the Middleton Hall co-owners I worked with."

She praised the health and wellbeing team for supporting her throughout the challenge.

She said: "I survived but weirdly enjoyed it and think I have found my sport.

"I felt a great sense of achievement, a bit of ‘I have done that’, even though I had blisters on my blisters."

She and Steph have raised £950 for Zoe’s Place, with donations still open.

Middleton Hall Retirement Village offers independent living, supported apartments and care services.

The 45-acre site includes the Orangery Bistro, a library, an art studio and a Health & Wellbeing Centre open to residents and external members.
