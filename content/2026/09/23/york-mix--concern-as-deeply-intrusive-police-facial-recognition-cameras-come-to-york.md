Facial recognition cameras will be deployed by North Yorkshire Police for the first time today (Wednesday).

The technology, which scans people’s faces and compares them to a police database, will help to identify wanted criminals, the force says.

But others feel it is “deeply intrusive” and treats “everyone as a potential suspect”.

After announcing it was to start using Live Facial Recognition (LFR) technology earlier this year, North Yorkshire Police will deploy it for the first time today.

It will operate in York city centre.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/police-facial-recognition-cameras-pa-3.webp)

A North Yorkshire Police spokesperson said: “LFR is used in carefully selected, intelligence led locations to

* help identify individuals who are wanted by the police or the courts;
* are suspected of committing an offence;
* are unlawfully at large or wanted on recall to prison;
* may breach bail conditions, a court order or another restriction by being in the deployment area;
* are missing and assessed as being at increased risk of harm; or present a risk of harm to themselves or others.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/police-facial-recognition-cameras-pa-2.webp)

“When the system identifies a potential match, officers are alerted and will immediately review the information, carry out further checks and determine whether action is required.

“Images that do not match the watchlist are deleted immediately and permanently. The watchlist itself is deleted at the conclusion of each deployment.”

## ‘Like showing ID at a checkpoint’

Melissa Dring, interim head of policy and campaigns at human rights charity, was concerned about the move.

She told ***YorkMix***: “Live facial recognition on our high streets is like being asked to show your ID at a checkpoint, something most of us wouldn’t expect, or accept, just to go to our local shops.

“It is a deeply intrusive form of surveillance that poses serious risks to privacy, treating everyone as a potential suspect simply for going about their daily lives.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/police-facial-recognition-cameras-pa-3.webp)

“Police forces continue to roll out this technology despite the absence of a clear legal framework governing its use.

“Liberty believes the public deserves far greater transparency, accountability and democratic oversight before facial recognition technology is deployed on our streets.”

The police added: “It is used as a precision crime-fighting tactic to locate people who are of interest to the police.

“It helps us reduce violence and the risk of harm, prevent and detect crime, apprehend and prosecute offenders, protect the public, secure the administration of justice and maintain public confidence. “
