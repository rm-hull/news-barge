Plans have been lodged for 65 new homes on a “characterful” estate in Barnard Castle.

Bellway has submitted proposals to build a string of new homes on a field north of Darlington Road and on the eastern edge of the town.

The developer said it wants to create a development "with a clear identity” while boasting a “visually attractive and memorable street scene”.

Located beside Stonebridge Homes' Ashtree Park development, Bellway wants to build 65 homes, including 13 affordable homes and seven bungalows.

It would boast a mix of detached, semi-detached, and terraced homes and will include seven two-bedroom properties, 16 three-bedroom properties, 26 four-bedroom properties, and three five-bedroom properties.

Affordable homes would be made up of seven two-bedroom homes and six three-bedroom homes.

Bellway said the appearance of these homes has been influenced by the area, with materials chosen to complement those found on the adjacent development.

![]()The proposed location for 65 new homes in Barnard Castle *(Image: GOOGLE)*

Also featured in the plans are short private drives in a bid to “prioritise cycling, walking, and wheeling” and to ensure that street views remain “uncluttered”.

There would also be “well-located and unallocated” on-street visitor parking bays and secure cycle storage space for each home.

The developer said it wants to create a “sustainable and legible living environment”, with existing hedges along the boundaries of the field "preserved and enhanced”.

It plans to have a large drainage basin at the site’s low point and a secondary central basin for sustainable drainage.

There is also the potential to deliver a buffer along the eastern edge with sporadic tree planting as well as woodland planting and trees to the south east.

The estate, which the developer said benefits from “convenient access to a range of local facilities”, would also boast a new walking route to the east through greenspace.

Homes in the centre of the development would be arranged to overlook a green space, which includes the potential to plant new trees and other plants.

Bellway has described it as “high-quality, sustainable residential development designed to meet local housing needs while creating a safe, inclusive, and characterful community”.

The planning document states: “A well-defined movement network prioritises walking and cycling, offering safe, pleasant routes and seamless connectivity to surrounding areas.

“The layout incorporates multi-aspect dwellings and active streetscapes to maximise safety and security across both public and private realms.”

The plans, which were validated on September 11, are yet to be decided upon by Durham County Council.
