A man has admitted a street spanner attack, but the sentencing court may never learn how badly the victim was hurt.

David Riddell, 29, appeared at Durham Crown Court ysterday, (Tuesday, September 22), for a plea hearing on charges arising from the incident in Stanley on Saturday, March 14, this year.

He admitted wounding another man with intent and carrying a spanner, an offensive weapon, in public on Church Street.

Judge Nathan Adams said he had already watched CCTV footage of the incident submitted by prosecutors.

![]()Defendant David Riddell will return to Durham Crown Court to be sentenced for Stanley street attack on October 22

Martin Towers, prosecuting, said the victim had refused to permit access to his medical records, leaving the video as the key evidence in the case.

Tony Davis, representing Riddell, told the court: “As you might imagine, there is a background to this, but there is no statement from the complainant, who has not supported the prosecution."

Mr Davis asked for the Probation Service to prepare a background report on Riddell before he is sentenced.

“He accepts the custody threshold may be crossed in this case.

“He does have some convictions, but these were some time ago, now.”

Judge Adams noted that Riddell’s last conviction was three years ago and he agreed to adjourn the case for a Probation Service pre-sentence report.

Addressing the defendant, Judge Adams told him: "You have pleaded guilty and the sentencing will take place back at this court, in October.

“The fact I’m adjourning for a report should not be taken as any indication as to the likely sentence.

“It’s a serious offence and it will be entirely up to the sentencing judge on that occasion.”

Riddell, 29, of Kay Street, Stanley, was bailed to return for sentencing on Thursday, October 22.

In a separate 2016 case, a man who admitted attacking someone with a wrench at a party near Stanley was jailed for 18 months.
