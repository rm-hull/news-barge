People in York are being challenged to walk 70 miles in October and raise money for Hearing Dogs for Deaf People.

The charity, which runs a training centre in Bielby, is inviting the public to join The Great British Dog Walk in October.

The 70-mile challenge reflects the £70,000 average lifetime cost of a hearing dog, while every mile and pound raised supports the charity.

![]()York residents are challenged to walk 70 miles for Hearing Dogs for Deaf People, supporting its Bielby training centre through The Great British Dog Walk *(Image: Supplied)*

Gina Evans, national community events manager at Hearing Dogs for Deaf People, said: "Every single person in York who takes on a walking challenge for The Great British Dog Walk will be directly helping deaf people.

"They’ll also get the added benefit of the many physical and mental health benefits of walking and, if you choose to take on the challenge with friends, family, colleagues or much-loved dogs, the many emotional benefits of spending time with others and being in the great outdoors."

Participants can complete the challenge however they choose, from familiar routes and dog-friendly paths to nearby landmarks.

Suggested York walks include the city walls, paths along the Ouse, Rowntree Park, Hob Moor and the countryside around Heslington East.

Registration is free, and participants can order a dog rosette to mark their achievement.

The more participants raise, the more chances they have to win one of two £100 vouchers in the charity’s prize draw.
