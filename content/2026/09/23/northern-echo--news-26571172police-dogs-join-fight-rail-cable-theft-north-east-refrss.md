Specialist police dogs are joining the fight against cable theft on North East railway lines.

The dogs are part of a wider operation protecting the East Coast Main Line alongside British Transport Police and Network Rail teams.

The wider strategy includes targeted patrols, CCTV, drones and forensic marking to deter and investigate cable theft.

John Grainger, joint head of performance for Network Rail and LNER, said: "Cable theft is not a victimless crime.

![]()Police dog Chloe *(Image: Network Rail)*

"It can cause significant disruption for passengers and freight operators, damage infrastructure and create serious safety risks on and around the railway.

"We’re committed to working closely with British Transport Police and our industry partners to prevent, detect and prosecute offenders.

"Specialist police dogs are another valuable tool in that effort, helping officers search large areas quickly, locate evidence and support investigations.

"Alongside other measures we’re taking, these animals help strengthen our ability to protect the railway and keep passengers moving safely and reliably."

The scheme follows a recent £110,000 investment by the East Coast Partnership, a Network Rail and LNER venture funding extra patrols at key locations.

![]()'Evidence' of cable theft found by Chloe during training *(Image: Network Rail)*

Cable theft is a serious and continuing threat, causing major delays, cancellations and safety risks across the rail network.

Cable thieves can cripple signalling and power systems, disrupting train services across wide areas.

The canine teams search large trackside areas, helping officers find suspects, stolen railway materials and vital evidence.

Some dogs can also detect railway equipment and forensic marking substances, helping investigations and prosecutions.

The dogs began training last week at a Network Rail depot in Doncaster and will support patrols and joint exercises to improve partners’ response to incidents.

The scheme is part of a growing security operation protecting the East Coast Main Line and limiting disruption for passengers and freight operators.
