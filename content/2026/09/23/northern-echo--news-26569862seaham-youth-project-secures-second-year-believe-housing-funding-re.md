A Seaham project creating opportunities for young people will continue for a second year after securing fresh housing association funding.

Youth Connect Seaham has received an £18,611 grant from believe housing to extend its work in Deneside and Eastlea for 12 months.

Groundwork North East and Cumbria runs the scheme, which launched last summer with £29,994 in community funding to tackle antisocial behaviour, improve neighbourhoods and build young people's confidence.

![]()Colleagues from believe housing joined local youngstersfrom the Youth Connect Seaham project to plant trees on an underused parcel of land at Ryton Crescent, Seaham *(Image: believe housing)*

Anne-Marie Parkin, community investment coordinator at believe housing, said: "Over the past year, Youth Connect Seaham has brought residents together, created new opportunities for children and young people and supported positive changes in the local area.

"From community activities to environmental projects, it is helping people feel more connected to where they live.

"We're pleased to be funding the project for a second year so this work can continue.

"We want residents to tell us what's made the biggest difference and what they'd like to see happen next, so future activities are shaped by the people who live there."

![]()Colleagues from believe housing joined local youngstersfrom the Youth Connect Seaham project to plant trees on an underused parcel of land at Ryton Crescent, Seaham *(Image: believe housing)*

The programme offers free sports sessions, creative workshops, outreach support and trips for children and young people.

Participants have taken part in activities promoting numeracy and mental wellbeing while learning about water, road, rail and fire safety.

The scheme has encouraged young people to explore their ambitions and discuss local issues.

Participants organised litter picks and began transforming a fly-tipping-hit area at Ryton Crescent by planting trees with help from believe housing staff.

Residents are being urged to help shape the project’s next year.

Julie Dower, neighbourhood manager at believe housing, said: "We're already seeing the impact this project is having in Deneside and Eastlea.

"By bringing together residents, local organisations and colleagues from across believe housing, the project is giving people the chance to get involved and make improvements in their communities.

"Residents are taking pride in where they live, and we've seen a reduction in issues such as fly-tipping."

Residents of Deneside and Eastlea can share their views at a series of drop-in events.
