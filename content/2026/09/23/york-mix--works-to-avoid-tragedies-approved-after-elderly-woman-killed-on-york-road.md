Works planned on a York road where an elderly woman was killed will help avoid future tragedies, the council’s transport lead has said.

City of York Council’s Labour transport executive member Cllr Kate Ravilious said £220,000-worth of improvements planned for Osbaldwick’s Outgang Lane would help protect pedestrians.

But she added no parking restrictions would be brought in until measures for the wider area had been considered following fears congestion could be driven into the village.

It comes as Cllr Ravilious approved the works at a council transport decision session on Tuesday (22 September).

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/outgang-lane-osbaldwick-york-aerial-gsv-1000x667.webp)

The works are among those planned as part of £5.25 million in improvements planned for York’s three permanent traveller sites up to 2030.

Proposed works include improvements to footpaths and installing street lights.

A council report on the proposals for Outgang Lane stated the road currently suffered from obstructions to and a lack of footpaths, poor lighting, potholes, inconsiderate parking and flooding.

It added the road had become busier since the Outgang Lane travellers’ site had grown from temporary accommodation to a permanent residential community.

Officials stated safety concerns had also been on the rise since an accident in the road which killed 73-year-old Betty Elizabeth Smith in January 2020.

An inquest in 2023 heard the resident of the travellers’ site died after a lorry carrying a skip reversed into her at the entrance to a waste management business.

#### Outgang Lane, York

Tuesday’s meeting heard safety concerns had been raised about both elderly people and children from residents of the traveller site, businesses based in Outgang Lane and people living in Osbaldwick.

But Keyline, which is one of those businesses, objected ahead of the meeting claiming the changes were unnecessary and incredibly dangerous.

The firm added they were extremely concerned the works would make problems including antisocial behaviour they faced from travellers worse.

Cllr Mark Warters, Restore Britain’s ward councillor for Osbaldwick and Derwenthorpe, said the proposals would lead to the further urbanisation of village roads.

The ward councillor said: “The industrial estate and Outgang Lane operated perfectly well for decades before, there is no need to create further chaos.”

Speaking on Tuesday, Cllr Ravilious said the works were really important and reflected the council’s aims of prioritising pedestrians and keeping road users safe.

The transport executive member said: “There was previously a pedestrian fatality in the road, these proposals will significantly reduce the risk for pedestrians and they aim to avoid tragedies like this happening in the future.

“I note the concerns about parking displacement, we’re going to look at the whole area and not do one set of parking restrictions in isolation.”
