On Monday, Rep. Ashley Hinson, President Donald Trump’s endorsed nominee in Iowa’s Senate race, made a public break from the president, calling for an end to the Iran War.

That was surprising considering that just last week, before Speaker Mike Johnson dismissed the House of Representatives a day early, Hinson voted against a War Powers Act resolution to rein in Trump’s war. This came despite two of her Iowa Republican colleagues running for re-election — Reps. Zach Nunn and Mariannette Miller-Meeks — voting for the resolution.

But the pain at the gas tank is becoming too much across the board.

Hence, she listed a series of policies to provide relief for Iowans, like pausing the gas tax and suspending exports of diesel fuel.

“While doing this, we should also ensure our highway trust fund is replenished, so that Iowans don’t suffer from crumbling roads and bridges while relief comes our way now,” Hinson said. “These actions to lower costs are needed ASAP, and the war needs to be brought to a successful and immediate end.”

![Rep. Ashley Hinson (R-Iowa) is now struggling to separate herself from President Donald Trump and his war in Iran.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/22/15/06/GettyImages-2292106594.jpeg)

In response, Josh Turek, the Paralympian-turned Democratic nominee for Senate, criticized Hinson for flip-flopping.

“You voted seven times to continue it, including just last week,” he posted on X. “It's time to actually end the forever wars and put Iowa and Iowans first.”

But on Tuesday, Republicans’ reasons for breaking with Trump on Iran became clear. Marist University released a poll showing Turek besting Hinson by eight points. Lest anyone think that poll is an outlier, a YouGov poll showed Turek with a one-point lead in Iowa.

All of this seems to show that Iowa, which has undergone a political transformation in the past 15 years to become a staunchly Republican state, is prime for a Democratic takeover.

For many Democrats, the last time they felt good about Iowa came in 2008 when Barack Obama turned his stunning win in the state’s presidential caucuses into winning the Hawkeye State by nine points in the general election. That same year, Democratic Sen. Tom Harkin would win his last term. Obama would win again in 2012 by just under 6 percentage points.

But that would be the beginning of a long decline. In 2010, Republicans won the governorship and took over the state House of Representatives. In 2014, Harkin would retire and Joni Ernst would take over his seat. In 2016, Iowa would by more than 15 points to vote for Trump and Republicans would take control of the state senate.

Democrats would win two House seats in the 2018 midterm elections, only for Iowa to have an all-Republican delegation by 2022. Joe Biden would lose Iowa and Joni Ernst would win her re-election in 2020.

But Trump has now taken a series of steps in Iowa that have put Republicans at risk in the state. For one, the war in Iran has caused the price of diesel fuel, which farmers use for tractors and other equipment, to skyrocket. According to AAA, the price of a gallon of diesel in the state is now $6.31 compared to $3.59 a year ago.

There’s also the tariffs, which have put a massive strain on farmers. China has since pledged to purchase American-made soybeans, but it maintains a 10 percent tariff on American goods, including agricultural products.

Another factor working in Democrats’ favor is the fact that Iowa’s unpopular Republican Gov. Kim Reynolds will not seek re-election. Most polls show the state’s Democratic Auditor Rob Sand beating Republican doctor Zach Lahn in the governor’s race.

Of course, Democrats voters have every reason to not take these numbers at face value. Most famously, in 2024, Ann Selzer, the most respected pollster in Iowa, showed Kamala Harris with a three-point lead in the state against Trump in the final days before the election, only for him to mollywhop Harris by double digits.

In addition, Hinson, while plenty conservative, has sought to portray herself as bipartisan and not a right-wing fire-breather on the issues. Republicans are also trying to recylce the issue of transgender athletes playing in women’s sports, even though Turek told NBC News and Sand told *Politico* that they oppose it.

Lindsay James, who is running in Hinson’s old seat, for her part is running an ad saying “Parents know what's best for our kids. Washington needs to stay out of it.”

But it’s not clear that hitting Democrats on transgender athletes will have the same salience in 2026 that it did in 2024, when Republicans messaged that Democrats cared more about cultural issues than they did the cost of living. Now, it’s the GOP that looks more focused on transgender people than the price of diesel and food.

Iowa’s conservative lean might still save Hinson and some Republicans in the state. But if the top-ticket Republicans lose, they will have few people other than Trump to blame for losing a state that he had secured in their column for the past decade.
