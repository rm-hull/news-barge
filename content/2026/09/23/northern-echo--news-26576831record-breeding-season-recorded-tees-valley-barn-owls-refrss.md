Conservationists are celebrating a record year for barn owls after volunteers counted more chicks in Teesside nest boxes than ever before.

Volunteers working with Tees Valley Wildlife Trust have ringed 541 barn owl chicks so far this year, more than double last year's total of 270 - and the highest figure ever recorded by the project.

![]()Barn owl. *(Image: Tees Valley Wildlife)*

The project is led by volunteer Colin Gibson, a licensed bird ringer with the Tees Valley Ringing Group, who has spent the past 15 years working to restore barn owl populations across the local landscape.

He said: "The breeding success for this season has completely exceeded all expectations.

"We knew we were in for an interesting year when we found birds had reached breeding condition and started laying two to three weeks early, probably an effect of the mild winter."

![]()Barn Owl chicks. *(Image: Tees Valley Wildlife)*

With volunteers and local landowners, the project has built more than 500 specially designed nest boxes across the Tees Valley and North Yorkshire.

Almost 200 of those boxes have been used for breeding this year - providing vital nesting sites for barn owls.

![]()Nesting box for barn owls across Tees Valley. *(Image: Tees Valley Wildlife)*

The number of owls had previously declined over the last century because of the loss of nesting sites in old trees and traditional farm buildings.

Colin said the mild winter, dry summer and plentiful food had driven this year's success.

He said: "It just goes to show what a difference a £100 barn owl box can make to conservation."

The boxes have also sheltered tawny owls, kestrels, stock doves, jackdaws, ducks, grey squirrels and the occasional wasp colony.

The project relies on sponsors paying £10 a year for ringing equipment, travel and new and replacement boxes, in return for updates throughout the breeding season and information about the general location of their box.

After the record-breaking year, the trust hopes to attract more sponsors and expand the scheme even further.

Colin said: "By continuing and developing our nest box project we will not only build a more optimistic future for the barn owl but hopefully reinstate the barn owl across the wider Tees Valley and northern North Yorkshire."
