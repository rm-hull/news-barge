A smokefree generation is just 100 days away, with people born in the North East set to be protected from legally buying tobacco for life.

The countdown to a smokefree generation will begin on Wednesday, January 23. From January 1, 2027, selling tobacco to anyone born on or after January 1, 2009, will be illegal under the UK Government’s Smokefree Generation policy.

Ailsa Rutter OBE, director of Fresh and Balance, said: "No other legal product than tobacco kills 2 in 3 of its lifelong customers.

"This is a vital step to help the next generation avoid the death and disease and to live healthier, wealthier lives in the future."

The legal age for buying tobacco will rise by one year every year until sales are phased out entirely.

The policy has strong support in the North East, where 64 per cent of people back it.

Cancer survivor Sue Mountain, 60, from South Tyneside, said: "It is far better and easier to prevent addiction in the first place.

"I still kick myself for ever starting – I think of the cost, the cancer and the years spent trying to quit.

"When you’re young, you never think you’re going to end up addicted, or how smoking is going to damage your life."

Smoking has killed more than 8 million people in the UK since 1970.

The measure is expected to protect up to 4 million young people across Great Britain from being sold tobacco over the next five years.

In the North East, 10.2 per cent of adults smoke, down from 29 per cent in 2005.

Despite the progress, smoking still kills 64,000 people in England each year and costs the North East more than £2 billion.

Local authorities and the NHS broadly backed the Tobacco and Vapes Act as it passed through Parliament.

Amanda Healy, director of public health at Durham County Council and chair of the Association of Directors of Public Health North East Network, said: "Smoking now costs our region over £2bn a year – it’s a cost felt most of all by families but it also places huge strain on the regional economy, local authority social care budgets and the NHS through sickness.

"This measure will help regions like ours which are most hit by the death and disease of smoking."

Dr Ruth Sharrock, respiratory consultant and clinical lead for tobacco dependency at NHS North East and North Cumbria Integrated Care Board, said: "Every day my team and I tell people they have lung cancer, COPD or emphysema caused by smoking, then watch their world collapse.

"In my own family, my grandparents’ and parents’ generations have suffered and died from smoking-related cancers and heart disease, while some are living with COPD.

"The Smokefree legislation will not only change the burden of tobacco-related disease facing the NHS, but also the outlook for my children’s entire generation – helping them escape tobacco’s legacy of harm for good."

Judith Hedgley, head of the public protection service at Middlesbrough Council and chair of the North East Public Protection Partnership, said: "Retailers also have an important role to play in helping protect the next generation.
