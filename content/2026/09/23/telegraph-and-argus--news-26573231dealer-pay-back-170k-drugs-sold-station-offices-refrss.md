A drug dealer who sold heroin, cocaine, and crack cocaine to undercover police officers from a Keighley private hire company has been ordered to pay back £170,000.

In June 2025, Nahmaan Sakander was one of four men jailed for more than 30 years at Bradford Crown Court for their roles in a conspiracy to supply Class A controlled drugs in Keighley.

Some of the deals took place in a private hire firm based at Keighley’s rail station.

At the time Sakander, Haroon Ishaq, Naveed Hussain, and Arsalan Syed Shah, all from Keighley, were the latest drug dealers to be caught as part of a large-scale West Yorkshire Police operation.

Sakander’s conviction came four months after his brother was stripped of his operator’s licence. It followed undercover police being sold drugs on the premises.

![]()Left to right: Sakander; Hussain; Ishaq and Shah *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

Dubbed “Saintpond”, the police operation targeted county lines drug-running by organised gangs between West and North Yorkshire and has been a huge success with multiple drug pushers snared for peddling heroin, crack cocaine, and cocaine on the streets of Keighley.

The four men all admitted three counts of conspiracy to supply Class A drugs.

They were sentenced as follows:

* Sakander, now 45, of Southlands Drive, Keighley, was jailed for 11 years and seven months.
* Ishaq, now 36, of Skipton Road, Keighley, received eight years and five months.
* Hussain, now 36, of Chatsworth Street, Keighley, got four years and six months.
* Shah, now 26, of Kendal Mellor Court, Keighley, was handed six years and three months.

Sakander also admitted an offence of possess, acquire and use of criminal property. On arrest £80,890 was recovered from his home address.

The sum of £7,740 was also recovered from Ishaq’s home address.

At Bradford Crown Court on Tuesday, a confiscation order was made under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

The court heard that Sakander benefited by £170,000 and that the amount available to the court was £289,912.36.

That figure was calculated as a result of cash seized by police as well as his interest in two properties: Sakander’s home address of 1 Southlands Drive, and 96 Grange Crescent.

His Honour Judge Ahmed Nadim made the confiscation order and told Sakander: “Should you fail to meet that order within the next three months you will serve 21 months’ imprisonment in default.”

The court previously heard that between June and November, 2023, Sakander, Ishaq, Hussain, and Shah sold drugs to undercover police officers through the “Barry” drug-dealing line, which supplied quantities of heroin, crack cocaine, and cocaine.

It was described as “an organised and structured drug-dealing enterprise”.

The quartet went on trial but after hearing the first witness speak, all four men changed their pleas to guilty.

Details of the company’s role in the drug operation were revealed during a Magistrates’ Court hearing earlier in 2025.

![]()Central Private Hire pictured hours after the court appeal hearing for Akmal Sakander *(Image: Newsquest)*

Akmal Sakander, operator of Central Private Hire, was stripped of his operator’s licence after the premises at the station were raided by police in April 2024.

Council officers said he was not a “fit and proper person” to run a private hire business considering what had happened under his watch.

In February 2025 Akmal Sakander appealed against the decision in a bid to regain his licence.

At the appeal hearing, magistrates were told that undercover police officers had allegedly been dealt cocaine, heroin, and crack cocaine by an individual in the company’s office.

That individual was Nahmaan Sakander, the brother of Akmal and an employee of the private hire firm.

Akmal Sakander had argued that he should not be stripped of his licence, as he had no idea his brother was dealing drugs on the premises.

But magistrates decided that the fact he was oblivious to a serious crime taking place in his business only proved he was not a fit and proper person to hold a licence.

The business closed after the appeal.

Commenting after sentences were handed down Detective Chief Inspector Tom Levitt of Bradford’s Precision Team said: “These sentences are part of Operation Saintpond where more than 60 arrests were made into the dismantling of drugs linked to county lines in Keighley and across the border in Skipton.

“So far 31 people have been convicted in relation to this operation and given more than 96 years in prison.

“Sakander gave the impression he was a legitimate individual running the family business. Without this operation he would have never been caught; he thought he was untouchable.

“I hope this operation shows along with the sentences that we take drug offences seriously in Keighley. Illegal drugs will not be tolerated in our communities.

“I would encourage anyone with information about drugs supply to contact West Yorkshire Police or Crimestoppers to support this continued disruption of those involved in the supply of drugs.

“We hope that this sentence will serve as a warning to others that if you are involved in the supply of drugs, we will act on the information we receive to identify you, arrest you, and bring you before the courts.”
