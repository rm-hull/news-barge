A man has denied two counts of murder after a devastating house fire killed a seven-year-old girl and her aunt.

Jaden Wright also denied two charges of grievous bodily harm with intent and two of attempted grievous bodily harm with intent under Section 18 of the Offences Against the Person Act.

The charges follow a house fire in Grangetown, near Middlesbrough, on August 26 that killed Natalie McDonald, 34, and Valentina Foster and injured four others.

![]()Valentina Foster, seven, and Natalie McDonald, 34, have been named locally as the two victims who died following a house fire in Grangetown.

The 23-year-old, who has no fixed address, also denied charges of grievous bodily harm and intentional strangulation involving another woman on August 25.

![]()Police at the scene of the fire on Birchington Avenue in Grangetown

Judge Francis Laird KC, the Recorder of Middlesbrough, adjourned the case until December 18 for a further case-management hearing.

Wright will stand trial on April 19 next year.

Cleveland Police said the fire was not being treated as linked to the fatal A66 crash that killed seven people, including police officers PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough.

Mrs McDonald’s family paid tribute to her, saying: "She had the biggest, most beautiful wedding; she sparkled like the shining beauty she was. She was loved by everyone, she was bright, bubbly and full of life, the heart and soul of our street and community, loved by all."

![]()Emergency services at the scene of a major residential fire on Birchington Avenue, in Grangetown, Middlesbrough

Valentina’s family said: “She was deeply loved and will be missed beyond words by her family, friends, and everyone whose life she touched.

“Our lives have changed forever, and we are struggling to come to terms with this devastating loss. Our daughter will always be in our hearts, and her memory will live with us forever.
