A North East hospital has won a national award in recognition of the life-changing support it provides to women recovering from brain injuries.

Cygnet Newham House, a 20-bed neuropsychiatric facility in Middlesbrough, received the Special Recognition Award at the Cygnet Psychiatrist Awards, hosted by Cygnet Health Care.

The award celebrates outstanding, person-centred care and a culture dedicated to improving patient outcomes.

Joanna Yarker, hospital manager at Cygnet Newham House, said: "Cygnet Newham House is honoured to receive the Cygnet Group Clinical Director Special Recognition Award, recognising the neurorehabilitation service we provide for females.

"This recognition reflects the passion, resilience and commitment of an exceptional multidisciplinary team whose dedication to clinical excellence, innovation and person-centred care continues to improve outcomes and transform lives.

"Following our exceptional CQC rating for 'Well-Led' and the 'Outstanding' recognition from Headway, we are proud and honoured to receive this award from Cygnet.

"This award is a testament to the hard work, professionalism and unwavering commitment of the entire team, whose passion for delivering high-quality care continues to transform lives every day."

The hospital supports women with acquired brain injuries (ABI), offering neurorehabilitation in a safe and supportive environment.

The award was presented by Dr Salim Matta, medical director at Cygnet Health Care, who praised the team’s compassionate and individualised approach.

Dr Matta said: "What sets this hospital apart is not simply the quality of its clinical pathway, but the depth of compassion and individuality behind every care plan.

"Their daily work reflects a hospital that never loses sight of the person behind the diagnosis.

"This year alone, Newham House has been rated Outstanding in 'Well-Led' by the CQC and Good overall, and achieved an overall Outstanding rating from national brain injury charity Headway – with top marks across Culture, Workforce Development, Service Development, as well as Governance, Leadership, Management and Quality.

"They have also secured Carers Trust Triangle of Care accreditation, cementing their commitment to families as genuine partners in care, not passers-by.

"The hospital’s excellence has been recognised well beyond its own walls.

"None of this happens by accident.

"It is built on a culture where staff feel genuinely valued, visible in small but telling details, like a 'moment of thanks' board where colleagues recognise one another’s kindness and teamwork day to day.

"The team has shown that rigorous clinical governance and heartfelt, human care are not competing priorities, but two sides of the same commitment."

Cygnet Newham House has also been named in the first-ever QN-Neuro report for the quality of its facilities.
