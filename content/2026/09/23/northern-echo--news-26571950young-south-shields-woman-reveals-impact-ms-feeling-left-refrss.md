A young North East woman is adjusting to a new way of life after a "scary" diagnosis.

Jess Newby, 25, from South Shields, was diagnosed in April with relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis (MS) following a sudden loss of feeling in her legs earlier this year.

She has since been left with neuropathic pain, balance problems, and episodes of bladder incontinence.

Her struggles come after a large lesion was found on her spinal cord.

Ms Newby said: "There's not a lot of case studies for young people with MS because not many people are willing to talk about it, especially young people.

"I'm not ashamed of it, it's just a very scary illness.

"My dad had MS, he passed away when I was 13. We always thought there is no way I would get it, but if you inherit enough genes, there's a chance."

![]()Jess with her Dad, Mark Newby, in hospital in 2011 *(Image: Provided)*

She has since become an ambulatory wheelchair user and now relies on the support of friends and family to get around.

Ms Newby is currently fundraising for driving lessons and an orthopaedic mattress to help manage her pain and regain some independence.

She has raised just over £100 towards her £768 target so far.

Her health struggles began while she was living in Manchester and studying music at university.

Miss Newby says that while at university, she spent a week in hospital due to migraines that caused temporary blindness.

Soon after, she began experiencing seizures and had to leave university.

Then in February, she lost all feeling in her feet while lying in bed.

Ms Newby said: "I was lying in bed and my feet started tingling, so I started walking around thinking it was my circulation.

"I was trying to get rid of it, but by day four, I couldn't walk unaided - and the numbness was in my waist.

"I had lost control of my bowel and bladder, and every step I took I couldn't feel it.

"It was like walking on clouds."

She was admitted to hospital, where doctors discovered three lesions on her brain.

Her MS diagnosis came just one week after she was also diagnosed with autism.

Now, Ms Newby is hoping to move into more suitable accommodation, ideally a council bungalow.

She said: "I'm using a wheelchair now because I'm so tired and I'm always in pain.

"It's a struggle; my brain gets frazzled."

She also spoke about the social impact of her diagnosis.

Ms Newby said: "It's hard; my friends want to go out, but I don't get invited anymore because people don't know how to handle it or deal with it.

"I was making myself tired and putting myself in pain to stop others feeling uncomfortable, but I can't do that anymore."

Despite her challenges, she is staying positive and plans to dress up as Lightning McQueen from Pixar's Cars films for Halloween, decorating her wheelchair as the cartoon racing car.

She said: "My dad had a great sense of humour, it's how he dealt with MS himself."

"A friend of my dad now looks after me and takes me to all of my appointments.

"We have a good laugh too, you have to."
