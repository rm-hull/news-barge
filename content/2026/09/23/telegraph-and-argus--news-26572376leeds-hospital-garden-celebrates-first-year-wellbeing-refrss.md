A WELLBEING garden is marking its first year as a nurturing space for staff and patients alike.

The White Rose Wellbeing Garden, located behind the Gledhow Wing at St James’s University Hospital, has become a peaceful retreat for patients, visitors and staff since opening last summer.

It offers a place to relax, reflect and reconnect with nature.

Antony Kildare, chair of Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, said: "The Wellbeing Garden at St James's has made a genuinely positive difference to this part of our hospital site.

"It has been wonderful to see so many people making use of the garden, whether that's taking time out to share a break with colleagues, holding meetings outdoors, or just enjoying the bright space during what has been an exceptionally good summer."

A patient in the Bexley Wing described its impact, saying: "It's lovely to visit the garden and feel the fresh air after being on the ward all week.

"So good to be somewhere that doesn't feel like a hospital.

"Wonderful to watch the bees and hoverflies on the herbs too."

The garden, created with support from the Royal Horticultural Society, includes an all-weather seating area, dozens of flowerbeds and pollinator-friendly planting.

It also includes plants known for their medicinal properties in a nod to its healthcare setting.

To mark the first anniversary, staff took part in a mosaic-making session, creating a permanent artwork which will be displayed on the beams of the garden’s all-weather sheltered seating space.

Celebrations also included posy-making, a chai pop-up stall and a vegan cake.
