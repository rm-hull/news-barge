A court has heard how the driver of a stolen car that collided head-on with a Range Rover following a police chase on narrow country roads had never held a licence in his life.

Qasim Hussain is now beginning a 21-month prison sentence and has been banned from the road for 32 months.

The three-minute police pursuit on May 6 this year was caught on video.

The court heard that after he was arrested, 31-year-old Hussain tested positive for cocaine.

Prosecutor Bashir Ahmed told Bradford Crown Court how the stolen Seat Leon was taken in a robbery on the night of May 5.

The following afternoon the Seat was spotted on Ferrand Avenue, Bierley, by police officers in a marked car.

Hussain was at the wheel of the Seat.

He accelerated away when he saw the police car behind him, ignoring its blue lights and sirens.

He turned onto Bierley Lane and speeded up to twice the 30mph limit before entering Greenfield Lane, a narrow country road where he narrowly avoided collisions with other vehicles.

![]()A motorist takes evasive action as Hussain speeds along a road between Bierley and Oakenshaw *(Image: CPS)*

Mr Ahmed said: “Eventually the defendant turned into a dead end into an industrial estate.

“That didn’t deter him. He turned the vehicle around, passed [the police] vehicle. The police officer did the same.

“The defendant drove into the offside of Mill Carr Hill Road and collided head-on with a Range Rover travelling on that road.”

Hussain, of Sherwell Rise, Allerton, was arrested from the Seat. A female passenger was also in the Seat.

The court heard that Hussain had never held a driving licence.

Both the Range Rover and the stolen Seat were written off as a result of the crash.

The driver of the Range Rover suffered whiplash and concussion as well as bruising from the activation of the car’s airbags.

![]()Hussain *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

The court heard that the collision also aggravated an earlier fracture to her back from 2025, which was still healing at the time the Seat hit her car.

The incident cost her £5,720 in lost earnings as well as a vehicle excess of £1,750.

The court heard that Hussain had 23 previous convictions for 48 offences including for theft and driving offences. In 2019 he was jailed for dangerous driving.

Hussain pleaded guilty on the day of trial to taking a vehicle without consent, dangerous driving, and having neither licence nor insurance.

Mitigating, Tom Doyle said Hussain’s lifestyle at the time had been “chaotic” as he suffered from drug-induced psychosis and depression but had “disengaged” from taking medication.

![]()A still image from a video of the police chase *(Image: CPS)*

He said Hussain had also made attempts to take his own life in the past.

He said Hussain, who appeared via video link from HMP Leeds, had been in custody for several months after being sentenced for affray, assault, and public order offences committed on Christmas Day.

He suggested Hussain had already been “punished to a not insignificant extent” and that immediate custody for the driving matters was not a fait accompli as he had already served a good chunk and would be out in a few months.

He added: “He is fully recognising what he did was wrong – that it has caused untold harm to the victim, and he wishes through me to apologise for this.”

Taking note of the camera footage of the chase and crash and observing that Hussain was trying to evade the police, Mr Recorder Patrick Palmer handed him a total of 21 months’ imprisonment and banned him from driving for 22 months with a 10-month extension.

He said time on remand would count towards his sentence.
