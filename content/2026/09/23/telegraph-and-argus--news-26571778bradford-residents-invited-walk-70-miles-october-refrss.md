A NATIONAL charity is encouraging people to step up and support a life-changing cause.

Hearing Dogs for Deaf People, which trains hearing dogs for people with hearing loss, is inviting residents in Bradford to take part in the Great British Dog Walk this October by walking 70 miles throughout the month.

The challenge aims to raise funds and awareness while encouraging participants to explore their local area on foot.

Gina Evans, national community events manager at Hearing Dogs for Deaf People, said: "Every single person in Bradford who takes on a walking challenge for The Great British Dog Walk will be directly helping deaf people.

"They’ll also get the added benefit of the many physical and mental health benefits of walking and, if you choose to take on the challenge with friends, family, colleagues or much-loved dogs, the many emotional benefits of spending time with others and being in the great outdoors."

The 70-mile goal represents the average £70,000 lifetime cost of a hearing dog.

Walkers are encouraged to complete the distance however they choose—whether by revisiting favourite routes, discovering new dog-friendly paths, or exploring nearby landmarks and green spaces.

Every mile walked, and every pound raised through sponsorship, will help to train more life-changing hearing dogs.

![]()Hearing Dogs for Deaf People, which trains hearing dogs for people with hearing loss, is inviting residents in Bradford to take part in the Great British Dog Walk this October by walking 70 miles throughout the month *(Image: Tom Powell)*

The charity has a puppy training hub in West Yorkshire and relies on public support to continue its work.

One in three adults in the UK is deaf, has hearing loss or tinnitus.

Many experience feelings of isolation and disconnection from the world around them.

Taking part in the Great British Dog Walk will help provide trained hearing dogs and essential support services to those who need them.

Alison, who is partnered with hearing dog Damson, shared how the scheme changed her life.

She said: "With Damson by my side, I had the confidence to learn to drive and move out of my parents’ home.

"I also met my partner and we’ve had our first child.

"She makes me feel I can give anything a try.

"I can’t imagine my life without her."

In Bradford, suggested routes include Lister Park and Cartwright Hall, the historic village of Saltaire, and the riverside walks at Roberts Park.

Those seeking longer walks can head to Ilkley Moor or Bingley Five Rise Locks.

Registration is free and participants can order a dog rosette to mark their achievement.
