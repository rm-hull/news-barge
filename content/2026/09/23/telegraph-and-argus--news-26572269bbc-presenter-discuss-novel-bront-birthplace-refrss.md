Reeta Chakrabarti will discuss her debut novel and the Brontë sisters’ influence on her work at the Brontë Birthplace.

The BBC News presenter will be interviewed by the Brontë Birthplace on Saturday, October 17. The event will begin at 6.30pm.

Chakrabarti is a journalist and broadcaster who has reported extensively at home and abroad for the BBC.

### Chakrabarti to discuss debut novel

Her novel, Finding Belle, explores belonging and identity. It examines what it means to work out who you are without the usual support of family around you.

The book has links to Charlotte Brontë’s Jane Eyre, making the Brontë Birthplace a fitting venue for the discussion.

Rosie Preston-Cook, PR and Media Lead at the Brontë Birthplace, said: “We were absolutely thrilled when she agreed to come and be interviewed at the Birthplace. It is a real honour for us.”

She said: “It will be a lovely evening where Reeta will be discussing her debut novel Finding Belle and the influence the Brontë sisters have had on her work.”

Chakrabarti was approached at the Bradford Literature Festival. The visit was described as one of the highlights of the organisation’s year.

Rosie said: “We aim to provide a programme of events that is accessible to everyone from supper nights to yoga and poetry and events like this which have a direct link to the Brontë story.”

![]() *(Image: Martin Greenwood)*

### Jane Eyre Influence

She added: “We are really keen to host a range of events at the Birthplace from author talks to craft session and guided tours and because Finding Belle is influenced by Charlotte Brontë's Jane Eyre it seemed the perfect location for Reeta to discuss the book.”

The Birthplace has previously welcomed visitors including the Queen, and received her subsequent patronage.

Rosie said: “We are so lucky that Reeta has agreed to come.”

She added: “It shows the Brontë Birthplace is not just preserving literary history, but actively contributing to literary culture today. Welcoming figures like Reeta Chakrabarti helps us attract new audiences, build our reputation and demonstrate the continued relevance of the Brontës'”

The spokesperson said: “We hope events like this encourage people to discover the Brontë Birthplace, engage with its history, and see how the Brontës continue to inspire writers today.”
