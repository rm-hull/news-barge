Two power station workers have completed a 300km trek in just 12 days, raising more than £2,500 for the Alzheimer’s Society.

Jack Coombs and Matty Secker from Hartlepool power station walked the Coast-to-Coast route from St Bees in Cumbria to Robin Hood’s Bay in North Yorkshire, covering some of the most scenic and demanding terrain in the UK.

Mr Coombs, maintenance group head at the power station, said: "It was a huge challenge, both physically and mentally, and there were some long days and sore legs along the way.

"But doing it together and knowing we were raising money for such an important cause gave us the motivation to keep putting one foot in front of the other.

"We also met some great people, which added to the experience.

"The scenery was incredible and it is an experience we’ll never forget.

"We are both incredibly proud to have completed the 300km and to have raised more than £2,500 for Alzheimer’s Society.

"A massive thank you to everyone who supported and sponsored us."

Their fundraiser supports Alzheimer’s Society, which is EDF’s corporate charity partner.

Since 2024, EDF sites across the UK have raised more than £600,000 for the charity.

Mr Secker, a technical advice engineer, said: "It was a fully enjoyable experience that I would encourage anyone to do, not only for a physical challenge but for a break from the routine and a chance to clear your head."

"We live in a beautiful country and most people do not choose to see it.

"Parts of the walk were very challenging, we had a few falls, one snapped pole, a few blisters and three packs of Compeed, but the views, company and characters we met along the way made it an unforgettable challenge, and to raise this much money for a charity close to my heart is an added bonus."

There are an estimated 982,000 people living with dementia in the UK, with Alzheimer's disease accounting for up to 80 per cent of these cases.
