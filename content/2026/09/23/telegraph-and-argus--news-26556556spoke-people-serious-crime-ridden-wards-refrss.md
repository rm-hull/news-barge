“Keep your head down.”

This was the message that became abundantly clear as the Telegraph & Argus visited and spoke to locals in three of the Bradford district’s worst areas for crime.

In some communities, the fear was perceptible - despite provisional police data showing a five per cent fall in crime.

Many people did not want to be photographed, they did not want to give their name, and in the majority of cases, they did not want to discuss anything - even off the record.

In places where residents fear being identified, it seems the instinct is not to challenge what is happening.

It is to keep out of it.

## Bradford worst place for serious crime in Yorkshire

The T&A was keen to gauge the feeling from locals after analysis by property data platform Crystal Roof, using figures derived from data.police.uk, revealed that Bradford has the highest rate of serious crime in Yorkshire.

Over the 12 months to July 2026, Bradford recorded 72,853 crimes, a rate of 120 per 1,000 at-risk population, against a regional average of 106.

Crystal Roof defines high-harm crime as offences with the most serious impact on victims, excluding high-volume but lower-impact categories like anti-social behaviour and shoplifting.

Bradford's crime rate peaked at 145 per 1,000 in 2018.

It stood at 124 in 2025 and 120 over the most recent twelve months.

## WYP dispute the numbers

West Yorkshire Police, however, have queried the figures.

In response, a spokesperson for Crystal Roof explained they have two possible explanations for why their numbers differed from WYP.

They said: "Anti-social behaviour. Our total of 72,853 includes every category published on data.police.uk, including anti-social behaviour. Police recorded crime figures don't usually include it, because it is treated as a "non-notifiable" incident, not a recorded crime. In Bradford it makes up around 7,000 incidents a year, which on its own could explain a gap of several thousand.

"Boundaries. We use the Bradford local authority district boundary. The force may be using a smaller area, such as the Bradford built-up area."

## 3 worst areas in Bradford

Bradford’s least safe ward records around six times the crime rate of its safest, with the **City** ward topping the list at 178 overall crimes per 1,000 at-risk people.

**Tong** follows at 137, and**Eccleshill** at 136.

The safest wards are Wharfedale (31) and Ilkley and Addingham (35.1).

The ward figures represent overall crime - not serious crime rates.

## City centre market 'smell like drugs'

In the city centre, most people we approached didn’t want to talk.

One man eating his lunch at Darley Street Market told us he only travels into Bradford for work and doesn’t live here, but that he feels safe.

![]()Xander Ford is a Greengates resident *(Image: T&A)*

A couple of market traders told us that customers, especially those who are elderly, are being put off shopping due to the number of addicts hanging about in the city centre.

They also complained that they have to keep an eye out for thieves.

Business owner Anis Rahman, 48, echoed the thoughts of a fellow stallholder that customers simply don’t feel safe.

He said that Darley Street Market “smells like drugs” to such an extent that another trader has to smell perfume to mask the aroma.

And with groups of people hanging around, he can “easily see why people feel threatened”.

He said he doesn't see many police patrolling the city centre and worries that crime is affecting business at the market.

## 'We're too close to Holme Wood'

The T&A then visited Tong Street.

One mum, who has recently moved to the area from another part of West Yorkshire, did not agree that this area seemed much worse than places like Dewsbury, for example.

She said: "It's the same everywhere. You’ve just got keep your head down.”

We noticed that as soon as we mentioned the word 'crime' people kept schtum.

A business owner told us that he was not willing to speak because “we’re too close to Holme Wood”.

One woman and her son told us that if she could afford to move, she would in a heartbeat, explaining that there had been two recent murders in her area.

![]()Images of the Holme Wood estate *(Image: T&A)*

Another gentleman out shopping said that his nearby village gets targeted by people living in the Tong Street and Holme Wood area because “they don’t burgle their own”.

He felt children were not disciplined enough by their parents.

## Holme Wood fears

On Bradford’s largest estate, Holme Wood, no one we spoke to would provide their name.

We were told that was a quick way to “get your car petrol bombed or shot in the street”.

One woman told us she loved living in the area – in spite of the levels of crime and deprivation.

![]()Images of the Holme Wood estate *(Image: T&A)*

She thought that abolishing national service was “the worst thing” to happen for general discipline in the country.

Like others she told us that if she saw a crime happening her approach is “carry on it's nowt to do with me” unless it was an elderly person, in which case she said she would be prepared to go to prison in defending them.

## What is crime like in Greengates?

The T&A also went to Greengates, which is part of the Eccleshill ward, where a woman shopping spoke to us at length.

She accepted that crime, specifically revolving around drugs, was high, but felt that the solution is much wider than simply blaming police or the council.

She felt solving the crime issue was a multifaceted, societal issue for which there is no simple solution.

She said: "There's racial tension, there's social tension."

Xander Ford, 33, lives in Greengates and first moved to Bradford about a decade ago for university.

![]()Xander Ford is a Greengates resident *(Image: T&A)*

He’s lived in various parts of Bradford and was surprised to hear that the Eccleshill ward ranked so highly for crime.

But did say he has witnessed various instances of crime locally and added that his female friends don’t feel comfortable walking through the B&M car park on an evening.

He is concerned that community Facebook groups can give residents an impression that crime is much worse than it really is - with some people sharing photographs of totally innocent people.

He added: "Educate them that a person of colour is not a criminal."

To combat the high crime rates across the Bradford district, Mr Ford would like to see more youth centres to “give them [the youth] something to do.”

Bradford Council were contacted for a comment.

A West Yorkshire Police spokesperson said: "We are aware of residents' concerns in relation to crime in Bradford and are working to continue recent reductions in offending.

"Along with other forces, West Yorkshire Police records crime according to Home Office recording standards.

"Provisional data shows 64,692 offences were recorded in Bradford between July 2025 – 2026, a fall of 5 per cent compared to the previous 12 months.

"Neighbourhood policing teams continue to work in communities to tackle crime at a local level while dedicated plain clothes officers work to tackle major offending.

"Significant recent cases have included the arrest and charge of three men with drugs offences earlier this month following a proactive operation in Bradford led by West Yorkshire Police’s Operation Pheonix county lines taskforce

"Heroin and crack cocaine with a street value of more than £100,000 was recovered in joint operations with Bradford City NPT, Bradford’s Programme Precision team and the Bradford South Neighbourhood Policing Team’s Operation Sweetgill initiative

"Other recent work includes proactive drug operations in the city centre, knife crime prevention operations and extra evening patrols to boost the nighttime economy.

"Live time facial recognition cameras have also now been deployed to the city centre on 17 occasions, resulting in 29 arrests.

"We continue to encourage residents to report offending and urge anyone who has concerns about crime or ASB to report it to us, via 101 or via the contact us options online at www.westyorkshire.police.uk/contactus"
