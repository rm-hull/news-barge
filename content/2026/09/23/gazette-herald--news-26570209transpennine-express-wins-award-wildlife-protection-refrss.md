TransPennine Express has won a major rail industry award for protecting and enhancing wildlife across the North.

TPE collected the Environmental Excellence Award at the National Rail Awards in London on Wednesday, September 16, after judges praised its transformation of railway land into wildlife habitats.

Paul Staples, engineering, safety and sustainability director at TransPennine Express, said: "We are incredibly proud to receive this award, which recognises the dedication and passion of colleagues and partners who have helped bring these projects to life.

"Our railway connects communities across the North, and has an important role to play in restoring the resilience and diversity of the natural environment."

TPE’s long-term biodiversity strategy has transformed unused land at stations and beside railway lines into wildflower meadows, hedgerows, woodlands and pollinator-friendly areas.

A project at Yarm station has increased biodiversity by around 40 per cent.

At Thirsk station, TPE has created around 800 square metres of wildflower grassland and 90 metres of hedgerow for local wildlife.

The award is the latest recognition of TPE’s sustainability work, showing how rail operators can help nature recover while running essential services.
