More than £119 billion in savings is due to mature by the end of the year, putting savers at risk of losing thousands of pounds.

Skipton Building Society warns savers who do nothing could see their returns plunge by up to 45 per cent.

Skipton has dubbed it ‘Maturity Season’, when large sums in fixed-term accounts mature and savers must decide where to put their money.

Skipton research shows a third of UK savers do not understand ‘account maturity’, while many are unprepared to move their money when their accounts mature.

Katie Horne, Flagstone’s bank relationship manager, said: "Many people like taking advantage of long-term savings accounts that offer rates of interest on cash they can afford to lock away for many months or even years.

"Fixed-term saving accounts can be a hugely effective way to keep your money working hard when you don’t need to spend it.

"But, in the course of our busy lives, it’s not easy to keep tabs on when different fixed-term accounts are due to mature.

"As a result, huge amounts of newly-matured savings roll over into non-interest-bearing accounts, which over time erodes the value of the cash they hold."

Ms Horne also highlighted cash platforms, which let savers move money between accounts more easily and avoid losing interest.

She said: "Twelve months ago, it was widely assumed that interest rates would fall over the course of 2026.

"This hasn’t happened. In fact, economists predict the next Bank of England base rate decision could push interest rates up again.

"This should create a buoyant, competitive savings market.

"For those who do a little research, it’s not hard to find inflation-beating fixed-term and instant-access savings accounts, whatever their circumstances."
