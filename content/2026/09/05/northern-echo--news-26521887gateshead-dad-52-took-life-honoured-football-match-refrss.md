Men’s mental health and suicide prevention charities have flocked to support a memorial football match.

The Jimmy McLeod Memorial Cup is being organised by 25-year-old James McLeod, from Gateshead, in honour of his late father, Jimmy McLeod. Jimmy took his own life in November 2025, aged 52, following a long history of mental health difficulties.

James has made it his mission to raise awareness of men’s mental health, embarking on challenges just as ’52 for 52’, during which he is running an average of 52 miles for 52 weeks. The challenge will be completed in December, when James will be running the Valencia marathon.

The Memorial Cup will be held on Sunday, September 6, at New Ferens Park, Durham. It will feature a friendly match between McLeod FC, consisting of family and friends, and Men Together Durham CIC.

James describes having a “shared identity and shared purpose” with Men Together, an organisation dedicated to improving men’s mental and physical wellbeing.

New Ferens Park, where the match will take place, is of particular significance to James. “It was the last place my dad came to see me play football before I moved away from the sport and focused on running”, he said.

Football was a huge shared interest between the Jimmy and his son. In addition to love of the sport, James credits his father with his desire to help others, something he “instilled in [him] from a young age”.

September is also a “special month” for James, as the 25th will mark his first birthday without his dad. It is also mental health awareness month in some parts of the world, he points out.

James has praised the amount of support the event has received from men’s mental health and suicide prevention charities, calling it “one of the most encouraging parts of organising the event”. Charities such as Movember, Andy's Man Club, Chasing the Stigma, and Mental Shift CIC are all endorsing the event.

“Having organisations of this nature get behind the memorial is particularly meaningful because the purpose of the day goes far beyond football or fundraising”, he said. “I want dad’s story to help encourage conversations around men’s mental health, suicide prevention and the importance of reaching out when life becomes overwhelming.”

North East shops have also flocked to support the Memorial Cup; alongside the match, there will be a raffle of items kindly donated by these businesses. This includes a bottle of vodka and tickets to a bingo night. Jimmy had previously worked in the bingo industry.

To play or spectate the match, a minimum donation of £5.20 is required. All funds raised will go to If U Care Share Foundation, which supports families affected by suicide.

James first worked with the Foundation in 2020, when he completed a 13 mile charity half marathon with ‘emotional beanbags’. He speaks highly of If U Care Share, which he is also working with for his ’52 for 52’ challenge, saying without the charity “there’s no me”. He hopes to become an ambassador for the charity within the next year.

A sports science student at a North East university, James emphasises the importance of physical exercise as an emotional outlet; this is partly why he decided to start the 52 for 52 challenge. He is currently completing a PhD researching autistic adults’ experiences of physical education in secondary schools.

At the Memorial Cup, James is hoping for a turnout of around 300 people. Anyone is welcome to attend, and tickets will be sold on the door. Should the event be successful, James will host the Jimmy McLeod Memorial Cup on an annual basis.
