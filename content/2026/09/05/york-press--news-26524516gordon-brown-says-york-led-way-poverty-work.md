York has “led the way” in addressing poverty in communities, Gordon Brown has said.

Speaking to The Press at the University of York where the former prime minister turned anti-poverty campaigner was giving a talk about his new book, Mr Brown said he wanted to “congratulate York” on the 125th anniversary of the first report by Seebohm Rowntree on poverty in the city.

Rowntree’s 1901 ground-breaking sociological book, Poverty: A Study of Town Life, investigated living conditions and poverty in York.

Mr Brown said he wanted to “congratulate” how “York, and really Rowntree, have led the way of the United Kingdom” in addressing poverty.

“Because [Rowntree] did not only expose poverty here, but actually it led the way to rethinking what poverty actually means,” he said.

“And it’s so important because poverty is not just being without heating and food. Poverty is not being able to participate in the normal activities of the community.

“So York has led the way. And you’re a sanctuary city, you’re a human rights city, and I think you’re making your mark right across the United Kingdom.”

He said lessons from Rowntree must continue to shape policies and discussions around poverty in the modern day, telling The Press: “We’ve got to continue to look at what’s happening in local communities.

“What was great about the Rowntree work was that it was looking at what was actually happening on the ground – it wasn’t just from on high.

“And I think there’s a lot of community organising happening now that gets people more involved in shaping the lives of their community – not just looking for services that are done by two people, but actually controlling and deciding how the services could be shaped.”

  Gordon Brown on stage with Rachael Maskell at the University of York on Friday night (September 4) during a talk on his new book, The Future Starts With Us 

*(Image: Dylan Connell)*

York Central MP Rachael Maskell chaired Mr Brown’s talk at the University of York on Friday night (September 4) and told the audience: “We are in a battle to glue communities back together.”

The Labour MP said 34.9 per cent of people were in relative poverty in York.

Mr Brown praised Ms Maskell, telling the audience: “No MP has been so brilliant at putting forward the case for social poverty than Rachael Maskell.

“I’m in awe of her energy – she asked to speak to me yesterday at six in the morning!”

He said that communities must work together to tackle poverty, adding that Rowntree would be “appalled” by the fact that around 4.5 million children in the UK are living in poverty. “To think that 125 years later, still we have that many children in poverty,” he said.

Mr Brown urged people to become more active in their communities and to unite to inspire change.
