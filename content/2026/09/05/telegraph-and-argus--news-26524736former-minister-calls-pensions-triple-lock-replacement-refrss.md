Former cabinet minister Baroness Therese Coffey has called for the pensions triple lock to be replaced.

Baroness Coffey, who was work and pensions secretary from 2019 to 2022, backed replacing it with a "double lock" system.

Introduced by the Conservative-Liberal Democrat coalition in 2010, the triple lock raises the state pension each year by whichever is highest: inflation, average earnings growth or 2.5 per cent.

Speaking in the Lords on Friday, Baroness Coffey said: "I think sometimes the triple lock gets blamed for a lot of things.

"To some extent, I would actually probably prefer some kind of double lock.

"The 2.5 per cent probably seemed the right thing at the time in order to achieve the policy outcome of getting pensions as a proportion of earnings up more quickly and it’s achieved that, I think, by and large."

Her comments came during a debate on a committee report examining the UK's readiness for an ageing population.

Peers from across the political spectrum also called for the policy to be reconsidered.

Conservative peer Lord Tugendhat said the triple lock was "much needed" when introduced but should now be replaced "without further ado."

Crossbench peer Lord Turnbull, a former civil service chief, was blunter, calling the policy "frankly idiotic."

Lord Turnbull said: "We have a chaotic system generating random windfall gains, and they are always gains, according to the movement of earnings and prices in particular years.

"The OBR has estimated that this could cost an extra two per cent of GDP."

Labour’s Lord Reid of Cardowan said the report should "more forthrightly consider and challenge the triple lock."

But not all peers agreed.

Conservative peer Lord Redwood called ending the policy a "great pity" and credited it with helping reduce pensioner poverty.

Labour peer Lord Liddle, who introduced the report, said he felt "a little guilty" that it "didn’t directly address the triple lock question" but that it was "obvious" the policy needed review.

Lord Liddle said: "The lack of any published strategy on ageing is a worrying indication that the Government would prefer not to face up to these challenges as an immediate question, and that the UK is therefore woefully unprepared for the future."

Treasury minister Lord Pitt-Watson said the triple lock was a Labour manifesto promise.

Lord Pitt-Watson said: "Till the end of this Parliament, the triple lock remains."
