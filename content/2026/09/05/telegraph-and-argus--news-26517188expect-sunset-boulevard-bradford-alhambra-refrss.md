SUNSET Boulevard - the tale of a struggling screenwriter pulled into the delusional, tragic world of an ageing silent film star - is heading for Bradford’s Alhambra Theatre next week.

Featuring one of Andrew Lloyd Webber’s most celebrated scores, including With One Look, The Perfect Year and As If We Never Said Goodbye, the “dark, intoxicating and fiercely theatrical” musical is presented by Diva Productions.

Leading the cast as Norma Desmond is Alison George, with James Banks as Joe Gillis.

The show blends live performance, video and multimedia reflecting how the characters are caught between reality and images they create.

* What made you want to tackle Sunset Boulevard?

Director Andrew Ashley: “It’s a show where everything comes together: an incredible score, fascinating characters and a story that’s much darker and more complex than people perhaps expect from a musical. Underneath all the glamour of Hollywood, it’s a brutal story about what happens when an industry decides it has finished with somebody. Norma was one of the biggest stars in the world and suddenly she’s living in this enormous house, surrounded by memories, trying to convince herself that her ‘return’ is just around the corner.

We live in a world where fame can arrive overnight and disappear almost as quickly. The need to be seen, to remain relevant and to control how the world sees you feels more prevalent than ever.

For Diva, it felt the right time to take it on. We’ve continued to grow in ambition, Sunset Boulevard gives us the opportunity to bring together great acting, huge vocals, movement, music and strong visual storytelling on a grand scale.”

* The musical is based on Billy Wilder’s iconic 1950 film. How much has the film influenced your approach?

“The film is in the DNA of our production, but we weren’t interested in simply recreating it. What excites me is finding ways to use the language of cinema theatrically. We’ve used video for years, it’s exciting to develop that on a much more ambitious scale. We’re using multimedia and live camera work at different points, so the audience can experience a moment simultaneously as theatre and cinema. You might be watching the actor on stage while also seeing a close-up of them projected behind them.

  Sunset Boulevard is a story of 'ambition obsession, love and manipulation' 

*(Image: Stephanie Thornton/Diva Productions)*

Norma has spent her life being photographed and projected onto screens, the camera becomes another storytelling device. It’s dark, fractured, cinematic. It’s about creating the feeling of a Hollywood that has left Norma behind. We’ve stripped away some of the more obvious ideas of glamour and concentrated on memory, image and decay.”

* Norma Desmond is one of musical theatre’s great roles. What does Alison George bring to the character?

“Alison has an amazing ability to give you both sides of Norma. Of course, you need somebody who can command the stage - Norma enters a room and everything changes. She’s funny, outrageous, demanding, occasionally terrifying, and Alison absolutely embraces that. But what has really impressed me is the vulnerability and truth she finds underneath it all. There are moments where the facade drops and you see this incredibly lonely woman who desperately needs somebody to tell her she still matters.

Vocally, it’s a huge role. The songs are iconic for a reason, and Alison brings enormous power to them. But ultimately, we’re not trying to create a caricature of Norma Desmond. We want audiences to understand her. They might not always like what she does, but hopefully there are moments where they genuinely feel for her.”

* Choreography plays a big role in this production. How have you approached that?

Choreographer Charlotte Wallis: “We have big ensemble numbers and dynamic moments. We’ve also used it to explore Norma’s her character in quite an abstract way.

Andrew: “The world outside Norma’s mansion has an incredible energy. These are young actors, writers, dancers and film-makers all chasing their opportunity, everything is constantly moving. We’ve used that to create a contrast with Norma’s world, where time has almost stopped.”

* What does bringing Sunset Boulevard to the Alhambra mean to the company?

Andrew: “The Alhambra is one of the great theatres in the region, there’s something very special about putting this show on that stage.”

Charlotte: “It’s an iconic theatre. The Alhambra is the perfect theatre to make the show feel as grand as it deserves.”
