## York landmarks that were built faster than York Station Gateway scheme

York Station c1900. Picture: Explore York Libraries and Archives *(Image: Picture: Explore York Libraries and Archives)*

THE Yorkshire Museum, hotels, theatres, and even the station itself are among the York buildings that took less time to construct than the current Station Gateway project.

Construction on the Station Gateway scheme began on October 16, 2023, and is expected to continue through 2026 and beyond.

This means that as of today, the works have been going on for just under two years and eleven months – however this timeframe does not include enabling works, which started in January 2022.

As reported by The Press in March, City of York Council’s city development lead Garry Taylor told councillors the ongoing Station Gateway scheme had been dogged by oversight problems from its early stages but they were now being addressed.

Since this time, a further £6.5 million has been spent and a row has erupted over who is to blame for the issues with the scheme – with opposition Liberal Democrat leader Cllr Nigel Ayre saying the overspend was one of the authority’s biggest financial failures.

Cllr Katie Lomas, the Labour council's executive member for finance, performance and major projects, responded that the Liberal Democrats should apologise for approving a fantasy budget and reckless contract before they left office, leaving the current administration to sort out the mess.

After sharing the historic buildings that took less time to build, here are some local projects completed in less time than the Station Gateway scheme:

  The Yorkshire Museum which has received a £2m cash boost to help fix its leaking roof 

*(Image: YMT)*

  **Yorkshire Museum and Museum Gardens**

First opening in the Georgian era, the Yorkshire Museum’s main purpose-built museum building was constructed between 1827 and 1829, on the St Mary’s Abbey site and took close to two years to complete.

It was originally created after an archaeological find of bones in a cave on the Moors proved that elephants, rhinos, hippos, hyenas, and elks had once roamed across the area.

This find had a huge impact on the people of York and led to formation of the Yorkshire Philosophical Society, who quickly outgrew their premises on Low Ousegate and raised money for a new tailor-made home for their exhibitions.

After buying the land from City of York Council, Archbishop of York laid the foundation stone of the Yorkshire Museum in 1827, which took around two years to build before the Museum and its Gardens opened to the public in 1830.

  


   **York Theatre Royal redevelopment**

In the first change to the building since1967, the York Theatre Royal was redeveloped in seven months between September 2015 and 2016.

During this project, the theatre added an open-plan foyer; doubled its cafe area with a new café and bistro and created a new box office, while the gothic colonnade has been enclosed by glass to make a new entrance.

It also reconstructed the main stage, upgraded the seating across all levels, improved access by installing a lift, improved lighting facilities, added new toilets, and repaired the roof.

The theatre, which was founded in 1744, is Britain's oldest producing theatre outside London and even with unexpected archaeological discoveries beneath the stalls (including small clay pipe fragments, pottery, animal bone and glass), it was completed in just over half a year.

   

*(Image: Jorvik Viking Centre)*

  **Jorvik Viking Centre rebuild**

After being devastated in the December 2015 floods, the Jorvik reopened its doors to the public following a £4 million pound renovation which much of the museum’s wooden and plaster set beneath up to 3ft (1m) of water.

The project was started in September 2015 and took the teams around eight months to complete, opening on April 14, 2017 – 33 years to the day after it had first opened to the public.

It involved a near-total re-creation of the visitor experience, including the ride’s sound system, displays and rebuilt Viking cityscape.

Several hotels – Malmaison (which saw the former Aviva offices in Rougier Street converted), Moxy York in Peasholme Green, and Hampton by Hilton in Piccadilly – each took under two years to be completed.

The scheme also looks set to last longer than the construction of the LNER Community Stadium at Monks Cross, which lasted from December 2017 to December 2020, and the development of York Railway Station itself.
