YOU can’t beat a good pie.

Whether it’s a traditional pork pie you’re craving, or something more quirky, an artisan pie-maker in Bradford is serving up a variety of fillings from Darley Street Market’s food traders.

Kneads Must Pies has opened in the market’s Street Kitchen, selling a range of pies and sides.

The pies come in an array of flavours, with regulars including pork; corned beef and tattie; and lentil and vegetable curry.

Alongside these there are special pies, with fillings such as pork and tikka masala and three-cheese and onion -Yorkshire cream cheese, Gouda and Applewood smoked cheddar with red and white onion. New flavours are planned throughout the year.

  The upper crust... pies come in a range of fillings.


The popular traditional pork pie is based on a tried and tested family recipe that has been passed down through generations of owner Chris Hall’s family.

There is also a vegetarian and a vegan option available every day.

Pie accompaniments include beans, peas, gravy and mash, with sauces and drinks also available.

All the fillings are sourced from fellow traders in the market, including Taplins Butchers, Solly’s Fruit and Veg, Roswitha’s Deli and Dale Farm Foods.

  All the pie fillings are sourced from fellow traders in the market


The business is owned by Chris Hall who runs it with his friend Sully Hemming.

It’s open Monday to Sunday during the Food Court’s regular opening hours.

Says Chris: “I’ve been looking at opening a pie shop for many years and when this opportunity came up, I jumped at the chance. I’m excited to bring artisan pies to Bradford, and supporting many of the other traders in creating them.”

  Accompaniments include beans, peas, gravy and mash.


Here Chris tells us more about Kneads Must Pies:

Q: How would you describe your business?

Handmade artisan pies, with traditional fillings to more interesting ones. All are made in the market using locally sourced ingredients from the fresh food in the market

Q: What’s the background?

I’ve been making pies in Jacobs Well pub for seven years but have now taken the plunge to open own handmade artisan pies.

Q: Do you see the business as part of the local community?

Yes, my fillings are made using fresh food from the market and we’re also trying to bring back some traditional fillings that may not have been seen in a while. I enjoy making interesting, flavoured pies such as lasagne pie, shepherd’s pie in a pie, brekkie pie, pork and tikka masala pie and chicken and spinach kariah pie.

  Traditional meat pie with mushy peas and gravy.


Q: Do you have themed events?

I’m looking into doing some themed pies to go with upcoming events in the market suck as Oktoberfest in September, with a pork and sauerkraut pie.

Q: What do you enjoy about being part of Darley Street Market?

It’s like a community.

Q: What kind of food do you like to cook at home?

Lasagne, gammon broths, stews roasts, sausage and mash, and pizzas.

Cllr Ian Walker, Bradford Council’s Executive Member for Neighbourhoods and Communities says: “We’re delighted to welcome Kneads Must Pies to Darley Street Market.

“Chris’s pies have been popular choice for regulars at the Jacob’s Well pub, so it’s fantastic to see this business expanding into the market as well as championing other traders by using the high-quality produce from within the marked.

“Supporting local businesses and encouraging collaboration between traders is exactly what Darley Street Market is all about, and I wish Chris and Sully every success in their new venture.”

l The recent opening of the pie shop featured a guest appearance with a link to a quirky story from Bradford’s past.

Bradford poet and showman Glyn Watkins brought his giant model of a steaming pie that he made to celebrate writer JB Priestley’s birthday. It was, says Glyn, the Bradford meat and potato pie that defied Hitler...

“The original model pie stood in the window of Robert’s Pie Shop, on Godwin Street,” says Glyn. “In September 1940 Priestley broadcast to the world about how he remembered how the giant pie steaming all the hours the shop was open.

“He explained that Bradford had been bombed, and he’d been told the pie shop was destroyed. He had planned to stand in the ruins: ‘...catching perhaps a last faint lingering whiff of that steam, to compose some kind of lament or elegy’.

“But to his joy he found that the pie was still there: ‘...steaming away like mad. Every puff and jet of that steam defied Hitler, Goering, and the whole gang of them. It was glorious’.”

After pie man Robert died the famous pie disappeared, until Glyn started putting on a show to celebrate Priestley’s birthday - and created a new steaming pie model.

Says Glyn: “When I came to Bradford in 1979 I discovered the joy of pie, peas and mint sauce, and thought it shameful that for years there wasn’t a single shop in central Bradford selling them. I was overjoyed to hear about Chris’ shop, I think he’s the best pie maker in Bradford. They’re better pies than my mum made!”
