Seven County Durham firms have been named and shamed by the Government after workers were denied thousands of pounds in wages.

The latest enforcement list names nearly 660 employers across the UK accused of failing to pay staff the National Minimum Wage.

More than 27,000 workers have received about £4 million in back pay, while the employers were fined £7 million.

Among those named are businesses in County Durham and Darlington.

  **The County Durham employers on the list are:**

• Beamish Park Golf Club Limited, Stanley, DH9, failed to pay £4,298.64 to one worker.

• PVR & Co. Ltd, trading as Premier - Tantobie Stores, Stanley, DH9, failed to pay £2,938.96 to two workers.

• Roseberry Care Centres GB Limited, Houghton-Le-Spring, DH4, failed to pay £1,757.71 to 25 workers.

• Milligans Durham Limited, Newcastle Upon Tyne, NE16, failed to pay £1,385.05 to 15 workers.

• Il Cancello Italian Ltd, Stanley, DH9, failed to pay £818.89 to nine workers.

  **The Darlington employers named are:**

• Maxideals Group Ltd, Darlington, DL3, failed to pay £4,033.33 to 51 workers.

• C & R Global Leisure Ltd, trading as The Turks Head, Darlington, DL3, failed to pay £797.77 to eight workers.

The Government said the latest naming round was the first since the Fair Work Agency was set up in April.

The new body was created under the Employment Rights Act to bring workers’ rights enforcement under one roof.

It will enforce minimum-wage rules and soon target employers who break the law by denying holiday or sick pay.

Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds warned: "Short-changing your staff isn’t a shortcut to success and we are determined to stamp it out."

Minister for the Future of Work Kate Dearden said: "Underpaying your staff is illegal, and we will not let workers foot the bill for their boss failing to follow the rules.

"We’re making sure workers get the hard-earned pay they deserve, and where employers fall short, they will be held to account."

Employers should check their payroll and contact Acas for support, the Government said.

More than £100 million in penalties have been issued to over 5,200 employers since the naming scheme began in 2011, the Government said.

More than £66 million has been repaid to 650,000 workers since 2011.

The current minimum wage rates from April 2026 are £12.71 for workers aged 21 and over, £10.85 for those aged 18 to 20, £8 for workers under 18 and £8 for apprentices.

Employers must pay workers at least the minimum wage for their age or apprentice status for every hour worked.
