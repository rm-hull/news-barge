HUNDREDS of people have had their say about the future of an historic property in Settle which has links to English composer, Sir Edward Elgar.

The charity owners of listed 'Dr Buck's House' - which was last used as a branch of NatWest bank, have been running a consultation over the summer.

North Craven Building Preservation Trust originally planned to hold three open days and survey around 300 local residents about the future of the building in the centre of town which bears a plaque describing how Sir Edward Elgar 'often stayed here as a guest of his friend, Dr Buck'.

  Composer Sir Edward Elgar stayed at the house as a guest of his friend, Dr Buck 

*(Image: Lesley Tate)*

But so great has been the interest, that there has now been nine open days with more than 300 people from across the UK paying a visit.

Due to the overwhelming interest, trust volunteers will continue to open the house on Tuesdays and Thursdays until the October half term school holidays.

More than 400 people took part in the survey, which was limited to just residents of North Craven. Two thirds responded that they where interested in the future of the building and three quarters agreed that redeveloping the house would have a positive impact on the area.

The Trust also tested out some ideas for the future of the building, which were well received. Some 72 percent of respondents supported plans to convert the upper floors to housing for long-term lets, while 74 percent were in favour of plans to open a new museum gallery on the ground floor.

A fundraising Langcliffe Tea organised by Victoria Murray, The Folly Coffee House’s manager, raised more than £1,200 in August.

Heather Lane, Chair of Trustees, North Craven Building Preservation Trust, said: "We’re delighted by the community’s positive response to our plans. Every penny raised and every opinion expressed helps us make the case to our funders about investing in the future of Dr Buck’s House.”

  Dr Buck's House, Settle 

*(Image: Lesley Tate)*

And, Caitlin Greenwood, Heritage Development Officer, said: "I’m just so grateful to the National Lottery Heritage Fund, and to National Lottery players, for helping us undertake this vital survey work. It’s so important that they support organisations like us to be able to consult the community on our plans.”

The survey was completed by Bluegrass Research, an independent market research company. It was funded by National Lottery Heritage Fund.

Dr Buck’s House was acquired by North Craven Building Preservation Trust in August 2023 and plans for its development were approved by North Yorkshire Council earlier this year.
