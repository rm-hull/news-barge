Two violent thugs who were involved in a brutal knife attack were captured on CCTV confronting their victim outside a shop.

The victim was forced to jump onto the top of a van to flee the scene as Michael Flanigan encouraged Charles Hoe to inflict the stab wounds.

The pair were later involved in ramming a stolen car into parked vehicles as masked men sought a house, Teesside Crown Court heard.

Chris Wood, prosecuting, said police were alerted to the incident when they were called by medical staff at James Cook University Hospital as they treated the man’s serious stab wounds.

The court heard how the victim refused to co-operate with police as they investigated the stabbing but they were able to track down CCTV footage from around Derwentwater Road in Grangetown, near Middlesbrough.

The victim had been dropped off at the shop by the occupants of a white van before a confrontation erupted with 20-year-old Hoe and Flanigan, 30.

Footage showed the pair confronting the man before Hoe attacked him with a knife before he managed to escape hanging onto the roof of a van as it drove away.

As they walked away from the scene, Hoe could be heard bragging how he had "done" the victim twice.

Dr Wood said the defendants were later involved in crashing a stolen car into other vehicles later that night after having driven masked men to try and find a house.

Hoe, of Wilton Way, Middlesbrough, pleaded guilty to inflicting grievous bodily harm with intent aggravated vehicle taking from March 4.

The 20-year-old read out a letter to the court where he apologised for his actions.

His co-accused, Flanigan, of Harwal Road, Redcar, pleaded guilty to wounding without intent and aggravated vehicle taking.

Rod Hunt, representing the 30-year-old, his client accepts that it is time that he ‘grew up’ and started behaving like an adult.

  Michael Flanigan 

*(Image: Cleveland Police)*

Recorder Marco Giuliani sentenced Hoe to a total of 80 months in a young offenders’ institute and Flanigan to 38 months in prison after telling him he should have been setting a better example to his younger accomplice.

He said: “Hoe, you were seen to lunged towards the victim, he was forced to climb onto the top of the van to try to get away from you.

“When you pull your arm back you can be seen to be holding a knife and whilst holding it, you continue to lunge towards him.

“The van then pulls away with him still on the roof of the van, so desperate were those men to get away.”

The judge said the victim suffered life-threatening injuries but was saved when medics stemmed his ‘catastrophic bleeding’.

Hoe was banned from driving for 40 months while Flanigan was banned for 32 months.

Speaking after the hearing, Detective Chief Inspector Nicola Barker, from Cleveland Police, said: “This was an horrific crime that centred on using serious violence to instil fear and harm.

“A significant amount of work went into gathering and obtaining evidential materials such as mobile and CCTV footage to successfully identify Flanigan and Hoe.

“It shows that even without a victim wanting to support the case, we can still rely on the evidence and take positive action, showing our commitment to protect the safety of the community by targeting and disrupting dangerous individuals.”
