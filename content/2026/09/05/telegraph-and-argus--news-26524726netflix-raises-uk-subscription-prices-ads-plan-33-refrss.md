BRADFORD Netflix users will have to pay more after the streaming service raised the prices of its UK subscriptions, with its cheapest plan jumping by a third in the latest round of hikes.

But some users will have to pay more sooner than new sign-ups.

New customers signing up to Netflix plans will pay the increased prices immediately. Existing subscribers will receive one month’s notice by email, although the timing will depend on their billing cycle.

The standard plan with adverts now costs £7.99 a month. This is a 33.4% increase from its previous price of £5.99.

Netflix’s advert-free standard plan has risen by 7.7%, from £12.99 to £13.99 a month.

Its premium plan now costs £20.99 a month, up from £18.99. The plan allows streaming on extra devices and lets customers add more members.

The cost of adding an extra member has also increased, from £4.99 to £5.99 a month.

This is the second UK price increase by Netflix this year. The company raised the cost of every plan in February.

A spokeswoman for Netflix said: “reflect improvements to our wide range of entertainment and the quality of our service”.

Netflix said: “We know members have never had more choices in entertainment, and we’re committed to delivering an experience that meets and exceeds their expectations.”

The changes affect customers paying for access to programmes including Adolescence, Stranger Things and Bridgerton. Films available on the platform include KPop Demon Hunters and Happy Gilmore 2.

New and upcoming releases this year include The Gentlemen 2, Black Doves 2 and Pride And Prejudice. Netflix has also announced new commissions including The Unheard, starring Kate Winslet, and Jonny Boy, starring Barry Keoghan.

The company generated 45.2 billion US dollars (£33.4 billion) in revenue during 2025, a 17% increase on the previous year. It recorded an operating profit of 13.3 billion US dollars (£9.8 billion).

Max Beckett, broadband and TV expert for price comparison site Uswitch, said: “The good news is that if you pay Netflix directly, you’re not locked in.”

He said: “If the new price doesn’t feel worth it, you can downgrade or cancel penalty-free at any time – dropping from standard to the ad-supported plan now saves £6 a month, or £72 a year, if you can live with the adverts.”

Mr Beckett said: “Free services like ITVX, Channel 4 and Tubi cost nothing at all.”

He said: “It’s also a good moment to add up what you spend on streaming and broadband together, and check whether a better bundle could cut the total.”
