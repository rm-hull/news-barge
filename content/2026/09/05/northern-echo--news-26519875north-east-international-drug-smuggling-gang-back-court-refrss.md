Members of an international drug smuggling ring who benefited to the tune of almost £5m importing cannabis will have to wait to learn how much they will have to pay back.

Jordan Stoker organised the shipment of more than a tonne of cannabis into the region from his home using a network of mules bringing suitcases crammed full of the Class B drug.

He roped in his mam, dad and brother, as he helped to flood the region with drugs while working for an organised crime gang with its roots in County Durham.

  Drugs seized as part of the operation to take down Jordan Stoker and his gang 

*(Image: North East Regional Organised Crime Unit)*

Despite orchestrating the importation of almost £5m worth of cannabis into the North East from Thailand he was ordered to pay back just £1 when he appeared in court in May.

Now, his co-accused have been told they will have to wait until December to learn how much they will have to pay back through a Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA) hearing.

  Drug lynchpin Jordan Stoker, left, with his trusted lieutenant Paul Lamb, right 

*(Image: North East Regional Organised Crime Unit)*

The finances of Brian Stoker, Christopher Sowerby, Shaun Sowerby, Ian Sowerby and Paul Lamb, will be dealt with at a hearing on December 7.

Teesside Crown Court heard how the defendants must submitted their evidence by next month before the prosecution responds by the end of November.

  The three Sowerby brothers. Christopher, left, Shaun, centre, and Ian, right. 

*(Image: North East Regional Organised Crime Unit)*

POCA investigations into Jordan Stoker’s mother, Sarah Leighton, and brother, Ethan Smith, have been discontinued.

   

*(Image: North East Regional Organised Crime Unit)*

At the hearing in May, Richard Herrmann, prosecuting, told Teesside Crown Court Stoker had been involved with £4,947,183.75 worth of drug trafficking of cannabis and cocaine during the conspiracy.

“There is an available amount of a nominal £1,” he said.

Mr Herrmann said Trelawny Brown had benefited £12,256.36 and an order was made that he had three months to pay the sum back.

Drugs mule Matthew Goodwin had benefited £26,061.26 and £6,960 seized by police was confiscated through the POCA powers.

Stoker, of Hambleton Road, Coundon, Bishop Auckland, was jailed for 19 years after pleading guilty to conspiracy to supply cocaine and cannabis, conspiracy to evade a prohibition on importation of a controlled drug, driving while disqualified and driving without insurance.

Lamb, of McCullagh Gardens, Bishop Auckland, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to supply cocaine and conspiracy to evade a prohibition on importation of a controlled drug. He was sentenced to seven years and two months.

Stoker’s father, Brian, of Waterloo Terrace, Shildon, was jailed for five years and two months after admitting conspiracy to evade a prohibition on importation of a controlled drug in his role as a driver.

Leighton, of Queen Street, Shildon, was sentenced to 22-and-a-half months in custody suspended for two years for possession of criminal property. She was ordered to carry out 250 hours of unpaid work.

Her son, Ethan Smith, was sentenced to 18 months in prison suspended for two years for the same charge. He was also ordered to carry out 250 hours of unpaid work.

The three Sowerby brothers, all of Teesdale Walk, Shildon, pleaded guilty to conspiracy to supply cannabis and conspiracy to supply cocaine.

Ian Sowerby was sentenced to ten years and ten months while 30-year-old Christopher and Shaun, 37, were sentenced to 13 years and one month in custody.

Drugs mules, Brown, of Rotherham, and Goodwin, from Wigan, were jailed for three years and four and a half months and three years and nine months respectively.
