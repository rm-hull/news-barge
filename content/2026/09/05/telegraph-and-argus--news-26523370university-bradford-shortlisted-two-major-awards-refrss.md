THE University of Bradford has been shortlisted for two categories at this year’s Times Higher Education (THE) Awards.

The nominations recognise the university’s work in supporting children’s health and promoting diversity in academia.

In the running for Outstanding Contribution to the Local Community, the university’s ‘Creating Active Schools’ (CAS) programme focuses on improving children’s health and wellbeing.

Dr Anna Chalkley, senior research fellow and co-lead on the project, said: "We are incredibly proud that CAS and the University of Bradford has been shortlisted for this award.

"This is particularly special because CAS is fundamentally a story about Bradford, partnership, and what can be achieved when research, practice and communities work together.

"CAS has grown through sustained collaboration with schools and an amazing network of partners across Bradford, bringing together research evidence, professional expertise and local knowledge to help schools create environments in which physical activity becomes part of the culture of the whole school."

Led by Professor Andy Daly-Smith, associate director at the Institute of Health and Social Care, CAS is an internationally recognised, research-led approach cited by the World Health Organisation.

It currently supports more than 400 schools across the UK.

The university is also shortlisted for Outstanding Contribution to Equality, Diversity and Inclusion for its Brad-ATTAIN programme (Bradford Pathways to Academia for Minoritised Ethnicities).

This £1.6 million initiative supports Black, Asian and other ethnically minoritised students to pursue postgraduate research and academic careers through internships, studentships and targeted mentoring.

Professor Udy Archibong, pro vice-chancellor (Equality, Diversity and Inclusion) and project lead, said: "Championing EDI is central to our mission and reflects our commitment to creating opportunities for all.

"The positive impact of Brad-ATTAIN on students, researchers and partners demonstrates the value of embedding an intersectional approach across higher education, helping to foster fairness, belonging and opportunity in an increasingly complex world."

The programme has contributed to significant changes in postgraduate representation at the university.

In 2022/23, 52 per cent of postgraduate researchers were from ethnically minoritised backgrounds.

This rose to 71 per cent in 2024/25, nearly three times the sector average of 24 per cent.

Professor Gillian Murray, vice-chancellor of the University of Bradford, said: "Being shortlisted in two categories at the THE Awards is a significant achievement and recognition of the impact our University is making."
