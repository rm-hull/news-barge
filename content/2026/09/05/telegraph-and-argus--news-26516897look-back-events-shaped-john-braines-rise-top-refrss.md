THE intention of my two features on John Braine is to historically describe his life and influences, not to make comment on his changing political or religious views and background.

* Birth and Early Life

Accredited in some publications as being from Bingley and others from Thackley, Braine was born in a small, terraced house in Westgate, Bradford in April 1922.

His parents, Fred and Katherine, lived with his mother’s parents. Katherine had been a library assistant for Bradford Corporation and Fred was a sewage works supervisor prior to moving to Thackley as supervisor of Esholt Sewage Works. John attended Thackley school from 1927 until 1933 and then spent five years at St Bede’s School in Heaton, failing his leaving certificate and leaving school at the age of 16.

* Work and Military Service

Between 1938 and 1940 he worked as a junior salesman at Pratts Furniture shop in Bradford, progress chaser at Hepworth and Grandage, Yeadon and a students assistant at Bradford Technical College in 1939. Records also show him as a volunteer air raid warden at Idle. From 1940 he worked at Bingley Library as a library assistant before being called up to join the Navy in 1942.

He trained as a wireless operator/ telegraphist but never saw operational service. He contracted TB and was discharged and sent for recuperation at Grassington TB hospital where he spent five months in 1944.

He returned to work at Bingley library, studied and passed the Leeds School of Librarianship exam at his fourth attempt in 1949.

- Bingley Little Theatre then London

Braine took an active interest in local amateur dramatics and in 1951 his play Desert in the Mirror was performed at Bingley Little Theatre to mixed reviews. His interest in theatre coincided with his relationship with a local Colonel’s daughter who he met at the theatre. In the same year he had his first manuscript, Joe for King, rejected by publishers.

He left the library to try to earn his living as a writer in London, where he was initially successful, selling articles to the New Statesman and The Tribune and writing for BBC radio. Unfortunately, his TB condition returned, necessitating further treatment at Grassington from 1952 until mid 1954.

* Newbiggin by the Sea - Northumberland

In summer 1954 he moved to Northumberland, as Liaison Officer with the County Library Service for four months. Living in Morpeth, he then transferred to Newbiggin as Librarian, whilst also working at the Children’s Library in Ashington two days a week. In October he married Helen Wood.

His outline manuscript for Room at the Top was accepted by Ayre and Spottiswood in 1955 for release in March 1957, having been previously rejected four times

Braine left Newbiggin in 1956.

* Dalton - West Yorkshire

In March 1957 he was working as £13 per week Librarian at Dalton library, Kirklees and living on Doncaster Road, Wakefield This was the same month that Room at the Top was published. By June, due to the immediate success of the book he was able to give up work to concentrate solely on writing.

* Bingley

John and Helen moved to Bingley in October 1957, living on Priestthorpe Road and paying cash for their new home. Room at the Top sold 5,000 copies in the first week and 35,000 copies in its first year. Within a year Braine was earning £1,000 a week and by 1960 ‘Room’ had sold half a million hardback copies.

Braine then devoted himself to writing and travel, writing three novels between 1959 and 1966 and undertaking lecture tours in the USA in 1959 and 1964.

* Woking

In 1966 the family left Bingley to live in Woking in a large six-bedroom house. Braine commuted daily to offices in the centre of Woking to write, and between 1968 and 1985 he wrote a further eight novels.

In the 1980s he experienced serious health problems associated with his lifestyle. Additionally, his marriage to Helen broke down and by the early 1980s they had separated.

- Hampstead

In 1983 he moved to a flat in Hampstead, London and in 1984 and 1985 his final two books were published. In late 1985 he commenced writing a column, ‘Share My Life’ for the Yorkshire Post - his last writing assignment.

He died in hospital on October 28, 1986 of a gastric haemorrhage, during an operation on a stomach ulcer. His funeral was held at Nab Wood Cemetery, Shipley.
