Donald Trump has claimed the US has “an idea for peace” as he sends his special envoy Steve Witkoff for talks with both Moscow and Kyiv.

Speaking to press on Friday, the president suggested the US had a fresh concrete proposal aimed at ending the war, which is now in its fifth year. He did not, however, elaborate on what that proposal would be.

Nor did he say if Witkoff or his son-in-law Jared Kushner would be traveling to Ukraine, Russia or both nations.

It comes after reports that Witkoff is “too scared” to travel to Kyiv for peace talks throwing planned negotiations into doubt.

The Kyiv visit was thrown into doubt amid claims that Witkoff is concerned about Russian warnings that he should not travel to the Ukrainian capital, the Kyiv Independent reported.

"The Russians don't want them to come here to Kyiv, and Witkoff is always sensitive to Russian sentiment,” a source told the outlet. "They're being told there that it's dangerous."

The pair were due to arrive in Kyiv by plane, with the outlet previously reporting that Witkoff does not like taking the train to Kyiv.

## Ukraine says two major Russian oil facilities halted after strikes

Two major Russian oil facilities have halted operations following Ukrainian strikes, Ukraine’s General Staff said on Friday.

Lukoil’s NORSI refinery in Kstovo, in the Nizhny Novgorod region, was forced to stop operating after a Ukrainian drone strike on 26 August. The refinery is around 800km from the Ukrainian border and is Russia’s fourth-largest oil refinery.

The General Staff also said the Novatek-Ust-Luga complex in the Leningrad region had suspended operations after a strike earlier this week. It said pipelines, a gas-condensate processing unit, and two hydrocracking columns were damaged, with oil-product spills and a fire also reported.

## ICYMI: Trump envoys head to Moscow and Kyiv with peace proposal

US special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are heading to Moscow and Kyiv in a fresh attempt by Donald Trump’s administration to revive efforts to end the war in Ukraine.

Trump told reporters on Friday: “We have an idea for peace”, adding that Witkoff and Kushner were being sent to see “whether or not we can get something done” and that “there may be a good chance that we’ll do it”.

The pair are expected to visit Moscow first before travelling to Kyiv on Sunday, Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed. It will be their first visit to Ukraine, while Witkoff has travelled to Moscow several times.

## Zelensky calls for temporary air truce during US envoys’ visit

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky has proposed a temporary halt to airstrikes while US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner visit Moscow and Kyiv, saying Ukraine will not launch attacks during the diplomatic mission.

“There will be no air strikes from our side, and Russia must do the same, ensuring there are no air strikes from its side for the time needed to hold these talks and handle the logistical matters related to this American visit,” Zelensky said in his evening address on Friday.

Zelensky said the envoys would travel to Moscow before heading to Kyiv on Sunday.

## Russia attacks Kyiv despite reported ceasefire, injuring two

Russian drones struck Kyiv and the surrounding region overnight, with at least two people injured despite a reported ceasefire.

Two women were hurt and taken to hospital in Boryspil, around 29km east of Kyiv, according to regional authorities, reported the *Kyiv Independent*. A nine-storey residential building was also damaged, with its roof and top floor catching fire.

The attacks continued into the early hours of Saturday after the ceasefire was said to have begun ahead of US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner’s visit to Ukraine and Russia.

Air-raid warnings were issued repeatedly across Kyiv and Kyiv Oblast through the night, while explosions were reported in the capital. Russian drones and glide bombs also targeted several other Ukrainian cities and frontline areas.

## Trump says Europe should repay US for Ukraine aid - ICYMI

Donald Trump said on Thursday that European countries should repay the US for military aid and ammunition previously sent to Ukraine.

He also appeared to signal a temporary halt to some US weapons sales to allies.

Trump said the US was building up its own stockpiles of munitions amid concerns over depleted supplies, particularly as the war with Iran continues.

“We are taking them for ourselves, the USA, rather than selling them to others, but the sales to Allies will soon again begin,” Trump said in a social media post.

He also said Europe should have paid for weapons provided to Ukraine and Nato, blaming the administration of his predecessor Joe Biden.

“Hundreds of Billions of Dollars was given to Ukraine and Nato, free of charge, that Europe would have paid for – If they were only asked, but we will be asking for that money, though somewhat belatedly!” Trump wrote.

The US and Nato last year established the Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List initiative, under which European countries pay for US-made weapons supplied to Ukraine.

Trump’s comments came after Reuters reported last month that the US Army had used much of its stockpile of highly accurate long-range missiles during the Iran war, raising concerns over US military readiness.

The wars in Ukraine and Iran are also creating economic and political pressure on the Trump administration, with rising energy prices a growing concern.

## Kyiv sees Europe's appetite for talks on frozen Russian assets - Reuters

Ukraine sees growing appetite among European allies for a serious discussion to be renewed on the use of Russian frozen assets, a senior government official has told the Reuters news agency.

The official said some allies wanted to avoid using frozen assets to keep them as a negotiating chip for future talks.

Kyiv thinks this is unnecessary and wants access to the funds now, they added.

## Finland investigating surveillance drone near south coast

Finland has said it is investigating whether a surveillance drone on the shore of its south coast has violated the Nordic country’s territory.

An investigation launched by the Border Guard authority seeks to determine where, when and how the aircraft entered Finnish territory and how it possibly crossed Finland's border.

Preliminary examination suggests the aircraft is a Geoscan 701 type drone, which is used by both state and civilian actors.

European country’s have repeatedly alleged drone incursions related to the Russia-Ukraine war in the past year, but the Border Guard said it could not confirm the drone's origin, operator or purpose at this stage.

The drone was intended for surveying and mapping and did not pose a danger to the public, the defence ministry said on Wednesday.
