LOCKHEED Martin Space UK has signed a long-term research deal with Northumbria University.

The Master Research Agreement (MRA) will drive space innovation, skills development and industry growth, marking the latest step in a wider strategic partnership between the two organisations.

The MRA follows Lockheed Martin’s £15 million investment in the North East Space Skills and Technology Centre (NESST), with £7.5 million earmarked for research and development activity at the university over the next decade.

Robert Lightfoot, president of Lockheed Martin Space, said: "This agreement is another example of Lockheed Martin delivering on its long-term commitment to the North East and to the future of the UK's space sector.

"By combining Northumbria University's outstanding research expertise with Lockheed Martin's global experience and technology leadership, we can accelerate innovation in critical space capabilities while creating opportunities for skills development, collaboration and economic growth."

NESST is being developed in partnership with Lockheed Martin UK, Northumbria University and the UK Space Agency.

It is expected to open later this year and achieve full operating capability in early 2027.

The centre will support regional economic development by connecting academia, industry and government to advance space technology, workforce training and investment in the North East.

The collaborative research programme will focus initially on three key areas: space-based optical communications, space-based energy and space weather and instrumentation.

The MRA also builds on Lockheed Martin’s wider engagement in the region, including its Space Camp initiative, which has encouraged young people in the North East to explore careers in STEM for the past four years.

In 2025, Northumbria University hosted a Space Camp for 60 Year 6 and Year 12 students and will host another camp in October 2026.

Earlier this year, a Memorandum of Understanding was signed between Lockheed Martin UK and North East mayor Kim McGuinness to support coordinated investment, skills development and supply chain growth in the region.

Professor Andy Long, vice-chancellor and chief executive of Northumbria University, said: "This agreement represents a significant step forward in our partnership with Lockheed Martin and reflects Northumbria's growing reputation as a leading university for research, innovation and enterprise.

"Through NESST and our wider collaboration, we are creating opportunities for researchers, students and industry to work together on technologies that will help shape the future of space exploration, communications and resilience."
