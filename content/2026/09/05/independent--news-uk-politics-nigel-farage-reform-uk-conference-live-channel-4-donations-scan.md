Nigel Farage is battling to contain a fresh scandal into donations after footage appeared to show senior Reform figures discussing how to disguise donations from foreign sources.

Senior Reform officials James Orr and Dan Jukes have stepped down and the party launched an investigation after the report was broadcast by Channel 4 News.

Mr Farage claimed the allegations broadcast by Channel 4 that his party breached political donation rules have been made because “they’re scared of us”.

The allegations appear to have caused splits at the top of the party. Senior Reform figures have given conflicting accounts of whether the suspensions are permanent, or temporary and pending an investigation.

Zia Yusuf, the party’s home affairs spokesperson, has distanced himself from the two suspended men, telling reporters of the investigation: “It’s just not something I am personally involved in.”

*The Times* reports that these claims are expected to widen the police investigation which is examining donations worth £500,000 made to the party.

## Reform's downbeat conference winds down

Reform's downbeat conference feels much more subdued today as the event winds down after a chaotic few days of protesters, a media storm around Nigel Farage's closest aides and questions over whether the party is even remotely ready for government.

With just a few main stage speeches remaining, many journalists and lobbyists have already left.

Meanwhile, only around half of the fringe stages are in use this morning, with many of the smaller events having taken place yesterday.

Mr Farage will be hoping this afternoon's main stage speeches - given by both Suella Braverman and Malcolm Offord - are upbeat enough to make up for what has been a difficult conference and lift the mood as Reform attempts to draw a line under the chaos and damning allegations that have marred the party's leadership for months.

## More protesters removed by security

 More protesters have been removed by security from the NEC as Reform's conference enters its final day.

A woman was escorted out of the building and sent to join other protesters who were holding signs and chanting "fascist scum, off our streets".

It comes after Nigel Farage's main stage speech was interrupted by four protesters last night and delayed for more than an hour as a result of security concerns.

## Yusef condemns hair-pulling incident

Zia Yusuf has condemned Reform UK members who pulled the hair of a heckler during Nigel Farage’s speech on Friday.

Asked whether he condemned the actions of some members who appeared in footage pulling the woman’s hair and grabbing her head, the party’s home affairs spokesman told a conference fringe event: “On the question of whether I condemn people punching people, yes I condemn people punching people.”

## Police seek protester after footage shows Reform audience pulling hair

West Midlands Police have asked a protester who was thrown out of the Reform UK conference to come forward after footage showed audience members pulling her hair and grabbing her head.

The force said it had not received a direct complaint but asked the woman to make contact so officers can carry out further investigations.

Demonstrators were thrown out of the main conference hall four times at the National Exhibition Centre in Birmingham, where Reform UK are holding their annual get-together.

They interrupted Nigel Farage’s keynote speech, shortly after his deputy Richard Tice had a banner displayed during his own address referencing the scandal around a donation to Mr Farage.

The Reform UK leader laughed during the disruption and said: “We’ve got a screamer. Oh dear.”

Addressing the conference hall, the Reform leader said, “that poor young girl, she looked like she was going to do herself an injury”, before adding, “boring, boring, boring”.

A statement from West Midlands Police said: “We’re aware of a video circulating from the Reform UK conference showing an incident with a protester.

“We haven’t received a complaint but would ask the woman involved to come forward and contact us so we can carry out enquiries.

“Anyone with any information should contact us on 101 quoting log number 924 of 5 September 2026.”

Activist group Climate Resistance claimed responsibility for the protest.

In a comment they said they were “horrified at the violence” from Reform conference audience members.

The group also said the protester is considering whether to come forward to police.

The end of the first main day of the Reform conference saw a series of protests disrupting speeches.

## Robert Jenrick to promise tax cut amid row over Reform donations

Robert Jenrick will pledge to raise the income tax threshold as Reform UK attempts to move on from allegations about its finances.

The party’s Treasury spokesman is expected to tell the final day of the Reform conference in Birmingham that he would increase the tax-free personal allowance to £15,000 within the first 100 days of taking office.

The threshold has been set at £12,570 since 2021 and is due to remain there until 2031.

Reform said the plan would save the average taxpayer £500 a year and take 2.9 million people out of income tax altogether, at a cost of £17.7 billion in its first year, rising to £21 billion five years later.

The party has previously said it wants to increase the personal allowance to £20,000, with Mr Jenrick expected to say he will approach the figure “step-by-step” when it is affordable.

Mr Jenrick will also use his speech to set out some £80 billion in spending cuts, primarily through slashing the welfare budget.

He is expected to say: “The hard choice for Burnham and for us is exactly the same. It’s either welfare waste, foreign aid and migrants — or the British worker. We choose the worker.”
