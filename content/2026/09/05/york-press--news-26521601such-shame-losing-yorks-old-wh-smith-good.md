**'Such a shame we are losing York's old WH Smith for good'**

IT’S a shame that TG Jones (WH Smith) is going from Coney Street.

I have many great memories of the WH Smith – scribbling your name as well as rude words in the writing pad to test pens.

Looking at the endless array of colourful greeting cards and wrapping papers – especially at Christmas.

Getting inspired to go on holiday by the travel books.

Being stared at by people for flicking through the magazines and newspapers and saying under their breath "it’s not a library".

  **Gabriel Dahmani-Rodwell,**

  *Tennent Road,*

  *Acomb,*

  *York*

  **Concerns over the loss of York's main Post Office**

AT the time of writing, I'm unaware (and please correct me if I'm wrong) that York citizens will have a hub to post large items.

My local Post Office on Micklegate closed, without warning, a few months ago.

Many local shops sell books of stamps, even singularly if you're fortunate, which is all very good for postcards (which I obviously don't send) or greetings cards, to deposit in a post box.

I understood York to be a city, and not a hamlet tucked away under a glowering sky, waiting for the mail coach to pass by every full moon.

I look forward to hear how things progress. In the meantime I'll be encouraging the much maligned pigeons that visit my balcony to bulk up and carry some parcels for me.

  *Ivan Wootton,*

  *Phoenix Boulevard,*

  *York*

  **Burnham's plans are not in the spirit of true devolution**

ANDY Burnham has emphasised in Parliament that he wants to increase the pace and extent of devolution, passing the right to make decisions closer to the community.

Passing power from central government to regional and local councils is something that Liberal Democrats have always supported.

At the same time, he has announced that regional Mayors, such as the Mayor of the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority, are to be given the power to overrule some planning decisions made by local councils.

This applies to housing developments over 150 dwellings, commercial developments over 15000 square metres and buildings 30m or higher. By doing this, Burnham is moving power from local level to regional level, the exact opposite of devolution.

Local councils have planning officers and planning lawyers who are experienced in advising councillors.

All councillors have to undergo extensive planning training before they are able to sit on a planning committee. I have served on a planning committee for 17 years and I certainly don't know everything, so professional advice is crucial.

The Combined Authority will need to recruit equivalent officers, adding to the cost of the Combined Authority and a subsequent increase in the precept it imposes on council tax payers.

The Mayor will have to undertake extensive planning training to enable him to make informed decisions as he has never served on a planning committee.

This is a very flawed idea, going against the very principles of devolution. Andy Burnham should abandon it.

  *Tony Fisher,*

  *Liberal Democrat councillor for Strensall ward and vice-Chair of the City of York Council Planning Committee*
