Officers are currently “engaging” with protesters clad in balaclavas blocking access to the Port of Dover, Kent Police has said.

In a brief statement, the force said: “We are aware of a protest currently taking place at the Port of Dover. Officers are in attendance and engaging with protesters.”

Videos circulating online show several protesters, dressed in black and wearing balaclavas, gathered at the port. They are milling around roads and roundabouts near the major port, forcing vehicles to a halt.

In a statement on X, the port said: “The Port of Dover can confirm that there is an ongoing public order incident happening around the town of Dover. This is affecting all routes in and out the port and the town.

“We are in contact with Kent Police to understand the situation. This situation is impacting our local community and our customers, so of course we are hoping for a swift resolution.”

The demonstration is being streamed on social media.*The Telegraph* reports that those in attendance were heard saying they were part of the hard-right group Patriot Platform.

They said the blockade is planned for three days, the newspaper adds.

Dover and Deal’s Labour MP Mike Tapp wrote on Facebook that he was in contact with the police regarding the situation.

He said: “I am fed up with idiots coming to Dover to cause disruption.

“I bet if you asked any one of those ‘protesters’ they wouldn’t know small boat crossing are down by 40 per cent and we are working hard to fix the mess.

“Go home, enjoy your weekend/get a job.”

Councillor Chris Vinson, leader of the Conservative group and member for Walmer on Dover District Council said the protest appears to be “illegal migration-related”, but said that the demonstrators do not appear to have placards or banners.

He said: “Overnight, clearly what appears to be several hundred masked men wearing black clothing and balaclavas seem to have co-ordinated and loaded into rental vans and then descended on roads leading in and out of Dover with the intent to block them, and that’s what they’ve successfully done, and it’s brought the town to a standstill.

“There are stacks of people who are stuck in their cars or in their workplaces as a result.”

He added: “I’m not aware that there’s been any violence, although clearly, having the path of your vehicle blocked by men wearing black paramilitary gear and balaclavas is extremely intimidating, especially when it’s not clear why they’re there and the police weren’t aware, and so haven’t been able to warn people in advance and make sure that they are made to feel safe.

“I know people who have worked night shifts in the port who are being prevented from leaving for fears for their safety.

“Women who are in cars feeling intimidated, people stuck in traffic – who obviously are without toilet facilities – queues way back on the A20 and the other approach roads into Dover.”

Access to the port had been partially restored via the M2/A2 by 10.30am, but access via the M20/A20 remained blocked, P&O Ferries said in an update on X.

National Highways said the A20 east between the A256 (York Street) and the A2 (Jubilee Way) remains closed.

While the west is “mostly clear”, the road remains blocked from Limekiln roundabout, it added. Travellers can expect 25 minute-delays on approach to the roundabout, with around one mile of congestion.

Shipping company DFDS said: “We are aware of the current traffic disruption affecting access to the port.

“Rest assured we will accommodate you onto the next departure once you arrive.”

P&O Ferries said its sailings are “still going ahead as scheduled”, adding that those who miss their departure will be offered the next available crossing.

Rail operator Southeastern reshared a Port of Dover travel update on X, adding: “If you’re delayed into the UK and miss your booked train, you can use the next available at no extra cost.”
