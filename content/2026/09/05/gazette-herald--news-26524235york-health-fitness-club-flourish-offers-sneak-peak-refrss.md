A NEW state-of-the-art health and fitness club has offered a sneak peak inside the historic York building it has taken over ahead of its opening.

The tour around Flourish, based at the Grade II-listed Georgian building The White House, in Clifton, comes ahead of its opening on Monday, September 7.

Vanessa Warn, one of the founders of the club, said it aimed to offer facilities typically only available in London up north but at prices affordable to all.

It comes after the company teased the club’s opening on social media in August, after taking over the building in June.

Facilities at the club include a gym, yoga studio, reformer pilates room, café and children’s creche.

A sauna room features an ice bath and shower, with charcoal binders offered to help with the loss of toxins.

  An Ultra Telsa seat in the health and wellbeing club Flourish, in Clifton, York 

*(Image: LDRS)*

A GP is set to be based in a doctor’s room two days a week, with women’s days offered once a week.

An Ultra Telsa chair for men who have had prostate cancer or women who have pelvic flooring issues treating them with shockwaves is also available.

The club is set to offer memberships and people can also come on a one-off basis, with prices yet to be confirmed.

  The reformer pilates room at the Flourish health and wellbeing club, in Clifton, York 

*(Image: LDRS)*

Sessions are set to be offered to both men and women and to people of all fitness levels.

Ms Warn said the club had already had around 200 sign ups, 1,200 followers online and a large amount of questions from people interested in visiting.

She added her and fellow club founders Claire Riddell and Stacey Dix set out to provide an offering that they had struggled to find outside the capital.

Ms Warn said: “I’ve been working with Claire for nine years, she was my personal trainer, and my sister Stacey joined us about six years ago.

  The gym in the Flourish health and wellbeing club, in Clifton, York 

*(Image: LDRS)*

“We used to take trips down to London together for health and wellbeing treatments and to facilities we couldn’t find in York, so we said why don’t we do something here?

“We looked at a couple of different places, I live in Bootham and we came to look around this building and it fit perfectly with what we wanted to do.

“This is a beautiful historic building that looks gorgeous.

“We’ve got a physiotherapist who worked for the men’s and women’s England football teams.


“People have asked about our reformer facilities because they’ve been referred by their GP.

“We have a café that’s going to be all about health, serving delicious food that’s all locally sourced.

“Our offer won’t be prohibitively expensive , our membership’s an investment that we hope will save people money because they don’t have to spend more on lots of different individual costs.

“I hope this will be a place where people feel welcome and see that it’s affordable to all.”
