A biopolymers company has set out plans for growth after securing commercial agreements and international interest in its plant-based plastics.

Chestnut Biopolymers, based in Darlington, has transitioned from proving its technology to building a commercial business, with applications now being developed across healthcare, fashion, agriculture, and forestry.

Aulden Dunipace, commercial director at Chestnut Biopolymers, said: "When we started this journey, the key question was whether we could create a majority plant-based material capable of performing in applications that have traditionally relied on fossil plastics.

"Today, we believe we have increasingly answered that question.

"The focus now is on how quickly we can convert growing commercial interest into large-scale production and deployment."

The company has signed a three-year deal in the fashion sector for products such as coat hangers, size markers, and mannequin applications.

In healthcare, it is developing reusable alternatives to single-use pulp items, with ongoing discussions in NHS supply chains.

In agriculture and forestry, its materials are being considered for tree guards, cable ties, and plant fastenings.

Further interest is emerging in cosmetics, IT and electrical products, sports media, and industrial fastenings.

Chestnut has established a formulation and testing facility in Darlington and says its materials have now been processed at scale via injection moulding, sheet extrusion, and blow moulding.

The company has also developed specialist conductive applications.

It is now seeing a shift from research and development to repeat orders and commercial agreements, with annual demand enquiries exceeding 40,000 tonnes across various markets.

Mr Dunipace said: "What has become clear is that we are not dependent on a single product or a single market.

"We are seeing opportunities emerge across multiple sectors where conventional plastics perform well during use but create long-term environmental challenges afterwards."

To support its next phase of growth, Chestnut Biopolymers plans to launch a fundraising campaign on Crowdcube.
