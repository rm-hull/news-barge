A man who murdered his 41-year-old stepmother by strangling her with her own headscarf will not face a further trial for downloading indecent images of children.

Back in March 23-year-old Abdul Sami was jailed for life, with a minimum term of 14 years and 91 days, after he was found guilty of murdering Rizwana Kousar at their family home in Kensington Street, Girlington, Bradford, in August 2023.

On Friday, Sami appeared via video link to HMP Leeds for a further Bradford Crown Court hearing in relation to three allegations of making indecent images of children.

The charges also dated back to August 2023 and alleged that Sami was in possession of indecent images in Categories A, B and C.

Prosecutor John Batchelor told the Recorder of Bradford Judge Jonathan Rose that the Crown were now offering no evidence against Sami on those charges after careful consideration of the evidence.

Judge Rose directed that not guilty verdicts should be recorded on all three charges and the trial due to take place could now be vacated.

At Sami’s sentencing hearing back in March the Honourable Mr Justice Turner explained that it would be for the Parole Board to decide if it was safe to release Sami after he had served his minimum term in prison.

Ms Kousar died in August 2023 a few days after she was found unconscious at the family home she shared with her husband and his son Sami.

Sami went on trial at Bradford Crown Court at the end of February after he denied murdering his stepmother, but the jury took just under three and a half hours to find him guilty and return a unanimous verdict.

Prosecutor Kama Melly KC described at the start of the trial how just before midday on August 15, 2023 Sami called 999 for an ambulance and reported that he had found his stepmother “half in and half out” of a bath with her head submerged in the water.

He was given directions to remove her and begin CPR, but Ms Melly said when paramedics arrived at the house one of them noticed a red mark across the front of the woman’s neck.

The court heard that the defendant’s stepmother never regained consciousness and was pronounced dead in hospital two days later.

Sami, of Mannheim Road, Bradford, denied the murder charge and stated that any mark to her neck had been caused by him removing her traditional headscarf before attempting CPR.

Miss Melly said Sami’s mobile phone was later examined by police and he was found to have been visiting “inheritance” websites at the end of May 2023.

He also visited a site in relation to the question “does a killer inherit from their victim’s estate”.

Miss Melly said he also visited a site for “inheritance for stepchildren” and on the day of the alleged attack another site asking “what is the chance of survival if the brain is starved of oxygen”.

Sami told police that some of the searches were in relation to his biological mother who had passed away in 2019 and others had been the result of seeing things on television.

Ms Melly also outlined detailed pathological evidence relating to injuries to the deceased’s neck and tiny haemorrhages consistent with 10–30 seconds of “forceful uninterrupted pressure” to the neck.

In a victim impact statement read by prosecutor John Batchelor Ms Kousar was described by her sister-in-law as being dearly loved by all her family and friends.

“That she is gone is unimaginable. Her loss has impacted us every day and will do so forever,” she said.

She said to think of Rizwana’s final moments was horrific and Sami had lied over and over to try to cover up his disgusting actions.

She said Sami had tried to make it look like an accident and had shown no remorse.

“She was pure of heart and had such a positive outlook on life. Always trying to see the best in people.

“She had so much to live for. You took it all away.”
