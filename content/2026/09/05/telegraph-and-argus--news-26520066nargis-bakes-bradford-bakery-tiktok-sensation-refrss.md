A BRADFORD baker whose cakes attract customers from across the UK has spoken about transforming a maternity leave hobby into a thriving business which is watched by hundreds of thousands of people on TikTok.

Nargis Shaheen, owner of Nargis Bakes on Great Horton Road, said she never expected her passion for baking to become a full-time career after spending two decades working in nursing.

The West Bowling entrepreneur opened her bakery in August 2024 and has since gained 44,000 followers on TikTok, with her most popular video attracting 421,000 views.

Speaking to the T&A, Ms Shaheen said baking was never part of her original career plans.

## From nursing to baking

"If you'd told me years ago that I'd end up owning a bakery, I would have laughed because I had no knowledge, no experience and no real interest in baking at the time," she said.

"My profession was nursing and I spent 20 years working in nursing.

"I was always more of a cook than a baker.

"The whole thing really started when I was on maternity leave with my daughter, who is now 10 years old.


"I tasted a lovely chocolate cake made by a friend and thought, 'I wonder if I could make something like that'.

"I made my own version at home and was so proud of what I'd created.

"Looking back at pictures of it now, I laugh because it was nothing like what I produce today, but at the time it sparked something in me.

"From there it just grew.

"I found myself constantly watching videos on Instagram and YouTube, researching recipes, looking at what other bakers were creating and teaching myself new techniques.

"It became a real passion."

## 'I became the person expected to bring something sweet'

As one of nine siblings, Ms Shaheen said she had plenty of opportunities to test her creations on family members.

"I come from a very large family. I'm one of nine children.

"There always seemed to be birthdays and celebrations happening.

"My mum's house was always the place where everybody gathered, and I became the person expected to bring something sweet.

"Every time I made something new, the family loved it and encouraged me to keep going.

"Slowly but surely people started asking me to make cakes for other occasions, and before I knew it I had registered a business from home.

"What started off as a hobby turned into something much bigger."

## TikTok success fuels growth

Ms Shaheen credits much of the bakery's growth to social media, particularly TikTok, where she regularly shares videos of her creations and behind-the-scenes footage from the bakery.

"The response on social media has honestly been unbelievable and overwhelming," she said.

"When I opened, I thought I would have a small display and a modest bakery where people from the local area would come in and buy cakes.

"I never imagined people would travel from all over the country to visit us.

"In the early days I was working crazy hours.

"There were times when I was here 18 hours a day and surviving on just a few hours of sleep. People were seeing all of that on my live videos.

"They could see the hard work, the graft and the determination that was going into making the bakery successful.

## Bringing viral desserts to Bradford

Part of the bakery's popularity has come from adapting social media trends and transforming them into fresh, handmade desserts.

Among its most successful products have been Crunch Cakes and other viral creations inspired by online trends.

However, Ms Shaheen said quality remains at the heart of everything she makes.

"One thing I've always been very proud of is that I've never taken shortcuts," she said.

"Since the day I opened, everything has been made fresh in-house from scratch. I've never relied on cake mixes or quick fixes because that's not what I want my business to be about.

"A lot of desserts become popular on TikTok and social media, and people love seeing us recreate them, but I always want to put my own stamp on things.

"I want customers to enjoy something that tastes homemade and special rather than something mass-produced."

She is currently developing new products, including her own take on the banana pudding made famous by New York's Magnolia Bakery.
