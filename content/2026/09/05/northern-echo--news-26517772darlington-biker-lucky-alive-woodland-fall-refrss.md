A mountain-biking teenager was told he was "very lucky" to be alive after plunging around seven feet from his bike in woodland.

Darlington's Bradley Bowes, 16, suffered facial injuries and a broken nose when his mountain-bike brakes failed as he rode with two friends in Errington Woods, near New Marske.

His mum, Claire Wilson, 55, said paramedics and mountain rescuers told the family Bradley might not have survived without his helmet.

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She said: "Both the paramedics and mountain rescue said if he did not have that helmet on, he probably wouldn't be here."

Bradley, who has just started studying public services at Darlington College and hopes to become a firefighter, was injured in the crash on August 24.

He fell around seven feet, landing face down.

  A mountain biker was rescued after falling from height and suffering facial injuries in Errington Woods woodland. 

*(Image: CLEVELAND MOUNTAIN RESCUE TEAM)*

Claire said: "He said he just let go of the bike and thought, ‘that's it,’ then landed face down.

"His head took the brunt of the fall, but he had the helmet on. He went to hospital with a broken nose, cuts and bruises, then was discharged a few hours later."

Claire said she drove from Darlington to the woods after Bradley’s panicked call but could not reach him because of her mobility problems.

She said: "He called me, so I drove from Darlington. But when I got to the woods, I couldn't reach him because my mobility isn't very good.

"I didn't know what to expect, but when they got him out of the woods I spoke to him before he was taken to James Cook hospital."

  Bradley Bowes, 16, suffered facial injuries and a broken nose after he fell off his bike in Errington Woods 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Claire said the incident had changed Bradley’s attitude to wearing a helmet.

She hopes Bradley’s experience will persuade other young cyclists to wear helmets.

Claire said: "I wanted to tell him I told you so after all the time I spent begging him to wear a helmet, but I would never. I cannot stress enough to other kids and parents how important helmets are, and we hope if one reads this and thinks twice before going out without a helmet, that's one less child who gets hurt.

"It can be really dangerous, and until something like this happens to you, it puts everything into perspective.

"Bradley was a very lucky boy."

  Bradley Bowes, 16, suffered facial injuries and a broken nose after he fell off his bike in Errington Woods 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Claire said the family had had problems with the bike since buying it earlier this year.

She said: "It had already been to the garage over the brakes because we kept saying they weren't right, and we have had to send it for repairs again after the accident.

"We got the bike back the other day and he put his helmet on and rode it to college, so I don't think it's put him off cycling."

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Cleveland Mountain Rescue Team confirmed they were called by paramedics at around 7.25pm to the crash.

Sixteen mountain rescue team members took part in the rescue, which lasted just under three hours.

The team drove its Land Rovers into the woods, taking one as close to Bradley as possible.

The North East Ambulance Service (NEAS) treated Bradley and secured him in a vacuum mattress before he was wheeled on a stretcher to the Land Rover.

He was driven back to the waiting ambulance and taken to James Cook University Hospital for treatment.
