Redcar and Cleveland Council has rejected plans by BT for so-called ‘street hubs’ in Redcar High Street and Eston High Street.

The company sought planning permission from the local planning authority for the units, as well as for advertising consent for internally illuminated LCD displays at the two locations, but this too was turned down.

Street hubs have replaced traditional phone boxes in some areas, allowing people access to free services such as wi-fi and calls.

Electronic advertising is also used in some instances to display public messaging.

Cleveland Police had objected to the plans, fearing the hubs could be utilised for criminal activity, specifically drug dealing, citing evidence from other areas.

A representative from the force wrote in his response to a consultation: “I am aware that colleagues around the country have experienced issues where these units are currently installed and active.

“Both of the proposed sites are within areas that suffer from anti-social behaviour, in fact having liaised with the Neighbourhood Policing Team the proposed site in Redcar is a hotspot for such.”

The council’s development engineers said the Redcar proposal could obscure a bus stop and prove a distraction for passing motorists.

Meanwhile, a council planning officer said: “The proposal is located within the designated centre where there is an existing range of street furniture including railings, light columns, bins and seating.

“The hubs are not considered to be a requirement of the location unlike the existing street furniture and would result in a cluttered appearance within the section of High Street where they are located.”

The Eston street hub was proposed outside the Miners Arms pub, close to an existing 5G mast installation.

The council concluded that in this instance, as well as potential crime and anti-social behaviour, there could be a detrimental effect on road network users and also an adverse impact on the character and appearance of the area.

Decision notices issued in respect of the applications said the applicant/agent had been advised of the concerns, but no amendments were submitted.

BT did not respond to a request from the Local Democracy Reporting Service for a comment, but a supporting planning statement received by the council said: “Overall BT Street Hubs will help future proof the high street making them smarter, safer, and more sustainable through their adaptable design and function.

“The proposed BT Street Hub structures are of a high quality, accessible design that would be a significant improvement when compared to the existing payphone kiosks.”
