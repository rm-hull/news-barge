**Why The Old Siam is one of York's best Thai restaurants. Review by MAXINE GORDON**

IT'S been many, many Prime Ministers since I visited The Old Siam restaurant at the top of Micklegate, the historic royal gateway into York.

So long ago, in fact, that the decor has completely changed, and – according to the fabulous and chatty waiting staff (more of which later) – the folks in charge too (although still Thai, we were assured).

My memories – hazy I will admit – seem to recall a dark interior with heavy, ornate wooden furniture which would not look out of place in a Thai version of Antiques Roadshow.

One thing I do remember, however, is that the food was fabulous. I don't really have an excuse for the long gap in returning, save for The Old Siam being on the other side of town from where I live, and York having so many newer restaurants which deserve a try (and a review).

The Old Siam mark II still evokes a sense of the East with its rich decor and accents of gold, but the tables and chairs are more in keeping with a traditional western restaurant (which is a shame).

Thankfully the same disappointment cannot be levelled at the menu, which is a nicely compact (hurrah!) gate-folded affair with dishes printed across the front and back, and packed with tantalising Thai dishes.

  The Old Siam in Micklegate, York. Photo - NQ


I'll own up now that Thai food is one of my favourites. I've never been to Thailand so can only judge by the restaurants I have visited. But ever since I had my first taste of Thai food as a teenager in Edinburgh I was won over.

Back then, it seemed so new and exotic. It was the 1980s and the most exciting eating out experience – even for someone living in the capital of Scotland – was a beef and bean quesadilla at Viva Mexico, or a chicken tikka masala at my parent's favourite local Indian restaurant.

Thai food brought flavours and ingredients I'd never tried before, combining the creamy, sweetness of coconut milk, with the citrus-sy notes of lime, lemongrass, and coriander, and the warmth of chillies. Thai food really delivered a holy culinary trinity of sweet, sour, and heat. I was a convert.

And it was the heady aroma of that unique combination that filled the air as we walked into The Old Siam on a Saturday night in mid-August (having just secured a table by telephone some 30 minutes earlier).

It set my taste buds salivating, and I couldn't wait to get going!

The menu at The Old Siam may be compact, but not at the expense of variety and interest.

Spilt into sections: 'Jub-Jib' listed an impressive 19 small plates/starters, from slow-braised pork rib (£11.95) to prawns and squid cooked in garlic (£11.50), and tempura garden veggies with a dipping sauce (£9.50).

We chose the duck roll (£9.95) and Kanom Jeeb, pork and prawn dumplings (£11.50).

  Duck rolls at The Old Siam in Micklegate, York. Photo - NQ


With our drinks – a large Singha (£8.95 for 630ml) and a Coke Zero (£3.50) we enjoyed a bowl of fresh and crispy, yet melt-in-the-mouth, prawn crackers with sweet chilli sauce on the side (£5.50).

Our starters came soon afterwards – beautifully presented on pretty dishes. Thoughtfully, our waiter brought us small plates so we could share.

We dived into the duck rolls first: two crispy rolls cut in two, so four pieces in all, with a small serving of rich, sweet and sticky hoisin sauce.

If you come to The Old Siam, you have to order these. They were a sensation and hit all the right notes. The duck was soft and full of earthy sweetness, enhanced by ribbons of soft-cooked leeks, all wrapped in a wafer-thin crisp.

In contrast, the dumplings were meaty and more subtle in flavour: but that was a good thing because they acted as a sponge for the out-of-this-world sweet soy sauce that came with it. The dip was so good we asked our waiter for the recipe! Obligingly, he came back with a note from the chef, revealing it was a mix of sweet and dark soy sauce, vinegar, sugar and salt. The exact quantities were not forthcoming, so I might have to experiment at home.

  Dumplings at The Old Siam in Micklegate, York. Photo - NQ


For our mains, you can pick from a choice of 12 options under 'Old Siam's Exclusive' (read: chef's favourites) which included massaman lamb (£23.50), the Chiangmai Signature coconut curry with egg noodles, crispy prawn tempura, pickled veg and a boiled egg (£20.95), as well as two seabass dishes (one steamed, one crispy).

Four dishes are listed under 'House Pairings: Experience Like A Local', from which I chose the jumbo king prawn pad thai (£18.95).

Over the page there is a 'mix and match' section, where you can freestyle and assemble your own dish from an array of protein options, cooked either in a wok or in curry, completed by a choice of rice or noodles.

My dining partner settled on a chicken panang curry (£14.95) with a side of coconut rice (£4).

  Panang curry at The Old Siam in Micklegate, York. Photo - NQ


Again, dishes looked appetising. They lived up to their presentation, and were a match in quality and taste for our starters.

I've had pad thai many times but this was my favourite.

Essentially a nest of soft noodles and beansprouts with pieces of egg – cooked in a wok – and topped with the likes of chicken or tofu, as well as crushed peanuts, pad thai sounds so simple, but is easy to disappoint.

It can be too chewy, or too sticky, or lacking in flavour, or congealed, or just strike the wrong balance between all the ingredients.

But at The Old Siam – as Goldilocks might have discovered if she'd been here – it was just right.

The portion might have looked on the small side, but it was perfect to suit my appetite (especially after my starters).

There were just three jumbo prawns, but they were large, and when cut into pieces and scattered in with the jumble of silky noodles, beansprouts, carrot, egg, and peanuts, I found I had a piece in almost every mouthful.

  Prawn pad thai at The Old Siam in York


My partner – who is another fan of pad thai – snuck a couple of forkfuls and concurred it was about the best he'd tasted too.

Panang curry is another favourite of ours (not surprising perhaps given its base is a red-curry sauce packed with lemongrass and coriander as well as lime and coconut). This was a winner, and I liked its heat profile too (listed as just one chilli, with three being as hot as you could go), which left my mouth warmed and alert rather than wanting to reach for a fire extinguisher (or another Coke Zero).

The chicken pieces were perfectly cooked and the coconut rice was delicious; slightly sticky and gently delivering a sweetness absent from the plain boiled variety.

Quite full, we asked to see the dessert menu (it wasn't offered). This consisted of a choice of home-made ice-creams, as well as the obligatory banana and pineapple fritters. A warm cookie and a brownie – both with ice-cream – are offered too, with prices from £6.95 to £8.50.

We shared a two-scoop dish of ice-cream (£6.95): one vanilla, one coconut. The vanilla was a bit chewy and a bit bland, the coconut one had all the flavour but had a gritty, slushy texture.

  Ice -cream at The Old Siam in Micklegate, York. Photo - NQ


I was going to say it was the only downside of the night.

But there was one other matter. The Old Siam is a noisy old place. Interiors without soft furnishings and wall and window coverings leave them echoey and we did struggle to hear ourselves across the table. As my partner observed: "If you sell beer and have got a big glass window, it is going to be noisy".

On clearing our half-eaten dessert, our waiter quizzed us. We told the truth, and he encouraged us next time to try some of the more unusual flavours, including the lemony Yuzu or Thai Tea.

He also filled us in on the history of the restaurant and how it had switched hands a few years ago but was still going strong, despite the challenges in hospitality.

Our bill came to £84.25 – which is a fair amount. The Old Siam is not the cheapest place in town, but if value rests on quality, then it has to be fair. Also, we could easily have done without the prawn crackers and ice-cream which would have taken the bill closer to £70.

No service charge was added, just a line reading: "Tips keep us smiling".

I smiled as I added on ten per cent at the payment point: happy to do so given the quality of the food and service we'd enjoyed.
