Tall Trees in Yarm was once one of the biggest names in North East nightlife, attracting thousands of clubbers as well as an extraordinary list of famous performers.

During its heyday, the venue on Green Lane became known far beyond Teesside, with its nightclub Macmillans, later widely known as Club M, hosting pop stars and some of the biggest DJs in dance music.

Names associated with Tall Trees over the years included Girls Aloud, Chesney Hawkes, Pete Tong and Dave Pearce, while former Prime Minister John Major was among the well-known guests to visit the wider venue.

But the list of musicians and DJs who appeared there extended much further.

*(Image: ARCHIVE)*

By the mid-1990s, Tall Trees had already established itself on Britain's clubbing map.

On October 28, 1995, BBC Radio 1's legendary Essential Mix was broadcast live from Tall Trees, featuring Pete Tong, John Digweed and Tall Trees resident Paul Bleasdale.

At a time when major clubs such as Cream in Liverpool and The Arches in Glasgow were helping define British dance culture, having Radio 1 broadcast from Yarm underlined just how highly Tall Trees was regarded.

Pete Tong would remain one of the biggest names associated with the venue.

The Radio 1 presenter and DJ was among a generation of performers who helped turn club nights into major events, and Tall Trees became one of the places where North East audiences could see nationally and internationally recognised DJs without travelling to cities such as Manchester or London. Carl Cox was another.

  Yarm's Tall Trees 

*(Image: ARCHIVE)*

The internationally renowned DJ appeared at Tall Trees during its famous Retro nights, alongside Pete Tong and Boy George, according to promoter Paul Taylor's later recollections of the era.

Retro became particularly synonymous with Tall Trees and drew clubbers from well beyond Teesside.

Paul Taylor, who masterminded the nights, later described a period when some of dance music's best-known figures were playing Saturday nights at the Yarm club.

Tall Trees was not limited to dance music, however.

Girls Aloud were among the pop acts who performed at the venue.

The group, which included Newcastle-born Cheryl Tweedy, became one of Britain's biggest girl bands after being formed on Popstars: The Rivals in 2002.

Chesney Hawkes, best known for his number-one hit The One and Only, was another performer to appear at Tall Trees.

As dance music changed during the 2000s, Tall Trees continued attracting major names.

The venue became closely associated with Goodgreef, one of the North's best-known trance and hard-dance brands.

A Goodgreef event in September 2007 brought Lisa Lashes, Kutski and Showtek to Club M, alongside a large supporting line-up.

DJ Mag later described Tall Trees as Goodgreef's "spiritual home".

In February 2012, one of the venue's major events was headlined by trance superstar Paul Oakenfold.

He was joined by Aly & Fila, Orjan Nilsen, Audrey Gallagher and Jordan Suckley for a Goodgreef Winter White Party.

Oakenfold had already established himself as one of the world's best-known DJs after playing clubs and festivals internationally and working with some of the biggest names in music.

Tall Trees was also able to attract acts who were filling considerably larger venues elsewhere.

In August 2010, deadmau5 appeared at the Yarm nightclub as part of a Bank Holiday event.

Promotional material at the time described it as a rare opportunity to see the Canadian electronic music star playing inside a UK nightclub while much of his schedule consisted of arenas and festivals.

Other internationally recognised DJs who appeared at Tall Trees during its later years included Dubfire, Nic Fanciulli and Joris Voorn.

For people who grew up going out on Teesside during the 1990s and 2000s, however, the performers were only part of the Tall Trees experience.

The venue had several different areas, meaning customers could have a quieter drink before moving into the nightclub as the night progressed.

Its reputation for attracting wealthy and well-known visitors also became part of its mythology.

Ferraris and Lamborghinis were not considered unusual sights in the car park during its peak years.

Tall Trees eventually ran into difficulties and closed, with the once-busy entertainment complex later becoming derelict.

The buildings were demolished, and the land was redeveloped for housing.

Today, the site looks completely different, but the nightclub continues to inspire considerable nostalgia.

A Tall Trees reunion was staged as recently as August 30, 2026, at Club Amadeus in Northallerton, with DJs including Rob Tissera and Paul Taylor helping recreate music associated with the venue's glory years.
