RESIDENTS are fighting a plan to build up to nine houses on a 'well used' car park.

Permission is being sought for between one and nine houses at The Sidings in Low Bentham, but with an indicative plan for two family sized homes.

The 'permission in principle' application to North Yorkshire Council seeks to establish whether development is acceptable, and if approved, will be followed with a fully detailed scheme.

Supporting statements on behalf of the owners of the land say it is envisaged that the site is suitable for the development of "two three bedroomed dwellings with associated amenity and parking spaces".

  The car park at The Sidings, Low Bentham

But many residents say ' Low Bentham Car Park' - is a vital amenity and its loss would have a negative impact on the community with one saying approving the plan would be 'insane'.

They say the car park is used by visitors to the Victoria Institute, many of who are elderly, amongst others, and without it, people would have to park on the roadside.

Bentham Town Council has asked for an extension of the time it can comment so members can discuss the plan at its meeting on September 7.

A campaign on Facebook has also been set up urging anyone against the proposal to register their comments on the council's online planning portal.

One objector on the council's planning portal states: "Bentham is effectively without any public transport and so cars and parking are essential.

"Many of the properties along Low Bentham Road and around the Low Bentham Institute do not have their own parking and the only alternative to this car park is roadside parking."

They add: "The Victoria Institute is thriving with many functions throughout the week including community lunches which are so valued by our elderly residents from both Low and High Bentham. "Many of the users are unable to walk very far so the close proximity of the car park is vital for safe drop offs as well as for parking."

Another states that Low Bentham already suffers from limited parking and the development if allowed will mean the loss of its only car park.

"The closure of this car park will have no benefit to the residents of the village and in fact will cause disadvantages to local residents, holidays makers and anyone attending the Victoria Institute for functions. I honestly believe if planning is granted its an insane decision that doesn't take into consideration of the local residents."
