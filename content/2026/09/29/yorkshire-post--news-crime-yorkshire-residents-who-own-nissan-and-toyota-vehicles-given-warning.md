# Yorkshire residents who own Nissan and Toyota vehicles given warning by police

## Owners of Nissan Navara and Toyota Hilux vehicles in Yorkshire have been told to take additional precautions following ongoing concerns around organised vehicle theft of these models of car.

Criminals are increasingly using sophisticated methods to target keyless vehicles, including relay equipment that can amplify the signal from vehicle keys inside homes and specialist electronic devices designed to bypass vehicle security systems, West Yorkshire Police said.

Intelligence has identified both Nissan Navara and Toyota Hilux vehicles as attractive targets for organised offenders.

A total of 37 Navaras and 21 Hilux were stolen in West Yorkshire in the two months up to the end of August.

![West Yorkshire Police is urging owners of Nissan Navara and Toyota Hilux vehicles to take additional precautions following ongoing concerns around organised vehicle theft.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/news/crime/smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65.jpg)


The force is encouraging vehicle owners to take simple but effective steps to reduce the risk of becoming a victim.

The guidance comes after three men were arrested in Bradford last week in connection with such offences.

All three have been bailed pending further enquiries.

The Force’s crime prevention team is urging owners to store vehicle keys and spare keys in a Faraday pouch or signal-blocking container, keep them away from external doors and windows and avoid leaving them in visible locations within the home.

Additional security measures such as steering wheel locks, aftermarket immobilisers and vehicle tracking systems can also provide valuable protection.

Dawn Wilkinson, Principal Force Crime Prevention Officer said: “We would encourage owners to think about vehicle security in layers.

“A Faraday pouch, steering wheel lock and vehicle tracker can significantly reduce the likelihood of theft and may deter offenders from targeting a vehicle altogether.

“We would also remind residents to keep vehicle keys out of sight and report any suspicious activity in their neighbourhood."

“And park vehicles in secure, well-lit locations whenever possible and to remove tools and valuables from vehicles overnight.”

Members of the public are asked to remain vigilant and report any suspicious activity involving individuals checking vehicle doors, examining parked vehicles, taking unusual interest in vehicles, or using electronic devices near driveways and residential properties.
