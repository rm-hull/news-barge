The Ukrainian leader said Russia is “increasing the number of people they recruit” though “various means” including enlisting foreigners, and called on foreign governments to “counter this”.

It comes after at least ten people were killed and a landmark building was set alight in Kyiv after a fresh round of Russian strikes on Ukraine.

Volodymyr Zelensky earlier accused Russia of "terrorising" Ukrainians after the latest attacks in which a market was set on fire and schools were targeted.

“Anywhere in the world, this choice of targets would be called by one word – terrorism. Russia has no military objective whatsoever in doing this – it is purely an attempt to break people and inflict as much harm as possible on our society,” the Ukrainian president said.

## Putin orders increase in size of Russian army

Russian president Vladimir Putin has ordered an increase in the size of the country’s army to 1.55 million active servicemen, according to a presidential decree issued on Monday.

The latest expansion comes as Russia and Ukraine face mounting casualties more than four and a half years into the war.

Under the decree, the overall strength of Russia’s armed forces will rise to 2,441,630 personnel, including 1.55 million active servicemen – an increase of 15,500 troops.

The move follows a similar expansion ordered by Putin in August.

## Explosions heard in Kyiv

Explosions were heard across the Ukrainian capital Kyiv early on Tuesday as Russia continued its near-daily attacks on the country.

Missile debris fell in the courtyard of a residential building, while a non-residential building caught fire elsewhere in the city, Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko said on Telegram.

The latest attack came a day after Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said nine people had been killed and more than 80 injured in Russian drone strikes across the country, including in Kyiv.

## Life in the village on the edge of Russia where locals fear the next hybrid attack

Residents across a Polish border community received three emergency phone alerts in a single day, warning that a missile or drone could enter from Ukraine.

Above the eastern village of Dorohusk, vapour trails carved through the pale blue sky while military helicopters rumbled overhead. Just two kilometres (1.24 miles) away, traffic at the border crossing into Ukraine was halted three times, with personnel evacuated from the checkpoint on one occasion.

Thursday’s alerts and operational stoppages highlight an uneasy reality along Poland’s eastern frontier, where the war in Ukraine — now four and a half years old — feels increasingly immediate.
