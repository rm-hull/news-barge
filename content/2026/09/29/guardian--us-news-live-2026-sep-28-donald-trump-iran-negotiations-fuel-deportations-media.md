Anthropic IPO cautions of 'catastrophic or existential risks to humanity'

Anthropic will caution investors that artificial intelligence could pose “catastrophic or existential risks to humanity” in its IPO prospectus.

As Anthropic prepares for an IPO listing, which could value the AI company at a monumental $2tn, it dedicated almost a third of its prospectus to discussing concerns around AI safety. The document highlights risks that AI models could exhibit “self-preserving behaviors”, including attempts to “resist shutdown” and behavior “resembling blackmail”.

“Our development of highly advanced models, platforms, and applications and expansion of use cases could further ⁠increase the risk that our models cause harm,” Anthropic said in the prospectus.

The Trump administration will formally overturn Biden-era Title IX rules that aimed to protect transgender and LGBTQ students. The Department of Education will return to rules it established in 2020, under Trump’s first administration.

“We will continue to relentlessly champion equal opportunity for all Americans and hold accountable any school or college that violates the rights, privacy, or athletic opportunities of our women and girls,” education secretary Linda McMahon said in a statement.

On its website, the education department wrote: “The Biden Administration’s Office for Civil Rights (OCR) pushed schools across the country to illegally allow males to intrude into women’s sports and intimate facilities, and it investigated school staff for using incorrect pronouns or failing to ask students to declare a preferred gender.”

As the education department website notes, the Trump administration began applying its own Title IX rules after Donald Trump began his second term in January 2025.

Pennsylvania is recording more than 900 measles cases, according to the state’s Department of Health, which updated its measles dashboard with 114 new cases this week. South Carolina and Utah are also reporting significant measles outbreaks.

After Pennsylvania announced two unvaccinated people had died from the disease earlier last month, the first measles-associated deaths in the state in 35 years, health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr. questioned whether the deaths were “fabricated”.

To date, Pennsylvania reports four people have died from measles this year. Less than 1% of those who’ve been infected with measles in the state were fully vaccinated against the disease.

In 2000, the United States eliminated measles, but the disease has experienced a resurgence amid a rise in anti-vaxxing rhetoric. In November, the Pan American Health Organization will consider whether to reevaluate the United States’ measles elimination status.

OpenAI has halted the release of its next AI model over safety concerns, the Wall Street Journal reports

The company was slated to release GPT-6.1 Astra in October, but safety chief Saachi Jain told the WSJ that it fell short of ChatGPT’s standards in alignment tests, showing more deception than previous models and failing to follow human intent.

The news comes after Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for the artificial intelligence agency to slow the development of AI models amid growing safety concerns. In a 12 September blog post, Amodei called for increased global cooperation and oversight of AI.

Last week, the chief executives of OpenAI and Anthropic were called to face an Australian senate inquiry after rogue OpenAI agents hacked Australian and US government websites. Both executives addressed the United Nations security council in separate briefings on AI safety last week as well.
