New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani unveiled a broad initiative Tuesday to tackle hate crimes against Jewish residents and counter antisemitism, seeking to ease anxieties among detractors who view his persistent condemnation of Israel as anti-Jewish bias.

Under the proposal, synagogues will receive additional protection funds, public school curricula will place greater emphasis on Jewish history, and municipal leadership promises to consistently showcase key Jewish religious and cultural landmarks across the city.

Stating that the core aim of the effort is to "confront the scourge of antisemitism now and in the future," Mamdani highlighted that while Jewish residents account for just 12% of the local population, they suffer approximately 50% of reported bias incidents.

"This is not only unacceptable, it's something we all have an obligation to change. And City Hall will lead the way," he said in a video.

![Mamdani, a Democrat and the city's first Muslim mayor, has long been a vocal supporter of Palestinian civil rights and has accused the Israeli government of committing a genocide in Gaza](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/29/16/United_Nations_Trump_Mamdani_99494.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

As a Democrat and New York's first Muslim mayor, Mamdani has consistently advocated for Palestinian civil rights and charged Israel's government with committing genocide in Gaza. Although he maintains that opposing Israeli policies is distinct from antisemitism, political adversaries contend that his sharp language surrounding the Middle East conflict has undermined Jewish safety in the municipality.

Tuesday's announcement represents the administration's strongest measures yet to confront antisemitism, featuring a vow from Mamdani to "build a city where Jewish New Yorkers are not only safe but celebrated."

Municipal authorities plan to expand grants for grassroots groups dealing with hate crime deterrence and victim aid. Furthermore, the city intends to collaborate with public educational institutions to handle bias grievances while running educational campaigns explaining human rights legislation and reporting mechanisms for alleged discrimination.

While expressing support for parts of the strategy, Union for Reform Judaism president Rabbi Rick Jacobs urged Mamdani to moderate his public commentary regarding Israel.

Jacobs stated: "For this effort to be effective, Mayor Mamdani must also commit to stopping his harsh anti-Israel rhetoric that makes Jewish New Yorkers vulnerable as there are clearly times when antisemitic attacks are fueled by anti-Zionism."

![Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu used a speech at the UN last week to attack Mamdani, calling him ‘the antisemitic mayor of New York’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/24/21/UN_General_Assembly_Israel_26815.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Brad Lander, a Jewish ally of Mamdani and former city comptroller, called the initiative a "thoughtful, strategic combination of education for tolerance, enforcement of civil rights, and investments in safety."

During his mayoral campaign last year, Mamdani drew intense criticism for refusing to reject the phrase "globalize the intifada," a slogan interpreted by some as inciting anti-Jewish violence, even though he never uttered the phrase himself and subsequently stated he would advise people against using it.

Additionally, the mayor came under scrutiny in June when he opted out of an annual parade supporting Israel, an event traditionally considered essential for local political leaders.

Speaking at the United Nations last week, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu devoted part of his speech to attacking Mamdani, accusing him of fabricating claims against Israel and labeling him "the antisemitic mayor of New York."

Mamdani has persistently demanded Netanyahu's arrest pursuant to an International Criminal Court warrant concerning alleged Gaza war crimes.

Israel's consul general in New York, Ofir Akunis, asserted on Tuesday that the mayor's stance toward Israel jeopardizes local Jewish residents.

Writing on social media platform X, Akunis posted: "Until you recognize that your language against the Jewish State, which you don’t recognize as the homeland of the Jewish people, is the fundamental reason for the rise of antisemitism in New York City, it will continue to increase."
