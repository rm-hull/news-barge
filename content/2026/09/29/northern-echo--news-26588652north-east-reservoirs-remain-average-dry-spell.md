Northumbrian Water is urging people across the North East to use water wisely, warning that reservoirs remain below average after one of the driest periods in years.

Northumbrian Water says rivers, reservoirs and groundwater sources will need several months of above-average rainfall to recover from the prolonged dry spell.

Reservoirs remain below normal for this time of year, with those in Northumberland, including Kielder Water, at 74 per cent capacity and those in Weardale and Teesdale at just 51 and 59 per cent.

Kieran Ingram, water director at Northumbrian Water, said: "The recent rain has helped a little, but it has been significant enough.

"Our reservoirs remain below normal levels, and it will take many months of wetter-than-average weather for rivers, reservoirs and groundwater sources to fully recover.

"That's why we're asking customers to keep up the fantastic water-saving habits they've developed over recent months.

"Every drop saved this year puts us in a better position for next year.

"The water we save today helps strengthen supplies for the future and supports the long-term health of our environment."

The company is urging customers to make small changes at home and in the garden to cut water use.

Tips include turning off the tap while brushing teeth, taking shorter showers, fixing leaks and running washing machines and dishwashers only when full and on eco mode.

Northumbrian Water also recommends reusing water, such as collecting water used to wash fruit and vegetables for plants and gardens.

"By continuing to save water now, customers can help protect local water resources, support the environment and build resilience for future dry periods."

The Environment Agency says nearly three-quarters of England remains in drought, and warns that recovery will take time.
