Donald Trump rolled out a new chatbot aimed at helping Americans interact with federal agencies and streamlining the processes associated with them on Tuesday morning at an event hosted by motivational speaker Tony Robbins as the service — America.gov — launched to the public.

“It’s going to save you tremendous time,” the president promised.

The tool can be used to direct Americans to services across the federal government in a manner similar to other existing AI chatbots, like the Gemini service integrated into Google’s basic search function. In a video presentation from Robbins and chief design officer Joe Gebbia, the designers teased greater functionalities in the future that did not appear to be available yet, like passport renewal and updating/obtaining other official documents through a one-click system.

“America plans to let you renew a passport right here in 2027. See what's coming,” the chatbot tells users today, with a link to a promotional video showing those features.

“Instead of forcing citizens to search through the endless maze of tens of thousands of terrible government websites and rules...you’ll now have one front door for every single question. Everything will be answered easily and quickly,” Trump promised, vowing that the site would save average Americans “hundreds” of hours per year filling out government paperwork, accessing benefits and searching for information.

![Donald Trump speaks at an event rolling out an AI chatbot tool for assisting with federal agency websites](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/29/15/download...jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Anyone who has used Grok, or Gemini, or ChatGPT will understand instantly how to use America.gov. Not complicated,” Trump said. The technology is reportedly built using Gemini and Grok code.

“Whether you’re a Scout leader who wants to reserve a campsite at a national park, a senior citizen trying to choose a Medicare plan, or an American veteran trying to get the benefits that you’ve earned, you so justly deserve, America.gov will be there to help,” said the president.

The announcement comes as Trump met Monday evening with Dario Amodei, the CEO of Anthropic who has warned that his industry’s growth needs government supervision and guardrails to avoid courting disaster. The president is due to meet with Amodei and other officials from Anthropic again on Tuesday.

Americans’ views on artificial intelligence are also changing at a rapid pace, with opposition to the growth of data center projects becoming a bipartisan issue in communities around the country. Both Democratic and Republican governors and other candidates have signaled support for pauses, bans or temporary moratoriums of various kinds as the expansion of data centers as well as the overall role of AI in society continues at a fever pitch.

A CNN/SRSS poll conducted this month laid out Americans’ fears in stark view. As many as 9 in 10 Americans now fear that artificial intelligence could surpass human control and intelligence, and view the technology with skepticism and disdain by similarly wide margins. Nearly half of those surveyed thought that AI would likely negatively impact their own personal economic outcomes in the future. Less than 20 percent said the opposite.

![Donald Trump congratulates members of the National Design Studio at the launch of America.gov](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/29/16/2026-09-29T152458Z_1202462127_RC23TNASELLS_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Seven in 10 Americans also say that the federal government is not doing enough to regulate the technology, a prospect that the president has seemingly dismissed out of hand as he remains one of the loudest cheerleaders for AI in Washington.

At the event on Tuesday, the president repeated his quip that he wants to change the name of artificial intelligence to “super intelligence”, before joking that the was going to begin referring to the “fake news” media as the “artificial news”.

The rollout and transfer of responsibilities from federal workers and agency websites to an AI agent raises both possibilities for streamlining government access as well as serious concerns about privacy, security, and economic ripple effects.

Both Grok and Google’s Gemini service, which the America.gov service are built on, carry disclaimers that information provided by AI agents may be incomplete, unreliable, or flat-out untrue. While the rollout of America.gov doesn’t yet mean that any individual agencies will undergo major changes, its launch follows the second Trump administration’s efforts to radically transform many federal agencies under both the cost-cutting and efficiency pledges of Elon Musk’s shuttered DOGE initiative as well as the efforts of Russell Vought at the Office of Management and Budget to further diminish government spending.

Trump administration officials could seek to further offload the work of federal agencies to the America.gov system in the future as many figures within the administration continue to push for agencies to be slimmed down and federal positions to be eliminated.
