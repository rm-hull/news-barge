**Harrogate Spring Water** has officially confirmed it will challenge the unanimous rejection of its bottling plant expansion plans, triggering a major political and community backlash just days before a statutory deadline.

Speaking on the record to the **Harrogate Informer**, Managing Director Richard Hall confirmed the company has formally notified North Yorkshire Council today of its intention to lodge a full appeal with the national Planning Inspectorate. The move aims to overturn the planning committee’s unanimous refusal of their detailed Reserved Matters application in April.

The company faces a strict drop-dead deadline of **17 October 2026** to submit its official paperwork. The appeal will proceed on the exact design footprint originally rejected by local councillors, with no alternative structural modifications or downscaling on the table.

### The ‘Silent Majority’ Defence

In a robust defence of the expansion, which requires felling trees in a section of Rotary Wood, Hall claimed that an unvoiced majority of local residents support the multi-million-pound development despite more than 1,000 formal objections lodged with the council.

> **Richard Hall, Managing Director of Harrogate Spring Water said:**
>
> We’ve taken time to consider it. We reviewed it and made a decision to proceed.
>
> There’s nothing particular about the time it’s taken; it’s just a planning process.
>
> We notified the council today that we will appeal, and we need to submit the paperwork by the deadline in ten days’ time.

The water brand said its position is backed by three independent consumer surveys conducted over the past two years by market research consultancy Censuswide. According to Hall, the data shows that 80% of Harrogate respondents consistently express support for the factory expansion.

Hall questioned the methodology of public campaign objection portals, comparing them unfavourably to industry polling. “If you put a link on an MP’s website and ask people to comment, you attract people who have the same opinion as that person,” Hall argued. “We would say that methodology is perhaps less robust than a Censuswide study. It tells us that a large percentage of Harrogate’s population supports our proposal, but they’re not willing to talk publicly about it—a silent majority.”

> **Richard Hall, Managing Director of Harrogate Spring Water, said:**
>
> We have designed our plans extremely carefully over a number of years and have consulted both with the community and with local planning officials to ensure they are compliant, which officers recognised when they recommended the plans for approval. We do not agree with Committee’s decision and so our next step is to appeal.
>
> We have done everything we can to understand genuine local community concerns, mitigate environmental impact and invest for sustainable development in the future, benefitting the local economy of Harrogate.
>
> Our appeal has a strong legal basis and our plans meet all the technical requirements. They will also have significant benefits for the town and I will ensure that our expansion is managed in a sympathetic, sustainable way.

### MP Vows to Intervene at Planning Inspectorate

The announcement has drawn immediate, fierce condemnation from **Tom Gordon MP**, the Member of Parliament for Harrogate and Knaresborough, who has vowed to escalate the battle straight to the national inspectors.

> **Tom Gordon MP stated:**
>
> I warned when the planning committee refused this application that we had to remain vigilant, and sadly today proves why.
>
> Local councillors looked at the evidence, heard from more than 1,000 residents who objected, and made the right call.
>
> Danone may not like that decision, but it was a democratic one, and it should be respected.

The MP strongly rejected the corporate justification regarding their planned environmental mitigation layout, emphasising the deep local connection to the established community asset.

> **Tom Gordon MP added:**
>
> A new two-acre plantation is not a like-for-like swap for a decades-old woodland that local people planted and love.
>
> I will be making the case to the Planning Inspectorate that the committee’s decision should stand, and I’ll keep working alongside Save Rotary Wood, Pinewoods Conservation Group and local residents until Rotary Wood is safeguarded for future generations.

### Community Group Launches Legal Defence Fund

The **Pinewoods Conservation Group (PCG)** has launched an immediate counter-offensive, declaring that the community is ready to raise funds to fight the corporate giant in court. The group heavily criticised the company’s decision to run restricted media briefings today before engaging with the people who actively care for the woodland asset.

> **Neil Hind, Chairperson of the Pinewoods Conservation Group said:**
>
> We are deeply disappointed that Harrogate Spring Water and its parent company, Danone, intend to appeal North Yorkshire Council’s decision to refuse their expansion plans.
>
> There was clear local, national and international concerns raised around this proposal. Rotary Wood is an established, publicly accessible community woodland, planted by local people and schoolchildren.
>
> It provides valuable wildlife habitat and a much-loved space for recreation and wellbeing. Pursuing a scheme that would sacrifice this woodland is the wrong decision for this location and our community.

The conservation group firmly dismissed the business benefits outlined in the corporate media package, highlighting that alternative solutions must be explored instead of dragging the town through a prolonged legal inquiry.

> **Neil Hind added:**
>
> The concerns have not gone away. Replacement planting cannot provide an immediate substitute for established woodland, its biodiversity or the value people place on it. Questions about the location, accessibility and long-term protection of replacement provision remain central, alongside concerns about additional traffic, noise and lighting.
>
> The claimed economic benefits should not be treated as outweighing these lasting environmental and community costs.
>
> We are also concerned that the company has chosen to brief the local press without communicating directly with the community groups, residents and councillors most affected.
>
> Meaningful engagement requires an open, two-way conversation with the people who use and care for this woodland. A press briefing is no substitute for that dialogue.

The PCG confirmed it will be actively gathering community resources to sustain a legal defence throughout the formal appeal process.

> **Neil Hind concluded:**
>
> An appeal will prolong uncertainty for everyone who has worked to protect Rotary Wood.
>
> We urge Harrogate Spring Water and Danone to reconsider this approach, respect the value of this community asset and explore alternatives that protect the woodland and compensate appropriately.
>
> We will also need to raise further funds to help defend Rotary Wood through the appeal, and would welcome support from anyone who wants to protect this much-loved community woodland.
>
> Pinewoods Conservation Group and community groups remains committed to defending Rotary Wood and ensuring that the community’s voice is heard throughout any appeal process.

### B-Corp Credentials and Community Pledges

Defending the brand’s community track record and its global B-Corp sustainability status, Hall highlighted legally binding economic and ecological pledges tied to the development’s Section 106 agreements, which include supporting 50 full-time-equivalent jobs on-site. He noted the company has a binding commitment to replace felled trees at a six-to-one ratio and deliver a guaranteed 10% net gain in local biodiversity. He added that the firm has already planted 3,400 trees within five miles of the site while the planning process was ongoing.

* **Job Creation:** The expanded operation is projected to support 50 full-time-equivalent jobs on-site alongside younger-person apprenticeships.
* **The Tree Guarantee:** A biding commitment to replace felled trees at a six-to-one ratio.
* **Biodiversity Net Gain:** A guaranteed 10% net gain in local biodiversity.
* **Advance Planting:** The firm has already planted 3,400 trees within five miles of the site while the planning process was ongoing.

> **Richard Hall added:**
>
> Being a B Corp means committing to deliver economic and social value to the community.
>
> We believe we’ve done that.
>
> If we put forward a proposal that has been consulted on, generates economic value and is sustainable, and there are objections, we will challenge them. That’s a democratic process.

### Investment Warnings

> **Tim Cook, Editor’s comment:**
>
> This can be framed in many ways. It can be viewed as bypassing local democracy and triggering a national appeal track by a conglomerate, Danone, or simply following due democratic process, that they have every right to take.
>
> The corporate strategy laid bare in our interview relies entirely on a “silent majority” narrative framework, backed by internal polls of just 200 people to actively diminish the recorded objections of over 1,000 residents and a unanimous, cross-party council refusal.
>
> On paper, the developer certainly holds strong legal grounds to mount this appeal because their baseline 2017 outline planning permission remains completely valid. By taking their case to the national Planning Inspectorate, they are using a legal loophole to override a unanimous decision made by Harrogate’s elected local councillors, who voted to reject the detailed expansion plans in April.
>
> Yet, by choosing to fight the planning decision at a national tribunal rather than adapting their plans, they have entered a major strategic blind-spot.
>
> Conspicuously absent from their corporate calculation is any mention of Rotary Wood’s formal legal protective shield as a registered Asset of Community Value (ACV). Because any build on an active ACV site alters the local landscape, the brand cannot simply force a layout change.
>
> By avoiding a compromise and doubling down on a contentious legal battle, they are running a massive risk. If their national appeal track ultimately fails or forces a significant layout renegotiation down the line, the entire fight will head straight to a fresh, statutory Asset of Community Value consultation, dragging the multi-billion-pound firm right back to square one with a highly motivated public campaign waiting.
>
> While the business rationale regarding supplier spend, regional economic footprints, and tree-replacement multipliers makes logical sense inside a corporate boardroom, it is not clear how that connects to the potential loss of the Rotary Wood in reality.
>
> With Tom Gordon MP stepping directly into the national inspectorate arena alongside the Pinewoods Conservation Group, this appeal will no longer be a standard technical planning debate.
>
> It is a high-profile stand-off on local self-determination, with a hard line in the sand being drawn by Harrogate Spring Water.
