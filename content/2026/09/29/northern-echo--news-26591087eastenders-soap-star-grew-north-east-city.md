For generations of TV viewers, Debbie Arnold has been a familiar face popping up in some of Britain's biggest soaps and television shows.

But before she was an EastEnders regular, a Coronation Street landlady or an Emmerdale character, Debbie Arnold was a Sunderland girl with showbusiness in her blood.

Born in Sunderland in 1955, Debbie grew up in a family where performing was very much part of everyday life.

Her father, Eddie Arnold, was a well-known impressionist, while her mother, Mary, worked as a theatrical agent, so perhaps it was little surprise that Debbie would eventually find herself heading for the bright lights.

And she got her first taste of television fame remarkably young.

At just 18, Debbie landed a job impersonating Marilyn Monroe on the television show Now Who Do You Do?, putting her talent for voices and impressions firmly on the map.

From there, her career went from strength to strength, with appearances in a huge range of popular British programmes including The Liver Birds, Minder, The Two Ronnies, Birds of a Feather, Holby City and Jonathan Creek.

But it is her soap career that has made Debbie particularly recognisable to television fans.

The Sunderland-born star has appeared in Coronation Street, Emmerdale, EastEnders, Hollyoaks and Doctors, taking on regular roles and becoming a familiar face across British daytime and primetime television.

For EastEnders fans, she is perhaps best remembered as April Branning, a character she played between 1995 and 1996.

She also has the unusual distinction of having played two different characters in Coronation Street, first appearing as Sylvie Hicks in the early 1980s before returning decades later as Carole Evans, landlady of the Weatherfield Arms.

Now 71, Debbie keeps in touch with her followers via regular videos including hair and beauty tips on Instagram.

Now, Debbie's website - debbiearnold.com - lists a number of her accomplishments, including 37 years in showbiz, seven West End appearances and two husbands - not forgetting the 10 dogs that she has owned.

Debbie's talents have stretched well beyond the soap set.

She has enjoyed a substantial theatre career, including a role opposite Omar Sharif in The Sleeping Prince, as well as working as a voice artist, presenter, producer and writer.

So while Debbie Arnold may have moved on from Sunderland, her story remains a particularly entertaining one for North East television fans.
