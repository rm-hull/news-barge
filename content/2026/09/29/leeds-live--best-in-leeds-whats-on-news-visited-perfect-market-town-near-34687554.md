# I visited 'perfect' market town near Leeds and one thing won me over

## The spot has been named the UK's first town to achieve a 100% recommendation rating in a major survey

The rain hammered down sideways, and the sky hanging over West Yorkshire looked like wet slate as Reach reporter Emily Wright climbed out of the car, despite it being the middle of June. It was precisely the sort of gloomy Northern morning that tempts you to jump straight back inside and drive off home.

Yet Emily hadn't made the journey in search of glorious weather on behalf of Yorkshire Live. She was here to find out whether a modest market town could truly live up to a wholly extraordinary reputation.

Eight-hundred year old Otley — nestled between Leeds and the Yorkshire Dales — has recently claimed a remarkable UK first. Out of more than 350 towns assessed over the past decade by research firm People and Places Insight, Otley stands as the only place ever to have achieved a perfect 100% recommendation rating.

Every last person among the 651 surveyed said they would encourage their friends and family to pay a visit.

A statistic like that sets an extraordinarily lofty standard. Yet within just 20 minutes of wandering through the town centre and speaking with local business owners, the miserable weather simply ceased to matter.

It turns out that Otley's true charm isn't merely its riverside parks or the iconic Chevin hillside looming in the background — it's the locals themselves, who carry a fierce pride for their hometown and absolutely refuse to let a bit of drizzle dampen their spirits. Having come from a typical London commuter town, Emily found the contrast quite remarkable.

Where she's from, the town centre serves largely as a thoroughfare for weary workers, where people keep their eyes down, dash towards the train station and rarely exchange so much as a pleasantry.

Otley, however, is an entirely different story. Wandering along the high street and through its market, which runs three days a week, the sense of community feels immediate and genuine.

At a time when town centres up and down the country are struggling, Otley's independent traders, who make up roughly three-quarters of all businesses, are thriving. This proved a contributing factor in 26% of the reasons visitors say they love the town, according to a benchmark survey.

Yet it isn't simply the goods on offer that keeps people coming back - it's the people behind the counters.

Emily's first port of call was Functional Drinks Club, which boasts what is believed to be the UK's most extensive range of independent kombucha. Owner Kevin Gibson uprooted his life and business to come here, drawn specifically by Otley's outdoor-loving, health-conscious culture, and has since rapidly outgrown his original premises.

"Otley is all about community — it's amazing, and that's one of the things that distinguishes it from surrounding towns," he said. "When anybody does anything from Otley, we're fiercely proud of Otley and what people do. Everybody is really supportive of the businesses."

Stroll down any cobbled street in the town, and you'll encounter that very same feeling.

A little further on, Bronny Lenwick took over a period building from 1891 that had stood empty for a year, transforming it into the Secret Garden Café, a heritage-inspired haven boasting a secluded and elegantly designed outdoor space.

"When we took it on, as we were doing it up, people were coming forward from the community saying it had been an independent shop since it was built," Ms Lenwick said.

"We have generations of people coming in, putting on events, and working together with other independent businesses is so lovely. When I opened on the street, the other businesses were just so supportive. It's like how it used to be."

That communal ethos reaches far beyond preserving high street traders. When a former Methodist church in the town came up for sale, locals were resolute in stopping it from being snapped up by a property developer.

Rather, the community joined forces and raised an extraordinary £800,000 to purchase the building themselves and convert it into a new community-owned centre called Otley Common.

Mick Bradley, the Mayor of Otley, views it as proof of local determination: "It speaks to an enduring spirit of entrepreneurship and renewal. It's a gritty Northern tale — you have to find your way in the world, and Otley does."

That commitment is evident everywhere you look. At independent speciality grocer Simon and Becky, husband-and-wife owners Simon and Becky Fishwick dedicated 16 years to running market stalls before taking the plunge with a permanent shop.

"When we moved into this shop, so many of our market customers followed us in, along with a whole new clientele," Ms Fishwick said. "The community here is just amazing in how they support people."

Simon agreed, gesturing towards their well-stocked shelves: "We started with a few hundred items, and now we stock thousands. There's just something special about Otley — the community, the local sports grounds, there are always little things going on that bring people together."

The statistics back this up. While 52% of those surveyed were locals, 38% lived within a half-hour drive, and an impressive 10% were visitors who had journeyed from considerably further afield.

By the time Emily made it back to the car, the Yorkshire drizzle persisted. But heading back towards the impersonal anonymity of the London commuter belt, she finally understood why every single person surveyed awarded this town top marks.

Otley may have clinched its "perfect" status on paper, but it won her over with something that simply cannot be manufactured - genuine, unfiltered town pride.
