Darlington was transformed for a day of live music and comedy as the Last Train Home Festival took over the town's venues.

The Last Train Home Festival brought regional and national performers to Darlington Hippodrome, The Hullabaloo and The Forum Music Centre on Saturday, September 5.

Festival-goers were treated to a packed programme of live music spanning rock, pop, neo soul, punk, indie and grime, with stages programmed by Darlington's Tracks, BBC Introducing and NARC magazine.

  Last Train Home Festival takes place in Darlington. Shakk of BBC Introducing welcomes Nano Kind to perform on the Noisy Daughters Stage (Hullabaloo Theatre). 

*(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

The event was designed to finish in time for festival-goers to catch the "last train home".

Scottish indie rock band Lucia & The Best Boys headlined the Noisy Daughters stage at The Hullabaloo.

The band has collaborated with Cvrches and The Last Dinner Party on their latest singles, while their second album, Picking Petals, was due for release in July 2026.

Teesside art-noise punks Benefits headlined The Forum stage.

  Last Train Home Festival takes place in Darlington. Nano Kind perform on the Noisy Daughters Stage (Hullabaloo Theatre). 

*(Image: CHRIS BOOTH)*

The band's uncompromising spoken word lyrics, sonic rage and electronic dance wall of sound have taken them around the globe, earning critical acclaim across national press and radio.

Also on the line-up were Newcastle's Leazes, Polyvinyl, Risco, Callum Pitt, The Callows, Katie Grace, Labyrinthine Oceans, Wench and Anna Karila.

Comedy fans were also catered for, with a Hilarity Bites stage featuring Gavin Webster, Silky, The Halls Of Ridiculous, Devin Gray, Maxine Wade, Dawn Bailey, Rob Mullholland, El Purnell, Anna Thomas and international, multi-award winning comedian Ray Bradshaw.

The festival ran from 1pm until 10.15pm, with tickets priced at £25 plus booking fee in advance or £30 on the door.

The event was open to those aged 14 and over, with under-18s required to be accompanied by a grown-up.

All venues were fully accessible, with companion tickets available.
