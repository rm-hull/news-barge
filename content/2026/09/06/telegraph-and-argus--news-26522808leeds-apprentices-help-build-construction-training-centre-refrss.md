A TEAM of apprentices has helped build a construction training centre for young people.

Six apprentices from Leeds-based mechanical, electrical, and public health engineering firm G&H designed and installed the MEP (mechanical, electrical, and public health) services for the new post-16 centre in Wortley.

The facility will support young people aged 16-19 disengaged from education and at risk of becoming Not in Education, Employment or Training (NEET).

Tom Noble, a first-year projects apprentice at G&H, said: "Being involved in this project has been incredible.

"I only started my apprenticeship in July and the initial weeks and months usually involve a great deal of observing and learning from others and gradually taking on more responsibility.

"To have the opportunity to step up and be hands-on undertaking and preparing the risk assessments and method statement, reviewing health and safety requirements and setting up the site safety board, ordering materials and coordinating the work, has been a fantastic experience.

"I learnt so much throughout the project and my mentors really did let me get on with it.

"Yes, I made some mistakes, but I had the support around me to learn from them and understand how I could approach things differently next time."

The apprentices delivered the project for Building Futures Together (BFT), a vocational training provider.

G&H design apprentice Zac Russell, electrical apprentices Dan Brook and Ellis Parker, and mechanical apprentices Jake Sherburn and Cory Taylor, also contributed to the project.

The new centre will open its doors in September and will offer hands-on training in electrical and plumbing skills, alongside an introduction to health and safety in construction and site preparation.

Learners will complete practical tasks including installing lighting circuits and sockets, removing and refitting radiators, and bending, joining and connecting copper pipes.

They will be able to achieve the City & Guilds Level 1 Award in Construction and/or Level 1 Diploma in Construction Skills.

Paul Churchill, development manager at G&H, said: "An integral part of our apprenticeships is gaining hands-on experience on live sites, with apprentices working closely with a qualified and experienced mentor.

"Although I have been on hand to oversee the six-strong team of apprentices undertake this unique project, they have truly owned it and worked collaboratively to deliver it.

"They have drawn on the experience they have gained during their apprenticeships and applied it effectively to deliver a new facility for BFT that will benefit the next generation of construction workers.

"I’m incredibly proud of them."

G&H, founded in 1998, delivers MEP engineering solutions across the UK.
