At least five people have been killed and five more injured after an Amazon cargo plane overshot the runway at Miami International Airport, according to authorities.

The plane hit multiple vehicles and burst into flames, sending thick, dark clouds of smoke into the air Sunday afternoon.

The Federal Aviation Administration said the aircraft overran the runway and crashed while landing around 2 p.m. The flight had been heading to Florida from Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport in San Juan, Puerto Rico.

“Five people lost their lives and five others were injured,” Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine Cava said at a press conference Sunday. “My heart is with all of the families who were affected and to everyone who is grieving tonight, and we’re also praying for the injured and for their families.”

Photos and videos shared online appeared to show the Prime Air plane had come to a rest beside a roadway. More than 60 Miami-Dade Fire Rescue units responded to the airport to extinguish the blaze, officials said.

![At least five people have been killed and five more injured after an Amazon cargo plane overshot the runway at Miami International Airport, according to authorities](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/06/21/2026-09-06T202346Z_1701192901_RC2WDNASZYYQ_RTRMADP_3_USA-CRASH-PLANE-MIAMI.JPG)

Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel confirmed the incident in a statement shared with *The Independent*. 

“ This is a fast-moving situation and we're still gathering details. We're working closely with local authorities and officials to understand exactly what happened,” Nantel said. “Right now, our absolute priority is the safety, well-being, and care of everyone involved. We're doing everything we can to support those affected."

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the FAA was sending investigators and issued a full ground stop at Miami International as emergency crews responded to the crash.

![All runways and taxiways were closed at Miami International Airport after the crash](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/06/20/2026-09-06T195234Z_497811147_RC2VDNA036UT_RTRMADP_3_USA-CRASH-PLANE-MIAMI.JPG)

All runways and taxiways were closed, and a ground stop is in effect, an airport spokesperson said. During a ground stop, all flights are put on hold and the airport is essentially shut down.

The FAA says it expects flights to be delayed even after the airport reopens.

The National Transportation Safety Board said it was “gathering information” about the crash.
