A serial domestic abuser concluded his reign of terror against a former partner by fracturing her spine and pelvis with his van.

It was one of a series of incidents of abuse committed by defendant Dominic Phillips against two women.

Forty-year-old Phillips was sentenced at Newcastle Crown Court on (Friday, September 4) for the catalogue of domestic violence offences against both victims.

Both were ex-partners of the defendant and reported the abuse after their respective relationships had concluded as they were in fear of the violence escalating.

*(Image: Northumbria Police)*

One woman came forward in 2024 to report to police the physical and coercively controlling behaviour she had experienced at Phillips’ hands over several years

It included Phillips punching her in the face as she attempted to end the relationship as well as bursting her lip on another occasion.

The abuse culminated in an assault during which Phillips drove his vehicle at the woman, pinning her against a wall, fracturing her spine and pelvis.

He then attempted to justify the assault by telling her: “This is what happens when you don’t do as you’re told.”

*(Image: Northumbria Police)*

In a subsequent relationship, Phillips again used his car to threaten the second victim, driving at speed towards a wall before braking at the last moment while in anger.

He then drove the woman to Hendon Beach cliffs, in Sunderland, before assaulting her, strangling her by putting his elbow and his knee on her neck until she passed out, then threatened to drive them both off the edge.

Phillips later also threatened her with a knife and then locked her inside his house.

She managed to escape the address and drive herself home, where she then called a friend to take her to hospital, having been left in “a terrible state.”

Both women came forward separately to make reports of domestic abuse, and Phillips was arrested and questioned.

During interviews, Phillips denied any wrongdoing, claiming he was lost in the dark and didn’t mean to scare the woman, relating to the Hendon Beach incident.

He also alleged he could not recall the circumstances around the incident where he struck the victim with his van.

When asked whether he was with the woman at the time the assault took place, he replied: “I couldn’t tell you.”

Despite his denials, Phillips, of Grey Terrace, Sunderland, was charged with a string of domestic abuse-related offences in relation to both cases.

He attended Newcastle Crown Court in January and March this year where he admitted the offences relating to both women.

They included controlling or coercive behaviour, two counts of intentional strangulation, causing serious injury by dangerous driving, putting a person in fear of violence, and assault occasioning actual bodily harm.

Phillips returned to the court on (Friday, September 4), when he was given a seven-year prison sentence, by Judge Tom Moran.

He will be subject of a further four years on extended licence upon his release from custody.

As part of the sentence, he was also made subject of restraining orders to protect both victims.

In an impact statement provided to the court, the first woman described the lasting impact of Phillips’ offending.

She said: “The negative impact this man has had on my life throughout the duration of our relationship, and continuing to this day, is something I would never wish on anyone.

“I have never truly experienced pain like having my pelvis and spine broken.

“It’s not just the pain, but the fear I had.

“Worry that if I speak up and tell the truth, what will he do to me next?

“Having to live with that, while he and his friends made derogatory marks about the injuries left me feeling humiliated and deeply regretful that I didn’t report this sooner.”

“This experience has permanently changed my outlook on life and on people.

“Talking about the abuse has been difficult, but I feel so proud that I am finally able to speak the truth after so many years of silence.

“I am so grateful that I was able to escape Dominic and his control.

“Whilst the impact of his actions remains, it has given me a sense of courage and relief knowing that I have played a part of protecting other potential women in the future by showing what lengths this man will go to if something doesn’t go his way.”

The second survivor of Phillips’ abuse said she was ‘terrified’ of him and what he was capable of doing.

She said: “This traumatic incident has had a profound and lasting impact on every aspect of my life, mentally, emotionally, physically and financially.

“My life has fundamentally changed as a direct result of what was done to me.

“I have been left with a visible scar on my forehead that is unlikely to ever fade.

“The injury is a permanent reminder of the violence I suffered.

“Since the attack, my mental health has deteriorated severely.

“I struggle to sleep and regularly experience vivid nightmares in which I relive the incident.

“The psychological impact has been overwhelming.

“What happened to me, and others before me, at the hands of Dominic Phillips, must not be allowed to happen again.

“The effects of his attack will remain with me for the rest of my life.”

Detective Constable Jonathan Doyle, who led the investigation, said: “Dominic Phillips is a bully who has inflicted the most horrific abuse on these two women.

“I can’t even imagine how terrified they must have been when they went through these ordeals.

“I am so grateful that they both decided to come forward and report their abuser to police, meaning he has now had his day in court and is in prison where he belongs.”

Det Con Doyle added: “Please remember there are also schemes in place where you can find out if a partner, or prospective partner, has a history of domestic abuse.

“The scheme is called Clares’ Law – and it could change your life.

“Clare’s Law is free to use, confidential, and available for anyone to use.”
