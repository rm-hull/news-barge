A County Durham village previously named among the UK's poshest places to live continues to stand out for its historic buildings, rural setting and proximity to Durham City.

Brancepeth, a few miles south-west of Durham, was included by The Telegraph in its lists of some of Britain's poshest places to live in both 2023 and 2024.

The village is centred around Brancepeth Castle, a large medieval building which has stood at the heart of the settlement for centuries.

The castle was once associated with the powerful Neville family and remains one of the village's most recognisable landmarks.

It is now privately owned and opens to the public on selected dates for tours and events.

Visitors have praised both its history and the restoration work being carried out.

One said: “The family that owns it is working hard to restore it and is very welcoming to those who visit.

“If you are interested in history, this is a fantastic place to see.”

Another of Brancepeth's best-known buildings is St Brandon's Church, which dates back to the 12th century.

The church was badly damaged by a major fire in 1998 before undergoing extensive restoration work.

Today it remains an important part of the village and sits only a short distance from the castle.

Brancepeth itself is made up largely of traditional stone properties, cottages and larger homes surrounded by countryside.


Its location means residents can enjoy a relatively quiet village setting while remaining within a short drive of Durham City.

Properties in and around the village are relatively limited in number, helping Brancepeth maintain a more secluded feel than many larger settlements surrounding Durham.

The surrounding area is also home to Brancepeth Castle Golf Club, which adds to the village's reputation as one of the county's more affluent locations.

Brancepeth's inclusion in national lists of Britain's poshest places has helped draw wider attention to a village which has long been known locally for its history and setting.

Visitors frequently highlight the castle as one of its main attractions.

One described it as being “full of history and grandeur”, while another praised the fact it remains a lived-in historic building rather than a conventional tourist attraction.

Despite being only a few miles from Durham City, Brancepeth remains relatively small and surrounded by open countryside.
