Ripon councillors are set to discuss raising objections to the Government’s plan to house asylum seekers at the old RAF base at Linton-on-Ouse.

The plan, which hasn’t been confirmed yet, will be on the agenda at Monday’s meeting (7th September) of the city council.

The Home Office announced earlier this year it would seek planning permission to use the site, near York, to house up to 1,200 asylum seekers while their claims were processed.

Reform-led Scarborough Town Council has already said it was seeking “urgent assurance” from the Home Office and North Yorkshire Council the seaside town would not be used as a placement area for those who had been processed without “a comprehensive, open consultation”.

Andrew Williams, an Independent councillor on Ripon City Council and North Yorkshire Council is also the Mayor of Ripon.

He explained why he wants the city council to discuss the issue.

“I think it’s important, given our relative close proximity (16 miles) that we indicate to the government our concerns in terms of the proposals to have 1,200 illegal immigrants on our doorstep.

“My concerns are that these are not people seeking asylum, they’re economic migrants of which people know very little about their previous background, and they’re free to roam as they please.

“And as a parent of a 13-year-old daughter, I’m extremely concerned that we’re having 12 hundred people who we don’t know pretty much anything about, potentially deposited on our doorstep.

“I’ll be asking the council to support the objections raised by Linton Parish Council, North Yorkshire Council, and the Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, in advising the government that this is the wrong location for these people to be placed, and I have to say that this is not a case of not in my backyard, because frankly, I don’t think they should be in anybody’s backyard left to roam unsecured.”

If the council votes to take further action they will write to the Home Office raising councillors’ concerns.
