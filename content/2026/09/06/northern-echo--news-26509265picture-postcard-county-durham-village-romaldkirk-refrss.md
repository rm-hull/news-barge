A County Durham village described as “picture-postcard” continues to attract visitors with its historic stone buildings, traditional green and quiet rural setting.

Romaldkirk, near Middleton-in-Teesdale, is regularly praised online as one of the area's prettiest villages.

Visitors have described it as “picturesque”, “timeless” and a place which has retained much of its traditional character.

The village is centred around a large green surrounded by historic properties.

Many of the stone buildings around Romaldkirk date from the 18th and 19th centuries, helping give the settlement an appearance which has changed relatively little compared with many larger villages.

  Church of St. Romald in Romaldkirk 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

A Grade II-listed 19th-century water pump and stone trough also stand on the village green.

Visitors have described the area around the green as having a “storybook feel” and say it is one of the features which makes Romaldkirk particularly memorable.

Another major landmark is the Church of St Romald.

The Grade I-listed building contains surviving medieval and earlier features and has long been one of the village's most important historic sites.

Among its features are Anglo-Saxon stonework, a 12th-century font and a medieval rood stair.

Visitors interested in local history regularly single out the church for praise, with some describing it as one of the most interesting village churches in the area.

Romaldkirk also has connections to several notable figures.

Engineer Thomas Page, architects Maxwell Fry and Jane Drew and Dales farmer Hannah Hauxwell all have links with the village or surrounding area.


Hannah Hauxwell, who became known nationally through television documentaries about her isolated farming life, is buried in the churchyard.

The village was also once served by its own railway station, which formed part of the former line running through Teesdale.

Food and drink also play a role in attracting visitors.

The Rose and Crown stands beside the village green and has operated as an inn for generations.

Visitors frequently praise the historic setting and traditional character of the pub, with some describing it as one of the highlights of a visit to Romaldkirk.

The village's location also makes it a useful stopping point for people travelling through Teesdale.

Middleton-in-Teesdale, Barnard Castle and attractions including High Force and the wider North Pennines are all within reach.

But it is Romaldkirk's small size and traditional appearance which appear to leave the strongest impression.

Visitors have described it as “an ideal escape” and a village which feels removed from modern development.

Its historic church, stone houses and distinctive village green have helped Romaldkirk maintain its reputation as one of County Durham's most picturesque locations all year round.
