A North Easr woman who scooped £119 million in the lottery seven years ago has now given away nearly half her fortune.

Frances Connolly and husband Paddy won the enormous EuroMillions jackpot in 2019 and estimate they have given away half of their winnings over the years.

The couple have set up the PFC Trust, which funds good causes across the North East of England.

  Frances Connolly, who won £115m on the lottery in 2019, as she launches her latest charity venture via her charity foundation the PFC Trust at Middlesbrough Velodrome. 

*(Image: TOM WILKINSON/ PA WIRE)*

Frances, a former social worker and teacher, said helping others was simply human nature.

The grandmother-of-three said: “It depends on what you want in life. If your ambition is to be the richest person in the world and be influencing presidents, then you need to keep all your money, or you need to keep a lot of your money.

“But I don’t know anybody who doesn’t share something.

“I think it’s in mankind to share when you get to a level where you’ve got enough.

“’Enough’ is a different level for everybody.

“Maybe if we had been younger I’d have kept more, maybe you’d be a bit more nervous about how much you could give away, but unless I intended to live forever – I was 50-whatever when I won it – so even being generous, I was well over halfway through.

“So as long as the kids are sorted and they had enough to live comfortable, nice lives, I didn’t see the point.”

  Frances Connolly, who won £115m on the lottery in 2019, as she launches her latest charity venture via her charity foundation the PFC Trust at Middlesbrough Velodrome. 

*(Image: TOM WILKINSON/ PA WIRE)*

Frances grew up in Northern Ireland and had been helping others since childhood, long before her lottery win changed her life.

Her father was a fan of country and western music, with Dolly Parton among his favourites.

Frances described the singer, who died last month aged 80, as an “inspiration” for her philanthropy.

It was reported after Parton’s death that she could have been a billionaire had she not given so much of her wealth to causes including child literacy.

Despite their huge fortune, Frances and Paddy remain conscious of value for money.

  Frances Connolly, who won £115m on the lottery in 2019, as she launches her latest charity venture via her charity foundation the PFC Trust at Middlesbrough Velodrome. 

*(Image: TOM WILKINSON/ PA WIRE)*

Paddy continues to run plastics businesses while the couple enjoy holidays and live in a large house on Teesside.

They have even bought a second-hand Cartier watch and a used Aston Martin.

Frances said: “We have a lovely life.

“I always tell people, we have the same life, we just live it bigger.

“You know, we haven’t particularly changed, if you talk to my friends.”

  Frances Connolly, who won £115m on the lottery in 2019, as she launches her latest charity venture via her charity foundation the PFC Trust at Middlesbrough Velodrome. 

*(Image: TOM WILKINSON/ PA WIRE)*

Seven athletes will take on gruelling challenges to raise money for the PFC Trust and mark Frances’s 60th birthday.

The 60 for 60 Challenge will see the athletes run, swim or cycle a total of 60 miles.

It begins with a family fun day at Middlesbrough Velodrome on Sunday, September 6 followed by tough events including the Great North Run.

Frances said: “They wanted to put something back into the community, and I’m delighted, I couldn’t have been given a better present.

“I am at pains to explain to people that despite being a millionaire, I can’t cure poverty, and I certainly can’t do everything that’s needed to be done on my own.

“I have a team of hundreds of people who work with me all the time.

“I put a message out – ‘we’re having a fun day on Sunday’ – there’s people baking cakes for us, people turning up to sell raffle tickets.

“We have 50 volunteers that come on the night of our ball.

“I get help and support every single day.

“I think people just need to be asked, and they just need to have that opportunity to do exactly what I do and change lives.”

  Frances Connolly, who won £115m on the lottery in 2019, as she launches her latest charity venture via her charity foundation the PFC Trust at Middlesbrough Velodrome. 

*(Image: TOM WILKINSON/ PA WIRE)*

The trust has bought respite caravans for young carers, funded loneliness projects and provided digital technology for older people. It has also delivered emergency food parcels and installed defibrillators at local sports clubs.

Paul Hewitson, who will run the Great North Run for the trust, said: “She has set such an example, and we wanted to help through sport, as we all know how vital it is for young people to live healthier lives.”

Donate at justgiving.com/campaign/60for60challenge.

The trust has also given Hartlepool teenager Lewin Tubuna a £1,000 grant towards competing at the Teenage CrossFit Games world finals in the United States.
