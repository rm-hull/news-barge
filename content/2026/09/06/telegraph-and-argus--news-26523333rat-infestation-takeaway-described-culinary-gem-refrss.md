A BRADFORD takeaway which markets itself as a "culinary gem" was forced to close after food hygiene inspectors reported a rat infestation.

A public complaint also alleged that a spider was found in a bag of collected food.

Abaseen Peshawari, on Willow Street in Girlington, was temporarily shut down by Bradford Council following after an inspection on January 8.

The food establishment was slapped with a one-star food hygiene rating and has since reopened for business.

## A 'flagship destination for Pakistani cuisine'

The takeaway’s website describes itself as a "flagship destination for Pakistani cuisine".

The website states: "Abaseen Peshawari Chapal Kebabs, nestled in the heart of Bradford at 16 Willow St, is a culinary gem for lovers of authentic Pakistani flavors.

"Known primarily for its signature chapli kebabs, this eatery blends tradition with generosity, offering some of the largest and most flavorful kebabs in the city.

"Each kebab bursts with a harmonious mix of spices, expertly balanced to satisfy both purists and new customers seeking genuine Peshawari taste.

"What truly stands out here is the consistency and care reflected in every dish.

"From the succulent chapli kebab to the comforting chicken karahi and the fresh, soft naan, each bite speaks of culinary craftsmanship."

The menu extends beyond kebabs, featuring masala fish and omelettes and Kawaa - a green mint tea that restaurant claims "elevates the dining experience".

## 'An active rodent infestation'

During the inspection environmental health officers found "an active rodent infestation" during their visit, with droppings present in both the storage area and open kitchen.

Contamination was found on food equipment, shelving, pallets, plug sockets and a dough mixer, creating what the council deemed "a significant risk of physical and microbial food contamination."

The report continued: "Clean food equipment and containers were being stored in a filthy large bowl beneath your equipment sink.

"There was an active rodent infestation within the kitchen and food storage area."

## Risk to public health

The premises were judged to be "an imminent risk to public health."

The business voluntarily agreed to close, and was not permitted to reopen its kitchen without written approval from the council.

Officers also found dirt and grease on touchpoints and beneath equipment, and said daily pest-control and opening and closing checks had not been properly carried out, despite records showing checks on earlier days.

The business was instructed to complete a full deep clean and disinfection of the kitchen, storage area, food-contact surfaces and equipment; remove all rodent droppings; clean storage containers; address the pest issue; and ensure relevant staff are properly trained to carry out monitoring and corrective-action procedures.

Abaseen Peshawari were approached for a comment but did not respond.
