## North Yorkshire luxury furniture firm welcomes third generation as apprentice

Riley Skinner has joined the family firm as an apprentice. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

A Harrogate family firm known for its luxury furniture is welcoming a new apprentice and the next generation of craftsmanship.

Riley Skinner began his apprenticeship on September 1 at Reeves Upholstery House, where he will work alongside his father, Mark Skinner, and his stepmother, Michaela Reeves.

The family business, based in Harrogate, has been producing bespoke upholstered furniture since 1979 and is known for creating pieces featured in luxury lottery prize homes.

Michaela Reeves said: "People might recognise our furniture from the beautiful lottery houses they see on television, but what they don’t always realise is that it comes from a family workshop here in Harrogate.

"We’re incredibly proud of those projects, but seeing Riley start his apprenticeship alongside his dad and me is special in a completely different way.

"My late dad, Ken, was a master upholsterer, and the care and pride he put into his work are things we want to pass on.

"There are no shortcuts to learning a trade like this.

"Riley is starting from the beginning, learning the skills properly and working towards becoming a master of the craft in his own right.

"I think Dad would have been so proud to see the next generation picking up the tools.

"For us, this isn’t simply about keeping a business going — it’s about keeping the knowledge, craftsmanship and family tradition alive."

Riley’s apprenticeship marks the start of a new chapter, as he learns the art of upholstery from the ground up.

He will be trained in restoring cherished furniture and creating custom pieces, guided by the expertise of his father and the wider Reeves team.

The business has supplied furniture for lottery houses for the past four years.

In addition to its high-profile projects, Reeves Upholstery House offers a range of reupholstery services to both domestic and commercial clients.

The team also creates bespoke furniture, headboards, and cushions.

Michaela Reeves said that while the lottery house projects are a point of pride, Riley’s decision to join the workshop is meaningful on a more personal level.

She hopes that by working alongside his father and stepmother, Riley will not only master the craft but also carry forward the values and tradition that have shaped the business for more than 40 years.
