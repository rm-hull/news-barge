Artists and creative workers are calling on Bradford Council to reconsider plans to divert £400,000 of arts funding.

The money, originally earmarked for cultural events and organisations, is now set to be used to reopen Sugden End Household Waste Recycling Centre and restore bank holiday openings at tips across the district.

Bradford Council has said the decision "does not affect current cultural investment commitments," but artists have criticised the move as "short-sighted".

Sue Dyde, a director of the women's performance troupe Bradford Belles, has launched a petition and organised a protest outside City Hall ahead of the executive meeting on Tuesday, where the plan could be signed off.

  The tip is due to reopen due to public demand 

*(Image: Staff)*

Ms Dyde told the BBC: "This is about the regeneration and a future for Bradford, not about just opening one tip.

"The buoyancy in Bradford as a result of the City of Culture was palpable.

"You know what you're losing and what you're gaining.

"It's perverse.

"It's a false economy."

She urged the council’s Reform UK leadership to "put the brakes on" and consult with those who understand the long-term impact.

Dave Gill, from community street band Peace Artistes, said the funding supports grassroots organisations that engage new audiences and attract visitors to the city.

Artist Morwenna Catt emphasised that while waste services are important, the arts sector generates revenue.

  Morwenna Catt of Ministry of Bees the 2019 Bradford Festival 

*(Image: Staff)*

The money was approved under the previous Labour administration in March, with half earmarked for events and festivals and the remainder for supporting cultural organisations.

Bradford Council said the funding is "not part of the council's existing culture budget and does not affect current cultural investment commitments or the development of future cultural funding programmes already being planned."

The Labour group has supported calls for a rethink, arguing that cultural events boost footfall and spending in town centres.

However, it also welcomed the reopening of the Sugden End tip, noting that improved council finances have made the move possible.
