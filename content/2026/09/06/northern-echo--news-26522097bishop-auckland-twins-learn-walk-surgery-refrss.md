Two Bishop Auckland twins had to learn how to walk again after undergoing major spinal surgery, years after their mum noticed their shoulders were at different heights.

Kate and Lucy Maughan were diagnosed with scoliosis at the age of 13, beginning a difficult period of hospital appointments, restrictive back braces and separate eight hour operations to straighten their spines.

Now 21, the sisters have rebuilt active lives and are preparing to take on the Great North Run together next weekend to raise money for Spinal Research.

Their mum Helen, who works as a nurse, first became concerned after noticing their shoulders were uneven.

Scoliosis causes the spine to twist and curve to one side.

  Kate Maugham’s spine before and after spinal fusion surgery. 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

In an effort to stop the curves worsening, Kate and Lucy were fitted with plastic moulded back braces which had to be worn for between 16 and 24 hours a day.

Kate, who is now studying law at Northumbria University as part of a solicitor apprenticeship, said having her twin sister alongside her made a difficult time more manageable.

She said: “It was really hard at that secondary school age to cope with wearing the braces for 16 to 24 hours every day and I’m just glad we had each other.”

After a year, the twins were told the braces had not worked and that they would both need corrective surgery.

Their operations at James Cook University Hospital, Middlesbrough, were due to take place in March 2020.

However, the Covid lockdown delayed the procedures by several months.

When they were 15, the sisters underwent spinal fusion operations on consecutive days.

The complex procedures each took around eight hours and involved metal rods and screws being inserted on either side of their spines.

They spent five days in paediatric intensive care before returning home to begin their recovery.

  Lucy Mauaghan just after her corrective surgery with twin sister Kate. 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Kate said: “The surgery involved us lying facing down so we both had very puffy faces afterwards.”

“It felt so strange sitting up straight, then standing and having to literally learn to walk again.”

“It was really hard to navigate all this just before going into Year 11 to do our GCSEs. Sitting for any length of time in the beginning and leaning forward was a real struggle.”

“But we are so grateful to our school and all our family and friends who gave us so much support and we have made really good recoveries.”

The pair have since returned to an active lifestyle.

Lucy studied pharmacology at Newcastle University, where she competed in powerlifting, and now works as a microbiologist for GlaxoSmithKline.

Kate is studying law while completing a solicitor apprenticeship.

Lucy said: “We have always been pretty active, especially after finding out we had scoliosis, and that’s really helped to get us through the tougher times.”

After watching last year’s Great North Run, the sisters decided to take on the 13.1 mile challenge themselves and use the occasion to raise money for Spinal Research.

They have previously completed the Darlington 10K, but say the famous route from Newcastle to South Shields will be their biggest running challenge yet.

Lucy said: “It was only after watching last year’s Great North Run that we both decided we wanted to challenge ourselves and raise money for Spinal Research, to help others who can’t walk or run.”

“We have done the Darlington 10K but this will be something else and I’m hoping all that positive energy from the crowds will get us over the line.”

Suzanne Redding, head of audience and communications at Spinal Research, said: “A spinal cord injury can happen in so many ways, from a traumatic injury to illnesses and random accidents.

“After decades of funding the best research and researchers we are now at a point where breakthrough therapies are coming through, where the impossible is becoming possible.”

“What we urgently need is the funds to get these treatments to paralysed people who need them. Which is why we are so grateful to Kate and Lucy, and all our Great North Run Team Spinal runners for supporting Spinal Research.”
