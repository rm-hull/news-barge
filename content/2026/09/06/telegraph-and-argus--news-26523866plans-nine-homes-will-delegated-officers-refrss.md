PROPOSED new housing developments in Craven of up to nine homes will soon be automatically decided by officers and not elected councillors, heard a meeting in Gargrave.

Changes to the National Planning Policy Framework (NPPF) recently received by North Yorkshire Council and shared with councillors detail national policy for the determination of planning applications.

Councillor Simon Myers, who sits on the council executive and who represents Mid Craven, told Gargrave Parish Council that the council would have to accept the changes, which will start at the end of October.

"The changes to the NPPF planning system that are coming through we really don't have any choice about. They include applications of up to nine houses will no longer be determined by the planning committee. If you are sitting in London, nine houses doesn't seem many, but if you are in a village in the Dales, nine houses can seem a lot."

Currently, ward councillors can 'call in' planning applications if there is sufficient reason to be determined by the Skipton and Ripon Planning Committee, which sits in Skipton or Ripon, and where members of the public can go along and have their say. That will no longer be possible under the new system, although people will still be able to register their concerns during the consultation periods."

Other changes to the NPFF include reserved matters applications for developments of up to 500 new homes which have had outline planning approved, will also be delegated to officers.

The national scheme of delegation splits planning applications into two 'schedules'. Schedule one applications must be delegated to officers for a decision, except when the application has been made by a councillor or officer; and schedule two applications will also be delegated to officers unless it is agreed by an officer and councillor that it should go to committee.

Applications that go before councillors will have to have one or more issues of economic, social, or environmental significance to the area, or at least one significant planning matter to do with the development plan.

A report to councillors by Martin Grainger, head of development management, and Catriona Gatrell, head of legal, says: "It is important that this process does not lead to delays in the determination of applications so that the council can continue to meet Government targets for the speed of decision making. The council is monitored on its performance in this respect and the Government has recently intervened elsewhere in the country where targets have not been met."

The report also encourages the saving of searches on the council's planning portal to ensure that notifications are received when applications are validated.
