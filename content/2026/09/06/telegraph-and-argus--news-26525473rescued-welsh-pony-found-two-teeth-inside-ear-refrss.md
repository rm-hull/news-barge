A Welsh pony rescued in Bradford had two teeth growing inside one of his ears.

King, a Welsh pony, was found tethered beside a road after members of the public raised concerns about his welfare.

He was discovered by Sarah Tucker, a field worker for World Horse Welfare, after emergency services had reportedly been called to the animal several times. King had been described as underweight and in need of urgent help.

The two-year-old pony was taken to World Horse Welfare’s Penny Farm Rescue and Rehoming Centre near Blackpool in October last year. He is now three years old and is being prepared for rehoming because his original owner did not come forward.

During his rehabilitation, staff noticed a lump near the base of King’s ear appeared to be causing him discomfort. Further checks showed that the lump was not an abscess, as first suspected, but a dentigerous cyst.

The condition is a congenital birth defect in which misplaced tooth tissue develops near the base of an animal’s ear. It is also known as an “ear tooth”.

Centre manager Adam Cummins said: "When we started to investigate the lump near the base of his ear, we found out that it wasn't an abscess at all.

"Instead, it was a rare developmental defect called a dentigerous cyst, also known as an 'ear tooth'.

"These are already uncommon but King's case was on another level, as he had two 'ear teeth' which is even more unusual."

Images released by the charity show the two growths after they were removed. They were yellow, with patches of red blood, and measured about two-and-a-half centimetres in length. One photograph shows the larger growth being held by a surgeon wearing gloves.

World Horse Welfare said dentigerous cysts can cause life-long pain if they are not treated. They can also lead to complications and abscesses.

King was placed under general anaesthetic before surgery at Oakhill Vets in Goosnargh, Lancashire. The operation was carried out by surgeon Guy Hinnigan, who worked for several hours to remove the growths.

Vet Kai Cahun said: "Our diplomat surgeon, Guy Hinnigan, worked for several hours to remove them, while navigating the many vital structures in the area and avoiding any damage to the nerves responsible for facial movement and ear function.

"In most cases, the abnormally developed tooth is relatively small compared to typical horse teeth.

"However, in King's case he had not one but two almost completely normal-sized teeth that had grown inside his skull."

Vet Kai Cahun said the practice had seen several similar cases over the years but "none with two very large teeth involved".

Vet Kai Cahun said: "King's case was certainly one of the most bizarre we've experienced."

The charity said King recovered after the operation and has since been nursed back to health at Penny Farm. Staff are now looking for a suitable new home for him.

His rescue followed several incidents involving the pony. He had slid down an embankment into a ditch and had also escaped and run on to a main road.

King's rescuer Sarah Tucker said: "I found out that the emergency services had been called out to King multiple times.

"He'd needed rescuing after sliding down an embankment into a ditch, as well as getting loose and running onto a main road.

"With his owner nowhere to be found, it was clear King needed to be taken to safety."

The charity is continuing to care for King while arrangements are made for his rehoming.

It said his owner had failed to come forward.

The charity’s photographs include an image of King tethered on grassland beside a road, with trees and shrubs behind him. Another shows the removed growths alongside a ruler.
