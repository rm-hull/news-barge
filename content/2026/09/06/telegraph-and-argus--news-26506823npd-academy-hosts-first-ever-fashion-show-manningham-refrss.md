A Bradford sewing school hosted its first ever children's fashion show in Manningham.

On Saturday, August 29 the T&A headed over to see seven girls show off their handmade creations.

The girls spent their summer at the Summer Sew Club perfecting their end-of-project garments.

Neelam Patel, 30, director of the NPD Academy in Bradford and fashion show host, was responsible for the day.

She said some of the girls had upcycled previous outfits, a process which requires knowing which materials can be reused and garments taken apart, adding that sewing your own clothes is “definitely” a slower-fashion alternative to buying new.

*(Image: T&A)*

Mrs Patel originally hails from Nuneaton, but moved to Bradford five years ago to be with her husband.

She set up her own sewing academy to inspire the next generation of designers in the city that was once described as the 'Wool Capital of the World', owing to its booming wool and worsted textile trade, particularly in the 19th century.

  One of the young designers modelling 

*(Image: T&A)*

## Mrs Patel's journey into design

Mrs Patel did not grow up with sewing, but after studying it at college and university, it felt natural to keep on doing it.

She's now set on spreading the joy and satisfaction of creating clothing from scratch to the people of Bradford, and beyond.

  The NPD Academy hard at work 

*(Image: The NPD Academy)*

But it was by no means a straightforward road to success.

At 16, her mum, despite not being a seamstress herself, encouraged Mrs Patel to take up the craft.

She explains that she did not come from background of tailoring, adding there was "no one teaching me to do it", but this did not stop her.

Mrs Patel told the T&A: "I tried it once, realised it was absolutely what I wanted to do."

  Another young seamstress taking to the catwalk 

*(Image: T&A)*

She studied the Western style of tailoring at college but wanted to get into the "Asian side of tailoring" but was struggling and realised she couldn't be the only person keen to learn the Asian techniques.

She said: "There must be other people like me too so slowly, slowly I have curated these courses".

  Two of the girls wearing their handmade costumes 

*(Image: T&A)*

She has since created text books so that adults and children can learn to sew in order to "keep the heritage alive".

When referring to the "Asian side of tailoring", Mrs Patel explains she means that "a lot of the silhouettes are quite different, the techniques and most importantly, the fabrics."

She says heavier fabrics require different techniques.

Of course, Asia contains many distinct textile traditions, often centred on hand stitching, embroidery, repair, quilting and decorative surface work, rather than a fundamentally different way of joining fabric altogether.

## Her advice?

Mrs Patel started from a tiny square foot bedroom with just a bed, a machine and a door and made her dream a reality, so she knows others can too.

She said: "I know it's definitely possible to not come from a background of sewing.

"It's definitely not as scary, it looks impossible to reach."

She would encourage anyone interested to give it a go and try out one of her classes.
