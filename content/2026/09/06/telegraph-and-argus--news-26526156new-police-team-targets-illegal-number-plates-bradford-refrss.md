A new national police team will target illegal number plates used to dodge fines and support organised crime in West Yorkshire.

The Department for Transport is funding the National Roads Policing Coordination Centre to improve enforcement and cooperation between police forces.

The centre will bring police forces together to share intelligence, collaborate more closely and use data more effectively.

It will also test technology that spots plates altered to evade cameras.

One example is specialised goggles that reveal number plates criminals have tried to hide from automatic number plate recognition cameras.

Illegal plates help drivers dodge speeding fines, parking penalties and clean air zone charges.

They are also linked to organised crime, including the movement of illegal goods by road.

Vehicles hidden from number plate cameras are harder for police to trace after thefts or serious offences.

Better detection should help officers find suspects sooner and reduce crime.

Roads Minister Justin Madders said: “We’re cracking down on illegal number plates with new policing resource and innovative tech.”

He said: “Illegal number plates are used for crimes from evading parking charges to drug trafficking and we’re stepping in to protect drivers.”

The Government said the work is part of its Road Safety Strategy, the first in more than a decade.

The strategy aims to save thousands of lives.

The strategy included consultation on tougher penalties for people using illegal plates.

It also considered tighter number plate standards and better enforcement.

Illegal plates come in several forms.

Cloned plates copy another vehicle’s genuine registration, making the car involved harder to identify.

Ghost plates use transparent film to make the registration hard for cameras to read.

Some drivers use vehicles with no number plates at all.

Drivers displaying number plates incorrectly can be fined up to £1,000.

Chief Superintendent Marc Clothier, the NRPCC’s deputy national coordinator, said: “Through the National Roads Policing Coordination Centre, our mission is to tackle the biggest risks to road safety, enhancing and developing the police response to criminality both on and off the roads.”

He said: “The police forces involved in our ghost plates operation have shown how effective a targeted and intelligence-led approach is in tackling offenders as well as educating and supporting drivers in ensuring their numberplates are compliant.”

The operation will help forces share tactics and find more uses for technology.

Police will also use intelligence to target offenders and businesses supplying illegal plates.

Mr Clothier said: “Thanks to the funding from DfT, we have a valuable opportunity to support road safety and roads policing across the UK with enhanced national coordination, consistency, collaboration and communication across policing, government and the wider road safety community.”

Policing and Crime Minister Sarah Jones said: “Illegal number plates are not a victimless offence. They are used by criminals to avoid detection, move illegal goods and stay one step ahead of law enforcement.”

She said: “By bringing forces together and making better use of technology and intelligence, this new team will help police track down offenders faster and disrupt organised crime groups that rely on our road network.”

The NRPCC’s work builds on efforts by the Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency and Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency.

Both agencies are working with police to improve how illegal plates are identified and tackled.

Their work includes supporting investigations into businesses that illegally supply number plates.

They are also helping police carry out roadside operations.

The Government said the centre would support enforcement across England, while Chief Superintendent Clothier described a wider UK role.

The work aims to improve information sharing between police, government and road-safety organisations.

Ministers said the focus would be on drivers using illegal plates to dodge penalties and organised crime groups using the roads.

The enforcement is part of wider efforts to make roads safer and help police tackle serious offences.
