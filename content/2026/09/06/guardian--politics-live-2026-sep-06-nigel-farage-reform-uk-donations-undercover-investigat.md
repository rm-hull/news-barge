Zia Yusuf’s post about Camden council leader is ‘straightforward racism’, minister says

Zia Yusuf, Reform UK’s home affairs spokesperson, has been accused of “pretty straightforward racism” by a Cabinet minister over comments about a potential Labour byelection candidate who was born in Somalia.

Yusuf wrote on X last week: “The front runner being foisted on the people of Holborn by Labour to replace the former Prime Minister Keir Starmer in our Parliament was born in Somalia. Do you understand what’s happening?”

The business secretary, Jonathan Reynolds, was asked what he thought of Yusuf’s tweet during an interview with Jon Sopel on Sky News this morning.

“Pretty straightforward racism, isn’t it?” Reynolds said, adding that the potential Labour candidate had “spent decades in this country” and made a “huge contribution to her local community”.

Minister says no bailout for Jaguar Land Rover amid job cut reports

Thousands of workers at Jaguar Land Rover (JLR) will be offered redundancies with job losses reported to be spread over two years. The job cuts will be unwelcome news for Andy Burnham, who has vowed to “safeguard sovereign manufacturing” and reindustrialise Britain.

When asked if the government would be willing to protect the jobs, the business secretary, Jonathan Reynolds, said it is not for him to “intervene” as he isn’t responsible for running JLR’s business.

“They need to know what the right footprint for them going forward is. I want to talk to them about the numbers,” Reynolds told the BBC, adding there could be financial support for long-term investment but not for a taxpayer-funded bailout.

Nigel Farage to be questioned as he battles to contain growing donations scandal

Good morning and welcome to our live coverage of UK politics. The Reform UK party leader, Nigel Farage, is scheduled to appear in an interview on Laura Kuenssberg’s Sunday politics programme at 9am, where he will likely be asked more about an undercover investigation broadcast by Channel 4 News which showed two of his senior aides appearing to plot to get around electoral laws on foreign donations.

Accepting donations from a foreign company, including donations in kind such as polling, could constitute a criminal offence and the party has been reported to the police.

Senior aide Dan Jukes and Reform’s head of policy James Orr have both stood down pending the outcome of an internal party investigation.

As my colleagues note in the story, Farage himself remains under pressure over a separate clip in which he was heard saying “it’s all above board” after one of the reporters who was posing as the UK-based son of a wealthy US backer told the Reform leader he had been in discussions with Jukes and Orr for some time.

“I’ll be the registered donor,” the son says. “But then dad will send the money to me, and then as soon as that’s done and all those conversations are had the money is available.”
