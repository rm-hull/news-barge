## Vineyards near York talk of bumper harvest as picking is about to start

The Divico grapes are almost ready to harvest at the Goose Beck Vineyard. *(Image: Pic supplied)i>*

Vineyards near York are gearing up for what could be their biggest harvest yet, with grape picking starting as soon as this coming week.

After a season of hot, dry weather followed by recent rain, grape growers say conditions are ideal for a bumper crop.

Tim Spakouskas, managing director of Nun Monkton-based Yorkshire Heart Vineyard, said: "The recent rain has played a major part in helping to swell the grapes in this year's harvest.

"It is looking good to be our biggest and best harvest to date, surpassing record figures from 2025 harvest."

Picking is due to begin on Monday, September 14, starting with early-ripening varieties such as Siegerebbe, Solaris, Rondo, and Ortega.

  Lisa Chasney of the Goose Beck Vineyard checks the sugar content of the grapes. 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

Harvesting is expected to take place three to four days a week over four to five weeks.

Mr Spakouskas said: "The vines have loved the hot dry weather this summer, producing lots of bunches and ripening earlier than usual.

  Tim Spakouskas of the Yorkshire Heart Vineyard and Brewery gathers last year's harvest 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

"With the hot dry weather we have very low disease pressure, so mildew and botrytis have not been an issue this year, meaning the grapes will be coming in with plenty of juice and in great condition."

Yorkshire Heart relies heavily on volunteers to bring in the crop.

  Peter Townsend and his father Ian at the Dunesforde vineyard in the Vale of York 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Mr Spakouskas said: "They get well fed and watered and get to take a bottle of wine home with them at the end of the day."

Not all vineyards have had an easy season.

Paul Chasney of Goose Beck Vineyard in Bilton-in-Ainsty said: "As a young vineyard, 2026 has provided some significant challenges.

"At Goose Beck we experienced late frosts which set the vines back but of course the warm sunny weather has helped them recover.

  The Yorkshire Heart Vineyard last year 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

"However, the very dry conditions are difficult for young vines as they do not have the deep root system that older vines develop.

"Our oldest vines have largely managed in the drought conditions but those under three years old have suffered and many are showing signs of stress."

Harvest at Goose Beck is expected to begin its harvest in around 7 to 10 days.

Mr Chasney said: "We have a great team of volunteers who helped us last year and during the course of this year a number of others have come forward."

Dunesforde Vineyard in Upper Dunsforth was due to start picking on Thursday but was delayed by rain. It will now start on Monday, weather permitting.

Owner Peter Townsend said: "It's looking like a good year.

"We had an excellent year last year.

"This year has been very similar.

"I suspect we will be in for a very big harvest.

"Quality is looking great.

"There have been very few problems this year."

Dunesforde is also seeking volunteers, who can contact the vineyard through the volunteers section of its website.
