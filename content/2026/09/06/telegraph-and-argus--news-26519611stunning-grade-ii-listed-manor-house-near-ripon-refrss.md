  *Step inside the stunning Grade II-listed Manor House near Ripon – a property perfect for family gatherings and making special memories*

Nestled in the picturesque village of Wath, Manor House, with its sumptuous interiors and perfectly manicured grounds, is the epitome of a dream home.

The Falkingham family purchased the property in 2006, and it is now enjoyed by others looking for a luxurious Yorkshire escape.

Elegant design, light-filled rooms and expansive grounds create a welcoming space for gatherings and get-togethers.

‘Now that the children are mostly grown up, we particularly value it as a place to bring everyone back together, and it’s equally rewarding to share it so other families or groups of friends can enjoy time together, making memories or celebrating,’ says owner Nick Falkingham.

‘The house has evolved over a period of approximately 500 years, initially as a rectory and later known as The Old Rectory.

   

*(Image: Nicholas Yarsley)*

‘It was previously owned by the Bemrose family. Many local friends remember coming here during the 1970s and ’80s, and their stories are often prefaced with:

“Ah, you live in the Bemrose’s house.”

‘Tulip Bemrose was largely responsible for the gardens we enjoy today. She was a prolific gardener and later moved to Masham.

‘After the Bemrose family, the house changed hands a couple of times before we bought it from the Reed family of Reed Publishing – Denys and Trish Reed.’

Nick credits Tulip – who also designed the famous walled herb garden at Tupgill Park in Leyburn – with the woodland planting, which includes snowdrops, crocuses, daffodils and bluebells, as well as the structure of the walled garden.

‘There is colour in the garden for much of the year,’ he adds.

On the design of the house, Nick says: ‘We designed it primarily for ourselves and believed that if we liked it, others might too. That has proved to be the case.

   

*(Image: Nicholas Yarsley)*

‘It’s often used for small, intimate celebrations, where people value the privacy, space and atmosphere of an English country house and gardens.’

He adds: ‘The house is Grade II-listed, so there are limits to what can be done. In truth, it was already very well resolved when we arrived, so we didn’t need to make major changes.

‘It was mainly about ensuring it functioned practically, including making sure bedrooms have lockable

wardrobe sections so the house can accommodate guests without disruption.’

When creating a vision for Manor House, Nick’s starting point came from reference images he used to build a mood board, and he also drew on his vast experience of working across the globe to bring a modern edge to the listed property, while retaining its historic character and charm.

‘Through my time owning N.Peal and Pure Collection, I spent many years working on photo shoots, often in carefully selected houses and locations around the world,’ he says.

   

*(Image: Nicholas Yarsley)*

‘Being exposed to those environments – and how they are composed, lit and lived in – gives you a sense of what works visually and why certain spaces feel appealing.

‘The aim was to bring a level of modern sophistication into a period rectory while remaining true to its character.

‘My wife [Anne-Marie] has more recently taken on a number of areas, particularly some of the bedrooms and softer elements, such as fabrics.

‘She also led the redevelopment of the apartment above the stables, where furniture from a ski chalet she previously owned has been incorporated.

‘It’s effectively our bolthole when we are in Yorkshire and have guests staying in the main house.

‘Before that, I worked with TOR Interiors, where

Tor Vivian helped translate those references into a cohesive scheme.’

Some of the initial direction was influenced by the work of interior designer Emma Sims-Hilditch, known for her modern interpretation of the English living aesthetic.

‘Much of the art has been sourced through Saatchi Art, alongside pieces Anne-Marie has collected,’ says Nick.

   

*(Image: Nicholas Yarsley)*

There are many lovely features in Manor House, which add to its charm.

Nick says: ‘The high ceilings and large Georgian windows make the house very airy and light.

‘The ancillary buildings adjoining form a cloister-like passage leading to log sheds, storage and the laundry room. Most of the windows have either wood-panelled enclaves or shutters.

‘The drawing room has a curved, built-in bookcase wall, which is a very characterful feature, and the French windows open directly onto the terrace. It’s a particularly peaceful room, especially on warm summer days.

‘The African-themed sunroom has two sets of French windows, which create a natural flow of air and make it a cooler space in the heat.

He adds: ‘We have preserved all of the existing features. We did expose a fireplace in the kitchen and install a log burner, which makes the kitchen very comfortable in winter.’

What does Nick love about the house?

‘The light and the space. It’s a large house, but it’s used fully,’ he says.

‘The grounds are a big part of it. Everything feels proportionate, with just the right amount of space.

‘Even when the house is full of family or guests, it absorbs people easily, so it can still feel calm and quiet.’

The local area adds to Manor House’s appeal, with Nick enjoying walking, riding out and spending time in the landscape.

He adds: ‘Beyond that – cycling, golf, fishing and eating out – there is a lot within easy reach, as well as simply spending time in the garden.’

   

*(Image: Nicholas Yarsley)*

  **Inside the house**

Entering the house, a beautiful hallway leads to a modern, bright, fully equipped kitchen with an Aga, a granite island and a kitchen table. Private chefs are available to book for dinner parties.

There is access from the hall and kitchen to a large, light dining room with tall sash windows and an open fireplace overlooking the gardens.

Outdoor dining is also an option, with a gas barbecue at guests’ disposal, as well as a large dining table and chairs on the patio. Weddings and events are possible here.

On the first floor, there are four bedrooms; a double, two kings – one with a zip and link bed – plus a master bedroom with a super-king. All are en-suite. On the second floor, there are three double bedrooms. One has an en-suite with a double bed and two single beds, while in the final two bedrooms – which share a family bathroom with a shower and bath – one has a double bed, and the other has a zip and link bed. There is an additional one-bedroom flat available within the grounds.

During a stay, guests have access to the full house and grounds, which boast a beautiful walled garden and outdoor sun loungers to relax on.

Inside, there is a conservatory room with a large L-shaped sofa and comfortable armchairs, a log burner and television with French windows leading out to the terrace.

This room also leads out to a covered outside passage to the front of the house, where there are more beautiful gardens to enjoy. The Drawing Room, with its large comfy sofas and open fireplace, is a room perfect for entertaining, with a snooker table for games nights. There is a hot tub, which can be booked for an extra fee.

   

*(Image: Nicholas Yarsley)*

  **Spotlight on the area**

Located in the heart of North Yorkshire, Manor House, which sleeps 16 to 18 people, is in a prime position to explore the best of all this corner of the world has to offer – Ripon, Masham, Pateley Bridge, Thirsk, Knaresborough and Harrogate are all easily accessible by car.

There are many enjoyable walking and cycling routes, as well as attractions close by, including Lightwater Valley, Fountains Abbey, Newby Hall, and the Himalayan Garden and Sculpture Park.

For guests with horses, Manor House also has stables that can be hired by separate arrangement. The property is dog-friendly, with well-behaved pets welcome..
