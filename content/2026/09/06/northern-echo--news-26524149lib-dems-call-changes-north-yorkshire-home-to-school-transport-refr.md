North Yorkshire opposition councillors are asking for three three immediate changes to the authority’s home-to-school transport policy amid claims families continue to be unfairly affected.

Liberal Democrats have made the call ahead of the publication of North Yorkshire Council’s post-implementation review (PIR) of the policy next week.

The Lib Dems say the review must not result in further delays after two years of concerns being raised by parents, schools and councillors.

The party ultimately wants the policy changed so children qualify for free transport to either their nearest suitable school or their catchment school.

However, councillors say another intake of pupils is now set to be affected by the existing arrangements and want three discretionary measures introduced in the meantime.

The first would provide a safeguard for pupils where travelling to their designated nearest school involves routes at high elevation, with the Lib Dems suggesting a possible threshold of 400 metres.

The second relates to council-contracted buses which already serve more than one school.

The group says eligible pupils should be allowed to use such services to attend either school, citing buses serving Richmond School and St Francis Xavier School, as well as Ripon Grammar School and Outwood Academy Ripon.

The third proposal concerns children whose nearest suitable school is outside North Yorkshire.

The Lib Dems want the council to also identify their nearest suitable school within the county and use its discretionary powers to provide transport there.

Councillor Andrew Murday said: “The PIR is due on September 8, but parents and schools have already spent two years telling the council where this policy is failing.

“We don’t need another year of delay while County Hall argues about the bigger question. Children are being affected now.

“These three fixes are simple, targeted and can be introduced within the existing framework. They would immediately address some of the most obvious unfairness without waiting for a wholesale policy review.”

Cllr Murday said the party remained committed to a “nearest or catchment” system in the longer term, which he described as simpler and fairer.

“Stop stalling, and start solving. These three quick fixes could be implemented now.”

In response, North Yorkshire council leader Carl Les told the Local Democracy Reporting Service that the policy was changed in a bid to reduce the annual school transport bill of more than £50m.
