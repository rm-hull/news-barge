A COUNCIL in West Yorkshire has assured families that those who need nursing care will still “absolutely” have a say in the care they receive under a new banding system.

During a Health and Adult Social Care Scrutiny Panel, there was discussion about Kirklees Council’s approach to funding independent sector care home fee rates for 2026/27, with concern raised over changes to its payment method to a simplified “banded” model.

The Council oversees areas close to the border with Bradford, including Birkenshaw, Cleckheaton and part of Oakenshaw.

According to the report outlined in the panel, fees paid to care homes are to be based mainly on a resident’s care needs rather than the type of room they have. Kirklees Council then plans to use these rates to pay independent care home businesses directly for a resident’s stay and care. For example, older residents and those with Physical Disability Care will now have funding categorised under a five-band structure.

For residents categorised under Band 1 (Residential), Kirklees Council will pay a base rate of £810.00 per week (£850.00 for dementia schemes), while for those in Band 2 (Nursing), the council will pay £900.00 per week (£910.71 for dementia schemes), with higher care needs in Bands 3-5 funded between £1,050 and £1,350 per week depending on care complexity.

For residents with learning disabilities or mental health care needs, Kirklees Council will pay rates ranging from £900 to £2,899 per week for Bands 1-3 based on care level, while individuals requiring higher-level specialist support in Bands 4-5 will be funded between £2,900 and £4,950 per week.

Fears were raised by Cllr Liz Smaje, who asked the Executive Director for Adults and Health, Michelle Cross, whether recipients’ families and recipients themselves would be able to challenge the band they were assigned to.

Addressing the chamber, Cllr Smaje said: “The banding is applied to someone’s care package, and the care facility is notified. Obviously, we have banding in other areas of the council, and there can be appeals for the banding.

“If a person or a person’s family should be given a different band, is there a process in place where people can say (care needs) haven’t been taken into account?”

In response, Ms Cross assured that this would “absolutely” be a possibility. Ms Cross said: “The reason we’re putting (the band system in place) is to stop the add-ons that sometimes happen within the care sector. It’s about making sure that we’ve got a consistent approach across the whole market, because I mean, again, you’ll appreciate that we haven’t had this model before.”

It is understood that for residents who need nursing care, the NHS pays a separate “Funded Nursing Care” (FNC) contribution, which does not alter the council’s care band assignment.
