A FORGOTTEN rickshaw left to gather dust has found new life as the centrepiece of a unique business in Bradford.

Launched by 29-year-old Ali Md from Thornbury, LA Rickshaw has transformed a traditional Bangladeshi rickshaw into a floral showpiece for special events.

## Where do I start...

Mr Md told the T&A: "So where do I start, I purchased this two years ago from Bangladesh and had to pay arm and a leg for it to get exported here.

"My daughter arrived into the world, and of course that was more important to us.

  Ali riding on the rickshaw in a park in Bradford 

*(Image: T&A)*

"I left the rickshaw aside, just didn't think anything of it really.

"It gathered dust in my shed and it was starting to rot."

  Ali riding on the rickshaw in a park in Bradford 

*(Image: T&A)*

But Mr Md didn’t forget about the rickshaw completely.

He had been struck by its craftmanship when he first spotted it in Bangladesh and then suddenly an idea came to him.

He said: "I couldn't think of any ideas then one day it came to my mind that why don't I strip it down and turn it into a floral rickshaw that brides and grooms can use for their wedding entrance.

  Ali riding on the rickshaw in a park in Bradford 

*(Image: T&A)*

"It's something unique and different."

Mr Md decided to turn the rickshaw into a vibrant floral display.

## My connection is personal

The rickshaw also holds deep personal meaning for Mr Md, who has roots in Bangladesh.

He said: "I’m from Bangladesh, so my connection is personal rather than simply being a tourist or someone who visited on holiday.

"Rickshaws are a familiar part of life and culture there, so seeing one and knowing its background made it particularly meaningful to me.

"It makes me feel incredibly happy and proud.

  Ali riding on the rickshaw in a park in Bradford 

*(Image: T&A)*

"Something that was once sitting unused now brings people happiness and becomes something they can enjoy and talk about.

"For me, it is also lovely to see an element of Bangladeshi culture being appreciated by people who may not otherwise have experienced it."

Mr Md believes the rickshaw has universal appeal, despite its cultural origins.

He said: "I think the rickshaw can appeal to anyone who appreciates something colourful, unusual and culturally interesting, regardless of their background."

  Ali riding on the rickshaw in a park in Bradford 

*(Image: T&A)*

## His advice to other entrepreneurs

He told the T&A: "My advice would be to go for it if you genuinely believe in the idea. It doesn’t always have to start as a big business plan.

"Sometimes an interesting idea comes from simply seeing potential in something that other people have overlooked.

"Do your research, understand the costs, be prepared for challenges and most importantly, make sure you are doing something you are passionate about."
