A Ukrainian sea drone shot and destroyed a Russian unmanned boat in what Volodymyr Zelensky’s navy says marks the “first-ever naval battle” between maritime drones.

The Ukrainian Sargan 3000 unmanned naval boat identified the Russian drone on the Black Sea and attacked with its powerful 12.7mm turret, the navy said on Telegram.

Footage shared from the Ukrainian drone showed a volley of fire hitting the other boat, which erupted with thick grey smoke before sinking. It was unclear when or exactly where the video was taken.

At least 12 people were killed on Saturday as the war on the ground raged on. Authorities in Ukraine’s Kramatorsk, Zhytomyr, Zaporizhzhia and Kryvyi Rih regions reported fatalities.

The Russian-installed authorities in Ukraine’s occupied Donetsk region also reported that three people were killed in Ukrainian attacks.

Ukraine’s foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said on Sunday that a Russian drone struck near the Ukrainian-Polish border in the small town of Yahodyn overnight.

"This is Putin's terror "knocking" directly on the doors of the EU and Nato. Russian barbarians are at your gate, dear partners," he added.

## Zelensky accuses Russia of expanding war on different fronts

Volodymyr Zelensky says Russia has ballooned its attacks on different frontiers, away from the war zone.

"Basically, the Russians have expanded the war away from the front, where they have no success, into the economy, and their tactics are as basic as it gets: destroying every site that could help people cope with the conditions of war," he wrote on Telegram.

"Russia wants to kill the Ukrainian economy and break our resistance," Zelensky added.

## Why Russia’s new 50-ft Soviet war monument has sparked fury

Russia and Japan are at odds again, this time over a 50-ft granite statue that shows a Soviet soldier using the butt of his rifle to shatter a border post engraved with the Japanese chrysanthemum emblem.

Japan’s prime minister, Sanae Takaichi, demanded that Russia remove the statue.

She said it was “unacceptable to erect a monument depicting the destruction” of the Chrysanthemum Crest, which is widely recognised as a symbol of Japan and the emblem of the Japanese imperial family.

Russia has strongly rejected the demand, framing the monument as legitimate historical memory of the Soviet Union’s 1945 victory over Japan and the end of the Second World War.

## Suspected drone sighting that closed Vilnius airport actually just birds, says crisis management centre

Lithuania said that a perceived drone sighting in its airspace today that saw Vilnius airport closed and prompted Nato to summon a fighter jet turned out to be a flock of birds.

The air alert was lifted and the airport reopened within 38 minutes, after the Nato jet made a visual identification, Lithuania's National Crisis Management Centre said.

"On initial radar readings, marks left by birds and drones can be similar," it added in a statement.

A jet had been summoned from Siauliai in northern Lithuania, the centre said, where the Italian air force is currently based as part of the Nato Baltic Air Defence mission.

## In focus: Ukraine bracing for a brutal winter as Russia steps up attacks on infrastructure

Ukraine is bracing for a brutal winter as intensified Russian attacks on the country's infrastructure and key export industries compound a deepening budget crisis, Ukrainian officials said on Saturday.

Economy Minister Oleksandr Kravchenko said that damage to infrastructure and fixed assets from Russian air strikes was estimated at close to $10 billion this year, while the broader economic costs of the attacks and the de facto blockage of its ports were seen at about 1.5 percentage points of gross domestic product.

"We anticipate a very difficult winter in terms of both critical infrastructure, which is being destroyed every day by Russian attacks, but also in terms of overall economic conditions in the country," Kravchenko said.

"This all comes when the budget and the fiscal space are very constrained," he told investors, officials and diplomats at a YES conference in Kyiv.

With fighting on the front line largely stalled, Russia and Ukraine have increasingly targeted logistics networks and other economic assets in an effort to undermine each other's war effort.

## Sweden, NATO’s newest member, is holding an election. Here’s what is at stake

Voters in Sweden will go to the polls on Sunday in a contest that will decide if the conservative government stays in power, potentially granting a hard-right party its first cabinet seats, or if the centre-left will regain control.

Prime Minister Ulf Kristersson is bidding for a second four-year term leading the European Union country, which recently became NATO’s newest member. His predecessor is attempting to reclaim office, with the battle over leadership largely overshadowing core policy debates.
