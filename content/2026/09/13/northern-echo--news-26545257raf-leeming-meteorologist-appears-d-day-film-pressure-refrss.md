An RAF meteorologist from North Yorkshire earned himself an unexpected cameo in a blockbuster movie after responding to an appeal from film producers searching for a skill that has largely disappeared from modern forecasting.

When producers sought someone who could plot weather charts by hand, one of the station’s senior meteorological officers realised he was the man for the job.

Pressure, released in UK cinemas on Friday, September 11, stars Andrew Scott and Academy Award winner Brendan Fraser.

The film depicts the 1944 clash between Group Captain James Stagg and US Colonel Irving P Krick over the date of Operation Overlord and the weather methods used before thousands of Allied troops landed on Normandy’s beaches.

The Met Office has called the D-Day forecast “the most important weather forecast in military history.”

Meteorologists once plotted weather charts by hand, but digital systems mean few still have the skill.

RAF Leeming senior meteorological officer Bill Shiel Dods joined the Met Office in 1984, when trainees were still taught to plot weather charts by hand.

Mr Shiel Dods has worked for the Met Office for more than 40 years and has forecast for RAF Leeming since 1993.

  Senior Meteorological Officer Bill Shiel Dods at RAF Leeming 

*(Image: AS1 Richard Dilworth - RAF)*

The meteorologist said seeing appeals from film producers two years ago searching for someone capable of making the manual calculations left him curious, as the profession now uses radars and satellites for weather mapping.

He said: “I was sent a two-minute script, and my team videoed me plotting surface charts.

"I sent that off and thought I probably wouldn’t hear anything – a few weeks later I went down to Borehamwood for a costume fitting!”

Filming his background role at Mentmore Towers in Buckinghamshire, Mr Shiel Dods praised the set designers’ painstaking recreation of a traditional forecast room.

“It took me back to when I joined Met Office – it felt utterly authentic and charts looked absolutely spot on from the era,” he said.

“Even the ashtrays were full of cigarette butts!"

"I was given a vintage watch to wear and prescription glasses," he added.

"They even changed my hair because they said I looked too German!”

  Bill Shiel Dods in costume while filming scenes for Pressure 

*(Image: RAF LEEMING)*

The meteorologist was able to get up close to stars Andrew Scott and Brendan Fraser but managed to not get too star-struck.

"People were just so busy and professional, trying to do a, a good job," he said. "The scene I'm in took pretty much a whole day to film."

Mr Shiel Dods said the work shown in Pressure still matters to him and his colleagues today.

He said: “There is a real connection between what we do and what Stagg was doing.

"Met Officers give the best unbiased, unvarnished forecast that we can so that the military can make safe operational decisions.”

He also drew on four decades of experience to reflect on the events shown in the film.

“I can empathise with Stagg and to an extent with Crick as well,” he said.

"They were making decisions which were mission critical, it wasn’t just people’s lives at risk – it was the fate of the rest of the war.”

"I could really put myself in his shoes.”
