Police have being dealing with “significant anti-social behaviour” in York city centre this afternoon and evening.

They have been responding to “individuals causing a nuisance and damage within business premises, climbing scaffolding, and being involved in shop thefts”.

There are also reports of gangs of youths throwing aerosol cans around.

One woman posted that, on Goodramgate, “there’s a large group of kids setting off deodorant bottles to spray everywhere and everyone. Then throwing them up in the air. One nearly landed on my son’s head.”

Another responded that “I saw them and they don’t give two monkeys about anyone else around them, laughing mocking people”.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/york-police-dispersal-order-13-sept-2026-map.webp)

A third saw youngsters “on Lendal Bridge throwing things in the road”

To tackle the problems, police have imposed a Section 34 Dispersal Order until midnight tonight.

This gives officers additional powers to direct individuals who are committing, or are likely to commit, anti-social behaviour to leave the area.

“Anyone who refuses to comply with a direction to leave, or who returns to the area during the period of the order, may be committing a criminal offence and could be arrested,” police said.

“We are appealing directly to parents and guardians to ensure they know where their children are this evening.

“If you believe your child may be among the groups involved, please contact them and arrange for them to leave the city centre.

“Our priority is to keep York safe for everyone. Additional patrols are in the area and officers will continue to take robust action against anyone involved in criminality, anti-social behaviour, disorder or theft.

“We would like to thank local businesses and members of the public for their patience and support while officers deal with the situation.”

They said anyone who witnesses criminal behaviour or has information about those involved should report it on the police website or call 101.

If a crime is in progress or there is an immediate threat to safety, call 999.
