The names of towns around England are usually steeped in history and have been shaped by Celtic, Anglo-Saxon, Viking, and Norman influences.

This can often mean the names are pronounced in strange ways that don’t match up with how they are spelt.

Yorkshire is known for having lots of confusing town and village names including Sowerby Bridge.

If you are visiting Yorkshire or more specifically Sowerby Bridge in particular, here is a guide on how to pronounce the market town’s name.


## How to pronounce Sowerby Bridge

Sowerby Bridge looks like it would be very easy to pronounce but it is more confusing than it seems.

However, if you are visiting the area, you might want to know how the locals say it to avoid any embarrassment.

Sowerby Bridge might look like it is pronounced sow-er-by bridge and arguments can even be made for it being pronounced like sour-by bridge.

But both of these pronunciations are wrong.

The town’s name is actually said like sore-bee bridge.

Bewildered Yorkshire has posted multiple videos on social media to help people with these commonly mispronounced place names.

One person on the video commented saying: “Grew up near Sowerby Bridge.

“Now live near somewhere else with Sowerby in the name, but it’s pronounced Sour-by.

“It causes regular debates.”

Another person replied and said: “My mum was born above Sowerby Bridge in Norland.

“Drives her mad when people call it Sow as in Cow-erby.”


### 
  **What can you do in Sowerby Bridge?**

Sowerby Bridge can be found just on the edge of Halifax making it easy to visit if you are already in the area.

If you are planning on staying in the area, you can stay on the wharf in one of the houseboats owned by Shire Cruisers and sail around the area.

The town is also home to Yorkshire Dama Cheese, who specialise in creating different flavours of halloumi as well as a few spreads and other products that could make an interesting gift.

There are lots of places to eat and relax in the town including The Navigation, a highly rated pub that offers roast dinners and sharing platters.

You can also stop by the Hogs Head Brew House, which is a boutique styled brewery in the heart of Sowerby Bridge where you can try the six ales brewed at the site or try some of the guest beers on offer.
