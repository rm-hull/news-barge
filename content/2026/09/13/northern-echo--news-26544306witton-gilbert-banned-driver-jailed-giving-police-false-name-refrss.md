A banned driver gave police another man’s name when he was stopped because he feared a longer driving ban.

Anthony Prior’s attempt to evade detection put suspicion on an innocent man and ultimately landed him in prison.

Prior, 32, of Oak Lea, Witton Gilbert, appeared at Durham Crown Court after admitting perverting the course of justice at a plea hearing in July.

Prosecutor Imogen Eastham-Brown said police stopped Prior on Stockley Lane, near Crook, at about 1.30pm on November 18, 2024.

  Anthony Prior, given short prison sentence for perverting the course of justice by giving police the name of another man when stopped at the wheel only three months into a driving ban 

*(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

Prior gave another man’s name, spelling it out and giving his date-of-birth, and told the officer he had no driving licence or other identification on him at the time.

He also claimed his phone had no social media profile which could verify his identity.

The man whose identity Prior used had no licence, and the defendant told police his father insured him to drive.

Miss Eastham-Brown said the man’s father was contacted, but after 20 minutes Prior, still claiming to be the other man, admitted he had no licence and was uninsured.

The man whose name Prior gave later told police he knew nothing about the stop.

Intelligence checks using a screenshot from body-worn footage identified Prior as the most likely match with the driver stopped in November 2024, and police visited him on November 5 last year.

Miss Eastham-Brown said Prior indicated he would plead guilty early in the case.

She said he has 18 convictions for 22 offences, including driving without a licence in 2009 and while disqualified in June 2024 for which he received a 12-month community order and a three-year driving ban.

“So, this offence was committed only three months into that.

“He deliberately gave a false name and date of birth and maintained this deceit for 20 minutes at the scene.”

She said Prior never changed his story, leaving police to suspect another man.

In mitigation, Amrit Jandoo said Prior’s “poor decision” when stopped was an “impulsive reaction”.

Mr Jandoo said the defendant feared a longer ban would hinder his attempts to find work to support his family.

He said Prior hoped to start his own business with the aim of “rebuilding himself.”

But Mr Jandoo conceded: “Clearly, perverting the course of justice strikes at the heart of the administration of justice and must be dealt with severely to prevent further offending.”

Judge Richard Clews said Prior’s record shows a history of motoring offences.

“Whatever his motivation may have been, I can’t help but take the view his principal motive was to avoid a further disqualification.”

But the judge said committing another offence so soon after the ban put the defendant at risk of a prison sentence.

The judge said giving the false name was intended to, “throw police off the scent, as it was the best part of a year before the true position came to light.”

But he said Prior committed the offence soon after receiving the community order and driving ban.

The judge said Prior’s benefit was that he was not charged with driving while disqualified.

“I appreciate a lot of water has gone under the bridge since November 2024 but in my judgement the law is that offences of this type are so serious only immediate custody can follow.”

Judge Clews imposed a six-month prison sentence, of which Prior may serve only 40 per cent.
