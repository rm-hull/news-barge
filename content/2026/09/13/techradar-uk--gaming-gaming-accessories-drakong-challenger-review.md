### TechRadar Verdict

The Drakong Challenger is a no-thrills Xbox and PC controller, shorn of bells and whistles to focus on top-tier performance instead, courtesy of its Hall Effect sticks and triggers and tactile buttons. The headphone jack is a welcome addition, but it lacks wireless connectivity and customization software. Still, these omissions are hard to complain about at this price point.

## Drakong Challenger: One-minute review

The Drakong Challenger takes aim at the budget controller market by offering a basic but high-performing pad. On the style front, the Challenger is very much a case of function over form. You’d be hard-pressed to tell it apart from your run-of-the-mill Xbox controller, although the green rings around the thumbsticks are one notable point of difference.

Just like that controller, the Challenger is very comfortable in the hand. It’s also surprisingly well-made, with a solid shell and planted buttons. What’s more, it’s pleasingly light, making it easy to use for long periods. All this makes it one of the best Xbox Controllers at the budget end of the sector in terms of construction.

 ![Top down three-quarter view of Drakong Challenger on a desk](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/mJbwnBdZz269jJ94ntrm5T.jpg) 


Although there’s no software to accompany the Challenger, you can assign the back buttons quite easily using the M button. A rumble notifies you when your assignments have been confirmed, which is a useful indicator.

The Challenger impresses in action. The triggers and sticks are as smooth and precise as you would expect from their Hall Effect technology, while the D-pad and face buttons are satisfyingly damped and quick to respond and rebound. The back buttons are a little less tactile, but they suffice nonetheless.

Given how inexpensive it is, you’d seriously have to consider the Challenger over a standard Xbox controller or any other, for that matter, so long as wireless connectivity is something you’re willing to sacrifice.

 ![Close-up of headset jack on Drakong Challenger on a desk with pink wall in background](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7CD4YB7Pw9bvtgZRLNty2D.jpg) 


## Drakong Challenger review: Price & availability

- **$34.99 (about £25 / AU$50)**
- **One month of Game Pass Ultimate included**
- **Good price-to-performance ratio**

The Drakong Challenger costs $34.99 (about £25 / AU$50) and is available now. It comes with a USB-C cable, as well as a code for a one-month free trial of Game Pass Ultimate.

The Challenger is cheaper than the official Xbox Wireless Controller, although that gamepad has the advantage of wireless connectivity. However, it doesn’t include a rechargeable battery as standard, nor does it have customizable back buttons.

## Drakong Challenger review: Design

- **Classic Xbox controller design**
- **Light yet solid construction**
- **No accompanying software**

As far as looks go, the Challenger barely departs from the traditional Xbox design. It has the same shape, size, and layout, with the only additional flourishes being green rings around the sticks and a subtle outline around the two halves of the pad. There’s no RGB lighting, despite what those aforementioned stick rings may lead you to believe.

However, all this isn’t to say the Challenger has a disappointing construction. After all, to many gamers, the Xbox controller form is comfortable and practical. And despite the plastics not feeling particularly premium, the Challenger is surprisingly solid and pleasingly light at the same time, which is always a winning combination.

 ![Close-up of center of Drakong Challenger, on a desk](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/vwbG2YPgbkD8BNhDMyUoaN.jpg) 


What’s more, the sticks and buttons are well fitted with no play in them at all, which isn’t something that can be said about most budget controllers. The back of the handles is textured for added traction, but the improvement is marginal given how subtle this texture is.

There’s no software available to customize the Challenger, but there are two mappable back buttons. I couldn’t find information on how to map them anywhere, but thankfully it’s a fairly simple process to figure out. You simply press the M button and a back button simultaneously until you feel a rumble, then press the button you want to assign and another rumble will confirm its activation.

The Challenger has no wireless connectivity, which is a shame but understandable at this price point. The included USB cable is quite long, but it isn’t finished in a braided fabric, which is something you often find on the best gaming mice to improve flexibility and reduce drag. It does at least seem quite durable, and the connection at either end was solid in my experience, despite the small amount of superficial wobbling at the controller end.

 ![Close-up of back button on Drakong Challenger, on a desk](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/yUwHSZnkhb2t2GWaDsSYNn.jpg) 


## Drakong Challenger review: Performance

- **Solid and responsive buttons**
- **Precise and tactile triggers and sticks**
- **Easy connectivity to Xbox and PC**

The Drakong Challenger might be a budget controller, but that’s not something you’ll notice when gaming with it, as every input is smooth, solid, and precise. The face buttons are also well damped and very responsive, rebounding quickly to allow for easy spamming when needed. The same goes for the D-pad, and despite its traditional four-pronged hat, it felt comfortable in my thumb, even when performing rolls and frantic inputs in *Tekken 8*.

On the shoulder buttons, the texture is subtle but noticeable, and feels quite nice on the fingers, although I’m not sure it provides much in the way of traction. The Hall Effect triggers are light but offer a surprising amount of control, which was especially helpful when accelerating and braking in *Assetto Corsa*. The Hall Effect thumbsticks were accurate and responsive when steering, too, as well as when aiming in* S.T.A.L.K.E.R. 2: Heart of Chornobyl*. I also appreciated their soft, grippy coating and textured rims for added purchase and feedback.

 ![Top down three-quarter view of Drakong Challenger on a desk](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/mJbwnBdZz269jJ94ntrm5T.jpg) 


The two back buttons are easy to reach, but they don’t feel quite as tactile as the other inputs, lacking depth of travel and dampening by comparison. Also, I found them quite easy to trigger accidentally, even when I didn’t have a particularly tight grip on the pad.

Surprisingly for an inexpensive controller, the Challenger features a headset jack. The cable connection is solid, and I didn’t notice any loss in audio quality relative to connections made elsewhere.

As for the pad itself, I had no issues connecting it via the USB cable to either an Xbox Series X or to my PC, being just as easy to use with the latter as the best PC controllers.

## Should I buy the Drakong Challenger?

## Buy it if…

**You want a solid build**

Despite how light it is, the Challenger’s shell and buttons are surprisingly sturdy.

**You want great value**

In terms of price-to-performance, it’s hard to beat this controller.

## Don't buy it if…

**You want to go wireless**

Part of the cost-cutting comes from its wired connectivity, which might seem a little outmoded in 2026.

**You want to tweak inputs**

You can remap the back buttons, but not any of the others. You can’t alter the behavior of the sticks and triggers, either — a missed opportunity given their Hall Effect tech.
