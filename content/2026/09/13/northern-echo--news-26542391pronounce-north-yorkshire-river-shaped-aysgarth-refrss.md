If you've ever spotted the River Ure on a map of North Yorkshire and wondered how on earth you're supposed to say it, you're certainly not alone.

The river flows through some of the county's most beautiful landscapes, passing through Wensleydale before eventually becoming the River Ouse.

Yet despite its importance, its name continues to catch out visitors and newcomers.

Many people assume it should be pronounced 'Ur', 'Yoor-ay' or even 'Your', but locals have long known the correct answer.

## How to pronounce the River Ure

The correct pronunciation is YOOR.

It rhymes with words such as 'pure' and 'cure', with a single syllable.

The name often surprises first-time visitors because the spelling gives very little clue as to how it is actually spoken.

It's a pronunciation you'll hear regularly across North Yorkshire, particularly in places such as Aysgarth, Middleham, Masham and Boroughbridge, where the river has shaped the landscape for centuries.

## Why the name is so unusual

The River Ure's name is ancient, predating many of the towns and villages that now sit along its banks.

Historians believe the name may have Celtic origins, reflecting the river's long history before the arrival of the Romans, Anglo-Saxons and Vikings.

Like many ancient river names across Britain, its spelling has evolved over the centuries while the spoken form remained largely unchanged.

That disconnect between spelling and pronunciation is a familiar feature of Yorkshire place names.

The county is full of locations whose names preserve traces of earlier languages and settlements, creating pronunciations that often make far more sense to locals than they do to visitors.


## The river that becomes the Ouse

The Ure rises in the Yorkshire Dales National Park near Great Whernside and follows a route through some of North Yorkshire's most famous scenery.

Along the way, it passes landmarks including Aysgarth Falls, Castle Bolton and Middleham before reaching Masham and Ripon.

Further east, near Boroughbridge, the Ure becomes the River Ouse, which continues south through York before eventually joining the Humber Estuary.

It has played a crucial role in shaping communities, agriculture and trade across Yorkshire for centuries.

## Not the only tricky North Yorkshire name

The River Ure is far from the only North Yorkshire name that leaves people scratching their heads.

Nearby Asenby is pronounced 'AYZ-uhn-bee', while Muker is famously pronounced 'Mew-ker' rather than 'Mucker'.

Another common stumbling block is Osmotherley, whose name is usually pronounced 'Oz-mudder-lee' by locals.

Like the River Ure, these names preserve centuries of Yorkshire history and prove that the correct pronunciation is not always obvious from the spelling.
