A controlling man kicked his terrified ex in the face after grabbing her by the neck, shoving her against a wall and biting her breast.

Ian Hind treated his partner “appallingly”, squeezing her neck until she was almost breathless, Durham Crown Court heard.

Prosecutor Emily Jo Willis said Hind and his now ex-partner had been together for about four-and-a-half years by January.

After a row about her going out with friends without him on January 31, she confronted Hind about seeing another woman, which he denied.

When she then threw a drink over him, Hind, 51, grabbed her by the neck and shoved her into the narrow gap between the bed and a wall.

He forced her down, telling her she would be sorry and demanding an apology.

Miss Willis said Hind’s partner was terrified and struggled to breathe.

When she fell silent, Hind released his grip but bit her breast, making her scream in pain.

She punched Hind and told him: “You will never do that again”, before fleeing to the bathroom.

But he followed and kicked her in the face, causing a “popping” sound.

Blood dripped on her hands and she asked Hind what he had done, throwing a mirror at him which he threw back in her direction.

The court heard she begged: “Just leave it. Let me get my stuff, I’ll just go.”

She reached her car and called police, only later realising that clumps of her hair had been pulled out.

The court heard she suffered cuts, bruises and swelling to her head, neck, face, body and legs, including black eyes and a broken finger. She has needed ongoing treatment for injuries to her nostrils and scar tissue in her mouth.

He was arrested and released on police bail on condition he did not contact her, but ignored it by messaging her and sending derogatory messages about her son.

Hind, Northbridge Park, St Helen Auckland, was sentenced at Durham Crown Court after admitting harassment and being convicted of intentional strangulation and assault causing actual bodily harm after a trial before magistrates in June.

He initially planned to challenge his convictions, but withdrew his appeal last month.

In an impact statement she read to the court, the victim described the long-term medical and psychological effects of the actions of Hind, someone she had trusted “completely”.

Looking back, she said Hind controlled her through abusive, increasingly angry behaviour and blamed her for any problem in their relationship.

She said she suffers flashbacks, anxiety and numbness.

Miss Willis said Hind has eight convictions for 11 offences, but none since a 2004 motoring offence.

Hind’s barrister, Ralf Xhepa, said his client was “up a height” with emotion after his conviction in June, but on reflection decided to abandon his appeal.

Mr Xhepa said Hind’s mindset has now “settled” and he accepts full responsibility and is “very remorseful.”

He said Hind recognised his problems with alcohol, having now understood how bad things had become, and has stopped drinking.

Mr Xhepa said Hind spent ten days in custody earlier this year after breaching his bail conditions.

He said the civil engineer owns his home and is in line for a bonus and possible promotion.

Hind also helps care for his father, taking him to medical appointments, and supports his daughter financially.

Judge Richard Clews told Hind he had behaved “appallingly” and caused his former partner “significant physical and psychological injuries".

But he accepted the Probation Service’s assessment that he poses a “low risk” of reoffending and could be rehabilitated in the community.

The judge said Hind's offences merited prison but after "much deliberation" said he “ticks all the boxes” for a three-year sentence to be suspended for two years under new sentencing laws introduced in March.

Hind must complete 41 rehabilitation and accredited programme sessions overseen by probation, as well as 200 hours' unpaid work.

The judge warned Hind that he would activate the prison sentence if he failed to complete any part of the community element of the order.

He also ordered him to pay his victim £2,000 compensation and £705 in court costs, at the rate of £150 a month.

A restraining order bans Hind from contacting or approaching his victim, or visiting two named streets in Newton Aycliffe, for ten years.
