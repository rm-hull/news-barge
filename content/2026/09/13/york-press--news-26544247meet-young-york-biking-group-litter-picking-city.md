## Meet the growing York biking group litter picking in city

Nomadic is combining litter picking with biking in York *(Image: Ryan Stephenson  (Image @backwheelmashing))*

A group of young cyclists are bringing energy and community spirit to the streets of York through fast-paced races and hands-on projects.

Nomadic, a crew of fixed-gear bike enthusiasts, organises unsanctioned "alleycat" races and litter-picking events across the city.

The group is made up of teenagers and people in their early 20s who share a passion for cycling and urban culture.

Tom, who leads the group, said: "We are one of the last sub-cultures left in York, we want to build up alleycat culture.

"Yeah, these alleycats are based off of New York courier culture and now Deliveroo, so doing the race is a bit like the job.

"The people that normally do best at these are good at Deliveroo."

  Nomadic is a crew of fixed-gear bike enthusiasts taking on litter picking efforts in York 

*(Image: Gabriel Macarthur)*

Alleycat races, inspired by New York’s messenger scene, challenge riders on both speed and city knowledge.

Riders must complete a series of checkpoints rather than follow a set route from start to finish, making each event a test of navigation and quick decision-making.

A Nomadic member who goes by 'Jiggs' said: "There's a whole culture surrounding it, we love fixed gear culture, it spans back to 90s New York and San Francisco."

The York crew is part of a wider international movement, with similar underground races taking place in cities across the UK.

Their social media presence has attracted attention from fellow enthusiasts, with some videos reaching hundreds of thousands of views.

In addition to alleycat races, Nomadic organises litter-picking events in residential areas of York as a way to give back to the community.

Tom said: "I think it does a lot of good for people, it gives them something to do.

  The youngsters are taking on hands-on projects in York. 

*(Image: Gabriel Macarthur)*

"We've got a massive problem with men’s mental health, youth violence, suicide and depression in the current day.

"We’re not supposed to do these, but I think it helps lots of people.

"Keeps people doing something.

"Almost everyone in our group was mega depressed before I started it all and now we’ve picked up loads of people."

Callum, 18, a newer member, said: "from working in a bike shop I got more obsessed, I’ve been to some races and find it fun.

  The Crew is combining biking with litter picking in York 

*(Image: Gabriel Macarthur)*

"Everybody involved is really friendly and it’s a nice environment to be in.

"Its definitely worth coming round to it."

However, the city centre’s pedestrianised zones have made organising races increasingly difficult.

Tom said: "There are drastic measures, they have made it illegal to cycle through town recently.

"None of us are doing anything ethically wrong – its just about getting people out."

York’s Local Transport Strategy (2024–2040) states the council’s aim to make cycling, walking, and wheeling "more attractive" through a "continuous network of safe and high-quality cycle, walking and wheeling routes."

Despite restrictions, Nomadic plans to expand its events beyond York.

Tom said: "I’m looking to find some private land, or a trail that we can hire close for the day so we can have a race.

"It would be an off-road race across some small obstacles or down some steep trials.

"I’m quite confident I could turn it into a national event, with some help from some international brands too.

"I want to host something my dad won’t tell me off for."
