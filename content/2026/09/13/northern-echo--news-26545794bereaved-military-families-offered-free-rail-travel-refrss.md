Hundreds of bereaved military relatives will travel free by train this Remembrance weekend under a scheme helping them honour loved ones who died serving in the Armed Forces.

For the first time, one next of kin of each service person who died while serving will qualify for free National Rail travel to Remembrance events on November 7 and 8, 2026.

The scheme extends the existing free travel offer for serving personnel and veterans during the annual Remembrance period.

Eligible relatives must apply for a free ticket through the Defence Medical Welfare Service by October 16, 2026.

Mikki Brett is among those who will benefit. Her husband, Chief Petty Officer Stuart Brett, died while serving in the Royal Navy in April 2023.

Mikki, who is due to attend the Cenotaph service on Remembrance Sunday, welcomed the announcement.

She said: "It's fantastic news that the in-service bereaved have been recognised for the support given to their loved ones with free train fares to remembrance events this year."

Rail Minister Lord Hendy said the move recognised bereaved families’ vital role in Remembrance commemorations.

He said: "Remembrance weekend is a time for the nation to come together to honour those who made the ultimate sacrifice whilst serving our country.

"Bereaved families are at the heart of these commemorations and the cost of travel should not prevent them from being there to remember their loved ones."

He said free rail travel would help more bereaved families join veterans, serving personnel and local communities at Remembrance events.

Minister for Veterans and People Calvin Bailey said Remembrance gave the nation a chance to honour the Armed Forces’ service and sacrifice.

He said: "For bereaved families, this is a deeply personal time.

"We must never forget that behind every name we remember is a family whose lives have been changed forever. Providing free rail travel will help bereaved relatives come together and honour their loved one without financial barriers."

The rail industry also welcomed the scheme’s expansion.

Ola Ogun, co-chief executive of the Rail Delivery Group, said railways had long supported Remembrance events and operators were proud to continue doing so.

"This year, we are especially pleased to extend that support to one next of kin of those who died while serving in the Armed Forces, recognising not only the sacrifice of those who served, but also the families who continue to carry their legacy and honour their memory," he said.

Armed Forces charity SSAFA also welcomed the decision.

A spokesperson said the change would let bereaved military families attend Remembrance events without worrying about travel costs.

"It is a deeply meaningful gesture that properly honours the sacrifices of our Forces families, ensuring they can stand together with the nation in remembrance," the spokesperson said.

The measure is expected to help hundreds of bereaved relatives attend services, parades and national commemorations across the country during a key weekend in the Armed Forces calendar.
