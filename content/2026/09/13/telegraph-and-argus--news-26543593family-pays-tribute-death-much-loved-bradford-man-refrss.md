THE family of a much-loved Bradford man who "would do anything for anyone" have paid tribute following his death after a battle with a serious brain tumour.

James Pilkington, of Bierley, died on August 17, 2026, aged 44, surrounded by his family.

Born on July 16, 1982, James was remembered by loved ones as a devoted family man, loyal friend and proud member of the community he called home throughout his life.

Paying tribute, his brother Sean Holden described James as someone who was always willing to help others.

"He was one of those people that everybody liked," Sean said.

"He'd do anything for anyone. He were very community and family orientated."

  James Pilkington and his son at Valley Parade. 

*(Image: T&A)*

## Bierley was 'everything' to him

James' connection to Bierley ran deep, with Sean recalling how proud he was of the area in which he grew up.

Sean recounted a long-running story among James and his friends.

He said: "There was a standing joke that came up through all this.

"Him and some of his close friends had been drinking one evening and decided they were all going to get tattoos with their names.

"His friends went out and got their names tattooed. James went last and when he came out, he'd got 'Bierley' tattooed on the back of his neck.

"Our local community was everything to him."

The brothers spent much of their childhood exploring Bierley Woods and the surrounding area.

"One of the big things was Bierley Woods, where we all grew up," Sean said.

"We all played out together where the local fishing ponds are."

## Career took him around the world

After leaving school, Sean pursued a career as a chef, with James later following him into the catering industry.

"Grandma and aunties taught us to cook," Sean said.

James initially worked as a chef at a bank's head office in Low Moor before discovering a passion for joinery, a trade that would take him around the world.

  James Pilkington in 2025. 

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Sean said: "He absolutely loved his job.

"They did shopfront fittings for Nike and Adidas in Germany. He loved all that side of it.

When he got diagnosed and had to quit work, he was devastated about that.

"I think he was more devastated about quitting his job than his actual diagnosis."

## Local community support praised

Despite facing a serious illness, Sean said James remained remarkably positive.

"Obviously he was upset, but he took it really well to be fair," he said.

"He just said, 'I've been dealt a blow. S**t happens. There's nothing I can do about it.'"

In his final weeks, James was cared for at Overgate Hospice in Elland, where Sean said staff were "absolutely fantastic".

He added that having family around him became increasingly important.

  James Pilkington and his family. 

*(Image: T&A)*

"He kept saying to me, 'I don't know what I'd do without you'," Sean recalled.

Sean also praised the support shown by the local community during James' illness and in the lead-up to his funeral.

"We're a close-knit community, we always have been," he said.

"It was just amazing how everyone came together."

## 'Everybody just loved him'

James' funeral was held on September 2 and reflected his greatest passions.

The Bradford City-themed service featured claret and amber floral tributes, a Bantams flag draped across the coffin, and a wake held at Valley Parade.

In a fitting tribute to his trade, the coffin was crafted from solid oak - James' favourite wood to work with.

  Flowers at the funeral 

*(Image: T&A)*

"We're a traditional Scottish family as well," Sean said.

"We had a bagpiper leading the procession and some of his friends carried the coffin down the drive."

Reflecting on his brother's life, Sean said: "He was just a genuine person, and he'd do anything for anyone.

"He was always there for his friends, and he kept those friends all throughout his life, friends he had from school.

"Everybody just loved him."

Asked how he would like his brother to be remembered, Sean's answer was simple.

"Just his love for his family and community."
