A Darlington family have spoken of their heartbreak after losing their seven-year-old son to a rare terminal illness just weeks after the death of their much-loved grandmother.

For Sinead Hall, 47, and partner Simon Haynes, 50, the past year has been marked by devastating loss after saying goodbye to both seven-year-old Parker and “the heart of the family” Vivienne within a fortnight of one another.

Parker died on June 30 following a three-month battle in hospital after developing sepsis and multiple organ failure.

  Parker Hall 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

His death came just two weeks after the family had mourned the loss of Sinead's mother, Vivienne, who died from cancer.

As the family continue to come to terms with the loss of both Parker and Vivienne, Sinead and Simon have paid tribute to their three children, Scarlett, 21, and twins Poppy and Max, 18, for the support they gave their grandmother and younger brother during their illnesses.

Sinead said: "My children have been through things that no young people should ever have to go through, but through it all they've shown a level of love, maturity and selflessness that breaks my heart and makes me incredibly proud.

  Vivienne sadly died after her fight with cancer. 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

"They spent so much time with Parker and so much time with Nan when they both needed them most. It wasn't something we ever had to ask them to do. They just did it because that's who they are."

The family had lived for years with the reality of Parker's condition after he was diagnosed with the rare genetic disorder Lesch-Nyhan syndrome.

The condition affected almost every aspect of his life and required round-the-clock care.

Simon said: "When Parker was three, we were told he had a terminal illness and might only have two years to live.

  Parker sadly died following a three-month battle in hospital 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

"From that day on, every morning I was waking up wondering what I was going to find when I checked on him.

"People see what's on the outside, but they don't see the reality of living with that fear every single day."

Parker also had cerebral palsy and spent much of his life in and out of hospital.

Despite his complex medical needs, his parents said he touched the lives of everyone who met him.

Simon said: "He couldn't talk, but he talked with his eyes. He always found a way to tell you what he wanted.

"The nurses used to tell us that everybody wanted to look after Parker because there was just something special about him."

  Parker sadly died following a three-month battle in hospital 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

Family life revolved around hospital appointments, medication schedules and sleepless nights as Parker's condition progressed.

Simon said: "There were years where nobody got a proper night's sleep. He was awake every few hours and needed 24-hour supervision.

"I look back now and genuinely don't know how we got through it."

Earlier this year, Parker was rushed to Newcastle's Royal Victoria Infirmary suffering from sepsis and multiple organ failure.

  Parker sadly died following a three-month battle in hospital 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

For three months, Sinead and Simon remained at his bedside while their children travelled regularly to Newcastle to spend time with their younger brother.

Sinead said: "They were there constantly. They held his hand, read stories to him, sang to him and played with him. They never stopped showing up for him.

"Parker absolutely adored them. Every time Poppy walked into the room, he would light up. Scarlett was like a second mam to him and Max had such a special bond with him as well."

Describing the impact of the double loss, Sinead said: "It felt like our whole world had fallen apart.

"We were grieving for my mam and then suddenly we were grieving for Parker too.

  Parker sadly died following a three-month battle in hospital 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

"To lose two people who meant so much to us in such a short space of time is something I still struggle to put into words."

Despite their heartbreak, the family say they remain immensely proud of their children and the way they have navigated months of grief.

Sinead said: "All three of them have shown so much bravery through everything that's happened. They've spent years caring for other people and putting other people first.

"I'm proud of them every single day."

The three siblings all share the same birthday, September 10, with Scarlett turning 21 and twins Poppy and Max celebrating their 18th birthdays.

  Sinead and Parker 

*(Image: FAMILY)*

To mark the milestone, Sinead has launched a fundraiser in the hope of giving them something special to look forward to.

She said: “After everything they've been through, I wanted them to have something positive to look forward to. All our savings went on mam's and Parker’s funeral.

“They've missed out on so much over the years because our lives revolved around caring for Parker.

"They've had holidays cancelled, plans changed and opportunities they couldn't take. They never complained. They just got on with it."

The family have since raised £817 of an £1000 target and have been “overwhelmed” from the support they have already received.

Sinead said: “We've been overwhelmed by the kindness people have shown us.

"The donations and messages mean so much because they show the children that people recognise everything they've been through."
