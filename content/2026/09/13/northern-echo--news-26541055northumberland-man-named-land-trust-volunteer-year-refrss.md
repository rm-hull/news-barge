A rainy dog walk in Northumberland led to a decision, and now that decision has earned national recognition.

Nigel Green, a volunteer from Slaley, has been named Volunteer of the Year by the Land Trust for his dedication to conservation work across Northumberland.

His journey began more than two years ago during a wet stroll through Northumberland Wildlife Trust’s Juliet’s Wood reserve, where he spotted volunteers and was inspired to join them.

Geoff Dobbins, estates manager for Northumberland Wildlife Trust, said: "Nigel's enthusiasm never fails to impress us all.

"He is always willing to go the extra mile, so when the time came for us to nominate someone for the Volunteer of the Year Award, it took us all of five seconds to recommend him for his work at Weetslade and Northumberlandia."

Since then, Mr Green has contributed hundreds of hours to maintaining wildlife reserves including Whitelee, Whittle Dene, Holywell Pond, and Kielder.

Much of his recent work has focused on the Land Trust’s Northumberlandia and Weetslade sites.

So far in 2026, he has volunteered 185 hours.

Mr Green’s volunteer duties have included everything from habitat management to supporting events, helping to ensure that Northumberland’s green spaces remain safe and enjoyable for all.

Frances Smiles, Northumberland Wildlife Trust Community Events Coordinator, said: "Northumberlandia and Weetslade are very popular sites, welcoming visitors every day who come to walk their dogs, enjoy nature or simply escape the hustle and bustle of everyday life.

"We also host a wide range of events at both sites, so it's important that they are maintained to the highest standards.

"The efforts of Nigel and his fellow volunteers are absolutely invaluable."
