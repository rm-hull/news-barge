A SWEETCORN seller has been ordered to stop serving customers with allergies after a string of hygiene breaches were found.

Sweetcorn is a trader operating out of Cannon Mills in Great Horton.

It was inspected on May 24.

During the visit, officers identified several failings, including poor allergen management, a lack of documented food safety procedures, and inadequate hygiene facilities.

  Cannon Mills in Little Horton 

*(Image: T&A)*

## 'Unaware of allergen management'

The report read: "At the time of the visit, the food handler was unaware of allergen management and control measures to prevent cross contamination.

"For this reason, until training and competence and understanding in food safety procedures regarding allergen containing foods is achieved, you must not serve consumers who declare a food allergy."

The report advised that the trader complete free online allergy training available through the Food Standards Agency (FSA).

## Other hygiene issues identified

The business was using cardboard to line a food steamer, which cannot be kept clean and poses a contamination risk.

Bare wood inside the food cart needed to be sealed to provide a smooth, cleanable surface, and officers recommended a cleanable floor covering around the cart during operation.

Personal hygiene facilities were also found to be inadequate.

The business was told to provide antibacterial soap, hand towels, and appropriate handwashing stations for staff.

## 'Stop storing food on the floor'

Officers also instructed the trader to stop storing food on the floor, use protective clothing such as aprons, and ensure a disinfectant sanitiser was available during operation.

The report continued: "As you prepare, cook, hot hold and serve open food, a documented food safety management system is required to be put in place, implemented and maintained.

"You must be able to determine all food hazards associated with the work activities taking place and put in place control measures to manage identified hazards."

The business was advised to use the FSA’s Safer Food Better Business pack for caterers to help meet legal requirements.

It was also told to keep receipts or invoices for all food supplies to ensure traceability.

## What Cannon Mills had to say

A spokesperson for Cannon Mills said: "We have majority of really good ratings for the food stalls."
