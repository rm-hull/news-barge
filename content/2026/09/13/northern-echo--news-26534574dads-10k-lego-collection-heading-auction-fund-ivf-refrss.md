A dad is selling a £10,000 LEGO collection through a Thornaby auction house to help fund IVF treatment as he and his wife hope to have a second child.

James Smith, 40, from Stockport, Greater Manchester, has consigned 36 lots to Vectis Auctions in Thornaby, with the sale due to take place on September 17.

The collection includes a Star Wars Millennium Falcon, an Imperial Star Destroyer, the USS Enterprise, Marvel-themed sets and a life-size interactive sci-fi cockpit.

  James Smith is selling his £10,000 LEGO collection - including a life-size cockpit - to help fund his wife's IVF treatment. 

*(Image: James Smith / SWNS)*

James hopes the sale will cover the remaining cost of their private fertility treatment.

He and his wife Jessica, 39, welcomed their first son in October 2024 after undergoing NHS-funded IVF treatment.

But when they decided to have another child, the couple learned they would have to fund any further treatment themselves.

Treatment costs about £9,000, and the couple are paying about £1,000 a month.

James said: "To be able to pay for the treatment would mean the world to us.

"It would be incredible to know that we are financially secure and to know we can afford to keep trying for another baby."

The couple underwent their first round of IVF in 2023 after struggling to conceive for about 18 months.

Doctors were unable to identify a specific cause for their fertility difficulties.

James said: "It was unexplained infertility; they never had an answer for us.

"Since then, trying for a second child has been privately funded."

  James Smith is selling his £10,000 LEGO collection - including a life-size cockpit - to help fund his wife's IVF treatment. 

*(Image: James Smith / SWNS)*

After welcoming their son, the couple pursued further treatment but have faced several setbacks.

A later embryo transfer led to a chemical pregnancy, while another round ended in miscarriage.

Despite the setbacks, the couple remain hopeful and have one blastocyst left for a future transfer.

James said: "At the moment we still have one blastocyst remaining and, when we are ready, we hope to go ahead with that transfer."

To ease the financial pressure, he is selling about 90 per cent of the collection he built over more than two decades.

Jessica said: "I am grateful to my husband for giving up his collection.

"It would mean the world to us if we were able to grow our family."

  The life-size cockpit, which James hopes will fetch £1,000 at auction. 

*(Image: James Smith / SWNS)*

Among the standout lots is a life-size interactive sci-fi cockpit which he restored himself and which is expected to attract strong interest from bidders.

James said: "I do love my LEGO collection, but I am even happier to let the collection go and build LEGO with our son and, hopefully one day, another child."

The sale will take place at Vectis Auctions in Thornaby, with the family hoping the proceeds will allow them to clear the remaining treatment costs in one go.
