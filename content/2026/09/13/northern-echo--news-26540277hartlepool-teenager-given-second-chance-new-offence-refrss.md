A teenager spotted trying to remove car number plates was attacked after turning his back on organised crime.

Days after receiving a suspended sentence for setting 12 vehicles alight, Ethan Chapman was arrested while trying to remove car number plates.

The 18-year-old was sentenced for his role in a masked gang which carried out an arson attack at a yard on Hartlepool’s Longhill Industrial Estate on April 6.

Teesside Crown Court heard the blaze was part of a “revenge attack” by an organised crime gang.

Chapman was given an 18-month sentence, suspended for two years, at a July 30 court hearing.

He was also ordered to complete 20 rehabilitation activity days and 150 hours of unpaid work.

Prosecutor Emma Atkinson said Chapman was arrested again on August 10 and charged with vehicle interference after police spotted him trying to remove the number plates from a car stolen a month earlier.

Chapman, of Shelly Grove, Hartlepool, admitted a single charge of vehicle interference.

Chapman’s barrister, Stephen Constantine, said he had turned his back on his criminal associates and been beaten for it.

He said: “He got himself with a group of people who in terms of criminality and age, were far more advanced than him.

“He did as he was told because if he didn’t physical threats were made to him and his family.”

Mr Constantine said Chapman took part in the retaliatory attack that led to his suspended sentence.

He said Chapman had been warned he could not simply leave the criminal lifestyle and was assaulted last week for refusing to carry out any more illegal acts.
