AN outdoor youth group which helps build confidence, teamwork and a sense of belonging to children from diverse backgrounds has been awarded a York Community Pride Award.

Run by environment charity St Nicks, Urban Wild Youth Group supports young people aged 11 and 16 from a range of backgrounds, including those with additional needs, in learning new skills outside.

The group was named the Best Community Project at the York Community Pride Awards at York Racecourse on September 10.

  


Vicky Blakey-Archer, CEO of St Nicks, said: “Urban Wild was born out of a desire to engage with young people and increase their sense of pride in their local area.

“It is an absolute testament to what you can achieve if you allow them the freedom to connect with nature and the autonomy to make decisions.

“I’m so grateful to the Police, Fire and Crime Commissioner, our team who have worked so hard to develop and deliver the project, and crucially, the young people for all their enthusiasm and for producing work that will inspire others to look after nature.”

Those at Urban Wild have been making a positive difference to the nature reserve in Tang Hall since the beginning of the year.

The young people dedicated a trail to educating visitors about the reserve and the animals who call it home.

A spokesperson said: “With the help of volunteers from Aviva and Portakabin, the teens researched and designed the new signs themselves, before spending a day over Easter building and installing them.

“The nature trail starts at the play park at St Nicks and can be enjoyed by people young and old throughout the year and has already received lots of positive feedback and engagement. It has been an incredibly rewarding process for the young people - they’ve worked together, grown in confidence and are rightly proud of their achievements.”
