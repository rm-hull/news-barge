Durham County Council’s cabinet is set to discuss whether to buy back a long-derelict Ferryhill care home, five years after the building was sold off.

An option to buy back Feryemount House in Ferryhill is due to be considered by cabinet members when they meet on Wednesday (September 16).

Newton Aycliffe and Spennymoor MP Alan Strickland, who previously raised concerns about the condition of the building, welcomed the move.

He said: “I have been campaigning on this issue for a long time and have repeatedly pushed the council to act.

“The state of the building is totally unacceptable and it is simply not good enough that it has been allowed to deteriorate for so long.

“I hope the council now makes the right decision and fixes this mess.”

The building was closed by Durham County Council in 2014 after the authority estimated it would need to spend almost £4.2 million over the following 10 years to bring Feryemount House and four other County Durham care homes up to the required standard.

Since then, several attempts have been made to bring the site back into use.

Plans were submitted in 2018 to convert the building into apartments, but the scheme failed to progress.

In 2021, Durham County Council sold the property to developer Care Co Plus Ltd.

However, the building has continued to deteriorate, becoming a target for vandalism, with smashed windows and graffiti attracting complaints from residents.

In July 2025, developers said work would finally begin on the site after it had become an eyesore in Ferryhill town centre.

Durham Constabulary had also carried out patrols in the area amid concerns over anti-social behaviour linked to the vacant building.

Details of the potential reacquisition are expected to be outlined after cabinet members consider the report next week.
