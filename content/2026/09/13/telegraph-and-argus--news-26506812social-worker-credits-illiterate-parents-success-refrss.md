A SOCIAL WORKER from Shipley has been named a finalist for a national award on the back of personal accolades made by his staff and care leavers - but he puts his success largely down to the values installed in him by his parents.

Sidiq Ali, 53, from Shipley, has been shortlisted for the Social Worker of the Year Lifetime Achievement Award after being nominated by a care leaver.

Mr Ali told the T&A he feels "deeply humbled and honoured" to have been selected as a finalist, but says he never went out "looking for recognition."

## 'Brings a genuine tear to my eye'

Of the messages supporting his application he said: "To see my journey reflected through their eyes brings a genuine tear to my eye.

"The real reward has always been knowing that I may have made a positive difference in someone's life."

Mr Ali, who grew up in Manningham during the 1970s and 1980s and was the first in his family to go to university, credits his parents, who couldn't read or write, for teaching him "the value of treating people with dignity and respect regardless of their circumstances".

He said: "My parents had a huge influence on my journey into social work.

"Growing up, I always felt a strong desire to help others and stand up for people who may not have a voice.

"Social work provided the perfect opportunity to turn those values into a career where I could advocate for vulnerable people, support families and help create positive change.

"Looking back, becoming a social worker felt less like a career choice and more like a calling."

## 'Impact of social work not always visible'

He continued: "Social work is valuable because it is fundamentally about people.

"It is about listening, advocating, protecting, supporting and helping individuals and families through some of the most challenging times in their lives.

"The impact of social work is not always visible in statistics, reports or performance measures.

"Sometimes it is found in a child feeling safe, a family feeling supported, a foster carer feeling valued, or a young person developing confidence and resilience."

He believes he has gained as much as he has given in his role, and as a result he has become "more reflective and more understanding of the complexities people face in their lives."

## 'Always remember the person behind the paperwork'

Reflecting on his career, Mr Ali recalled some of the best advice he has received.

He said: "One of the best pieces of advice I received was to always remember the person behind the paperwork.

"People are experts in their own lives and often what they need most is someone who genuinely hears them and values their perspective. Listen more than you speak."

It's also been a deeply enlightening venture for Mr Ali.

He admits the occupation has "shaped" him into the man he is today.

He said: "It has taught me patience, resilience, empathy and humility.

"Throughout my career, I have tried to hold onto those principles and they have never let me down.

"This work has reinforced my belief in the importance of human connection, kindness and treating everyone with dignity and respect."

The award ceremony will take place in London later this year.

Mr Ali said: "I am incredibly excited and honoured to be attending as a finalist.

"Being invited to the House of Commons and recognised alongside so many talented and dedicated professionals is already a tremendous achievement.

"I am looking forward to celebrating not only my own journey but also the contributions of everyone working across social care."

## What some of those under his care have said

Please note we have not included names of care leavers for anonymity purposes.

One care leaver said: "When I experienced bullying at Bradford College, Sidiq arranged for me to transfer to a college in Keighley, where I felt safer. He encouraged my interest in football, connecting me with local clubs in Bradford and Leeds and regularly transporting me to matches across Yorkshire.

"Had it not been for Sidiq supporting me for those early years of my life I don’t think I would be were I am today. I am working as a restaurant manager, and managing my own tenancy and living a happy and healthy life."

Another care leaver said: "Sidiq made sure I had a balanced and enjoyable life. He took me to the cinema, out to eat, to the gym and swimming pool, and to museums, which helped me stay active, supported, and positive. He also invited me to his house a couple of times and kindly offered me food and tea during Eid and Ramadan."

Principal Stuart Nash of New College Bradford said: "What sets Sidiq apart is his authenticity and moral leadership. He is highly respected by professionals and deeply trusted by young people, many of whom credit him with advocating for them at critical points in their lives."
