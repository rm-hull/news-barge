A PRIVATELY built family app has secured a state innovation grant to support its launch.

Bradford-born Ben Peel, 41, received 50,000 Swedish kronor from Almi, Sweden’s state-owned business development agency, for Nuity—a privacy-focused family app he designed, built, and coded independently.

The app is currently in private beta testing with real families and is expected to launch publicly.

Mr Peel said: "Every family I know runs a group chat, and it sits on a platform built for strangers, selling their attention back to them.

"And after research I have discovered a majority (56 per cent of UK adults) have no will and no idea where anything is kept.

"Those are the same family, and nobody had ever put them in one private, secure place.

"I went looking for it, but it didn't exist, so I built it."

Nuity offers a digital space for families to organise daily life and preserve family history, with features including shared memories, photographs, a living family tree, private friend circles, and tools for managing affairs after a death.

It does not include a public feed, advertising, or data harvesting, and each family retains control of its own information.

The grant was awarded following a pitch to Almi’s innovation council in September.

Funds will be used for an independent GDPR review and a security audit of the app’s privacy architecture ahead of its public release.

Mr Peel, originally from Wibsey in Bradford and a former student of Buttershaw Upper School, previously worked as innovations manager at gin distiller Sipsmith and founded John Robert’s Botanic Request Gin before relocating to Sweden.

He said: "I’m a Bradford lad who ended up in Holland, Sweden and with my laptop I decided to build something new.

"Getting a Swedish state agency to back it, as a one-man band with no funding behind me, is not something I expected.

"It says the idea stands up outside my own kitchen."

Nuity is currently being tested daily by families in a private beta.

Early access registration is available at nuity.app/early-access.

A spokesperson for Nuity said: "Nuity is a private app for one family.

"It holds everyday life and family history in the same place: profiles and shared memories, a living family tree, private circles for the people around a family as well as those in it, and legacy tools for estate planning and management for what happens afterwards."

The app does not merge family data into a centralised master record.
