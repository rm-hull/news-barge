There was a buzz in the Denes this weekend as Darlington beekeepers opened the doors to their apiary.

The Darlington Beekeepers Association (DBKA) welcomed the public into their grounds at the old bowling green off Widdowfield Street on Saturday (September 12).

The event, which took place between 2pm and 4pm, saw crowds enjoy a live beekeeping demonstration and even have the opportunity to try and take home some honey.

As well as showing the public around, the DBKA, which was established in 2012, tried to teach others about the importance of honey bees through a number of information displays.

Darlington Mayor Councillor Helen Crumbie was among those attending, even throwing on a white beekeeper's outfit herself.

Darlington MP Lola McEvoy also stopped to pose with her own jar of Denes Apiary Honey.

The DBKA currently manage two apiaries, one in the Denes near Darlington town centre and another at Onpath Wind Farm near Sadberge.

The Denes site is home to approximately 20 hives, with each hive being able to house 30,000 to 60,000 bees during the summer months.

  Darlington Beekeepers Association opens the doors to its apiary 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

From the Denes, bees produce “exceptional” wildflower honey from the rich variety of plants and flowers in the Darlington area.

The group trains dozens of new beekeepers every year and currently boasts more than 70 members.

Members also sell locally produced honey and the different plant varieties produce different flavours and colours throughout the season.

Buying the honey enables the group to maintain the colonies needed for pollination and support its mission to provide excellent facilities and resources for training aspiring beekeepers.
