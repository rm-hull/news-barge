The University of Bradford says its focus on employability is helping students move successfully into work and further education.

This comes amid growing national concern about graduate unemployment.

The university reports that 88 per cent of its graduates are in employment or further study within 15 months of graduating.

Of those, 78 per cent are in work and 10 per cent are continuing their education.

The university also reports that 84 per cent of those employed graduates are working in highly skilled roles.

   

*(Image: Newsquest)*

Zailie Barratt, associate director at the University of Bradford, said their success was due to "combining high-quality academic teaching with practical experience, employer engagement, careers advice and developing opportunities for students to build the confidence and skills that employers really value."

She said the university’s strong partnerships with businesses help ensure students are ready for the workplace before they graduate.

Ms Barratt said: "Employer voice is really important to us, that we understand what employers are really thinking or wanting, and that we're able to communicate that back to our students."

She identified promising career opportunities for graduates in sectors such as health, engineering, manufacturing, AI, and computing.

Ms Barratt said: "Technology and AI are booming really. Health and life sciences as well."

When asked about the wider debate on whether too many young people are attending university, she said the focus should be on giving individuals the right opportunities.

She said: "I don't think the answer is to say whether people should go to university or not.

"I think the question should be whether they get the right education, skills and opportunity to achieve their ambition."

The University of Bradford currently supports around 9,000 students each year.

Some commentators argue that the UK has focused too heavily on degrees at the expense of apprenticeships and technical education.

When asked whether she agreed, Ms Barratt said there is no single path to success and argued against a one-size-fits-all approach to education.

She said: "I don't see it as an either-or choice.

"We need a strong education system that gives people different routes into successful careers."

The debate comes as the Centre for Social Justice estimates around 700,000 unemployed graduates are claiming benefits.

A Rewiring Education report argued that the UK’s education system has become unbalanced, leaving many graduates competing for limited graduate-level jobs while employers struggle to recruit workers with technical and practical skills.

Figures from the House of Commons Library show 739,000 unemployed people aged 16 to 24 between April and June 2026, up 97,000 from the previous year.

The youth unemployment rate has risen to 16.2 per cent, and more than one million young people are now classed as not in employment, education or training (NEET) for the first time since 2013.
