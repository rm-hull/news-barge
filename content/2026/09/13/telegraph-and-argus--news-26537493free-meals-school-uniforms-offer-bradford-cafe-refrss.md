A CAFE in Bradford is relaunching its free children's meals initiative this month while also offering support with everything from school uniforms to furniture for struggling families.

The founder of Lily's Cafe, which is located on Mill Street in Wibsey, Gemma Jackson said the project is about much more than providing a hot meal, with families able to access practical help and advice without any eligibility criteria or means testing.

Speaking about the return of the scheme following the summer holidays, Ms Jackson said: "Come and see us. We can help. If you're struggling with things like school uniform, or your child's struggling at school, we have so many people that are great in different areas.

She added: "We can help with food bags, clothing and furniture. If you need beds or things like that, we can help with all those things."

   

*(Image: T&A)*

## Thousands of meals served

The initiative first launched a year ago and provided around 8,000 hot meals for children over the past 12 months.

Ms Jackson said the scheme had become a vital resource for many families facing rising living costs, while also providing a safe and welcoming environment for children.

"A lot of parents out there are struggling with food prices going up," she said.

"It's okay giving food bags and things, but they're not hot meals.

"Sometimes you can't afford to put the electric or the gas on to make a hot meal."

She added that for some families, the choice between heating and cooking remains a reality.

Ms Jackson explained: "It shouldn't be a choice, really, but prices have just gone up."

## Open to everyone

Unlike some support schemes, the free meals initiative does not have any eligibility requirements.

Ms Jackson said anyone is welcome, regardless of their financial circumstances or background.

"There's no questions asked at all," she said. "It's not about tags or labels. This is about giving a better future to children."

She added that many families using the service are working households who are simply finding life difficult amid ongoing financial pressures.

"A lot of people have misconceptions, but 95 per cent of the parents that come in work. They're trying to do their very best for their children. They're just struggling."

## More than a meal

As well as providing food, Lily's Cafe offers children activities and a place to socialise after school.

Ms Jackson said the relaxed setting can be particularly beneficial for children with additional needs and gives families an opportunity to spend time together.

Adults are also invited to eat alongside their children.

"It's nice to see families eating together," she said.

"There are a lot of mums and dads that go without food to feed their children."

The initiative is funded entirely through donations, with Ms Jackson praising residents and businesses who have contributed food, money and supplies.
