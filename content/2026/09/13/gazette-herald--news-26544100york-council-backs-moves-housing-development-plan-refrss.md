MOVES to avoid potential delays to a York housing development have been approved.

City of York Council's executive has given the go ahead to buy stores and garages at the Willow House site through a compulsory purchasing order (CPO) if necessary.

Cllr Dave Merrett, the council's Labour housing spokesperson, said efforts to acquire the buildings voluntarily was ongoing but had proved difficult in some cases.

But Liberal Democrat opposition leader Cllr Nigel Ayre said the site was among those where little progress had been made on building affordable housing.

The decision on Wednesday, September 9, comes as works on the site of the empty former care home, in Long Lane Close, near Walmgate, are set to begin next year.

Council officials are currently working to procure a lead development for the scheme which would see 36 one and two-bed affordable homes built.

They would be offered with social rents and through shared ownership schemes, with plans for the scheme approved in May.

Willow House is set to be torn down to make way for the development.

Councillors heard on Wednesday talks with leaseholders to acquire the remaining parts of the site were ongoing.

The meeting heard approving the use of a CPO in principle would avoid unnecessary delays if the council is unable to reach an agreement to acquire the buildings.

Labour Housing Executive Member Cllr Merrett said the move was an important step in getting the scheme completed.

The executive member said: "Getting the Willow House site redeveloped is important.

"There's potentially a lot of people who will be rehoused if we can get this scheme going."

But opposition Liberal Democrat leader Cllr Ayre said progress on the scheme had been slow.

The opposition leader said: "This is another site that by now should be providing affordable homes.

"But there's not a brick in the ground."

It follows the approval of the council's plan to improve the wider Walmgate area on Tuesday, September 8.

Council funding worth £250,000 for the scheme comes alongside £500,000 from the council's Local Transport Grant and £217,000 from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

Along with the homes at Willow House, Phase One of the Neighbourhood Improvement Plan includes security measures to curb antisocial behaviour and a shared community garden scheme.

Road improvements for pedestrians and cyclists, public artworks, improvements to Walmgate's parade of shops and plans for a heritage trail are also featured.

Work on the improvements is set to begin from next spring.
