Three people have been ordered to pay more than £7,500 between them after being found guilty of breaching council enforcement notices relating to neighbouring plots of land in County Durham.

Pamela Duddin, Anthony Tiffin and Victoria Tiffin were convicted following trials at Peterlee Magistrates’ Court on Wednesday, September 2.

The cases all concerned land on the north-east side of St Aidan's Terrace in Trimdon Station, known as Deaf Hill.

Duddin, 53, of Sunnyside Terrace, Trimdon Grange, was prosecuted over Plot 3, while Anthony Tiffin, 55, of Meadow Road, Trimdon Village, and Victoria Tiffin, 47, also of Meadow Road, faced separate proceedings relating to Plot 2.

  Peterlee Magistrates Court 

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

All three had pleaded not guilty to breaching enforcement notices when they appeared before magistrates on April 1.

In Duddin’s case, Durham County Council had issued an enforcement notice requiring the use of Plot 3 for storage, recreation and leisure to permanently cease.

She was also required to remove a small aluminium storage container, furniture, chairs and other domestic items and paraphernalia.

Hardstanding created on the site was required to be excavated and removed, with the areas backfilled with earth and 10cm of topsoil before being reseeded with grass.

The court found that the requirements had not been complied with by December 10, 2025.

Duddin was found guilty following her trial and fined £490.

She was also ordered to pay £1,567 in costs and a £196 victim surcharge, leaving a total bill of £2,253.

She was ordered to pay £50 per month from September 30.

Anthony and Victoria Tiffin were separately prosecuted in connection with Plot 2 at Deaf Hill.

Their enforcement notices required the land to cease being used for the siting of a static caravan and associated paraphernalia and to be returned solely to agricultural use.

The caravan was required to be removed, along with a timber animal shelter, stables and a chicken coop on the southern boundary of the site.

The notices also required hardstanding to be excavated and removed, with affected areas and trenches backfilled with earth and topsoil and reseeded with grass.

Anthony Tiffin was fined £1,375 after being found guilty.

He was also ordered to pay £1,567 in costs and a £550 victim surcharge, taking the total to £3,492.

A collection order was made, and he was ordered to pay £50 a month from September 30.

Victoria Tiffin was fined £150 and ordered to pay £1,567 in costs and a £60 victim surcharge.

Her total financial penalty was £1,777.

The court ordered deductions from benefits to recover the money, with the outstanding balance due by September 16.

Together, the three defendants were ordered to pay £7,522.
