A new creative neighbourhood made from repurposed shipping containers is set to transform the heart of Middlesbrough.

Little Boro will turn Centre Square into a colourful container village for Middlesbrough Art Week, with exhibitions, performances, workshops and interactive installations celebrating the town’s creativity.

Running from September 24 to October 3, the project was inspired by the layout of early Middlesbrough and forms part of this year's Middlesbrough Art Week theme, An Island of Neighbours.

Organisers say the village reflects the “Made in Middlesbrough” identity behind the town’s bid to become UK City of Culture 2029.

  Middlesbrough Art Week returns on September 24. 

*(Image: Tees Valley Combined Authority)*

The project will give locals and visitors space to explore art, share ideas and meet creatives from across the region.

Visitors enter Little Boro through an archway designed by internationally renowned artist Morag Myerscough before exploring activities across the village.

One of the main attractions at the event is Boro 2099: Middlesbrough Reimagined - a miniature city created by 100 participants imagining Middlesbrough’s future, complete with gardens, lakes, bridges, rabbit cafés and even tiny Boro kits.

Middlesbrough creative group Sawdust will display interactive structures, while local artists and students showcase work exploring identity, heritage and community.

Highlights include artwork made with Middlesbrough residents celebrating local heroes, a film inspired by Teesside’s coastline and club culture, and a large sculpture reflecting the town’s multicultural communities.

  Centre Square will host 'Little Boro' as part of Middlesbrough Art Week. 

*(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

Deputy Mayor Philippa Storey, Middlesbrough Council's executive member for culture, said: "Middlesbrough Art Week is nationally renowned and Little Boro is a perfect celebration of the creativity and energy of our cultural scene.

"As we make our case to become UK City of Culture 2029 and look ahead to our bicentenary in 2030, projects like Little Boro show the ambition, imagination and spirit that can help shape Middlesbrough's next chapter."

Storey said the space was open to everyone and urged residents to visit the village for themselves.

Liam Slevin, director of Middlesbrough Art Week, said: "You don't need to know anything about contemporary art to come along.

"Visitors can explore an exhibition, make something, bring the family, meet an artist, stop for 10 minutes or stay and discover the whole village."

Free workshops, talks and family activities will run throughout the 10-day event, giving people chances to meet artists, try something new and join Middlesbrough’s growing cultural scene.

Visitors can explore Little Boro free of charge in Centre Square between September 24 and October 3 as part of Middlesbrough Art Week.

Middlesbrough Art Week has become the largest contemporary arts festival in the North East, showcasing dozens of local artists alongside hundreds of international artists each September.
