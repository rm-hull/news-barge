PREVIOUS commentators assumed that because John Braine was Librarian at Bingley, he based his novel Room at the Top on events that primarily happened there.

He was in fact also Librarian at Newbiggin by the Sea, Northumberland and at Dalton, Kirklees. His experiences at these locations plus Bingley helped shape the final published story.

In a 1969 documentary A World of My Own, Braine stated: “If you build up towns in a novel and use a real place in its entirety, you’re far too much bound down. The best thing is to build a place of your own.”

l Idle - Bomber Shot Down.

On the evening of May 1941, a Junkers bomber was hit and crashed into houses in Idle, killing four people.

Amongst these were the mother, father and grandmother of baby Garth Boyd who, despite severe burns, survived.

  Scene of the German Junkers bomber which crashed in Idle in 1941.


John Braine, then a 19-year-old ARP Warden, was a witness. The event planted in his mind the seeds of a story about a tragically orphaned child who grows up with a ruthless desire to succeed.

In the final version of Room at the Top Joe Lampton, the main character, returns to the fictitious town of Dufton to revisit the home of his parents, who were killed by a bomb, leaving him an orphan. Dufton is a grim, working-class industrial town and becomes associated with loss, poverty and a life Joe wants to leave behind.

Joe moves to the fictitious Warley to start a new life, improve his social position and claim his position at ‘The Top’.

Events show Joe trying to escape one world and enter another. However, in aspiring to reach the top of society, which equals success in his mind, Joe is willing to give up the morality that his dead parents taught him.

Amazingly, in 1958, Garth Boyd now 17 and an amateur actor, was an extra in the Room at the Top film.

After filming had taken place, he heard Braine being interviewed and being asked how he got the idea for the book. Braine recounted that he had been an ARP warden and had seen a baby boy being pulled out of house wreckage and had often wondered what would have happened had he survived. Garth then made Braine aware that he was that baby and that he was certainly still alive!

l War Service

John Braine joined the Royal Navy, was discharged to a TB hospital and then spent several years in studying for his librarianship qualifications.

The fictitious Joe Lampton joined the RAF and spent time as a prisoner of war, using some of this time to study for his accountancy qualifications. Similar parallels without being the same.

l Theatre

In 1951 Braine had his first play performed at Bingley Little Theatre and met and had a relationship with a colonel’s daughter and amateur actress, before moving to London.

In the novel, joining the local theatre company is Joe Lampton’s attempt to join Warley’s middle/upper-class world, and he becomes involved with the local amateur theatre group.

Here he meets Susan and Alice, both of whom he has relationships with, which are complicated and have far reaching repercussions.

These relationships were thought to be ‘racy’ at the time and were a major reason for the novel’s success.

  A plaque at Newbiggin by the Sea commemorating John Braine .


l Newbiggin by the Sea

In 1954, after a further period in Grassington Sanitorium, Braine moved to Morpeth, working in Newbiggin as Librarian and at Ashington Children’s Library. It seems that he was seeking further atmosphere/detail of the working class/middle class dynamic for his novel.

  A 1956 proof copy of Room at the Top.


In 1969 he declared that Bingley and Morpeth were his favourite small towns and that the river in the fictitious Warley was of Morpeth, not Bingley.

Whilst in Northumberland he visited Seaton Deleval Hall, the home of the Deleval family. Braine claimed that he was thinking about the Delevals when he created the fictitious wealthy Sinclair family.

The Sinclair Folly, which Joe Lampton in the novel climbed up to see, was in Braine’s imagination a combination of St Davids Ruin in Bingley and Seaton Deleval Hall.

  John Braine, second right, at Dalton and Kirklees Library dinner and presentation, 1980.


l Dalton - Kirklees

Early 1957 saw Braine working as Dalton Librarian, still researching prior to his novel being published in March.

By June, due to the immediate success of the book he was able to give up work to concentrate on writing. In October he and his family moved to Bingley and could afford to pay cash for their new home.
