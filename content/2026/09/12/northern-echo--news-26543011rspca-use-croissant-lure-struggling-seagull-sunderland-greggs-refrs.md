A croissant helped save the day in an unusual wildlife rescue outside a city bakery.

RSPCA officers used the pastry to lure a seagull into a safe area outside a Greggs in Sunderland so they could catch and free the bird, which was struggling to fly due to fishing line tangled around its legs.

The rescue took place on Monday, September 7, after concerned members of the public contacted the RSPCA.

It was the first wildlife rescue for Trainee Animal Rescue Officer (ARO) Anth Hodgson, who attended the scene with his mentor, RSPCA Inspector Carl Larsson.

   

*(Image: RSPCA)*

Mr Hodgson said: "Witnesses said the gull was struggling to get around and had red string tied around his legs.

"We went along to the High Street and quickly located him and, although his mobility had been affected by the line around his legs, he still gave us the run-around."

Staff at a nearby Greggs gave the team a croissant, which they crumbled up and used to lure the gull down from a lamppost and into the bakery entrance.

They were then able to corner and catch the bird with a net.

The team then carefully restrained the gull and cut away the fishing line.

Fortunately, the bird had not suffered any injuries and was released immediately.

Mr Hodgson said: "It was my first proper wildlife job and I’m really proud of how we dealt with it and the outcome.

"It’s always so special to see a wildlife animal running or flying back out into the wild where they belong, knowing that we’ve probably saved their life that day."

The RSPCA is now using the incident to highlight the dangers of discarded fishing gear.

Inspector Larsson said: "Fishing litter can be extremely hazardous to wildlife."

"Sadly we’re called regularly to wild animals - particularly birds - who have become entangled in lines and hooks which have done major damage to them.

"We know the majority of anglers are very responsible, but it only takes one piece of discarded kit to cause birds like this gull a great deal of suffering."

The RSPCA is urging anglers to dispose of all fishing litter responsibly to help protect wildlife.
