Drive west through Teesdale and the landscape starts to change.

Towns shrink into villages, the valley narrows, and rolling countryside gives way to dramatic moorland.

At the centre of it all is Middleton-in-Teesdale.

Many visitors pass through this traditional County Durham village on the way to High Force, missing one of the Durham Dales’ most characterful communities.

Stone buildings, independent shops, cafés and pubs crowd the centre, with the River Tees and open North Pennines countryside close by.

The North Pennines National Landscape calls Middleton the “capital” of Upper Teesdale and a base for exploring the dale.

## Upper Teesdale’s capital

For a small place, Middleton is lively enough to reward a wander.

Businesses promoted by This is Durham include The Tees'pot, Samuel James Deli Cafe, the Teesdale Hotel, the Forresters Hotel and Restaurant, Middleton Antiques and Interiors and the village fish-and-chip shop.

That mix helps distinguish Middleton from some of the tiny settlements further up the dale.

Come for a walk or waterfall, then stay for lunch, coffee or a browse instead of treating the village as a starting point.

Behind the pretty streets lies a history shaped by industry.

In the mid-19th century, Middleton was the London Lead Mining Company’s headquarters, and its expansion reshaped communities across the upper dale.

As mining expanded, the company transformed Middleton, leaving buildings that still shape the village’s character.


## Waterfalls on the doorstep

But the countryside beyond Middleton gives the village its extraordinary setting.

Continue west along the B6277 and one of County Durham’s best-known landmarks soon comes into view.

At High Force, the River Tees plunges over the Whin Sill, surrounded by woodland and Upper Teesdale’s increasingly wild landscape.

A path from the High Force Hotel site leads through the woods to the waterfall. Check conditions before travelling because bad weather can sometimes affect access.

But High Force is far from the only reason to explore this stretch of the river.

Further along the Tees, Low Force and the Bowlees waterfalls show the river in a very different mood.

The North Pennines National Landscape recommends Bowlees Visitor Centre as a base for exploring High and Low Force.

The broader landscape is equally impressive.

Moorland, crags, white farmhouses and barns define Upper Teesdale as the road heads west towards Forest-in-Teesdale.

## A village made for walkers

Middleton’s setting means many visitors arrive in walking boots.

The Teesdale Way follows the river, while other routes link Middleton with Mickleton and Romaldkirk.

A former railway route towards Barnard Castle is also popular with walkers and cyclists.

For a gentler outing, follow the road through the dale for some of County Durham’s finest scenery.

Middleton works because it gives visitors somewhere to return to afterwards.

After a day out, visitors can return to the village for tea and cake, a pub meal or fish and chips.

High Force will inevitably remain the landmark that draws many people into Upper Teesdale.

But Middleton-in-Teesdale shows why communities around famous attractions should not be treated as places to drive through.

Its mining history gives it character, its businesses give it life and its setting opens the way to increasingly spectacular countryside.

Barnard Castle may have the bigger name and High Force the bigger pictures, but Middleton quietly holds Upper Teesdale together.

For walking, waterfalls, independent shops and some of County Durham’s finest countryside, make Middleton the destination, not just part of the journey.
