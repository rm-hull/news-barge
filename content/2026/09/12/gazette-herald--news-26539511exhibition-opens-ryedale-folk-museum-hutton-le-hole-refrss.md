An artistic pilgrimage through some of the North’s most historic landscapes is the focus of a new exhibition.

Simon Crawford: Shining Lights of the North opens on September 12 at Ryedale Folk Museum in Hutton-le-Hole and runs until November 1.

The exhibition features sketches and paintings by artist Simon Crawford, inspired by the Cistercian abbeys of Rievaulx, Byland, and Fountains.

Jennifer Smith, director at Ryedale Folk Museum, said: "Simon’s work combines his fascination with landscape, history and artistic tradition in a really thoughtful way.

"These abbeys have captivated artists for generations, and it’s very inspiring to see the ways that Simon has returned to them throughout the seasons, paying tribute whilst responding very much in his own style."

Simon’s artworks reflect a year-long journey along the Shining Lights of the North Way, a walking route that links the three abbeys and follows the historic paths once travelled by monks.

Jennifer said: "The exhibition has a strong sense of this journey and of looking closely at places which are both familiar and deeply layered with history.

"Simon’s work gives visitors the opportunity to look again at these extraordinary places.

"The exhibition really connects the present-day landscape with its history and stories across centuries."

By revisiting the abbeys throughout the year, Simon has explored how changing light, weather, and atmosphere shape each site.

His work is part of a long tradition of artists inspired by these landscapes, including JMW Turner, John Sell Cotman, and Thomas Girtin.

Simon said: "This exhibition is an exploration of the landscape in front of me, and my own feelings about a place, whilst being very aware of the ghosts of the artists who came before me."

The exhibition is housed in the museum’s Art Gallery and is free to visit, as it is not part of the paid entry area.

The gallery is open daily except Fridays.
