The UK's most stolen individual car model in 2026 has been revealed in new data.

The data, analysed by Tempcover and sourced from the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA), covers cars stolen between January 1 and June 30.

Electric and hybrid vehicles showed up in increased numbers in the overall top 20, which marked a shift in theft patterns.

For the whole of 2025, no fully electric cars were in the top 20, but four are included for the first half of 2026.


However, one car model topped the rankings for being the most stolen, and had a bit of a gap to second place.

## What was the UK's most stolen car in the first half of 2026?

The Nissan Juke Tekna CVT was the most stolen car for the first six months of 2026, with 197 thefts recorded.

This put it 37 ahead of the second most stolen model, the Toyota C-HR Dynamic HEV CVT, which saw 160 thefts.

Jake Lambert, temporary insurance expert at Tempcover, said: "This goes beyond just one car model; it signals a distinct tactical shift in vehicle crime.

"While high-value cars remain targets, the DVLA data highlights the heightened security risk facing mass-market keyless vehicles."

Discussing why the Nissan Juke Tekna CVT might have topped the list, Jake explained: "High-volume crossovers like the Juke Tekna sit right at the intersection of widespread road presence and accessible technology.


"Because the Tekna is a high-spec trim, it features keyless entry and push-button start as standard, leaving it exposed to relay attacks, where thieves intercept fob signals to bypass the immobiliser."

He noted the risk is not unique to the Juke, but common across most modern keyless vehicles.

Jake added: "High production volumes simply mean more cars on the road running the same technology, which pushes them higher in the national statistics."

## How to stop your car from being stolen

Some criminals use relay attacks to unlock cars, so you can combat this by placing the keys inside a metal box or signal-blocking wallet to block the signal.

The RAC also recommends parking in a well-lit public area to reduce the chances.

It adds: "If you need to use a car park, try to find one that's security patrolled, covered by CCTV, or that is in a secure area."


Utilising a steering lock or brake lock can also be helpful, and it could be useful to install a tracking device in case a thief gets away with your car.
