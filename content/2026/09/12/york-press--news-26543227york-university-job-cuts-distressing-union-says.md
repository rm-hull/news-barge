There have been "significant spikes in workload" for staff after more than 600 roles were axed at the University of York, a union has said.

The University and College Union (UCU) also said there have been "increases in reports of anxiety and stress-related issues at work" after the cuts at the university.

A University of York spokesperson said it has been a "difficult and unsettling time for our community", adding that it moved "swiftly to protect the quality of our teaching and the student experience and maintain our position as one of the UK's top universities".

It comes after figures published by the Times revealed that there was a reduction of 660 roles at the University of York in the last year — the third highest percentage change in the sector at 12.5 per cent, falling behind the University of Huddersfield and Leeds Beckett.

The findings followed the University of York announcing a large-scale voluntary severance scheme in 2024 as it looked to save £34 million.

A spokesperson for the UCU's York branch said the cuts at the University of York have had a "distressing impact on the staff who have lost their jobs", and resulted in the Russell Group university "losing so much expertise and experience from our staff base".

They said the cuts have "hit hard across academic departments and support services".

The union's spokesperson also said it has "seen significant spikes in workload for remaining staff, with consequent increases in reports of anxiety and stress-related issues at work". "This has inevitably affected the experience of students, but we have been really touched by the level of support and understanding from our student community."

They added that the UCU has been "campaigning against the erosion of staff pay" across the sector for almost two decades, adding: "The sector has well-documented continuing problems with casualisation of the workforce and a proliferation of precarious short-term contracts for staff."

The union called for universities to be "institutions dedicated to teaching and research, and income generation should be a means to that end, not an end in itself".

Its spokesperson added that "all of the ways to generate income" suggested by the University of York "involve the work of staff". "The student experience is likewise sustained by the work of staff."

They also said the government's "current funding model for the university sector is unfit for purpose: it burdens students and their families without providing real benefits to universities".

The University of York spokesperson said: "York was one of the first to take comprehensive action to tackle the financial challenges many other universities now face, moving swiftly to protect the quality of our teaching and the student experience and maintain our position as one of the UK's top universities.

"We were able to achieve the vast majority of the savings through lowering operational costs and voluntary severance schemes, which returned staffing levels to 2022 levels.

"This has been a difficult and unsettling time for our community, but we are now focused on creating new ways to generate income so we can reinvest further in the student experience."
