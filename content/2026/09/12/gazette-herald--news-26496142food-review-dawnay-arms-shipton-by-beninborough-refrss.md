We are very fortunate to have so many wonderful venues to drink and dine in, where operators do their best, producing top notch food and service at a good price.

But is it enough in today's hyper-competitive world?

A case in point is the Dawnay Arms in Shipton-By-Beningbrough.

I discovered this delightful and traditional village pub on a trip with York CAMRA one day. The beers were good and I saw the food menu, which looked fine.

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

I returned soon after for Sunday lunch with mum and dad and yes, the food was great, and the service was lovely, from friendly staff. It was reasonably priced too. So happy with everything, we returned several times.

But sadly, not long after, the former landlord cut the number of trading days to three before eventually leaving, saying he could not make it work.

  The Sunday menu 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

The Dawnay Arms is owned by Punch Pubs and has since re-opened with new people in, with it open daily and serving food from Tuesday to Sunday.

Tripadvisor confirms the venue is under new management and it gives the Dawnay Arms an exceptional 4.9 stars out of five. Google also awards a rather good 4.6 stars out of five.

Seeing the Dawnay Arms back open and seemingly better than ever, we thought we would pay a return visit.

  The Ham Hock and Tempura Prawns starters 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

We arrived just after 1pm, having booked, parking at the front, but there is also a car park at the back.

We entered and were soon shown to our tables.

There were a few other diners but sadly, the place had a somewhat empty feel.

At least there were other drinkers in the beer garden at the rear.

We ordered our drinks, with Dad going for a pint of Boltmaker (£4.70), me a pint of Farmers Blonde (£4.60 and mum half of Guinness (£2.70). All three were well-kept and good.

  The lamb mains 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

We ordered our meals, going for a Ham Hock (£8.95) and Tempura Prawns (£8.95) to share between us.

The server brought three small plates and we all got to try both, with us all enjoying the two dishes. Dad especially liked the Ham Hock, saying it was tender and full of flavour, which it was. Mum is a lover of prawns and they also were a delight. They were tender, succulent and a little juicy.

It all made for a very pleasant start to the meal, with decent portions to boot.

  The beef mains 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

The mains soon followed, with mum going for the lamb roast (£15.95), dad going for the salmon (which only came in large at £17.95) and me settling for the beef (£15.95).

We all remarked at the amount of food, feeling the portions were large and generous.

There was plenty of choice on the plate with a delicious, smooth mash, swede, red cabbage, baked potato, stuffing and broccoli. There was also a dish of cauliflower cheese to share and each plate was topped with a large Yorkshire pudding.

Now, the meat and fish portions were generous and with mum not having the appetite she used to, I can say the lamb was deliciously tender and tasty. The beef was too, if a little overdone for my taste. Dad was more than happy with his salmon, so if dad is happy, it must have been good.

  A table to treasure 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

All the vegetables were cooked right, with highlights being the smooth and creamy mashed potato, the swede, the red cabbage and the cauliflower cheese, not that there was anything wrong with the rest. The vegetables were more than enough for us all.

We did our best with the Yorkies, but struggled, though I have to add the centre of mine was soft like a pancake and that part was a real treat.

We ordered a couple of extra halves and soon it was time to pay the bill.

This came to a very agreeable £84.40, so I rounded it up to £90 when I paid at the bar.

  The pub interior 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Fortunately, some more customers had entered, some dining at the front, some taking a large table nearby and others were drinking at the back.

But sadly the Dawnay Arms was not busy and it deserves to be.

This is a delightful, traditional pub in a pleasant village just a few miles from York on the roadside.

We had all enjoyed our food. It wasn’t gastropub or anything too fancy, just traditional, pub classic cooking done right. The service and value also could not be faulted.

  The bar 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

The only thing lacking was atmosphere due to a lack of customers.

I am not alone in giving the praise this lovely pub deserves.

The latest review on Tripadvisor lauds the Dawnay Arms as “Friendly and atmospheric with quality food and service,” giving it five stars out of five.

The review continued: “What a welcome! I rang the evening before to book a table and had a delightful conversation with the landlady who was most informative and helpful. The evening itself was as good as expected with a great menu and a warm reception.”

The latest Google review also gives five stars out of five.

It said: “Went out to celebrate our anniversary at this friendly pub which has recently welcomed new landlords Claire and Alun.

“Excellent food which was piping hot, well-presented and very tasty. We had lasagne which was clearly homemade and full of flavour. It came with garlic bread which was perfectly cooked - lightly toasted and just the right amount of garlic. Food came with homemade coleslaw and a dressed salad which was fresh and crunchy.

“Since we were celebrating, we also ordered a portion of chips. These were homemade chunky cut chips which were well seasoned and cooked to perfection - crispy on the outside and soft in the middle.”

  A table to treasure 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

They added: “Will most definitely be back again and was great to see the village pub full again with a relaxed hubbub of chatter.”

Let’s just hope the relative quiet of the recent Sunday was a one-off as the new publicans deserve to succeed and prosper at what they and their lovely staff are delivering.

And like the last reviewer, we too will be back.
