If you've ever driven into Durham and spotted signs for Houghall, there's a good chance you've immediately wondered whether you're saying it correctly.

The name of this historic corner of Durham City catches out students, visitors and even some County Durham residents thanks to its unusual spelling and notoriously confusing "ough" combination.

Many people instinctively pronounce it as "Hoe-all" or "Huff-all", but locals have a very different way of saying it.

## How to pronounce Houghall

The correct pronunciation is HOFF-all.

The first syllable rhymes with "off", while the second sounds exactly like the word "all".

It's a pronunciation that surprises many newcomers because the spelling bears little resemblance to how the name is actually spoken.

The confusion comes from the fact that "ough" can be pronounced in several different ways in English.

Compare words such as "though", "through", "rough", "cough" and "bough", and it is easy to see why visitors struggle when they encounter Houghall for the first time.

For Durham locals, however, it's simply HOFF-all.

## Why the name is so unusual

Like many place names across County Durham, Houghall has roots stretching back centuries.

The first part of the name is believed to be linked to an old word describing a hill, ridge or spur of land.

That fits its location on elevated ground overlooking the River Wear on the eastern side of Durham City.

Over time, spellings remained largely unchanged while pronunciation evolved, leaving modern visitors with a place name that looks very different from how it sounds.

Houghall is far from alone in this respect.

Across the North East, centuries of Anglo-Saxon, Viking and Norman influence have created place names that routinely puzzle outsiders.

## A rural corner of Durham City

Today, Houghall is best known as the home of East Durham College's Houghall Campus, one of the region's leading centres for land-based education.

The campus specialises in areas including agriculture, horticulture, animal care, environmental conservation and countryside management.

Despite being only a short distance from Durham city centre and Durham Cathedral, the area retains a strong rural character.

Open fields, woodland and riverside paths surround the estate, offering a surprising slice of countryside on the edge of the city.

The area is also home to Houghall Discovery Centre, run by Durham Wildlife Trust, which welcomes visitors exploring nearby nature reserves and walking routes along the River Wear.


## Not the only County Durham place name people get wrong

Houghall is just one of several County Durham names that regularly leave visitors scratching their heads.

Nearby Cassop is pronounced "Cass-up", while Tow Law rhymes more with towel than toe.
