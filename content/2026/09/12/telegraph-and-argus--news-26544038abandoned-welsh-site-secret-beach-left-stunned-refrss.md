Hidden away on the north coast of Anglesey sits an abandoned brickworks with its own secret beach - arguably one of the most fascinating places you will find anywhere in the UK.

Porth Wen Brickworks dates back to the 1850s and was used to make firebricks from local quartzite.

It has sat abandoned for 77 years, having closed in 1949, and is now the perfect spot for an adventure.

   The Porth Wen Brickworks has sat abandoned for more than 77 years. 

*(Image: Patrick Glover)*

I have written several stories about the Port Wen Brickworks over the years, with it regularly named among the UK's best hidden beaches and coastal works .

I became so intrigued by the site and its history that I had to go and visit.

And boy, did it exceed all expectations.

## How to get to the Porth Wen Brickworks

There are several routes that lead to the abandoned brickworks site, all of which are on the AllTrails app.

I recommend the Bull Bay to Porth Wen Brickworks (and back) circular route.


Not only does it lead to the Porth Wen Brickworks, but it also takes you through the Anglesey countryside and offers picturesque coastal views over Bull Bay.

You may even catch a glimpse of dolphins swimming off the coast, if you're lucky.

Plus, you can finish the walk with a visit to the Trecastell pub for a pint and a feed.

The circular walk from Bull Bay is around 8km long, but shorter options are available.

## Why Porth Wen Brickworks is an 'out of this world' experience

As you head across the North Wales countryside (from Bull Bay), the Brickworks suddenly rises from below the clifftops across Porth Wen Bay.

As you make your way around the clifftops towards the brickworks, you get a great view from above, building the anticipation.

   Have you been to Porth Wen Brickworks before? 

*(Image: Patrick Glover)*

Eventually, you reach the gate above the Porth Wen Brickworks and descend a steep trail to the abandoned site (take care when walking down).

Once at the bottom, what you find can only be described as out of this world.

You can wander through the eerie remains of the brickworks, including the domed beehive kilns, chimneys, hoppers, and old worker cottages.


Venture past a two-storey building, formerly used as a storage facility, which has fallen into disrepair, and you will find a secret beach.

It is the perfect place for a swim, sunbathing, or a picnic while enjoying the surrounding views.
