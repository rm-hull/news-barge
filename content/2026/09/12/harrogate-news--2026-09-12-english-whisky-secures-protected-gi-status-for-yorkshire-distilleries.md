**HARROGATE** — Distillers across North Yorkshire are celebrating this weekend after English Whisky officially secured protected Geographical Indication (GI) status from the government, marking a historic legal milestone for the fast-growing regional spirits sector.

The new statutory designation, confirmed today [12 September 2026] by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra), legally recognises English Whisky as a distinct spirit category. The framework introduces strict regulations governing production provenance, dictating that any spirit carrying the “English Whisky” label must be produced entirely from UK cereal grains, mashed, fermented, distilled, and matured within England.

The legal protection places English producers on equal statutory footing with established Scotch, Welsh, and Irish whisky families. The surge in domestic production has seen the number of operational English distilleries climb from 46 in 2023 to more than 70 today, with industry estimates valuing maturing stocks held in casks across the country at approximately £1 billion.

## North Yorkshire Distilleries Toast Historic Milestone

The regulatory rollout directly impacts several high-profile independent producers operating across the North Yorkshire agricultural footprint. Key regional beneficiaries include **Whittaker’s Distillery** based in Nidderdale,**Ellers Farm Distillery** near Stamford Bridge,**Cooper King Distillery** in Sutton-on-the-Forest, and**The Spirit of Yorkshire Distillery**, famed for its Filey Bay single malt portfolio.

Local producers have welcomed the announcement, noting that the rigid legal framework protects the integrity of their investment while reinforcing the link between regional distillation and British farming infrastructure.


**Jenni Ashwood, Marketing Director at Spirit of Yorkshire Distillery:**

This GI has been painstakingly developed over a number of years to protect the quality, integrity and focus on provenance and flavour, that has defined English Whisky over the past two decades. It provides the solidly robust framework from which the category can flourish and thrive long into the future. As farmers, as well as distillers, we’re particularly pleased that the GI stipulates that to be labelled an ‘English whisky’, it must be made from UK cereal grain, thereby directly supporting British agriculture.


## Strict HMRC Verification and Global Export Push

To utilize the new protected status in commercial trade, English whisky houses must undergo formal clinical auditing and verification via HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) under the strict Spirit Drinks Verification Scheme. The regulatory system covers all phases of production, including mashing, fermentation, distillation, maturation, and final bottling layout protocols, with baseline guidance due to be published shortly.

Government leaders stated that the expansion of the sector plays a critical role in driving skilled manufacturing employment and tourism footfall into rural economies, with bottles currently exported to more than 30 international markets.


**Dame Angela Eagle, Environment Secretary:**

The UK is home to world-class whisky production, from grain to glass. Standing proudly alongside Scotch, Welsh and Irish whiskies, this new protected status is recognition of English Whisky’s distinctive character alongside the expert craftsmanship that goes into its production. It’s a milestone that will be felt far beyond the distillery gates, bringing jobs, investment and pride to communities across the country.



**Morag Garden, CEO of the English Whisky Guild:**

Securing GI status is a landmark achievement for English Whisky and recognition of the quality and innovation across our growing industry. We are grateful to Defra and to everyone who contributed to this collaborative effort. This milestone will support future growth and international recognition for English Whisky.



The local industry rollout follows extensive consultation loops led by the English Whisky Guild, design options to shield independent distillers from counterfeit foreign imports, and ongoing efforts to secure domestic supply lines across the agricultural landscape.
