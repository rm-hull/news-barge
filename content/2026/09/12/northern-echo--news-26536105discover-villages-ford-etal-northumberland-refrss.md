Northumberland is packed with villages boasting spectacular views and ancient buildings.

Fewer still pack a medieval castle, Victorian art, a working watermill, a steam railway and the county’s only thatched pub into one day out.

That is what sets Ford and Etal apart.

Tucked into the valley of the River Till in north Northumberland, the two villages and nearby Heatherslaw sit just a few miles inland from some of the county's most famous destinations.

Bamburgh and Holy Island draw visitors towards the coast, while the Cheviot Hills dominate the landscape to the south.

Yet Ford and Etal remain far less famous.

*(Image: Harry Little/CAMERA CLUB)*

Visit Northumberland calls the estate and Heatherslaw two of rural Northumberland’s “best hidden gems”.

## A steam railway through the countryside

The most unusual way to see the area is by steam railway.

Heatherslaw Light Railway is England’s most northerly steam railway.

The 15-inch-gauge line runs 6.4km between Heatherslaw and Etal Castle, with return journeys taking about 50 minutes.

The route follows the River Till, where passengers may spot birds and, occasionally, otters.

Families can travel between two of the estate’s main attractions without getting back in the car.

Heatherslaw is also home to a reminder of the river’s long history of use.

## A mill working for more than 700 years

Heatherslaw has had a mill for more than 700 years.

The corn mill still uses water power to grind locally grown wheat into flour, showing visitors a process that has largely vanished from everyday life.


The mill is powered by a 16-foot waterwheel driving three pairs of millstones to produce stoneground wholemeal flour from locally grown wheat.

It is a working mill, not a static reconstruction, with the machinery and noise of real flour production.

From Heatherslaw, the steam railway heads to Etal and its much older attraction.

Robert Manners built Etal Castle in the 14th century to defend the area during repeated wars between England and Scotland.

English Heritage now cares for the ruins, which overlook the surrounding countryside.

The peaceful Border countryside hides a past marked by repeated violence.

In September 1513, James IV of Scotland captured both castles before marching to the nearby Battle of Flodden, where he was killed.

## Northumberland’s only thatched pub

Etal’s quiet charm makes it easy to slow down.

The Black Bull stands beside the village shop and tearoom, and Visit Northumberland calls it Northumberland’s only thatched pub.

The pub sits within sight of Etal Castle, making it an easy stop after exploring the ruins or leaving the train.

  Ford Castle 

*(Image: Harry Little/CAMERA CLUB)*

In Ford, the character changes again.

Lady Waterford Hall was commissioned in 1860 as a village school.

Louisa, Marchioness of Waterford, decorated its walls with huge paintings influenced by the Pre-Raphaelite movement.

Over 21 years, Lady Waterford painted 16 life-size biblical watercolour panels using local tenants, farmworkers and schoolchildren as models.

The former school is now a gallery and village hall, offering a striking contrast to the estate’s castles and industrial heritage.

The wider Ford and Etal estate also has walking and cycling routes, Ford Moss Nature Reserve and independent shops selling local produce and crafts.

## A whole day, not just one attraction

The best way to visit may be to skip the single-attraction approach altogether.

Start at Heatherslaw’s corn mill, take the railway to Etal, explore the castle, stop for lunch or a drink, then head to Ford and Lady Waterford Hall.

Together, they turn a string of small attractions into a full day out.

The estate’s main heritage attractions are seasonal, so check opening dates before travelling, especially outside spring and summer.

Ford and Etal’s Christmas Market returns on November 28 and 29, with more than 70 stalls, entertainment and vintage buses linking Ford, Etal and Heatherslaw.

For most of the year, the appeal is simpler.

Ford and Etal pack Northumberland into one compact escape: beautiful villages, Border history, local food, art, river scenery and a steam train linking it all.
