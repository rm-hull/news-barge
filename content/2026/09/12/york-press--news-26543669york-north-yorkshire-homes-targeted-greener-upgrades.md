A new strategy aims to cut carbon emissions and energy bills by transforming homes and buildings across York and North Yorkshire.

The region's Retrofit Strategy, adopted by the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority (YNYCA), sets out plans to deliver greener, warmer and more affordable buildings.

The strategy supports the use of low-carbon technologies such as solar panels, air source heat pumps and advanced insulation to improve existing buildings.

It also forms part of the region’s wider goal of becoming England’s first carbon-negative region by 2040.

York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith said: "Everyone in York and North Yorkshire deserves a warm, comfortable home with energy bills they can afford.

"We're proud of our county's heritage, but with more than a quarter of our homes built a century ago, we have the opportunity to cut energy costs by upgrading our housing stock with modern technology.

"Our new Retrofit Strategy sets out a clear plan to go further and faster to make our buildings fit for the future while tackling fuel poverty, building local supply chains and unlocking new green jobs."

Targets include raising 250,000 homes to Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) rating C or higher and retrofitting more than 62 per cent of existing business premises by 2038.

Carolyn Frank, of the Federation of Master Builders and chair of the Retrofit Strategy Partner Advisory Group, said: "The adoption of the strategy means the start of increased delivery and unlocking the opportunity that all partners have identified after thorough research and knowledge sharing.

"With so many homeowners considering not just saving money through retrofit for energy efficiency but also making home improvements for increased comfort, we need more skilled, high-quality builders to deliver for them and investment in upskilling will be needed to ensure this huge amount of work is done to a high standard."

Nick Atkin, chair of the York and North Yorkshire Housing Partnership, added: "Our region is home to some of the country's oldest and least energy-efficient homes, many of which were never designed for today's climate challenges or energy costs.

"Our partnership strongly supports this strategy.

"This is not just an environmental priority.

"It is an economic and social necessity that will improve lives and support sustainable growth."

The strategy was formally adopted by YNYCA’s cabinet on Friday.

Retrofitting work is already underway, including improvements at Honeysuckle House, an independent living community in York.
