A sapling from the well-known Sycamore Gap tree will be planted at a care village near York.

The Lavender Fields Care Village in Barmby Moor will receive the sapling as part of a newly developed sensory garden designed for people living with dementia.

The planting will take place on Monday, September 21, at 1pm.

Chris Mitchell, chairman of Lavender Fields Care Village, said: "We are incredibly proud to be opening our new sensory garden and to be doing so in partnership with Dementia Forward.

"The planting of the Sycamore Gap Tree of Hope makes the occasion particularly meaningful."

The sensory garden has been created in partnership with Dementia Forward and offers a peaceful, stimulating space for residents, families, and visitors to enjoy together.

The 'Tree of Hope' will serve as a symbol of resilience and remembrance.

Jill Quinn, chief executive officer of Dementia Forward, said: "We were inspired to apply for a Sycamore Gap sapling by a woman caring for her husband, who has young-onset dementia, and felt it could be a powerful symbol."

The event will be attended by residents, families, staff, and civic guests, including the chairman of East Riding of Yorkshire Council, Linda Johnson, and the mayor of Pocklington, Roly Cronshaw.
