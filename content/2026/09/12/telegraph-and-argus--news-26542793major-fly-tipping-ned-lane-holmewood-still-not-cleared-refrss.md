A major fly-tip has still not been cleared, two weeks after councillors raised it directly with senior managers at Bradford Council.

The dumped waste on Ned Lane, in the Holme Wood and Bierley area, is believed to be the result of organised commercial fly-tipping.

Councillors say the operation would have required a heavy goods vehicle and industrial equipment.

Matt Edwards, councillor for Holmewood and Bierley and leader of the Green Party group on Bradford Council, said: "Two weeks ago I raised this directly with senior managers at Bradford Council and demanded they deal with it.

  The fly-tipping Cllr Matt Edwards is referring to 

*(Image: Cllr Matt Edwards)*

"I was given assurances it would be prioritised.

"Two weeks on, it has not moved."

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

## 'But out here in BD4, we get excuses'

He said the council has not visited the site to investigate or gather evidence, raising concerns that no effort is being made to identify those responsible.

Mr Edwards said: "Worse still, it does not appear that anyone from the council has even attended to gather evidence.

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

"That means nobody is even attempting to identify who did this.

"What gets me is that when a big fly-tip turns up in other parts of the district, the council can shift it quickly.

"They find the resources.

"They get it done.

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

"But out here in BD4, we get excuses.

"This was not a bloke with a transit van.

"It took a heavy goods vehicle and industrial equipment to pack this load.

"Somebody planned it and somebody got paid for it.

"That is organised crime, and it is being treated like a litter problem."

## One of the most fly-tipped roads in the district

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

In January, rubbish dumped at the entrance to Black Carr Woods was left for months despite being reported.

Councillors say the problem has now returned to the same location, which they attribute to the council's failure to take robust action.

Mr Edwards said: "The ask for Bradford Council is simple.

"Get this cleared and get enforcement cameras in these lanes so the people responsible actually get caught, prosecuted and have their vehicles taken off them.

"Until that happens, we will be stood here again in another two weeks looking at the same mess."

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

The latest incident follows a visit to Bradford by the Prime Minister, during which he announced a national crackdown on waste crime.

## Resident is sick of 'having having to constantly fight'

A resident living in the area, who wishes to remain anonymous, told the Telegraph & Argus that the problem extends beyond fly-tipping.

The resident said: "I’m simply being honest about how many of us living in the area feel.

"I have lived here for around 15 years, and during that time I honestly cannot say that I have seen any meaningful or sustained improvement in the outer areas of BD4, particularly around Ned Lane, Holme Lane and the surrounding areas.

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

"In fact, it often feels as though residents in these areas are having to constantly fight just to receive the most basic level of services.

"We pay our council tax like everyone else, including many residents who are in the higher council tax bands, yet we seem to have to repeatedly contact the council about things that should be dealt with as part of normal services.

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

"We have had to continually chase road sweeping, report fly-tipping, raise concerns about grit and snow clearance, and complain about overgrown hedges and general maintenance.

"It is incredibly frustrating to feel that the outskirts of BD4 are overlooked."

The resident pointed out that while CCTV was previously installed in the centre of Holme Wood, it was vandalised and cut down.

They said the surrounding areas with persistent fly-tipping remain "largely unprotected."

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

The resident added: "I sincerely hope that residents on the outskirts of the area will not once again feel that investment is concentrated elsewhere while our streets continue to suffer from fly-tipping, poor maintenance and a lack of visible services.

"I completely understand that tackling fly-tipping is difficult and that catching the people responsible isn’t straightforward.

"But I think residents need to see more visible action and investment, rather than continually being asked to report problems and then wait for something to happen."

  Photos of fly-tipping on Ned Lane sent in over the last few days, weeks and months by a local resident 

*(Image: Local resident)*

They welcomed the prospect of a meeting with councillors and council officials, saying it would allow residents to share their experiences and discuss practical solutions.

However, the resident said they felt "saddened and let down" by the ongoing situation.

They said: "After 15 years of living here, it is difficult to feel confident that things are improving when we are still raising many of the same issues year after year.

"We aren’t looking for an argument – we simply want the same level of attention, investment and basic services that residents elsewhere in Bradford receive."

Bradford Council was approached for a comment.
