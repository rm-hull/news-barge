CHILDREN in Yorkshire are calling time on childhood traditions earlier than many parents realise.

A study by My Nametags, a name label company, found that children in Yorkshire and the Humber typically stop calling their parents “mummy” or “daddy” by age eight.

The survey of 1,000 UK parents also revealed that the last bedtime story and hand holding usually ends by age seven and a half, while belief in Father Christmas fades by eight.

Lars B. Andersen, managing director at My Nametags, said: "It’s true what they say, children aren’t small forever and those special moments that parents share with their children, such as holding their hand or putting them to bed, can be gone before you know it.

"As parents, we often don’t realise it’s the last time we did something with our children until after it’s happened, and that can be emotional."

Eighty per cent of parents described these “parenting lasts” as one of the saddest parts of raising children.
