A LOCAL highly-regarded music teacher has opened up about his experiences with homelessness and multiple sclerosis (MS) in an effort to show others that a positive mindset can genuinely change your life.

Nigel Passey, owner of Space Kid Records in Shipley and in Eccleshill, has built up a fantastic reputation for his recording studio and music school supporting young people, including home-educated children and those with special educational needs (SEN).

But it's not been a straightforward journey.

   

*(Image: T&A)*

Originally from the Midlands, Mr Passey found himself living in West Yorkshire and in a difficult relationship in his 20s.

When the relationship abruptly ended, he claims he was forced to continue paying rent for a property he could no longer occupy.

He found himself thrust into homelessness - and everything looked and felt hopeless.

At that time, he had been signed to a record label and was also working in a bar in Chapel Allerton but could not afford hotel - especially with having to make rent each month.

For around three to four months he found himself sofa surfing and sneaking into his place of work to try and get some kip in between shifts.

   

*(Image: T&A)*

He told the T&A: “Cos I had keys for the bar, I would pretend and say 'Bye', then go back in and sleep in there.”

However, this plan only worked when he had the keys.

The rest of the time, he had to wander the streets because sleeping on others sofas' was limited. He could only stay for so long before he was politely asked to leave.

On sofa surfing, he said: “It got to the point where some of the parents, bless them, were like, 'You need to go now'. Bar work meant unsocial hours, getting in late to other people's house.

“If I wasn’t locking up [at work], I was walking round Chapel Allerton, Roundhay, Leeds, until I'd go back into work and everyone would think, ‘Oh Nigel's been home’.”

He would try and get into work as early as he could so he could have a shower - but then one day he collapsed at work.

The homelessness had coincided with the Christmas period so he had been forced to walk round Leeds “in next to nothing, no winter coat, trying to sleep on the streets."

   

*(Image: T&A)*

He was rushed to hospital.

It turned out he had pneumonia.

He explained: “I’d lost a load of weight, had pneumonia, had nowhere to go.”

His brother, who had no idea what had been going on, drove up from Birmingham to see him.

Mr Passey said: “Cos this is the thing, no one knew what was going on.”

Other than his bass player at the time, no one knew of his sorry state of affairs.

His now wife began helping him find flats.

However, he still had to pay rent for his ex-partner, so had a week off the bar shifts and then was back in work.

But then he started getting tremors in his hands and legs.

Doctors told him there was something on his brain.

Mr Passey said: “I went into close down mode and thought screw it, I'm done, this is it, if I'm going out I'm going out on a high type thing.”

He was diagnosed with multiple sclerosis a few months later.

It just so happened that the day he was diagnosed was also the first day of his UK tour - and he was still homeless.

He embarked on a three week tour, and spent a lot of it drinking.

He said: "It wasn’t till after that the penny dropped and I went into a spiral of depression."

But, it was while reading an article where a woman described MS as a 'death sentence', that Nigel decided he wasn’t going to accept that fate for himself.

After reading the article, he pondered “How can I turn this negative into this positive?”

Somewhere along the way he got his accommodation sorted, and his life began to improve.

He’s now had MS twelve years, been in wheelchair six times, tried four different treatments and had multiple relapses - but this hasn't taken the wind out his sails.

He admits being diagnosed with MS in his mid-20s had challenges, such as being helped to go to the toilet.

He describes the time his father in law had to pull him out of a bath at 25 as “degrading”.

Mr Passey is now happily married with two girls.

In his own words he said: "Because all I knew was music, I couldn’t just sit down and do nothing.”

He’s been gigging, working and writing ever since - not to mention setting up Space Kids Records - and the rest is history.
