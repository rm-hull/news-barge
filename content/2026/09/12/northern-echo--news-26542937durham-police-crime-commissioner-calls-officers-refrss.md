A POLICE commissioner has called on the Government to prioritise frontline policing over structural reform.

Durham Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen urged ministers to restore officer numbers towards 2010 levels and address what she calls an "unfair" funding formula, instead of exploring costly police force mergers.

In a public statement, Ms Allen said: "For the past five years, I have lobbied government to return the police officers we have lost and replace the outdated and unfair funding formula, so money follows crime, risk and need."

Durham remains 120 officers short of its 2010 workforce, while neighbouring Cleveland is down by around 200 officers.

Ms Allen said: "They matter when residents want to see neighbourhood police in their towns and villages.

"They matter when we need officers targeting drug dealers and organised crime gangs."

The call for increased funding comes following the deaths of two police officers, PC Matthew Blades and PC Tom Clough, who were among seven people killed in a head-on collision on the A66 involving a car travelling in the wrong direction.

In her statement, Ms Allen also addressed public fears following a series of recent violent incidents.

She said: "We have seen homes rammed and vehicles deliberately set alight.

"These have been extraordinary and deeply disturbing events that have left families grieving and communities frightened."

Ms Allen argued that restoring £10 million to Durham’s budget could fund the 120 officers needed to get back towards 2010 staffing levels.

She criticised the pursuit of structural reforms, such as potential police force mergers, which Greater Manchester Chief Constable Sir Stephen Watson has suggested could run into billions of pounds.

She said: "My message to Government is simple: spend taxpayers’ money strengthening the frontline, not on a costly bureaucratic restructure that the public neither asked for, voted for, nor was promised in any manifesto."
