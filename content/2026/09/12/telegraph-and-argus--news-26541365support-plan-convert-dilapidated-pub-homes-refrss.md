A PLAN to convert a Craven pub closed for six years to residential use has been given the backing of nearby residents.

The Wellington Pub Company submitted plans in January this year for the conversion of the listed Cross Keys at East Marton, near Skipton, to two homes and is still awaiting a decision.

The pub, next to the A59 and overlooking the Leeds and Liverpool Canal, closed in early 2020 and, despite the efforts of two letting agents, remains empty and increasingly dilapidated.

One nearby resident says the proposal will 'breathe new life' into what has become a dilapidated building, while another says it has been sad to see the building deteriorate and adds there are not enough people living in the village to support a pub, adding there are two others fairly close – the Tempest Arms at Elslack, and The Bull at Broughton.

The pub, built in the late 1700s, has a main bar and two restaurant areas; a four-bedroom staff flat, two car parks and an enclosed beer garden overlooking the canal.

Supporting documents with applications for full planning permission and listed building consent say the property has been entirely vacant for a 'considerable amount of time and is becoming increasingly dilapidated'.

It closed in January 2020, even before the Covid-19 pandemic and the following impact on the hospitality sector.

It is stated: "The property was put on the market to let but regrettably this has not yielded any interest and so has remained closed. As would be expected from a property not being in use for such a long period of time, the property is dated and is in need of substantial refurbishment."

It is proposed that the pub is converted into two three-bedroom homes.

The car park at the back would become gardens for each of the properties and the parking area at the front kept as parking and extended into the current decked area.

The separate car park, accessed off the road to the horse livery business and cafe, will be removed, and its access blocked up.

Agents for the Wellington Pub Company say that the Cross Keys will fall into further disrepair if it is left unoccupied.

A marketing report, carried out by Fleurets, leisure property specialists, said details of the property were sent out to more than 6,500 parties, it was advertised on Fleuret's website and there was also a video viewing, which showed the condition of the pub and led to a reduction in the number of viewings.

Fleurets state: "The general feeling is that the amount of work and investment required to make this property fit to trade as a public house is not viable given the limited trade potential of the premises."
