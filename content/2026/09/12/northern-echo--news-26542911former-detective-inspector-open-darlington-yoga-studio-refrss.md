A former detective inspector who turned to yoga after stress forced her to retire early from policing is preparing to open a new studio in Darlington.

Caroline Shields, 50, from Darlington, said the idea behind the studio was to create a welcoming community space for people to improve both their physical and mental wellbeing.

The new venture, Yoga At Number2, has been years in the making for Caroline, who has been teaching yoga in Darlington for the past five years after discovering its benefits for her wellbeing.

Before becoming a yoga teacher, Caroline spent 24 years in policing, working as a detective inspector in the public protection and safeguarding unit.

  Yoga @ Number2 in Darlington. 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

However, she said the demands of the role eventually took its toll.

She said: “I became really unwell with stress and had to take early retirement. A friend suggested yoga and, if I'm honest, I'd never really thought much of it before.

“But it helped me massively. It made me wonder why I'd never discovered it years earlier because it made such a difference to my life.

“That's why I wanted to start teaching. I wanted to share something that had helped me so much with other people.”

  Caroline Shields. 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

Caroline began running classes across Darlington in hired venues but said finding suitable spaces was often a challenge.

The 50-year-old said: “I've been teaching yoga for a few years now and it can be quite difficult hiring space in church halls and community venues.

“Sometimes they're cold, sometimes they're damp, and I started thinking about what it would be like to have a dedicated space.

  Yoga @ Number2 in Darlington. 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

“It started as a little idea, but once I began looking around the town centre it really grew into a dream. I kept thinking, 'I could add this and I could add that', and it gradually became much bigger than just finding somewhere to teach classes.”

The studio will offer a range of classes including traditional yoga sessions, chair yoga, Pilates and Tai Chi, as well as a style known as Ashtanga yoga which Caroline says is currently unavailable elsewhere in Darlington.

  Yoga @ Number2 in Darlington. 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

She believes the studio will fill a gap in the local fitness and wellbeing scene and cater to all abilities.

She said: “We'll have beginner sessions, lunchtime classes for people working in the town centre, evening sessions and weekend workshops.

“I want people to feel they can come through the door whether they've done yoga for years or have never tried it before.”

  Yoga @ Number2 in Darlington. 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

While Caroline is passionate about yoga, she says the studio's wider purpose is to help tackle stress, anxiety and loneliness.

She said: “One thing I've learned over the years is just how much people need connection. Loneliness affects all sorts of people, regardless of age or background.

  Yoga @ Number2 in Darlington. 

*(Image: ANDY FUTERS)*

“I run some daytime classes now where people stay afterwards for a cup of tea and a chat. What started as a yoga class has turned into a genuine community.

“People support each other, meet up outside class and have formed real friendships. I'm incredibly proud of that and I'd love to create even more opportunities for people to feel connected and included.

“I don't want it to feel intimidating or exclusive. I want people to walk through the door and feel comfortable straight away.

“I've always been somebody who wants to help people and that's what this studio is really about.”

The studio is expected to open in Darlington town centre in mid-September, at 2 Chancery Lane.
