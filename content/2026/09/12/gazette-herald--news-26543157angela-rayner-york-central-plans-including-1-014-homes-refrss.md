YEARS of talk is set to be turned into action following the approval of plans for a major York regeneration project, Angela Rayner has said.

The housing secretary's comments follow the approval of more than 1,000 homes, a hotel, new station entrance and offices at York Central.

David Skaith, mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said the decision was a big moment for the city and would strengthen links across the North.

City of York Council's leader Cllr Claire Douglas said it was a major step forward in one of the most significant regeneration projects in the city's history.

It follows the council's planning committee's vote to approve plans for the regeneration of the site off Leeman Road on Thursday, September 10.

Work on the site is expected to start next summer, with the first buildings finished by 2029.

The £2 billion scheme, led by developers McLaren Property and Arlington Real Estate, covers 45 hectares and would see at least 2,500 homes built.

It would grow the city's economy by a fifth, adding around £1.1 billion, and create around 6,500 jobs.

Housing Secretary Ms Rayner said York Central was a fantastic opportunity to transform derelict brownfield land into thousands of new homes, jobs and green spaces.

The secretary of state added it would give more people the chance to live and work in a great city.

Ms Rayner said: “By unlocking one of the biggest regeneration projects in the country, we’re turning years of talk into action to drive growth in every region, raise living standards and build the homes communities desperately need.”

Labour mayor Mr Skaith said the project would ensure York remains a fantastic place to live, work and invest for decades to come.

The mayor said: “York Central is going to transform the city, that's why it's a big moment to see these plans move forward.

“Soon we'll see York Central take shape and transform how people see our county.”

Labour council leader Cllr Douglas said she looked forward to turning the project into reality.

The council leader said: “We look forward to working with our partners to ensure the project reflects the social and economic needs of people across all York communities.”

McLaren Property Group chief executive John Gatley said the real work could now begin on the project after many years of hard work.


Tom Bridges, Home England's North East lead, said Thursday's decision was a defining moment for a project decades in the making.

Network Rail's business development director Stephen Hind said the project showed what was possible when under-used railway land was repurposed.

Allan Cook, founder of Arlington Real Estate, said York Central was designed to breathe a new kind of life into an already vibrant city.

National Railway Museum director Craig Bentley said they were excited to welcome the next phase of the project as work continues on its Central Hall expansion.

Plans approved on Thursday form the final part of the first phase of the scheme, following infrastructure works and the approval of a Government Hub set for completion in 2028.

The latest application features a 213-bed three or four star hotel, a new entrance at the western end of York Station and a seven-storey Innovation Hub office block.

An operator for the hotel has yet to be named.

They would all face onto a new public space dubbed Coal Drops Square and the area would also feature shops and leisure spaces on the ground floor of the buildings.

A total of 1,014 homes would be built across three plots, along with a convenience store.

A new park is also planned.
