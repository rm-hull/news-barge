Three schools in East Riding are now harnessing the power of the sun.

Withernsea High School, Kingsway Primary in Goole, and Marshlands Primary in Goole have had solar panels installed through a Department for Education grant.

The panels are expected to save around £71,000 a year in energy costs and cut emissions by 75,000kg of CO2 annually.

Councillor Victoria Aitken, cabinet member for children, families and education, said: "I'm delighted that three of our schools now have the benefit of solar power – reducing costs for the schools and decreasing carbon emissions.

"The cost savings benefit every child, as each school can now reinvest these savings within the curriculum."

The panels also offer a real-time learning opportunity for pupils, who can track energy generation, carbon savings, and financial benefits.

Withernsea High School has also received electric vehicle chargers for four vehicles as part of the scheme.

Cllr Aitken said the installations build on existing solar projects across the East Riding.

She said: "The installations follow the completion of solar carports at our leisure sites, vehicle depots, Champney Treasure House roof and roofs of over 300 council-owned homes.

"Pupils can see how renewable energy helps tackle climate change and deliver a sustainable future.

"The project will also help inspire children and young people to become the innovators, engineers and environmental leaders of tomorrow."

East Riding Council measures carbon emissions from all schools and corporate buildings and is working toward a net zero target by 2050.
