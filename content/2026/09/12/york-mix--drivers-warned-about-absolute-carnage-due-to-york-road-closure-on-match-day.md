Drivers are being warned about ‘absolute carnage’ today (Saturday) due to a road closure on the day of a York City home game.

The northbound carriageway on Monks Cross Link, Huntington, is closed between the A1237 roundabout and Monks Cross Drive until Friday, 6 November.

It’s to allow for works connected with the building of hundreds of homes at the Monks Cross Village Gardens development.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/monks-cross-link-road-closure-york-google-map.webp)

Last weekend, residents took to social media to complain about “gridlock” in the Monks Cross area caused by the closure. One called it “absolute chaos”, with a ten minute journey taking over an hour.

This afternoon’s York City home game against Swindon Town will attract a near sell-out crowd.

One fan who got stuck before offered this advice on Facebook: “If going to the Swindon match, set off a bit earlier than you’re used to doing.

“The Monks Cross Link road works are causing absolute carnage. Last week backed up on to the A64.

“Getting out again will be interesting. Take a book.”

City of York Council is urging people to travel by bus. A spokesperson said: “Traffic at Monks Cross is likely to be heavy this weekend, especially with York City playing Swindon Town on Saturday.

“One of the best ways to get to Monks Cross, and the way that reduces congestion for everyone, is to catch the bus.”

It suggests:

- The **9** that goes from the centre of York all the way to Monks Cross.
- **12** links Woodthorpe and south west York with the city centre and then on to Monks Cross
- **81** will bring you from Malton through to Monks Cross and in to the city centre and back
- **20** links Rawcliffe and Osbaldwick via, the city centre and Monks Cross

The council adds: “There’s also a special match day shuttle run by Pullman.

“There’s one from the Stonebow for home fans and one from York Railway Station and Exhibition Square for away fans.

“Both services are £3 single and £4 return adults and £2 flat return fare for children.”
