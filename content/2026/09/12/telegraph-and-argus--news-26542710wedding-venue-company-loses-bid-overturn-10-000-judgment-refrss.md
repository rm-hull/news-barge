A wedding venue company, whose premises were forced to close, still owes £10,000 after a judge rejected its attempt to overturn a County Court judgment.

The Monal By Jalwa Ltd had applied to Leeds County Court to set aside a judgment entered against the company following a dispute over a cancelled wedding booking worth £11,500.

However, court documents seen by the Telegraph & Argus show a district judge dismissed the application and allowed enforcement action to continue.

## 
  **Wedding affected by venue collapse**

The dispute stems from the partial collapse of the former church building on Bradford Road, Cleckheaton, which was being operated as Monal By Jalwa.

The incident made headlines in September 2025 after masonry fell from the front of the building, narrowly missing pedestrians.

According to Fozia Aziz, her family had paid £11,500 for a wedding package and were due to hold a wedding reception at the venue on the day of the collapse.

The family say they only became aware of the incident through social media and later learned the venue would be unable to host the event.

Although alternative accommodation was offered, the family claim it was substantially inferior to what had originally been booked.

After attempts to reach a resolution failed, a claim was brought against The Monal By Jalwa Ltd by family member Nasir Aziz.

## 
  **Court judgment remains in force**

The family subsequently obtained a County Court judgment for £10,000.

The company later applied to have the judgment set aside, arguing it had not received the court papers relating to the claim.

However, court documents show the matter came before District Judge Bond at Leeds County Court in July this year.

In an order seen by the Telegraph & Argus, the court recorded that the defendant accepted the address used for service was the company's correct address and that the court was satisfied the judgment had been correctly entered.

The order states: "The application is dismissed" and "The stay on enforcement is lifted."

As a result, the £10,000 judgment remains in force.

## 
  **Bailiff action resumes**

Following the court's decision, the family have resumed enforcement proceedings in an attempt to recover the outstanding debt.

Documents supplied to the Telegraph & Argus show enforcement agents from DCBL had previously attended the venue in April after a Writ of Control was obtained.

According to an enforcement report, an agent attended the Bradford Road premises but was unable to gain access, finding the site secured and undergoing refurbishment work.

The report states that after the visit, a representative of the company contacted the enforcement agent and said it was the first time they had heard about the matter.

The court later rejected the company's application to set aside the judgment.

## 
  **'The next chapter' of the story**

Mrs Aziz described the legal action as the latest development in a story that began with the collapse of the venue building.

She said: "At the time, the collapse and the effect it had on the venue were reported. What wasn't known at that stage was that the closure and the resulting circumstances would ultimately lead to a substantial contractual dispute, a County Court judgment against the company, an application to set that judgment aside, and now the court's decision to dismiss that application and allow enforcement to continue."

The Telegraph & Argus has approached The Monal By Jalwa Ltd for comment.
