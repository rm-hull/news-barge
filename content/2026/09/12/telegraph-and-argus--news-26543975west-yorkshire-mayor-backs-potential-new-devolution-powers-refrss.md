West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin has backed plans to give regional leaders the power to introduce a levy on overnight visitors after discussing the proposal with fellow UK mayors at Number 10.

The meeting on September 10 focused on giving local leaders more control over decisions affecting their areas.

A visitor levy, dubbed 'tourist tax', would be a percentage-based charge added to accommodation stays. Government say money raised could be spent on high streets, public transport, tourism projects, cultural activities and local events.

The proposal is part of the government’s wider plan to move powers from Westminster to regional and local authorities. It follows the transfer of powers to local leaders to help drive investment in communities.

   

*(Image: Supplied)*

### Brabin backs visitor levy proposal

Mayor Brabin said: "Devolution is delivering and it is right that mayors get these new powers to introduce an overnight visitor levy."

She said the charge would ask visitors to make a small contribution towards the places they visit.

She said: "Our beautiful region has so much to offer, from Brontë Country to the Hepworth Gallery, the rolling moors to our bustling city nightlife, arts and theatre to beautiful architecture."

The mayor also said revenue could support the region’s economy and cultural offer.

She added "By asking people to make a small contribution, like in many other European countries, we can continue to invest in culture, grow our economy and ensure people want to visit for generations to come."

   

*(Image: Newsquest)*

### Local leaders could control how revenue is spent

Under the proposed arrangements, mayors and leaders of Foundation Strategic Authorities would decide whether to introduce a levy. They would also decide how income was spent, after consultation with residents and local businesses.

The government said authorities would be able to set arrangements to suit local needs. This could include exemptions for some types of accommodation.

Ministers said the levy would be based on a percentage of accommodation costs rather than a fixed fee. They said this would help protect people taking lower-cost holidays from paying a disproportionate amount.

   

*(Image: Bradford College)*

### Further powers for mayors under devolution plans

In a social media post on September 11, Mayor Brabin said the idea was among several issues discussed with mayors at Number 10. She also referred to proposals to give mayors more powers over education for people aged 16 to 19.

That proposal would aim to help young people gain skills needed by employers in their areas. She also listed plans for mayors to have the final say on major infrastructure projects, with the aim of allowing work to progress more quickly.

Her post finished with: "More local power. More local delivery."

Visitor levies already operate in countries including Germany, Italy, the Netherlands and Canada, as well as parts of the United States.
