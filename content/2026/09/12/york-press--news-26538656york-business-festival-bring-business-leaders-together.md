## Major York business event to tackle range of issues facing employers today

Professor Brendan Paddison, dean of York Business School at York St John University, at the opening of last year's business festival. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

York is set to host a major gathering of business leaders, entrepreneurs and industry experts.

The York Business Festival, organised by York Business School at York St John University, will take place on October 14 and 15.

It promises two days of practical workshops, keynote speeches, panel discussions and networking opportunities.

Professor Brendan Paddison, dean of York Business School, said: "York Business School at York St John University is delighted to be coordinating this year’s York Business Festival.

"The Festival a celebration of the businesses, entrepreneurs and people who make York such a vibrant and ambitious place to live, work, invest and study.

"We hope you enjoy this year’s York Business Festival, make new connections, discover new ideas and, perhaps, find the beginnings of your next collaboration or opportunity."

The festival is free to attend and is designed to support organisations across York and North Yorkshire with innovation, growth and collaboration.

It is part of York St John University’s civic mission to remove barriers to participation and ensure businesses of all sizes can benefit from the expertise on offer.

Day one will begin with a keynote from Dave Callan, founder of ONTO Coaching, followed by two parallel conference tracks.

Events include a public sector procurement panel featuring Chloe Wilcox, head of procurement at City of York Council, and Paul Revell, head of commercial strategy at York St John University.

Other sessions will cover financial planning, marketing, the creative economy, workforce development and AI in business.

The day will end with networking drinks from 7pm to 10pm.

Day two will open with a keynote from Sir Richard Harpin, founder of Homeserve and Growth Partner.

Workshops and masterclasses will then cover leadership, risk management, AI, employment law and digital marketing.

The festival concludes with an "innovation supper" from 6pm to 8pm.

The event is built around collaboration rather than commercial sponsorship, giving attendees direct access to the expertise of academics and researchers from York Business School and the wider university.

York Business School’s Business Clinic and Law Clinic will also be highlighted during the festival.

The Business Clinic offers consultancy services to organisations throughout the academic year, covering areas such as marketing, business planning, digital strategy and HR.

From September 2026, the clinic will be based at the university’s new Kings Manor site and will be led by Dr Rebecca Biggins.

The Law Clinic provides free legal advice to businesses and the wider community, while also offering practical learning experiences for students.

Professor Paddison said: "York Business Festival embodies that spirit of collaboration.

"It brings together entrepreneurs, business leaders, educators, students and community partners to exchange ideas, learn from one another and explore how we can collectively shape the future of business in York.

"This year’s Festival showcases the ambition, resilience and diversity of York’s business community.

"From established organisations to start-ups and entrepreneurs, our city is home to businesses that are innovating, creating jobs, investing in people and contributing to the prosperity and character of York and the wider region."
