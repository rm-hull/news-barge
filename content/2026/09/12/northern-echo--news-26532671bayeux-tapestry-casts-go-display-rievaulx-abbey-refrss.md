TICKETS to see the Bayeux Tapestry in London are sold out until December and such has been the buzz of excitement about the opening of the exhibition this week, the next batch are expected to sell out very quickly when they are released on October 21.

However, a special out-reach exhibition is coming to a North Yorkshire abbey next year to bring the tapestry closer to people unable to see it, although Memories has a theory the Middlesbrough fans may find it a little too close to home for comfort.

In 1816, artist Charles Alfred Stothard was commissioned by the Society of Antiquaries to produce the first complete colour drawings of the tapestry. He also created plaster casts by applying wax directly to the embroidery – a technique that would not be considered today due to conservation concerns.

  Rievaulx Abbey 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Three of his rarely seen casts of key scenes are to be displayed at Rievaulx Abbey, on the North York Moors, from March to May next year as part of the British Museum’s Bayeux Around Britain initiative.

The museum describes the casts as "almost as exceptional as the Bayeux Tapestry itself", and Rievaulx, near Helmsley, is the only northern venue included in the tour.

Just as the tapestry depicts the Norman conquest of Britain in 1066, so the abbey at Rievaulx was founded in 1132 by the French Cistercian order of monks to spearhead the monastic colonisation of northern Britain.

  Aerial view of Ionic Temple at Rievaulx Terrace, Helmsley, North Yorkshire 

*(Image: Dominic Lipinski/English Heritage)*

The three casts coming to Rievaulx show scene 30 which depicts Harold being crowned king of England, scene 46 which shows William the Conqueror receiving news of Harold’s whereabouts, and scene 48 which shows William in full armour with a spear on the attack.

But let’s go back earlier in the story to scene nine, before Harold has been crowned.

In the tapestry, he is seen sailing to Normandy in the hope of persuading William, whom he then considered to be a friend, to back his claims to the English throne when Edward the Confessor dies.

Harold, though, is blown off course and lands in the neighbouring territory of Guy, Count of Ponthieu, whose men seize Harold and take him to the count.

  Scene nine, with Harold on the left, approaching Guy on the throne, with the spy leaning on a pillar 

*(Image: Unknown)*

In scene nine, Harold is shown wearing English leggings (the French, by contrast, wear culottes which are cut off at the knee) and holding his sword peacefully down as he approaches Guy.

Guy sits on a throne with his sword powerfully pointing up as he demands Harold pays a ransom to secure his release.

But, in a scene which may be familiar to supporters of Middlesbrough FC, peering out from behind a pillar is a spy, recording what he hears and sees so he can relay any crucial information back to his boss.

His boss is William. Because he is Harold’s friend at this point, he orders Guy to release Harold without any ransom changing hands.

  The 'spy', leaning on a tree, captured at Middlesbrough FC's training ground at Rockliffe, Hurworth 

*(Image: Unknown)*

Indeed, this “spygate” scene is believed to have been re-enacted by Southampton earlier this year when they had a spy hiding in plain sight next to a tree at Middlesbrough’s training ground in Hurworth, recording what he saw of the Boro’s tactics and relaying the crucial information back to his team manager.

It’s amazing how history repeats itself, even after 950 years.
