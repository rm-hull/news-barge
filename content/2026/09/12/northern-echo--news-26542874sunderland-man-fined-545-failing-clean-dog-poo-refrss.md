A man has been fined more than £500 for failing to clean up after his dog.

Jordan Lee Heron, of Hyde Street in Hendon, was prosecuted by Sunderland City Council after community safety officers witnessed him leaving dog mess near allotments off Corporation Road on February 9.

When officers challenged Heron, he admitted he had nothing with him to clean up after the dog pooed and refused to give his details.

Further enquiries by the council’s community safety team, working with Northumbria Police, led to his identification.

He was issued a £100 fixed penalty notice, which went unpaid.

Councillor Bill Blackett, cabinet member for environment, transport and enforcement at Sunderland City Council, said: "The vast majority of dog owners across Sunderland act responsibly and clean up after their pets.

"Unfortunately, it only takes a small minority who don't clean up after their pets to have a real impact on the cleanliness and enjoyment of our neighbourhoods and public spaces."

The case was heard at South Tyneside Magistrates’ Court on Tuesday, September 8, where Heron failed to attend and the case was proved in his absence.

He was fined £222 and ordered to pay £150 in legal costs, £85 in council costs, and an £88 victim surcharge - totalling £545, payable within 28 days.

The offence was a breach of Sunderland City Council’s Public Spaces Protection Order, which requires people in charge of a dog to remove its faeces from land covered by the order.

Cllr Blackett warned that ignoring a fixed penalty notice would not make the issue disappear.

He said: "This case also demonstrates that refusing to provide details or ignoring a Fixed Penalty Notice does not mean the matter will simply go away.

"We will make further enquiries where necessary and, where there is sufficient evidence, cases can be taken before the courts.

"The message is straightforward - be a responsible dog owner, make sure you have something with you to clean up after your dog and always pick up after it."
