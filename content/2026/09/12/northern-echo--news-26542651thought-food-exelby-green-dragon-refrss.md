What’s not to like about community pubs?

That’s a community pub in the sense of one that has been purchased by members of a community, usually through a share offer, often to save the pub from permanent closure.

We have got a good number in our part of the world. The George and Dragon at Hudswell, near Richmond, was something of a pioneer and it was followed by the Countryman’s Inn at Hunton, near Bedale, the King’s Arms at Gunnerside, in Swaledale, the Farmers’ Arms at Muker, also Swaledale, the Old Horn, Spennithorne, in Wensleydale and The Travellers Rest, at Skeeby, near Richmond.

We should not forget The Foresters Arms at Carlton in Coverdale which predates them all but was set up slightly differently from the others as a community co-operative in 2011.

While the precise ownership model may vary, what they all share is the commitment of the locals to make them work. When you have ‘skin in the game’, so to speak, you are more likely to want to patronise your local because it really is your local.

Before anyone shouts that I’ve missed one out, there is also of course the Exelby Green Dragon, south of Bedale, and not to be confused with The Green Dragon in Bedale.

  The Exelby Green Dragon 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

It became a community pub in 2018 after a period of closure. The local community raised £345k to buy and refurbish the building – that’s some achievement for a village of only 200 souls.

Since then it has become an exemplar for this sort of thing. The Plunkett Foundation, a national charity which supports rural community businesses, features it on its website as example of how to go about doing a community pub.

Not only did they save the pub, the group went on to create some affordable housing in an adjoining cottage, and set up a café and deli.

While we had called in on a number of occasions, we had never eaten there and we put that right with friends Carl and Suzanne.

The refurbishment carried out eight years ago is wearing well. The traditional feel of a cosy country inn is belied by the fact that it is actually a very big space, carved up into different areas with varying degrees of intimacy. The formal dining space at the back feels a bit cavernous.

Which is why when locals began gathering for the regular quiz night (it was a Wednesday) we were offered the opportunity to move to the dining room. We declined, preferring the hubbub of the bar.

We were looked after by Becky Westgarth, who is tenant with partner Sam Harrison. Becky looked familiar and eventually the penny dropped: we knew her from the George and Dragon at Hudswell where she and Sam are also the tenants. They obviously like running community pubs.

She was a marvel. When we arrived, she appeared to be running the place on her own and although help behind the bar arrived with the pub quiz regulars, she took our drinks orders, our food orders, served our meal and cleared afterwards all the while looking after a growing number of customers. And it all looked effortless.

  The Exelby Green Dragon 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

Somebody was equally busy in the kitchen and doing a good job.

Carl and Suzanne were impressed by the pie of the day (£19.95) – individual steak with excellent shortcrust pastry and plenty of tender beef in a glistening gravy. They came with buttery mash, kale and extra gravy on the side. Carl opted for some French fries rather than the mash.

Sylvia’s wild garlic chicken Kiev (£21.95) passed what she says is the key to the success or otherwise of this classic: have the garlicky buttery juices been retained within the breast meat? Leakage during the cooking almost always results in dry chicken.

A tentative prodding of the breadcrumbed mound proved it passed with flying colours. A stream of golden juice spurted across the plate.

And just in case there wasn’t enough garlic, a pat of deep green garlic butter came with it, along with a dressed salad leaves and French fries.

  I had one of the night’s specials – tagliatelle with a rich beef ragu (£19) which was very nearly brilliant. The pasta was fresh, not dried, and cooked bang-on *al dente*. The ragu had been cooked long and slow and was deeply flavourful.

  The Exelby Green Dragon 

*(Image: Malcolm Warne)*

The only problem was the ragu’s consistency which was too thick to coat the pasta so what was great pasta and a great sauce didn’t come together. The result was that the pasta was too dry and had begun to stick. A bit of a shame.

We shared a couple of really very good burnt Basque cheesecakes served with a gooseberry compote, Greek yoghurt and honey ice cream (worth every penny of £10) and did our best to answer some of the questions in what must be one of the toughest pub quizzes around. Sample question: did Jack Johnson win the world heavyweight boxing championship on New Year’s Day in 1908 or Boxing Day?

With a couple of coffees, a couple of halves of Rudgate bitter, a bottle of Theakston’s excellent Nowt Peculier and pink gin, the bill was a very reasonable £126.30.
