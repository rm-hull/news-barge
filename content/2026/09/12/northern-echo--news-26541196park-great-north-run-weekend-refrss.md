Drivers heading to this year’s Great North Run are being warned that extensive closures will affect Newcastle, Gateshead and South Tyneside on race day.

According to organisers, public transport remains the recommended option due to limited parking and the scale of traffic disruption expected along the 13.1-mile route.

Tens of thousands of runners and spectators will pack Tyneside for the event, which starts on Newcastle’s Central Motorway and finishes at The Leas in South Shields.

The Red Arrows are due to carry out their traditional flypast at 10.52am, shortly after the first runners set off.

For those travelling by car, choosing the right parking spot could save hours of frustration.

### 
  **Avoid parking near the route**

The key advice for drivers is simple: do not park near the start line.

Large sections of Newcastle city centre and the race route will be closed throughout the day.

Closures around the start begin on Saturday evening and continue until Sunday evening, affecting the A167, Central Motorway and Tyne Bridge.

The Central Motorway and Tyne Bridge will face restrictions from morning until mid-afternoon, while sections of the A194 towards South Shields will also close.

Because of this, motorists attempting to drive directly to the start or finish are likely to face delays.

   

*(Image: NQ)*

### 
  **Best option**

For many spectators, the easiest option is to park outside the city and take the Metro.

Park-and-ride facilities are available at several Metro stations, and transport officials urge visitors to use them instead of driving into Newcastle.

MetroCentre is also highlighted as a useful alternative for those approaching from the A1.

  *Options include:*

- 
    *Regent Centre*
- 
    *Four Lane Ends*
- 
    *Northumberland Park*
- 
    *MetroCentre*

From these locations, visitors can take the Metro to Newcastle for the start or continue to South Shields for the finish.

### 
  **Newcastle city-centre parking**

If you do need to park in Newcastle city centre, car parks away from the immediate race route are likely to be the safest bet.

  *Options include:*

- 
    *Eldon Garden*
- 
    *Grainger Town car parks*
- 
    *Quayside multi-storey*
- 
    *Dean Street car park*

Another option option is to pre-book a private driveway or off-street space through JustPark.

Drivers should arrive early and expect diversions around Haymarket, Claremont Road, the Town Moor and the Central Motorway corridor.

### 
  **Finish-line parking**

Spectators heading for the South Shields celebrations may find parking near the finish easier than parking near the start.

Car parks around South Shields Interchange offer straightforward access to the finish, while Pier Head and Harbour Drive are also popular on race day.

Drivers should expect closures and restrictions throughout the day on roads approaching the finish, including sections of the A104, Newcastle Road, Prince Edward Road and the Coast Road.

### 
  **Key traffic disruption**

The biggest expected closures include:

- 
    *The A167 and associated roads near the start area from Saturday evening.*
- 
    *Newcastle Central Motorway (A167M) between Great North Road and the Tyne Bridge.*
- 
    *The Tyne Bridge itself during the race period.*
- 
    *The A194 eastbound towards South Tyneside.*
- 
    *Prince Edward Road and sections of the approach to South Shields seafront.*
- 
    *The A183 Coast Road near the finish area.*

Officials say roads will reopen according to local conditions and how quickly runners clear each section of the course.
