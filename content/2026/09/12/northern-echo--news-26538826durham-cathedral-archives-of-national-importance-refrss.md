A collection containing three copies of the Magna Carta and centuries of records charting life in the North East has been recognised as being of national importance.

Durham Cathedral's Priory Archives and Library has been awarded Designated status by Arts Council England, placing it among just nine collections across the country to receive the recognition in the latest round of awards.

The Designation Scheme highlights museum, library and archive collections considered to be of national or international significance because of their quality, importance and research value.

A spokesperson for Durham Cathedral said: "We're delighted that Durham Cathedral's Library and Archive Collections have been recognised as one of nine new Designated Collections of national significance through Arts Council England's Designation Scheme.

   

*(Image: Durham Cathedral)*

"The Designation Scheme shines a spotlight on the excellence, diversity and creativity to be found in museums, galleries and archives across England.

"We're proud to see the significance of these remarkable collections recognised, helping to ensure they can be cared for and shared with future generations."

The Durham collection was recognised as the largest and most complete archive of a medieval monastery still housed in its original location.

   

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Among its treasures are three issues of the Magna Carta, alongside extensive records documenting society, culture, religion and the economy of medieval North East England.

The award is expected to strengthen the collection's profile with researchers, funders and visitors, while helping to underline its importance as a resource for understanding the region's history.

Arts Council England's Designation Scheme was established in 1997 and is designed to showcase some of the country's most significant cultural collections outside the national institutions.

   

*(Image: Supplied)*

The scheme assesses collections on a range of criteria, including their national significance, importance to local communities, quality, and value for academic research.

Durham Cathedral's archives were recognised for the way they illuminate major historical developments while also providing a detailed picture of everyday life in the North East over hundreds of years.

The cathedral was joined by eight other institutions receiving Designated status, including Dorset Museum's Thomas Hardy Memorial Collection, the University of Liverpool's science fiction collection and the World Rugby Museum's rugby union archive.

The recognition means the cathedral's collections will now be formally acknowledged as being among England's most significant cultural and historical resources, securing another prestigious accolade for one of the region's best-known heritage landmarks.
