A Darlington couple say their world was "turned upside down" after a horror crash forced them to postpone their wedding and left the bride-to-be unable to walk.

Alixandra Cadden-Young, 47, and her fiancé Andrew Calder, 48, were travelling home from the Filey Food Festival in North Yorkshire on August 9 when they were in a head-on smash on the A171 at Moorsholm, at about 6pm.

The pair had been trading with Andrew's piccalilli and chutney business Calder's Kitchen before the crash.

Emergency services, including the air ambulance, were called and four people were taken to hospital.

  The scene of the crash 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Alix, a solicitor and co-owner of Sunderland law firm Longden Walker & Renney, said the crash marked the beginning of a life-changing ordeal that has left her bed-bound and reliant on Andrew for even the simplest daily tasks.

The 47-year-old said: "Apparently I shouldn't have survived, which is a terrifying thought.

"We've seen pictures of our van and it's completely crushed."

Alix said the last thing she remembered seeing was a car directly in front of them before impact.

"We were on our way back from the Filey Food Festival where Andrew had a stall with his business.

"We were looking forward to getting home for a glass of wine and chatting about the wedding as we drove across the Yorkshire Moors towards Darlington."

  Alixandra Cadden-Young 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

"That's it for me. I remember nothing until I was lying at the roadside. I was pulled out through the passenger window.

"The air ambulance and several ambulances arrived, and I was taken to hospital.

Cleveland Police confirmed an investigation into the crash remains ongoing.

  Alixandra Cadden-Young after the crash 

*(Image: SUPPLIED)*

What should have been one of the happiest periods of the couple's lives has instead become a daily struggle.

She said: "We both have really busy lives, we're both very into fitness, we've got the children and our businesses.

"We were on our way home thinking about the wedding, thinking about having a glass of wine, and then suddenly I'm involved in this, and our lives have completely changed.

"Andrew is now my full-time carer.

"I can't go to work, I can't get out of bed, I can't do anything. My entire life is on hold."

The couple were due to marry on September 5, however they spent the day instead trying to come to terms with everything that had happened.

  Alixandra Cadden-Young, 47, and her fiancé Andrew Calder, 48 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Alix said: "We've had to rearrange the wedding for January and I'm hoping I'll be able to walk down the aisle by then.

The crash caused Alix extensive injuries, including fractures on both of her legs and her pelvis.

The 47-year-old said: "There were nine fractures altogether, or 11 depending on how you count them.

"I'm not allowed to weight-bear for 12 weeks after surgery on my hip and because I've also got fractures in my hands, I can't even use crutches properly.

"I can manage a few steps with a special frame but really I can't do anything. I can't be left alone.

"It's just an absolute nightmare."

  Alixandra Cadden-Young, 47, and her fiancé Andrew Calder, 48 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

A keen runner, Alix completed the Darlington 10K just a week before the crash and had been planning to run a marathon with Andrew.

She said: "Andrew and I had decided we were going to run a marathon together in the next couple of years.

"To go from that to where I am now, it's just hard to get my head around.

"We don't know what the long-term issues are going to be until everything heals."

Alongside the personal impact, the crash has also caused uncertainty over Calder's Kitchen, Andrew's food business, which produces piccalilli, chutneys and other condiments sold at food festivals and through independent retailers across the North East.

After the crash, he was left with a broken toe and a broken finger.

Andrew said: "As a small business it's extremely worrying.

"It's a very challenging time in the current economic climate anyway.

"You get by month to month, paying the bills, and then suddenly half to two-thirds of your income is wiped out. It's impossible and, to be honest, it keeps me awake at night.

"Everything we would normally do to generate income, whether that's food festivals, markets or events, we're not able to do at the moment.

"Our biggest concern now is whether we can recover sufficiently to maximise the Christmas trading period.

"Christmas accounts for around 40 per cent of Calder's Kitchen's annual income.

"Losing a significant part of that period could have consequences that extend well beyond the weeks immediately after the crash."

  Alixandra Cadden-Young, 47, and her fiancé Andrew Calder, 48 

*(Image: STUART BOULTON)*

Amid the uncertainty, the couple say they have been overwhelmed by the support they have received.

Alix said: "People we met at the crash site have kept in touch and friends from the food community have rallied around us.
