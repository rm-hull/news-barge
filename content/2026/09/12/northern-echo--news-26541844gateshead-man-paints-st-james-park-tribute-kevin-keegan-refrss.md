A North East artist is painting a tribute to the late legend Kevin Keegan after he was overwhelmed by the scenes outside St James' Park in the wake of the former Newcastle manager's death.

Ronnie Pattison, an artist from Gateshead, was deeply moved by the outpouring of emotion and tributes left by fans and has begun work on a painting capturing the sea of flowers, scarves, and messages outside the Gallowgate End of St James' Park.

Mr Pattison said: "I was devastated when I heard the news of Keegan’s passing.

"He was my boyhood dream when it came to football.

"I remember his testimonial and him being lifted out in the helicopter, which had never been done before.

"And then there was the way he managed the club and the entertainers team. It was fantastic.

"It really hit us hard."

  He is raising money for the Sir Bobby Robson Foundation. 

*(Image: Steve Brock)*

Rather than a portrait of Keegan, the painting focuses on the collective tribute from the community, showing the deep impact he had on the North East.

Mr Pattison said: "I’m not actually painting Kevin Keegan.

"There are one or two tributes that show you a little bit of him.

"For me, the biggest tribute I can do is a painting of everyone else’s tributes – the shirts, photographs, scarves, hats etc.

"If it’s on somebody’s wall, they’re going to see it every day of their life."

The project is also supporting a cause close to his heart.

   

*(Image: NORTH NEWS AND PICTURES)*

Half of the proceeds from mounted prints will be donated to The Sir Bobby Robson Foundation, part of Newcastle Hospitals Charity.

Mr Pattison said: "My mother battled cancer, and if the Foundation was around when she was fighting it may have helped her and others like her.

"Since her death 34 years ago the Foundation’s work has really touched our lives."

So far, he has already raised £700 from supporters before the painting was even started.

He said: "It’s not something I like to cash in on.

"But customers were saying, ‘Ronnie, you need to paint something.’

"And it just felt right."

Mr Pattison has set up a temporary studio at One Strawberry Place, directly opposite St James’ Park, where the public and nearby workers are welcome to watch the artwork progress.

He expects the project to take four to six weeks to complete, though he admits it could take longer as the detail develops.

   

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

Fans will be able to pre-order prints by leaving a deposit, with the deposit going directly to The Sir Bobby Robson Foundation.

The funds raised will support the completion of the Sir Bobby Robson Institute, a new £30 million cancer drug trials centre currently under construction at the Freeman Hospital in Newcastle.

Anisa Rahim, campaign officer at The Sir Bobby Robson Foundation, said: "We’re grateful to Ronnie for his heartfelt support and we’re sure his painting will prove popular.

"The money raised will help us complete the Sir Bobby Robson Institute and allow us to expand the number of clinical trials of new cancer drugs within the North East, and further international research into cancer."

The Institute aims to be fully operational by 2027, serving millions across the region and contributing to cancer research worldwide.
