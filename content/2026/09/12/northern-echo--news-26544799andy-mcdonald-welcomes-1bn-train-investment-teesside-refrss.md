North East rail passengers have been promised faster, more reliable journeys under major Government plans hailed by an MP as a boost for the region.

Middlesbrough and Thornaby East MP Andy McDonald welcomed the £1 billion investment in 29 new trains, which ministers say will improve services across the North and strengthen Teesside connections.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the investment will provide “faster, more reliable journeys” across the North.

The British-built Adessia Stream fleet will create 350 jobs and help the TransPennine Route Upgrade increase capacity by 30 per cent, improve accessibility and add luggage space.

Mr McDonald called the scheme “a huge step forward” for Teesside’s links with the rest of the North.

The Manchester Airport-to-Saltburn route is set to get “thousands of additional seats a day”, with stops at Yarm, Thornaby, Middlesbrough and Redcar before Saltburn.

The direct service was extended to Saltburn in May 2022, with selected weekday trains serving communities across the North.

The Department for Transport says production is due to begin in 2028, with the trains entering service in late 2034 under Great British Railways.

  Middlesbrough and Thornaby East MP Andy McDonald has welcomed plans for a new train fleet serving routes across the North 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Mr McDonald linked the plans to Middlesbrough station’s upgrades, calling the improved Teesside connections “a particularly exciting time” and saying they were “exactly what I had in mind” as shadow transport secretary.

“I always envisioned a public national rail body being the guiding mind for our railway network and this is what this Labour Government and Great British Railways will deliver,” he said.

The new Adessia Stream trains are the first of their kind to be used on a UK mainline route, and will run free of emissions when on non-electrified sections in another step towards sustainable rail travel, which Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander celebrated as “fit for future”.

Accessibility upgrades include step-free boarding and eliminating gaps between platforms and carriages, making it easier for wheelchair users and people with luggage or pushchairs to board.

  Concept image showing proposed step-free, gap-free boarding arrangements on the new train fleet 

*(Image: DEPARTMENT FOR TRANSPORT)*

Each train will have three wheelchair spaces, two accessible toilets, family-friendly areas and storage for bikes and pushchairs.

Chris Jackson, managing director of TransPennine Express, said: "This is a transformational £1 billion investment in the future of rail travel across the North, delivering new trains for our customers alongside new infrastructure, new jobs and long-term economic benefits. Our customers will benefit from more seats and better accessibility, including level boarding.

"Britain has a proud railway heritage, and this project puts the UK at the forefront of the next generation of cleaner rail technology."
