A North East teen is calling for tougher rules on alcohol delivery apps.

Anissa Khalfa, 18, from Jarrow, is campaigning for urgent action to restrict rapid online alcohol deliveries after the death of her cousin Tom in March 2025 at the age of 33.

Speaking openly for the first time about Tom, Ms Khalfa says his access to alcohol through delivery apps intensified his addiction and directly contributed to his death.

She said: "Tom suffered from alcohol addiction for a couple of years before his death, worsened by his own mental health problems.

"He was like an older brother to me and seeing him struggle was extremely difficult, especially as the alcohol made everything much worse for him."

Ms Khalfa says she began campaigning around four years ago when she realised how online deliveries were contributing to Tom's drinking and intensifying his health problems.

"He was obtaining large amounts of alcohol at different times of day and night - often two or three times a day", she said.

Tom's family repeatedly contacted delivery companies to block orders, but found there were no effective safeguards to prevent him from accessing alcohol.

Ms Khalfa, who is campaigning alongside Balance, Alcohol Change UK, and the Alcohol Health Alliance, believes app companies must introduce better protections.

She said: "Despite trying to find ways to block the deliveries, we could never access his account.

"When family, friends and doctors know that somebody is seriously ill from alcohol, there should be a right on these apps to be able to impose a block."

Tom died from ketoacidosis and acute pancreatitis, both linked to prolonged alcohol use.

She said: "I still feel bitterly angry though and wonder what would have happened had he not fallen into the online delivery trap which ended his life far too young.

"We don’t want others to go through this, but at the moment there will be thousands of others struggling with alcohol where online deliveries are putting their lives at risk."

Alcohol Change UK says more than one in five UK adults use rapid delivery services to order alcohol at least weekly and that people drinking above the recommended 14 units per week are nearly twice as likely to use these services regularly.

The charity is calling for better age and intoxication checks, restrictions on delivery times, and exclusion schemes to block access for vulnerable individuals.

Susan Taylor, head of alcohol policy for Fresh and Balance, said: "Anissa’s family’s shocking story is one of many coming out across the country on how rapid deliveries have destroyed people’s health, fuelled alcohol addiction and added to pressures faced by the NHS."

Dr Richard Piper, CEO of Alcohol Change UK, said: "Fast alcohol, straight to our doors and available round-the-clock, combined with weak and inconsistent safeguards, create a situation in which consumption can spiral out of control."

Professor Sir Ian Gilmore, chair of the Alcohol Health Alliance, said: "Alcohol is not an ordinary consumer product, yet regulation has failed to keep pace with how it is now largely sold.

"We urgently need stronger safeguards for online and rapid delivery services to ensure greater convenience does not come at the cost of greater alcohol-related harm."
