A County Durham parkrun community has raised thousands of pounds for lifesaving equipment and local charities after a medical emergency at an event last month.

The Jubilee Parkrun team in Spennymoor said it had been “overwhelmed” by support from the parkrun community and the wider public.

Regular participant Simon Pickering launched a fundraiser to buy an automated external defibrillator (AED) for the event.

Organisers said Turkington Engineering Services (TES) met the original target with a donation, but the active GoFundMe campaign continued to attract more money.

The campaign raised a further £1,555, including £240 from The Brewers Arms and £200 from Teesside Industrial Services (TIS).

Organisers donated the surplus to local causes and charities, including the Great North Air Ambulance Service.

The parkrun team also arranged CPR and defibrillator training for volunteers.

Organisers thanked everyone who donated, volunteered or offered support.

The Jubilee parkrun team said the response showed the strength of the local community, raising enough to buy potentially lifesaving equipment and support charities and community groups across County Durham.
