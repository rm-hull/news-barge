A new Channel 4 show following police officers, street rangers and council staff in the North East hopes to challenge perceptions of policing by showing "the people behind the uniform", its executive producer has said.

Geordie Coppers, which premieres on Monday, September 14, follows Northumbria Police's neighbourhood teams alongside NE1 Street Rangers and Newcastle City Council workers as they tackle crime, anti-social behaviour and welfare issues in the city centre.

The series focuses on everyday policing rather than major crime investigations.

  Why Newcastle was chosen for a new Channel 4 documentary on policing. 

*(Image: CANDOUR)*

Speaking ahead of the launch, executive producer Gilly Greenslade said the idea came after Channel 4 expressed an interest in a police series that looked beyond murders and major investigations, with Candour Productions pitching the show.

She said: "We started chatting to Northumbria Police and it turned out that within their neighbourhood policing team they had this quite unique way of working that is very joined up with the council and street rangers.

"We thought that sounded interesting.

"Obviously Geordies are known for their pride in their city and a certain charm.

"Someone said it's a small city with a big heart."

Greenslade said one of the show's main aims was to highlight the work carried out every day by neighbourhood officers and revealed the production was based in the Bigg Market in the city centre.

And the show even highlights the affection officers have for North East favourites Greggs - including a scene of a street ranger offering advice to someone who might have stolen a sausage roll.


"I think people want to know that there are bobbies out on the beat," she said.

"The thing that was surprising for me going into the neighbourhood policing team is how much more it is than just bobbies on the beat.

"They're running undercover operations to make sure the night-time economy is safe.

"They're taking e-bikes off the road that are going too fast.

"They've got this reactive approach when something is kicking off, but they've also got a very proactive approach to keeping the city safe."

The announcement trailer offers a glimpse of what's to come, featuring officers and city centre teams dealing with nightlife-related incidents, stop and searches, police chases and making arrests.

  Behind the scenes of filming for Geordie Coppers. 

*(Image: CANDOUR)*

In one lighter moment, the trailer even shows the discovery of an abandoned adult nappy and viewers will see everything from frontline policing to welfare support for vulnerable people.

Among those featured is breakout star Street Ranger Callum, 19, who hopes to join the police.

Gilly said: "There was an outbreak of a particularly dangerous spice on the streets that was mixed with fentanyl and they were having to go round doing CPR on people..

"You see him talking to a woman who's just come out of rehab and needs a hostel for the night, and he's there helping her.

"I certainly wasn't doing that when I was 19."


The series is due to air just weeks after communities across the North East rallied in support of police following the fatal A66 crash near Bowes which claimed the lives of seven people, including two Northumbria Police officers.

Gilly said she hoped the programme would help the public gain a better understanding of the people doing the job.

She added: "We all want the environments that we live in to be nice, safe places to go.

"It's not about us versus them. It's about creating nice, safe environments for people to live in and positive environments for people to live in.

"The police do come under a lot of scrutiny and we need that."

She continued: "But I think people forget that behind the uniform these are people and they're people who are generally really well-intentioned and want to go out and want to serve the public and want to make their environments and their cities safe places for everybody to enjoy.

"I hope that it will humanise the police a bit and maybe go some way to changing people's opinions about the kind of people who take up that career path."

The series will provide an inside look at Newcastle's City Safe partnership, which brings together Northumbria Police, NE1 Street Rangers and Newcastle City Council to help keep the city centre safe.

The programme promises "warmth, wit and plenty of Geordie spirit" alongside police chases, stop-and-search operations and nightlife incidents.

Geordie Coppers begins on Channel 4 on Monday, September 14, at 10pm.
