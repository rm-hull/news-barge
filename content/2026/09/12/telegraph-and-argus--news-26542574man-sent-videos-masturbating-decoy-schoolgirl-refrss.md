A MAN who sent videos of himself masturbating to a 14-year-old girl was actually messaging a decoy profile operated by an adult from a paedophile hunter group.

Mark Davis, 56, of Pool Court, Barkerend, Bradford, will now have to register as a sex offender for life and will be subject to a Sexual Harm Prevention Order for the same period.

He avoided immediate jail time.

Instead, a judge at Bradford Crown Court handed him 30 months’ imprisonment suspended for three years.

Prosecutor David Povall told the court how the decoy profile was set up to indicate the person involved was 18.

When Davis made contact and said “Hey delicious”, the decoy pretending to be the girl revealed she was in fact just 14.

Davis began to introduce sexual elements into his conversations.

He told her she was “gorgeous”, asked for photographs, told her he had “naughty” pictures and videos, and sent her a picture of himself.

He then sent videos of himself masturbating and performing oral sex on sex toys, as well as instructions on how to masturbate.

The messaging began on September 12, 2024, and carried on for a few more days.

Davis was arrested on September 18 and immediately admitted that he had been speaking to a 14-year-old.

He described some of the conversations as fantasy but that he had said “graphic things”.

The court heard that there had been no previous offending of a similar nature, but he did have a conviction for drink-driving.

Defence barrister Rebecca Young described Davis as “socially isolated” and that he had experienced trauma throughout his life and had recently been diagnosed with cancer.

She said he had used alcohol and drugs “as a coping mechanism” and that his addiction was “an explanation as to why a man of his years would suddenly commit an offence of this nature.”

She said he had expressed genuine regret and remorse, and that realistic rehabilitation was possible.

His Honour Judge Stephen Earl took attempting to incite a child to engage in sexual activity as the main count and handed Davis a total of 30 months’ imprisonment, which he suspended for three years.

He said there were “significant mitigating factors” in relation to his difficult childhood, his medical conditions, and lack of previous convictions.

He told Davis: “Since March of this year it is possible to sentence someone who receives three years or less now to a suspended sentence, and I will do so in your case.”

He added: “I do believe there is work that can be done with you which will increase the realistic prospect of you not committing further offences.”

He ordered Davis to undertake 55 rehabilitation activity requirement days and a nine-month alcohol treatment plan.

He ordered the forfeiture and destruction of the devices he had used and said he must pay a £228 victim surcharge.

Registration as a sex offender, and a Sexual Harm Prevention Order, will be indefinite.
