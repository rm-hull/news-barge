The 45th edition of the Great North Run will take place on September 13.

It will take runners on the iconic 13-mile journey from the heart of Newcastle to the coast in South Shields.

This year is projected to be the biggest run ever, with over 63,000 people participating.

With a record number of runners, it is to be expected that the spectator count will also dwarf previous years.

Those spectators may be in need of a coffee, snack, or pint after hours of waiting.

In preparation for the needs of those watching from the sidelines, we have compiled a guide to the best-rated and most popular spots along all 13 miles of the route, from NE1 to the North Sea.

## Food near start:

North Terrace, which lies on Claremont Road just off the A167 where the race begins, is a popular pub among locals.

  North Terrace


It offers top quality pizza and beer, along with outdoor seating if the weather allows.

Open Sunday 12pm-11pm, North Terrace is a good choice for anyone hungry, or thirsty, after seeing the runners off.

## Spots near the river with a view of the Tyne Bridge:

To the north of the river on the Quayside, The Hooch will be open from 10am on Sunday to receive those wishing to watch the runners cross the Tyne Bridge.

The cafe and bar boasts the “best views in town," and is praised in reviews as a “first-class" venue despite its less than extensive selection.

The Hooch offers a cafe-style food menu of cakes, pastries, small plates and sharing dishes, perfect for brunch after the run begins.

Cocktails, mocktails, gin and whiskey are also on offer if staying for a drink on the Quayside appeals.

If the south bank of the Tyne appeals more, the Lock and Quay Pub opens at 12pm, featuring a unique view of the Tyne Bridge from near the Swing Bridge to observe runners crossing into Gateshead.

  View of the Tyne Bridge from the Lock and Quay


The dog friendly spot is well rated by locals, who praise their Sunday dinners, burgers, wings and fries.

## Food between Gateshead and South Shields:

Located further down the route, right next to Felling Bypass, Fitzgeralds Wardley is a well-known former music hall with a new identity, perfectly suited to watch runners cross from the A184 on to Leam Lane from their outdoor terrace.

Open from 11.30am on Sunday, Fitzgeralds offers a wide range of wines, roast dinners & grill options, with customers praising the “helpful" and “friendly" staff, as well as wheelchair accessibility.

Towards the end of the route, just off Prince Edward Rd, Mio’s Cafe is ideal for anyone craving traditional deli food, or any ice cream and bakery items.

  Mio’s South Shields


Praised for “fabulous food," and a “lovely atmosphere," Mio’s is ideal for a spectator coffee break before moving towards the finish line.

## Food near finish line:

Colmans Seafood Temple sits just beyond the finish line on Sea Road, and is a massively popular seafood restaurant and oyster bar, having been named one of the best in Britain repeatedly.

  Colmans Seafood Temple


Beautiful sea views add to the award-winning restaurant’s appeal, although after the run it will be almost certainly full capacity!

Another South Shields favourite is the Bamburgh, a pub just off Coast Road on Bamburgh Avenue.

Open from 12pm on Sunday, Bamburgh offers traditional pub grub alongside a selection of burgers and light bites.

  The Bamburgh Pub from the Coast Road, South Shields


Recent reviews praise “reasonable pricing," “friendly staff," and “great value for money.”
