A Darlington bakery founded to help young people overcome barriers has grown rapidly after a major expansion saw its workforce triple.

Bakerman, based in Clark's Yard, was launched during the Covid lockdown by owner Dan Booth with a vision that extended beyond baking bread.

The not-for-profit bakery was established to provide employment and training opportunities for young adults facing barriers including exclusion, neurodivergence, poverty and poor mental health.

Since reopening following a major extension last summer, the business has gone from strength to strength.

  Bakerman, Darlington 

*(Image: Andy Futers)*

The expansion has allowed the bakery to significantly increase production, introduce new product lines and grow its workforce from four employees to 12.

Dan Booth, 40, said the past year had exceeded expectations.

  Bakerman, Darlington 

*(Image: Andy Futers)*

He said: "It's been absolutely flat out.

"We've pretty much trebled the amount of different products that we make since the reopening.

"Being able to expand meant we could add a full pastry section to the bakery, which has made a huge difference.

"It's been a big learning curve and a lot of hard work, but honestly it's going better than we planned for at this point a year on.

"We're able to offer far more than we could before, and customers seem to have really embraced that."

  Bakerman, Darlington 

*(Image: Andy Futers)*

The additional space has enabled Bakerman to expand beyond its artisan bread offering, with customers now able to choose from a wider selection of pastries, cakes and lunch items.

When Bakerman expanded last year, one of the key aims was to create a dedicated training facility alongside the bakery, making it easier to provide opportunities for those looking to develop new skills and gain employment experience.

The project was supported by the local community through a crowdfunding campaign which raised more than £18,000, helping fund new equipment and increase production capacity.

Dan said the growth had enabled the business to create jobs while continuing to invest in the future of young people in the town.

  Bakerman, Darlington 

*(Image: Andy Futers)*

He said: "We've gone from four to 12 staff, which has been fantastic.

"It's been a big step up in what we're able to bring to Darlington.

"The whole idea behind Bakerman has always been about creating opportunities for people, so being able to grow the team and create more jobs is really important to us.

"We've been able to expand what we do while still staying true to the reasons we started in the first place."

  Bakerman, Darlington 

*(Image: Andy Futers)*

The bakery's success has also been recognised by customers, who have nominated Bakerman for this year's Northern Echo Best Baker competition.

Dan said he was delighted to discover customers had put the business forward.

He said: "It's always lovely to be nominated.

"I wasn't actually aware that it was customers who had nominated us, so that makes it even nicer.

"It's always nice to be recognised for the good work that we do and to know people appreciate what we're trying to do here.

"Being shortlisted shows that customers value what we do, and that's something we're really grateful for."

Bakerman acquired the premises next door, allowing the original bakery to become a dedicated training and baking facility.

  Bakerman, Darlington 

*(Image: Andy Futers)*

Bakerman was also crowned the best bakery in County Durham at the National Bakery Awards in 2024.
