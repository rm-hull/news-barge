**Alfie Joey on the great work that is done each year by everyone taking part in the Great North Run**

FOR many years I commentated at the start of the Great North Run for the BBC, standing above the Rose Bridge just off Claremont Road.

The sight of all those different colours and costumes, all carrying different stories was never less than awe-inspiring.

For me, that is when the Great North Run becomes so much bigger than the biggest half marathon.

Every vest that names a charity is representing a cause that wants to make a difference. Wants to help a child. A parent. A brother. A sister. To remember someone who has died. To support someone who survived. Someone who is desperately hoping another family won’t have to go through what theirs did.

And suddenly those 13.1 miles mean something very different. The organisers say charity runners raise around £25 million every year.

Just think about that.

Twenty-five million pounds raised because ordinary people decided that if they were going to run 13 miles, they might as well make every one of those miles count for the good. When miserable people use the phrase ‘do-gooders’ as if it is a negative to ‘do good’, I’d be inclined to point to what those charity runners do tomorrow- it is a great day for the ‘do-gooders’.

And some of the causes being represented are incredibly close to the heart of the North East. I’d be here till the cows came home if I told you about every charity and this article would be the length of an old Yellow Pages telephone directory, so I thought I’d take a snapshot of some fantastic people and causes to show the wide spread variety of brilliance that happens because of this one day!

And if you don’t know where to put the spare change you’ve found down the back of the couch, any of the following would be more than grateful and deserving of your support. And if you’re stood among the crowds tomorrow, I’d love you to give them a huge encouraging cheer.

  **The Sick Children’s Trust**

Their runners are directly helping families who have a seriously ill child in the RVI or the Freeman Hospital. The charity provides ‘Homes from Home’ close to hospitals all over the country, allowing parents to stay near their child rather than facing expensive hotels, long journeys or sleeping in hospital chairs. More than 3,200 families were able to stay in one of its ‘Homes from Home’ during 2024/25.

Think about that if you see a Sick Children’s Trust vest running past.

  **Louie’s Charity**

This is a similar charity to The Sick Children’s Trust after Addison and Shannon Taylor, the proud parents of a remarkable little boy named Louie, who spent a significant portion of his brief life in hospital, decided to help other families who go through similar trauma. Louie’s Charity have around 55 in the Junior/Mini GNR today (including young Vincent who cannot wait to get his hands on his first medal! Good luck Vincent) and they also have 15 in the GNR proper, with fantastic support from Taylor Wimpey, who generously threw a 2nd annual ball for the charity on Thursday.

  **St Oswald’s Hospice**

Another organisation woven into the fabric of life in our region.

Their Great North Run team is made up of people running in support of its patients and families. If you know St Oswald’s, you’ll know it isn’t just a building or a service. For countless families, it represents care, dignity, comfort and support at some of the most difficult moments imaginable. The charity is also gearing up to auction off the most colourful collection of painted Peter Rabbits you may have seen dotted about the Toon- (details below).

  **US Active**

This is a newer charity to me and I love finding out about anyone trying to support young people with, or at risk of developing mental health difficulties, helping them to become physically active and, in turn, transforming their lives. ‘US Active’ helps young folk from the age of 12 to 25. Symbolically, the ultimate event to help this charity has got to be the Great North Run and they have 10 runners supporting them this year. Good luck!!!

They also have a cool, funky fundraiser in October- (details below).

  **Miscarriage UK**

This charity that offers support and information to anyone affected by miscarriage, ectopic or molar pregnancy. It is a national charity but it helps so many people I know locally, I had to give it a mention.

Anna Hodgson from Wideopen is taking on the challenge of the Great North Run for the charity and said every step is ‘for our baby girl and every family affected by pregnancy loss’. Anna works in Durham and recently spoke very powerfully about her experience on BBC Newcastle.

All of the above charities are reminder that the Great North Run isn’t only about the causes that are easy to talk about, but it is important that we talk people about the uncomfortable challenges many people go through. I also know that so many of these causes attract the most resilient, loveliest people. The very best of humanity.

What a privilege it is that, for one extraordinary day, our region gets to see all those stories, all of those causes, running, jogging together in a tidal wave of goodness from the Tyne Bridge to South Shields.
