A project is inviting people across England to help track one of nature's most spectacular seasonal changes as forests prepare for autumn following a summer of unusually hot and dry weather.

Forestry England has launched autumn leaf watch, a citizen science scheme running from September to November, inviting visitors to record and share observations of changing leaves at Dalby Forest.

Chris Sorenson, forest management advisor at Forestry England, said: "Autumn is one of nature’s most spectacular seasonal events, but it also gives us a fascinating insight into how the nation’s forests are responding to the world around them.

  Forestry England is inviting people to take part in its autumn leaf watch citizen science project at Dalby Forest, helping track seasonal changes following a summer of unusually hot and dry weather 

*(Image: Forestry England/Crown copyright)*

"The timing and intensity of autumn colour can vary significantly with weather and growing conditions, and understanding those changes is increasingly important as our climate changes."

Observations shared through Forestry England’s new online autumn colour tracker will help to build a picture of how autumn colour develops across different regions, habitats and tree species.

The project also offers the public a chance to connect with nature while helping to improve understanding of seasonal change.

Forestry England manages more than 1,500 woods and forests across England, including Dalby Forest, and in 2025-26 the nation's forests attracted 326 million visits.

The organisation recently launched autumn leaf watch to help track the changing season.
