A woman from Yorkshire has been named this year’s winner of the Great British Sewing Bee.

Anna, who lives in York, started as one of 12 contestants in the hit BBC show, but her brilliance, creativity and pure talent saw her show up week after week with pieces which moved judges to tears.

She began the final episode in a strong position, having already had seven transformation challenge wins and a hattrick of Garment of the Weeks.

Feeling confident, she said: “Before the competition, I wanted the confidence to believe in myself.

“In my head it feels like I’ve already won.”

  Judge Patrick Grant was reduced to tears after Anna's win 

*(Image: BBC)*

Anna competed alongside fellow finalists Beth and Emma.

The first challenge required them to sew a bow tie and cummerbund.

The suit accessories needed to be precise, with judge Esme expecting to see neat pleats in the cummerbund, with perfectly placed elastic – and a bow tie to match.

Anna, who selected red satin material for her design, said the pattern challenge was one she had not always been comfortable with.

  Anna joined her fellow contestants Beth and Emma for the final episode, which aired on Tuesday (September 15) 

*(Image: BBC)*

But she added: “It pushes me out of my comfort zone from making the same things over and over again.”

Anna discovered her love for sewing after repairing and altering pre-loved clothes to help the environment.

During lockdown she started researching fashion history and recreating historical garments, including a fabulous Queen of Hearts costume she said she is most proud of.

Her pieces, inspired by period silhouettes and techniques, have a gothic, grunge and flamboyant theme to them – something which has been showcased throughout the competition.

  Anna's mum Brenda, dad Steve and boyfriend Sebastian were all overjoyed by her win 

*(Image: BBC)*

After panicking about having to quickly reassemble her bow tie, which had become too thick after interfacing, it all came together for Anna.

She wowed judges with her creation, which judge Patrick Grant said, apart from some tiny issue with the banding on the bow tie, was “absolutely spot on”.

The 27-year-old further impressed judges with her transformation of tableware fabric which she used for her inventive beach club outfit.

Taking a piece of white linen, she cut a slit down the middle and ruched the bottom to make a fun silhouette, enhanced by orange pompoms, a red band and a white flower in the middle.

  Before she declared the show's winner, Anna scooped wins for her designs in the pattern and transformation challenge 

*(Image: BBC)*

The details were not initially intended, but it was Anna’s quick bursts of creativity which impressed Patrick.

He said: “You’ve pulled off making something with so much embellishment chic.

“The pompoms could really gimmicky but just don’t.”

Esme said it had been difficult when deciding the winner of the round, but Anna’s design was “just cool”.

## 'I'm going to take that to the grave'

With two final wins under her belt, an elated Anna said: “There was a period when I was having a bit of a wobble, but I managed to claw it back and Esme said it was cool – I’m going to take that to the grave.”

For the contestant’s last appearance in the Sewing Bee studio, Anna was joined by her close friend, who would be modelling her creation for the ‘Sewing Bee Gala’.

The gala was inspired by the Met Gala, with the theme of metamorphosis.

  Anna's final creation was inspired by the beautiful colours and lifecycle of damsel flies 

*(Image: BBC)*

Anna inspired all with her design, which featured the iridescent wings of a male and female damsel fly.

She said: “I was inspired by the lifecycle of a damsel fly.

“The colours of the male (blue) and female (green) when they come together to mate in the sky is just beautiful.”

Her blue dress was boned to give the illusion of the insect’s exoskeleton, with the wired wings stitched on the dupion silk blue dress.

  Patrick said that Anna had a "very rare gift", with Esme praising her for her creative spark 

*(Image: BBC)*

The dress won the approval of judges, but it was a design by fellow contestant Beth that won their hearts, making it the “closest competition in several years”.

Ultimately, it was Anna who was named the 2026 Sewing Bee champion.

Speaking about this, an emotional Anna said: “I never saw this coming.

“I’ve learnt so much in this competition. It’s so incredible.”

Patrick was reduced to tears when talking about Anna’s journey in the studio.

He said: “I am so delighted for Anna. When extraordinary craft people make things with skill, imagination and extraordinary storytelling, it affects you deeply.

“She’s got a really rare gift.”

Esme followed: “From the very beginning of the show, I saw Anna’s creative spark.

“That was really important to me.”
