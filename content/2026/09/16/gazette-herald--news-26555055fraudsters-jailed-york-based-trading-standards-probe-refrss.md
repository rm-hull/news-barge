Fraudsters who persuaded homeowners to pay vastly inflated prices for unnecessary or poor-quality roofing and building work have been jailed for a combined 22 years after being brought to justice by a York-based team.

The fraudsters were part of a 22-person criminal operation that extracted more than £1 million from 43 victims across Yorkshire, the North East and North Lincolnshire between May 2021 and May 2024.

Lord Bichard, chair of National Trading Standards, said the offenders were behind "calculated criminality that caused serious financial and emotional harm".

They "deliberately targeted people who were isolated, unwell or grieving" and took "life-changing sums of money, before trying to hide the proceeds through an extensive network of bank accounts", he said.

Cllr Jenny Kent, City of York Council's executive member with responsibility for trading standards, said the scale and complexity of the fraud was "quite shocking", adding: "These criminals have clearly caused a great deal of misery through their actions."

The York-based National Trading Standards Yorkshire and Humber Regional Investigation Team said the fraud started with homeowners being cold called and approached about relatively small jobs, such as clearing gutters or replacing a roof tile.

But once work began, the homeowners would be told that much more extensive and urgent repairs were required – with prices rapidly escalating into thousands or tens of thousands of pounds.

*(Image: Supplied)*

Frederick Finney, 48, of HMP Holme House, previously of Bickerton, West Yorkshire, was one of the architects of the conspiracy and used a network of money launderers to receive and move victims' money.

The network included Frederick Finney's wife, Carla Finney, 48, of Hillfield Close, Seamer, near Scarborough, described by prosecutors as a "prolific money launderer", who operated 14 bank accounts through which £381,152 linked to the conspiracy was identified.

  Carla Finney who was jailed for five years after being convicted of conspiracy to defraud and conspiracy to convert criminal property 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Among those targeted was a woman in her late 70s from New Marske, near Salburn-by-the-Sea, who was persuaded that her main roof needed replacing.

Over the following year the woman paid £94,700 for the work, but a chartered building surveyor later found the work had been carried out very badly and valued it at just £3,107.

Frederick Finney was jailed for six years and nine months after pleading guilty to conspiracy to defraud and conspiracy to convert criminal property.

Carla Finney was jailed for five years after being convicted of conspiracy to defraud and conspiracy to convert criminal property.
