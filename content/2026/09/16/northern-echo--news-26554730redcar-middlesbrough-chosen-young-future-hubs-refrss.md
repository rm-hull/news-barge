Thousands of young people across Teesside will soon have access to new support hubs designed to tackle mental health struggles, unemployment and youth violence.

Redcar and Middlesbrough are among 42 areas chosen for Young Futures Hubs, bringing essential services together for young people aged 10 to 18.

Support will be available up to age 25 for young people with special educational needs and disabilities (SEND).

The announcement has a particular resonance for Teesside, where concerns around youth violence and knife crime have grown, with high-profile campaigns launched in recent years.

The Northern Echo's long-running knife crime campaign brought together politicians, police leaders, charities and bereaved families across the North East to demand better prevention, education and support for young people at risk of violence.

Those involved included Sir Keir Starmer, Redcar MP Anna Turley and Health and Social Care Secretary Yvette Cooper.

Awareness of knife crime in the North East increased following the deaths of several young men, including Jack Woodley, 18, who was fatally stabbed as he left Houghton Feast in October 2021, and Connor Brown, who was killed outside a pub in Sunderland in 2019.

Families of other victims, including Chris Cave, 17, who was fatally stabbed in Sunderland in 2003, and Tomasz Oleszak, who was killed in Gateshead in 2023, continue to campaign for action aimed at preventing further tragedies.

The hubs will offer mental health, employment, training and youth activities while helping prevent serious violence and support vulnerable young people before problems escalate.

They will connect young people with trusted youth workers, sports and creative activities, mental health services, skills training, jobs and crime prevention programmes.

The Government says the aim is to create a "one-stop shop" where young people can access support without having to repeatedly tell their story to different services.

Speaking on Facebook, Redcar MP Anna Turley welcomed the announcement, saying: "Every young person deserves the very best start in life, wherever they live."

She added: "The hub will help ensure the right support is available at the right time, alongside sports, creative activities and trusted youth workers, mental health support, skills and training, routes into employment, and crime prevention."

The rollout is part of the Government's National Youth Strategy, Youth Matters, shaped by 14,000 young people and backed by more than £500 million for youth services over the next decade.

Announcing the programme, Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy said: "Last year we spoke to thousands of young people who told us that they needed somewhere to go, something to do, and someone to talk to.

"That's what these new Young Futures Hubs will give them.

"It is a symbol of this government wrapping their arms around young people, telling them we believe in their generation and that we know they can succeed."

  Keir Starmer and Yvette Cooper talk to Tanya Brown and Zoey McGill who both lost there sons to knife crime

The programme was launched after a decade of deep cuts to youth services.

Government figures show spending on youth services fell by 73 per cent between 2010 and 2023, while more than 1,000 youth centres closed nationwide.

Ministers say the hubs are designed to reverse that trend by rebuilding local youth services and connecting support that is often delivered separately.

Under the wider plan to halve knife crime over the next decade, Young Futures Hubs will work alongside multi-agency intervention panels in areas hit by serious youth violence.

Youth Minister Vicky Foxcroft said: "We need to do something different to ensure that young people are supported to thrive.

"By co-creating every Young Futures Hub and Transformation plan with young people, we are ensuring they are the architects of their own future."

The Department for Culture, Media and Sport said councils will receive funding to set up, run and maintain the hubs.

Three more of the 42 hubs will be established across the wider North East, in Newcastle, Northumberland and South Tyneside, alongside those in Redcar and Middlesbrough.

The latest rollout builds on eight pilot hubs, including one in Newton Aycliffe, with the Government committing £70 million to the Young Futures programme between 2026 and 2029.
