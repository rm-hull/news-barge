Landowners across North Yorkshire are being encouraged to plant more trees to help combat the growing threat of drought following what has been described as the UK's hottest summer on record.

According to the Met Office, this year’s prolonged heat and low rainfall surpassed records set just last year, with England receiving only three per cent of its average rainfall in July.

The Yorkshire Dales Millennium Trust (YDMT) is offering funding and support to landowners in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, Nidderdale, and the Forest of Bowland to support tree planting and boost landscape resilience.

Carol Douglas, woodlands manager at YDMT, said: "We are looking to support landowners who would like to plant trees; whether it’s just one or two in a field, a small copse with connecting hedgerow, scrub creation, riverside planting or a much larger woodland creation scheme."

YDMT has supported the planting of more than 1.7 million trees since 1997.

Trees play a vital role in retaining soil moisture, reducing erosion, and providing shade for livestock during extreme heat.

James Newhouse, vice chair of YDMT and a farmer near Long Preston, said: "Having a place to get out of the heat is very important to us."
