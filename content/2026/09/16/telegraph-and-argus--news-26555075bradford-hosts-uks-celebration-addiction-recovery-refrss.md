Just a year ago, Steve Rowe from Baildon was undergoing treatment for alcohol addiction in a Bradford rehabilitation centre.

Last weekend, he stood among hundreds of people at Bradford Live helping deliver the UK's biggest celebration of recovery from addiction.

The recovering alcoholic, who has now reached 12 months of sobriety, was one of the many people taking part in the UK Recovery Walk 2026, which brought visitors from across Britain and overseas to Bradford.

"This time last year I was actually in rehab," Steve said. "I've just done 12 months sober from alcohol. When I came out, I was looking for facilities and support groups that would help me stay alcohol-free.

"I tried a few different groups and eventually ended up at Create Strength Group Bradford. I've been going there since the turn of the year."

Steve said his battle with alcohol worsened after he lost his job following an epileptic seizure and spent months recovering from shoulder surgery.

His drinking escalated until a hospital admission became a turning point.

"My daughter and my sister were at my bedside when the doctor said if I carried on drinking, I may as well stop taking my medication because they were counteracting each other," he said.

"That was the wake-up call I needed.

"Within two weeks of leaving hospital, I'd booked into rehab in Bradford."

## 
  **Finding support in recovery**

Following treatment, Steve said he found a community through recovery groups in Bradford that helped him rebuild his life.

"When I first came out, I tried Alcoholics Anonymous, but a lot of the people were much further along in their recovery than I was," he said.

"When I went to Create Strength Group there were people who were days into recovery and others who had been clean for 10 or 15 years.

Now a year on, he says the difference in his life is remarkable.

"I'm a completely different person," he said. "I've got confidence, I've got purpose back in my life.This time last year I wouldn't have contemplated being in a position where I was helping out at something like this.

"My family have noticed the difference. They're really proud. The people in the groups support each other and look after each other.

## 
  **Thousands gather in Bradford**

The UK Recovery Walk returned last weekend with three days of events celebrating recovery from addiction and challenging stigma.

The programme included a national recovery conference, a celebration dinner, a recovery village, the recovery walk itself and a recovery ramble through the district.

People travelled from across the UK and beyond to attend, including a delegation from Finland.

Dave Memery, chief executive of UK Recovery Walk, said Bradford was proud to host the national event after successfully bidding to bring it to the district.

"The UK Recovery Walk is all about making recovery visible," he said. "We often see addiction because it's visible on our streets, but what people don't always see is recovery.

"Recovery can be hidden away in meetings and recovery cafés. The idea of the walk is to bring it out into the open and show people that recovery is possible."

## 
  **Challenging stigma**

Mr Memery said the event was designed both to challenge misconceptions about addiction and to offer hope to people who may be struggling.

He said: "They're someone's brother, sister, husband or wife. They're members of families and communities...but it's also for people who may be worried about their own drinking or drug use. We want them to know recovery is possible, and it's happening right here in Bradford."

The walk itself saw participants complete a 1.1-mile route through the city centre before returning to Centenary Square for live entertainment, family activities and community celebrations.

For Steve, however, the weekend represented something even bigger.

"It shows people that recovery isn't something to hide away," he said. "You can recover, and you can get your life back."
