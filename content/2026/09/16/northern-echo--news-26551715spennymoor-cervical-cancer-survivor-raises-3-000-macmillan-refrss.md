A County Durham woman who successfully overcame cervical cancer is raising thousands of pounds for Macmillan Cancer Support while encouraging other women to attend potentially life-saving screening appointments.

Rebecca Lawrence, from Spennymoor, was diagnosed with cervical cancer in October 2025 after a routine screening appointment.

Doctors initially thought they had caught the cancer early, but tests after Rebecca’s full hysterectomy found cancer cells in her lymph nodes, upgrading her diagnosis to stage three.

From January to March 2026, Rebecca had five weeks of daily radiotherapy alongside weekly chemotherapy.

A post-treatment scan showed no evidence of cancer, and Rebecca, 44, is now having regular check-ups.

Rebecca, who has two sons, said the diagnosis shocked her and her family.

  Rebecca, Dean and their two sons Ethan and Adam. 

*(Image: REBECCA LAWRENCE)*

“Initially it's just shock and your whole world kind of turns upside down,” she said.

“The worst goes through your mind but for me and my family we've had this mantra: 'fighter not victim'.

“You can't just succumb to it and let it take over. You've got to fight back, which is the way we approached the whole thing.”

Throughout her treatment journey, Rebecca received support from Macmillan Cancer Support, something she says inspired her to start fundraising for the charity.

She said: “You don't get to see it until you're in there.

“They help with everything. I didn't know how to tell the kids, so they helped us with that advice and support. They offered financial support.

“When I was in the chemo wards they would come and check on me weekly just to have a chat and see how I was going. They were always there and I could pick up the phone if I ever needed a chat.

“There was a lady in the chemo ward who was a volunteer and she would make teas and coffees and it keeps you going.

“You see the hands-on support they give people and you don't see that until you're on the journey. So I really wanted to give back to them.”

In March, Rebecca signed up for Macmillan’s Mighty Hike to raise money for the charity.

On September 5, Rebecca, her sister and friends walked 26 miles along the Northumberland coast.

   

*(Image: REBECCA LAWRENCE)*

The group completed the gruelling route and has raised almost £3,000, with more donations still coming in.

   

*(Image: REBECCA LAWRENCE)*

“I thought, what can I do?” Rebecca said.

“I decided to do the Mighty Hike. It was 26 miles and I walked across Northumberland and dragged a few of my friends and my sister with me. We've raised around £3,000.”

   

*(Image: REBECCA LAWRENCE)*

Fundraising has become a family effort, with Rebecca’s husband Dean Lawrence raising a further £2,000 for Macmillan by completing the Great North Run.

  Rebecca and her husband Dean. 

*(Image: REBECCA LAWRENCE)*

Rebecca is also organising a ladies’ day on September 26, with afternoon tea and fizz, to raise more money for Macmillan’s services.

The Northumbrian Water operations manager is also using her experience to highlight cervical cancer and the importance of screening.

“Cervical cancer is one that probably doesn't get as much attention as other female cancers,” she said.

“At work I've been sharing information about the importance of screening and advice from Macmillan.

“A couple of people have reached out to say it helped them and that they had gone for screening.

“If I can stop one person from going through what I've had to go through then it would be all worthwhile.”

Rebecca hopes her story will encourage more women to attend screenings and seek medical advice about health concerns.

She has already signed up for another challenge and hopes to run next year’s Great North Run.
