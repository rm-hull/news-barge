**HARROGATE**— Harrogate-headquartered disability and sight loss charity Henshaws is facing a complete top-down governance overhaul following the sudden announcement that both its Chief Executive and Chair of Trustees are stepping down simultaneously.Chief Executive Sally Daniels will officially vacate her role in October, followed immediately by Chair of Trustees Alistair How. While the charity’s official statement frames the dual departures as a managed transition to “the next stage of development,” the simultaneous exit of both executive and non-executive heads represents a total clean sweep of the organisation’s highest leadership levels.

The radical shake-up arrives amidst ongoing regional scrutiny regarding historical operational hurdles and staffing challenges at its flagship Bogs Lane campus in Harrogate. In recent years, the specialist college—which relies heavily on statutory local authority funding to house vulnerable young adults—has faced intense local pressure over management standards and the retention of specialised care staff.


**Sally Daniels, outgoing Chief Executive, said:**

It has been a privilege to lead Henshaws and to work alongside so many committed colleagues, volunteers, Trustees and supporters. I am incredibly proud of what we have achieved together and, most importantly, of the difference our teams make every day to the people we support.



The sudden nature of the overhaul is reflected in the board’s reliance on temporary caretaking measures. Chief Operating Officer Tom Harte has been bypassed for an immediate permanent promotion, stepping in instead as Interim Chief Executive while trustees launch an expansive national search to completely replace the management structure.

Similarly, Vice Chair Pete Bulkley will take over from Alistair How to stabilize the board. Daniels and How are slated to remain in secondary advisory capacities until December to manage “key projects” and cushion the operational fallout of the departures.

For an organisation deeply reliant on corporate sponsorships, local trust donations, and public funding, the simultaneous leadership vacuum exposes major friction behind the scenes. By clearing out the historical executive team at once, the charity is clearly attempting to draw a definitive line under legacy management complaints and project a sense of regulatory reform to external funders.


**Tom Harte, incoming Interim Chief Executive, said:**

It is a privilege to have been asked to lead Henshaws as Interim Chief Executive. Having been part of the organisation and its Executive Leadership Team, I know first-hand the commitment, expertise and ambition that exists across Henshaws, and the difference our teams make every day to the people we support. We have clear ambitions for the future and a strong platform from which to deliver them. My priority will be to provide continuity and leadership through this period, while continuing to improve and develop our services and ensuring that the people we support remain at the centre of every decision we make.
