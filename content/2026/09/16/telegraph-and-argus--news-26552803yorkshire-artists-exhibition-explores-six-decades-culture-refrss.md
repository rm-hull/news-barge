FROM Saltaire Teddy Boys to Whitby Goths, artist Claire Baxter has brought together six generations of fashion and music - and the Yorkshire places that shaped them - in a new exhibition.

What happens to our memories when the places that hold them change, disappear or take on a completely different life?

For award-winning Yorkshire artist Claire, the answer is Bygone Yorkshire - a collection of six oil paintings that journey through six decades of music, youth culture and fashion, from the Teddy Boys and Mods of the 1950s and 1960s through punk, New Romantics, 1990s pop and Goth.

Created over five months, the collection brings together two powerful forms of nostalgia: the people and subcultures that defined each generation, and the Yorkshire places that formed the backdrop to their lives.

Each painting is rooted in a real location which has been transformed by time. Some have disappeared altogether, others have been demolished or changed beyond recognition, while one - Whitby Abbey - remains standing but has long since lost its original purpose.

The result is a visual journey through a Yorkshire that has changed, while exploring something that remains remarkably constant: our memories of the places, music, clothes, friendships and moments when we felt we belonged.

  Artist Claire Baxter explores youth culture over decades


“From Teddy Boys, Mods and Punks to New Romantics, Pop and Goths, each generation had its own visual language and its own way of expressing itself,” says Claire.

“I wanted to explore another layer of nostalgia - the places that were part of those memories. Some have gone, some have changed and some are still standing but have a completely different purpose.

“By placing each generation in these settings, I could bring together two different kinds of memory - the people and the places.”

The six decades, and six Yorkshire stories are:

  Blue Suede Shoes: Teddy Boys of 1950s Saltaire. Images: Claire Baxter


* BLUE SUEDE SHOES - 1950s, Saltaire

Says Claire: “Teddy Boys and Teddy Girls embody the youthful rebellion of the post-war generation against the imposing backdrop of Saltaire’s mill - a place whose industrial purpose and surrounding world have been transformed by time.”

  My Generation: Mods gather with their scooters on Scarborough's seafront, capturing the freedom and energy of a generation 

*(Image: Claire Baxter)*

* MY GENERATION - 1960s, Scarborough

“Mods gather with their scooters outside the former Luna Park on Scarborough’s seafront, capturing the excitement, freedom and energy of a generation discovering its own identity.”

  Anarchy: Punks stop for fish and chips outside the former Knares borough bus station


* ANARCHY - 1970s, Knaresborough

“Punks stop for fish and chips outside the long-demolished Knaresborough bus station. The scene reflects the raw energy and desire for change that characterised the decade.”

  The Look of Love: New Romantics outside Harrogate's old market hall 

*(Image: Claire Baxter)*

* THE LOOK OF LOVE -1980s, Harrogate

“New Romantics gather outside Harrogate’s much-missed Old Market Hall, celebrating the drama, imagination and elaborate fashion of a generation determined to create its own visual identity.”

  Girl Power: Leeds nightclub Majestyk evokes era of Nineties pop


* GIRL POWER - 1990s, Leeds

“Outside the former Majestyk nightclub, the painting evokes the era of 1990s pop and the cultural impact of the Spice Girls, whose message of independence and individuality became one of the defining symbols of the decade.”

  Spellbound: Goths look out to Whitby Abbey ruins


* SPELLBOUND - 2000s, Whitby

“The atmospheric ruins of Whitby Abbey provide the backdrop for a generation of Goths.

Unlike the other locations in the collection, the Abbey itself remains - but its original purpose as a working abbey belongs to another age, giving the painting another dimension to the idea of Bygone Yorkshire.”

The places changed. The memories remained. Running through the collection is the idea that nothing stands still.

“Time doesn’t stand still. Places change. Buildings disappear. Buildings survive but take on new meaning. Music evolves. Fashion moves on. Subcultures merge into something new,” says Claire.

“But the memories remain. They remain in the places we remember, the music we listened to, the clothes we wore, the friends we made and the moments when we felt we belonged to something.

“This collection is my way of bringing some of those moments back to life.”

Claire Baxter’s work has attracted national and regional attention. In 2020, she was selected as the official artist for the Tour de Yorkshire, an appointment which generated significant media interest before the event was cancelled during the Covid pandemic.

With Bygone Yorkshire, Claire returns to a subject deeply connected to her own Yorkshire identity, creating a collection that combines contemporary painting with social history, music, fashion and collective memory.

* Bygone Yorkshire runs from Friday, September 18 to Sunday, September 20 at Claire Baxter Gallery, 3 Castlegate, Knaresborough, North Yorkshire.

The original paintings and limited edition prints will be available from the gallery and at www.clairebaxterfineart.co.uk
