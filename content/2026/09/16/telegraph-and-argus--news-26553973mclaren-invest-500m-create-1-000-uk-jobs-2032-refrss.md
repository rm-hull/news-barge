SUPERCAR manufacturer McLaren is to invest £500 million in Yorkshire and Surrey in a move that will create 1,000 new jobs.

The company said the jobs could be created across its bases in South Yorkshire and Woking, Surrey, by 2032, it estimates.

Plans include expanding the South Yorkshire research and development site and opening a new factory in the UK.

The jobs are expected to be created by 2032, according to McLaren.

McLaren operates a lightweight carbon-fibre chassis production facility at the University of Sheffield's Advanced Manufacturing Research Centre (AMRC) in Catcliffe, Rotherham

McLaren’s announcement comes as First Secretary of State Louise Haigh and Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds are expected to tour its Technology Centre in Woking on Wednesday.

Mayor Andy Burnham said: “McLaren is doubling down on Britain, keeping the design, the engineering and the building of every future car here, and taking on a thousand more people to do it.”

He said the investment would create apprenticeships, jobs and growth beyond McLaren’s factory gates.

Mayor Andy Burnham said: “I want young people to see that and know there is a future for them in making things, wherever they grew up.”

The Government is keen to sell the investment as a win for Britain.

Britain faces a precarious economic situation in part due to global turmoil caused by the wars in the Middle East and Ukraine.

Chief executive officer Nick Collins said: “We are investing in the future of McLaren and in advanced manufacturing in the UK.”

Chief executive officer Nick Collins said: “This investment will give us the platform to grow, develop our next generation of cars and build on what makes us distinctive, while strengthening skills, jobs and Britain’s global competitiveness.”
