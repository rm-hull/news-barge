CONCERNS about speeding in a residential York street have prompted plans to tackle the issue.

Surveys in Alness Drive, in Woodthorpe, done following concerns from residents found more than three fifths of vehicles using it exceeded the 20mph speed limit.

A City of York Council report stated installing speed humps at a cost of around £150,000 would improve road safety and bring wider benefits to the area.

But objectors claimed the ramps could damage vehicles and disputed the council's findings about problems with speeding on the street.

It comes as the plans are set to go before a council transport meeting on Tuesday, September 22.

They would see four speed humps installed in Alness Drive between the Moor Lane and Acomb Wood Drive junctions.

Two more humps would be placed in Acomb Wood Drive, just beyond the junction with Alness Drive.

Alness Drive has been subject to 20mph speed limits since a city-wide roll out of restrictions in 3/4.

A council survey in September last year recorded average speeds of 22mph southbound and 23mph northbound.

Speeds reached as high as 26mph southbound and 28mph northbound.

The installation of humps has been proposed after officials decided they would be preferable to speed cushions and raised tables.

Road repairs and resurfacing is also planned as part of the proposals.

Officials stated they hoped the scheme would encourage motorists to drive at more appropriate speeds which would improve safety and reduce the amount of enforcement work required.

They said: "Speed concerns were raised by residents and reviewed, and this review confirmed speeds being above the recommended levels.

"Subsequent annual speed surveys have since confirmed that the speeding issue is an ongoing concern."

The council consulted 448 homes in Alness Drive and nearby streets on the plans but only 14 per cent of them responded.

Supporters numbered 15 of the 63 households which responded, while 28 objected and 20 did not give a preference.

Those backing the plans said it would improve the road for pedestrians and cyclists, including children.

They added problems with speeding had grown in recent years, with cars and motorcycles coming off Moor Lane too quickly and that road being used by boy racers and cut-through traffic.

But those against the plans said speed ramps would be ineffective, poorly maintained and some drivers would ignore them and speed anyway.

They added there was no evidence of accidents or injuries requiring the measures and the scheme would be a waste of money which would be better spent elsewhere.
