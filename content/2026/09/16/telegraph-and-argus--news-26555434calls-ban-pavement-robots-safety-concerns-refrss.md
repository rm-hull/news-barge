Delivery robots are facing calls for a ban on pavements over safety and accessibility concerns.

Pedestrian campaigners and disability groups say the autonomous robots, already operating in cities including Leeds, pose a hazard to people with visual and mobility impairments.

Living Streets said the UK currently has no specific regulations governing the use of pavement robots.

The charity believes the unmanned devices are putting vulnerable pedestrians at risk.

Dr Amit Patel, a disability campaigner who is blind, said: "If you can see, walk or hear, you probably won’t be affected by delivery robots, but for those of us who’ve worked so hard to get our independence back, this is yet another hurdle.

"Some people tell me that it’s easier and safer to jump in a taxi and go to an out-of-town shopping centre than it is to visit their local high street because it’s just not safe.

"If we want to boost our local economies, we need to fix the legislation around delivery robots."

A YouGov poll commissioned by Living Streets found that 80 per cent of respondents believed the robots could pose problems for blind people, while 79 per cent thought they could be an issue for those with mobility impairments.

Of those who expressed an opinion, 60 per cent said robots should not be allowed on pavements.

The robots are already operating in areas including Leeds, Milton Keynes, Bristol, and Cambridge.

Ben Foley, campaigns and policy lead at Wheels for Wellbeing and a wheelchair user, said: "For many people who use wheelchairs, tricycles and mobility scooters to get around, too often there isn’t enough unobstructed width on the pavement.

"In some places pavement robots already create extra obstructions, as they try to get down pavements where there isn’t enough width for a wheelchair user to go the other way.

"If they are introduced more widely, there’s a real danger that large numbers of disabled people will have our routes blocked."

Living Streets is calling on the Government to immediately take steps to ban autonomous delivery robots while there is no clear regulation and legislation.

Tana Braun, the charity’s director of external affairs, said: "Our pavements are not for sale and should be for people.

"Delivery robots are putting pedestrians at risk and creating accessibility issues."

A spokesperson said: "Everyone should be able to use our pavements safely, and while we welcome innovation and advances in technology, it’s vital that pedestrians and vulnerable road users are put first.

"We will carry out trials to see how pavement delivery robots can operate safely alongside pedestrians, so we can make sure the right safeguards are in place and update the law."
