Durham Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen is urging employers to make road safety a boardroom priority as the campaign spreads across Britain.

County Durham transport firm Stiller Warehousing & Distribution Ltd has signed up to the Life is Precious campaign, which targets the "Fatal Four": speeding, drink and drug driving, failing to wear a seatbelt and using a mobile phone at the wheel.

The company has unveiled a newly branded trailer as part of the campaign, developed in partnership with Durham Constabulary’s Roads Policing Unit, to promote the road safety message to motorists nationwide.

Ms Allen, who is also the national joint lead for road safety for the Association of Police and Crime Commissioners, said: "If you drive for work, road safety is workplace safety.

"Employers have both an opportunity and a responsibility to help prevent tragedy before it happens.

"Every death on our roads leaves a family facing consequences that last a lifetime.

"That is why Life is Precious is such an important campaign—its message is simple, powerful and impossible to argue with."

Stiller is the second County Durham transport operator to promote the campaign on its vehicles, following KATEM Logistics Ltd last year.

Ms Allen is now calling on more businesses, regardless of fleet size, to support the campaign and help drive change.

She said: "I am delighted Stiller is helping us take that message across the country.

"But I want this to go much further.

"Whether a business operates two vans or 200 HGVs, I want employers asking themselves: are we doing everything we reasonably can to keep our employees and everyone else using our roads safe?

"That means good driving-for-work policies, effective management of risk and taking drink and drug misuse seriously.

"Stiller is demonstrating what responsible employers can do through comprehensive workplace testing covering its whole workforce.

"Road safety belongs in the boardroom as well as behind the wheel.

"I want more businesses to become road safety ambassadors, join us and help make these messages impossible to ignore.

"The more organisations we bring with us, the more lives we can save."

The campaign comes amid rising concerns about substance misuse and work-related road risk.

Department for Transport figures show that the proportion of commercial drivers involved in fatal or serious collisions who tested positive for drink or drugs rose from three per cent in 2015 to 11 per cent in 2025.

Research by National Highways and RoadSafe also found that nearly one in three road deaths involves a driving-for-work journey.

Stiller has already implemented a comprehensive drug and alcohol testing programme for employees at all levels of the business.

Brian Simpson, managing director at Stiller, said: "When we introduced testing, drugs were the main issue we identified.

"From an insurance and due diligence perspective, we knew we needed to take action.

"We now test all employees, not just drivers, right through to director level.

"We carry out testing where there are concerns about impairment, following workplace incidents and as part of our pre-employment checks.

"Road safety is everyone’s responsibility and we are proud to support the Life is Precious campaign and help spread these important messages."

Mari Johnson launched the campaign after her mother, Elaine Sullivan, and stepfather, David Daglish, both from Seaham, were killed on the A1(M) in 2021.
