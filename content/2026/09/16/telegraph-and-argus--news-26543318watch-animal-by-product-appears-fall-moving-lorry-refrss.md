A resident has raised concerns about repeated spillages of animal by-products from lorries passing through part of the Bradford district.

An incident occurred outside Ford Hill Newsagents in Queensbury.

The resident filmed the spillage at 8.10pm on Friday, August 28, 2026.


## 'A marked increase in spillages'

The resident, who has lived in the area for the past 40 years, claims that in recent months "there appears to have been a marked increase" in spillages from lorries.

They added: "These incidents are causing considerable concern and frustration among residents living in the surrounding area, particularly because of the extremely unpleasant and lingering odours that can remain following such spillages.

"This issue has reportedly been raised with the relevant authorities on numerous occasions, yet residents continue to see little evidence of meaningful action being taken."

## What Omega Proteins had to say

Leo Group is one of the UK’s leading specialists in animal by-products and renewable energy. It operates a plant in the Bradford district under the Omega Proteins name.

The site, which is in Thornton, renders high risk animal by-products collected from farms and abattoirs across England that are unfit for consumption including fallen stock, specified risk material and animals infected with disease.

The Telegraph & Argus has been unable to confirm which company operates the vehicle involved in the spillage.

A spokesperson for Omega Proteins did not confirm nor deny that it was one of its lorries, but said: "Omega Proteins operates a modern fleet of high-quality trailers and has a continuous programme of fleet renewal to maintain the highest possible standards.

"Our drivers and operational teams receive ongoing training and regular refresher training, supported by full-time trainers.

"We take every reasonable precaution to minimise the risk of any spillage occurring during transportation and have established procedures in place to respond appropriately should an incident occur."

Omega Proteins was Leo Group’s first rendering-plant acquisition and has been owned and operated by the group since 1999.
