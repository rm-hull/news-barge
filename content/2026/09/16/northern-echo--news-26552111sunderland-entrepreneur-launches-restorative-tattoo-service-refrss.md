A North East entrepreneur is helping people rebuild their confidence after cancer, surgery and trauma through specialist restorative tattooing designed to make a lasting difference to their lives.

Louise Stone, based in Sunderland, set up her social enterprise Pigments in Positivity earlier this year to offer treatments including 3D areola and nipple tattooing, MCA scar revision and paramedical scar camouflage.

Her services support people whose bodies have changed due to mastectomy, breast reduction, surgery, trauma or gender-affirming procedures.

  Louise Stone, founder of Sunderland-based social enterprise Pigments in Positivity 

*(Image: Helen Smith Photography)*

Ms Stone said: "I had this broad idea and knew I wanted to create something that could genuinely help people, but I really didn't know where to start when it came to setting up a community interest company.

"I went onto the BIC website, booked an appointment and my business adviser Anneline has been absolutely amazing ever since.

  Louise Stone, founder of Sunderland-based social enterprise Pigments in Positivity 

*(Image: Helen Smith Photography)*

"I genuinely couldn't have done it without her help.

"She has helped me identify potential funders, introduced me to referral partners and local voluntary-sector networks, as well as helping me shape a clear pilot project.

"Having an adviser who understands that I’m trying to build both a clinically responsible service and a sustainable social enterprise has made the whole process feel far less isolating and much more achievable."

  Louise Stone, founder of Sunderland-based social enterprise Pigments in Positivity 

*(Image: Helen Smith Photography)*

Pigments in Positivity was established with support from the North East BIC through the Wear Together initiative, which helps local residents and community groups establish and grow social enterprises and co-operatives across Sunderland.

Ms Stone’s decision to operate as a social enterprise was driven by her determination to remove financial barriers for those in need of treatment.

She said: "These treatments can make such a difference to someone, but I appreciate that not everyone will be in a position to afford them.

"If somebody has been off work for a long period because of cancer treatment, surgery or another traumatic experience, they may not have the finances to access something that could genuinely help them feel better about themselves.

"That’s why I wanted to create a social enterprise.

"If people can afford treatment, that’s great, but if they can’t, I want to be able to find ways to support them too."

The impact of her work has already been felt by clients.

One woman who had a double mastectomy told Ms Stone that receiving realistic areola tattoos felt like the final step in her recovery.

She said: "Restorative tattooing is sometimes misunderstood as being purely cosmetic, but for many people it can represent closure, confidence and a sense of ownership after an experience where choices about their body may have been taken out of their hands.

"It’s amazing to be able to do something that can make that kind of difference to somebody."

Through the BIC, Ms Stone has begun building relationships with other social enterprises, charities, healthcare professionals and potential partners across the region.

Anneline Dowell, social enterprise business adviser at the BIC, said: "Louise’s passion for helping people feel more confident and comfortable in their own bodies is clear to see.

"She has identified a real need for these specialist services and is creating a social enterprise that has the potential to make a lasting difference to people across the region.

"It’s been fantastic to help Louise turn her idea into Pigments in Positivity and we look forward to following her journey as the organisation continues to grow."

Pigments in Positivity operates from the Eagle buildings in Sunderland and delivers treatments in a private, inclusive and supportive environment.

Louise aims to make services available on a sliding scale or free of charge wherever funding allows.

Wear Together forms part of Sunderland City Council’s Business Support Programme and is funded by the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority through its Integrated Settlement.

More information about Pigments in Positivity is available at www.pigmentsinpositivity.org.uk.
