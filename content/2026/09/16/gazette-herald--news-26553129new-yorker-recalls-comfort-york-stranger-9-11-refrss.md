A New Yorker who lived through the immediate aftermath of the September 11 attacks has described how an unexpected telephone call from a stranger in York was an 'emotional lifesaver'.

Alan Brownstein was travelling from New York’s LaGuardia Airport to Washington on the morning of September 11, 2001, when his flight had been delayed on the tarmac and the captain announced that a plane had struck the World Trade Center.

Alan was 56 at the time, and President and CEO of the American Liver Foundation (ALF) headquarters in New York City.

"I assumed it was an accident involving a small plane," he told The Press. "The captain then announced that another plane hit the South Tower. We knew immediately that America was under attack.

"Just before 10am, we watched on TV, in disbelief, as the South Tower collapsed in a cloud of dust. The terminal fell silent. We knew instantly, the world had changed."

As Alan and other passengers watched events unfold on television in the terminal, his wife Patty and son Jeremy feared he might have been aboard one of the hijacked aircraft.

*(Image: Alan Brownstein)*

Unable to contact him because telephone networks were overwhelmed, they did not initially know where he was.

Patty, a nurse at Mount Sinai Hospital, prepared to receive casualties but the expected flow of injured people never arrived as almost everyone in the towers had been killed.

Alan's office was two blocks from the World Trade Centre, on Maiden Lane, where 35 colleagues were left traumatised by the attacks and their aftermath.

He said office windows were struck by 'blackened ash and debris' when the first tower collapsed. Staff remained in the building until the afternoon, when they were escorted down the stairs through dust and debris.

"The endless fire filled the air with asbestos with a number of us contracting cancer," said Alan.

He later developed two cancers diagnosed as World Trade Centre-related.

Against that backdrop, a call from a stranger named Nick, from York, took on a profound significance.

  Alan and Patty Brownstein with family. 

*(Image: Alan Brownstein)*

'Nick from York' had no connection to the Brownstein family but had simply felt compelled to contact someone in New York and offer comfort to an American after watching the attacks unfold.

Alan's son Jeremy, who was also on the phone call, said the circumstances felt 'almost impossible'.

"Out of all the phone numbers, all the houses, all the people in New York City he could possibly have called, somehow our number was the one he picked," he said.

   

*(Image: Alan Brownstein)*

But it was Nick’s kindness that mattered most.

Jeremy added: "I remember how genuinely kind he was. He was calling simply because he was saddened by what had happened and wanted to reach out to somebody in New York City and America and say so."

The call showed the family that the attacks were not only devastating New York or the United States, but people across the world were grieving with them.

"There was something deeply reassuring about that," Jeremy said. "For a little while, it made it feel like we weren’t alone.

"Here was a person thousands of miles away who had been affected enough by what happened that he picked up a telephone and called a complete stranger in another country."

  Happy times for the Brownstein family. Pictured, from left, Alan Brownstein with his granddaughter Hannah, wife Patty and grandson Tyler. 

*(Image: Alan Brownstein)*

Alan shared the story with other Americans during the year that followed, saying it brought comfort to many.

He said memories of the attacks returned with renewed force as the 25th anniversary approached. Yet his thoughts repeatedly turned to Nick and the connection created by that brief conversation.

Alan wrote to the Letters page at The Press to express his heartfelt gratitude to 'Nick from York' and said he and his wife felt emotional when they learnt it had been published and was a reminder 'of reflection, compassion and mutual support when tragedy strikes'.

He added: "He gave me real comfort at a time when I needed it most and I still feel that gratitude today."

The United States marked 25 years since the attacks on September 11, 2026.

A total of 2,977 people were killed in New York, at the Pentagon and near Shanksville, Pennsylvania.
