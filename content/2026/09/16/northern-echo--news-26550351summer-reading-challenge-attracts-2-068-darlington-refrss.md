Hundreds of children and families took part in summer activities at Darlington’s libraries, boosting creativity, learning and reading while book borrowing soared.

Darlington and Cockerton libraries ran summer events for young people, including crafts, Rhymetime, adventure play, animation, slime-making and virtual reality.

This year’s programme had added significance during the National Year of Reading 2026, a UK-wide initiative led by the Department for Education and National Literacy Trust.

  Darlington and Cockerton libraries attracted hundreds of children and families to summer activities, boosting creativity, learning, reading engagement and book borrowing across the town 

*(Image: Supplied)*

The campaign aims to rekindle a love of reading by linking books to people’s interests, from sport and music to baking and family activities.

Councillor Libby McCollom, Darlington Council’s cabinet member for local services, said: "This summer's activities have been a huge success and demonstrate the vital role our libraries play in supporting children’s learning, creativity and wellbeing.

"As we celebrate the National Year of Reading 2026, it is particularly encouraging to see more children borrowing books and taking part in the Summer Reading Challenge.

  Children taking part in summer activities in Darlington Library 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"Reading opens doors to new opportunities, and I am delighted that so many young people have embraced it this summer."

More than 10,000 children’s books were borrowed from Darlington and Cockerton Libraries in August, up 17 per cent on the same month last year.

The figures cover physical loans only, not digital downloads from the libraries’ eBook and audiobook service, BorrowBox.

The Summer Reading Challenge attracted 2,068 entries.

The entrants were almost evenly split by gender: 54 per cent female and 46 per cent male.

A Summer Reading Challenge prize-giving event will be held at Darlington Library on Thursday, September 17.

Successful participants are being contacted and invited to the celebration.

The National Year of Reading 2026 has the theme “Go All In”, celebrating how reading can deepen people’s connections with what they love.

National Literacy Trust research shows why it is vital to foster a love of reading in childhood.

Children who enjoy reading are three times more likely to have good mental wellbeing than those who do not.
