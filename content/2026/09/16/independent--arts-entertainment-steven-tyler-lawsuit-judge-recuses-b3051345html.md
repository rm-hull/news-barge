A second judge has removed herself from a lawsuit accusing Aerosmith frontman Steven Tyler of having an illegal sexual relationship with a girl beginning when she was 16 and he was 25.

Torrance Superior Court Judge Patricia A. Young notified attorneys Tuesday that she was recusing herself from the case. A hearing to reassign the lawsuit is scheduled for Friday before Los Angeles Superior Court Judge Lawrence P. Riff.

A minute order issued by Young’s clerk did not explain her decision.

![A second judge has removed herself from a lawsuit accusing Aerosmith frontman Steven Tyler of having an illegal sexual relationship with a minor girl](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/04/29/22/2234223905.jpg)

Young had been assigned the case last fall after Inglewood Superior Court Judge Tamara Hall disqualified herself a day before a scheduled hearing on Tyler’s attempt to dismiss some or all of the lawsuit.

The woman sued Tyler in December 2022, alleging the singer persuaded her mother to grant him legal guardianship when she was 16, allowing her to live with him while the pair had a sexual relationship.

She alleges the relationship lasted about three years beginning in 1973.

According to the lawsuit, the woman became pregnant in 1975, meaning Tyler was allegedly both the father of her unborn child and her legal guardian. She claims Tyler pressured her into having an abortion despite her Catholic faith.

![Tyler, 78, formed Aerosmith in Boston in 1970](https://static.the-independent.com/2024/02/01/19/SUPER_BOWL-HISTORIA_DEL_MEDIO_TIEMPO_27709.jpg)

The woman says she eventually made a “conscious decision to leave and escape the music and drug-addled world,” seeking freedom from what she described as a sexualized culture surrounding Tyler and the music industry.

Tyler’s attorneys have challenged the lawsuit and maintain that most of the woman’s claims concern alleged conduct in Massachusetts, Oregon and Washington and would be barred by statutes of limitations in those states. They say only one claim has a sufficient connection to California.

In recent pretrial filings, Tyler’s lawyers also asked the court to prevent two of the woman’s sons, Seth and Heath, from testifying at trial.

The attorneys argue the sons were not alive when the alleged abuse occurred and therefore have no firsthand knowledge of it.

They also contend that any testimony about their mother’s character or emotional distress would be cumulative and potentially prejudicial given testimony expected from expert witnesses.

Tyler, 78, formed Aerosmith in Boston in 1970.
