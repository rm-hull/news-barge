Floral displays, green spaces, and community effort have earned top honours for towns across the region in this year’s Northumbria in Bloom Awards.

The annual ceremony at The Fed in Gateshead today (Wednesday, September 16), celebrated towns, villages and neighbourhoods that improved their surroundings through gardening, environmental work and community involvement.

Darlington took home two major awards, winning the Most Improved Entry and topping the City Centre category.

Assessors praised Darlington’s green spaces, colourful planters and hanging baskets, along with the continuing development of its 'Yardens' initiative.

They also praised the pride shown by residents, businesses and the council.

  South Park in Darlington, which was named Best Park at the Northumbria in Bloom 2026 Awards. Judges described the Victorian park as "a haven for adults, children of all ages and wildlife". 

*(Image: Chris BOOTH)*

Darlington's South Park also won the Best Park award, with judges calling it “a haven for adults, children of all ages and wildlife”.

Steve Harker, leader of Darlington Borough Council, said: "I am absolutely delighted that we have won three golds at this year’s Northumbria in Bloom.

"These awards are testament to the hard work of all the volunteers, staff and Friends groups who make our public spaces look so fantastic throughout the year - I'd like to give a huge thanks to everyone who helped make this happen."

He added: "Protecting and promoting the local environment is one of the priorities in our Council Plan and these awards show how, by working together, we can ensure Darlington is a great place to live, work and visit."

Bishop Auckland also won the Towns and Small City category.

The award citation highlighted the town’s transformation, from vibrant planting schemes and market-place improvements to nature-themed murals.

They also recognised the contributions of Bishop Auckland in Bloom and The Auckland Project to the town’s regeneration.

There was further success for the town as Bishop Auckland Hospital Community Garden won the award for Grounds of Hospitals, Universities and Other Public Buildings.

  A colourful floral display in Darlington town centre, which won the City Centre category at the Northumbria in Bloom Awards. 

*(Image: Darlington Borough Council)*

Judges praised the site as a calm and welcoming space for patients, staff and residents, highlighting its mix of seasonal colour, permanent planting and fruit trees.

The Percy Boydell Award for Best Overall Entry, the highest honour in the competition, was claimed by Washington Village, which also won the village category and Best Entry in Spring.

Assessors praised its dedicated volunteers, colourful displays and strong partnerships with local businesses and Sunderland City Council.

  Hanging baskets and floral displays brighten a shopping street in Darlington following the town's Northumbria in Bloom success. 

*(Image: Darlington Borough Council)*

Robert Germany, chairman of Northumbria in Bloom, said: "In bloom communities tackle everything from litter, graffiti and mitigating anti-social behaviour through to conservation, biodiversity and improving horticultural standards.

"It is wonderful to see all the excellent work that communities are doing to protect and enhance their local environments.

"These awards are all about recognising and celebrating this work and all that our communities have collectively achieved in enhancing the overall environment of the North East."
