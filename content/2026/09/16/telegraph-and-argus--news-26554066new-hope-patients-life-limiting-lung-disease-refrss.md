A POTENTIAL breakthrough in the treatment of a rare and life-limiting disease has been made by researchers.

The discovery, made by scientists at the University of Bradford, offers new hope for patients with pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), a progressive condition that narrows blood vessels in the lungs and puts increasing strain on the heart.

Researchers have identified cryptolepine, a naturally occurring compound, and related substances as possible treatments for correcting the underlying biological defects of the disease.

Dr Md Talat Nasim, associate professor at the University of Bradford, said: "This research represents an important milestone in a journey that began for us in 2014.

"Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension is a devastating disease that can have a profound impact on patients and their families.

"Our research has identified compounds capable of correcting signalling defects that sit at the heart of the disease.

"While there is still a long way to go before this could become a treatment for patients, the results are extremely encouraging and provide a strong foundation for further development."

The research, published in Research Connections, revealed that cryptolepine could restore balance to disrupted signalling pathways caused by mutations in the BMPR2 gene.

These pathways regulate how cells grow and function in the lungs.
