A 40-year-old intelligence officer has died in a road collision in Ukraine, the Ministry of Defence has confirmed.

Major Paul Wilks was killed in a traffic accident away from the front lines on Saturday 12 September.

Having commissioned from Sandhurst in 2012, he spent his entire military career serving in the Intelligence Corps.

His Commanding Officer described him as a "man of great intellect", who frequently spoke of his deep love for his wife and two sons.

Major Wilks completed multiple operational tours, including to Afghanistan, receiving two Operational Service Medals.

He was also awarded the Long Service and Good Conduct Medal on 7 May 2026.

His Commanding Officer said: “It is with profound sadness that we honour the memory of Major Paul Wilks.

“Even in high-pressure environments, Paul maintained a sharp sense of humour and a genuine warmth that connected him to everyone in this unit. He was a man of great intellect, yet he remained a mate to those he served with.

“He spoke often of his love for his wife and two sons, expressing his deep gratitude for the support they provided, which allowed him to serve with such distinction.

![Major Paul Wilks spent his entire military career serving in the Intelligence Corps](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/16/17/01M2NJ0NDJXSFSTYPP9BJ1206T.jpg)

“It is because of that foundation that he was able to meet the challenges of his role, and it is in the course of his devotion to duty that he made the ultimate sacrifice.

“Major Wilks represented the finest traditions of UK Defence. It was a privilege to serve alongside him. We will miss him.”

Lieutenant General Matthew Jones, Colonel Commandant Intelligence Corps, described Major Wilks as “one of the most talented intelligence officers of his generation”.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said: “Major Wilks served this country with pride and dedication. His loss will be deeply felt by those around him.

“The tributes paid by those who worked with Paul illustrate his exceptional talent and ability, underscored by genuine warmth, compassion and a desire to support others.

“His loss is a poignant reminder of the enormous sacrifice that our Armed Forces and their families make in the service of our country.

“My thoughts remain with his family, loved ones and colleagues.”

There is a small number of British personnel in Ukraine who provide security to diplomats and support the country’s armed forces.

British paratrooper George Hooley, 28, was killed in Ukraine in December when he watched Ukrainian forces testing a new defensive capability.
