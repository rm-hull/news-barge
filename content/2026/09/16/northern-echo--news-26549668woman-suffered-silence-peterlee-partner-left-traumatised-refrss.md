A woman suffered in silence as her immature partner subjected her to an increasingly controlling and abusive relationship, a court was told.

The victim eventually ended the relationship, but Jordan Joseph Penman left her fearing what he might do next.

Her anxiety was only slightly eased when Penman was remanded in custody eight months ago, after he smashed his way into her mother’s home and grabbed her phone.

Penman initially denied his offending, but on the day of his trial in June, the 27-year-old, of Tweed Close, Peterlee, admitted controlling and coercive behaviour, criminal damage to a mobile phone and window, common assault, two breaches of a non-molestation order, burglary and fraud.

  Durham Crown Court told victim of abusive partner Jordan Joseph Penman 'suffered in silence' for many months before ending the relationship


Prosecutor Martin Towers said the relationship began well, but Penman once smashed a mirror because he did not want her to go out without him.

The incident was reported to police, and Penman was released on bail with conditions.

It was a sign of things to come as Penman’s angry outbursts became more frequent, with him “being awful” one day and “sweet talking” her the next.

She said “the bad days got worse”, while Penman would disappear for a week at a time to use cocaine.

Penman hit the back of her head, temporarily affecting her vision, struck her nose until it “popped”, and punched her thigh and ribs over old messages.

Despite her sore ribs, she chose not to seek medical help.

Mr Towers said she sought medical help only when her injuries were severe, preferring to recover on her own.

She ended the relationship on November 1 and moved in with her mother in Hartlepool, but Penman persuaded her to visit his Peterlee home and refused to let her leave.

She eventually contacted police, and Penman was arrested before being released on bail with a condition not to contact her.

The victim was also granted a non-molestation order, which was served on Penman on December 19.

But on Boxing Day evening, Penman smashed into her mother’s Hartlepool home with a brick and stormed upstairs to the bedroom where she was hiding with her sister.

Mr Towers said Penman appeared “full of rage” as he wrestled the phone from his ex-partner’s grasp.

Both women screamed in terror, and one was struck in the face by Penman’s elbow.

Penman fled with the phone and his ex-partner’s bank card, which was inside its cover.

He used the card to withdraw £500 without her permission and spent the money at bookmakers.

Mr Towers said it a “flagrant breach” of the order which the defendant breached again on December 31 by "having the audacity" to send his ex-partner a “Happy New Year” text message.

Penman was arrested on January 16 and made no comment in a police interview.

The victim said she was left depressed and traumatised by his actions and felt he was ruining her life.

She feared he would one day cause her serious harm.

Michael Forrest, in mitigation, told the court: “Mr Penman on drugs is a different Mr Penman when in periods of sobriety.

“Unfortunately, they have been rare as he has used illicit substances since the age of 13.”

Forrest said: “He accepts he has done wrong and his drug use was the cause.

“His cocaine abuse was the catalyst and he knows he made some bad choices.”

Mr Forrest said Penman accepted his behaviour was “utterly stupid” and that while his partner “became a woman, he remained a boy.”

“He understands how immature he was during the relationship.”

Mr Forrest said Penman had shown signs of growing up since being remanded in January, choosing a drug-free wing and becoming an “enhanced prisoner” responsible for cleaning.

Judge Amanda Rippon told Penman his partner endured much of his violence in silence, “trapped” in the abusive relationship and “in fear and intimidated.”

But when she did end it, his behaviour did not change.

The judge said Penman’s conduct since his arrest and remand, however, gave her hope that he could be rehabilitated.

She imposed a three-year sentence, suspended for three years, calling it an “exceptionally merciful course”.

But she warned it was not “a get out of jail free” situation, as Penman must comply with 12-month GPS monitoring and drug rehabilitation orders, plus a 120-day alcohol abstinence order.

A five-year restraining order prohibits him from approaching or communicating with his ex-partner.

The judge warned that any breaches would come back before her and she would jail him, with “no mitigation”.
