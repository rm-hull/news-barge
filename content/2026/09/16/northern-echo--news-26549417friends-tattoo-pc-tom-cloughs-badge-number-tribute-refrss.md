Four childhood friends of fallen police officer PC Tom Clough have found a permanent way to carry his memory with them forever.

The friends, who first met Tom when he joined their school in Year 10, grew up together, spending time in Marske and remaining close throughout their lives.

Shaun Taylor, Dale Smith, Chris Overfield and Daniel Moore have now all had Tom's badge number, LZ3234, tattooed on their bodies as a lasting tribute following his death.

  Shaun Taylor, Dale Smith, Chris Overfield, Daniel Moore. 

*(Image: SOCIAL MEDIA)*

Shaun said: "We just wanted something to remember him by. Something we can talk about together. It's a memory that we can have forever."

Tom’s death and the loss of his colleague Matty have devastated the group.

"We're all broken if I'm honest," Shaun said. "It's been difficult to accept, not just Tom but his colleague, Matty. It's been difficult."

The friends were also overwhelmed by the turnout for Tom's funeral, saying the number of people who came to pay their respects reflected the impact he had on so many lives.

"He spent his life serving the public," Shaun said. "It fills you with pride at the amount of people who came out to commemorate him. It's really powerful."

Hundreds of officers paid respects to their fallen colleague, many wearing a patch bearing a the thin blue line and the officers’ names and collar numbers.

Members of the public also gathered along the route, some clutching flowers and others bowing their heads as the cortege passed.

Reflecting on Tom’s career, Shaun added: "He was in the Marines, the Army, three different police forces. What an achievement for such a young age."

Among the memories that remain strongest for his friends is the way Tom ended every phone conversation.

Shaun said: "Every time Tom ended a phone call, he would say, 'Take care', he was just lovely."

PC Tom Clough died alongside colleague PC Matthew Blades when their marked police car was hit head-on by a VW travelling the wrong way on the A66 at South Bank.

A fundraiser set up by the Cleveland Police Federation for the two families raised £1,203,932, with the money due to be split equally between them.
