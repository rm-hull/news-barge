A developer has been accused of “capitalising” on “lucrative” Yarm by bidding to build 600 new homes - with residents fearing it “sets a precedent” for further new estates.

  Taylor Wimpey came under fire yesterday (September 15) as it __kicked off its appeal against the refusal of its controversial plan for new homes off Green Lane.__ 

The developer said the £133m plans came amid an “acute and serious local need” for housing as Stockton Borough Council faced a “significant shortfall" of new homes in the area.

The council argued that the new estate would cause “substantial and irreversible” harm and would have “significant adverse impacts” on roads, branding it “unsustainable”.

  Fields off Green Lane in Yarm, where Taylor Wimpey wants to build 600 new homes 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

And as the nine-day public inquiry began on Tuesday, several Yarm residents stood up in protest of the application, saying they fear it would “change the character of the town”.

Yarm Town councillor Yvonne Wallace, who launched a ‘Save Yarm’ campaign last year has had 4,700 people back her petitions, accused the developer of “dressing up” the reason behind the plans as a housing shortfall.

“I want to be very blunt here. Taylor Wimpey is a powerful, commercial company, capitalising on the need for housing,” she told the inquiry.

“They are not proposing to build housing in Yarm that Yarm may need and housing that will cause less problems. They have chosen Yarm and this development because it is lucrative.

  Petition campaigner and Yarm town councillor Yvonne Wallace on True Lovers\' Walk, Yarm, in front of Yarm Viaduct. Picture: LDR.


“This might be dressed up by the representative of Taylor Wimpey when at the planning committee meeting they said this application would take people off the streets.

“Really? I’m not sure about that.”

Ms Wallace said this was the “most concerning” of the applications submitted for Yarm in recent months, due to it being a “built up” area.

She raised concerns over traffic as it is, saying it can be "extremely dangerous” when cars turn around due to congestion - and that the new estate could "multiply” dangerous manoeuvres.

"I am not opposed to my housing. What I am opposed to is the wrong housing in the wrong area, which this application is,” she said.

   

*(Image: P+HS ARCHITECTS / TAYLOR WIMPEY)*

Councillor Malcolm Palmer, speaking on behalf of the town council, said there were “serious concerns” when it came to traffic and impact on roads.

He argued the transport assessments focused “largely on the immediate impact of this individual development rather than the cumulative effect of most developments”.

Cllr Palmer also argued the estate would “change the character of the town”.

“This proposal is being driven primarily by achieving the housing density rather than responding positively to the rural setting and character of Yarm,” he said.

  Fields off Green Lane in Yarm, where Taylor Wimpey wants to build 600 new homes 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

  “Yarm and Eaglescliffe are particularly constrained, because they're effectively on one principal road. Additional development [will] inevitably increase pressure on this road network."


## 
  **‘Unacceptable’ traffic woes**

Retired chartered surveyor Michael Brazell said the volume of traffic from 600 extra homes is not only “unacceptable” but “sets a precedent” for other planned estates.

“Leven Bank, including the Fox Covert junction on the far side of the bank, is widely recognised as a constrained and potentially hazardous route,” he said.

  The public inquiry into a new 600-home estate in Yarm has kicked off in Thornaby 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

“Long standing highway issues have never been satisfactorily addressed, and again, there are no proposals by the appellant to mitigate the impact of yet more traffic than this application.”

He slammed numerous “aggressive” applications over the last 18 months, which are all “using a housing shortfall, and the presumption in favour of development, to justify building outside the defined limits of the local plan”.

He also presented a compilation of photographs from accidents on the bank, which he said include five serious injury incidents since 2009.

Debbie Watt, who has lived on Leven Bank since 2014, also raised concerns over the impact of more traffic on the Grade II listed Leven Bridge.

She recalled several crashes outside her home, and noted how the bridge already has issues with “standoffs” as large vehicles struggle to get past the “pinch point”.

  Fields off Green Lane in Yarm, where Taylor Wimpey wants to build 600 new homes 

*(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

“Every time I hear a crash, I dread to leave my gate to see what's happened and whether somebody needs help,” she said.

“The impact [of crashes] can be felt inside my home. My house shakes, I see my cups of tea moving on the table, and my pictures fall off my walls.”

Craig Marshall said since he moved to Yarm 40 years ago, he has experienced a “gradual erosion in the quality of life”.

“When we moved into Yarm, there was such a thing as a green belt,” he said.

Mr Marshall branded road congestion as a “major problem” and said Yarm Medical Centre was “already overstretched”: “Getting an appointment there is very difficult.”

## 
  

  **Developer - ‘serious’ housing shortfall in area**

  Plans for the estate, first unveiled in June 2025, were __rejected this year__ following almost 600 objections.

At the inquiry yesterday, Barrister John Hunter, for the council, said the farmland was “unsuitable” for so many new homes.

  A satellite map of the site where Taylor Wimpey proposes to build up to 600 homes in Yarm. Picture: Taylor Wimpey.


He argued road fears have “still not been adequately assessed or mitigated” and concerns over electricity capacity remain “unresolved”, given Northern Powergrid’s previous comment on how the area’s network has “limited capacity to support additional homes without upgrades”.

But Sasha White KC, for Taylor Wimpey, cited a national housing crisis as a key reason as to why the application should be approved.

He said the council currently had "serious and significant shortfall" of new homes, at least 1,162 homes over a five-year period and 809 affordable homes since 2017/18.

Mr White also noted how “every statutory consultee with technical expertise supported the application” and that the case in favour of the appeal is "compelling".

Addressing concerns over impact on roads and electricity, Mr White noted how both National Highways and Northern Powergrid did not object.

He said the council’s highways, transport, and design manager concluded residual impact on the network “would not be severe” following proposed mitigation measures.

The inquiry continues.
