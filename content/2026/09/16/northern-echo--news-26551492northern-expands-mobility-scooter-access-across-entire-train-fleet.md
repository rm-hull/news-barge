Passengers with mobility scooters can now travel on all train types across Northern’s fleet with a free permit, it has been announced.

The change follows an update by Northern to allow smaller mobility scooters on its Class 158 trains, which previously restricted access due to space limitations.

Alex Hornby, customer and commercial director at Northern, said: "We are determined to create an accessible railway for everyone and continue to make further improvements for our customers across Northern.

"With more people using smaller scooters, we decided it was time to review the rules and find a new way forward to enable more passengers with mobility scooters to safely board more services."

Current permit holders are being contacted and advised that they can now use Class 158 trains, provided their scooters meet the specified size requirements.

They will receive a new permit confirming their scooter model is suitable for safe use on these trains.

Northern introduced its scooter policy in 2020, but at the time, most scooters were too large to manoeuvre in the narrow spaces of the Class 158s.

Growing use of smaller, lighter scooters prompted a policy review.

After consultation with passengers and staff, testing different models, and reassessing more than 150 stations, Northern decided to update its rules.

Mr Hornby said: "I would like to thank Janet Kidd, Tony Jennings and Sue Buckingham, as well as Shopmobility Leeds for their support in making this happen.

"We’re committed to giving everyone the confidence to travel by train and have a conductor on board every service to provide assistance.

"We will continue to create further improvements and it is really pleasing to see satisfaction levels amongst customers with accessibility needs continue to increase."

More than 3,000 passengers, using around 330 different types of mobility scooters, have already received a free permit from Northern.

These passengers can travel from 262 stations across the network.

Northern is also preparing for its largest-ever fleet investment, with plans to purchase up to 450 new trains in three phases, including around 130 in the first phase.

The operator aims to award contracts by early 2027, with the first trains expected to be delivered in 2030.

A key focus of the investment is improving level boarding by ensuring new carriages meet requirements at platforms that conform to the network standard.

Northern is the UK’s second-largest train operator, running 2,650 services a day to more than 500 stations across the North of England.
