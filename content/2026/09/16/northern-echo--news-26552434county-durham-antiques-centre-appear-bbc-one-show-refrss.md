A County Durham antiques centre will appear on BBC One this week after being chosen as a filming location for Antiques Road Trip.

The episode features Durham Antiques Cooperative at The Old Curio in Sherburn Hill - and airs on BBC One on Thursday afternoon (September 17) this week.

Owners Nikki and Andrew Noble Gregory were delighted when the production team got in touch, especially as the business had been open for only eight months.

Nikki said: "It was genuinely exciting because the call came completely out of the blue.

"We were contacted to ask whether we had any availability for filming during the May Bank Holiday week, and I was amazed that they had even found us.

"Durham Antiques Cooperative had only officially been open for around eight months, but the team had discovered the centre through our social media."

The timing worked perfectly, with filming taking place on a day when the venue was closed to the public.

Nikki wanted the centre’s resident dealers, Ian and Stuart, to take centre stage instead of the owners.

"They are the people who bring the centre to life every day, and I felt it would give a much more authentic experience," she said.

The crew arrived early and were welcomed with breakfast sandwiches, tea and coffee from the site’s Carthouse Café.

   

*(Image: DURHAM ANTIQUES COOPERATIVE)*

Nikki said the production team were impressed by the historic former cooperative building, which dates back to 1910.

Its Edwardian shopping arcade, large windows, natural light and glazed sky dome provided an eye-catching filming backdrop.

   

*(Image: DURHAM ANTIQUES COOPERATIVE)*

Antiques experts Natasha Raskin Sharp and David Harper also made a strong impression on the team.

"When TV antiques experts Natasha Raskin Sharp and David Harper arrived, they couldn't have been lovelier," Nikki said.

"They were incredibly warm and immediately put everyone at ease."

Harper was especially complimentary, saying the building was ideal for showcasing antiques and housing independent dealers.

The four-hour shoot featured resident dealers Ian Denning of Durham Shabby and Stuart Robinson of H & K Vintage and Retro.

Both were nervous about appearing on television, but Nikki said the presenters quickly put them at ease.

"I think both Ian and Stuart were a little nervous at first, understandably, but Natasha and David are veteran dealers and presenters and very quickly put them at ease," she said.

"Before long everyone seemed to be having a great time, and there was plenty of laughter throughout the day."

After filming, the team had lunch before heading into Durham to explore the history of Durham Mustard for another segment.

For Nikki, the experience meant more than appearing on television.

"We were still such a new antiques centre, so simply being found and chosen was an achievement in itself," she said.

"But more than that, it gave us an opportunity to represent Durham, showcase our fantastic resident dealers and put Sherburn Hill and this wonderful old cooperative building in front of a much wider audience.

"After more than a century of history within these walls, it was lovely to see the old building getting another opportunity to shine."

The Old Curio brings together three businesses under one roof: The Durham Antiques Cooperative, home to around 20 independent antique dealers; The Carthouse Café; and The Vintage Sofa Warehouse, which specialises in new and vintage furniture.

Antiques Road Trip follows experts travelling around the UK in search of treasures while competing to make the most money at auction.

The episode airs on BBC One at 4.30pm on Thursday.

The couple established The Old Curio around six and a half years ago, initially selling vintage sofas and chairs before expanding into antiques and a café.
