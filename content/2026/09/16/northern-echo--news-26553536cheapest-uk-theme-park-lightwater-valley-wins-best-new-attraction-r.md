A North Yorkshire theme park named among the UK's cheapest has won gold for its newest attraction.

Lightwater Valley, near Ripon, has won Gold for Best New Attraction at the UK Theme Park Awards, marking a significant milestone for the park as it continues its revival under new ownership.

The family attraction, which was recently ranked among the cheapest theme parks in the UK, announced the achievement on social media, describing it as its first award in a long time.

In a post, the park said: “Putting Lightwater Valley back on the map.

“We're hugely delighted to see Lightwater Valley take home gold for Best New Attraction at the UK Theme Park Awards.

“This is Lightwater Valley's first award in a long time, and it feels like a significant moment for everyone involved.”

The award comes after a new attraction called the Lightwater Wheel opened in July.

It offers theme park fans the chance to ride around a big wheel while getting the best sky-high views of the park.

Lightwater Valley's new owners previously outlined ambitions to restore the attraction’s reputation and establish it once again as a major destination for families across Yorkshire and the North East.

The theme park said the award reflected the progress made since those plans were unveiled.

The post continued: “It's been a huge year of investment, growth and development, with an ambitious vision for the future of the park - and this recognition shows the progress being made.

  Lightwater Valley is a firm favourite amongst families


“A massive congratulations to the entire Lightwater Valley team. There's a lot more still to come.

“Lightwater Valley is firmly back on the map.”

The award was given for the park's new attractions introduced as part of its ongoing redevelopment programme.

The Ripon attraction was previously ranked among the best-value parks in the country, with tickets available from around £25 and free parking helping to secure its place near the top of the national rankings.

The park has changed dramatically in recent years.

Once home to The Ultimate, which held the record as the world's longest rollercoaster when it opened in 1991, the attraction has increasingly focused on younger families while continuing to invest in new rides and experiences.

Following the park's takeover earlier this year, Mellors Group managing director James Mellors said the aim was to put the attraction “back on the map” through new rides, fresh investment and an expanded offering for visitors of all ages.
