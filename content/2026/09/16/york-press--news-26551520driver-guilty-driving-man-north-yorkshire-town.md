A DRIVER drove his car onto the pavement and pinned a pedestrian against a wall in a road rage incident, York Crown Court heard.

Anne Richardson, prosecuting, said Danny Charlotte, 55, was steering his car through Selby town centre on October 14 last year when a pedestrian crossed the road in front of him and the two exchanged words.

Charlotte stopped the car at a set of traffic lights and the pedestrian asked him what he was doing.

Charlotte swore at him and the pedestrian spat at him through the open car window and walked away.

Charlotte then drove onto the pavement at the pedestrian and pinned him against the exterior wall of an estate agent's.

"It was a deliberate decision to drive up the pavement," said Ms Richardson.

The pedestrian suffered pain in his leg and a headache and was off work for five days but no bones were broken.

The estate agent's door was damaged.

Charlotte reversed his car and drove off, said the prosecution barrister.

When he'd been arrested, he told police he "had no intention of hitting" the pedestrian and was turning his car.

He claimed to be afraid of the pedestrian during the incident.

Charlotte, of Johnson Street, Selby, pleaded guilty to dangerous driving.

"You could so easily have caused him serious or very serious injury," Judge Tim Clayson told him.

"It was a moment of madness but very dangerous behaviour."

He also said the spitting could have acted as a provocation.

He gave him a 12-month prison sentence suspended for 18 months on condition Charlotte does 10 days' rehabilitative activities.

Charlotte was also banned from driving for 18 months and must take an extended driving test before driving alone again.

Defence barrister Lucy Brown said losing his driving licence would hit Charlotte hard because he has physical health problems.

He apologised for his actions.

She gave no further mitigation after the judge said he would suspend the prison sentence.
