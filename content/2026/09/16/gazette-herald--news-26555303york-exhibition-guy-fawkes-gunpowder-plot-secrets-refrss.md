A new exhibition in York will retell the story of the Gunpowder Plot.

The exhibition, entitled The Gunpowder Plot: York’s Untold Story, has opened at the Bar Convent Heritage Centre today, Wednesday, September 16.

  Dr Hannah Thomas with a 15th century processional cross. 

*(Image: Anthony Chappel-Ross)*

Visitors will be taken beyond the legend of Guy Fawkes, as the exhibition delves into the wider context of Catholic life in York at the time and explore the underground networks involved in the plot.

  Gunpowder Crucifix, late 16th- early 17th century, on display at the Bar Convent Heritage Centre. 

*(Image: Photography by Anthony Chappel-Ross)*

Rare artifacts are on display, including the Gunpowder Crucifix, the only surviving object from the raids on Catholic properties following the discovery of the plot.

This artifact belonged to Edward Oldcorne, a York-born priest who was hanged, drawn, and quartered for being a priest, despite his innocence.

  York exhibition of Guy Fawkes and Gunpowder Plot secrets features the Gunpowder Crucifix with ‘A Man For All Treasons' by Sarah Hall Baqai. 

*(Image: Photography by Anthony Chappel-Ross)*

Other items linked to Margaret Clitherow, a notable figure in York's Catholic history, will also be on show.

These include a 16th-century commemorative etching of her death, a 1650s handwritten biography, and a vestment that belonged to her relative, Father Robert Middleton.

  Margaret Clitherow's Biography, 17th century. 

*(Image: Photography by Anthony Chappel-Ross)*

The exhibition also highlights previously unexplored connections between Margaret Clitherow and James I, and between Margaret Clitherow and Guy Fawkes.

The exhibition is housed in the unique surroundings of a Catholic convent secretly established under new laws passed in reaction to the plot's discovery.

  Jessica Diamond, of York St John University, with a 17th century engraving of Saint Margaret Clitherow's death. 

*(Image: Submitted)*

Dr Hannah Thomas, the special collections manager at the Bar Convent, said: "Everyone has heard of Bonfire Night and Guy Fawkes and most will be familiar with the plot to blow up parliament on 5 November 1605, but here at the Bar Convent we have curated a Gunpowder Plot experience unlike any other.

"Guy Fawkes, Christopher and John Wright and Thomas Percy; of the eight plotters, these four men were from York or were connected to the city.

"What was happening in York at that time and what were the links between these men?

"This is the first exhibition of The Gunpowder Plot to explore the wider context of Catholic life in York and the underground networks at the time.

"It is truly fascinating to discover connections between Margaret Clitherow and James I, and between Margaret Clitherow and Guy Fawkes; these stories have never been fully examined before.

“Through our fascinating collections we are able to tell the story through rare original objects connected to real local people, and to present this in the unique surroundings of a Catholic convent secretly established under new laws passed in reaction to the Plot’s discovery.

“To enhance the exhibition, we have also designed a new self-led walking trail across the city to key locations and iconic landmarks that give context to the Plot and we will be hosting some special events for those looking to delve deeper.”

  Dr Isobel Staton with the 16th century etching of Margaret Clitherow's death. 

*(Image: Submitted)*

The exhibition will run until December 5.

  Sister Ann Stafford, Local Superior, added: "This exhibition is a testament to the courageous women who secretly founded this house in the wake of the plot, and the people who came before them.

  

  “With a fascinating exhibition, café, hidden chapel and even a guest house on site, the Bar Convent is the perfect place to discover how the events of 1605 left a mark on our city and shaped our community today."

The two-floor exhibition space has been transformed to tell York's story of the Gunpowder Plot, and visitors can explore a new self-led walking trail across the city to key locations and iconic landmarks that give context to the plot.

The exhibition is open Monday-Saturday, 10am-5pm, with the last admission at 4pm.

Admission applies.
