A County Durham women's centre says women and girls are carrying everyday items as improvised weapons to feel safer on public transport.

The Just for Women Centre in Stanley says this behaviour highlights a wider issue of fear and anxiety affecting how women travel, particularly at night.

Women supported by the centre report changing routes, avoiding buses altogether, or altering their clothing to reduce unwanted attention.

Debbie Rogan, who has worked at the centre for thirteen years, says her daughter rings her on public transport so she doesn't feel alone.

  The centre in Stanley. 

*(Image: JUST FOR WOMEN CENTRE)*

She said: "One of my daughters has to travel on two buses to get home.

"She will sit on FaceTime with me for the hour or two it takes her to get home because she doesn't feel safe.

"When I've spoken to people here at the centre, they felt the same.

"They have perfumes or deodorants in their bags ready, or keys between their fingers, anything to keep themselves protected.

The group are now calling for better lighting, increased staff presence and safer waiting areas for public transport.

Linda Kirk, founder of the centre, said: "Women and girls should not have to carry hairspray, perfume or keys between their fingers just to feel safe getting from A to B.

"When everyday items become ‘protection,’ it tells us something is deeply wrong.

"At the centre, we hear these stories every day and they are not exaggerations, they are lived realities.

"It is 2026 and safety should not be a personal burden carried by women alone.

"We need transport systems that respect women, protect women and allow them to travel without fear."

Next month, the group will be going to Parliament as part of their Young Girls' Project.

The young women who are part of the project will meet with their MP, Luke Akehurst, and will be given the opportunity to share their experiences, ask questions, and represent their community directly.

Linda said: "Everyone says the same thing, they feel nervous and anxious using public transport.

"It's outrageous that women are having to weaponise themselves.

"This is something that needs to be addressed."

Linda is a former social worker and set up the Just for Women Centre in 2011, after selling her coffee shop business.

The founder said: I thought for many years that we needed a women's centre in Stanley.

"We've done an awful lot of work - we've been part of policies that have been put through Parliament.

"We've got a fantastic team, a good counselling service.

"We fight to keep these doors open, it's not been easy.

"We've helped thousands of women and young girls."

The centre works with women and girls to build confidence, provide trauma‑informed support, and holds regular workshops.

Rachael Smith, Durham County Council’s section manager for transport and infrastructure, said the council is bringing in changes to improve safety.

She said: “As a White Ribbon accredited organisation, we take our role in providing safety for women and girls seriously. No one should have to take extra measures to feel safe, and we do everything we can to make sure steps are taken to help women feel secure.

“In partnership with the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority (MSA), we are bringing a raft of improvements to bus stations and bus stops across the county to improve accessibility and safety as part of the Bus Service Improvement Plan. This includes improved lighting, accessibility and new CCTV systems in places like Stanley Bus Station and Dalton Park.

“Many of these upgrades are already complete and we will continue to monitor relevant locations, and work with partners like North East MSA and Arriva to identify any sites that may need further safety improvements. Any future funding support from the MSA for these would, of course, be welcomed.

“The MSA has also just completed a report compiled with the help of women and girls in the region on how we can all work to make travel safer, and we’ll be using this when making future plans.

“We’d encourage anyone who feels they are in immediate danger, or who witnesses violence against women and girls to call 999 immediately.”

Go North East, which provides bus services to Stanley, spoke out following concerns.

“I also want to reassure passengers that our buses are generally safe places to travel and incidents of violence onboard are rare.”
