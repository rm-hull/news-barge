A RESTAURANT on a major road connecting Bradford with Halifax has shut - with its owner citing ongoing challenges facing the hospitality industry as the reason behind the closure.

Nana’s Social Dining, on Bradford Road, closed for good on Sunday.

The closure marks the end of an era for the Panesar family, with Raj, his wife Kay and daughter Sunny Pointon having been a familiar part of Northowram's hospitality scene for almost two decades.

The Indian tapas restaurant broke the news in an emotional social media message to customers.

  Raj and Kay Panesar at Nana's with Matthew Wolfenden from Emmerdale. 

*(Image: Raj Panesar)*

## Family decides to sell

The restaurant said it had made the “incredibly difficult decision” to sell Nana’s Restaurant after two years.

“We are incredibly grateful to every single person who walked through our doors and helped make Nana’s what it became.

“Thank you for believing in us, supporting us and becoming part of the Nana’s family.”

  Nana's Social Dining 

*(Image: Raj Panesar)*

## 'No relief from the government'

Nana's was inspired by Raj's grandfather, Naranjan Kalsi, whose dedication to helping others left a lasting impression on the family.

The restaurant aimed to stand out with Indian tapas-style dishes.

While the concept was initially well received, Raj said the challenges facing the hospitality sector had become increasingly difficult.

“The hospitality business at the moment is super hard,” he said.

“After a fairly good start when we first opened two years ago, we tried to do something quite unique and create an Indian tapas-style menu. Maybe it's not for everybody.”

Raj pointed to rising costs and wider economic pressures as key factors behind the decision.

He said: “We're getting no relief from the government.

“The VAT is still extortionate compared to other countries around Europe.

"Taxes went sky high, national insurance went through the roof and the wars in Ukraine and Iran have had a massive effect on products, with gas and electricity costs going through the roof.”

The Prime Minister is expected to host a series of events in No 10 aimed at pitching his Government as a “partner for growth” that can offer a stable platform for investment in the UK.

Reviving Britain’s high streets has been a key focus for Mr Burnham since he took over, promising to give councils new powers to block vape and betting shops and pledging to cut business rates for pubs.

## The future of the site

Raj said the decision was also influenced by a desire to step back and enjoy life more at the age of 66.

But he is not leaving the restaurant trade altogether.

He will continue running 22 The Square in Crosshills and a pizza takeaway in Birkenshaw.

The site will also remain open under a new tenant, who has already taken over the lease.

## 'It’s been an absolute pleasure'

In a final message to the community, Raj said: “Thank you to everybody who supported us for 20 years. It’s been an absolute pleasure.

“We’ve made lots of memories, lots of friends. I’ve seen babies born. I’ve seen relationships grow, I’ve seen relationships break up.

“I’ve seen marriages, I’ve seen proposals.

"We employed a lot of local people and we're very much about investing locally.

"If I can say one thing to the community, it's this: invest locally. Use your local amenities and services, because that's the only way people will survive.”
