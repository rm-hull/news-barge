The York Food & Drink Festival is returning for 10 days of food, drink, demonstrations, tastings, trails and entertainment.

The popular annual festival will fill York’s appetite from **Friday 18 to Sunday 27 September**.

With hundreds of things to see, taste and do, this year’s festival combines the much-loved market and street food offer in Parliament Street, with an expanded programme of cookery demonstrations, workshops, tastings, tours, family activities and evening events – and a new venue!

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/yff-Y-Produce.webp)

A major new addition for 2026 is the St Sampson’s Centre, which will become a new hub for the festival’s demonstration and evening programme. A dedicated theatre will host free daily cookery demonstrations throughout the festival, alongside workshops, tastings and special events.

“The programme is really about giving people more reasons to come into the city and discover something new,” said festival director, Michael Hjort.

“We’ve got some fantastic chefs and food producers involved, but also a huge range of activities where people can get involved themselves. The new St Sampson’s Centre gives us the opportunity to develop this side of the festival considerably.”

The 2026 demonstration programme features an exciting line-up of chefs and food personalities.

- *Great British Bake Off* star**Freya Cox** returns to the festival stage on Saturday 19 September, bringing her celebrated vegan cooking. T
- he new St Sampson’s Centre also hosts MasterChef 2025 personalities **Claire Syrenne and Darren Gurry** .
- UK Pastry Champion **Florian Poirot** returns to the festival stage with his patisserie skills lessons.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/yff-Guildhall-Dinner.webp)

Other highlights also include:

- The festival’s popular **Taste Trails** return from 18–27 September, offering visitors a self-guided food tour around the city. A dedicated**Vegan Taste Trail** will also offer around 20 stops featuring vegan samples and alternatives.
- A **Medieval Feast** at Barley Hall
- A **Gascony four-course menu** at The Chopping Block
- **Mead Cocktails and Sagas** with Horns of Odin, combining Viking-inspired storytelling with mead cocktails.
- **Great Yorkshire Tasting** at St Sampson’s Centre, bringing together Yorkshire wines, beers and spirits and giving visitors the opportunity to meet the producers.
- **Assize of Ale** returns on Saturday 26 September – starting at York Mansion House, participants will visit city pubs before finishing at the festival, with the traditional event followed by a fish and chip supper and ale.
- York Cocoa Works will explore the history of drinking chocolate and offer a very different **“NOT Chocolate” tasting** , showcasing ways of using cocoa pod and plant materials beyond chocolate itself.
- Yahala Mataam will host a hands-on **Syrian cooking session** , where participants can make falafel, hummus and baklava before sharing a mezze-style meal together.
- **Live for St Leonard’s** , with live music across Parliament Street and St Sampson’s Square from 18–26 September.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/yff-live-for-st-leonards.webp)

The festival continues to centre on its free-to-access public programme, with the food market running daily from 10am–5pm alongside festival bars and evening entertainment.

The 2026 programme brings together York’s independent food businesses, Yorkshire producers, chefs, community organisations and cultural venues to create a festival that is as much about discovering York as it is about eating.

To see the full programme, including dates, times, venues and ticket information, head to the York Food Festival.
