A Bishop Auckland College student once feared unlikely to survive at birth is celebrating landing her dream job, inspired by the support she received as a child.

Erin McMenamin, 20, from Newton Aycliffe, was born at just 24 weeks and weighed 1lb 7oz.

She faced significant developmental delays and spent four months in hospital, but has now secured a permanent role as an enhanced learning support assistant at Walworth Primary School in Newton Aycliffe.

She said: "Having needed extra support in primary and secondary, I think that's why I love working in specialist schools.

"I wasn't in one myself, but working with children with complex needs is easier for me to understand because of my own early years."

Her mother, Anna, said: "Erin is an absolute superstar and I am so proud of her.

"She had lovely support back in the day from teaching assistants, and that has probably, in some way, played a part in why she has wanted to go down this path for a career."

Erin’s early life was a fight for survival.

She was born 16 weeks premature alongside her twin sister Rachel, who tragically died just nine hours after birth at James Cook University Hospital in Middlesbrough.

Erin survived but suffered major complications affecting her heart, lungs, veins, stomach and bowel.

Her mother recalled the "worrying length of time" Erin spent on a ventilator and feared she might die.

Doctors warned that Erin might need long-term breathing support if she survived.

Anna said: "We were warned that (if she survived) it would be very likely that she would need breathing support at home, however, in the style of Erin, I walked into the hospital room one day during her time at Leeds and the staff had huge grins on their faces.

"She was off the ventilator and I, for the first time after months of her being born, heard her little cry.

"Erin has since surprised us many times with her amazing resilience and determination."

Developmental delays meant Erin struggled to sit, stand and walk, but she defied the odds and now inspires others.

After making local headlines 20 years ago, Erin is now using her experience to support children facing similar challenges.

Erin said: "I did a placement at Walworth Primary during the last academic year, and before we broke up for summer, jobs came up.

"I applied, got an interview and got the job.

"I have volunteered in mainstream schools before but I loved the challenge of this specialist side of things at a specialist SEMH school.

"The staff there are so lovely and inspiring to work with, and although it is challenging at times, I have loved that side of it, and find it particularly rewarding."

Erin studied at Carmel College in Darlington before enrolling at Bishop Auckland College to train in teaching support.

She completed her T-Level Teaching Assistant course with a high merit.

She said: "I knew I wanted to do it, but doing two full academic years on placement while at Bishop Auckland College has reaffirmed what I wanted to do."

Erin spent three days a week at college and two days on placement, and praised the college staff as "amazing."

Her success comes as Bishop Auckland College celebrates strong outcomes across its T-Level and BTEC programmes, with many students progressing into employment or further study.

Looking ahead, Erin said she has no plans to leave Walworth Primary any time soon.

She said: "I can't see myself wanting to leave Walworth. I love working there.

"In the future, I might like to take up teaching and come back to Bishop Auckland to study, because it is something I do love, particularly teaching the small classes even if I enjoy teaching the bigger classes too.

"I do like everything about it."
