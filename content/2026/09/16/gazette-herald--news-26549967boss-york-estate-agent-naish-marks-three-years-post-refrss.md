The boss of a York estate agent who said he "became an estate agent because I disliked estate agents" has celebrated three years in the role.

Matthew Hendry took over High Petergate-based Naish Estate Agents in August 2023 with what he called his "rather unusual motivation".

Under Matthew's leadership, Naish has marketed properties ranging from around £150,000 to more than £5 million and has completed the sale of a 36-apartment portfolio in Toft Green.

Naish has also picked up silverware with Matthew as managing director: named York's Best Estate Agent for Sales at the British Property Awards in both 2024 and 2025, before winning York's Best Agent for Lettings in 2026. Matthew was even named Estate Agency Managing Director of the Year at the 2026 SME Awards, while Naish received the Sales Integration Service Excellence Award.

  Matthew Hendry, managing director at Naish Estate Agents 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"I knew there was a huge opportunity, but I don't think I could have predicted everything that's happened," Matthew said. "We've won awards, grown the team, taken on some fantastic properties and built a really strong reputation.

"But the thing I'm probably proudest of is the team. It's easy to talk about growth and awards, but none of that happens without good people who genuinely care about what they're doing."

## 'I still believe there are things estate agents can do better'

Matthew admitted that there is an irony to his time at Naish - the man who entered the industry because he disliked estate agents has now spent three years building one.

But he said: "I still believe there are things estate agents can do better. That's what keeps me motivated.

"I don't want to just build a bigger estate agency. I want to build a better one."

Matthew said one of his plans as managing director included Naish's sales and lettings sides of the business, grown the team and introduced a more technology-led and personal approach to dealing with clients.

Matthew said communication is "fundamental" to providing the type of service he felt was missing from estate agents.

"I remember what it was like being a client," he said. "You're selling your home, or you're buying somewhere that's potentially going to be your home for the next 10 or 20 years, and sometimes you can feel like you're chasing people for information. We wanted to completely change that.

"If something happens, the client should know about it. If there's an update, they should know about it. It sounds simple, but I think it's incredibly important."

Looking ahead, Matthew wants Naish to retain its identity as an independent York business.

"We're still a local independent business," he said. "That matters.

"We know York, we know the market and we know our clients.

"Being independent means we can make decisions quickly and try new things without having to go through layers of corporate management."
