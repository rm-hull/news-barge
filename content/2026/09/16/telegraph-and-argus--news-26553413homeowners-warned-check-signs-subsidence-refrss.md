HOMEOWNERS are being urged to check their properties for signs of subsidence following this summer’s prolonged heatwave.

Experts from MyBuilder.com, a platform for hiring tradespeople, say that hot, dry conditions can cause the ground beneath homes to shrink and shift, putting foundations at risk.

The warning comes as subsidence payouts reached an estimated £307 million in 2025 following the UK's hottest summer on record.

Andy Simms, a home expert from MyBuilder.com, said: "When there has been such a lack of rain, moisture evaporates from the soil.

"Clay soils in particular can shrink, and foundations start to move and lose the support they need.

"As the ground moves in an uneven fashion, so does the foundation above it, causing cracks to appear."

Mr Simms advised homeowners to stay alert for tell-tale signs of subsidence, including diagonal cracks that are visible both inside and outside the property, sticking doors or windows, and cracks wider than 3mm.

He said: "Noticing it early on can prevent larger problems later down the line."

Homeowners are advised not to attempt DIY repairs and instead consult a structural engineer or chartered surveyor if they spot warning signs.

Cracks wider than about 5mm, those spreading quickly, or running diagonally should be treated as serious.
