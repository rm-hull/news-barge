A MUSEUM is offering the chance to adopt a piece of railway history as a unique Christmas gift.

The National Railway Museum in York has launched an adoption scheme, allowing supporters to symbolically adopt one of six historic railway objects from its collection for as little as £3 a month.

Options include the Shinkansen bullet train, the last-constructed British Railways steam locomotive Evening Star, and Queen Adelaide’s Saloon, the oldest surviving royal railway carriage in Britain.

Anthony Coulls, senior curator of rail transport and technology at the National Railway Museum, said: "We know people are looking for gifts that have a real story behind them.

"Whether you're buying for a railway enthusiast, a history lover or someone who's simply hard to buy for, adopting an object offers a unique way to connect with some of the most fascinating objects in our collection.

"Every adoption helps us care for these extraordinary pieces of railway history and share their stories with future generations."

Other options include Coppernob, a rare steam locomotive damaged during a German air raid, Diesel Railcar No. 4, and a model railway carriage linked to a 1901 murder case.

Donations raised through the Adopt an Object scheme will help conserve railway heritage, support learning programmes and ensure these extraordinary stories remain accessible for years to come.
