*60 Minutes* opened its 59th season Sunday with its smallest premiere audience in at least a quarter-century following CBS News Editor-in-Chief Bari Weiss’ overhaul of the flagship program.

About 7.9 million viewers watched the season premiere, down from roughly 10 million for last year’s opener, according to Nielsen data cited by *The New York Times*.

The decline was sharp among adults aged 25-54, a key advertising demographic. Preliminary Nielsen ratings, cited by *Mediaite,* showed viewership in that group fell 33 percent.

The episode — featuring an interview with an Air Force colonel shot down over Iran, a report on President Donald Trump’s pardons and a profile of a football coach — was the program’s least-watched season premiere since at least 2000.

“We are so proud of the exceptional journalism on display in our season premiere and we can't wait to bring our audience another amazing show this coming Sunday,” CBS News told *The Independent* in a statement. The network, citing Nielsen data, said Sunday’s season premiere was the week’s number one “non-sports primetime program” among adult viewers and that the show reached some 116.7 million video views across all social platforms.

![Norah O'Donnell during the first episode of the new season of 60 Minutes Sunday. The episode had about 7.9 million viewers, making it the least-watched premiere in decades, according to new data](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/16/11/59/Screenshot-2026-09-16-at-7-39-38-AM.png)

The lackluster ratings come after months of upheaval at *60 Minutes* and CBS News more broadly, following billionaire David Ellison’s acquisition of Paramount — CBS News’ parent company — and his appointment of Weiss as editor-in-chief last October.

Upon taking her leadership position, Weiss ousted several *60 Minutes* veteran correspondents and staffers. She has also been accused by critics of steering coverage in ways favorable to the Trump administration and Israel, claims she has emphatically denied.

“What’s being called ‘editorial interference’ is in reality the job description of an editor in chief,” Weiss told *The Wall Street Journal* in June.

Longtime correspondent Scott Pelley was fired in June after reportedly accusing Weiss of “murdering” the flagship news program. A month earlier, correspondent Sharon Alfonsi was dismissed after publicly clashing with Weiss over the decision to pull a segment on a brutal Latin American prison. Anderson Cooper also departed the program in May, citing the importance of editorial “independence” in his final address.

In July, CBS News announced a slate of new hires to fill the vacancies created by those departures.

“An exceptional lineup of journalists will be joining 60 Minutes, carrying on its tradition of fearless, independent and impartial reporting,” Nick Bilton, the show’s new executive producer, wrote in a July 28 memo shared on X. “I can't wait for them to get started.”

Bilton said five journalists would join the weekly broadcast for its 59th season. They include Ross Douthat, a conservative *New York Times* opinion columnist and podcaster, and Norah O’Donnell, a CBS News senior correspondent.

Ahead of Sunday’s season premiere, Weiss was given a “blank check” to promote the revamped program, according to *Puck News*.

“Sources familiar with the plans say that CBS will blanket the airwaves and major markets with a multipronged, cross-platform campaign to emphasize the show’s new look and feel and commitment to its 60-year legacy,” the outlet reported in August.

The *Times* cautioned that television ratings are influenced by myriad factors.*60 Minutes* typically follows NFL games, and both CBS and Fox aired games Sunday. Last year, by comparison, CBS had the Sunday NFL lineup to itself.

*60 Minutes* still remains the most popular news show on television. Last year, it garnered about 9.1 million viewers per episode, marking a 9 percent increase from the previous year.
