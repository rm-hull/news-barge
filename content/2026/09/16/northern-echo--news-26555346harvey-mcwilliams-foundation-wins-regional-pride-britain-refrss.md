A Darlington charity founded in memory of a football-mad teenager Harvey McWilliams who tragically died in 2021 has been named the North East's Charity of the Year.

The Harvey McWilliams Foundation has won the Pride of Britain Regional Award for North East Charity of the Year 2026, a recognition that founder Michael McWilliams says came as a "complete surprise".

Harvey, a doctor in the making and huge Sunderland football fan, tragically lost his life to suicide at the age of 18 in 2021 when he was studying at the University of Exeter.

Harvey regularly donated his old boots and kit to other young players who needed them.

  Harvey McWillams


Inspired by that generosity, his brother Luke came up with the idea of continuing Harvey's legacy by helping others access football equipment.

Since then, the foundation has grown into a major community project, providing football boots, kits and winter jackets to children and young adults across the North East who might otherwise struggle to afford them.

Co-founder and dad, Michael, said the recognition was emotional and unexpected.

He said: "To be honest, I was shocked when our name was announced.

"Winning an award like this was the last thing I expected.

"Being nominated was already a huge achievement for a small charity like ours, so to actually win it came as a real surprise.

"Of course it is lovely to receive recognition, but what means the most is that it shines a light on what Harvey used to do and what we are continuing to do in his name.

"It shows that the idea of helping people through football is making a difference."

  Harvey McWillams


The charity has expanded far beyond Darlington since it was created and now supports families across County Durham, Teesside, Newcastle and North Tyneside.

Michael said the foundation has already distributed more than 5,400 pairs of football boots this year alone and helped nearly 3,000 families.

He said: "It goes to show that what we're doing is working.

"But it has always been about more than boots.

"People see a pair of football boots, but what we see is confidence, friendship and opportunity.

"Children are making new friends, parents are meeting people, communities are coming together and people are feeling included.

"That is what gives us so much comfort."

He added: "We all know the cost of living is difficult and lots of families are facing challenges.

"If we can remove one barrier and help a child take part in sport, then that can have a huge impact on their life."

  Michael and son Luke


For Michael, the award is especially meaningful because it carries Harvey's name.

He said: "It's hard to put into words what it means.

"It is a beautiful thing because every time people see the name Harvey McWilliams they are seeing the story of a young man who believed everyone should have the chance to play football, no matter their background.

"Harvey had a big heart and he was always giving things away to help other people.

"Seeing his name recognised across the North East gives us a huge amount of comfort.

"We are very proud because we know that wherever Harvey's name is, we are helping somebody in that community.

"We know we are supporting individuals and families and continuing his legacy."

The award win now means Michael will travel to London next month, where all of the regional winners will attend the national Pride of Britain ceremony and walk the red carpet.

Looking ahead, the foundation hopes to expand its work even further by placing football boots directly into schools and community venues across the region.

  A boot cleaning event from the Harvey McWilliams Foundation at Arthur Wharton Foundation in Darlington


"Our mission is to help as many children and young adults as possible," he said.

"We want to keep growing and reach people who may not be able to travel to us.

"We've already trialled boot libraries in schools and we'd love to see those rolled out across the North East."

"We also want to work with more community centres and local organisations so equipment is available where people need it most.

"We've got lots of ambitions for the future, but the most important thing is continuing to help people in Harvey's name.

"That's what this has always been about."
