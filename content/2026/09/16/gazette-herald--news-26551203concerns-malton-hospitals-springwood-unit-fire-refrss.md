CONCERNS have been raised about the possible permanent loss of dementia services in a North Yorkshire town following a hospital fire.

The Springwood unit at Malton Hospital, which provided care for older patients with complex needs including dementia, was destroyed in a blaze in July.

The fire, believed to have been started by a solar panel in the roof, gutted the 14-bed facility.

Thirsk and Malton MP Kevin Hollinrake said he has now written to the Tees, Esk and Wear Valley NHS Trust (TEWV) which runs the unit twice to ask if they will be rebuilding it.

Mr Hollinrake said: "They have not confirmed whether they will or not.

"And they said they are basically going to go to consultation on it, whether basically services might be better placed at York or Scarborough.

"I’m always worried about that because all the services end up getting centralised in those places.

"It’s less convenient, it’s not as good for local people, particularly when they visit their relatives, indeed for the relatives themselves."

Demolition work began last month after engineers said areas affected by fire were structurally unsafe.

Mr Hollinrake described the unit as an "excellent" facility and encouraged constituents to share their views during the upcoming public consultation, which is being organised by Tees, Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust.

Mayor of Norton on Derwent, Cllr Di Keal said: "It is essential that the Springwood Centre is rebuilt in Malton.

TEWV has a responsibility to the residents of Ryedale to rebuild and reopen this well used and highly respected facility.

"There is a real need to provide services for older people living with complex mental health disorders close to the communities where they live, rather than having to travel to Scarborough, York or further afield. Retaining these local services puts much less pressure on the patients and makes it easier for friends and family to visit regularly.

"Obviously the building was virtually destroyed by the devastating fire in July, but the time has now come for the Trust to be honest with the public and make it clear if they intend to rebuild Springwood, which I absolutely believe they should do."

In 2013, a campaign led by Cllr Lindsay Burr and backed by the Gazette & Herald, helped overturn plans to reduce services at Malton Hospital.

The campaign called for services to be expanded at Malton Hospital not reduced, with Cllr Burr saying: "We must never let our hospital be under threat again.”

Cllr Burr, who now represents Malton on North Yorkshire Council, said she had reached out to officials straight after the fire.

"After my conversations I was very, very concerned, I was given no confidence in future services

"We may not see these services resumed which is yet another blow to the area.

"Facilities are closing left right and centre before our eyes without locals having any say what so ever it’s just not good enough.

"I’ll fight to keep any facility open and not stop."

A spokesperson for the trust said: "A detailed assessment by structural engineering specialists has advised that the areas affected by the fire are structurally unsafe.

"While part of the building looked unaffected from the front, the structure has been badly damaged and is no longer safe.

"Following advice from structural engineers and our insurers, the whole building needed to be demolished.

"We recognise that this is really sad news.

"Springwood has been a valued part of the community for many years, supporting people and families across the area.

"We know the ward holds a special place in the hearts of many former patients, carers, staff and local residents and once again, we would like to take this opportunity to thank everyone who supported the response to the fire."

The Trust said that it continued to support an increasing number of people to receive care in their communities.

"Going forward, we plan to continue to develop more community-based support, to ensure care rightly remains local, in the place a patient calls home.

"If inpatient care is needed, this can be provided through our assessment and treatment wards across North Yorkshire and York."

The trust said staff from the Springwood unit had been redeployed to other wards "across North Yorkshire and York."

They said consultation would form part of a process to understand local need and commissioner support, in terms of developing further community-based support.
