A much-loved independent North East bookshop has been awarded £1,000 by HarperCollins after being singled out for praise by a bestselling author.

Drake – The Bookshop in Stockton received the donation through HarperCollins' national scheme, The Bookshop I’d Like to Thank, which celebrates the impact of independent booksellers across the UK.

The shop was personally nominated by Adele Parks, author of Our Beautiful Mess and Eyes on You, who grew up nearby.

Richard Drake, co-owner of Drake – The Bookshop, said: "We are so thrilled to have been in the forefront of Adele's mind and were blown away when we got the phone call telling us of this news.

"The fact that Adele has chosen the bookshop from where she grew up speaks volumes about her love of Teesside and just how much people from the area value the bookshop.

"As someone once said about us, 'the most magical little bookshop... a little piece of heaven in what can be a crazy world.'

"Adele's fans love it when she comes home to do events with them and we are thrilled to be able to support her in our own little way.

"We are looking forward to being able to put together an event with local schools that champions another Teesside author and allows children to see where they are from in a book, possibly creating more best-selling authors of the future."

The £1,000 award is one of 21 given to independent bookshops across the UK, each selected by a HarperCollins author as part of the National Year of Reading.

Ms Parks said: "There are so many incredible independent bookshops and I’m often impressed by the owners’ creativity and tenacity but Drake’s is a standout and has a very special place in my heart.

"I grew up near Stockton and it was the town I went to buy my books, from a second-hand market stall or later WHSmith.

"There wasn’t much choice and no curation.

"Teesside is an economically and socially challenged area; when I first heard that someone was bravely opening up an independent bookshop in Stockton, my first thought was 'they’re brave,' and my second thought 'it won’t work.'

"I have never been more thrilled to be proven wrong.

"In particularly trying circumstances Drake's have answered the thirst of the Teessiders who do want a community, do want to be entertained, learn, empathise and escape through books.

"My sister teaches at a local primary school and she waxes lyrical about Mel and Richard’s support of the reading curriculum and libraries at her school and many others.

"They arrange countless events bringing authors to children who really need and want their horizons broadened but so many others write off or neglect.

"Stockton is as far from chi-chi as imaginable and many indie book shops thrive in prosperous areas; it takes incredible effort, passion and commitment to thrive in areas that are less fortunate.

"The shop itself is so welcoming, an oasis.

"I genuinely believe Drake’s are changing lives and giving opportunities, not simply selling books."

HarperCollins’ scheme aims to support independent booksellers during the National Year of Reading, the UK-wide campaign encouraging the nation to read more in 2026.

The funds can be used by the winning shops in any way they choose to help grow their business and reach more readers.

Tom Dunstan, sales director at HarperCollins UK, said: "Independent bookshops take risks on books, build relationships with readers, and champion authors in ways that simply can't be replicated anywhere else.

"'The Bookshop I'd Like to Thank' is our way of saying that HarperCollins hugely appreciates and values what they do, and what better time to do it than in the National Year of Reading.

"We're enormously grateful to all our authors who took part so enthusiastically – the warmth and passion in their nominations has been remarkable."
