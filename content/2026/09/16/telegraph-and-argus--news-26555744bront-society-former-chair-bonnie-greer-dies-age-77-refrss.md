A FORMER chair of the Brontë Society has died at the age of 77, her husband has confirmed.

David Hutchins announced that playwright and novelist Bonnie Greer passed away.

She played a significant role in the modern history of the Brontë Society, serving as its president from 2011 until 2015.

## 'Extraordinary ability to bring people together'

Mr Hutchins said: “It is with profound sadness that I announce that my beloved wife, Bonnie Greer, has died aged 77 after a brief illness.

“Bonnie was an acclaimed playwright, author, critic, cultural and political commentator whose intelligence, courage and distinctive voice made an enduring contribution to British cultural and public life.

“To me, she was my partner, confidante and inspiration, and I feel profoundly privileged to have shared my life with her.

“Those closest to Bonnie will remember her warmth, humour, generosity and extraordinary ability to bring people together.

“At this immensely difficult time, we ask that our family’s privacy be respected. She will be deeply missed.”

## Who was Bonnie Greer?

Greer was born in Chicago and lived in New York City before moving to Britain in 1986.

She became a citizen in 1997.

She was awarded the Verity Bargate Award for best play for Munda Negra (1993) and she was also the author of the novels Hanging By Her Teeth (1994) and Entropy (2009).

In October 2009, her memoir and music history, Obama Music, was published.

Some of her other works include the biography Langston Hughes: The Value Of Contradiction (2011) and the play Marilyn And Ella (2008) about actress Marilyn Monroe and singer Ella Fitzgerald.

Greer was appointed to the British Museum’s board of trustees in 2005 and later served as deputy chairwoman.

She also served on the boards of the Royal Opera House and the London Film School and was appointed a fellow of the Royal Society of Literature in 2022.

Greer appeared as a panellist on Newsnight Review and Question Time and served as Chancellor of Kingston University in Kingston upon Thames, London.

She was appointed an OBE in 2010 for services to the arts.

## Role in the Brontë Society

A lifelong admirer of literature, Greer brought a fresh perspective to the organisation, which is dedicated to preserving the legacy of sisters Charlotte, Emily and Anne Brontë and operates the famous Brontë Parsonage Museum in Haworth.

During her presidency, Greer championed efforts to broaden the appeal of the Brontës’ work and attract more diverse audiences.

She argued that the Brontës belonged to the world rather than a narrow literary elite and wanted the society to connect with younger people and communities across Yorkshire, including Bradford’s large and varied population.

After several months of internal disagreements, she resigned as president in June 2015.
