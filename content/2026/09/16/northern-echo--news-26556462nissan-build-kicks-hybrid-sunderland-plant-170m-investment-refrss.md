Nissan will build its European Kicks hybrid in Sunderland, where it has made cars for 40 years, it has been revealed.

Nissan will invest £170m in its North East plant to build the compact crossover in Britain, after making it in Japan, Brazil and Mexico.

Nissan regional chairman Massimiliano Messina said: “This is fantastic news for our European region and demonstrates the agility and speed with which we can bring new models to new markets.”

  The Nissan plant in Sunderland 

*(Image: Nissan/PA Wire)*

“Kicks is a compact crossover, with the unique e-POWER hybrid drivetrain, exciting design and innovative technology.”

“Electrification with choice is what European customers want, and the introduction of Kicks will further demonstrate how Nissan has options for all.”

North East Mayor Kim McGuinness said: "I’m delighted that Nissan is building their Kicks model in Sunderland.”

"It’s a £170m vote of confidence in North East car making and the people who work in our industry.”

"We’ve backed our automotive sector in the electric transition and it is paying off with four models on the books at Nissan now.”

"This is good news for North East workers and North East jobs."

Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds said: “Nissan has been at the heart of British manufacturing for four decades, with Sunderland producing millions of vehicles that supported thousands of skilled jobs and helped establish the North East as a global automotive powerhouse.”

“The decision to build this new model in Sunderland is a huge vote of confidence in the UK’s manufacturing expertise and automotive future.”

The Sunderland-built Kicks uses an electric motor powered by a petrol engine that generates electricity and recharges the battery.

It will be the 10th model built at Nissan’s Sunderland plant since production began in 1986.

Wearside workers have built 12 million cars since the first Nissan Bluebird rolled off the line.

Mike Hawes, chief executive of the Society of Motor Manufacturers, welcomed the announcement, saying: “The UK sector has the fundamental strengths to secure growth.”

“But to unlock that potential, we must continue to strengthen our competitiveness – supporting a healthy market, tackling the high cost of business, especially energy and additional employment costs – and ensuring trade works for manufacturers.”

“Do this and yet more companies will choose to build the next generation of vehicles here in Britain.”

Paul Butler, chief executive of the North East Automotive Alliance, said: “The North East has established itself as one of Europe’s leading automotive regions, attracting significant investment in vehicle electrification, battery technology and advanced manufacturing.”

“Nissan’s continued commitment to Sunderland is not only a celebration of 40 years of success but also a clear statement that the region remains a competitive and attractive location for future automotive investment.”
