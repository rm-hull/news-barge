Residents fighting to save their County Durham homes from demolition have spoken of their relief after winning the first stage of their High Court battle with Durham County Council.

Save Our Homes in Horden campaigners have now won permission for a full judicial review, in which a judge will decide whether the planning decision was lawful.

Among them is Ray Bellingham, 68, who has spent his whole life living in his Third Street home with his sister, Moira.

Mr Bellingham said the court decision had brought a huge sense of relief after years of uncertainty.

  Third Street, Horden 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

He said: "It was very emotional to win the first hurdle because the thought of being forced out of our home is something you can't bear thinking about.

"This isn't just a house, this is our home and all we have ever known. There are so many treasured memories in this house and the very fact we could be forced out of it is just so scary."

While welcoming regeneration investment, Mr Bellingham said residents felt demolition should be a "last resort".

He said: "We want to stay in our house. We're not against refurbishment or renewing the area, but what we're trying to get across to people is that the area could be redeveloped without demolishing people's homes.

  Fifth Street, Horden. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"Demolition should be the last resort.

"We want to work with the council and see if these homes can be reworked and revitalised.

"These houses were the birth of Horden. There's a lot of history involved and it's such a shame. The council are literally wiping the history of Horden away."

The campaign’s latest victory comes less than a year after Durham County Council approved plans to demolish homes on Third and Fifth Streets despite fierce resident opposition.

Campaigners packed County Hall before the vote, carrying placards and urging councillors to reject the plans.

Keith Derby, press secretary for Save Our Homes in Horden, said last week's hearing at Leeds High Court was a day he will never forget.

Mr Derby, who has lived on Ninth Street for the past decade, was one of three campaigners who attended court on September 7.

He said: "People had told me, 'You're not going to beat the council's barristers'

"We went into the court feeling it was probably 50-50. We'd never been through anything like this before and we'd never been in a High Court before.

"When the judge ruled in our favour on both grounds and granted permission for a full substantive hearing, we were in total shock.

"We just felt like we'd won the lottery to be honest."

  Horden residents at Durham County Hall. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Mr Derby said the ruling had given residents fresh hope that their voices were finally being heard.

He said: "It was a momentous occasion because we're just ordinary residents taking on a local authority with top barristers.

"So many people said we wouldn't be able to do it, but so far the little people are winning.

"This isn't a battle against regeneration. It's about protecting homes and a community that people care deeply about. We believe there is another way forward."

Campaigners say many of the century-old homes could be restored rather than demolished.

He said: "There's nothing wrong with the vast majority of these houses. They've been here for more than 120 years.

"They're not falling down. They just need investment and care."

Durham County Council has invested £6.2 million in the Horden Masterplan, with another £4.5 million from the North East Combined Authority.

Following the High Court hearing, Cllr Joe Quinn, Durham County Council’s Cabinet member for planning, assets and investments, said: “We note the court’s decision that the claim for Judicial Review can proceed to a substantive hearing and are naturally disappointed that there will be further delay to what is a ‘once in a generation’ opportunity, and further expense for all parties.

He added: “The plans would improve quality of life for local people by ensuring a higher standard of housing, and tackling the high number of empty properties in the Numbered Streets owned by absentee landlords.”

Cllr Quinn said the council homes they want to deliver would be available for social rent, ensuring they would be affordable.

He added: “Our Masterplan for the community has been well received during public consultations in recent years. While we recognise there would be people directly affected by what we are proposing, we would help them access housing if they wish.
