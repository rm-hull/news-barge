A REFORM UK councillor’s suggestion that he is midway through setting up a community rangers scheme has met with criticism - with one politician branding it "dangerous vigilantism."

Wyke Councillor Ian Reed claims he is setting up the neighbourhood rangers scheme in direct partnership with Nottinghamshire Safer Streets (NSS), an initiative set up to tackle neighbourhood crime, anti-social behaviour (ASB), and violence against women and girls.

Nottinghamshire did benefit from a Home Office-funded Safer Streets programme – but that funding finished in 2024.

They have not done anything under the Safer Streets brand since then and were just one of a number of areas that received Safer Streets funding, so there will be similar schemes across the country.

Cllr Reed says he is concerned about women's safety, shop thefts, county lines and "kids roaming in gangs 'cos there's nothing else to do."

  A screengrab of an image shared by Cllr Reed appearing to show two AI rangers. 

*(Image: T&A)*

## He wants to 'take back our high street'

On social media he shared what appears to be an AI image of two neighbourhood rangers in paramilitary-style uniform, alongside accompanying text.

The Reform UK councillor, who says he is a former policeman himself, told the T&A the community policing initiative is designed to “take back our high street” and suggested that "local police are up for it".

Local political group leaders have voiced their concerns about the scheme.

Cllr Alun Griffiths, Leader of the Liberal Democrats Group on Bradford Council, said: “This proposal is at best badly thought-out populism and at worse dangerous vigilantism."

Cllr Rebecca Paulson, Leader of the Conservative Group on Bradford Council, said: “I’d always recommend Councillors build up a positive working relationship with your Neighbourhood Police Team to jointly work on key priorities.

"This would be a much better way forward than some sort of vigilante scheme."

  A screengrab of a social media post referring to the neighbourhood rangers by Cllr Reed. 

*(Image: T&A)*

## 'If someone goes rogue… that’s the last thing we want'

Cllr Reed claimed the NSS are currently “putting a package together” for a six-month pilot scheme in his ward, with potential to roll it out across Bradford - but confirmed the proposal was not Bradford Council run.

When the T&A queried how the proposal would work in practice, he assured that all of the volunteers would be "fully vetted" and "upstanding" members of the community - such as former police and military personnel.

At first, he said there would be just one paid coordinator and the rest would be volunteers, who would “carry body cameras and two-way radios."

He added: “If someone [a community volunteer] goes rogue… that’s the last thing we want”.

He explained that he was still “working out logistics with the police” but he claimed that anti-social behaviour (ASB) will be dealt with verbally by the police-trained volunteers, any crime will go straight through to police and anything advisory will go through to the area coordinator.

He praised the NSS, describing it as "extremely successful" and suggesting the "Nottingham guys carried handcuffs".

From the T&A's research, it does not appear to be the case that handcuffs were ever carried by the NSS.

  A screengrab of a social media post referring to the neighbourhood rangers by Cllr Reed. 

*(Image: T&A)*

## Plan is 'not clear'

Cllr Imran Khan, Leader of the Labour Group for Bradford Council said: “It’s not clear what the Reform councillor is talking about here with his new volunteer force and whether he’s consulted local police partners and other agencies before making his announcement.

“Residents I speak with would like to see the police, council and other local agencies well-funded and empowered to crack down on antisocial behaviour – not having separate volunteer vigilantes on the streets taking matters into their own hands.

“The right approach is to build a strong partnership on a local level with the support of the Mayor and national government, which is renewing public services.”

## 'The answer is not untrained volunteers'

Cllr Hawarun Hussain, Deputy Leader of the Green Group on Bradford Council, said: "Everyone wants safer streets, but the answer is not untrained volunteers patrolling dressed up like police officers.

"Community safety is serious work that belongs with the police, and individuals taking the law into your own hands undermines the very things that keep us all safe. If Reform are serious about cutting crime, we need proper neighbourhood policing and investment in the youth services that stop young people being dragged into trouble in the first place.

"Elected councillors have a responsibility to be honest with residents, not to intimidate them or promote schemes that have not been thought through."

The T&A contacted Cllr Reed for further clarification on the scheme, but he did not respond to the request for more information.
