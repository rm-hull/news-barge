A council facing a projected £5.4 million overspend by the end of this council year is nonetheless making progress to reduce costs, says a senior councillor.

Coun Mike Payne said the projected out-turn for 2026-27 was down on the overspend at the end of the last council year, 2025-26, and work to remedy the situation would continue at Calderdale Council.

Familiar pressures are giving the senior councillors problems as they try and balance the council’s books, with rising adults’ and children’s social care costs an issue for the Reform UK administration, as they were for their Labour predecessors.

Reform won control of the council in May’s local elections.

Coun Payne (Reform UK, Sowerby Bridge), who is Cabinet member for Corporate Services, Assets and Resources, said the council was facing pressures to meet demand on those statutory services.

But the predicted overspend, he said, was “down from the near £8 million left to us – it’s not acceptable to this Reform administration.”

Coun Payne said encouraging measures had been taken and the new Cabinet had acted quickly by acting on “financial oversights…challenging spending and robust recovery measures.”

In four months progress had been made in reducing the council’s debt and improving its management while protecting front line services, he said.

The measures taken in key areas and decision-making will “return Calderdale to a balanced financial position while delivering long-term financial stability,” said Coun Payne.

Leader of the Council, Coun Dan Sutherland (Reform UK, Illingworth and Mixenden) said: “Value for money is absolutely key.”

Cabinet was fully committed to bringing the overspend down and there had been a significant shift in a short space of time, he said, while adding “there is still a long way to go.”

Coun Sutherland said long-term measures would form part of the budget council process for 2027-28.

Councillors were told a projected overspend of £5.4 million is predicted by the end of 2026-27.

Noticeable variances are within the Adults and Well-being directorate, which is set to be £4,6 million overspent, while Children and Young People’s Services is expected to be £1 million overspent.

Some if this is offset by, for example, centrally controlled council spending which is set to underspend its budget by £500,000.

The report for the new administration, as Labour predecessor Cabinet members reported for two years, shows savings largely being met – but outstripped by the rising demand for the social care packages.

In recent years council reserves have been used to plug shortfalls but as each year goes by this option becomes more limited.
