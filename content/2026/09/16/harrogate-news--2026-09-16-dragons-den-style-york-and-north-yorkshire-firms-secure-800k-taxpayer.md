**YORK**— Micro-businesses and regional entrepreneurs across York and North Yorkshire have secured a combined £800,000 in public funding following a series of high-stakes, Dragons’ Den-style competitive pitches at West Offices.

The cash injections represent the first major wave of allocations from the “Great Ideas” scheme, a £2.5 million initiative tucked inside regional Mayor David Skaith’s wider Business Innovation Fund. Successful applicants walked away with individual grants of up to £60,000 each, targeted at backing artificial intelligence integration, recruitment tech, and export expansions.

The highly competitive funding panel—which was recently tracked by television crews for the BBC’s *One Show*—brings sharp scrutiny to how the Combined Authority intends to distribute its multimillion-pound investment and development pots to stimulate the regional economy.


**David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said:** With transformational grants and expert advice, we’re giving our businesses the tools they need to take their Great Ideas to the next level. Whether it’s about using AI to unlock productivity in factories or using sustainable thinking to help our farmers save water and money, we’re backing our businesses.



Among the highest-profile local winners was Belle Richardson, founder of York-based enterprise SealStop, who secured a maximum £60,000 grant to rapidly accelerate the commercial rollout of a new product to market.

The scheme is being delivered in partnership with private business support and funding specialist UMi. Regional analysts note that the deployment of public money via competitive pitch panels is increasingly becoming the preferred blueprint for devolved authorities looking to bypass traditional, slower local government bureaucratic channels.


**Simon Goon, Chief Technology and Investment Officer of UMi, added:** Support like this offers a great opportunity to help small and medium sized businesses overcome financial barriers and receive the specialist support required to innovate and grow. I am confident that going forward, it will help more exciting businesses to innovate and become a blueprint for others across the length and breadth of the UK.



The overarching Business Innovation Fund remains split across five core public programmes—including dedicated start-up support and international export grants—as the Combined Authority attempts to fulfil its mandate to attract external commercial investment to North Yorkshire post-devolution.
