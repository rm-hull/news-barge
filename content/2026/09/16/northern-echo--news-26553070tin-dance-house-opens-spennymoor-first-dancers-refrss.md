A County Durham arts charity has welcomed its first dancers to a new purpose-built centre after months of fundraising and preparation.

TIN Arts said TIN Dance House on Coulson Street, Spennymoor, is now open and hosting dancers for the first time.

TIN Arts said in a social media post: “Today (September 14), TIN Dance House officially welcomed its very first participants through the doors, and we couldn't be happier to finally see our new home come to life.

“After months of preparation, it feels incredibly special to see the space being used for exactly what it was created for - giving our participants a place to dance together and call their own.”

The opening marks a major milestone for the charity, which raised funds to turn a former Durham County Council building into a fully accessible centre where disabled and non-disabled dancers can train, create and perform together.

TIN Arts said the centre will remove barriers that have limited opportunities for disabled performers and provide a permanent base for its growing programmes.

TIN Arts was previously based at the Old School House in Framwellgate Moor.

The centre will provide high-quality studio space and strengthen links with local, regional and national arts organisations.

TIN Arts executive director Martin Wilson previously called the project transformational.

Mr Wilson said: “For too long, we've had a community that welcomes everyone, but a building that doesn't. The TIN Dance House will remove those barriers for good.

“This is about creating a home where every body belongs.”

The charity thanked its funders and supporters, including the Sir James Knott Trust, The Barbour Foundation, The Catherine Cookson Charitable Trust, Durham Freemasons, Durham County Council and the Better Youth Spaces Fund, delivered by Social Investment Business.

TIN Arts also thanked members of the public who donated through its “A Home for Every Body” campaign.

“This is just the beginning for TIN Dance House, and we're so excited for everything still to come as we continue to settle in, develop the building and make it our own,” TIN Arts said.

TIN Arts champions inclusive dance and the arts, bringing disabled and non-disabled artists together through performances, training and creative projects across the North East.

TIN Dance House gives the organisation its first dedicated permanent home and a base for future generations of dancers.

Its previous work included showcasing more than 1,000 performances and delivering 20,000 workshops to over 120,000 young dancers and their families.
