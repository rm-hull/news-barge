A North East NHS trust has been ranked among the best-performing organisations in the country, according to the latest national performance figures.

South Tyneside and Sunderland NHS Foundation Trust (STSFT) is now ranked 15th out of 134 trusts across England and is the third-best large acute trust, based on recently published data from NHS England’s National Oversight Framework (NOF).

The NOF evaluates NHS organisations on areas including access to services, patient safety, effectiveness, experience of care, finance, productivity, and innovation.

Alison King, director of performance at STSFT, said: "Providing high-quality care is our top priority, whether that’s through the treatment we offer in our hospitals or through our extensive community services.

"These results show that our teams are helping more people receive the care, treatment and support they need without unnecessary delays.

"All our teams work hard to make this possible and I want to thank them for everything they do."

The trust is now rated as Segment 1, the highest category in the framework, indicating strong performance and no need for additional support.

For the first quarter of the current financial year, STSFT achieved an overall average score of 1.88 out of 4.00, with lower scores reflecting better performance.

It was ranked first in England for eliminating waits of more than 52 weeks for planned hospital treatment or community care, ensuring community patients are seen within 18 weeks of referral, and the speed of setting up clinical trials.

The trust has also maintained a balanced financial position while improving patient care, and aspires to achieve Advanced Foundation Trust status—a new NHS designation recognising excellence in patient care, leadership, financial management, and partnership working.

STSFT’s performance aligns with the goals of the NHS 10 Year Health Plan, which includes expanding digital services, delivering more care closer to home, and supporting people to stay healthier for longer.

Efforts are underway to invest in new technology, enhance services in community settings, and empower patients to take earlier action to improve their health and wellbeing.
