Police live facial recognition is now live in Bradford city centre as one onlooker told the T&A he had seen a man in handcuffs sitting in the back of a police van.

West Yorkshire Police has begun using the technology in the area as part of a wider effort to improve public safety and identify individuals who may pose a risk - and today, it is being used right in the heart of Bradford.

## WYP say it's about locating and protecting people

Inspector Justin Adams of the Bradford City Neighbourhood Policing Team said: "The Live Facial Recognition Team have again supported City NPT not only in helping us locate wanted individuals but also in supporting our commitment to protecting vulnerable people.

"It has helped officers find a high-risk missing person quickly, ensuring they received the support and safeguarding they needed at the earliest possible opportunity.

"The technology also helps us identify those on orders who may cause harm, enabling officers to intervene, engage and take action to keep our communities safe.

"Local businesses and traders have broadly welcomed the use of LFR over the last six months, which is now supported by funding from the Knife Crime Concentration Fund."

The force says live facial recognition helps prevent and detect crime, protect vulnerable people and arrest those wanted on warrant or recalled to prison.

## How does it work?

Cameras on police vans capture live images of people in public spaces and compare them with a police watchlist.

If the system identifies a potential match, a trained officer reviews the alert before any action is taken.

Images of individuals who do not match the watchlist are deleted immediately.

West Yorkshire Police says LFR is typically deployed at large public events, in busy city and town centres identified as crime hotspots, and during intelligence-led policing operations where there is a clear operational need.

As we reported in August, since February 2026, live facial recognition camera vans have been deployed in Bradford 17 times, resulting in 29 arrests.

The system also flagged 21 individuals subject to Sexual Harm Prevention Orders, who were then checked for compliance.

## It is not without controversy

Privacy campaigners have raised concerns about the potential for increased public surveillance.

However, West Yorkshire Police says each deployment is authorised by a senior officer, proportionate to the operational need, and clearly signposted at the location.

The force emphasises that LFR is not intended for mass surveillance, and all non-matching images are deleted immediately.
