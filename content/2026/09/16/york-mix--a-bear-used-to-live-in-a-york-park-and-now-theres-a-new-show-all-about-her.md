A remarkable piece of York history is being brought back to life.

*Bear and the Band,* a new show combining storytelling, animation and live brass music, in a premiere performance at the Yorkshire Museum on**Saturday 24 October**.

And it’s inspired by a true story – there really was a bear living in the York Museum Gardens.

According to the York Museums Trust, when the gardens were set up in Victorian times, it included a “menagerie complete with a bear, monkeys and a golden eagle!

“This may have been contained inside the multangular tower.

“If that is so, it proved to be inadequate as the bear reportedly escaped on more than one occasion.

“In 1831 Professor John Phillips, first keeper of the museum, reached the final straw when the bear got loose and chased him around the gardens. Luckily he managed to reach an outhouse before things got more serious.”

That same year, the bear was sent to London – and she travelled there on top of a stagecoach.

When writer Martin Watts came across a letter from London Zoo to the Yorkshire Philosophical Society about the bear, it inspired him to write more adventures for the bear, including the book *Bear and the Angel*. 

Now, returning to the very gardens where the real bear once roamed, Martin’s story will be brought vividly to life through live storytelling, animated film by award-winning creative designer and filmmaker Andy Burns, and live music from Kirkbymoorside Town Brass Band.

“That letter from 1831 was the starting point,” said Martin. “It is such an extraordinary piece of York history that I couldn’t let go — the idea of a bear travelling to London on top of a stagecoach!

“From there, the story grew into an adventure about Bear, friendship, kindness and standing up to people who think they can have everything for themselves.

“To bring it to the Yorkshire Museum, within the Museum Gardens, is a very special moment. We are really looking forward to sharing the story in the place that inspired it.”

The combination of music and animation grew out of a collaboration with Sarah Woodward, director of Kirkbymoorside Town Brass Band, who originally approached Martin about putting music to a reading from one of his books.

Martin then invited Andy Burns to bring his illustrations to life through animation.

The result is an energetic, atmospheric and nail-biting adventure for all, but particularly “bears, children, fighters against injustice, and grown-ups who have not completely grown up.”
