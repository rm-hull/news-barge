THE challenge of any musical biopic is to allow the story to shine through whilst avoiding any temptation to introduce audience-pleasing cheap tricks - aka the finale mega mix!

And The Ballad of Johnny & June is a class-act that does just that.

I saw this musical for the first time just a few months ago but this time, was able to simply ‘watch’ the production again without the need to be scribbling notes and unravelling something new.

And what a joy it was second time around, pretty much as it was the first.

  Christina and Christopher shine on stage 

*(Image: Pamela Raith)*

Whether you love or loathe country music, there’s little denying that Christopher Ryan Grant and Christina Bianco as Johnny and June are a professional triumph in their respective roles as two of America’s greatest loved country stars, he as the twitching superstar who descends into drug-fuelled rages, and she as the eternally smiling, all American girl who eventually crumbles under the pressure of being married to a broken, singing icon.

And I have the greatest respect for anyone who not only reproduces the voice of a musical icon worshipped by millions, but also manages to deliver on that second strata of complexity: mannerisms and facial expressions, which Grant does to a tee.

  Ryan O'Donnell as John Carter Cash 

*(Image: Pamela Raith)*

Country and western isn’t my first genre of choice, however, the closer you get to this musical, the more you have to admire the directorial genius of two-time Tony award winner, Des McAnuff, who achieves much with just a handful of players and musicians.

Somehow, Johnny Cash’s life passes before you, narrated cleverly by his adult son, John Carter Cash, played by talented Halifax boy, Ryan O’Donnell. He really nailed the role of ‘Southern Boy’ and his accent never faltered.

Scenery is at a minimum, however, clever staging and Amanda Zieve’s lighting plot create moments that last seconds but convey the passing of time as though Johnny and June’s lives were on fast forward.

This show was everything Legend - The Music of Bob Marley, wasn’t, for that production unashamedly turned into a ‘concert’ in homage to reggae’s biggest star and, for that reason alone, left me disappointed. This musical was, by comparison, a triumph, achieving the necessary balance.

In between, of course, there are the songs: I Walk the Line, Ring of Fire, Jackson, A Boy Named Sue, Hurt and many more iconic tunes.

Taken together, you have a very clever, solid musical, supported by a great live band, including some of the actors, but driven by a strong storyline that keeps you involved beyond the music.

Grant and Bianco, both Americans, really are excellent, each with great theatrical pedigrees.

Overall, this was an excellent production, anchored by a raft of talented swing actors, and will appeal to different people for different reasons. On the one hand there’s the country music, however, beyond that, committed theatre goers will appreciate The Ballad of Johnny & June for its strong storyline, and quality direction.

A rootin’ tootin’ humdinger of a show! Recommended.

* The Ballad of Johnny & June is at Leeds Grand Theatre until Saturday, September 19.
