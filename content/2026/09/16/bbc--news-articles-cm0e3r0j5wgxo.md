![The headline on the front page of the Times reads: 'King: Grief has coloured earl's claims over Diana.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/809/cpsprodpb/4b0a/live/a498ad90-b216-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

Earl Spencer, the brother of Diana, Princess of Wales, has ignited a fresh row with the Palace following claims in his upcoming book, which feature heavily in today's papers. The book claims that King Charles told Spencer in the days after Diana's death in 1997: "Rest assured, we'll forget her soon enough", the Times reports. The paper headlines on a Buckingham Palace official statement that hit back at the book, saying grief can "colour memory".

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mail reads: '"Rest assured, we will forget her soon enough".'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/810/cpsprodpb/5008/live/24d59dc0-b216-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

The Daily Mail was the one that published the exclusive extract from Earl Spencer's upcoming book, Swan Song: Diana, My Sister, which it says "lifts the lid on an incendiary row with the now King over funeral arrangements". Spencer alleged the then-Prince of Wales subjected him to a "stream of anger" after he insisted Diana would not have wanted the young Princes William and Harry to walk in her funeral procession.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph reads: 'Palace hits back at Diana's brother.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1040/cpsprodpb/8fb6/live/8bfd4c10-b210-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

The Daily Telegraph also reports that Buckingham Palace has "hit back" at accusations made by Earl Spencer, which it describes as a "highly unusual move". In the official statement, the Palace said "pain of fraternal grief can cloud reason, affect judgment and colour memory in ways others do not recognise, even many years after such a loss," the paper reports.

![The headline on the front page of the Sun reads: 'Recollections may vary...again.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/3840/cpsprodpb/5d25/live/2cf98250-b216-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

"Recollections may vary...again", the Sun headlines after the "war of words with Prince Diana's brother". The paper points to the Queen's famous "recollections may vary" statement over claims made by Prince Harry and Meghan in 2021, and how they are echoed in the Palace's latest statement.

![The headline on the front page of the Independent reads: 'Palace on Spencer's Diana claims: "Grief can cloud reason".'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1536/cpsprodpb/4db6/live/67231e30-b218-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

Noah Woods, who disappeared on Tuesday, is pictured on the front page of the Independent after police confirm a body was found near where the toddler vanished.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror reads: 'Heartbreak.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/8761/live/53698080-b210-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

"Heartbreak", the Daily Mirror reports. Three-year-old Noah went missing from a playground in Brantham, Suffolk, after becoming separated from a relative. Police said a child's body had been found in a nearby pond.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Express reads: 'We are "paying the price" for Labour's economy failure.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/4907/live/58ca4d70-b210-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

Meanwhile, Tory leader Kemi Badenoch warns Britain is "paying the price" for Labour's economic failures after inflation rose to 3.1% with warnings it could top 4% next year, the Daily Express reports.

![The headline on the front page of the i Paper reads: 'Burnham warns of "challenging" Budget after 3.1% inflation.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/831/cpsprodpb/fb91/live/67a2a810-b210-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has said the government "won't take risks with people's living standards" after the 3.1% inflation news, the i Paper reports. According to economists, capital gains tax may be one of the most likely tax rises in the upcoming Budget next month, the paper says.

![The headline on the front page of the Metro reads: '"Putney pusher" suspect dead.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/828/cpsprodpb/67a7/live/b7b72510-b210-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

Nine years after the "Putney Pusher" mystery began - when a jogger pushed a woman into the path of a bus during a run on Putney Bridge - the suspect of the incident has been found dead, the Metro reports. Nicholas Brandram was arrested in June this year on suspicion of attempted grievous bodily harm. Last week he was released under investigation.

![The headline on the front page of the Daily Star reads: 'Face of Putney Pusher Suspect.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/ce81/live/b7b25b60-b216-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

The "face of Putney Pusher Suspect" is the Daily Star's lead image after the 44-year-old banker was found dead at his home. The paper reports that the man was a direct descendent of Queen Victoria.

![The headline on the front page of the Financial Times reads: 'Fed raises rates despite Trump pressure in bid to tame prices.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1056/cpsprodpb/e145/live/a68b1670-b210-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

US interest rates have been raised for the first time in more than three years, the Financial Times reports, despite US President Donald Trump's "calls for lowering borrowing costs".

![The headline on the front page of the Guardian reads: 'Prisoners to "double up" in single prefab jail cell units.'](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/648b/live/4bf586d0-b217-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

And the Guardian says the prison service in the UK has drawn up plans to "double up" hundreds of prisoners in cells built for one person, according to leaked documents.

The new allegations made in Earl Spencer's book are on several front pages. The Daily Mirror , externaldescribes as "shocking" his claim that within days of Princess Diana's death the-then Prince Charles told him that she would be forgotten soon enough.

According to the Daily Telegraph, external a source close to the King says the alleged comment was "just not in his nature" especially not at that time when he was "absolutely grief-stricken".

The Palace's rejection of the claims is also on the Sun's , externalfront page, under the headline "Recollections may vary..... again."

The Times, external says the row between Earl Spencer and the King will be seen as a further crisis for the monarchy. The paper says the Royal Family is preparing for a series of allegations in the book.

People close to the artificial intelligence companies, Anthropic and OpenAI, have told the Financial Times, external that proposals - put forward in recent days by their bosses, on how to constrain AI's development - are creating tensions among staff.

The paper says one of the ideas is to embed outside evaluators within the labs to oversee model development, but some fear this could compromise the security of their prized technology.

Writing in the Daily Express, external, the Conservative leader, Kemi Badenoch, argues that Andy Burnham will drag Britain back into the 1970s. She says the government wants to spend more and tax more.

The i Paper, external highlights the prime minister's pledge that the government will exercise the "highest degree of prudence" in next month's Budget. The paper also says the Chancellor John Healey is likely to increase capital gains tax.

The Guardian, external reports that plans have been drawn up to "double up" prisoners in temporary prefabricated cells built for one person, because of a shortage of prison spaces in England and Wales.

The paper says using the prefabricated cells to house two inmates could increase the capacity of the prison estate by 476. The Ministry of Justice says it is "pulling every lever available" to ensure there is space to lock up dangerous offenders.

And finally the Daily Mail, external says it's spoken to outraged parents in Somerset after a new headteacher of Frome Community College suspended 25 pupils in the first week of term. 

The headteacher Dan Jeffries says the punishments were for "persistent disruption," but some parents likened his strict regime to that on a military base.
