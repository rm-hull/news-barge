A FORMER police officer who did an art degree after retiring is exhibiting paintings to raise funds for the Alzheimer’s Society.

David Hemsley retired from West Yorkshire Police in 1992 and went on to fulfill an ambition by obtaining a BA degree in art and design at Bradford College.

“I went on to use my art skills at Chapel Grange Special school and Rhodesway School in Bradford to assist children with special needs,” said David.

“Now, at my stage in life, I have decided I would like to contribute to the Alzheimer’s Society’s Forget Me not appeal, to fund research into this condition that affects every member of society in some way.

“Dementia is a living death and a great sadness to observe for those who are close.”

At the age of 83, David is a prolific artist, working in his studio at home.

He currently has an ongoing exhibition of his smaller paintings at the Ling Bob pub in Wilsden, with the support of licensee Thanos Dimou, and is donating all the funds from sales to the Alzheimer’s Society.

David’s artwork - which includes colourful paintings of wildlife, trees, plants and flowers - is inspired by a range of interests.

  David's paintings are on display at the Ling Bob in Wilsden. 

*(Image: David Hemsley)*

“I have always been interested in the spiritual association the Native Americans and the Australian Aborigines have with nature,” he says.

  Wildlife and the natural world inspire David to paint 

*(Image: David Hemsley)*

“I joined the Bradford City Police then after six years service, together with my wife and two boys, became

‘£10 pound poms’ and went to Queensland, Australia.

“We returned after two years and I re-joined Bradford City Police and stayed in as it went through the changes, ending up as West Yorkshire Police.

“In 1991, together with other officers, I drove 4,000 miles to deliver medicines and goods to a number of Romanian orphanages and schools.

“The trip affected me emotionally in many ways and my way of processing this was to do a painting of the dying orphans imprisoned behind the bars of their metal cots with no chance of life.”

He adds: “My other paintings all have a reference to humanity’s attachment to nature. I believe we are part of nature, not independent, and that today we are astonished by how nature replies to our lack of understanding of it.

“If we continue to destroy nature we are destroying the body that we require to exist in.”
