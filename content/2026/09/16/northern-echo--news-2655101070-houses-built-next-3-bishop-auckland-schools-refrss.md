Plans to build 70 new homes on land close to three education sites in Bishop Auckland are set to go before councillors this week.

Durham County Council's South and West Area Planning Committee will meet on Thursday, September 17, to consider proposals for the development on land at Kitchen Avenue.

The scheme has been recommended for approval by planning officers, but needs to be given the nod by councillors at the meeting.

Developer Gleeson Regeneration wants to build 70 properties on the 2.56-hectare site, which was previously occupied by homes before they were demolished in 2004.

The plans include seven bungalows, 10 two-bedroom houses, 38 three-bedroom houses and 15 four-bedroom houses, along with roads, parking and green space.


It sits between several education facilities.

Saint Wilfrid's Catholic Primary School is directly next to the northern boundary, while St John's School and Sixth Form College and Bishop Auckland College are located immediately to the west.

Planning officers say the development would provide much-needed housing in a sustainable location close to schools, public transport and other facilities.

The report notes the council currently cannot demonstrate a full five-year supply of deliverable housing sites, giving added weight to new housing proposals.

The scheme would include seven affordable homes, while the developer would also make financial contributions towards education, healthcare, biodiversity and open space improvements.

These include £88,385 for nursery provision, £57,946 for special educational needs and disabilities places, £39,445 towards GP capacity and more than £110,000 for public open space projects.

Only one objection was submitted during the consultation process.

Concerns were raised over parking and vehicle movements on a nearby section of Cheesmond Avenue, but Durham County Council's highways officers raised no objections to the plans.
