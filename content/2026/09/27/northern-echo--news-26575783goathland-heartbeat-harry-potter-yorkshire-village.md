A North Yorkshire village known to millions as the setting for Heartbeat and Harry Potter continues to attract visitors with its historic railway, stone cottages and dramatic moorland setting.

Goathland, around nine miles from Whitby in the North York Moors National Park, has become one of the county's most recognisable villages after appearing on television and the big screen.

For almost two decades, it doubled as the fictional village of Aidensfield in ITV's hugely popular Heartbeat, while its historic railway station later appeared as Hogsmeade Station in Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone.

But Goathland's history as a visitor destination stretches back much further than either production.

![]()Goathland *(Image: Keith Rowland/CAMERA CLUB)*

The village is surrounded by open moorland and is characterised by traditional stone buildings, shops and pubs, with the North Yorkshire Moors Railway running through the valley below.

Its appearance made it a natural choice for Heartbeat, which first aired in 1992 and was set in the fictional North Yorkshire village of Aidensfield during the 1960s.

Goathland provided many of the programme's familiar outdoor locations during its 18-series run.

Although Heartbeat came to an end in 2010, its connection with Goathland remains a major part of the village's identity.

Visitors can still recognise locations from the programme, while reminders of Aidensfield remain around the village.

Visit North Yorkshire says Goathland became even more popular with visitors after appearing as Aidensfield in the television series.

Its television fame was followed by another screen appearance which introduced the village to a new generation.

Goathland railway station was used as Hogsmeade Station in Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone, released in 2001.

In the film, Hogsmeade is the station used by pupils travelling to and from Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry.

Away from the cameras, Goathland remains a working station on the North Yorkshire Moors Railway.

The heritage railway runs for 24 miles between Pickering and Whitby, taking passengers through the North York Moors and stopping at Goathland along the way.

Goathland station retains much of its traditional appearance and includes a tea room in the former goods shed as well as a shop.

Its period character, combined with its Harry Potter connection, has helped make it one of the railway's best-known stations.

![]()Goathland *(Image: Keith Rowland/CAMERA CLUB)*

However, visitors were travelling to Goathland long before Heartbeat or Harry Potter arrived.

One of the area's best-known natural landmarks is Mallyan Spout, a waterfall hidden in a wooded valley close to the village.

Water falls around 70ft over the rock face, with a walking route descending through woodland to reach it.

Visit North Yorkshire says Mallyan Spout was already attracting visitors to Goathland during the 19th century.

The arrival of the railway also helped establish the village as a destination for people wanting to explore the surrounding moors.

Today, Goathland remains a popular starting point for walks through the North York Moors.

One route follows the course of the former railway towards Grosmont, while walkers can also head through the small settlement of Beck Hole.

The combination of railway and walking routes means visitors can arrive in Goathland by heritage train before exploring the village and surrounding countryside on foot.

There are also cafés, traditional pubs and shops in the village, while its position puts it within relatively easy reach of Whitby and other parts of the North York Moors.

Goathland's television and film appearances have undoubtedly changed the way many visitors see the village.
