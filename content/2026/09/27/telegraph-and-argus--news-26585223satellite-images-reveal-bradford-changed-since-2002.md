A remarkable set of satellite images shows just how dramatically Bradford has changed over the past 23 years.

Google Earth images from 2002 and 2025 show large areas of vacant, industrial or underused land redeveloped for homes, shops and businesses.

Growth has been most visible around the city centre and major transport routes, where former brownfield sites have been redeveloped.

The transformation has been driven by a number of landmark projects that have altered the city's landscape and helped bring new homes, jobs and investment to Bradford.

## The Broadway

One of the most significant developments during the period was The Broadway in the city centre.

Google street view images show the site prior to development in July 2008 and after July 2023.

The £260 million shopping and leisure complex opened in 2015 and has become one of the biggest regeneration projects in the city's recent history.

The site dramatically altered the appearance of the city centre and attracted hundreds of millions of pounds of additional investment into Bradford.

The area around has also seen substantial change.

New shops, businesses and infrastructure improvements have strengthened links between the city centre and one of Bradford’s main transport hubs.

## Significant development along Canal Road

Further north, the Canal Road corridor from the city centre towards Shipley has seen major industrial and business growth.

Former mills, warehouses and underused plots have been replaced by modern business units and distribution centres, making the area look increasingly built-up in aerial images.

Housing development has also played a major role.

Across Bradford, numerous former industrial sites have been converted into residential estates, helping to meet demand from one of the youngest and fastest-growing populations outside London.

New homes have appeared on Bradford’s eastern, southern and western fringes, replacing land that was vacant or undeveloped in the early 2000s.

## Regeneration projects across the district

More recently, regeneration projects such as Darley Street Market and and the wider Bradford City Village scheme have continued to reshape the city centre.

![]() *(Image: T&A)*

The long-term plan includes up to 1,000 new homes, public spaces, shops and community facilities, showing Bradford’s transformation is far from over.

Together, the projects explain Bradford’s spread of development over 23 years, as vacant land has given way to homes, businesses and regeneration schemes.
