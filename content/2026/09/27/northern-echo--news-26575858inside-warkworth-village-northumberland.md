Warkworth’s enormous medieval castle is the Northumberland village’s hardest landmark to miss.

Yet one of its most remarkable historic sites is almost completely hidden.

A medieval chapel is carved into the rock beneath trees beside the River Coquet.

No road leads to its entrance, and visitors cannot simply walk in.

Warkworth Hermitage is reached by boat.

It is one of several unusual landmarks in a village with a castle, a fortified medieval bridge and a coast within walking distance.

## The castle above Warkworth

Warkworth lies close to the Northumberland coast, between Alnwick and Amble.

The village is dominated by Warkworth Castle, which stands high above the surrounding streets on a rocky spur encircled by the River Coquet.

The castle was once home to the powerful Percy family, still associated with Alnwick Castle.

Its distinctive cross-shaped keep remains visible from around Warkworth today.

Below it, Castle Street is the historic spine of Warkworth.

The street runs between the castle and St Lawrence's Church, creating one of Warkworth’s finest views.

Shops, galleries and inns fill the historic buildings along the route.

Visit Northumberland calls the village small but packed with a “wealth of history”.

Some of that history is much harder to spot.

## The secret chapel reached only by boat

Warkworth Hermitage lies about half a mile upstream from Warkworth Castle.

Hidden beneath a wooded bank on the River Coquet, the hermitage contains a 14th-century chapel carved into the rock.

Its secluded position means visitors cross the river by boat to reach it.

Religious hermits once lived there, creating a stark contrast with the huge fortress above the village.

Visitors follow the river from the castle before crossing to the site when it is open.

Visit Northumberland describes it as accessible only by boat and notes that seals and otters can sometimes be seen in the river around it.

## A remarkable medieval bridge

Visitors could cross Warkworth’s other historic structure without realising its significance.

The medieval bridge spans the River Coquet near the village centre.

Visit Northumberland says it is believed to be the only surviving fortified medieval bridge in England.

Together, the bridge, church, hermitage and castle pack centuries of history into a small settlement.

St Lawrence’s Church dates from the 12th century, and its tall spire marks the end of the main street opposite the castle.

The result is a village centre framed by medieval buildings at both ends.

## From castle to coast

Warkworth Beach offers a very different landscape of dunes and sand, with views towards Coquet Island and along the Northumberland coastline.

Visitors can take in the village, castle, riverside and beach in one trip.

Nearby Amble offers another base for exploring the coast, while Alnwick and its famous castle lie to the north.
