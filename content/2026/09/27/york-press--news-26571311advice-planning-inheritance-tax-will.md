## Have you planned for Inheritance Tax in your will?

Ramsdens Solicitors has provided advice on Inheritance Tax and wills. *(Image: Pexels)*

Inheritance Tax (IHT) is something many people are aware of, but it is often misunderstood or overlooked when drafting wills.

In simple terms, it is a tax that may be charged on your estate after you die, including your property, savings, investments and personal belongings.

In the UK, IHT is usually payable if the value of your estate exceeds £325,000.

Anything above this threshold may be taxed at 40 per cent.

Whilst this may sound straightforward, the reality is that many estates are affected, particularly where property values have increased over time.

There are important exemptions and allowances that can reduce the amount payable.

For example, assets left to a spouse or civil partner are generally exempt.

There is also an additional allowance when passing your home to direct descendants, such as children or grandchildren.

In some cases, couples can combine their allowances, potentially passing on up to £1 million without Inheritance Tax being due.

However, without proper planning, families can still face unexpected tax bills.

In some situations, loved ones may need to sell property or other assets to cover the cost, which can add financial pressure at an already difficult time.

This is particularly challenging where there is no clear plan in place or where a will has not been reviewed to reflect current circumstances.

IHT is not just a concern for the very wealthy.

Changes in property values and personal circumstances mean more families can be affected than they might expect.

Despite this, many people delay seeking advice or assume their estate will fall below the threshold.

There are steps that can be taken to reduce the potential impact, such as making gifts during your lifetime under the seven year rule, or structuring your estate in a tax-efficient way through trusts or settlements under the nil rate band.

However, the rules can be complex, and mistakes are costly, particularly if they are not considered as part of a wider estate plan.

A professionally prepared and regularly reviewed Will that considers IHT liabilities plays an important role in this process.

It helps ensure your wishes are clearly set out and that your estate is managed as efficiently as possible.

Taking the time to review your will with a specialist lawyer can provide reassurance that you have considered IHT and taken steps to protect your loved ones.

It can also help avoid unnecessary stress, delays and expense in the future.
