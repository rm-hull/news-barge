A Bradford woman has spoken out about losing the father of the New Jersey family she worked for.

Louise Rayner, who grew up in Frizinghall, was living in the United States on September 11, 2001, when Bob Lawrence died in the attacks on the World Trade Centre.

Mr Lawrence, aged 41, had just started a new job in New York City.

September 11 was his second day at work.

Ms Rayner had been living with his family for two and a half years and working as their au pair.

She met the Lawrences after another au pair she met on the plane introduced her to them.

Ms Rayner said: "I hadn’t told anyone about it before, but this year felt like such a significant anniversary."

She remembered seeing Mr Lawrence leave for work the day before the attacks.

Ms Rayner said: "I saw him leave the day before.

"Someone picked him up, and when he came home that afternoon, we chatted.

"But on Tuesday morning (September 11), he went to work at 6am.

"So we didn’t see him."

Ms Rayner recalled that the landline started ringing that morning.

She said: "I remember thinking, 'if it’s important, they’ll ring my mobile.'

"And then they did."

A friend called and told her to turn on the news.

Ms Rayner watched the attacks unfold on television.

She said: "I stood and watched the whole thing on the TV.

"We lived in a town where a lot of Bob’s family lived too.

"The house became the hub that everyone congregated at."

  Louise Rayner with Toland and Bobby Lawrence 

*(Image: Louise Rayner)*

Mr Lawrence’s children, Toland and Bobby, were 11 and nine at the time of their father's death.

Their mother, Suzanne, was in Texas.

Grounded planes and cancelled trains meant it took her three days to return home.

For a period, the family did not know what had happened to Mr Lawrence.

Ms Rayner said people were arriving at hospitals without remembering how they had got there.

Ms Rayner said: "It was surreal for a few weeks afterwards.

"Nobody really knew what had happened.

"Some people were showing up in hospitals with amnesia, saying that they couldn’t remember what had happened to them.

"In that regard, there was a level of hope, as we didn’t know whether that had happened to Bob.

"For months, there were missing person posters in Penn Station."

Mr Lawrence’s body was never recovered.

The family later held a memorial service.

Speaking about Bob, Ms Rayner said: "He was a massive family man, and a big character.

"He loved sports.

"I got on with him very well."

She explained that she grew closer to the family after his death.

Ms Rayner said: "I wasn’t as close to the Lawrences until after that day.

"It was like there was a new sort of thread of connection between us."

Ms Rayner described meeting the family as a “series of sliding doors”, reflecting on how a change in her travel plans led to their meeting.

She said: "Part of me felt that I shouldn’t have been there.

"I had changed my original flight and that’s the only reason I met the Lawrences.

"But I left knowing I’d done all I could.

"I didn’t feel like I was abandoning them.

"I made sure that a good friend, another English au pair, took over the job once I left."

She eventually returned to the UK and settled in Baildon with her husband and three children.

The Lawrence family have visited her in Bradford and attended her wedding.
