A vintage lighting business is shining brighter thanks to a new kiln and cutter.

Jewel Street Lighting, based in Saltburn, restores antique, vintage, and retro lighting, breathing new life into old designs.

With the support of a grant from the Tees Valley Rural Prosperity Fund, the company has now expanded its operations and creative capabilities.

Sarah Hill, who runs the business, said: "Historically, we have lost a couple of contracts because of the time it takes, but now I’ve got a bigger kiln so a commission will take me half the time.

"I think we were at a point where we weren’t making enough money to sustain ourselves, so the grant was absolutely crucial.

"It’s put fire back in my belly that I can actually do this – and I have the equipment to do it now."

The funding also allowed the business to invest in a new cutter tool, enabling Ms Hill to broaden her product range.

Ms Hill said: "The new cutter is going to make a lot of difference in cutting the double-glazed units down and even creating lights out of bottles."

The grant scheme, funded by the UK Government through the UK Shared Prosperity Fund, was designed to support rural businesses and community groups across Redcar and Cleveland.

Ben Houchen, Tees Valley Mayor, said: "We’re proud of the work we do to unlock well-paid jobs and careers for people through major projects.

"But this is a great example of a little bit of money going a long way in Redcar and Cleveland.

"We need to give our small businesses the support they need to flourish."
