A man has denied racing with his friend in their cars on a Bradford road just moments before a crash that left his friend with injuries that he did not survive.

University student Husayn Ali, 21, was pronounced dead in hospital three hours after his Skoda Fabia veered across Bolton Road to collide with a VW Golf driven travelling in the opposite lane on March 7, 2024.

A trial at Bradford Crown Court has heard that he was racing at 65mph with his friend Mohammed Awais Iqbal, who was following close behind in a Seat Leon Cupra, and failed to negotiate a bend on the road, which has a 30mph speed limit.

In an interview with police Iqbal, now 24, of Beechwood Drive, Wibsey, denied that he was involved in racing or reckless behaviour or encouraging anyone else to do it prior to the accident.

He said he had seen Mr Ali’s car “swerving from side to side” and had put on his hazard lights to warn him how badly he was driving.

He said he was not doing it to encourage Mr Ali and denied that the incident was anything to do with him.

The jury heard how Mr Ali was driving too fast for the road and that Iqbal was trying initially to keep up with him and then activated his lights to warn him to slow down.

   

*(Image: Newsquest)*

However, unlike Iqbal, Mr Ali failed to slow down as he entered the bend and defence barrister Allan Armbrister suggested that it was his driving that caused the accident that led to his death and the serious injuries suffered by passengers Bilal Hussain and Harris Butt.

It was suggested that Iqbal’s dangerous driving did not encourage or contribute to the accident at all.

The jury heard that Iqbal had no previous convictions and was of hitherto good character.

On the fourth day of the trial Iqbal repeated his denials that he was racing or encouraging Mr Ali to go faster on the night of the incident.

The jury heard that the Seat had been bought at auction and was found to have a missing catalytic converter and resonator.

Its lights had also been modified so that the indicators and dipped front headlights flashed on alternative sides of the car as opposed to flashing together.

The engine had also been “tuned” to enhance the Seat’s performance to the extent that it was increased to 400 brake horsepower.

On the night of the crash Iqbal and Mr Ali had met up following a mutual friend’s birthday celebrations. In three cars they headed for a dessert parlour on Leeds Road.

It was during this brief journey that the collision happened.

CCTV footage played in court showed Iqbal’s Seat following Mr Ali’s Skoda as both cars drove at normal speed and passed through sets of traffic lights.

Prosecuting barrister Gerald Hendron suggested that Iqbal turned on his flashing hazard lights not to warn Mr Ali about his driving but to encourage him to speed up.

He said: “This was just all a bit of fun between some guys having a laugh [and] driving their cars, wasn’t it?”

Iqbal replied: “I disagree.”

Hendron: “You putting on your flashing lights was just entertainment – a signal to go fast.”

Iqbal: “I disagree.”

Hendron: “You were driving far too close to him, distracting him, and encouraging him. And, undoubtedly, he was driving dangerously but you were driving dangerously also, together.”

Iqbal: “I disagree.”

Hendron: “You were far too close to him, and you reduced what options he did have as that car started to slide because you were so close.”

Iqbal: “I disagree.”

Hendron: “If you had backed off, in all likelihood he would have driven round that bend at 30 miles an hour because there was nobody watching him drive.”

Iqbal: “I disagree.”

Iqbal denies causing death by dangerous driving, dangerous driving, and two counts of causing serious injury by dangerous driving.

Her Honour Judge Kirstie Watson is expected to give her summing up on Monday, after which the jury will retire to consider its verdicts.
