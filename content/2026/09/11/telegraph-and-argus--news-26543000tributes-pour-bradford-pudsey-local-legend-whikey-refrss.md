A MUCH-LOVED man who visited the same pub everyday for the last 10 years has died.

Phillip Ward, known to many as 'Whikey', passed away at the age of 72.

Mr Ward was a familiar face in the Pudsey Tavern on Lowtown in the town centre.

The landlady at the pub said: “He was a customer for around 10 years.

“Over that time, he became a much-loved and familiar face to our staff and regulars.”

## 'A private, no-fuss kind of man'

Mr Ward was described as a private person who avoided attention.

He worked at Morley Electrics for more than 50 years and was known for his routine at the pub.

The landlady said: “He was a very private, no-fuss kind of man.

“In fact, he was the sort of person who wouldn’t even tell you when his birthday was, because he knew that meant we couldn’t buy him a card or a drink!

“That was just Whikey.”

## Always arrived at the same time

Despite his private nature, Mr Ward was described as cheerful and positive.

He usually arrived at the Tavern at around 2.30pm each day.

The landlady said: “He was also one of the biggest supporters of the Pudsey Tavern and was a daily customer, usually arriving around 2:30–2:35 pm.

“You could set your watch by him.”

## 'He had so many stories about Bradford'

One phrase became linked with his arrival at the pub.

The landlady said: “One of his most famous lines was, ‘You heard owt?’ - something we’ll all remember hearing when he walked through the door.”

Mr Ward also spent time in Bradford city centre with his partner Julie.

He had a strong memory of the city's pubs and nightlife, and often spoke about places that had closed or changed.

The landlady said: “He had so many stories about Bradford, particularly its pub culture and he also loved a good curry.

“He was always recommending curryhouses and telling people where to find the best one.”

## 'He was the best big brother'

Mr Ward's sister, Beverley Bruce, also paid tribute to her older brother.

Ms Bruce said: “He was the best big brother - always so caring & protective of me.

"He was a legend.”

Mr Ward’s death has affected staff and regulars at The Pudsey Tavern.

The pub said there is now a moment each afternoon when people remember him arriving through the doors.

The landlady said: “His passing has had a huge impact on us at the Pudsey Tavern.

“He wasn’t simply a customer - he was part of the Pudsey Tavern family.”

## Pub crawl arranged in tribute

Some of Mr Ward’s friends have organised a Bradford pub crawl in his memory set to take place tomorrow.

The event will remember his connection with Bradford, its pubs and its people.

The landlady said: “Whikey’s name and memory will forever live on here.

“He was a true one-off, he will be greatly missed and the Pudsey Tavern will never quite be the same without him.”
