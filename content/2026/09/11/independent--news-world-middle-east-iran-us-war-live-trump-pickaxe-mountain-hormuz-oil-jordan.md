Iran-backed Houthi militants have captured Yemen's strategic port city of Mokha, raising fears it could bring them closer to targeting shipping through a critical waterway to the Red Sea.

It puts the Houthis closer to their goal of targeting shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb waterway and could give Iran another potential point of leverage in its war with the US.

Mokha is around 50 miles from Bab el-Mandeb, a geopolitical chokepoint connecting the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden and through which about 12 per cent of the world’s goods are typically shipped.

Its importance has grown since shipping through the nearby Strait of Hormuz has been halted.

Meanwhile, Iranian officials have mocked the US military after claiming they captured a cutting-edge sea drone and “picked it up like a plastic bottle”.

Taking to X, Iran’s embassy in Ghana said it had caught “the most advanced ghosts sub ever” deployed by US forces in the Strait of Hormuz.

“It came in cocky, like a tourist who doesn't read signs. Our guys picked it up like a plastic bottle in a fishing net. Fully intact. Still blinking like an idiot,” the embassy added.

## Trump says he does not regret Iran war despite midterm election risks

Donald Trump has said he does not regret launching the war against Iran, despite concerns that the conflict and rising oil and gas prices could hurt Republicans in the November midterm elections.

“I don’t believe in the word ‘regret’. You can always question yourself a little bit,” Trump said in an interview with Fox News.

“If I had it do again, I would do exactly as I did,” Trump said.

Trump rejected suggestions that the war had left some of his supporters demoralised, saying: “I don’t think they’re demoralised. I think they’re very proud of the fact that I’m not letting Iran have a nuclear weapon.”

Trump also repeated his claim that the war would end immediately after the midterm elections. “It ends right after the election,” he said, while accusing Tehran of trying to influence the vote.

## Iran hints at withdrawal from nuclear non-proliferation treaty

The “political moves” by the UN’s nuclear watchdog could push countries to withdraw from the Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), a military adviser to Iran’s supreme leader has warned.

Mohsen Rezaei made the comments a day after the International Atomic Energy Agency’s 35-member Board of Governors passed a resolution referring Iran to the UN Security Council over its alleged breaches of nuclear non-proliferation obligations.

## White House advisers tell Trump the Iran war could last his entire term - report

Donald Trump has said that the Iran war will end soon after the November midterm elections, claiming Tehran is trying to influence the vote.

“I think the war is going to end immediately after the election because they can’t hold out any longer,” Trump told reporters.

“They’re desperate to try and affect the election.”

But the *Wall Street Journal* reported that senior White House advisers, including JD Vance and Marco Rubio, have warned him privately that the war could continue until the end of his presidency in January 2029. 

## Democratic senator accuses Trump of ‘lying’ about end of Iran war

Democratic senator Ed Markey has accused Donald Trump of lying about the “unwinnable Iran war”.

## Yemen risks a return to civil war as Iran-backed rebels target a crucial shipping route

Yemen is moving closer to a return to civil war. The escalating events in the Arab world's poorest country are closely linked to the war in Iran and global efforts to ship oil out of the Gulf region.

Here’s a look at an emerging conflict involving an Iranian-backed rebel group's efforts to keep neighboring Saudi Arabia from using a crucial shipping alternative to the Strait of Hormuz, and how that could tear Yemen apart once more.

What’s happening could return Yemen to civil war

Yemen is home to over 40 million people who fear a return to the civil war that killed 150,000 and brought many others to the brink of famine in one of the world’s most sprawling humanitarian crises. A ceasefire with Yemen’s internationally recognized government and a wider coalition backed by the Saudis began in 2022.

Now that truce is buckling.

## Residents flee Mokha after Houthis take control

Residents have said Houthi forces could be seen throughout Mokha on Thursday, as locals fled the city after their capture.

Mokha resident Amin al-Mohammdi said roads from the west coast leading to Mokha were closed and shops were shuttered.

Local Mohamed Salem said he saw vehicles belonging to Houthi forces in the streets.

Three doctors told the AP they fled a Mokha hospital after it was seized by the Houthis.

The doctors spoke on condition of anonymity, citing fears for their safety.

They said other residents were fleeing south, toward the city of Aden, which is controlled by the Saudi-backed forces.

## Envoy warns of 'new and more dangerous phase' in Yemen war

The UN Security council has been warned that the international community must act to address “a new and more dangerous phase” in the Yemen war.

Hans Grundberg, the UN Special Envoy for Yemen, told a UN said the Houthis' gaining control of Mocha gave them “a direct presence on the approaches to one of the world’s most vital straits.”

Speaking to the council on Thursday, he said: “When I briefed this council last month, I warned that Yemen was facing the greatest risk of a return to large-scale conflict since the 2022 truce. That risk has now turned into a harsh reality.

“This renewed war should alarm us all. It effectively marks the end of the relative calm that Yemen has experienced for over four years since the UN-brokered truce.

“It not only threatens to plunge Yemen into a prolonged and expanding war, but also risks yet again dragging the country into the wider and unresolved regional confrontation, with global repercussions.”

## Houthis acting as 'agents and tools of Iran'

The Houthis have been accused of acting as “agents and tools of Iran” after capturing a key Red Sea port city on Thursday.

US representative Jenifer Neidhart de Ortiz told a UN Security council: “This escalation is unacceptable, and those who enable it bear responsibility for its consequences.”

Yemeni government, Iranian and regional sources told Reuters the Houthi advance down the coast this week came with direct guidance from Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps.

Houthi spokesperson Mohammed Abdulsalam said, without elaborating, that the group's operations were defensive and would stop once attacks on Yemen stopped.

## Projectiles hit two vessels near Oman's Khasab - UKMTO

Four unknown projectiles hit two vessels 4 nautical miles west of Oman's Khasab, causing a fire on one vessel and`leaving the status of the second vessel unknown, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said on Thursday.

The master of another vessel reported seeing the projectiles hit the two vessels about 6 nautical miles north of his position, it added.
