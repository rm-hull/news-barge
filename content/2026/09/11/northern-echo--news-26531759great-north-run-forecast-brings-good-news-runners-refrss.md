Thousands of Great North Run runners will face mild temperatures and mostly dry conditions across Tyneside this weekend.

Early forecasts for the world's biggest half marathon suggest temperatures will start in the mid-teens on Sunday, September 13, before edging up slightly through the day, with a fresh breeze but little sign of significant rain.

The race, which is the world's largest half marathon, runs from Newcastle city centre to South Shields seafront, where the final mile can be much cooler and windier than the opening stages.

Forecasts put temperatures at 17C to 19C during the race, with mostly cloudy skies and moderate winds.

The temperatures should reduce the risk of overheating while keeping conditions comfortable for spectators along the route.

However, runners are being advised not to focus solely on temperature.

Coastal winds could be a factor as runners head for the South Shields finish after more sheltered sections through Newcastle and Gateshead.

The forecast may change before race day, but there is currently little sign of extreme heat, heavy rain or storms.

Tens of thousands of runners and huge crowds are expected, with organisers hoping the favourable forecast holds through race weekend.

The current picture suggests the North East could deliver exactly the kind of weather many runners dream of: cool, dry and comfortable enough to chase a personal best.

### What to expect on race day:

- Temperature: Around 17C to 19C during race hours
- Conditions: Mostly cloudy with lengthy dry spells
- Wind: A noticeable breeze, particularly along South Shields seafront
- Rain risk: Currently low according to available forecasts
- The forecast could still change before race day.
