A West Yorkshire First Bus driver has been crowned the best in England after winning a national competition.

David Nairn, from Leeds, took the top title at the national competition, beating more than 100 finalists from across the country.

## 'I cried for the first time in 15 years'

The First Bus driver said he was still coming to terms with the win after being named the UK’s top driver at the event, which took place on Blackpool Promenade on September 6.

Mr Nairn said: "I still haven’t quite got over it yet, to be quite honest with you.

"They go, 'Top bus driver of the year is David Nairn,' and that is when I cried for the first time in 15 years.

"I wasn’t expecting to win."

   David Nairn won five trophies during the competition. 

*(Image: Richard Harrison)*


## From sixth place to national champion

Encouraged by his partner, Mr Nairn entered the 2023 competition and finished sixth.

He returned this year with a relaxed attitude, not expecting to improve on his previous result.

Mr Nairn said: "I thought if I come top 20 I’ll be happy."

The win marks the second consecutive year First Bus has taken the title, with last year’s winner Michael Leech hailing from Halifax.

It is the first time in 20 years the operator has retained the award.

## A new career behind the wheel

Mr Nairn joined First Bus in 2021 after working in retail.

He said: "July 2021 I passed my test.

"It was time for a new challenge.

"I’ve always had a passion for machines and things like that, I like big stuff that move."

  David Nairn cuddles his mascot Daphne with Jennifer Moss, Training and Recruitment Manager and Neil Green, Operations Manager of the First Bus depot in Bramley showing off the awards he won. 

*(Image: Richard Harrison)*

He credited his trainer, Gail Lee, for her support.

Mr Nairn said: "I was only one of her first handful of trainees that she got passed.

"She seemed dead proud."

## The challenge of becoming the UK's top driver

Ten First Bus drivers from Yorkshire reached the finals.

The competition tested drivers on practical manoeuvres along a three-mile course, including pulling in at stops within two to four inches of the kerb.

Drivers were also assessed on a 25-question Highway Code theory test.

Mr Nairn said: "They are the most obscure questions, it’s a bit like doing Pointless for the highway code."

  David Nairn and his mascot Daphne with the trophy for Bus Driver of the Year at the First Bus depot in Bramley. 

*(Image: Richard Harrison)*

He walked away with over £4,000 in prize money, also earning the titles of highest-placed driver from a depot in England and the best performer for First Bus.

## Daphne the dog brings good luck

Mr Nairn brought his dog Daphne to the event and credited her as a good luck charm.

He said: "She was a bit of a good luck charm.

"She comes absolutely everywhere with us."
