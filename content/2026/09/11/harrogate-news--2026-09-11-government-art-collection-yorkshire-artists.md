**HARROGATE**— Artworks created by a generation of visual artists from Yorkshire and the Humber have been officially acquired by the** Government Art Collection (GAC)** to represent British culture on the international stage.

Ten new pieces by eight regional creators were secured as part of the fourth instalment of the five-year **Art X-UK project**.

Administered by the Department for Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS), the scheme ensures that national collections reflect creative industries operating outside of London.

**Washington DC, Paris, Ankara, and Abu Dhabi**.

## Local Identity on a Global Stage

The selection features several prominent contemporary artists whose studio practices are rooted firmly within Yorkshire’s creative ecosystems:

- **Grace Clifford:** A Sheffield-based sculptor who utilizes everyday objects and industrial mediums to explore working-class community heritage. Her newly acquired work features a sculpted bronze pub stool.
- **Kedisha Coakley:** A sculptor whose featured works,*Collection 15* and*I AM HERE: Breadfruit II* , trace the historical intersections of colonialism, botany, and Black British identity.
- **Sam Metz:** A Hull-based artist whose work explores stimming as a creative practice, supported heavily by accessible studio communities at The Art House in Wakefield.

## Strengthening Regional Arts Ecosystems

The acquisitions program operates in partnership with the Contemporary Visual Arts Network (CVAN England) and regional bodies like the Yorkshire and Humber Visual Art Network (YVAN). The collaboration aims to leverage place-based cultural identity to bolster the UK’s broader international creative reputation.


Arts Minister **Ruth Mackenzie** welcomed the regional rollout, stating:

Through Art X-UK, we are recognising creative talent from every part of the country, championing regional artists and helping them to flourish on an international level.



Sharon Gill, CEO of the Yorkshire and Humber Visual Art Network, noted the selection highlights the significant depth, ambition, and diversity of active studio practices currently operating across the county.
