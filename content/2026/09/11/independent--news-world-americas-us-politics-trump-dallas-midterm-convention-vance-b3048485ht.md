President Donald Trump and his Republican Party concluded an unconventional gathering in Dallas on Thursday, seeking to bolster their midterm election prospects.

They are betting that a prominent display of party cohesion will rally supporters and help Republicans overcome historic midterm losses.

Here are five takeaways from the two-day event.

## GOP bets on fear of Democrats to overcome voter anger over prices and Iran

The Republican National Committee had framed the convention as an opportunity to steer voter attention toward Trump's legislative goals and celebrate the party's policy achievements.

Yet Trump, alongside numerous convention speakers, devoted the majority of their addresses to denouncing Democrats as extreme left-wing figures who pose a threat to the nation. U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent alleged that Democrats intend to convert the United States into a "socialist hellscape."

Targeting political rivals and using fear in election efforts is a tactic both major parties have embraced for decades. Trump has consistently positioned matters such as immigration and crime at the center of his campaign message.

![Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent alleged that Democrats intend to convert the United States into a "socialist hellscape."](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/11/09/2026-09-10T224133Z_1738695600_RC21GNACQ3W5_RTRMADP_3_IRAN-CRISIS-USA-BANK-SANCTION.JPG)

This sharp focus on Democrats highlights the political obstacles Republicans face in their effort to maintain their majorities in Congress. Amid public discontent regarding high prices and the ongoing conflict in Iran, speakers repeatedly insisted that a Democratic-controlled Congress would ultimately present a far greater threat.

## Trump dangled a $5,000 ‘dividend’ for every American adult if GOP wins

During the address, Trump set out a series of ambitious commitments, several of which could prove hard to deliver.

Most notably, he proposed providing every American adult with a $5,000 "Trump dividend" should Republicans triumph in the elections and maintain their control over both houses of Congress.

This scheme encounters the exact impediment that stalled his earlier proposed $2,000 tariff rebate—which failed to gain congressional backing—namely, convincing legislators to approve spending that exceeds $1 trillion.

![During the address, Trump set out a series of ambitious commitments, several of which could prove hard to deliver](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/11/07/2026-09-11T035841Z_1690800685_RC2RGNAQECNY_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-REPUBLICANS.JPG)

Furthermore, Trump pledged that oil prices would decrease "as soon as we win the war with Iran," forecasting that the fighting would finish "right after Election Day," November 3.

However, the conflict shows few signs of resolution since U.S. and Israeli forces launched strikes against Iran in February, impeding Trump's attempts to reach an end through military leverage or diplomatic talks.

## Trump urged supporters to ‘pretend’ he was running in November

Trump and many leading Republicans maintain that his MAGA supporters demonstrate lower turnout when he does not feature on the ballot, as will occur this coming November.

"Pretend that I'm on the ballot," Trump said to cheering supporters. Yet Democratic strategists may well have reason to celebrate, interpreting his insistence on putting himself at the center of the campaign as helping their efforts to make the vote a national referendum on an unpopular president.

![Trump urged his followers to pretend that he was on the ballot](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/11/08/2026-09-11T033859Z_957102481_RC2RGNADRTZT_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-REPUBLICANS.JPG)

This strategy remains a gamble that could alienate vital swing voters necessary for winning congressional seats. These voters have steadily drifted away from Trump and his party across several key issues, such as the economy, the war, and immigration raids.

## JD Vance offered first glimpse of how he could reshape MAGA in 2028

Should Republicans lose control of either congressional chamber in November, it will likely accelerate debates over who will lead the party's 2028 presidential ticket. Vice President JD Vance occupies a central position in those discussions.

Delivering a Thursday night keynote, Vance offered a preview of how he might position a future presidential run. While embracing Trump’s populist message and aggressive stance against Democrats, he placed far greater emphasis on family, faith, and children’s futures, framing the race as a battle to protect what he termed Americans' "birthright."

Vance was greeted by chants of "48, 48" – referencing his potential as the 48th U.S. president after Trump, the 47th. "We got to take care of business this November, then we'll worry about what comes next," he said.

A key question is whether Trump will name a successor, though the president gave no hint of his thoughts after taking the stage following Vance. His backing would carry immense weight after a decade reshaping the party and building a loyal base.

Trump later told reporters on Air Force One returning to Washington that Vance performed "very well" and called him a "very talented guy."

"He's been a great vice president, and I think he did a very good job," Trump said.
