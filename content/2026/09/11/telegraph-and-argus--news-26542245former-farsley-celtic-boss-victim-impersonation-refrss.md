FORMER Farsley Celtic boss Russ Wilcox is at the centre of one of football’s most extraordinary stories this year.

Now in charge at Gainsborough Trinity, someone has been impersonating him and making approaches to players over transfer deals and wages.

The club are furious, not least because it puts their whole financial operation at risk, and have released an explosive club statement, telling the Wilcox imposter to “get a life”.

It read: “Gainsborough Trinity Football Club is aware of an individual who has been contacting a number of players across different levels of the non-league game, falsely claiming to be our first-Team manager, Russ Wilcox.

“The individual concerned has purported to represent Russ and Gainsborough Trinity FC and, in some instances, has made approaches to players involving potential transfers and wage offers.

“We want to make it categorically clear that these approaches have not been made by Russ Wilcox, nor have they been made on behalf of Gainsborough Trinity Football Club.

“We strongly condemn the actions of the individual responsible.

“Impersonating a football manager and making false representations to players is completely unacceptable and has the potential to cause unnecessary disruption and reputational damage to players, managers and clubs.

“We would like to thank the managers and club official who have contacted Russ directly after receiving or becoming aware of these approaches.

“Many have done so precisely because they know Russ, know the professional standards by which he conducts himself, and immediately recognised that this is not the manner in which he would approach or attempt to recruit a player.

“The club have made both the Northern Premier League and The Football Association aware of these incidents.

"Finally, to the person responsible: enough is enough.

“Stop impersonating our manager, stop wasting the time of players and clubs across the non-league game and, quite frankly, get a life.”

The statement is signed off by Gainsborough CEO Matt Boles.

Wilcox had a solid playing career in the Football League with the likes of Northampton, Hull and Scunthorpe, before going on to manage The Iron.

He spent time in the dugout with York City, while he spent 15 months in charge at Guiseley, from February 2022 to May 2023, when they were a National League North side.
