A new report is calling for greater diversity in NHS leadership to better reflect the communities they serve.

Progress Under Pressure: Health, which focuses on the North East, argues that the success of shifting health services from hospitals to community settings depends on having representative voices at the top.

Commissioned by executive search consultancy Castle Peak Group and produced in partnership with technology consultancy Opencast, the report draws on insights from current and former senior executives and non-executive directors across the region’s NHS trusts and health organisations.

The research identifies key barriers to greater diversity, including traditional recruitment practices, a preference for prior NHS experience, and the demanding nature of board roles.

James Carss, chief executive of Castle Peak Group, said: "There is no question that the health sector has made progress on inclusion.

"But progress has been too slow, and senior leadership remains less diverse than the communities the NHS serves."

The report calls for changes to recruitment processes and culture, including the use of diverse shortlists, interview panels, and more accessible board roles.

It also calls for reviews of job descriptions and recruitment processes, and action to make board roles more accessible.

Mr Carss said: "If we continue recruiting in the same way, we will continue to get similar outcomes.

"We need to be prepared to do things differently if we want different results."

The report notes that the complexity and public scrutiny surrounding NHS governance can discourage candidates from outside the sector, particularly those seeking their first non-executive position.

It recommends building on successful approaches already in use, such as shadow boards, mentoring, staff networks, and targeted leadership programmes.

Harry Armstrong, managing director of business development at Opencast, said: "The NHS is entering a period of profound transformation.

"Technology and new models of care will only succeed if the people making decisions understand the communities those changes are intended to serve."

He stressed the importance of inclusion and engagement, adding: "Diversity, inclusion and meaningful engagement with patients, staff and communities need to be at the heart of that transformation."

The report also points to the specific challenges facing the North East, including lower employment, higher child poverty, and lower life expectancy than England as a whole.

It concludes that NHS trusts are not simply healthcare providers but anchor institutions, with the potential to create opportunity and help shape the communities in which they operate.

The report was launched at an event at Opencast’s offices in Hoults Yard, Newcastle, with speakers including Sarat Pediredla and Odeth Richardson.
