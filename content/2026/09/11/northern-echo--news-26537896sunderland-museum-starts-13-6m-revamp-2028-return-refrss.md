A £13.6 million redevelopment is under way at Sunderland Museum & Winter Gardens.

The historic venue closed its doors to the public to begin a major two-year refurbishment led by Bellrock Consulting, appointed by Sunderland City Council in 2023 via the NEPO Framework.

The project will modernise the museum and protect more than 108,000 artefacts.

Leo Heath, associate director and lead project manager at Bellrock, said: "Managing a project of this scale, with such regional significance, presents unique logistical and engineering challenges.

"We are working within a Grade II listed building which features three distinct construction phases and methodologies alongside the bespoke Winter Gardens.

"With the last major intervention dating back to the early 2000s, much of the refurbishment will involve the building's fabric as well as incorporating modern designs to further improve Sunderland Museum’s offering to its visitors."

The project is supported by The National Lottery Heritage Fund and match funding from Sunderland City Council, Arts Council England, the Museum, Estate and Development Fund (MEND), and The Wolfson Foundation.

Councillor Ciera Hudspith, cabinet member for culture, tourism and heritage at Sunderland City Council, said: "Sunderland Museum & Winter Gardens has been a cherished cornerstone of our community for generations.

"While we begin preparing the collections for their temporary hibernation, having Bellrock’s expert project management support and cost consultants ensures our heritage is protected, and our vision is realised for future generations."

Much of the refurbishment will involve the building’s fabric and incorporate modern designs to further improve Sunderland Museum’s offering to its visitors.

It will also protect the living botanical collection and glass dome of the Winter Gardens.

The next critical phase for Bellrock is to manage the contractor's procurement and formal appointment as part of a Pre-Construction Services Agreement.

A full reopening is planned for December 2028.
