**HARROGATE**— A class of primary school children from the district have taken part in a dedicated educational screening visit to their local opticians, following classroom observations regarding potential vision difficulties.

A group of 27 pupils from **Willow Tree Primary School** attended the locally owned**Specsavers store on Beulah Street** for comprehensive testing. The initiative was organized after teaching staff identified persistent signs of vision impairment among several students during daily classroom activities. Following the screenings, one in four participating pupils was found to require prescription glasses, while one child received a formal referral to their GP for further clinical assessment.

## Identifying the Warning Signs in the Classroom

The outing was coordinated by Emma Astin, SENDCo and Assistant Headteacher at Willow Tree Primary, who was prompted by ongoing discussions with parents regarding early development markers. National health statistics indicate that approximately one in five young children operates with an undiagnosed sight condition.

Because children rarely identify gradual changes in their own visual acuity, healthcare professionals urge parents and educators to look out for behavioral indicators. Common warning signs include frequent squinting, sitting unusually close to digital screens, regular eye rubbing, recurrent complaints of headaches, or repeatedly losing place while reading text.


**Emma Astin, SENDCo and Assistant Headteacher:**

On behalf of the pupils and our school, we greatly appreciate the wonderful team at Specsavers Harrogate for welcoming us into their store. I decided to organise this visit because, during class time, I noticed some pupils struggling to engage. As a parent, it can sometimes be easy to overlook these signs, especially since it’s not always obvious when a child needs glasses. Often, difficulties with concentration, focus, and learning can stem from vision problems, and these are more easily noticed by a teacher, who is with the children in these environments and can observe behavioural changes that can prevent students from reaching their full potential. The eye tests our pupils received were extremely valuable. We discovered that several children were unaware they needed glasses, and I genuinely believe this experience will positively influence their learning and overall school experience.


## Free NHS Provision and Community Access

Under current UK health guidelines, all children under the age of 16, alongside those in full-time education up to the age of 18, remain fully entitled to free routine NHS eye tests. Clinical bodies recommend that school-aged children undergo visual assessments annually due to the highly visual nature of modern primary and secondary curricula.


**Haroon Ashiq, Optometrist Director at Specsavers Harrogate:**

It was a pleasure to welcome Emma and her students to our store. The children were incredibly polite and a credit to Willow Tree Primary and their families. Emma genuinely understands the importance and impact of our vision, especially how it can affect a child’s confidence and their engagement in the classroom. At Specsavers, we are passionate about making eye care accessible to everyone in our community. That’s why we’re always open to having conversations with teachers and helping raise awareness among families, highlighting that even something as simple as an eye test can make a big difference in supporting their children’s learning. Our doors are always open, and now is a perfect time to schedule an appointment, particularly with children heading back to the classroom. Early detection of any concerns can help children begin their new school year on the right foot.



Following the group screening session, the Beulah Street clinical team has been welcoming the affected families back into the branch to select appropriate frames ahead of the winter term layout.
