Whorlton Bridge will reopen on September 25, nearly seven years after it was closed on safety grounds.

The bridge near Barnard Castle was shut when inspections found it could no longer safely carry vehicles or pedestrians across the River Tees.

Originally opened in 1831, the bridge was built to carry horse-drawn carts transporting coal and connected communities on either side of the River Tees for nearly two centuries.

Following its closure, Durham County Council launched an ambitious restoration programme to safeguard it.

However, as work progressed, the scheme became far more complex than originally anticipated.

  Engineering work on Whorlton Bridge during its major restoration, showing scaffolding, temporary supports and cables surrounding the historic River Tees crossing ahead of its September 25 reopening. 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

Engineers dismantled the Grade II-listed bridge piece by piece and found that about 96 per cent of its 1,800 components were beyond repair.

Rather than repairing the original parts, specialists were forced to manufacture replacements designed to match the bridge’s historic appearance while meeting modern engineering and safety standards.

The work also required special consent due to the bridge being both a listed structure and a Scheduled Monument.

Images released earlier this year showed the reconstructed deck and suspension chains back in place as the work neared completion.

The scheme received funding through the Government’s Levelling Up Fund, although the overall cost rose from an initial estimate of £8.4 million to more than £12 million because of the additional work required.

Durham County Council confirmed the reopening on September 25.

It will mark the end of a near seven-year closure and the beginning of a new chapter for one of the North East’s most important historic structures, restoring a key link between Whorlton and neighbouring communities.
