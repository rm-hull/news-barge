**HARROGATE**— Infrastructure engineering teams are scheduled to arrive on The Stray next week to begin extensive ground surveys ahead of planned modifications to Harrogate’s wastewater network.

Starting from **Thursday 17 September 2026**, Yorkshire Water will conduct targeted ground investigations to evaluate soil conditions and monitor regional groundwater tables. The operation marks the start of the local phase of a broader**£1.5 billion county-wide storm overflow reduction programme** running between 2025 and 2030.

## Three 30-Metre Boreholes Approved for Montpellier Area

The upcoming survey will involve contract partners Ward and Burke drilling three specialized boreholes—each measuring 20cm in width and penetrating 30 meters into the substrate. The specific zone earmarked for the machinery is the expanse of public parkland situated between **Montpellier Hill and the Esplanade**.

The drilling phase is programmed to last for up to six weeks. According to project documentation, the data compiled from these deep-ground samples will directly guide engineers in determining the technical layout for future permanent works intended to reduce emergency discharges into **Oak Beck** from Harrogate’s combined sewer network.

Combined storm overflows (CSOs) act as relief valves during heavy downpours, discharging heavily diluted wastewater into local rivers and streams to prevent the urban pipe system from backing up into surrounding commercial properties, residential gardens, and highways.

## Stray Reinstatement Commitments and Wider Funding

Authority project managers stated that necessary ecological and arboricultural tree-root surveys have already been completed across the site ahead of the heavy machinery arrival. The utility company has committed to completely backfilling the test holes and fully restoring the impacted grass on The Stray to its original condition once the data collection wraps up in late October.


**Athif Khan, Yorkshire Water Project Manager:**

Our storm overflows are discharging more than we, and our customers, would like, and we’re making good progress in bringing the frequency and duration of those discharges down. Last year, we saw a 24.5% reduction in discharges following the investments we’ve been making.

These boreholes are the next step in our plans to reduce discharges into Oak Beck and improve our impact on the environment. We understand how important The Stray is for our customers in Harrogate, and we’ll do everything we can to keep disruption to a minimum. Once completed, we will return The Stray to its current condition. We thank everyone in advance for their patience and understanding.



The current £1.5 billion asset upgrade builds upon a preliminary £180 million investment completed over the preceding financial years. The framework is part of a larger £8.3 billion company-wide environmental and service improvement fund, with identical storm overflow mitigation projects currently active across Leeds, Bradford, York, and Sheffield.
