A man accused of grooming, raping and sexually abusing a teenager has described the allegations as ‘sick’.

Mark Faulds allegedly used alcohol, cigarettes and cannabis to make the girl more compliant, a court heard.

The 41-year-old faced 14 charges but was formally cleared of seven after the judge ruled there was no evidence to support them. Judge Nathan Adams told the jury the teenager’s evidence that sex was consensual meant they had to return seven not-guilty verdicts.

Faulds repeatedly denied having sex with the teenager or grooming her with alcohol and drugs.

His barrister, Paul Cleasby, asked whether Faulds had ever told the teenager he fantasised about getting her into bed. He replied: “No, it’s just sick.”

Faulds denied ever having sex or any sexual contact with the teenager.

Mr Cleasby asked Faulds about a condom recovered from his home that carried both their DNA, and how it got there.

Faulds said he must have used the condom while performing a sex act on himself before the teenager later retrieved it from the bin.

Asked about condoms found in a field near his home, Faulds told the jury he used them while performing a sex act before throwing them away.

Asked how the teenager knew where to find them, Faulds said: “I ‘stupidly’ told her"

Prosecutor Annelise Haugstad had said Faulds used cigarettes, drugs and compliments to groom the teenager before the abuse escalated.

Forensic evidence heard earlier in the trial was that Faulds' DNA was found in semen on a condom in woodland, alongside traces of the teenager's DNA, although this did not prove intercourse.

The court previously heard that the teenager's aunt secretly recorded her account after the girl said she would deny it if anyone else was told.
