SKIPTON'S MP is urging Craven people to have their say on the future of transport over the next more than 20 years.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, is consulting on his Local Transport Plan until November 4.

The plan will shape transport investment and priorities - such as bus services - across the region until 2050.

People are being asked to take part in an online survey, and there will also be a consultation event at Skipton Market on Wednesday next week (September 16) between 9am and 3pm.

The Mayor's draft plan, the subject of the consultation, sets out proposals covering roads, pothole repairs, buses, rail services, active travel, road safety, accessibility, rural connectivity and links to major towns and cities across the North.

It also includes plans to improve transport access to jobs, healthcare, education and local services, particularly for people living in rural communities.

Sir Julian said: "Transport is one of the issues residents raise with me most often, whether it is concerns about potholes, bus services, road safety, access to appointments or improving rail links across our area.

"This consultation is an important opportunity for people across Skipton and Ripon to have their say on the long-term future of transport in our part of North Yorkshire.

"The decisions taken now could influence investment and transport priorities for decades to come, so I would encourage residents, businesses, community groups and local organisations to take a few minutes to complete the survey.

"Our area has unique challenges, particularly for people living in rural communities, and it is vital that their concerns are taken into account."

Mayor, Mr Skaith, says for too many people accessing everyday opportunities is harder than it needs to be because of poor transport, and doing nothing is 'not an option'.

He added: "Too many of our residents have had their freedom taken away by declining bus services. Too many friends and family members have their lives cut short or changed forever in road collisions.

"And our ever-increasing demand for travel continues to result in too many greenhouse gases being pumped into the atmosphere, affecting both the air we breathe and our long term climate.

"People tell me how they want better public transport, less congestion and a transport system that is kinder to the planet."

Consultation events are also taking place in Hawes, Richmond, Catterick Garrison, Harrogate, Helmsley, Whitby, Stokesley and Scarborough, with more in the pipeline.

To take part in the consultation, visit: [https://yorknorthyorks-ca.gov.uk/](https://yorknorthyorks-ca.gov.uk/)
