THE 9/11 terrorist attacks are a horrific memory I will never forget.

I have lived in Bradford and worked as a journalist for more than 20 years.

As an American, September 11, 2001, is a day I will never forget.

How can anyone express their feelings about an attack like this?

I remember turning the TV on and watching the scenes in New York unfold.

The pure shock of it all is hard to put in words.

  The Ground Zero memorial in New York


## 'Heartbreaking scenes'

When the first plane hit the North Tower, I thought, like millions of others, that it was a horrific accident.

About 20 minutes later, the second plane hit the South Tower and we realised this was something far more sinister.

Watching the scenes unfold was heartbreaking.

There was panic from people everywhere.

People leaping from the burning towers to escape the heat are images I will never forget.

  Visitors at the Ground Zero memorial in New York view the names of victims of the 9/11 attacks


## Actions of passengers save countless lives

The full scale of the 9/11 attacks soon became clear.

Another plane hit the Pentagon, while broadcasters reported that a fourth had crashed in a Pennsylvania field.

Later, we learned that passengers on United 93 fought back against the hijackers to stop the plane reaching its intended target, the Capitol in Washington, DC.

Their actions saved countless lives.

Firefighters and other emergency service personnel worked quickly at the World Trade Centre to try and save the thousands of people trapped or trying to escape from the towers.

  A message on display at the Ground Zero memorial in New York


## 'Outpouring of love, could not ease my grief'

I remember the shock of seeing the South Tower collapse into a vast cloud of dust and debris.

That feeling of dread only worsened when the North tower also went down.

I was abroad and remember speaking to my mum and dad afterwards.

I had to work the next day, and although I went in, my colleagues were there to support me.

I appreciated the outpouring of love, but it could not ease my grief.

## 'Hope for a better world'

The attack was felt by Americans and people around the world.

The shock of what happened is impossible to measure and reminds me of atrocities throughout history, when people have been forced to mourn the actions of aggressors.

We can hope for a better world, but in times of tragedy we must remember those who risked their lives to save others.

Even today, seeing the Twin Towers in archive footage or old films makes my heart sink.

New York’s skyline is breathtakingly beautiful in its own way – by day and at night.

The absence of the two World Trade Center buildings from that skyline is so sad.

New York has changed, and so has the world.

A memorial to the victims now stands at Ground Zero for future generations to visit.
