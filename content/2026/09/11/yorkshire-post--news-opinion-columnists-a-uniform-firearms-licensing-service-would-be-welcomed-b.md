# A uniform firearms licensing service would be welcomed by Yorkshire but recommendations miss the mark - Roger Seddon

## What do people in Helmsley and Hull have in common? One thing that is definitely not the right answer is having a firearms licensing service that is fit for purpose. This is not altogether surprising given that there are five separate and autonomous firearms licensing authorities covering the ridings and cities of Yorkshire.

The lengthily named police watch dog, His Majesty’s Inspectorate of Constabulary and Fire & Rescue Services (HMICFRS), has published a report after an inspection of licensing teams that makes 23 recommendations, which address some of the widely held concerns about the licensing regime in Britain as it stands. The trouble is, once they started writing recommendations, they couldn’t stop, resulting in a report that is a curate’s egg of extreme contrasts. To those that know firearms licensing, it is clear that some recommendations would go a long way to fix the perennial issues - while others would crash the system overnight.

The positive contribution that shooting makes to Yorkshire is immense, be it through food security and pest control, game shooting income bolstering the rural economy, or putting gold medals in the trophy cabinet; it’s crucial that we don’t let bad decision-making ruin it. This isn’t a small issue, after all, there are over 50,000 shotgun certificate holders in Yorkshire and God’s own country is considered the world over to be God’s gift to game shooting.

Whilst some of HMICFRS’s more disastrous recommendations may have grabbed the newspaper headlines, the first one they wrote down is perfectly sound: consolidate the 43 licensing departments across England and Wales into one, centrally administered and overseen body, a bit like the DVLA.

The Countryside Alliance has campaigned for this for years and it is now gathering momentum and widespread support, from several policing bodies, ex-top cops and other organisations that represent legitimate gun owners. It would deliver consistent and efficient licensing that keeps the public safe and provides a good service to gun owners, irrespective of which police for the area they live in, be they in Helmsley or Hull.

However, at least five of the recommendations would be totally disastrous, and that is not hyperbole. HMICFRS suggest licensing the 1.3 million shotguns in the same, intensely bureaucratic way as high-powered rifles. If they did this, all licensing departments would collapse overnight; after all, many of them cannot cope with their current admin burden and this move would triple that workload in an instant. The question that this raises is: what problem are they trying to fix? Suitability checks are already the same for shotgun and firearms owners; when legally held shotguns are used nefariously, the cause is police forces not applying the legislation consistently and as intended.

Other recommendations are cast in this same die - trying to fix a problem that doesn’t exist. Face-to-face medicals and competency tests are other examples where the justification HMICFRS gives is that other countries do it, so we should follow suit. Where is the evidence that people are being hurt or killed because these requirements are not in place? If there were any, HMICFRS would have given it.

The frustration, for those of us that want to see meaningful and effective change, is that in amongst a little wheat HMICFRS has added a hefty dose of chaff, which the government must now thrash out to reach good policy decisions.

Roger Seddon is shooting campaign manager at the Countryside Alliance.
