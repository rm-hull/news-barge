North Yorkshire Police has responded to questions about why more serious charges were not brought following the death of Bailey Chadwick.

The 19-year-old died following an incident on the B6265 at Lupton Bank, near Pateley Bridge, during the early hours of Sunday 20th July 2025.

Court proceedings relating to Bailey’s death concluded last week, prompting questions from members of the public about the charges brought in the case.

Police said its investigation involved witness accounts, CCTV enquiries, forensic examinations, digital evidence, expert reports and suspect interviews.

![](https://mmo.aiircdn.com/409/687f37f6d0f61.jpg)


The evidence was then reviewed by the Crown Prosecution Service, which is responsible for making charging decisions in serious cases.

A police spokesperson said:

“It is not possible for the police or CPS to simply select the most serious offence available.

“Every criminal offence has specific legal legislation that must be proven.”


Prosecutors must consider whether there is enough evidence to provide a realistic prospect of conviction, including what actions can be attributed to a suspect and what intent can be proven.

Police said this can create a gap between public expectations and the charges which the available evidence and legal thresholds allow.

The spokesperson added:

“While many people may feel a more serious offence should be brought, charging decisions must be based on evidence and legal tests rather than public opinion, speculation or emotion.”


Police also stressed that the courts, rather than officers or prosecutors, are responsible for determining guilt and imposing sentences.

The force said it recognised that not everyone would agree with the outcome of the case and added that its thoughts remained with Bailey’s family and everyone affected by his death.
