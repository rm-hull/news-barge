A BT street hub offering free Wi-Fi and phone services in Teesside has been rejected over fears it could fuel anti-social behaviour.

Stockton Borough Council rejected the nearly three-metre-high digital unit planned for Yarm Lane on September 1 after objections from police, community safety teams and nearby businesses.

Planning officers said the hub’s free Wi-Fi, phone calls, digital information and illuminated advertising could increase crime and anti-social behaviour in an already vulnerable area.

  A BT street hub. 

*(Image: BT)*

The council also said the structure would be visually intrusive for the surrounding area.

The applicant - telecommunications company BT - planned to build the hub on a widened pavement outside a row of local businesses, including a dance school, shops and restaurants.

Stockton Council’s community safety team said the area had a long-standing anti-social behaviour problem and feared the hub could undermine efforts to cut crime across Teesside.

Cleveland Police warned that the hub could become a focus for crime and disorder.

The force said 734 crimes, including violence, theft, robbery and drug offences, were recorded within half a mile of the site between June and July 2026.

Police also cited similar problems at other regional hubs, including one in Middlesbrough town centre allegedly used to arrange drug deals through its free phone service.

Six more objections came from local residents and business owners.

Sharon Donnelly, of Stockton Decorators Merchants, said: "After more than 40 years in business, the area of Brunswick Street, Skinner Street and Hartington Road has really, sadly, been left to decline by Stockton Council in my opinion and we need no further opportunities for it to decline any further."

Karen Heritage, who runs a nearby shop and the dance school, said crowds gathering where children are often present could be dangerous.

Local resident Joe Fraser added: "Allowing planning consent would result in a significant detriment to the local amenity, a significant adverse effect on local businesses due to reduced custom, and a knock-on-effect on employment with a risk of a loss of jobs."

BT argued that the hub would improve connectivity and tackle digital exclusion in the area with free Wi-Fi, phone calls and better mobile coverage.

  BT has over 900 street hubs across the UK. 

*(Image: BT/PA Wire)*

The company said more than 900 street hubs had been successfully installed across the UK as part of a plan to modernise outdated telecommunications infrastructure.

It also submitted a plan setting out how it would work with councils and police to tackle anti-social behaviour and misuse.

However, planning officers said the benefits did not outweigh concerns raised by police, safety teams, and local businesses.

A similar proposal about 90 metres away had already been refused by Stockton Council and rejected on appeal.

BT plans for similar street hubs in Redcar and Eston were also rejected after concerns about crime, anti-social behaviour and their impact on the surrounding areas.

Plans submitted on September 4 for another hub in Guisborough have yet to be decided by Redcar and Cleveland Borough Council.

The proposal formed part of BT's plans to replace old phone boxes at Yarm Lane and Oxbridge Lane with hubs offering free Wi-Fi, free calls and access to emergency services.
