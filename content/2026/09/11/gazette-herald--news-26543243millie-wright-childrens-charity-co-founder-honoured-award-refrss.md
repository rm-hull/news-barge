The co-founder of a York charity who transformed her unimaginable grief into lasting support for children with life-threatening illnesses has been honoured with an award.

Ceri Wright is an extraordinary individual whose strength, compassion and determination turned the loss of her daughter Millie into the creation of the Millie Wright Children’s Charity.

She was awarded the Outstanding Contribution Award at York Community Pride in a ceremony held at York Racecourse on September 10.

Speaking of Ceri on the night, presenter Duncan Wood said: “Ceri has become a beacon of hope for children and families facing some of life’s most devastating circumstances.”

In March 2021, Ceri’s daughter Millie became seriously unwell.

Despite multiple attempts to seek help during Covid restrictions, it was only through Ceri’s instinct and persistence that Millie received blood tests which revealed acute liver failure.

What followed was a relentless and heart-breaking journey: a life-saving liver transplant, followed by the devastating diagnosis of Acute Hepatitis Associated Aplastic Anaemia — an incredibly rare condition affecting only around one in a million people.

Throughout months in intensive care, Millie endured intense treatment: dialysis, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and eventually a stem cell transplant.

Ceri stood beside her daughter every step of the way, advocating fiercely for her care while witnessing the unimaginable.

Despite moments of hope, Millie tragically passed away aged just 13 after complications from infection.

In the face of such profound loss, many would understandably retreat. Ceri instead chose to act.

Determined that no other family should experience the same lack of support she encountered, she helped found the Millie Wright Children’s Charity in Millie’s memory.

The charity addresses a critical and often overlooked gap: support for children with life-threatening, non-cancer conditions, particularly those with liver, renal, and haematological illnesses.

Ceri recognised a stark inequality — while families of children with cancer often receive structured support, those facing other critical illnesses frequently do not. She has made it her mission to change this.

Through the charity, Ceri has provided financial support for families travelling long distances for vital treatment, delivered practical help during hospital stays – including meals and essential supplies – offered emotional and bereavement support, and raised awareness about the inequality in healthcare support systems.

She continues to campaign for fairer support for all seriously ill children, not just those with a cancer diagnosis.

Alongside this, Ceri actively fosters community healing. Through events, remembrance evenings, and her own therapeutic work, she has created safe and supportive spaces where families can connect, reflect, and rebuild.

Speaking about her award, Ceri said: “Through Nige, the charity trustees and those who have helped us during fundraisers, we have helped support so many children.

“The charity means that families of children with life-threatening illnesses have the emotional wellbeing and support that I never had – and that’s Millie’s legacy.”
