**WESTMINSTER**— A fresh legislative bid to legalise assisted dying in England and Wales has failed to become law this afternoon, following a historic and highly charged free vote in the House of Commons.The

**Terminally Ill Adults (End of Life) Bill**, brought forward by Labour MP Lauren Edwards, was narrowly defeated at its Second Reading by a majority of 16 votes. A total of

**286 MPs voted against the legislation, compared to 270 voting in favour**, halting the bill’s parliamentary progression and ensuring the practice remains illegal under the Suicide Act 1961.

## An Emotional, Personal Speech on the Commons Floor

The decisive vote followed hours of intense debate, featuring a prominent, deeply personal speech from Harrogate and Knaresborough MP **Tom Gordon**. The Liberal Democrat representative drew directly on two traumatic deaths within his own family history to argue against maintaining the current legal framework.

Gordon told MPs about his mother’s uncle, who was terminally ill and cared for by his mother in his final months. It was Gordon’s mother who ultimately discovered him deceased on the floor of his home, positioned as if he had been trying to reach the opposite side of the room where his shotgun was kept.

He further shared the account of a former neighbour, a local pig farmer, who was experiencing severe pain from a terminal illness. Gordon recalled hearing a loud bang from across the road on a summer’s day during his youth, running over with his grandmother, and discovering the man dead.


**Tom Gordon MP:**

I will never forget that.



Gordon asserted that while he could not confirm if either man would have explicitly opted for an assisted death under different laws, they deserved better than what happened to them. He characterized violent, isolated end-of-life occurrences like these as the current status quo for individuals facing unbearable suffering.

## A Mother’s Fight With Cancer

The Harrogate MP also spoke intimately about his mother, a single parent who survived two separate breast cancer tumours and is currently in remission. Gordon described how she lives with the constant anxiety of a potential recurrence, and noted the comfort she and her local cancer support group find in the concept of having personal control over their final medical choices.


**Tom Gordon MP:**

I had to face the conversation with my mum about what would happen to my younger sister, who was only five or six at the time, if my mum – a single parent didn’t survive. That is not a conversation I will ever forget, and it’s one of the things that motivates me to speak on this issue as often, and as prominently, as I do.



Gordon, who previously worked on the Bill Committee during an earlier parliamentary session, revealed he frequently called his mother for guidance regarding the legislation.


**Tom Gordon MP:**

For my mum, she’s made clear that if her breast cancer were to return, she wouldn’t want to go through the ordeal and trauma of surgery and chemotherapy again. She already lives with lasting nerve damage and a reduced quality of life as a result of the cancer she’s already had.



## The Legislative Divide: Arguments For and Against

The proposed framework sought to grant mentally competent adults diagnosed with fewer than six months to live the statutory right to request medical assistance to end their lives, subject to independent two-doctor verification and expert panel oversight. The debate exposed deep divisions across all major political benches.

### Key Arguments in Support:

- **Alleviation of Suffering:** Offering a compassionate exit for patients enduring intractable physical and emotional distress that specialized palliative care cannot relieve.
- **Bodily Autonomy:** Ensuring mentally capable adults retain the legal right to dictate the timing and manner of their own deaths.
- **Ending the Legal “Postcode Lottery”:** Creating a regulated domestic framework to replace the dangerous trend of individuals traveling to overseas clinics like Dignitas.
- **Healthcare Allocation:** Lowering long-term invasive medical interventions for patients who explicitly choose to conclude their treatment paths.

### Key Arguments in Opposition:

- **Societal Coercion Risks:** Concerns that vulnerable, elderly, or disabled individuals could feel an institutional or familial pressure to choose death to avoid being a physical or financial burden.
- **The “Slippery Slope”:** Fears that initial legal definitions and safeguards would be steadily expanded over time by the courts or through anti-discrimination lawsuits.
- **Deflecting from Palliative Deficits:** Arguments that passing an assisted option while regional hospice and palliative care networks face severe underfunding creates a false, compromised choice.
- **Medical Ethics:** Resistance to involving healthcare professionals in actively prescribing life-ending substances, altering the core life-preservation mandate of medical staff.

## “The Campaign Continues”: Gordon Vows to Fight On

Following the afternoon’s narrow result, Tom Gordon joined parliamentary colleagues and cross-party campaigners on Parliament Green to reassure supporters that the campaign for end-of-life reform would proceed despite the legislative setback.

Standing alongside fellow Liberal Democrat MPs Vikki Slade and Freddie Van Mierlo, Gordon insisted that an amended version of the framework can eventually be brought back before the house.


**Tom Gordon MP:**

This conversation is going in one direction around the world: towards giving people autonomous choice. This isn’t a conversation that started with this Bill in parliament; we’ve been having it as a country for generations. It’s not the end. The campaign continues.



Because the Private Members’ Bill failed to clear its Second Reading hurdle, the legislation will not advance any further in the current parliamentary session.
