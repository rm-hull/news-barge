## York firm's research project aims to boost UK fruit production

AgriSound of York is working on how to use bees to boost pollination at fruit farms. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

A new research project is aiming to transform pollination in UK fruit production.

Led by AgriSound in collaboration with the James Hutton Institute and James Hutton Ltd, the project began on July 1 and will run to June 30, 2028.

It is backed by Defra’s Farming Innovation Programme in partnership with Innovate UK.

Titled Precision Pollination to Accelerate Breeding and Boost Productivity in Fruit Production, the project seeks to make pollination measurable, manageable, and more resilient for commercial growers.

  



Dr Casey Woodward, CEO of AgriSound, said: "Pollination is mission-critical to fruit production, but it is still too often managed by assumption.

"We are very happy to be working with JHL and JHI in this project to support not just fruit producers but also breeders.

  The Polly device from AgriSound. 

*(Image: Pic supplied)*

"Our goal is to make precision pollination scalable across crops and useful as a day-to-day production tool."

The project will build on AgriSound’s existing technology, which uses Polly bioacoustic sensors and AI analytics to monitor pollinator activity within crops.

The system currently focuses on bumblebees in strawberries, but the new research will expand monitoring to include honeybees, bumblebees, solitary bees and hoverflies across fruit crops including strawberries, blueberries, raspberries, blackberries, and cherries.

Dr Susan McCallum, soft fruit geneticist and breeder at the James Hutton Institute, said: "We are excited to be working with Agrisound on this innovative project, combining cutting-edge technology with data driven insight to better understand pollination and its impact on soft fruit productivity.

"This collaboration has the potential to deliver valuable, real-world insights that will help growers and breeders make more informed decisions and build a more resilient and sustainable future for fruit production."

The project will also investigate how flower traits and plant genotypes influence pollinator attraction and pollination efficiency, supporting both growers and breeders with practical, data-driven insights.
