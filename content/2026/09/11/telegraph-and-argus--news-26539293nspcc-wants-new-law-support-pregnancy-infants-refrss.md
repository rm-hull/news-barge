THE earliest years of a child’s life are a time of great opportunity, but also a time of great vulnerability. Especially as they are completely dependent on the adults around them to keep them safe.

Last year, the NSPCC Helpline - which is a free and confidential way to raise concerns about a child’s safety or wellbeing - referred almost 3,700 children aged two and under to local safeguarding agencies.

Some of the main reasons for referrals between April 2025 and March 2026, included sexual abuse and exploitation, emotional abuse, domestic abuse, physical abuse.

But the highest reason by far for referrals being made by the NSPCC Helpline to local safeguarding teams related to concerns about a child experiencing or at risk of neglect, with almost half (1,661) being based on this topic.

One neighbour who contacted the NSPCC Helpline with their concerns said: “I’m worried about the two-year-old next door. I hardly ever see her, but I do hear her screaming endlessly and no-one consoles her.

“I know crying is normal - it’s the parents’ other behaviour that gives me further concern. They are shouting at each other at all hours, shouting at the young girl, slamming doors, well past midnight. I don’t know if there is physical abuse going on as well, but I think there could be.”

Around one in six referrals from our Helpline to local safeguarding teams, like police or children’s social care, relate to children aged two and under, and would only be made when a practitioner believes there’s a serious concern about a child or family and they need urgent support.

The NSPCC is here to help children, babies and families across the country. Sometimes that means speaking directly to them, and sometimes that involves influencing the country’s decision-makers on their behalf.

That is why we urged MPs on both sides of Parliament to consider and vote for the Infants, Parents and Carers private members’ Bill.

The Bill aims to make support during pregnancy and the first two years of a child’s life a legal requirement across England.

It seeks to make sure professionals assess the needs of babies, parents and carers, make sure they have the right information and support, and ensure all babies receive the support they need in their first 1,001 from services such as health visiting, family hubs and perinatal mental health support.

Abuse and neglect can be devastating, and experiencing it at such a young age can cast a long shadow and affect children’s physical and emotional health, their learning and their capacity to form positive relationships throughout their lives.

The first two years of a child’s life play an enormous part in shaping their lifelong development and wellbeing, but for too many families their postcode can dictate the help they receive.

We want to ensure every baby and infant, regardless of where they live, gets a safe and nurturing start in life.

Kirsty (not her real name), accessed the NSPCC’s Pregnancy in Mind service while living in Yorkshire.

She said: “I was terrified when I first became pregnant. I had suffered with anxiety for quite a long time but had learnt to manage my mental wellbeing as an adult, yet finding out I was pregnant ramped up my anxiety into full gear.

“I suffered from panic attacks, palpitations and sometimes felt afraid to leave my home or go to work. So, I knew I needed help.

“When my midwife referred me to the NSPCC’s Pregnancy in Mind programme I was initially nervous to go, but being in a room full of people in the same kind of position as you made me feel normal for once. I could talk about my experience openly in a safe environment.

“I firmly believe that it’s so important that all parents are offered this support before children are brought into the world, so they can be happy and healthy parents.

“I feel fortunate to have had access to a service such as this and think it would be fantastic for services that offer help to new parents to be standard for everybody that was pregnant, and beyond - whether you are struggling with your mental health, or just need a helping hand.”

We hope the Bill passes and helps families across England during those critical early years, but the NSPCC is here to help parents, families, babies and children whenever they need support.

And if you have concerns about the safety or wellbeing of a child, the NSPCC Helpline - 0808 800 5000 - can help ensure they get the support they need.
