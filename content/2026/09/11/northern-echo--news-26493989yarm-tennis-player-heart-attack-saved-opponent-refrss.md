A tennis player who suffered a heart attack while on court has praised his opponent who speedily sought a defibrillator to save his life.

Bryan Wrenn, 61, was playing in a match for Yarm Tennis Club against Saltburn on Tuesday, August 11, when he collapsed to the floor.

Despite his teammates and his opponents looking on in panic, Bryan, from Yarm, only recalls waking up in an ambulance on his way to North Tees Hospital.

But, luckily for him, one of his opponents from Saltburn had taken first aid and defibrillator training days earlier, helping to save the tennis player's life.

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

"It wasn't a particularly tough match; it was good-spirited and wasn't stressful in any way." Mr Wrenn, an online teacher, said.

"I was running quite a bit, but I didn't feel dizzy or feel pain in my chest or anything."

"All I remember is waking up in the ambulance and feeling okay and wondering what had happened and then was told that I'd keeled over on the tennis court."

Mr Wrenn later found out he'd been saved on the court by his opponent and a defibrillator bought by Yarm Tennis Club about ten years ago after a fundraising effort.

"They got the defibrillator on me in around three minutes, and my wife, who is a nurse, told me that if it was over four minutes, you start to lose brain function," he added.

"By the time I got to North Tees Hospital, I was fully awake, I didn't feel bad, and I was ready to go home."

  Bryan Wrenn, pictured with his family, Wife Mellany and children Elizabeth Amara, 9, and Alexandra Achillies, 6, and members of Yarm Tennis Club. Bryan had a heart attack whilst playing a game and was saved by the defibrillator and an opponent 

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

At the hospital, the 61-year-old was fitted with the same heart monitoring device as footballer Christian Eriksen, who collapsed on the pitch for Denmark.

Mr Wrenn was also told that he couldn't play tennis for six weeks and was told not to drive for six months.

He also has to have regular check-ups, heart monitoring and was told to scale back on stressful activities.

But he remains upbeat, and has expressed his delight that his tennis club had a defibrillator and that his opponent was trained in first aid.

  Bryan Wrenn, pictured with his family, Wife Mellany and children Elizabeth Amara, 9, and Alexandra Achillies, 6, and members of Yarm Tennis Club. Bryan had a heart attack whilst playing a game and was saved by the defibrillator and an opponent 

*(Image: NORTHERN ECHO)*

"I'm so glad that there was the equipment on hand, and that my opponent knew what they were doing; he helped save my life."

While Bryan doesn't recall what happened during the incident, his tennis partner during the game, Martin Williams, 71, has described Mr Wrenn's collapse as a "scary experience".

Mr Williams, who had his back to his partner heard a "scuffle of shoes" then turned and saw people running over to Bryan.

"I noticed Bryan in a heap and people rushing over to him; no one knew what had happened," he said.

"One person then went for the defibrillator, another rang 999; it all happened so fast."

Bryan received two shocks in total after he stopped breathing.

"The defibrillator talked him through the whole process and analysed withere Brayn was breathing and told the person when to shock Bryan," Mr Williams added.

"Two police officers then arrived on the court and confirmed that the Saltburn player was doing everything correctly, and then 30 seconds later, an ambulance arrived to take Bryan to hospital.

"I'm convinced that without that intervention from the defibrillator, Bryan might not be here now."

Martin, who is part of the Yarm Tennis Club committee, says this incident shows that all sports and leisure clubs should have a defibrillator and called on clubs to acquire or fundraise for them "as soon as possible".

"It was vital in this scenario and unthinkable what would have happened without it," he said.
