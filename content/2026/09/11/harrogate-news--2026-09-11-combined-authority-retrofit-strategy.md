**HARROGATE**— A new infrastructure policy blueprint addressing building energy efficiency and greenhouse gas targets across York and North Yorkshire has been formally adopted by cabinet members today.

The **Retrofit Strategy** establishes a long-term administrative framework intended to coordinate future actions between the Combined Authority, local district councils, and the regional construction sector. The document outlines a plan to transition older properties away from fossil fuels by encouraging the commercial use of low-carbon technologies, such as solar panels, air-source heat pumps, and updated insulation systems.

## Authority Targets and Projected Metrics

The strategy, which follows a public consultation period conducted last year, sets out several specific statistical milestones that the authority aims to achieve over the coming decade as part of its stated regional environmental goals:

- **Residential Targets:** An authority ambition to see 250,000 homes reach an Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) rating of C or higher by 2038.
- **Commercial Targets:** A target to modify more than 62% of existing business and commercial premises within the same timeframe.

York and North Yorkshire Mayor **David Skaith** stated that the policy provides a clear framework to address the region’s older housing stock, noting that more than a quarter of local homes were built over a century ago. According to the mayoral office, the plan is designed to upgrade buildings while targeting fuel poverty and supporting local supply chains.

## Workforce Development and Initial Pilots

Representatives from the Federation of Master Builders have noted that the practical application of the strategy will require significant long-term investment in upskilling regional tradespeople and training the next generation entering the construction industry to ensure work is completed to standard.

The policy is also supported by the York and North Yorkshire Housing Partnership. Speaking on the release, partnership representatives characterized the strategy as an economic framework to make older housing association properties more affordable to heat.

While the broader policy document was formally finalized today, initial localized trials funded through the authority’s Net Zero Fund have already commenced. At Honeysuckle House, an independent living facility managed by the City of York Council, the installation of an updated heating system reportedly lowered winter monthly fuel costs for participating residents from £100 down to £60.
