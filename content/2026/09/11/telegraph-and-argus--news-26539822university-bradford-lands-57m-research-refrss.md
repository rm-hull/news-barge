THE University of Bradford has secured more than £57 million in research and innovation funding over the past five years.

The funding is supporting projects in cancer treatment, artificial intelligence, healthcare, and new materials and technologies, with more than £8 million secured for the most recent academic year.

Professor Sherif El Khamisy, pro-vice-chancellor for research, innovation and global engagement, said: "Research is most powerful when it improves lives.

"These investments are supporting work that tackles some of society's biggest challenges, from children's health and cancer to artificial intelligence, transport and healthcare.

"What is particularly pleasing is the breadth of expertise being recognised, which feeds into our commitment to economic regeneration and upskilling the workforce.

"These projects demonstrate how research at the University of Bradford is creating new knowledge, supporting innovation and delivering impact locally, nationally and internationally."

The funding has come from organisations including the National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), Innovate UK, UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), charities, and industry partners.

It has enabled a wide range of research and innovation projects, including more than £1.4 million to evaluate the long-term impact of the Creating Active Schools programme in Bradford primary schools.

The scheme aims to address health inequalities by improving physical activity and has been shortlisted for a Times Higher Education Award.

Researchers in the School of Optometry and Vision Science have received nearly £890,000 from the Medical Research Council (MRC) to develop new methods for early detection of glaucoma-related retinal damage, with the goal of preventing avoidable sight loss.

More than £216,000 from the NIHR has gone towards OrQA-UK, an AI-enabled organ assessment tool designed to improve the use of donated organs and help more patients receive life-saving transplants.

Cancer research has also benefited, with funding supporting work on antibody-drug conjugates for breast cancer and new approaches to prostate cancer treatment and screening.

This includes £188,039 from Neuroblastoma UK for brain cancer research, £185,167 from Worldwide Cancer Research for breast cancer therapies, and £12,450 from Animal Free Research UK for prostate cancer studies.

The University has also secured more than £1.1 million through an NIHR Mental Health Research Leader Award, supporting improved understanding and future capacity in mental health research.

Other funded projects include research into childcare reform, financial wellbeing, pregnancy loss support, precision medicine, and drug testing technologies.
