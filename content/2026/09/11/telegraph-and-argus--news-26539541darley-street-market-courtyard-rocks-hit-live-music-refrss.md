ONE year on from opening, Darley Street Market’s outdoor space was transformed into a live music venue for the inaugural Live at the Courtyard line-up of concerts.

Now the new music festival has been hailed a huge success, with more than 1,200 tickets sold and concert-goers praising the artists, atmosphere and city centre venue.

Across three days music fans enjoyed headline performances from Leeds-Bradford rock favourites Pulled Apart By Horses, singer-songwriter and former X Factor star Lucy Spraggan, and million-selling indie band The Hoosiers, alongside a line-up of supporting artists including Meryl Streek, Lucy Robinson and Marie Franc.

  Meryl Streek works up the crowd


The event showcased the versatility of Darley Street Market, with audiences enjoying live music alongside food from the Street Kitchen and drinks from the Northern Monk bar. Music fans gathered in the Courtyard and on the Street Kitchen balcony to enjoy the performances in a vibrant, welcoming atmosphere.

  Marie Franc on stage. Pic: Bradford Markets


The mini-festival built on the market’s growing reputation for live music, combining nationally recognised acts with talented local performers across a range of musical styles and genres to appeal to audiences of all ages.

The Friday night opening concert saw Meryl Streek deliver a high-energy support set before Pulled Apart By Horses treated fans to a mix of old favourites and new material, including First World Problems and Ride or Die, finishing with a pair of crowd-pleasing covers.

Illustrator Asma Ali also captured highlights of the evening through a special live illustration station.

Local artist Lucy Robinson warmed up Saturday evening’s audience with a captivating performance with her band before Lucy Spraggan delivered a packed 70-minute set. Mixing audience requests with fan favourites, Spraggan performed tracks such as Loaded Gun and Jeremy Kyle, delighting the crowd.

  Lucy Spraggan in the Courtyard. Photo: Sarah Robinson/sarah in the pit 

*(Image: Sarah Robinson)*

Sunday’s finale began with an impressive set from Marie Franc, featuring tracks including Warthog, Pendle Hill and Vauxhall Casanova.

Then The Hoosiers brought the weekend to a memorable close with an energetic set featuring hits including Worried About Ray and Making a Monster, alongside cover versions of Pink Pony Club and Girls Just Want to Have Fun.

The crowd sang along as the band ended with their best-known hit, Goodbye Mr A.

Bradford Council says feedback from those attending has been “overwhelmingly positive”.

One concert-goer said: “We had the most lovely time - lunch at the Darley Street Market, followed by a really fun gig watching The Hoosiers with plenty of time to enjoy singing along.”

Another added: “Great energy from the bands and they got really involved with the crowd.”

Others praised the organisation, venue and atmosphere:”Really well organised. Super friendly staff and clientele.”

“Great stage set up, sound, lighting, bar area and organisation.” “Fantastic venue and staff, really enjoyed the show.”

The positive reaction was echoed across social media, with attendees describing the event as a “fabulous way to spend a Sunday afternoon”, while others said it was “great for Bradford to have a new venue” and praised the Courtyard as a “lovely venue for a gig, nice atmosphere, family friendly, top music” as well as being “great space and really well run event - the sound was perfect”.

Alongside the concert programme, the Courtyard also hosted the second Bantams in the Courtyard FanZone of the current season, helping to create one of the busiest weekends since the market opened.

Says a Bradford Markets spokesperson: “Live at the Courtyard has been in the planning for a long time, and this first gig series was designed to bring more headline acts to the city centre, as well as being a showcase for local acts, using Bradford and West Yorkshire businesses to provide the infrastructure.

“This first year was a test case to see how it would work in the Courtyard and we were delighted that it all worked so well, and that the reaction has been so positive.”

Cllr Ian Walker, Bradford Council’s Executive Member for Neighbourhoods and Communities, said: “It’s fantastic to see Live at The Courtyard get off to such a successful start. The combination of nationally recognised artists, talented local performers, great food and drink, and such a positive atmosphere created a memorable weekend for everyone who attended.

“It’s been wonderful to see so many people enjoying the space and supporting Bradford’s live music scene, and we look forward to exploring opportunities to build on this success in the future.”
