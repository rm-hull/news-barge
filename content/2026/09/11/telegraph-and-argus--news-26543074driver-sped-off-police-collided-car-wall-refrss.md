A driver who sped off from police after being signalled to stop collided with a car and a wall before jumping from his Ford Fiesta and running off.

Haden Walsh had earlier filled a jerry can with fuel before telling petrol station staff that he had no money and leaving.

Bradford Crown Court heard how Walsh, 35, formerly of Ladstone Towers, Sowerby Bridge, had taken the fuel from a service station in Huddersfield at about 6.25pm on March 22 last year.

When confronted by staff he swore and was abusive.

Four hours later his car was spotted by police on Halifax Road in Todmorden.

It was followed and signalled to stop but Walsh ignored the blue lights and sirens and instead drove off.

He turned onto a narrow street and, pursued by the police car, crashed into a car and a drystone wall before being arrested.

On June 18 police went to a reported collision in Todmorden.

Walsh said he had been a passenger in a VW Golf with false plates.

However, CCTV from the scene showed he had been the driver.

He was arrested at the scene.

Walsh was also dealt with for two shop thefts, on July 24 and 27 this year, when he stole beer and food from Morrisons in Todmorden, and beer and food from B&M in Sowerby Bridge.

He later pleaded guilty to dangerous driving, making off without payment, two shop thefts, driving whilst disqualified, driving without due care and attention, having no insurance or test certificate, and forging a registration mark by displaying false plates.

The court heard that Walsh had 14 previous convictions for 35 offences including theft and driving offences.

In mitigation the court heard that Walsh, who appeared in court via video link from HMP Leeds, had left home at 16 and had been trying to find his way in life ever since.

He had been without family support, he was isolated, and his life had been blighted by mental health problems for which he had spent a significant period in hospital.

Sentencing Walsh, His Honour Judge Stephen Earl described "a litany of offences" over an 18-month period of which dangerous driving was the most serious.

He said: "An accident was caused at least once if not twice in relation to a parked vehicle and a wall.

Those are matters which are deeply concerning because it shows just how clearly you were unable to drive that vehicle as a result of your circumstances."

He sentenced Walsh to a total of 12 months' imprisonment suspended for two years, banned him from driving for three years and ordered him to take an extended re-test before driving again.

He told Walsh: "In terms of disqualification, I do accept that everyone should have a light at the end of the tunnel but frankly you have had more tunnels than I have had hot breakfasts."

He also ordered him to pay compensation of £69.24 to the shops from which he stole items.

He must also complete up to 30 rehabilitation activity requirement days.
