A retired North Yorkshire firefighter has recalled the “surreal” experience of seeing Ground Zero just days after the 11 September 2001 terrorist attacks.

Bruce Reid, now 60, flew to New York in the immediate aftermath of the atrocity to attend funerals for lost emergency workers.

Bruce served in the North Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service for nearly 36 years across stations including York, Acomb, Harrogate, and Scarborough.

On the day of the attacks 25 years ago today, he was working at his side hustle as a decorator when he heard the news.

“I felt absolutely compelled to go,” he said. “As soon as I knew 343 firefighters had died doing their job, I just felt utterly compelled that I had to go.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/bruce-reid-911-colleagues.webp)

and Phil Wheelhouse. Photograph: Bruce Reid

Bruce made contact with New York Fire Department headquarters in Brooklyn. He was put in touch with Rick Steinberg, an officer responsible for informing firehouses about fallen personnel and funeral arrangements.

Because so many firefighters had been killed and their surviving colleagues were working continuous recovery shifts at Ground Zero, funeral attendance across the city was unusually low.

Mr Reid was invited over and asked to attend some service. He stayed in a New York fire house.

After asking his colleagues in North Yorkshire if anyone wanted to take part, three firefighter colleagues joined him in funding themselves to the US.

Upon arriving in New York, the group was taken to a firehouse in Far Rockaway, Queens, where they were welcomed by local crews.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/bruce-reid-911-firefighter-uniforms.webp)

Over the following days, the group attended multiple services across the city, including a funeral at St Patrick’s Cathedral on Fifth Avenue and a memorial service at Yankee Stadium.

“To be there to see the emotions with not just the firefighters but the whole of New York, it was just a real moving experience,” he said.

“You felt proud to be there to stand shoulder to shoulder with New York firemen, and just to be allowed to be welcomed into that brotherhood.”

Bruce added: “I’ve always looked at the fire service as one big brotherhood, whether it’s in North Yorkshire or West Yorkshire or nationally, and to feel as if you had that international brotherhood feeling as well”.

During their trip, the group visited Ground Zero a number of times.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/bruce-reid-911-ground-zero-2.webp)

“The enormity of it is just surreal,” he said. “You just think that this is something of some sort of elaborate film set. Although you knew what had happened, it was just very difficult to comprehend”.

Bruce said that while at the site, the firefighters were offered equipment to help search through the rubble, but he declined.

“I felt as if that wasn’t what we went to do,” he said.

To mark the 25th anniversary, Bruce has been looking back through his photographs of the trip.

He is planning a return trip to New York with his wife between Christmas and New Year, where he hopes to revisit the Far Rockaway firehouse and attempt to track down contacts he made during his time there.
