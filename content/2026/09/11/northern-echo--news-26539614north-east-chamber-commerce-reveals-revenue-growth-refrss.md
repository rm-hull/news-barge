A NORTH East business group has reported a rise in revenue, with support to more than 3,500 companies.

The North East Chamber of Commerce said revenue increased to £3.1 million in the year to December 31, 2025, supported by year-on-year growth across all revenue-generating departments.

This success comes despite what the chamber has described as a "challenging" economic climate.

During its Annual General Meeting at Newcastle Cathedral on September 8, members also elected Natasha McDonough, founder and managing director of MMC Research, as the chamber’s new president.

She succeeds Deborah Walton of Palintest, who completed her two-year term.

Louise Hunter of Northumbrian Water was appointed vice president.

John McCabe, chief executive of the chamber, said: "The impressive increases we were seeing in our income streams were simply being outpaced by rising costs.

"By the midway point in the year, we took the decision to enter a consultation with staff which saw four senior management roles being made redundant.

"Redundancy packages added to the costs incurred in 2025 but this decision to reduce our headcount in a limited way has contributed significantly to a much-improved cost base in 2026.

"Having taken this step and despite the economic headwinds, the fundamentals of the Chamber’s business model remained strong and have continued to improve in 2026."

Rising costs, including higher employer national insurance contributions, led to a net operating loss of £315,000, though this was anticipated and covered by cash reserves.

The chamber reported net assets of £4.25 million, boosted by investments in its Newcastle and Durham offices.

The refurbished Newcastle office is now fully tenanted and generated more than £400,000 in revenue last year. The Durham office refurbishment is nearing completion.

The chamber also placed £1 million into long-term investment funds, which has already delivered a significant return on investment.

It achieved Living Wage accreditation at the end of 2025.

Mr McCabe said: "Last year we took difficult decisions to control our costs but remained focused on the delivery of our long-term objectives of membership growth and diversified income.

"I’m delighted to report that on the evidence of 2026 so far, the strategy is working and growth is returning to the Chamber."

The chamber supported more than 600 North East businesses with international trade in 2025, processing over 10,000 documents for imports and exports.

It also delivered phase two of the Local Skills Improvement Plan with the Tees Valley Combined Authority.

Mr McCabe said the chamber is on course to finish 2026 £22,000 ahead of budget and £250,000 ahead of 2025.
