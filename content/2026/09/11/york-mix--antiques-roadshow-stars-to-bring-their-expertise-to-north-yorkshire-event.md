Familiar faces of Sunday evening TV are bringing their expertise to a York abbey this month.

Susan Rumfitt, of the BBC *Antiques Roadshow*, will give three unique talks at the jewellery showcase, Acquire at Ampleforth, on**Friday 18 to Sunday 20 September**.

For nearly two decades viewers saw her on screen evaluating fine gems, historical heirlooms, and notable pieces like a gold bracelet belonging to Queen Victoria. Now she’s turning all of that experience into a trio of entertaining and informative talks at Ampleforth Abbey.

Susan’s talks take place as follows:

- **Friday 18 September, 11am:** Edwardian Elegance & Art Deco Freedom with a fascinating talk inspired by Edwardian and Art Deco glitz and glamour.
- **Saturday 19 September, 11am:** Cocktails and The Fashion Conscious exploring how Dior and Chanel shaped the post-Second World War fashion world.
- **Sunday 20 September, 11am:** Creative Freedom – A Jewellery Revolution looking at 1960s when jewellery design became creative and fun and how that inspired our contemporary designers.

“I’m really excited to give these Talks at Acquire Ampleforth, bringing to life the magnificent jewellery history of the 20th century,” said Susan.

“From the elegance of the Edwardian period, where lace work was a fashion must and an influence on other garland styles of jewellery, to the abundance of colour we see post World War II, these talks have something for all tastes.”

Alongside her own talks Susan has also invited fellow BBC *Antiques Roadshow* expert Steven Moore to join her in-conversation about Steven’s book ‘The Stuff of History’, on Sunday 20 September at 2p.

Described as a ‘curated compendium of curious objects and forgotten people’, Steven’s book explores overlooked historical figures and everyday objects.

Susan will also be accompanied by a couple of experts who are at the top of their field; Silversmith Brett Payne and Sarah Leighton.

Acquire at Ampleforth is a free exhibition event, but the guest talks are ticketed. Tickets for the talks can be purchased via the Ampleforth Abbey website here.
