THE Spence Building at Preston Park Museum has welcomed more than 100,000 visitors in its first year.

The Stockton building opened on September 13, 2025, and has played a key role in establishing the museum as one of the top cultural destinations in the North East.

Councillor Nigel Cooke, cabinet member for environment, leisure and culture at Stockton Borough Council, said: "One year on, The Spence Building has exceeded our expectations.

"It has enabled us to welcome new audiences, bring internationally significant exhibitions to the Tees Valley and make the museum’s collection and its stories more accessible to visitors."

Designed to expand the museum’s exhibition, learning, and events capacity, The Spence Building has delivered an ambitious programme of displays and activities.

Exhibitions have included Tracks of Change—part of the international S&DR200 Festival—and William Powell Frith’s Gateway to the World, which featured works from the Royal Collection.

The museum also showcased Corridors by artist Rebecca Louise Law, the light and sound experience Perfume by digital artist Yann Nguema, and the family-friendly All Aboard!, a train-themed playscape.

Earlier this year, the museum celebrated one of Stockton-on-Tees’ most beloved entertainers with an exhibition featuring internationally acclaimed performer La Voix.

Visitors also enjoyed Matisse: Drawing with Scissors, a Hayward Gallery Touring exhibition featuring 35 lithographic prints inspired by some of Henri Matisse's most celebrated works.

Councillor Cooke said: "What makes this anniversary special is the way local communities, schools, volunteers and visitors have helped shape the building’s first year.

"Volunteers have contributed more than 11,500 hours of their time, helping to bring experiences to life across the museum, park and gardens."

A total of 148 schools took part in hands-on sessions last year, with more than 5,000 pupils exploring topics from Victorian life and the Second World War to art and the natural world.

The museum has also delivered a busy calendar of family events, including a new Halloween Trick or Treat evening, A Magical Victorian Christmas, Wild Encounters, Circus Skills Day, and the Teddy Bear’s Picnic.

Preston Park also worked with partner organisations during the year to reflect the diversity of the wider community—most notably for Dementia Action Week, when it hosted a silent disco for people living with and affected by dementia.
