**HARROGATE**— Children and young people across York and North Yorkshire are set to benefit from a new

**£400,000 Cradle to Career Fund**, following official cabinet approval at a Combined Authority meeting today.The long-term initiative, unveiled by Metro Mayor

**David Skaith**, aims to support young people from birth, through education, and directly into the workplace. Rather than building a new infrastructure from scratch, the initial phase of the fund draws directly on established, pre-existing charitable models and international templates to launch two 12-month early-years pilots:

- **The Scarborough Baby Box Pilot:** Modeled directly after the flagship national welfare scheme operating in Finland since the 1930s, this project distributes developmental toys, books, and practical healthcare items like thermometers to new parents to support early child development.
- **The Selby Literacy Drive:** An expansion of the globally recognized**Dolly Parton Imagination Library** —a turnkey literacy programme that has already delivered over 7 million books across the UK since 2007—which will provide registered families with a free book through the post once a month until their child turns five.

## A Long-Term Strategy for Regional Skills

The broader framework of the Cradle to Career scheme is designed to address the region’s long-term economic and educational needs. Beyond early literacy, the strategy maps out future interventions spanning primary, secondary, and post-16 education ages.

Planned initiatives include dedicated careers days, local career hubs, and targeted apprenticeships and internships. The authority intends to focus these opportunities heavily on key regional growth sectors, including teaching, construction, and STEM-based subjects.

By adopting ready-made pilot frameworks, the Combined Authority’s administrative structure aims to ensure rapid delivery, tying early child development directly to the regional pipeline for future employment and skills.

## Cross-District Political Support

The targeted rollout has drawn strong backing from senior local government leaders across the county, reflecting a coordinated approach between the Combined Authority and established local councils.


**Mayor David Skaith emphasized his personal connection to the rollout, stating:**

As a father of two young children I know first-hand the joy, challenges, and anxieties that come with welcoming a new life into the world. The original Finnish Baby Box has supported families in Finland for over 80 years, and I am proud to have the opportunity to bring this successful and established initiative to the county.




**North Yorkshire Council Leader Cllr Carl Les welcomed the investment, adding:**

This new funding is extremely welcome as it will help to give children and young people in North Yorkshire the best possible start to their lives. We are committed to working within the combined authority to make sure that all of our children and young people can get the support they need.
