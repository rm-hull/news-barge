REPAIRS to a riverside wall are likely to cost around £100,000.

A section of footpath alongside the A65 has been closed since October last year when a 13ft stretch of the River Aire in Gargrave, near Skipton, retaining wall collapsed.

North Yorkshire Council, which is responsible for the wall, says work is due to take place in the summer of next year, when river levels are lower and because of the necessary permissions from the Environment Agency.

Gargrave Parish Council has recently described the footpath closure as an eyesore and called for the work to take place as soon as possible.

North Yorkshire Council's bridges manager, Philip Richardson, said: “We understand that the footpath closure has been inconvenient, but this was necessary to keep people safe.

“We are aiming to include repairs to the wall in next year’s programme of works. As the work needs to be carried out when river levels are lower, it would not be practical to start before next summer.

“We also need permission from the Environment Agency to work within the river, and obtaining this approval can take several months.

“Unfortunately, other schemes across the county have been identified as higher priorities, which has meant that repairs to the wall have not yet been able to start.”

A procurement report approved by the council in October last year, said while it was not possible to give an exact cost of the work needed to be done, it was likely to be in the region of £100,000.

The report says next to the collapse the wall is 'severely bulged' for about 32ft.

"Elsewhere, the wall appears generally in reasonable condition, though further inspections are required," says the report.

"Dense vegetation is present along the entire length of the wall which is obscuring inspection and causing damage. We deduce that the failure was caused by a combination of tree roots, and the wall being loaded beyond capacity."

The report continues that the council considered carrying out the repairs this summer and that a temporary repair to the wall is not considered feasible because of the nature of the river.

It adds: "If the wall is left as is for an extended period of time, in the event of a significant flood event, there is the risk of fill washout from behind the wall which could lead to a road closure."
