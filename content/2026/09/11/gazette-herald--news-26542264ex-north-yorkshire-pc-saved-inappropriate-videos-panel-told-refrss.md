A former North Yorkshire Police officer had extreme videos from a stag do WhatsApp group chat saved on his phone, a misconduct panel was told.

The panel heard that before joining the force, Callum Storr was a member of the WhatsApp group “Big Rose Stag Do” in 2018 where the two extreme pornographic videos involving animals were shared.

It was proven by the panel that Storr, 30, who joined North Yorkshire Police in 2020, saved the two videos to his phone, an allegation he had denied.

The material was found when Storr’s mobile phones were seized and reviewed by police following him being arrested for being in an inappropriate sexual relationship with a domestic abuse survivor.

Storr had denied the offence of being in a relationship with the survivor of domestic abuse, but it was also proven by the misconduct panel.

The panel found that Storr, who resigned while under investigation, had committed gross misconduct and would have been dismissed if he was still a serving police officer.

A report following the misconduct hearing said the inappropriate videos were shared in the stag do WhatsApp group, of which Storr was a member, in October 2018.

Storr’s WhatsApp settings were not set to automatically download the media shared in group chats, the report said, meaning he would have “chosen to save, or in the alternative, if they had been automatically saved, they were the only two items that he had chosen to retain”.

Storr denied knowing the videos were on his phone and said he did not interact with them, which the panel rejected, according to the report.

The report said the panel was also “reinforced in its view” that the videos were “specifically retained” by Storr “by the fact that his phone was noted to also contain extreme material which was of a sexual nature, racist, Islamophobic, antisemitic and otherwise offensive. The origin of this other material is not known.”

## Ex-PC has 'shown no remorse or insight into his actions' - panel

Meanwhile, the report said Storr entered into “an intimate, personal relationship” with the survivor of domestic abuse in 2022.

Storr met the woman after he and another police officer were called to a domestic dispute involving the woman and her then partner, the report said.

The report said the woman alleged that a week after Storr was called to the dispute, the then police officer started following her on social media and they exchanged messages via Snapchat.

It said the woman later had sex with Storr on two occasions after her relationship with her former partner ended.

The woman told police and Storr was arrested on suspicion of misconduct in a public place.

Storr claimed he was a “victim of [the woman’s] behaviour]” and that North Yorkshire Police “did not do enough to protect him”, according to the report.

But the report said the panel rejected Storr’s claims, adding: “He had plenty of opportunity to reflect on his activity and had detailed guidance as to what would be expected of him to protect himself. He simply chose not to put that into action.”

The panel said Storr, who did not attend the misconduct hearing, “has shown no remorse or insight into his actions and has not accepted his responsibility for his actions”.

“He offered no comment in interview,” the report said. “The only positive the panel has been able to identify is that he has resigned from the force.”
