A BRADFORD bakery and tearoom known for its quaint and tranquil ambience is in the running for an award.

The T&A's Best Bakery 2026 competition recently launched, with readers invited to share their favourite businesses.

Nominations were made through our online voting platform, and they have now been whittled down to the final 10.

Today, we are highlighting Miss Butterfinger’s, located on The Green in Idle.

  Miss Butterfinger's in Idle 

*(Image: UGC)*

## Opened in 'memory of my nanna'

Emily Beaton, who founded Miss Butterfinger’s in 2015, said: "I started Miss Butterfingers tearoom after leaving college as a qualified chef.

"The tearoom was opened in memory of my nanna who loved to bake which I remember fondly when I was younger.

"Even now we bake some of her recipes within the tearoom.

"Her favourite colour was yellow which is why we decorated the shop in such a bright and uplifting colour.

"We’ve also incorporated birds and robins as she loved those too.

"We have created a quaint and tranquil ambience where you can step back in time and listen to the 1940s music whilst enjoying traditional home baking, including our quirky cream teas served on tricycles.

"We are known for our homemade scones."

## Customers thanked

Speaking about being nominated to the T&A competition, Ms Beaton said: “It’s so lovely to hear we have made in into the top 10 and we thank all our customers for their support it means the world to us and we wouldn’t be here without them.

“We have so many loyal regulars who come into the tearoom and we often hear comments that people just enjoy sitting and watching the world go by from our comfy chairs in the window.

“To be able to create such a welcoming and warm environment is such an achievement as we always thrive to provide a cosy place to escape too.

“We are a small family-run tearoom with such an amazing team.

“It’s a pleasure to be able to work every day with them while doing something we love!”

The winner will be chosen by readers, who can cast their vote by filling out the voting coupon printed in the Telegraph & Argus.
