A former student has been named vice-chancellor of Teesside University, where his higher education journey began.

Professor Mark Simpson has taken up the role at the university, where he first enrolled as a student before rising through academic and leadership positions, most recently serving as deputy vice-chancellor.

His appointment coincides with a period of national recognition for the university, including being named Times Higher Education University of the Year 2025 and Daily Mail Modern University of the Year 2026.

Professor Simpson said: "It is an immense privilege to become Vice-Chancellor and Chief Executive of Teesside University, an institution that has shaped my own life and career and one that continues to transform lives every day through the power of education.

"Teesside University has an important role to play in supporting economic growth, improving opportunities for local people and helping our communities thrive. I am excited about what we can achieve together."

He succeeds Professor Paul Croney OBE, who retires after 11 years in the role.

Professor Croney leaves behind a legacy of growth and impact, having strengthened the university’s academic reputation, invested in campus transformation, and deepened its regional and national influence.

Professor Simpson has pledged to spend his first 100 days engaging with students, staff, employers, civic leaders, and community partners.

He plans to build on the university’s recent successes and shape its future priorities through a programme of stakeholder engagement.
