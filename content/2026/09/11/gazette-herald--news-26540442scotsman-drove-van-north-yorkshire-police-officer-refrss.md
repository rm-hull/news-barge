A police officer could have died when a man on the run drove a van at him at high speed, York Crown Court heard.

The officer thought he had blocked in dangerous driver Harrison Douglas Long by parking his police car across the way out of a narrow cul-de-sac in Scarborough, said Oliver Kilvington, prosecuting.

The policeman, who was wearing hi-vis police uniform, got out of his car and standing by the driver’s door told Long “stop right there”.

“The defendant drove at the police officer at high speed, front wheels spinning to gain traction,” said Mr Kilvington.

“The police officer had to take evasive action to move away from the oncoming vehicle.”

Long’s van crashed into the police vehicle’s offside and then was forced up into the air as it crashed into the rear of a parked car and drove off through the gap between the two cars.

The policeman’s legs were inches away from the van and he felt the rush of air as it drove past him, said Mr Kilvington.

In a personal statement, the policeman said: "I had never before this incident feared for my life."

But he now realised he "could have got hurt or even killed" had the van hit him that day.

“When I looked at him a few seconds before it happened, the driver looked like a cornered animal with only one way out,” the police officer said.

“He didn’t care I was a person, he didn’t care I had a family and he certainly didn’t care I was in the way.”

He said the incident left him shaking and when he returned to his family at the end of his shift it occurred to him: “I very nearly didn’t come home. It is clear the driver of that van didn’t care who he hurt.”

The police officer sent out an alert and his colleagues located and arrested Long later the same day near Malton despite the 35-year-old abandoning his van in Scarborough, cycling off on a mountain bike and getting into a second vehicle as a passenger.

Long, 35, of Bridge Street, Selkirk, Scotland, pleaded guilty to dangerous driving, driving without insurance and driving whilst disqualified. He was jailed for 14 months and banned from driving for two years and seven months. He must take an extended driving test before driving alone again.

David Camidge, for Long, said he had had a difficult childhood and had pleaded guilty.

Mr Kilvington said when the policeman first tried to stop Long, he drove off through Scarborough, suddenly switching lanes at the last moment at traffic lights before turning into the cul-de-sac and doing a three-point turn before driving at the officer.

In her personal statement, the owner of the parked car, which was written off, said it had cost her family financially including the loss of a holiday.
