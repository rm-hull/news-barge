North Yorkshire is full of places whose names are harder to say than they first appear.

From Fylingthorpe and Hunmanby to Muker and Rievaulx, the county has plenty of settlements that can leave visitors second-guessing themselves.

Among them is Asenby, a picturesque village near Thirsk that is often mispronounced by those passing through for the first time.

## How do you pronounce Asenby?

If you're not from the area, it would be easy to look at the spelling and assume the village is pronounced "Ass-en-bee".

However, locals know differently.

The correct pronunciation is AYZ-uhn-bee, with the first syllable sounding like "aze".

It is the kind of North Yorkshire place name that catches newcomers out, but once you know it, you'll never forget it.

So next time you're discussing the village or following directions through the area, it is AYZ-uhn-bee, not "Ass-en-bee".

## Where is Asenby?

Asenby sits around three miles south-west of Thirsk, between the market towns of Thirsk and Ripon.

The village is surrounded by open countryside and farmland and lies close to the River Swale, giving it the peaceful rural character that much of this part of North Yorkshire is known for.

A collection of traditional homes, quiet lanes and green spaces give Asenby the feel of a classic Yorkshire village, while its location near the A168 means residents remain within easy reach of larger towns and transport links.

Despite its modest size, Asenby forms part of a landscape rich in history, agriculture and local tradition.


## What's the history behind the name?

Like many settlements across Yorkshire, Asenby's name reflects the area's Viking heritage.

The "-by" ending is derived from Old Norse and is commonly associated with places founded or influenced by Scandinavian settlers more than 1,000 years ago.

The suffix can be found in dozens of Yorkshire place names, from Whitby on the coast to Selby in North Yorkshire.

It is one of the region's most recognisable linguistic links to its past.

## Why do Yorkshire place names cause confusion?

Many of Yorkshire's place names have evolved over centuries, meaning their modern pronunciations are not always obvious from their spellings.

Local accents, historic influences and centuries of use have all helped shape the way names are spoken today.

Asenby is just one example of a village where local knowledge makes all the difference.

And while visitors may stumble over the pronunciation at first, saying AYZ-uhn-bee correctly is a quick way to sound like a local.
