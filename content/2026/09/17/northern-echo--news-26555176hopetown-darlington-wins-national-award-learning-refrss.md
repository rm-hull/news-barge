Hopetown Darlington has won a national award for its learning programmes and work with young people, it has been announced.

The attraction won the Sandford Award for Heritage Learning - a national benchmark for excellence in heritage education.

Councillor Libby McCollom, cabinet member for local services at Darlington Council, said: "We are delighted that Hopetown has been recognised with a prestigious Sandford Award.

"This achievement reflects the dedication of our team in creating inspiring learning experiences that bring Darlington's remarkable railway story to life for young people.

  Hopetown Darlington has won a national award for its learning programmes and work with young people 

*(Image: Hopetown Darlington)*

"Hopetown is much more than a visitor attraction.

"It is a place where future generations can discover how innovation and ambition from our town helped change the world.

"By connecting learners with their heritage in engaging and meaningful ways, we are inspiring curiosity, creativity and a greater understanding of the role they can play in shaping the future."

The Heritage Education Trust presents the award with Lincoln Bishop University to recognise outstanding heritage learning programmes.

Opened after a major redevelopment in July 2024, Hopetown Darlington tells the town’s internationally significant railway story through interactive exhibits, historic collections and the immersive 'Experiment!' time-travel simulator ride.

Assessors hailed Hopetown’s schools programme for connecting young learners with Darlington’s globally important railway story.

They praised the site’s personalised approach, saying: “Schools are treated with care and consideration, and learners as individuals, with their needs identified and catered for by an excellent Learning and Access Officer.”

The report praised “brilliant differentiation for all learners” and the team’s support for schools before, during and after visits.

Teachers praised the team’s communication and care for their pupils.

Since opening, Hopetown has welcomed 375 school visits and 14,520 pupils, making it a leading heritage-learning destination in the region.

It also runs programmes for home educators, opening up Darlington’s railway history to more young people.

Sarah Gouldsbrough, Hopetown’s learning and access officer, said: "We're incredibly proud to receive this recognition.

"From the very beginning, our aim has been to create learning experiences that are inclusive, engaging and memorable.

"Seeing thousands of young people connect with Darlington's railway story, ask questions and leave inspired is what makes this work so rewarding."

Assessors also praised Hopetown for telling the Stockton and Darlington Railway’s story from a distinctly Darlington perspective, connecting learners with the people and ideas that shaped the modern world.

The Sandford Award was officially announced on September 14.

The official presentation ceremony will take place at Kensington Palace on November 17.
