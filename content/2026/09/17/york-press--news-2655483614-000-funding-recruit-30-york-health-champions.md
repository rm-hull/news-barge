New funding is set to bring health advice, support and wellbeing information closer to more people across York.

City of York Council has received £14,000 from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority to expand its Community Health Champions programme and recruit 30 volunteers.

The money will recruit, train and support volunteers who help residents find health information and services in their neighbourhoods.

Councillor Lucy Steels-Walshaw, executive member for health, wellbeing and adult social care, said: "Where people live has a significant influence on their health, and many residents in York continue to face barriers to accessing the support they need.

"Strengthening community-based approaches is an important part of addressing this."

The money will extend the scheme to Westfield, Clifton and Hull Road.

After training, the champions share health advice and direct residents to services covering mental wellbeing, healthy lifestyles and long-term conditions.

David Skaith, mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: "Movement, activity, and sport play a huge role in improving mental health and tackling social isolation.

"This is a fantastic example of how we are backing ambition at a grassroots level."
