A YOUNG man, who police had been urgently searching for as part of a public appeal, was found dead in a coastal town last year, a two-day inquest heard this week.

Zak Joshua Bayes, 28, of no fixed abode, was found dead in Whitby on March 19, 2025.

Assistant Coroner for North Yorkshire and York Jonathan Leach said Mr Bayes died from multiple traumatic injuries due to a fall from height.

The two-day hearing in Northallerton, which concluded on Wednesday morning this week, heard that Mr Bayes had been having difficulties with mental health from an early age and had previously been admitted to hospital under the Mental Health Act 1983.

He had also spent time in prison in Glasgow, the inquest heard.

In July 2023, police in North Yorkshire issued an appeal after Mr Bayes had gone missing.

The appeal stated the force had become concerned for Mr Bayes' welfare after he had gone missing from Scarborough having last been seen by staff at Cross Lane Hospital.

A photo of Mr Bayes was released by North Yorkshire Police as part of this appeal.

A police spokesperson said at the time: "Officers are very concerned for his welfare, and are urging anyone who sees him or knows where he is to get in touch."

The inquest heard on Wednesday morning that Mr Bayes was seen looking out to the river in Whitby at 1.20pm on March 19, 2025, the day he died, by a lady who was out for a walk.

Mr Leach gave the conclusion of suicide with regards to Mr Bayes' death.

He offered his condolences to the family and friends of Mr Bayes in respect of their loss.

It comes after dozens of people took part in a sponsored walk from Scarbrough to Whitby this summer to raise awareness of the charity The Mental Shift.

Campaigners sought to raise awareness of mental health after Kade Purvis, from Whitby, died earlier this year at the age of 27, just one year younger than Mr Bayes at the time of his death.

Mr Purvis' sister Megan Ahmed, who organised the walk and is also a cousin of Mr Bayes, told the BBC that young men should be urged to "speak up" about mental health.

She added: "We urge anybody struggling to speak up and get help, or to get somebody to advocate for you."

In May, Whitby Town FC, the club Mr Purvis followed, confirmed a memorial to the late young man.

A seat at the club's ground will be marked as A27, bearing the message "Kade - Forever 27".

The Mental Shift has also shown support for Mr Bayes following his passing.

A statement issued by the charity organisation in February said: "Zak Bayes was an amazing young lad who had his whole life ahead of him, and he had a family who cared and loved him. Unfortunately, Zak’s battle got too much and he saw that taking his life was the only way out.

"Zak’s mum has supported us for the past year with the belief that if Zak had support like this then he could still be here today.

"This is no exception for his cousin, Megan Ahmed, who is running the Manchester Marathon to raise vital funds for our support services."

The Samaritans say that whatever you’re going through, you can call them for free, at any time, from any phone, on 116 123.

The helpline is available 24 hours every day.
