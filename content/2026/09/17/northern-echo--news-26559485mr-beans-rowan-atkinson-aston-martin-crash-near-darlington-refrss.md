Rowan Atkinson's long-standing love of fast cars once brought him back close to his County Durham roots, and ended with a rare Aston Martin in the barriers.

The Mr Bean and Blackadder star, who was born in Consett, has spent decades collecting and racing performance cars.

One of the more dramatic episodes came at Croft Circuit near Darlington in 2001.

Atkinson was competing in an Aston Martin Owners Club event when the car he was driving left the track and crashed into a barrier.

The vehicle was a rare Aston Martin V8 Zagato, one of the more distinctive high-performance models produced by the British manufacturer.

   

*(Image: BBC / PA)*

Atkinson walked away from the accident without serious injury.

The incident was another example of how seriously the actor takes his motoring.

Rather than simply collecting rare cars, Atkinson has regularly driven them on track and competed in historic racing events.

He has taken part in races at venues including Goodwood and has spoken publicly over the years about his enthusiasm for both classic and performance cars.

That makes the Croft accident particularly notable for North East fans.

The circuit sits near Darlington, only a relatively short distance from the area where Atkinson grew up.

Before becoming one of Britain's most recognisable comedy stars, Atkinson was born in Consett in County Durham and spent his early years in the region.


His later motoring career has included ownership of some extraordinary cars.

They have ranged from a 1939 BMW 328 Roadster and a 1964 Ford Falcon Sprint to a Honda NSX and the McLaren F1 he famously crashed twice.

The McLaren accident in 2011 became particularly well known after the repair bill was reported at around £910,000.

Atkinson later sold the car in 2015 for an estimated £8 million.

His skills behind the wheel have also been showcased on Top Gear.

Atkinson recorded a lap of 1 minute 42.2 seconds as the programme's Star in a Reasonably Priced Car, putting him at the top of the celebrity leaderboard at the time.

His name has returned to the motoring headlines after being linked with the BBC's planned revival of Top Gear.

The programme is set to return following a lengthy absence, although the BBC has not yet confirmed who will present the new series.
