York CAMRA has revealed the best beers of this year's beer festival at St Lawrence Church.

As reported today, for the first time in the UK, no or low alcohol beers were also judged.

Judging of these ales took place on Tuesday, with the other categories judged yesterday, the day the beer festival started.

The tastings were 'blind', meaning the judges did not know what brands they were drinking.

Beers were marked on their aroma, the initial taste, the aftertaste and their likeability.

And if you thought you recognised one of the beers, you were told not to say what brew you thought it was.

The overall winner was Gathering Wood, a 6.5 per cent stout from the Horsforth brewery.

  The judges of the finals of the York CAMRA Locale competition. 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

Gaining silver in the overall category was Beeching Axe from the Harrogate Brewery, with bronze going to Parachuting Hedgehog from the Sunbeam Brewery.

In the NoLo category, the winner was Nowt Perculier, from Theakstons brewery, with Nowt Mary from the Ilkley Brewery getting silver and the Ilkley Brewery's Daydreamer getting bronze.

In the Pale/Amber/Bitter up to 4.5% ABV, Tockwith-based Rudgate Brewery took gold with their Citra beer, with silver going to Trinity from the Horsforth Brewery.

In the Pale/Amber/Bitter category ABV 4.6% and above, Beeching Axe from Harrogate took gold, with Brew York's Minster ESB taking silver.

In the dark ales up to 4.5% ABV category, Parachuting Hedgehog from Sunbeam took gold, with Plough On from York-based Trig brewery taking silver.

In the dark ales ABV 4.6% and above category, Gathering Wood from Horsforth took gold, with Wallop from Malton-based Brass Castle taking silver.

  The aroma of the ales were also judged. 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

And in the speciality category, gold went to Brew York for Tiramoosu Tonkoko and silver going to Turning Point of Knaresborough for New Bermuda.

Steve Gorton, who organised the tastings and judging, says the tastings went smoothly and took place quicker than expected as they were keen to enjoy the ales.

Initially, there were 24 judges in the heats and eight in the final.

The NoLo beers were judged on Tuesday.

Steve said: "The NoLo drinks are certainly good. The darks fared better than the pales here. That can be expected because they have more flavour."

"But Theakstons Nowt Perculier winning makes sense due its malty taste. It works like Guiness Zero. We have a range of NoLos on the wine stand."

The overall winner, Gathering Wood, features coffee and chocolate malt and is based on an 1890 recipe.

Steve said: "It is very much coffee to the fore and some chocolate underpinning it. For me, it's a smooth beer but at 6.5% I would be very careful with quantity."

Earlier, some of the judges, me included, also remarked that the beers in the tastings also seemed better than last year.

John Pybus of The Blue Bell Inn added: "There was a phenomenal range of beers and competition was very tight. No matter who the winner is, everyone in the final should be proud of their beers. Thank you to the breweries that made this happen."

The beer festival runs until Saturday.
