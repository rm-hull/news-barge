North East health experts are urging more people to become organ donors, warning that someone will die today waiting for a life-saving transplant.

Regional organ donation nurses at University Hospitals Tees made the appeal during Organ Donation Week, which runs from Monday, September 21 to Sunday, September 27.

The national campaign highlights the continuing need for donors, with more than 8,000 people waiting for transplants in the UK.

Sam Allan, a specialist nurse in organ donation, said: "Confirming your decision to donate on the NHS Organ Donor Register makes it clear to your family that you want to be an organ donor, leaving them certain of your decision at what is a difficult and emotional time.

"We need more people in Teesside and North Yorkshire to confirm their decision on the register today to save more lives now and in the future."

Since the NHS Organ Donor Register launched in 1994, donors have helped save thousands of lives after their deaths.

But demand is still rising, with around 100 people on the University Hospitals Tees waiting list alone.

During Organ Donation Week, the trust will share former patients’ transplant stories to show the impact of donor decisions.

The stories include a nurse marking 10 years since her transplant and children waiting for kidney donations.

Jonathan Murray, a kidney consultant, said: "Every day, lives are lost with people dying while waiting for an organ transplant, but most of us will accept an organ if we need one.

"It only takes two minutes to register your decision.

"Two minutes now could save up to nine lives.

"With more people, both adults and children, waiting for transplants, it's more important than ever to confirm your organ donation decision on the NHS Organ Donor Register.
