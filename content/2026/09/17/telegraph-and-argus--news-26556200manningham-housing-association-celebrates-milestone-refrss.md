A HOUSING association is celebrating four decades of supporting communities.

Manningham Housing Association (MHA), based in Bradford, marked its 40th anniversary with a celebration at Bradford Live, recognising its long-standing service in providing affordable housing across the district.

The event featured cultural performances from Dixons Manningham Academy Choir, a traditional Baul music group, and the Punjab Roots ensemble.

Guests also enjoyed an awards ceremony and the cutting of a commemorative cake.

Rupert Pometsey, chairman of MHA, welcomed attendees including Kersten England CBE, Deputy Lieutenant of West Yorkshire.

Mr Pometsey said: "At that time, many people in the district were living in chronic, overcrowded conditions with reports of up to 18 people sharing a single house.

"In the early 1990s, Manningham Housing Association achieved global recognition when the United Nations hailed its community-focused social housing and regeneration initiatives, describing them as 'hugely successful'."

He explained that MHA was established in 1986 following research by the Bangladeshi Youth Organisation, who identified a critical need for larger homes within Bradford’s BME communities.

Today, MHA manages more than 1,400 homes across Bradford and Keighley, with 75 per cent of its properties designed for large families.

Mr Pometsey admitted the association faced challenges a decade ago when its governance rating was downgraded, but credited the turnaround to new leadership.

He said: "The arrival of Barrington Billings as MHA chair in 2017, and the appointment of Lee Bloomfield as MHA chief executive the following year, set the organisation on course for an unprecedented run of success."

Under their guidance, MHA regained the highest possible G1, V1 regulatory ratings and achieved several industry accolades.

Mr Pometsey said: "As a result of the dedication and professionalism of our incredible staff, the association has earned and retained the Customer Service Excellence quality mark.

"We have also become the first housing association in the world to achieve the international Standard for Diversity and Inclusion in HR Management, and won numerous regional and national awards."

MHA chief executive Lee Bloomfield stated the association’s recent achievements were only the beginning.

Mr Bloomfield said: "Earlier this year, the board approved our new corporate strategy which restated our vision to provide quality homes, empower BME and other communities, and create opportunities that help people to live well and thrive.

"Every one of the new homes will have the potential to change the lives of each individual and family who takes up residence."
