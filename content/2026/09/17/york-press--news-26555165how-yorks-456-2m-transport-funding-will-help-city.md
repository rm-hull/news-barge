DEVOLUTION is one of those words that can make people’s eyes glaze over.

But behind the jargon is a simple idea: decisions about York should be made closer to York.

People in Whitehall cannot know our city and our communities as well as the people who live here. They do not see first-hand where a road needs repairing, which bus service is under pressure or what would help a local high street thrive.

I champion York Outer every day, and too often Westminster just doesn’t get York. We are not just a beautiful historic city or a place people come for a weekend away. We are a living, growing city, surrounded by villages and communities with their own challenges, ambitions and priorities.

For too long, councils and community organisations have had to compete for short-term funding pots and then wait for decisions made hundreds of miles away.

That approach has often left places like York frustrated by bureaucracy and unable to plan properly for the future. Devolution gives us the chance to do things differently. It brings powers and funding out of Westminster and into the hands of local leaders who understand our area and can be held accountable by local people.

And I think our new Prime Minister really gets that. He has seen first-hand in Greater Manchester what can happen when local leaders are trusted with the powers and funding to get things done, rather than constantly having to wait for permission from Whitehall. Having a Prime Minister who understands the difference devolution can make gives us a real opportunity to go further.

Of course, the important question is not how powers are organised on a chart. The question is whether devolution is actually making a difference to people’s lives.

The answer is yes.

One of the clearest examples is public transport. More than three million £1 bus journeys have now been made by under-19s across York and North Yorkshire, with almost half of those journeys taking place in York. For many young people, cheaper bus travel means better access to school, college, apprenticeships, jobs and social opportunities. At a time when many families are feeling financial pressure, that matters.

We are also seeing real progress when it comes to our roads.

Few issues generate more correspondence from residents than potholes and the state of roads. Whether people drive, cycle, walk or use public transport, they want roads that are safe, reliable and properly maintained.

That is why the first long-term transport funding is so significant for our city. Worth £456.2 million over four years, it provides the certainty needed to plan ahead rather than relying on short-term annual funding. And in highway maintenance alone York's portion has increased from £4.4 million last year to £7.1 million this year, to repair roads, tackle potholes and improve the transport network.

Alongside this investment, the Mayor's Active Travel Fund is supporting improvements that will make it safer and easier for people to walk, wheel and cycle. Projects across the Knavesmire and Bootham Stray, and support for schools are all helping create a network that works better for everyone.

Devolution is also supporting the organisations that make such an important contribution to life in York. Through the Mayor's Moving Forward programme, funding is being directed towards physical activity, active travel and men's mental health.

Here in York, support has gone to organisations including Next Door But One, Menfulness, York Mind and Haxby Netball Club. These groups understand their communities better than anyone. They know where support is needed and often intervene long before problems become crises.

Our high streets are benefitting too. Funding has supported projects including Light Up Haxby and Wigginton, York Fashion Week, and a new art project along Fulford Road.

These projects are all different, and that is exactly the point. What works in one community will not necessarily work in another. Devolution allows local people to shape local solutions rather than relying on a one-size-fits-all approach.

Rail improvements are also coming down the track, with Haxby Station progressing and two trains an hour between York and Scarborough on the way. Together, these improvements will strengthen local connections and support economic growth across the area.

This is about York having a stronger voice and more influence over the decisions that shape our future. The lesson from the last two years is clear: when decisions are made closer to the communities they affect, we get better outcomes. Devolution is already delivering for York. Now we must make the most of the opportunities it brings and ensure those benefits continue to be felt across every community.

Before I finish, I want to take a moment to recognise some of the positive things happening across our city. As schools return, I would like to wish every student, teacher and member of school staff a successful year ahead.

There has also been plenty to celebrate on the sporting front. York Knights ended the season on a high and continue to be fantastic ambassadors for our city. York City, meanwhile, have continued to show why our club belongs, at the very least, in League Two!
