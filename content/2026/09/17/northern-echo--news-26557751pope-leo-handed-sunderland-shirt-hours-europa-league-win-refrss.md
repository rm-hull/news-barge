A Sunderland shirt was handed to Pope Leo XIV ahead of the club's first European campaign in 53 years.

The red and white shirt, bearing the name "Pope Leo" on the back, was presented to the Pope in Rome by former Hexham and Newcastle bishop Seamus Cunningham on Wednesday (September 16).

The gift reached the Vatican via Sunderland AFC's club chaplain, Fr Marc Lyden-Smith, who had been given the shirt by club chief operating officer Paul Kingsmore.

Leo said he would be offering prayers for the Black Cats before their historic return to continental football, according to Fr Lyden-Smith.

And it appears the blessing may already have worked.

Later on Wednesday evening, Sunderland enjoyed a dream start to their Europa League adventure, beating Dutch side AZ Alkmaar 1-0 on Wearside in the club's first European match since 1973.

Fr Lyden-Smith said: "He offered prayers for the team as we begin our European campaign."

Images released from the Vatican show Pope Leo holding the famous red-and-white striped shirt alongside Bishop Emeritus Cunningham during the presentation.

The moment has sparked excitement among Sunderland supporters, many of whom have joked that a touch of divine intervention may have helped Regis Le Bris' side secure a memorable European victory.

One fan said: "Haway the Pope."

While another pointed out, "Last time the pope held up a Sunderland shirt we made a cup final."

Because this is not the first time Sunderland have enjoyed a connection with the head of the Catholic church.

Back in 2013, Fr Lyden-Smith presented Pope Francis with a Sunderland shirt bearing the words "Papa Francesco" on the back during a General Audience in Rome.

That visit also proved to be a good omen for the Black Cats.

Just days later, Sunderland beat fierce rivals Newcastle United 2-1 in the Premier League at the Stadium of Light.
