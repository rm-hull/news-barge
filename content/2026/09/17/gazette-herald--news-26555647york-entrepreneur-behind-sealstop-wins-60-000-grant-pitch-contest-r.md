A York entrepreneur whose business helps prevent costly water leaks has secured a share of £800,000 after a Dragons' Den-style pitch competition.

Belle Richardson, founder of York-based SealStop, won a £60,000 grant to bring her product to market.

The funding came through the Great Ideas programme, part of Mayor David Skaith’s Business Innovation Fund supporting innovation across York and North Yorkshire.

Ms Richardson said: "We secured the £60,000 that we pitched for, which is incredible, and now means we can bring out our product.

"It’s really sped up getting that new product to market, it’s so exciting, it puts me and the business exactly where we want to be."

She encouraged others to apply for similar opportunities, adding: "I would definitely recommend other businesses get involved in projects like this, getting in front of people and the grant process because it’s so rewarding."

A former NHS radiographer at York and Malton hospitals, she developed the product with husband Jason after repeated water overflows on their smallholding near Pocklington.

Launched in 2024, SealStop has since gained national recognition and industry awards.

The pitch event at West Offices in York awarded grants of up to £60,000.

Winning projects included AI-led manufacturing improvements, recruitment technology, micro-farming and products designed for international growth.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: "With transformational grants and expert advice, we're giving businesses the tools to take their Great Ideas to the next level.

"Whether it's about using AI to unlock productivity in factories or using sustainable thinking to help our farmers save water and money, we're backing our businesses.

"The Great Ideas fund is here to match your ambition and shows that when we say York and North Yorkshire is open for business, we mean it."

The BBC’s One Show recently followed both SealStop and the Incredible Plant Company as they pitched their ideas to the panel.

Great Ideas was developed in partnership with business support and funding specialist UMi.

Simon Goon, chief technology and investment officer at UMi, said: "It was fantastic to join the Mayor’s team and other panellists for the Great Ideas pitch and to meet the inspiring entrepreneurs involved in the initiative.

"Support like this helps small and medium-sized businesses overcome financial barriers and get the specialist support they need to innovate and grow.

"We are delighted with the results it has generated so far and are confident that it will help more businesses innovate and become a blueprint for others across the UK to realise their potential."

The wider Business Innovation Fund is a £2.5 million programme offering tailored support and grants to help businesses put innovative ideas into action.
