Businesses and organisations from the North East are set to showcase the region’s global wind-energy strengths at a major conference.

The North East and Tees Valley will showcase their world-class infrastructure, supply chains and expertise at WindEnergy Hamburg from September 22 to 25, one of the world’s biggest industry events.

The delegation is being led by Energi Coast, North East England’s offshore wind cluster, alongside the North East Mayoral Strategic Authority and Tees Valley Combined Authority.

  Port of Blyth 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Key ports and businesses will also attend to showcase the region’s potential for growth.

WindEnergy Hamburg 2026 is expected to attract around 43,000 visitors from 100 countries and about 1,600 exhibitors from 40 countries.

The event brings together manufacturers, suppliers, planners, investors, service providers and decision-makers from across the onshore and offshore wind industries.

The event gives regions and supply-chain organisations a global platform to showcase their expertise, attract investment and build partnerships in the clean-energy transition.

   Steel River Quay on the River Tee 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen said: “Taking our offer to WindEnergy Hamburg is about showing the world what we have delivered and how we have all the ingredients to deliver more.

“We have the ports, the land, the skills and the supply chain to help drive the next generation of offshore wind growth.

“This is a huge opportunity to bring in more investment to create high-quality jobs. We already do a huge amount in the sector and we’re growing fast. We want to cement our position at the heart of this industry and make our ambitions for even more growth a reality.”

The region’s ports offer deep-water quays, heavy-lift facilities and direct access to major North Sea offshore-wind projects.

  Port of Tyne 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Strategic sites offer around 8,500 acres of developable land, while a pro-business approach aims to make investment faster, simpler and more competitive.

Industrial expertise, specialist offshore skills and organisations including ORE Catapult, the Net Zero Industry Innovation Centre and CPI support large-scale clean-energy projects.

Kim McGuinness, North East Mayor, said: “We are the English home of green energy with one of the strongest and most established offshore wind clusters in the world. But we want to go even further.

"Through my Plan for Green Jobs, we're going to double the number of green jobs in the North East to 50,000 in the next decade, including 10,000 in offshore wind.

“The North East is a global leader in clean energy, advanced manufacturing and industrial development, and our message to investors is simple: if you want to invest in the region’s offshore wind market, we can help you do it.”

The Rivers Tees, Tyne and Wear provide access to some of England’s deepest east-coast port facilities, alongside the Port of Blyth.

The North East Investment Zone and Teesside Freeport offer incentives for clean energy, green manufacturing and advanced engineering.

Strategic sites including Teesworks and Blyth Energy Central offer development land to attract investment and help supply chains expand quickly.

Joanne Leng MBE, CEO of NOF / Energi Coast, said: “The Energi Coast cluster, owned and operated by NOF, continues to lead the way.

"The cluster has over 25 years’ experience in offshore wind and is recognised globally for bringing decades of experience and expertise to the sector. Most of the businesses in the cluster are already taking their expertise to the global market.

“The Crown Estate has said a new offshore wind leasing round in 2027 could accommodate around 6GW or more, predominantly off the coast of North East England. Energi Coast is ready to embrace the opportunity for further offshore wind farms locally, and the businesses in the region will work collaboratively on shallow fixed, deep fixed and floating developments as they present in the future.
