Bradford Council has defended the safety measures in place during work on one of the district’s biggest road improvement schemes, following concerns raised about pedestrian access near a secondary school.

Resident and parent Dave Thompson highlighted several perceived safety concerns regarding the Thornton Road works near Beckfoot Thornton.

The Council admitted that strong winds had displaced some barriers at one stage, temporary traffic signals had malfunctioned, and Belisha beacons had been removed during the roadworks, adding that these issues were now resolved.

## 'There are a number of issues'

Mr Thompson told the T&A: "There are a number of issues with the work that is being done outside the school.

"The island above the mini roundabouts is out of action, meaning that, rather than cross Thornton Road to then cross using the island on Chat Hill Road, they would need to cross School Green where there is no island meaning they have to cross both carriageways in one go.

"The crossing below the double mini roundabouts is also closed meaning there's no crossing there either."

Construction work on the Thornton Road Improvements Project began on Monday, July 20, 2026, marking what the Council described as the "start of a major investment to make one of Bradford's busiest routes safer, greener and easier to use for everyone."

## The main issues

Mt Thompson believes the main issues facing residents relates to the zebra crossing which he claims "gets very busy in normal times, to the point where school staff have to shepherd students across to ensure a flow of traffic is no more."

He continued: "There are no Belisha beacons on the roadside to give any indication that there's a crossing there, which, I would imagine means that the crossing is, in legal terms, no more than than an island in the road, not compelling drivers to stop.

"There do remain on the road some, very worn, markings but for those who don't know the area there is zero indication of a zebra crossing meaning there is no obligation for drivers to stop."

He's also unhappy with the "big pile of soil and tarmac" that he said has been taken out of the road "reducing visibility for drivers coming up Thornton Road of people waiting to cross (it is a staggered crossing so no obligation to stop but it's still wise to allow drivers full visibility of any crossing)."

## 'How they can think this is sufficient is beyond me'

He claims that the council have informed him that the crossing has been reinstated, as of Wednesday, September 2 but, he says "it's not been reinstated to any degree of safety - the blue barriers where there should be railings have blown down in this morning's wind meaning it's even more dangerous."

"How they can think this is sufficient to state it's been reinstated is, quite frankly, beyond me."

He also raised concerns about communication from the council, saying he received information only through social media rather than official channels.

Mr Thompson said: "I'm extremely disappointed and concerned that the council see fit to carry out this work near a school without taking into account the safety of students and staff who have to get to and from the school."

A Council spokesperson said: “The central traffic island remains safe from a construction perspective, with appropriate cones and barriers in place. However, strong winds caused some of the barriers to become displaced. Additional weights will now be added to help prevent this from happening again.

“The Belisha beacons were temporarily removed to facilitate the removal of the existing island and enable two-way traffic to continue moving through the scheme wherever possible. The contractor is exploring the option of installing temporary Belisha beacons to improve visibility while works are taking place."

The council spokesperson continued: “The newly created parking area is not coned off and remains open to allow vehicles to enter and exit freely. Cones were temporarily put in place for a short period last week to prevent vehicles from parking in the area before the works had been completed and it was ready for use.

“Local residents were informed by letter that parking on both sides of the road east of Leaventhorpe Lane would remain unaffected, ensuring that ample parking capacity continues to be available in the area.

“The current pedestrian diversion at Chat Hill Junction has been designed so that people crossing the road only need to focus on traffic approaching from one direction at a time. As the junction is operating under four-way temporary traffic signals, this arrangement removes the possibility of traffic approaching from both directions simultaneously. Alternative crossing arrangements are also being considered, including an option that would reduce the crossing distance to a single traffic lane.

“There were delays caused by malfunctioning temporary traffic signals at the junction of Thornton Road and Godfrey Street on Monday this week. It was unfortunate as it happened on the first day back at school , but the signal issue was fully resolved on the day and made fully operational again.

“We appreciate the patience and understanding of residents and road users while these improvements are being delivered.”

The T&A contacted Beckfoot Thornton for a comment.
