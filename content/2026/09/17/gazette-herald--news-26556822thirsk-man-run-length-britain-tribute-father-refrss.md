A RUNNER is taking on an epic 870-mile challenge in memory of his late father to raise £100,000 for charity.

Archie Whitfield, 24, from Thirsk in North Yorkshire, will set off from Land’s End to John O’Groats on September 19, 2026, aiming to run the entire length of Great Britain.

The endurance feat is in honour of his father, Dave Whitfield, a teacher and Green Party councillor who died in December 2023 after living with Cushing's disease caused by a pituitary brain tumour.

Mr Whitfield said: "Every challenge I've taken on has been about Dad.

"Running 870 miles from one end of the country to the other is probably the biggest thing I've attempted yet, but that's exactly why I wanted to do it."

The run is part of a series of challenges Mr Whitfield has taken on in memory of his father, through which he has already raised more than £25,000 for The Brain Charity.

The charity supports people with neurological conditions and their families.

His latest goal is to raise £100,000 through what he describes as one of his most physically demanding feats yet.

Previous challenges have included running to all 20 English Premier League stadiums in 25 days—a total of 835 miles—followed by the London Marathon.

He has also run 250 miles between all 12 Scottish Premiership stadiums in seven days and 365 miles between all 18 Portuguese Primeira Liga stadiums in 10 days.

Each challenge has been inspired by the bond he shared with his father through football.

Matt Meaney, CEO of The Brain Charity, said: "What Archie is doing is extraordinary.

"Running 870 miles from Land's End to John O'Groats is an immense physical challenge, but what makes his fundraising journey so powerful is the deeply personal reason behind it."

Mr Meaney said the funds already raised are "already making a real difference."

Mr Whitfield is encouraging people to follow his progress and help him reach his £100,000 target.
