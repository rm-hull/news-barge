Plans are progressing to make safe and repair the historic Grade II listed Middleham Bridge after it suffered significant damage in a crash involving a heavy goods vehicle.

The bridge, which links Middleham and Leyburn, has remained closed to all traffic, pedestrians and cyclists since the collision earlier this month (September) due to concerns over the structural stability of one of its decorative masonry arches.

We are in the process of appointing a specialist contractor to undertake the works and install temporary scaffolding to support the damaged arch, enabling engineers to carry out detailed inspections and emergency repairs safely.

Subject to final arrangements being completed, the initial phase of works is expected to begin in the coming weeks.

This will involve stabilising the structure, carefully dismantling unsafe sections of the damaged arch and undertaking temporary repairs to make the bridge safe.

We will seek to recover repair costs from those responsible. However, the total cost of the works is not yet known and will depend on the outcome of ongoing structural assessments and the scope of the final repair programme.

The temporary works are expected to be completed by the end of next month (October), enabling the bridge to reopen. However, this remains dependent on the condition of the structure once work starts and the availability of specialist contractors.

Executive member for highways and transportation, Cllr Malcolm Taylor, said: “We recognise the disruption that the closure of Middleham Bridge is causing for residents, businesses and visitors and we would like to thank everyone for their continued patience while we progress this complex programme of work.

"Given the bridge's age, historic significance and listed status, it is vital that we fully understand the extent of the damage and ensure that repairs are carried out in a way that preserves its heritage while maintaining public safety.

"Our priority is to make the structure safe as quickly as possible and reopen the bridge, while also developing a longer-term solution that protects this important landmark for future generations.”

Subject to the necessary approvals, including listed building consent, the permanent repair programme is expected to begin in spring 2027.

This is likely to involve rebuilding the damaged historic arch and restoring its masonry features in a way that respects and preserves the bridge's heritage significance.

As part of the project, we are also exploring whether the decorative arch could be reconstructed with increased clearance to help reduce the risk of future collisions.

Cllr Taylor added: “The arches are among the oldest and most significant elements of the bridge and the council is working closely with conservation specialists to ensure their historic character is protected throughout the repair process.

“Additional signage measures are also being considered to further reduce the likelihood of future incidents.”

The signed diversion route will remain in place while the bridge is closed and further updates will be provided as more information becomes available.
