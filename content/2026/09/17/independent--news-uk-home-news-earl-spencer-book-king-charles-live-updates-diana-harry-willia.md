King Charles has made his first public appearance since his “unprecedented” response to claims made by Princess Diana’s brother Earl Spencer in his bombshell book.

The book, which is set to be serialised in the *Daily Mail*, alleges that in the days after Diana’s death, King Charles had said: “rest assured, we’ll forget her soon enough”.

His remarks were reportedly made to Earl Spencer during a major dispute over funeral arrangements after Diana died in a car crash in Paris on 31 August 1997.

But Buckingham Palace has hit back at the claims ahead of Charles’ address to AI leaders this afternoon, saying that the King believes “grief can cloud reason”.

Royal commentators have labelled the statement as unprecedented, saying it is very unusual for the Palace to comment on books.

Editor-in-chief of *Majesty Magazine* Ingrid Seward said: “It’s unprecedented, as far as I can think. It’s very unusual, let’s say. Very unusual.

“I imagine it’s because the King was very upset and wanted to set the record straight – more on behalf of his sons than anything else.”

## You must ensure AI serves humanity and natural world, King tells AI chiefs

The King has told tech bosses to ensure AI is a force for good.

At a summit on artificial intelligence, he told them: “The decisions taken at this formative time will shape the world inherited by future generations. And so, the task before you is not merely to advance technology, but to ensure that it remains firmly in the service of humanity, community and the natural world. As we embrace what is new, we must not lose what is timeless.

“I can only applaud those of you who are so deeply committed to harnessing artificial intelligence for good, so that we might see a future where human potential remains boundless, whilst technological development is carefully curated to enhance our success.

“I really am enormously grateful for the presence in this room of many of our greatest technological minds, and indeed, for the thoughtful stewardship demonstrated by those gathered here today as you consider the fundamental principles that should – and must – guide the development of artificial intelligence in the years ahead.

“I very much look forward to returning to hear the conclusions of your productive – and I hope harmonious! – discussions later on today.”

## Humanity must remain sacred, Charles says

The King has told tech leaders that “humanity must remain sacred” as artificial intelligence technologies continue to develop.

He said: “So, today, I want to ask two simple questions, building on the message I sent to the United Kingdom AI Safety Summit in Bletchley in 2023:

“First, how can we harness the benefits of AI, with safety at its heart?

“And, second how can we build international co-operation and consensus to achieve this, so no nation is left behind – but the human race is left in place?

“This may sound hyperbolic. I have long thought it is just enlightened realism…”

## We must not lose control of our destiny or souls, King tells AI leaders

King Charles has warned tech bosses that people want assurances that “we will not lose control of our destiny or our souls”.

“Ladies and Gentlemen, I suppose we might readily agree that the development of AI – its substance and its pace – are both intriguing and deeply concerning in equal measure. AI is already showing its immense capacity to improve and save life – for example, in the field of life sciences and medicine.

“Yet, those who have created these technologies are now increasingly warning that AI risks developing darker capacities – perhaps even to take life. In this regard, if I may say so, there seems urgency in adequately considering the existential dangers of such technologies falling into the wrong hands, and being used in potentially catastrophic ways. Surely, we need sufficient means of control before it is all too late?

“Those in our world who value deeply our humanity and its vital moral component are anxiously seeking your reassurance that we will not lose control of our destiny or our souls…”

## King warns of existential dangers of AI in wrong hands

The King has warned of the “existential dangers” of AI systems falling into the wrong hands, as he addressed tech leaders.

Speaking to bosses who have gathered for a summit on the technology, Charles said: “I am so grateful to you all for stepping away from your busy lives and making the journey to this rather remote but uniquely special corner of Scotland, at Dumfries House.

“As I was trying to explain to Jensen [Huang, chief executive of chipmaker Nvidia], this place is perhaps particularly meaningful to me, as it has become something of a living laboratory for what I have referred to – in a book I co-wrote some 16 years ago – as the philosophy of Harmony, which emphasises the balance, order, and relationships between ourselves and the natural world – a central truth to creating a future in which we are a part of nature, not apart from it.

“If Harmony has any relevance to today’s discussions, it is perhaps as a reminder that innovation must always be considered within the wider systems upon which all depend.”

## King arrives at AI summit amid uproar over bombshell claims

The King has arrived at a summit on artificial intelligence (AI) featuring leaders from some of the world’s biggest tech companies.

Charles arrived at the event in Dumfries House in Cumnock, East Ayrshire, dressed in a dark grey suit.

He met the bosses of Nvidia, Anthropic, Google DeepMind and OpenAI, along with a representative from the Vatican, minister for AI Kanishka Narayan and others.

In a speech opening the event, he is expected to say the new technology should be in the “service of humanity, community and the natural world”.

The event comes after an excerpt from a new book called Swan Song, about Diana, Princess of Wales written by her brother Earl Spencer, was published by the *Daily Mail* claiming that after her death in 1997 Charles said “rest assured, we’ll forget her soon enough”.

## How much did Harry and William know of Earl Spencer's claims?

Sources have claimed that Prince Harry was “aware” of the upcoming publication of Earl Spencer’s book about Princess Diana, but that he and William were not made aware of the contents of the book.

The *Daily Mail* reported Harry discussed the book with Earl Spencer during his visit to Althorp in July.

But a source told *The Telegraph* that the brothers were not given prior notice of allegations made in its pages.

It is understood Diana’s sons have not read the book.

It comes as Harry moved back to the UK with his wife Meghan Markle and their two children Archie and Lilibet.

## Palace's response was 'unprecedented', royal commentator says

Buckingham Palace’s decision to hit back at Earl Spencer’s claims over King Charles is “unprecedented,” a royal commentator has said.

Editor-in-chief of Majesty Magazine and author of Catherine And Diana, The Women Who Shaped The Monarchy, Ingrid Seward told the Press Association the palace’s response was “very unusual”.

“I imagine it’s because the King was very upset and wanted to set the record straight – more on behalf of his sons than anything else,” she said.

Commenting on how the claims will affect the King, Ms Seward told PA: “I think it’s very unpleasant for the King.

“For anyone to say something like that it’s extremely upsetting and I’m delighted he has hit back.”

She said she did not believe the palace would say anything further once the book has been published in full.

“They never comment on books,” she added.

“They might be unhappy about it but they always refrain from making comment.

“But this is very personal so it’s different, and also it’s said by Diana’s brother.”
