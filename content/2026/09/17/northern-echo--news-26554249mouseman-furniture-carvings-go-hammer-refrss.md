Rare and sought-after pieces by famous Yorkshire furniture maker Robert “Mouseman” Thompson will go under the hammer at a sale celebrating his work.

The Mouseman and Yorkshire Critters Sale takes place at Tennants Auctioneers on October 3, marking his 150th anniversary with early and contemporary furniture and carvings from his workshop.

A 1930s English oak Monk’s Chair from the Horlicks Collection is expected to fetch £3,000 to £5,000.

  Monk's chair 

*(Image: Supplied)*

An auction-house spokesperson said: "The chair was one of a set commissioned for the offices of ‘Messrs Horlick’ in Slough, each bearing the Horlicks coat of arms, and according to the ledgers of Robert Thomson were invoiced at a cost of £6 each."

A rare 1928 English oak Writing Box made for Annie Elizabeth Kilvington is estimated at £1,000 to £1,500.

A 1934 burr oak Dish Top Stool presented to Elinor Gertrude Lupton, chair of governors and benefactor at Leeds Girls’ High School, is also estimated at £1,000 to £1,500.

  Monk's chair 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Two 1930s Mouseman clocks are expected to fetch £2,000 to £3,000 each.

Highlights include a 1930s English oak Table with a cruciform base, estimated at £1,500 to £2,500, and a 2010 limited-edition Hovingham Estate English and burr oak Octagonal Coffee Table, made from oak from two ancient Hovingham Estate trees, estimated at £2,500 to £3,500.

The sale also includes Mouseman and Critter carvings.

Two English oak Pheasant carvings, among the most popular Mouseman pieces, are each estimated at £3,000 to £4,000.
