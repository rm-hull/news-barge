An inspirational young heart patient from the North East has completed the Mini Great North Run in the electric wheelchair that has helped transform his independence and quality of life.

Eleven-year-old Zain completed the event on Saturday in the chair provided by Red Sky Foundation, a North East heart charity that stepped in after his family struggled to secure the equipment he needed.

Zain lives with a complex congenital heart condition that has left him with "half a working heart," which means he tires easily and finds everyday activities extremely challenging.

  Zain with his family 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Dr Sergio Petrucci MBE, founder of Red Sky Foundation, said: "Seeing Zain cross that finish line on Saturday was incredibly special.

"When his parents first reached out to us, we knew we had to help.

"At the time, we wanted to give Zain more independence and allow him to save his energy for the things an 11-year-old should be enjoying.

"To now see him taking part in the Mini Great North Run in the very wheelchair we were able to provide is just fantastic.

  Inspirational 11-year-old Zain completed the Mini Great North Run in an electric wheelchair provided by Red Sky Foundation 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"This is exactly why Red Sky Foundation exists – to make a real, tangible difference to the lives of children and families living with heart conditions.

"We are so proud of Zain.

"He is a true Red Sky superstar and heart warrior."

  Inspirational 11-year-old Zain completed the Mini Great North Run in an electric wheelchair provided by Red Sky Foundation 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Earlier this year, the charity provided Zain with an electric wheelchair, which has already made a huge difference in his life.

It allows him to move independently at school, conserve energy, and spend more time enjoying life with friends and family.

On Saturday, that independence took him even further as he joined thousands of young people at the Mini and Junior Great North Run.

*(Image: Supplied)*

Dan Tucker, vice principal of Thorp Academy, previously described the wheelchair as having a "life-changing impact" on Zain.

He said it gave him greater independence and allowed him to engage fully with his peers and education in a mainstream setting.

His mother, Gulfareen Chohdry, said the effect on their family life has been "massive", as Zain now has enough energy left after the school day to play when he gets home.

Zain himself said: "I feel so much more independent at school now.

"Having total ease of access around the building has changed everything for me."

His achievement at the Mini Great North Run is a powerful example of how the right equipment and support can transform the life of a young person with a complex heart condition.

Red Sky Foundation supports babies, children and adults living with heart conditions across the region and beyond.

The charity funds life-saving and life-changing equipment, services, and support for those who need it most.

Its mission is to improve the lives of people needing cardiac care—whether born with heart conditions or who develop them later in life—by providing ongoing support to them and their families.

This includes funding equipment and resources for the Children's Heart Unit at the Freeman Hospital in Newcastle and other cardiac units in the region.

The charity also supplies defibrillators to schools and public areas and works with education and council partners to deliver training in CPR, defibrillator use, and broader cardiac care.

Red Sky Foundation collaborates with third-party organisations to offer additional education and support to families affected by heart conditions.

Dr Petrucci said: "Zain is just one example of the many children who benefit from the generosity of our supporters and the dedication of our team.

"His determination and courage inspire us all to keep pushing for better resources, equipment and care for heart patients across the North East and beyond."
