A Newton Aycliffe woman completed the Great North Run to raise money for the charity whose hearing dogs have transformed her aunt’s life.

CAD technician Michelle Mlatilik ran the 13.1-mile race on September 13 for Hearing Dogs for Deaf People.

She was inspired by her aunt Karen, also from Newton Aycliffe, who has benefited from two hearing dogs.

  Michelle Mlatilik from Newton Aycliffe completed the Great North Run to raise money for Hearing Dogs for Deaf People 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Michelle said: "Watching my aunt’s hearing dogs alert her to sounds is incredible.

"I’ve witnessed first-hand how her first hearing dog, Vimmie, and now her second dog, Gryffin, help her—not just practically, but emotionally too, as constant companions."

Karen said: "I’ve been so incredibly lucky to be matched with my first hearing dog, Vimmie, and now Gryffin.

"It’s only possible because of the fundraising efforts of wonderful people like Michelle, the volunteers who support the charity in so many ways and, of course, the staff.

"Huge thanks to everyone involved."

  Michelle Mlatilik from Newton Aycliffe with her Great North Run medal 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Michelle took up running in 2022 after joining Slimming World and losing three-and-a-half stone.

A friend persuaded her to enter the Wolf Run obstacle race in Warwickshire in 2023, sparking her passion for endurance events.

Michelle had never run a 5K before, but went on to tackle four ultramarathons in 2025.

They included the 30-mile Pen Llŷn, The Wall’s 70-mile route from Carlisle to Newcastle, the 100-mile Rat Race 100 and The Lap, a 47-mile race around Lake Windermere.

She said: "On the day of the Great North Run, it was really warm, which I think caught a few people off guard, so the main thing for me was making sure I didn’t overdo it in the heat.

"The support from the crowds was incredible - they were three or four deep all around the course, and because you had your name on your bib, they shouted your name and words of encouragement as you passed.

"It was absolutely buzzing."

Michelle finished in 3 hours 8 minutes, just short of her three-hour target.

She said: "The best part was getting across the finish line and knowing that I’d actually done it.

"I was in floods of tears.

"I felt very proud of myself and very lucky that I was physically able to take on the run.

"The first person I rang afterwards was my mum.

"Then I went to the Hearing Dogs tent, where I had a cuddle with hearing dog Elf."

Michelle hopes her story will persuade others to support the charity or take up running.

She said: "If my story - about someone who has gone from doing no exercise at all to completing a 100-mile ultra marathon two years later - can inspire one person to go out and get fit and raise money for Hearing Dogs, then it’s been worth doing."

In July, she completed the 100-mile Edinburgh to Glamis Castle ultra in 47 hours 37 minutes.
