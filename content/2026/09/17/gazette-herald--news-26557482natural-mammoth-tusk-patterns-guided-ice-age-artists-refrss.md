ICE Age artists found inspiration in the natural world in more ways than one.

A new study has revealed that 18,000-year-old female figurines from Ukraine, known as "Venus figurines," were engraved using the natural growth lines of mammoth ivory as a design guide.

Dr Aimée Little, from the Department of Archaeology at the University of York, said: "This discovery offers an extraordinary window into how Palaeolithic peoples perceived and engaged with their material world.

"Identifying visual evidence of how mammoths were integrated into artistic traditions, both through the use of their ivory and the deliberate inscription of its natural patterns, represents a genuine breakthrough in our understanding of prehistoric art and material culture."

The international research team used high-resolution microscopy and 3D modelling to analyse 18 figurines from the Mizyn site in northern Ukraine.

They found the carvers deliberately incorporated Schreger and Owen lines—distinctive rings found in mammoth tusks—into the engraved surface patterns.

The study, published in Heritage Science, was undertaken by teams at the University of York, the University of Stavanger, and the National Museum of the History of Ukraine.

The teams at the University of York, University of Stavanger, and the National Museum of the History of Ukraine said the findings challenged traditional views on how Stone Age art evolved across Europe.
