THERE’S a chance to find out about a new art and music project at a special ‘open studio’ event this weekend.

Parlour brings together Bradford artists Ben Holden and Chris Fleming-Wall.

Ben is a prominent multi-disciplinary artist, designer, printmaker and musician. Known for his vibrant, playful and high-energy style, his work spans across various mediums including murals, abstract prints and commercial graphic design. Ben was a touring musician in Bradford band This Et Al as well as being involved in other musical projects over the past 20 years.

Chris is an artist and musician with a history in film study. Now teaching and coaching in London, Chris writes the stories and narratives for Parlour, bringing them to life.

Over the past year the artists have been working together on new music as a duo - Ben at his Shipley-based studio and Chris in London. Ben has been sending ‘musical postcards’ as demos to London, with Chris reciprocating with words and vocals, creating a collection of new demos which they have recorded, at Lamigo Bay in the Scottish Highlands and HOHM Studios in Bradford.

  Some of the artwork the duo have produced. 

*(Image: Parlour)*

The result is an album featuring nine tracks “guided and shaped by stories projected through the human experience, all grounded in a lasting friendship, the need to express both the earthly and the dream-like and in the sheer joy of the creative process”.

The duo visited museums, discussed their cultural differences and delved deep into poetry, painting and sound, gaining inspiration. Blending their personal journeys with the history of recorded sound and cinema, they created music and painting that “honours the past while remaining an authentic expression of the present”.

Produced by Jamie Lockhart of Greenmount Studios and Tom Goodall of HOHM Studios, Parlour was produced in just eight days, a testament to the “creative telepathy” that has developed between the artists. Arriving in remote Lamigo Bay, Scotland from London and Saltaire in the summer of 2025 and on to urban Bradford in the spring of 2026, their recording sessions experimented with the cinematic, orchestral pop and soul, using a range of analogue and digital techniques, and in settings both natural and industrial.

In response to the music, Ben was inspired to create a new collection of large-scale paintings, creating a painting for each track - making for an immersive audio-visual experience.

Parlour is described as “a space to explore painting and music, working together to transform life into art.”

Find out more this weekend at an open studio event. Says Ben: “You can grab a sneak peak of the paintings where the work was made, original artwork, prints and other work. Learn about the project and purchase take-away mini-prints of the paintings with lyrics and links to stream the music.”
