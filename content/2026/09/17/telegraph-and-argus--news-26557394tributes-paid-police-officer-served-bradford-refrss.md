THE death of a "well-respected" police officer has prompted a wave of tributes from colleagues and the wider community.

PC Dominic Clegg, who served with West Yorkshire Police across Bradford and Leeds before moving to a role in learning and organisational development at its headquarters at Wakefield's Carr Gate, was honoured by the West Yorkshire Police Federation (WYPF).

The dad-of-two passed away following a "brave battle with mental illness".

## 'Well-respected colleague'

A spokesperson for the WYPF said: "We were devastated to learn of the unexpected death of our colleague PC Dominic Clegg.

"He was a well-respected colleague, and our thoughts are with all his family, friends, and colleagues.

"Funeral arrangements are yet to be confirmed but will be placed on the force website once known."

## Wife pays tribute

His wife Ruthie Clegg, shared a heartfelt post on social media.

She said: "Life has taken an unexpected turn and I have lost my heart and need time to heal.

"I'm taking some time away to be with my family and navigate the months ahead."

## 'Beautiful human being who was deeply loved'

A GoFundMe page has been set up by Hannah Cooper, former police officer and founder of Silence Between Sirens, to raise funds for PC Clegg's family.

Ms Cooper said: "On the 12/09/26, Ruthie and her two children tragically lost their beloved husband and dad, Dom, after his brave battle with mental illness.

"Dom was a beautiful human being who was deeply loved and his loss has left an unimaginable hole in the lives of those closest to him.

"While nothing can take away their pain, we hope the police, blue light family and wider community can come together to ease some of the financial pressure ahead and give Ruthie the space to focus on her children as they grieve for their dad."

More than £8,000 has been raised so far.

You can support the GoFundMe here: Fundraiser by Hannah Cooper : Supporting PC Cleggs Family

## More support needed for emergency workers

Silence Between Sirens was established by Ms Cooper, who spent 16 years in the force.

Her aim is to ensure that emergency personnel are able to access mental health support and feel it is safe to speak up when they are struggling.

WYPF has also encouraged anyone affected by PC Clegg’s death to seek support.

They said: "Anyone who is affected by PC Clegg's passing is encouraged to speak with their line managers, colleagues, local Federation Reps, or contact one of the various support networks available on the force intranet."
