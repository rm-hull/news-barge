## York CAMRA makes UK-first move with award for best NoLo beer

Nadine Waehning a senior lecturer at the University of York; Victoria Wells, the university’s Professor of Pubs, and York St John student Kaan Cetin. *(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

History was made at the York CAMRA beer festival this week, with a UK-first in judging no or low alcohol (NoLo) beers in a competition to find the best brews.

The move came as researchers at the University of York revealed new-look materials, which are being distributed free to pubs across the city to highlight the availability of NoLo drinks.

This followed the launch of a guide at last year’s York Camra beer festival, which once more is being held at St Lawrence Church, beginning yesterday (Wednesday) and running until Saturday.

A campaign involving York University researchers also included publishing research which revealed pubs can boost sales by offering low or no-alcohol beers, wines and spirits.

The campaign is spearheaded by Professor Victoria Wells, Professor of Sustainable Management in the School for Business and Society, and Dr Nadine Waehning, Senior Lecturer in Marketing in the School for Business and Society at the University of York.

Victoria says since the launch last year, the materials highlighting the availability of no or low alcohol drinks have been rolled out to 65 venues in York.

  Andy Mee of the Alcohol Free Drinks Company 

*(Image: Darren Greenwood)*

This was followed up with a map showing the venues where such drinks are available.

Nadine says they also talked to York CAMRA to suggest an award for NoLo drinks, which the 1,1100-member branch agreed to, the first award of its kind nationally.

Nadine says research shows that pubs selling low or no alcohol drinks did not suffer financially and they face no risk in stocking such products.

“Some venues found offering no-lo drinks increases their revenue,” she said.

“The Duke of York in Kings Square said they will offer it on draught.”

“Paul [Crossman] of the Slip Inn and The Swan confirmed they do increase revenue.”

The new promotional materials have been designed by York St John design and graphics student Kaan Cetin.

Kaan explained he sought to build on the designs that fellow York St John student Sam Armstrong created last year.

This led him to create a design with a ripple effect to highlight alcohol and liquids, which he said was very effective on beer mats.

Kaan said: “It’s more visually interesting, a pattern in branding gives people something to look at.”

“Visibility is important in this campaign. The NoLo team want these drinks to be easy to spot. If you have these designs available, you will be able to see where you can get these drinks from the bar. It also fights the idea of people being embarrassed to ask for NoLo drinks.”

The NoLo campaign was enhanced by the return of Andy Mee of Tockwith-based The Alcohol Free Drinks Company, who was displaying some of the ever-increasing range of NoLo beers, wines, spirits, cocktails and other drinks.

Andy says he was pleased to be raising awareness of such drinks.

He told the Press: “More and more venues some form of alcohol-free drinks. Many don’t realise there is so much choice. I am keen to get it through to people that there is and to work with Nadine on the campaign.”

“Business is doing very well. More and more venues are realising this is still beer, it just happens to have no alcohol in it. Generation Z and many young people don’t drink alcohol. But they want an adult experience. They don’t want pop.”
