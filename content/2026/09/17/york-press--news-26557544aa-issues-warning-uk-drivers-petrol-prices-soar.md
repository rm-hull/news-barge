Petrol prices have risen by more than 8p a litre across the UK since the start of September.

The average petrol price was 170.3p a litre yesterday, compared with 166.2p a week earlier and 161.8p at the beginning of the month.

Diesel ended the day at 192.7p a litre. This was 5p higher than a week ago, when the average was 187.7p, and 9.1p above the start-of-month figure of 183.6p.

Diesel remains 6.4p below its all-time record of 199.1p a litre, set during the early months of the Ukraine war.


Figures came from the Oil Market Journal, an industry-recognised source which also supplies fuel price information to the AA.

Petrol wholesale cost increases have shown some sign of slowing. Prices have remained between 79p and 81p a litre since the start of September.

Diesel wholesale cost increases remain severe, although they are still below the peak reached in June 2022.

Northern Ireland recorded the largest rise in pump prices during the past week. However, its average road fuel prices remain at least 5p a litre below those in even the cheapest English region.

Scotland remains the most expensive part of the UK for both petrol and diesel on average. It recorded the smallest pump rises last week.

Outside Northern Ireland, and excluding Costco sites where customers must pay a membership subscription, cheaper fuel is available in 11 towns and cities in England, Scotland and Wales.

Petrol was at least 10p a litre below the UK average in those locations. Prices were registered with Fuel Finder between September 10 and September 15.

For diesel, 39 town and city locations were charging 10p a litre or less below the UK average. Those prices were also registered with Fuel Finder between September 10 and September 15, excluding Northern Ireland and Costco sites.

Luke Bosdet, the AA’s spokesman on pump prices, said: "It is a huge question for the fuel trade: how can so many fuel stations be competitive and charge £80 less to refuel a Transit-type van compared to the UK average while the vast majority charge way more and put business customers under immense financial strain?"

Mr Bosdet said: "The 39 showing up on Fuel Finder since September 10 won’t be the only ones as a few others will have registered these ultra-competitive prices before then.

"Fuel Finder at least gives motoring consumers and businesses some chance of locating the cheaper fuel stations and taking their business away from the over-priced ones. We need more drivers to realise their consumer power and use it."

He said: "Why can so many forecourts still charge 10p below average for diesel? Because the presence of competitive fuel retailers and drivers prepared to go to them means the stranglehold of postcode lottery high pump prices can be broken."
