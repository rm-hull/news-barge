A primary school is celebrating strong SATs results, with pupils achieving above-average scores in reading and writing.

St Mary’s Catholic Primary School in Chippenham, part of the Dunstan Catholic Educational Trust, saw 96 per cent of pupils reaching the expected standard or above in writing, compared to the national average of 73 per cent.

In reading, 88 per cent met or exceeded the expected level, outperforming the national figure of 75 per cent.

Lucy Buckland, headteacher at St Mary’s, said: "We are incredibly proud of our pupils and the hard work they have put into their learning.

"It is particularly pleasing to see such strong results in reading and writing, which have been a real focus for us this year.

"Taking part in the National Year of Reading has given us even more opportunities to celebrate books and reading with our pupils and families, and our reading breakfasts have been a wonderful way to bring that into the wider school community.

"We hope the results reflect not only what our children have achieved, but also the enjoyment and confidence they are developing as readers."

In maths, 76 per cent of pupils reached the expected standard, slightly above the national average of 75 per cent.

Overall, 68 per cent of pupils achieved the expected standard or above in reading, writing, and maths combined, five points higher than the national figure of 63 per cent.

The school also excelled in the Year 1 Phonics Screening Check, with 95 per cent of pupils meeting the expected standard compared to 80 per cent nationally in 2025.

St Mary’s has been making the most of the National Year of Reading, prioritising reading for pleasure across the school.

Pupils take part in daily 'Read for Pleasure' sessions and have access to a wide variety of high-quality books.

The school also runs reading breakfasts, inviting families to join their children in school to read together.

St Mary’s has opened its library to families, encouraging parents and children to choose books and spend time reading together.

These activities are designed to make reading a core part of school life and to foster a lifelong love of books.

Helen Taylor, CEO of the Dunstan Catholic Educational Trust, said: "These are excellent results for St Mary’s and a real credit to the pupils and staff.

"The school’s focus on reading, supported by the involvement of families, is helping to build strong foundations for children as they progress through their education.

"We are delighted to see that reflected in these results."
