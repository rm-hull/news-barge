Community facilities could be improved if plans are agreed for a new cafe and solar panels at a church.

Plans have been drawn up to boost carbon efficiency and attract more visitors to Chapel Allerton Methodist Church.

Leeds City Council has been asked to approve a planning application for photovoltaic roof panels, two air source heat pumps and a cafe seating area.

Internal alterations and new entrance doors are also proposed for the church on Town Street, within Chapel Allerton Conservation area.

A design report said: “The proposals have been designed to improve the building’s environmental performance and support its continued community use while respecting its existing architectural character and conservation-area setting.”

It is hoped the plans will boost the church’s role as a community meeting place in the north east Leeds suburb.

The report said: “The introduction of a modest café within the existing lobby area represents a complementary and ancillary use that will enhance the church’s role as a community hub.

“The development will strengthen the function and attractiveness of the church as a community asset, helping to secure its ongoing use and viability.”

The solar panels would be mounted parallel to the existing south-facing roof slope, the report said.

It said: “Their regular arrangement and dark finish will provide a simple, coherent appearance against the roof.

“The heat pumps will be finished in black and positioned at high level on the north elevation, away from the principal entrance.

“Their location and restrained appearance will limit their prominence within the street scene and wider conservation area.”

The cafe and seating area would be installed in the existing ground floor space.

The report said: “It will provide an informal and welcoming space for church users, visitors and the wider community.”

Leeds City Council is considering a full planning application which is out for consultation until October 2.
