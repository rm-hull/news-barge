A BRADFORD district man who mistreated 13 horses has lost his appeal against a 10-year ban.

Henry Brewer, from Esholt Lane in Shipley, was originally convicted on March 26, 2025, of seven animal welfare offences involving the horses and ponies kept at sites in Baildon and Esholt.

The offences included four counts of causing unnecessary suffering to five horses and three counts of failing to meet the needs of 13 horses.

Brewer was sentenced to 14 weeks in prison, suspended for 12 months, and given a 10-year ban on owning equines.

He was also ordered to complete 100 hours of unpaid work, a 10-day rehabilitation activity requirement, and pay court costs.

However, he appealed both his conviction and sentence.

## 'A very upsetting case for our rescuers'

The appeal was heard at Bradford Crown Court from September 1 to 3 but was dismissed by a district judge and bench, who upheld all seven charges.

The original prosecution was brought by the RSPCA after concerns were raised in January 2023 about the welfare of horses under Brewer’s care.

An RSPCA spokesperson said: "This was a very upsetting case for our rescuers to investigate.

"Seeing this number of horses, including a foal, being let down by their owner, who continued to fail to provide them with suitable care despite advice and notices from us, was frustrating and very sad."

## Horses had 'limited access' to water and hay

The horses, kept at Old Hollins Hill and Park Lane in Baildon and a third site in Esholt, were found to have limited access to water and hay.

RSPCA officers raised concerns about the body condition of several animals, prompting them to leave a notice for improvement.

On January 18, 2023, RSPCA officers visited the Baildon sites with police and a vet.

  Many horses and ponies were found to be underweight and malnourished 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

Three horses, including a young foal, were removed from Old Hollins Hill due to being underweight.

Despite regular follow-up visits and attempts to work with Brewer, welfare issues persisted, and there was still not enough hay for the animals.

The RSPCA eventually issued formal warning notices for several of the remaining horses.

## Foal found lying on bricks and concrete

On March 6, 2023, RSPCA officers returned to the Esholt site with police and local authority staff.

They found a nine-month-old skewbald colt collapsed in a stable with no bedding, lying on uneven bricks and concrete.

The colt had a body condition score of just one out of five, with three considered ideal.

  A foal was found lying on bricks and concrete 

*(Image: RSPCA)*

Police removed the colt and additional horses from both Esholt and Old Hollins Hill.

The animals were placed in the care of the RSPCA.

A specialist equine vet, who examined the horses, said: "The poor body conditions of some of the horses in this case were very obvious to see.

"They were visually in varying degrees of very poor condition.

"The owner had been previously warned by the RSPCA Inspector about providing supplementary forage at all times for the horses and to improve body conditions."

They added that, if the horses had been left in the field and not removed by the RSPCA, it is likely that their conditions would have worsened.

## Brewer told vet horses were 'fine'

Brewer told the vet he believed the horses’ condition was "fine".

The court found Brewer guilty of causing unnecessary suffering by failing to address poor body condition, weight loss, and lack of endoparasitic treatment.

He was also found guilty of failing to provide adequate nutrition, parasite control, veterinary care, hoof and dental care, and a suitable environment for the horses.

Four ponies were euthanised by a vet due to poor health, but others have recovered and some have been rehomed.

Three foals were born at the RSPCA’s specialist equine facilities to mares who were already pregnant.

## 10-year horse owning ban remains

Brewer’s 10-year disqualification from owning equines remains in place.

He must also complete the unpaid work and rehabilitation activity as part of his suspended sentence.
