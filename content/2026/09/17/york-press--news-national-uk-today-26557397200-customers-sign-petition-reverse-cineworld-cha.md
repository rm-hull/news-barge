This week, many of Cineworld’s Unlimited members have complained about changes to its ticket booking system and now a petition for it to return to how it was has been started.

The change that has caused frustration among film fans is that customers are no longer able to book tickets for fellow Unlimited members.

Instead, anyone paying for the monthly subscription can only book a ticket for themselves, meaning they will need to change their seats once tickets have been booked if they want to sit next to another Unlimited member.

A message shared with Cineworld customers said: “You will no longer be able to book tickets for family members or friends who also have an Unlimited membership.

“Each Unlimited member will need to book their own tickets.

“Our Unlimited Terms will be updated to reflect this change.”

People shared their frustration across social media, including on X, where one person said: “Dear @cineworld my son is disabled and has an unlimited card.

“How am I meant to book tickets for him online?

“Why have you changed a good system and replaced it with this.

“The person who came up this needs sacking.”

## Cineworld customers start petition to reverse Unlimited ticket booking change

Unhappy with the change to the membership, some Cineworld customers have signed a petition which calls for it to be reversed.

The petition, titled “Restore booking privileges for Cineworld Unlimited members”, has been signed 230 times at the time of writing.

It was started by Simon Cooke on Change.org and includes details of the impact the Cineworld change is having on customers: “Unfortunately, this feature has been revoked, and it has deeply affected our cultural and social outings.

“For many Unlimited members like myself, the joy of sharing these cinematic experiences with loved ones has been significantly curtailed.

“It’s not just about watching films; it’s about connecting, sharing emotions and creating memories with those who matter most to us.

“Cineworld’s decision to remove this feature may seem minor to some but it deeply impacts the way we socialize and celebrate art.

“In an era where digital connections often outweigh personal interactions, gatherings at the movie theatre are one of the few opportunities to foster genuine, shared experiences.”

Cooke added: “We urge Cineworld to reconsider and reinstate this vital component of the Unlimited card.

“By doing so, they would be acknowledging the community aspect of film watching and fostering relationships, which is fundamental to their identity as an entertainment provider.”

The cinema chain said it is “working on an improved solution” to allow Unlimited members to book in groups in the future.

A Cineworld spokesperson told Newsquest: “As communicated to our Unlimited customers, Cineworld is launching a new and improved mobile-first website and app for our customers.

“The change has been introduced as part of the wider evolution of our booking platforms.
