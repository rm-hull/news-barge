Darlington’s Ruth Hansom has been named a finalist in the National Chef of the Year competition again.

Originally from Darlington and now chef-owner of Hansom Restaurant in Bedale, she is one of just 10 chefs shortlisted for the UK’s top culinary honour.

David Mulcahy, competition director of National Chef of the Year, said: "Reaching the National Chef of the Year final is an outstanding achievement.

"Every chef who has made it this far has demonstrated exceptional skill, commitment, and professionalism throughout the competition.

"Year after year, the standard continues to impress the judges, and we look forward to seeing how Ruth rises to the challenge on the day."

Ruth first made history in 2016 as the first female winner of the Young National Chef of the Year title and made the final of National Chef of the Year last year too.

She has since gained further recognition through appearances on the BBC’s Great British Menu.

The final will take place on Tuesday, October 6 at Le Cordon Bleu in London, where the 10 chefs will compete in front of a distinguished judging panel.


Mark Birchall, chair of judges and chef patron of Moor Hall, said: "I’ve been incredibly impressed by the commitment and effort that’s gone into the competition so far.

"I know how much pressure chefs put themselves under, but also how much they grow and learn from the experience.

"My advice to Ruth is to keep it simple and focus on creating delicious, brilliantly executed dishes rather than trying to overcomplicate things."

The National Chef of the Year competition is organised by the Craft Guild of Chefs and judged by some of the most respected names in the industry.

Finalists are selected through a rigorous process that tests creativity, technical skill, flavour, presentation, and the ability to perform under pressure.

The winner will be announced at an awards ceremony later that evening, attended by leading figures from across the hospitality industry.
