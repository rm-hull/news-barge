Good morning.

Donald Trump has threatened the EU with “serious tariffs” after the European Commission president, Ursula von der Leyen, invited Canada to become the EU’s first “associate member”.

The US president called the idea of Canada joining the EU “laughable” and said that, should the EU move forward with the plan, Washington could impose “very serious tariffs or stop trading with Europe”. “If it’s a good intention, that’s fine. If it’s a bad intention, we’ll put very heavy tariffs on Europe,” he said.

- 
  **Why does the EU want closer ties with Canada?** Von der Leyen told the European parliament that, as democracies faced a “fracture in the international rules-based system”, Brussels and Ottawa needed to pull closer together. “This is a partnership not against anyone else, but for our common strength,” she said.
- 
  **How has the EU responded to Trump’s comments?** France’s Europe minister, Benjamin Haddad, said on Thursday that Washington had no power to veto EU decisions. “It is not up to the United States, or anyone else, to choose the political and geopolitical direction of the Europeans,” Haddad told the Public Sénat TV channel.
- 
  **What’s next?** Canada’s prime minister, Mark Carney, has been addressing the European parliament on Thursday. Follow our live coverage here.

## **US Federal Reserve raises interest rates for the first time since 2023**

![Warsh stands at a podium, with two American flags behind him](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/19b4fea96ad4f86785e9a78c0025b23bb4d7eaeb/0_0_5295_3530/master/5295.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The US Federal Reserve voted on Wednesday to raise interest rates for the first time since 2023, from 3.75% to 4%, as the central bank continues to fight to tamp down inflation. “The plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long,” said Kevin Warsh, the Fed chair.

- 
  **Did Warsh identify what is driving inflation?** Warsh acknowledged changing geopolitics, but avoided calling out the US-Israel war with Iran by name.
- 
  **How did Trump respond?** Shortly after the announcement, Trump said in a Truth Social post that interest rates should be 1% or less and criticized the US trade deficit.

## **House speaker calls early recess, sidestepping AI regulation frenzy and a vote to impeach Pete Hegseth**

![Johnson stands in front of an open door into his office, facing the camera but looking slightly to the side](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/acb7dd70e98c30783d00a8a7ada783396a8fe00d/0_0_8640_5760/master/8640.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The Republican speaker of the House, Mike Johnson, announced Wednesday that he would be sending lawmakers home one day early ahead of the midterm election recess – a decision that lets House members avoid voting on Republican Thomas Massie’s resolution to impeach the defense secretary, Pete Hegseth, and a frenzied attempt to propose legislation on artificial intelligence.

- 
  **What has the response been to Johnson’s decision?** “I think it shows the cowardice of the Republican party. They are canceling votes to do business for the American people because they are afraid that that impeachment resolution of Pete Hegseth would pass on a bipartisan basis,” the Democratic representative Pramila Jayapal told MediasTouch.
- 
  **What’s going on with AI?** More than 100 House Democrats had urged Johnson to cancel the upcoming recess to allow them to craft legislation on AI safeguards. But Johnson told reporters on Tuesday that “we cannot have a moratorium on the development of AI”.

## **Stat of the day:The world’s rivershad one of their driest years in more than three decades in 2025**

![Boats sit on dry, cracked ground by the Danube River in Petrovaradin, Serbia.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/cb6985d7649c89c505ce62b30cb3468b2345dad2/0_0_8323_5549/master/8323.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Last year was among the driest years on record for rivers across the world, with 36% of the global river basin area documenting river flows below or well below normal, according to an assessment of the global water cycle by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO). Overall, the amount of freshwater held on or under the ground was 42% below normal across the world last year.

## **Don’t miss this: How the attention economy overwhelmed children’s TV**

![Old-fashioned televisions are stacked against a red-orange backdrop. Upon each screen are images from various children televisions shows: Teletubbies, Sesame Street, Bluey, Prehistoric Planet, Cocomelon and Mr Rogers](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/04583e475a30ce4d8b4c133214e900ef7d3af245/164_318_4594_3675/master/4594.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Pacing is everything when it comes to children’s entertainment, writes Natalia Kucirkova, a senior lecturer in early years and childhood studies at Manchester Metropolitan University – something that should be especially concerning in an attention economy that overwhelms and overshouts. When media moves too quickly, it risks losing the time needed for a child to make meaning. A child watching a character comfort a friend may not be able to recognize the repair or imagine doing the same thing with his or her own peers if the scene moves too quickly. A child must notice that another person feels something different in order for the child to develop empathy, Kucirkova writes – something that takes time.

## **Climate check: Global heating was a destabilizing factor in the glacier collapse that caused the Nepal-Tibet floods**

![An aerial view of a town destroyed after a catastrophic glacial flood in Devighat Bazaar, Nuwakot, Nepal](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/0d4c471b83e2aad31168203ad0e0c4a7892689be/487_0_2453_1962/master/2453.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Researchers found that the climate breakdown likely weakened the glacier that collapsed last month, causing catastrophic floods that killed hundreds of people in Nepal and Tibet. In the region where the collapse happened, July and August 2026 were the warmest on record locally, reaching 5C above the 30-year average in the days before the collapse. In this region, the climate breakdown has caused about 2C of warming since preindustrial times.
