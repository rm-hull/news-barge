Drivers in the UK are being urged to carry out three essential vehicle checks in the next week.

Motorists are being encouraged to check their tyres, fluids and lights before autumn arrives.

Ensuring your car is ready for changing weather conditions could help motorists avoid costly penalties, a motoring expert has said.

James Harding, tyre specialist at Blackcircles.com, said: "As we head into autumn, there are additional road risks motorists must be aware of.


"Colder temperatures make seasonal checks such as those around vehicle fluid levels, the condition of tyres, and lighting efficiency, more important.

"Checking your tyres should be a priority.

"With autumn comes more rainfall and fallen leaves.

"Your tyres need to have sufficient grip and be able to channel water effectively through the tread.

"If the tyre tread is below the legal minimum of 1.6mm, the car’s handling can be impaired, increasing the risk of an accident."

Tyre checks are a key part of autumn vehicle maintenance.

Drivers are advised to ensure tyre tread depth meets the legal minimum of 1.6mm.

A simple way to check this is by inserting a 20p coin into the tread.

If the outer band of the coin is visible, the tread is too low and the tyre should be replaced.

Tyre pressure should also be checked regularly, as it can drop in colder temperatures.

Under-inflated tyres can affect vehicle handling and grip, increasing the risk of accidents.

Drivers found with illegal tyres could face fines of up to £2,500 and three penalty points per tyre.

In addition to tyres, motorists should check and top up all essential fluids.

This includes engine oil, brake fluid, coolant, and wiper fluid.

Drivers are also reminded to check their vehicle’s lights as daylight hours get shorter.

All headlights, brake lights, tail lights, fog lights, and indicators should be tested to ensure they are working correctly.

Faulty or missing lights can result in fines of up to £1,000 and three penalty points.

Driving without necessary lights at night or in poor visibility can also lead to a fixed penalty of £50 to £100.

Mr Harding warned that autumn brings additional hazards, including fallen leaves that can hide potholes, road markings, and debris.

He said: "In areas where fallen leaves are particularly obstructive, motorists should drive slowly and with care, avoiding thick coverings of leaves where it is safe to do so.

"Potholes can cause significant damage to vehicles, from suspension damage to punctured tyres."

He added that a recent survey by Blackcircles.com found the average Briton has spent £200 on pothole-related vehicle repairs.

Mr Harding said: "So, remaining vigilant and cautious when road conditions are obscured is critical for both your safety and your finances."

With weather conditions already starting to change, drivers are encouraged to carry out these checks as soon as possible.

Taking simple steps now could help avoid breakdowns, accidents, or unexpected fines in the months ahead.
