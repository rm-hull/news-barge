A Keighley man will complete a 1,400-mile cycle ride in memory of his brother, who died by suicide.

Gaz Mullen, 25, is cycling from Bradford to Benidorm to raise money for Survivors of Bereavement by Suicide, a charity supporting families affected by suicide.

He lost his brother Jamie, who was 28 at the time, last September.

Mr Mullen plans to arrive in Benidorm on September 17, the anniversary of Jamie’s death.

Mr Mullen said: “Jamie was the most outgoing person I have ever met.

"He was a very hands-on person who was always busy.

"He loved bikes - motorbikes and dirt bikes.”

### 'There's a lot of unanswered questions and blame'

The challenge began after Mr Mullen decided to change his own life.

He said he believed Jamie would have wanted more for him.

Mr Mullen said: “I was going down a slippery slope with drinking, and I knew that was not what my brother would have wanted for me.

"He was always so proud of me.

"I wanted to do something to make him proud, as well as help other families who have gone through what mine has.”

Jamie’s death had a major effect on the family.

Mr Mullen said the news created questions and feelings of blame.

Mr Mullen said: “It’s a massive, life-changing thing that happened to all of us.

"When you lose someone to suicide, it’s not quite the same as losing somebody to old age or illness.

"There are a lot of unanswered questions and blame you put on yourself.”

### Jamie believed he needed to 'man up'

Through the cycle ride, he wants to raise awareness of men’s mental health and encourage people to speak about their feelings and seek help.

Mr Mullen said: “Jamie struggled with his mental health but kept it to himself.

"I spoke to him the day before he passed away, and one of the last things he told me was that he had been feeling suicidal.

  Jamie Mullen

"Because he was such a confident person, always bubbly, the life of the party, it was a huge shock.”

Mr Mullen said: “The last thing he said was that he needed to man up.

"I think that, as men, that agenda is pushed onto us.

"Jamie hadn’t realised that there was support out there for him.”

### 'I want men to know it's okay to speak out'

Mr Mullen has been sharing videos about the journey online.

The aim, he said, is to show men that asking for support is possible and should happen earlier.

Mr Mullen said: “For men who are struggling with mental health, I want it to be more normalised.

"And it should be more normalised to get help early on.

"With my videos, I’m trying to be as open and transparent about my own mental health to inspire as many men who are struggling as possible.

"I want to let them know it’s okay to speak out.”

### Plans to sprinkle Jamie's ashes in Benidorm

Benidorm was chosen because it has a connection with both brothers.

When Mr Mullen was 18, Jamie invited him on a holiday there with his friends.

Mr Mullen said: “He invited me along on his holiday, and it was the craziest experience ever.

  Gaz and Jamie Mullen

"It holds a special place in my heart.

"I’ve got some of Jamie’s ashes, and I want to spread them when I get to Benidorm."

### 'I'll crawl there if I have to'

The physical demands of the ride have also tested Mr Mullen.

He had not regularly exercised or cycled before starting the journey, and has spent around eight hours each day riding while away from his family.

Mr Mullen said: “I’ve never really done any exercise before, and definitely not cycling, so it’s been a huge test.

"But I’m doing it. That’s the main thing.

"I will crawl to Benidorm if I have to.”

### Friends and family at the finish line

Family and friends are expected to meet him at the finish line.

Mr Mullen said the anniversary would be difficult, but hoped completing the challenge would help.

He said: “I’ve got a lot of family and friends flying out to meet me at the finish line.

"It’s going to be a very difficult day, but with the achievement, I’m hoping it will make everything a little bit easier.”

More than £5,000 has been raised so far for Survivors of Bereavement by Suicide.
