## 'We've been rooting for her' - hospice celebrates Anna's Sewing Bee win

Anna in a St Leonard's Hospice Shop in York *(Image: St Leonard's Hospice)*

St Leonard’s Hospice has celebrated the success of retail team member Anna for her “incredible” win in this year’s Great British Sewing Bee.

The hospice, which Anna has dedicated herself, said it had been rooting for her, awing at her achievements each week.

A spokesperson told The Press: “We’re incredibly proud of what Anna has achieved, and it’s been brilliant watching her on the show.

“Working as part of our Retail team, Anna has brought such creativity, enthusiasm and dedication to her role.

“It’s been wonderful seeing those qualities recognised on such a public stage.”

  


Anna, 27, moved judge Patrick Grant to tears as he spoke of her 10-week journey in the Sewing Bee studio.

  The 27-year-old said she had learnt "so much" through the competition 

*(Image: BBC)*

He said: “I am so delighted for Anna. When extraordinary craft people make things with skill, imagination and extraordinary storytelling, it affects you deeply.

“She’s got a really rare gift.”

Anna’s inventive pieces over ten episodes saw her take home eight transformation challenge wins, along with a hattrick of Garment of the Weeks.

In the final episode, her transformation of a tablecloth into an outfit ready for a beach club won praise from both judges.

Esme Young said it had been difficult when deciding the winner of the round, but Anna’s design was “just cool”.

The competition was described as the “closest in several years” by Patrick after fellow contestant’s final Met Gala-inspired dress won the hearts of him and Esme.

  Judge Patrick Grant was reduced to tears after Anna's win 

*(Image: BBC)*

Speaking about her win, Anna said: “Costume Design is my next goal, and I absolutely loved the Literary Icons week!

“It is my dream to costume design for a period drama film so to have the chance to show my design idea for a literary classic on TV was a really special moment.

“Even though I didn’t have quite enough time to finish it to the standard I would have liked, I was still really proud of what I made.

  Anna's mum Brenda, dad Steve and partner Sebastian were all overjoyed by her win 

*(Image: BBC)*

She added: “I do have a love of corsetry, and I got into sewing by fashion history and how you can manipulate the shape of a body through fabric and structure.

“I also work at a local Hospice. I started in January this year, and it’s a very special place.

“It is a treasured charity in York, and I feel good to be able to give something back to the community.

“I really find it very rewarding.

“My fellow Bees at the Final, Emma and Beth – I am so close to them, and I still hang out with them - I was rooting for them as much as myself.

“It was like being with friends. I would love to thank my close friends and family for all their encouragement along the way.

“My partner has always encouraged me to follow my passions. Everyone has been so supportive.”

Anna said she has also had lots of support from people in the street in York and on social media.

She said: “York is a very friendly city. I have been recognized and as people have said such lovely comments and how the series has inspired them to sew, it’s been a really nice experience.”
