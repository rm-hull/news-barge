Residents, councillors, animal welfare advocates and health professionals gathered in Bradford city centre to call for stronger action against the anti-social use of fireworks.

The protest, held at Norfolk Gardens this past Saturday, focused on concerns about them being set off late into the night in residential areas.

The demonstration was organised by Inayah Sher, who said the campaign was aimed at tackling anti-social behaviour rather than ending all firework celebrations.

She said: "This protest is mostly about antisocial use of wedding fireworks.

"We're not against fireworks in general. We're not against Bonfire Night, we're not against Diwali.

"We expect fireworks on those religious and annual events."

  Inayah Sher at the protest on Saturday. 

*(Image: T&A)*

## Illegal users have 'no respect for anyone'

Inayah said residents had reported fireworks being set off for extended periods, often late into the night.

She said: "These are happening from 4pm until 2am. Those people who are using fireworks, they have no respect for anyone."

Inayah called for tougher penalties for those found using fireworks illegally and suggested the use of surveillance cameras in areas where repeated complaints are received.

She said: "We want stronger enforcement. We want more powers to be given to the police."

She added: "We want those people that are using them illegally to be penalised heavily.

"Forget about £90, we want a £5,000 fine and six months prison sentence."

## What has the council done to address concerns?

In June, the T&A reported that Bradford Council was launching a campaign to tackle the anti-social use of fireworks.

The authority said it was working with wedding venues, supporting Government plans for tougher firework laws and partnering with West Yorkshire Trading Standards to tackle illegal sales.

## Health concerns raised

Health concerns were also raised during the demonstration.

Shahid Islam, a principal research fellow at the Bradford Institute for Health Research, said repeated exposure to noise pollution can have wider consequences for communities.

He said: "Noise and air pollution and lack of greenery are known for reducing life expectancy.

"The other thing about noise pollution is it is one of the causal factors for dementia.

"One of the big problems that we hear from our neighbours, some of them are here today, is the constant din caused by late night fireworks."

He argued that stronger regulation was needed and suggested fireworks should only be available through licensed events.

  Rose the dog at the protest on Saturday 

*(Image: T&A)*

## 'I can't leave the house'

Among the personal stories shared on the day was that of Jennifer Peers, who attended alongside her assistance dog, Rose.

Jennifer explained that Rose plays a crucial role in her day-to-day life, and that both are affected by fireworks.

She said: "If there's fireworks, she won't leave the house, which means I can't leave the house."

She also described the impact fireworks had when Rose was expecting puppies.

Jennifer said: "Because of the fireworks she went into early labour and all the pups died because they came early."

## Police need to 'step up'

There were some in attendance who offered an alternative view.

Firework shop owner Wayne Richardson said he understood residents' frustrations but believed the focus should be on enforcement rather than restricting legitimate sales.

He said: "These are local issues that I'm seeing here today and they're in certain parts, in certain cities within the UK.

"This is not happening in every town."

On illegal use, Richardson added: "This is where the police need to step up with the powers.

"They should prosecute, maximum six months in prison.

"Unlimited fine, or on the tamer side, on the spot penalty."

  Councillor Ishtiaq Ahmed speaking at the protest on Saturday.

## Councillor calls for greater education

The debate over how best to tackle the problem was reflected by Councillor Ishtiaq Ahmed (Heaton and Frizinghall, Green Party), who questioned whether a complete ban would be effective.

He said: "I'm not so sure calling for an outright ban will actually seize the late-night fireworks because that will only push the market for fireworks underground and it'll create a whole black market."

He added: "What I'm more in favour of is create a wider awareness and education program around the impacts of late-night fireworks on communities, people, elderly, wildlife, animals."

Cllr Ahmed also argued that enforcement of existing laws should be strengthened.

He said: "I've not known anybody to be prosecuted for late night fireworks thus far, and I think if that precedent is set, people realise that they will get prosecuted."

The T&A have approached the Department for Business & Trade for comment.
