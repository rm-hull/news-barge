A County Durham sports centre has been put up for sale, with the charity behind it saying they can no longer afford to run the facility.

Crook Community Leisure blamed rising energy and maintenance costs for the decision and said it wants to secure the site’s future.

The current centre was developed after a public vote in 2013, when the project secured £500,000 in funding from a community fund.

The facility opened in 2017 and has since provided a base for residents, community groups and junior sports.

But the charity says the financial pressures facing community leisure facilities have changed significantly.

  Dorothy Winter at the leisure facility 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

Trustee Dorothy Winter said: “This is a decision we have not wanted to make, but we have to be realistic.

"The costs of running a facility such as this have increased enormously and, as trustees of a charity, we have a responsibility to ensure that we act according to Charity Commission rules and sadly know when enough is enough.

“We have done everything we can to keep the centre operating, but we simply cannot continue to subsidise it indefinitely.

“We are selling because we have to, not because we want to.”

Covid, soaring energy and utility bills, higher National Living Wage and National Insurance costs, and an ageing site have put the charity under severe pressure.

Despite healthy membership and efforts to raise income, the volunteer trustees say they can no longer responsibly use charity funds to subsidise the centre.

The trustees hope organisations or developers with the money and vision to invest can give the site a sustainable future.

The site includes the existing leisure centre, sports facilities, a running track, football pitch and adjoining land.

  Dorothy Winter at the leisure facility 

*(Image: SUBMIT)*

The charity previously explored an extension and extra facilities, but planning difficulties scuppered the plans.

Barnard Castle estate agent Jonathan Wallis is marketing the property with an offers-over guide price of £187,500.

The centre hosts community activities, including twice-weekly meetings for more than 60 local people, and supports junior football clubs that use and help maintain its facilities.

The trustees also intend to keep the centre open for as long as possible during the sale process.

Staff have been told, and the trustees will support them through the transition and will work to keep their jobs under a new operator.

Dorothy added: “We know people will be disappointed, upset or angry about this decision, and we completely understand that.
