A York MP has backed government plans for tighter controls on the loudest fireworks to protect veterans, neurodiverse people and pets.

The Department for Business and Trade is consulting on a number of proposals which include restricting the noisiest fireworks to professional displays.

A public consultation is underway after years of pressure from animal welfare charities, veterans’ groups and community organisations.

Residents, businesses and community groups across York and North Yorkshire are being urged to have their say before the deadline, Wednesday, October 7.

  York MP Rachael Maskell backs firework rules to protect pets and veterans. Pictured, right, with Kate Dearden, MP for Halifax and Minister for Consumer Protection, discussing the Labour Government's action on fireworks. 

*(Image: Submitted)*

The reforms target a small number of extremely loud pyrotechnics blamed for distress among residents, pets and wildlife, while allowing organised displays to continue as normal.

York Central MP Rachael Maskell has spoken about the issue in Parliament and has thrown her weight behind calls for tighter controls and a review of the volume of fireworks.

Minister for Consumer Protection Kate Dearden said “extremely loud fireworks used by individuals at all hours… has become a nightmare for many communities”, adding the government wants to take them “out of the hands of those who use them irresponsibly”.

Ms Maskell told The Press: “The RSPCA has called for stricter regulation of the sale and use of fireworks and the Labour Government has responded to this long-running campaign by opening a consultation for business, consumer groups, charities and people to respond with their views and contribute to the evidence regarding the use of fireworks.

"The previous Government said that the current regulations were comprehensive and wouldn’t take further action.

“I believe that most people use fireworks in a responsible, safe and appropriate manner. The sale of fireworks to the public is prohibited except for from licensed traders, and from unlicensed traders for Chinese New Year, Diwali, Bonfire Night celebrations and New Year.

“I support tighter controls on fireworks and would welcome a review of the volume of ‘bangs’ in fireworks which can have a profound impact on veterans, on people who experience mental health challenges, on neurodiverse people; as well as on animals. I have spoken in Parliament about this before and will be closely following the Government’s action.”

Animal welfare groups such as the Dogs Trust have long campaigned for change, highlighting that many dogs are affected by fireworks, with many owners also distressed by seeing their pets frightened and anxious.

Combat Stress has also said how sudden explosions can trigger traumatic memories for veterans, leading to higher rates of distress during firework season.

The consultation proposes targeted reforms to reduce noise and risks by restricting the loudest fireworks to professional use only, alongside a review of the banned list of pyrotechnic products and possible updates to requirements for lower-risk items.

Officials say the aim is to tackle the biggest public concerns, particularly noise and antisocial behaviour, while protecting traditions and organised displays that bring communities together.

Responses can be submitted directly to the consultation by email to [fireworksconsultation@businessandtrade.gov.uk](mailto:fireworksconsultation@businessandtrade.gov.uk) or on the government consultation page online [https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/fireworks-and-pyrotechnics-in-the-uk/fireworks-and-pyrotechnics-in-the-uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/fireworks-and-pyrotechnics-in-the-uk/fireworks-and-pyrotechnics-in-the-uk)
