Rishi Sunak has praised efforts to save GP services in remote North Yorkshire after visiting a medical centre whose future had been in doubt.

The Richmond and Northallerton MP visited Reeth Medical Centre, which serves Swaledale and Arkengarthdale, to meet Central Dales Practice managing partner Lynn Irwin and University Hospitals Tees representatives.

Mr Sunak said: "It was important to me that every possible option was explored when we were told the practice was going to close.

  Richmond and Northallerton MP Rishi Sunak at the Reeth Medical Centre 

*(Image: Supplied)*

"Lynn came forward with a practical solution, and I am very pleased that Central Dales Practice is now providing GP services from Reeth again.

"I want to thank Lynn and her team, University Hospitals Tees, the ICB, Cllr Yvonne Peacock and everyone else who worked hard to make this happen.

"It is particularly important for people in Swaledale and Arkengarthdale to have access to healthcare close to home."

  Richmond and Northallerton MP Rishi Sunak at the Reeth Medical Centre 

*(Image: Supplied)*

University Hospitals Tees has secured a five-year lease, allowing Central Dales Practice to run the centre as a branch surgery for more than 1,600 patients.

The surgery currently opens on Tuesdays, Wednesdays and Thursdays, with medicines available for collection on site.

The centre’s future was thrown into doubt when Dr Mike Brookes and his wife, Marie, announced their retirement and no replacement GP was found.

  Rishi Sunak with representatives from Central Dales Practice and University Hospitals Tees at Reeth Medical Centre 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Sunak urged the NHS Humber and North Yorkshire Integrated Care Board to rethink the closure and keep a local GP service running.

He then worked with health leaders and Councillor Yvonne Peacock as Central Dales Practice developed plans to take over the site as a branch surgery.

Lynn Irwin first contacted Mr Sunak about Central Dales Practice running Reeth as a branch surgery.

The local primary care network and University Hospitals Tees then helped develop the proposal.

Mr Sunak also heard plans to expand community services across Swaledale, Arkengarthdale and the wider Dales.

They include support for housebound patients and more specialist healthcare closer to home.
