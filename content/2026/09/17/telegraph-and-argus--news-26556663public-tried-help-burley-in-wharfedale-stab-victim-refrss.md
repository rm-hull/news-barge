A jury has heard how members of the public who tried to help a stab victim were met with a gruesome sight.

John Robinson, 30, of Derry Hill, Menston, is on trial at Bradford Crown Court. He denies murdering Mason Jones on March 1 this year in Burley-in-Wharfedale.

He also denies manslaughter and having a bladed article in public.

The third day of the trial heard from eyewitnesses to the immediate aftermath of the stabbing, which took place in an alleyway leading to a flat off Iron Row, near to Main Street, at about 9.50am.

One of the first people on the scene was a driver who had been making deliveries in the area. He saw Mr Jones staggering onto Main Street.

In a statement read to the court, he said it was clear that Mr Jones had an extremely severe abdominal injury.

At that point Mr Jones fell down onto his side before turning onto his back.

The driver said Mr Jones was not responsive enough for him to talk to him, although he did open his eyes. He suggested placing Mr Jones in the recovery position and also recalled trying to open his airway.

He said: “I remember the incident because it shocked me.”

Another witness who was a first aider saw 30-year-old Mr Jones lying on the pavement with his feet hanging off the kerb.

She said he was unconscious when she knelt next to him and that another woman was putting pressure on his abdomen.

On opening his jacket, she said she could see a significant wound to his abdomen.

She said the other lady kept pressure on the wound, and a different woman was speaking to the injured man’s friend [Will Clarke], trying to comfort him.

She said he had a can of lager in his pocket, which he took out.

She said: “He was very worried about the injured man and kept asking if he was going to die.”

She was told the injured man was called Mason and said: “I remember saying his name to him whilst he was unconscious.”

The jury heard that Mr Jones had two seizures while he was lying on the pavement.

The trial has previously heard how Mr Jones and Mr Clarke travelled from Bury, where they were on a night out together, to Burley-in-Wharfedale at the invitation of Annabel Ramsay.

But they were ejected from her flat following an argument between Mr Clarke and Miss Ramsay’s friend and spent some time wandering around the village before returning to flat to ask to charge a mobile phone.

By that time Miss Ramsay had contacted her on/off boyfriend John Robinson and urged him to come and help her get rid of them. He travelled there in an uber.

It is alleged that Robinson stabbed Mr Jones during an altercation in a narrow alleyway leading to Miss Ramsay’s flat.

Another witness was walking his dog at about 9.50am when he heard shouting and swearing.

His attention was drawn to two men who were accepted to be Mr Jones and Mr Clarke.

In a video interview with police, he said: “I heard voices. I thought, ‘What are they up to?’ Then I heard shouting and this started to get louder. Then I heard the F-word. I thought there must be something more serious.

“Then one of the lads came out sideways in a hurry followed by the other one going backwards, falling as he went.”

He described a woman, previously named in court as Annabel Ramsay, “lurching forward with her hands on him [and] pushing him, and he is going backwards.

“She was shouting and screaming at him.”

He added that “she pushed him out of the alleyway. All she had on were a bra and pants.

“[The second man] then got hold of his side and his stomach, and with the other lad ran [out of Iron Row] and turned to go on Main Street.

“Then the lad fell into the road. His pal got him under the arms and pulled him onto the footpath.

“[Clarke] said: “Ring an ambulance! Ring an ambulance! He’s been stabbed!”

The man was one of several members of the public who called 999.

The court also heard from a taxi driver who picked up both John Robinson and Annabel Ramsay at 9.59am.

He said Robinson smelt strongly of “weed”, or cannabis, and said he was “quiet” rather than “chatty” as he had been on previous journeys.

He had been hired to go to Robinson’s address at Derry Hill in Menston but spotted an ambulance further down the street, which he remarked upon.

In his statement he said: “Neither of them responded or spoke. They sat in silence. I did find this odd as John is usually quite chatty.”

The trial continues.
