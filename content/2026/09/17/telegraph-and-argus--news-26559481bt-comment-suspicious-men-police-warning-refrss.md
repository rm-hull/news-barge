A BROADBAND provider has commented after police issued a public warning over "suspicious" people claiming to represent them.

The force said men were reported knocking on doors in Cleckheaton on Tuesday asking to check residents' phone lines and internet access saying they were from BT/Openreach.

## What have BT said about it?

BT has now confirmed to the Telegraph & Argus that these men were legitimate sales agents.

The provider said it was a third party field sales team working on behalf of BT and EE.

## Full BT statement

A BT spokesperson said: "We can confirm that a third party field sales team working on behalf of BT and EE were present in the Cleckheaton area on Tuesday.

"BT or Openreach engineers wouldn’t be sent out to a property without a prior arranged appointment.

"However, we do work with third party field sales teams, like Money Expert (part of the Credico Group) who work throughout England and Scotland letting consumers know about the latest BT and EE offers available in their area."

## ID carried

BT emphasised that all field sales agents carry identification and that their credentials can be verified.

The spokesperson said: "Field sales agents will always carry ID - this can be verified by calling the number on the ID Card for Money Expert, and for all our other Partners BT Security can be contacted on 0800 321 999, quoting the agent’s ID number from their badge."

## 3-lap system for door-to-door visits

The company also explained its approach to door-to-door visits.

The spokesperson said: "The field sales team operate a 3-lap system when working in an area, meaning they will call again if there has been no answer previously, or if they were asked to come back later.

"If a resident doesn’t want to be contacted, they can contact EE and ask to be removed from this marketing method or simply answer the door and inform the Field Sales Agent."

A spokesperson for West Yorkshire Police said: "The statement from BT appears to confirm they did have a third-party sales team in that area."
