A top York private school is “thriving” — despite it facing a deficit of more than £580,000, its chair of governors has said.

An annual report of the Mount School, in Dalton Terrace, said the 223-pupil school had an operating deficit of £582,701 for the year ended August 31, 2025, compared with an operating deficit of £789,514 in 2023/24.

After a pension remeasurement charge, the total deficit at the Mount for 2024/25 was £970,474, the report said, meaning £582,701 must be raised.

But the school’s chair of governors, Greg Willmott, told The Press the Mount is confident it “can grow the school as we need to reverse the half-a-million-pound deficit position”.

  Greg Willmott, the Mount School’s chair of governors 

*(Image: Alex Griffiths)*

He said the Mount is “well on track” with the school's four-year plan to drive growth, which it is two years into.

Mr Willmott also noted that his three daughters attended the school and said: “If I had any question about sustainability of this school, I wouldn’t be risking my kids’ education on something that’s about to fall over.”

He added: “Despite the economic climate, the Mount is thriving.

"We are celebrating an outstanding inspection, the prep school is award-winning, we’ve opened our new preschool and completed major campus investments.

"Driven by a clear plan focused on offering high-quality education, innovation and sustainable growth, we remain confident in our bespoke Quaker educational offer — a nurturing co-educational prep, and proudly all girls for senior school and sixth form.

"We are fully committed to delivering exceptional opportunities for all our pupils, a tradition of success we have proudly maintained since 1785.”

  The Mount School's head teacher Anna Wilby with pupils 

*(Image: Duncan Lomax)*

The report said fee income at the Mount fell from £4.52 million in 2023/24 to £4.29 million in 2024/25.

But it said pupil numbers were maintained at the school, which counts Dame Judi Dench and writer A S Byatt among its former pupils, despite wider declines across the independent sector.

Mr Willmott said the Mount started the September 2026 school term with a six per cent growth in pupil numbers.

He also said the school opened three new facilities, including a “state-of-the-art” dance studio, as it works to widen its offering for pupils.

  A new dance studio which has opened at the Mount School in York 

*(Image: Duncan Lomax)*

Mr Willmott added that the Mount remains “fully committed to investing in a bright innovative future, delivering a bespoke education for our pupils, both academically and beyond the curriculum”.

He said the Mount, along with independent schools across the UK, has been “operating against an exceptionally challenging national backdrop, including the introduction of VAT on school fees, the removal of business rates relief and significant increases in employment costs”.

But he said it is crucial for the Mount to “provide increased value, rather than scaling back” its offering at a time of “increased pressure on households”.

He ruled out cuts at the school, saying: “Our quality of education will be constantly enhanced, cuts and retrenchments do not form part of our strategy.”

  New facilities at the Mount School in York 

*(Image: Supplied)*

Mr Willmott pointed to national issues for the schooling sector, such as fewer creative subjects being offered and increasing classroom sizes, and said: “We are not going that way. We are going the other way.”

He said the Mount has created a new business and economics department, maintained full provision for ancient and modern languages, and is “investing heavily” in creative and expressive arts.

“We are investing in all those facilities which are going to be increasingly hard for pupils to find [elsewhere],” he said.

## Chair rules out school selling off more of its estate

Meanwhile, the report revealed the Mount’s cash reserves improved significantly in 2024/25 compared to the previous year, rising from £472,818 to £1.27 million.

Net assets increased from £475,688 to £2.89 million, following a revaluation of the school estate.

The report also said the Mount secured a £1.3 million five-year loan from Hampden Bank last August.

Mr Willmott said the Mount has been “steadily and deliberately” reducing the size of its estate, originally built to accommodate 450 pupils, over the last seven years.

The school’s estate is now “the right size it needs to be”, Mr Willmott said, ruling out any future building sales in the near future.

He said the last piece of the estate to be sold was a Victorian church, near Holgate Bridge in Dalton Terrace, which has been converted into the Moxie Centre for Art.

The sale of the church brought in £1 million for the Mount and the net proceeds will be invested in the school's long-term strategy, the report said.

A £900,000 “injection” in the Mount has also been secured through fundraising and donations, it added.

Elsewhere, Mr Willmott said the Mount plans to increase pupil numbers by expanding its catchment area.

It also opened a new preschool, bringing in an extra 45–50 pupils once full.

Mr Willmott noted that the Mount previously increased pupil numbers by reopening its prep school to boys, as it traditionally was, but he ruled out opening the whole school to boys, saying the senior school’s key goal is to specialise in girls’ education.
