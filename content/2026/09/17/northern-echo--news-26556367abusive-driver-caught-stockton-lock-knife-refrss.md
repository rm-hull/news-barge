An "abusive" driver spotted outside a suspected drugs den was caught carrying a lock knife on his keyring.

Carl Farrington stopped in the Stockton street but drove off when officers approached his car, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The 57-year-old pulled over a few streets away and was found with a lock knife on his car keyring.

Prosecutor Elisha Marsay said officers were on Brittannia Road dealing with suspected drug dealers when Farrington drove into the street.

Farrington became abusive before driving off and being arrested a short distance away on Castlereagh Road, she said.

“He was initially detained and arrested for failure to stop but, after the discovery of the knife on the defendant’s car keys, he was further arrested for that offence,” she said.

Farrington, of High Street, Wolviston, admitted possessing a bladed article after his arrest on July 24.

Farrington was serving a suspended sentence for possessing a class A drug with intent to supply when he committed the latest offence, the court heard.

Farrington panicked when he saw the men approaching his car and did not initially realise they were police officers, his lawyer Stephen Constantine said.

He said: “He panicked because he wasn’t entirely sure who was approaching him, he drove away and when it became apparent it was a uniformed police officer, he stopped.

“He thought it was one of the group of suspected drug dealers.”

Judge Nathan Adams sentenced Farrington to six months in prison, suspended for 18 months.

“The police saw you pull up at a property they were investigating,” he said. “It was a marked police car. It was an officer in police uniform who asked you to stop and instead you drove off.

“I don’t accept for a moment that you didn’t realise they were police - it would have been abundantly clear.”

The judge ordered Farrington to complete 15 rehabilitation activity requirement days for breaching his suspended sentence and committing the latest offence.
