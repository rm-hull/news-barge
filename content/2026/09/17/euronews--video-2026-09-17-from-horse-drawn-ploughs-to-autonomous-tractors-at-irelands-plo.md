The 95th edition took place at Screggan, near Tullamore in County Offaly, from **15 to 17 September**, drawing more than** 350 competitors**and** tens of thousands of visitors** from Ireland and across the EU.

Traditional horse-drawn ploughs and vintage machinery stood alongside modern tractors, while veteran farmers demonstrated loy digging, an Irish method of turning soil by hand with a narrow spade.

The event also showcased the **future of farming**, with drones, robots and autonomous tractors highlighting the growing role of automation and precision agriculture.

Manitou’s MLT 625e, a battery-powered compact telehandler, was named Machine of the Year after judges found it matched the dimensions, lifting capacity and performance of comparable diesel models.

A restored Porsche tractor displayed by students from Mercy Secondary School in Ballymahon also drew attention as part of a charity raffle supporting Ronald McDonald House Charities Ireland.

The championships took place as **Irish and European farmers** faced rising**costs** and an**ageing workforce**, highlighting the need to combine traditional skills with new technology.

Often described as **Europe’s largest outdoor event**, the Irish Ploughing Championships attract between** 250,000**and** 300,000 visitors**over just** three days**, temporarily turning a quiet site in Offaly into one of Ireland’s most populated areas.
