A court has heard how two young men were part of a balaclava-clad gang that threatened a youth with a machete and robbed him of his car.

Zaid Khan has now had nine months added to an existing period in youth custody.

Salim Mohammed avoided immediate custody but was handed 16 months youth custody suspended for 18 months.

Prosecutor Nicola Hoskins told Bradford Crown Court how, on November 27, 2024, the 17-year-old victim had driven with two friends to a shop on Gain Lane after arranging to sell vapes to someone known as “Maz”.

When he arrived, he was approached by “Maz” but saw five other males wearing balaclavas.

As he turned to look his car keys were removed from the ignition and as he made to get out of the vehicle his iPhone was snatched from his hand.

Miss Hoskins said: “He did manage to get out of the car and told the males that he wasn’t going to give them anything.

“But a second male patted him down and removed from his pocket six £10 notes and a vape.

“That second male instructed [the victim] to tell his friends to leave the car. In doing so he pulled up his jacket and appeared to show a bladed article [tucked into his waistband].”

The victim was then punched to the ground. Four members of the gang got into the car, which contained a box of 30 vapes, and drove off.

The car was later found parked at a nearby snooker club. The victim’s mobile phone was found abandoned in undergrowth.

The gang left the scene in a taxi. Khan and Mohammed were identified through that booking and via CCTV.

When they were arrested both gave “no comment” answers in interviews.

Khan, 21, of no fixed abode, Bradford, claimed he was a victim of mistaken identity. Mohammed, 20, of Tagore Court, Bradford, said he had not been involved.

The court heard that the victim of the robbery had not provided a statement.

The court heard that Khan was of good character at the time but had received a period of youth custody in July this year for kidnapping.

Mohammed had no previous convictions.

Mitigating for Khan, Jeevan Mann said he was just 19 at the time and had never experienced a custodial sentence.

He said Khan, who appeared in court via video link from HMP Doncaster, was now experiencing “the full consequences of bad decisions he made as a younger man” and that he was “genuinely remorseful”.

Mitigating for Mohammed, barrister Mohammed Rafiq said the behaviour had been out of character for an immature 18-year-old at the time who had committed no offences before the robbery, and nothing after.

He conceded that Mohammed had tried to minimise his involvement “but the reality of the matter is that he was with his so-called friends.”

Sentencing the pair, Mr Recorder Shufqat Khan said: “This was an offence where arrangements had been made for the victim to come to a location.

“I do conclude [that] it was planned because five of you were wearing balaclavas.

“When the victim was robbed, violence was used towards him. One of you had a machete as well.

“You two were part of that group that committed that serious offence.”

In addition to his suspended sentence Mohammed was ordered to carry out 150 hours of unpaid work and to undertake 25 rehabilitation activity requirement days plus the Stepping Stones programme.
