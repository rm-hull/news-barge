A North East community interest company that rescues reusable timber and helps people build skills and confidence has launched a crowdfunding appeal after losing its only van.

The Newcastle-based Reclaimed Timber Project CIC is raising £6,960 to hire a replacement vehicle for six months and support its volunteers.

Led by managing director Georgina Llewelyn and directors Karl Denton and Alex Goldberg, the organisation saves timber from landfill and sells it to the community at low cost.

  The Reclaimed Timber Project CIC managing director Georgina Llewelyn, right, and volunteer-turned directors Alex Goldberg have launched a crowdfunding appeal to raise £6,960 following the loss of its van 

*(Image: Supplied)*

It also gives volunteers practical experience, confidence and a chance to connect with others.

Ms Llewelyn said: "We’re asking for help because we genuinely want to get back to doing what we love.

"What we’ve built is about so much more than timber. It’s about people, the environment and our local community.

"There are volunteers ready and waiting to come back.

"There are people who have found friendship, routine, purpose and confidence through being part of this project.

"We miss them, and we know they miss us.

"We know this is a difficult time for everyone, but we’re asking people to help us get over this hurdle.

"If we can get back on the road, we can start rescuing timber again, supporting our volunteers and rebuilding our community."

Without a van, the CIC cannot collect timber, leaving volunteers without the routine and connections the project provides.

The target would cover a caged tipper van for six months at around £240 a week, plus £200 a month for fleet insurance.

The temporary hire would let the organisation restart collections while it seeks funding for a permanent vehicle.

The project keeps timber out of landfill, turns waste into affordable resources and gives isolated people a supportive place to belong.

Volunteers say the project has given them skills, structure and a sense of belonging.

The CIC is urging people to donate, share the appeal or put it before potential backers.

The team says small donations can make a big difference.
