A man accused of fatally stabbing a stranger told police the incident was an accident.

John Robinson denies murdering 30-year-old Mason Jones on March 1 this year in Burley-in-Wharfedale.

He also denies manslaughter and possession of a knife in public.

On the fourth day of a murder trial at Bradford Crown Court, Robinson, a jury heard how Robinson claimed to have slipped as he held a Japanese Katto chef’s knife, and that he unintentionally stabbed Mr Jones once outside the home of his on/off girlfriend Annabel Ramsay.

Robinson, 30, told police: “The first thing I said to Annabel was, ‘I think I stabbed him’.”

He added: “We were pretty scared, so we went to my house.”

The court was shown CCTV of the pair leaving in a taxi for Robinson’s home in Derry Hill, Menston, around three miles away.

Meanwhile an ambulance had been called for Mr Jones, who was lying in the street with a severe wound to his abdomen.

He later succumbed to his injury: a 16cm deep stab wound caused by Robinson’s 17.4cm-long chef’s knife.

The defendant told police he had previously worked as a chef at Bradford Golf Club but was now a full-time carer for his elderly grandmother.

Robinson was asked in the police interview if it was normal for him to take his knife every time he went to Miss Ramsay's flat. Robinson said he usually took it in a bag for cooking.

The trial previously heard how Mr Jones and his friend Will Clarke had arrived at Miss Ramsay’s flat at her invitation after travelling from Bury where they had been out drinking together.

However, after arguments between Mr Clarke and Miss Ramsay’s friend Nicole Clements, who had been with her throughout the night of February 28 and into March 1, the two men were thrown out.

They spent some time wandering around Burley-in-Wharfedale before returning to the flat to ask if they could charge their mobile phones.

After one altercation, Miss Ramsay rang Robinson and asked him to join her. He arrived in a taxi that she booked and paid for.

He was at the flat at about 9.50am on March 1 when Mr Jones and Mr Clarke called once again.

Robinson had taken with him a bag that contained a charger, socks, boxer shorts and his chef’s knife in a protective leather sheath.

In his interview with police Robinson described Jones banging on the door of Miss Ramsay’s flat.

He said he was aggressive, asked to be let in, and was used racial abuse towards him.

He said: “They were angry. It looked like they wanted to beat somebody up.”

He described being scared and “picked up the first thing I could see to protect Annabel” when he saw Mr Jones with his hands around Miss Ramsay’s neck.

In the brief confrontation that followed Robinson, holding the knife, took two steps outside, said “Go away”, and then slipped.

In the course of slipping, he stabbed Mason Jones once through the black puffer jacket he was wearing. The knife pierced his abdomen.

He told police: “I stabbed him by accident.”

Robinson was arrested from his home at about 11.20am the same morning and taken into custody.

The trial has previously heard how members of the public tried to help Mr Jones as he lay on the pavement of Main Street.

After being stabbed in the narrow alleyway outside Miss Ramsay’s flat, he staggered into Iron Row and on into Main Street, where he collapsed.

Among the witnesses to the incident was a woman whose home on Iron Row overlooked a courtyard near to the alleyway to the flat.

She recalled hearing a loud bang and people shouting at about 7,30am. When she looked out of her window she saw two men. One was in a black puffer jacket and the other had on a “distinctive” jacket that was white at the top and black

CCTV had previously shown Mr Jones to be dressed all in black. Mr Clarke was wearing the white and black jacket.

Some time later, which she estimated to be after 9am, the two men returned.

Once again there was banging and shouting. She saw both men go into the courtyard to the alleyway.

She said: “Something’s happening with somebody in that alleyway.

“I only saw an arm of a person [in] a stone-washed denim coloured long-sleeved top.

“That arm – it was the left arm of that person – went towards the waist-ish area of the man with the puffer jacket.”

She added: “I did not see a face.

“Just seconds after that a girl came running out from the alleyway into the courtyard, wearing a bra and a thong.

“She shouted ‘F***off, you di** head!’

“The two males walked out of the courtyard onto Iron Row and then I lost view as they walked down Main Street.”

The trial continues.
