Bradford Synagogue has praised the support of West Yorkshire Police as the first anniversary approaches of the terrorist attack on a Manchester synagogue that shocked Jewish communities across the country.

The comments come as the first anniversary approaches of the terrorist attack at Heaton Park Hebrew Congregation Synagogue in Manchester, where two men were killed and three others seriously injured after a vehicle was driven into worshippers and members of the congregation.

## Attack felt across Jewish communities

Suzie Cree, from the Board of Trustees at Bradford Synagogue, said the attack had affected Jewish communities both in the UK and beyond.

She said: "Clearly the attack on Heaton Park Synagogue has had an effect on Jewish communities in this country and elsewhere."

As the anniversary of the attack approaches, Ms Cree said the synagogue had taken steps to strengthen security measures.

## Security measures strengthened

Ms Cree said Bradford Synagogue had received support and guidance from both West Yorkshire Police and the Community Security Trust.

She said: "We have taken appropriate actions to increase security, assisted by advice from West Yorkshire Police and the Community Security Trust."

The comments come ahead of Yom Kippur, one of the most significant dates in the Jewish calendar, when many members of the Jewish community will gather for worship and reflection.

## Praise for police support

Ms Cree paid tribute to West Yorkshire Police for its continued assistance following the Manchester attack.

She said: "We are most grateful to West Yorkshire Police for their diligence and support."

The synagogue said the support had helped reassure worshippers while ensuring appropriate security arrangements remain in place.

## Community support welcomed

Ms Cree also highlighted the backing received from people across Bradford in the wake of the attack.

She said: "We greatly appreciate the overwhelming support we have received from our local neighbours and from across Bradford."

She added that the synagogue remained committed to building on relationships developed with other communities and faith groups across the district.

## Looking ahead

Despite the impact of the Manchester attack, Ms Cree said Bradford Synagogue looked forward to continuing its work with communities across the city.

She said: "We look forward to maintaining and deepening the excellent relationships we have had with all sections of the community, particularly the faith communities, for many years."

The comments come as many communities prepare to mark the first anniversary of the Manchester synagogue attack on October 2.
