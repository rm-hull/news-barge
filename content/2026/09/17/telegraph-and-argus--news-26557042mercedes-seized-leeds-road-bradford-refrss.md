A MERCEDES with tinted windows and the driver not wearing a seatbelt was seized by police after officers spotted the vehicle on a Bradford road.

The stop was carried out by West Yorkshire Police's Operation Steerside team on Leeds Road.

Police said the darkness of the vehicle's window tints immediately caught officers' attention.

In a social media post, the team said: "Given the darkness of the tints on this Mercedes-Benz, there's no doubt that we saw it before the driver saw us."

Officers added that the driver was not wearing a seatbelt when the vehicle was stopped.

An examination of the driver's side window found that just 16 per cent of light was passing through the glass.

The legal requirement for front side windows is 70 per cent.

## 'All too familiar' insurance claims

According to police, the stop then led to a series of discussions over the driver's insurance status.

Officers said the motorist initially claimed to be a named driver on an insurance policy before later suggesting they had third-party cover through another policy.

Police said further checks revealed another insurance document produced by the driver was fraudulent.

The driver then claimed that insurance had been arranged through a "Snapchat broker", which officers described as a familiar explanation during roadside stops.

In the social media post, police said they embarked on "an all too familiar journey through the excuses and tall tales of the insurance that never was".

## 
  **Mercedes seized at the roadside**

With no valid insurance in place, officers seized the Mercedes.

Police said the driver had been planning to travel to the West Midlands but would instead have to continue the journey by public transport.

The Operation Steerside team said officers checked train times and found the motorist could reach the West Midlands area by about 7am the following morning.

In a tongue-in-cheek message, officers added: "Who doesn't love seeing the sun rise from the comfort of a rail carriage anyway?"

Police said the driver is expected to face action in relation to a number of alleged road traffic offences.
