In an increasingly complicated car market, the Suzuki S-Cross offers a refreshingly straightforward alternative.

It has a familiar 1.4-litre turbocharged petrol engine, a choice of manual or automatic transmission, physical controls for many functions and a clear focus on practical everyday driving.

That simplicity does not make it feel old-fashioned in every respect: the S-Cross has a bold exterior, 48V mild-hybrid assistance and a strong range of standard safety equipment.

Tested here in 1.4 Boosterjet Ultra Auto form, it is not an SUV designed to deliver driving thrills. Instead, its strengths are reliable handling, sensible performance, compact dimensions and good value for money. It is an easy car to understand and an even easier one to live with.

## A more confident design

The S-Cross looks considerably more substantial than its dimensions suggest. Its raised bonnet, piano-black grille, angular wheel arches and three-element LED headlights give it a confident, upright stance. At the rear, the wider combination lamps and horizontal garnish create a greater sense of width, while roof rails and an integrated spoiler add the familiar details expected of a modern SUV.

It remains a manageable size, measuring 4,305mm long and 1,785mm wide. That makes it convenient around town and less awkward in car parks than larger family SUVs. The 430-litre boot is practical, although it does not lead the class for capacity or versatility.

Suzuki’s decision to retain 17-inch wheels across the range is helpful to both comfort and running costs. Ultra models receive polished versions, along with leather upholstery, a panoramic sliding sunroof and a 360-degree camera. The result is a car that looks better equipped than its price might suggest, even if it lacks the visual drama of some rivals in this segment.

## Simple but spacious interior

The interior is less ambitious than the exterior. Its design is functional and logically arranged, with a high-set driving position and plenty of adjustment in the seat and steering column. The controls are easy to identify, and there is no need to navigate complex menus for every basic function.

The cabin will not impress anyone expecting premium materials or a particularly adventurous design. Much of the dashboard is made from hard plastic, and the overall appearance is more durable than luxurious. However, the straightforward layout is useful, and the seats provide comfortable support for longer journeys.

There is reasonable space for adults in the rear, although the panoramic roof fitted to Ultra versions slightly reduces headroom. The back seat is also less well equipped than some newer rivals, with no rear ventilation outlets and limited charging provision. These are small omissions, but they matter in a family car.

The nine-inch touchscreen fitted to Ultra models is larger than the seven-inch unit available on Motion versions, and it includes navigation, Apple CarPlay and Android Auto. It is not the quickest or most sophisticated system available, but its menus are generally easy to operate. Smartphone mirroring is likely to be the preferred option for many owners.

## Sensible performance

The 1.4-litre K14D Boosterjet engine produces 129PS and 235Nm of torque. Its 48V mild-hybrid system uses an Integrated Starter Generator fir extra assistance during initial acceleration, when the turbocharger is still building boost. This gives the S-Cross a useful sense of flexibility at low engine speeds.

The engine is right-powered for this type of car. It is not especially quick, but it has enough strength for everyday use, motorway driving and overtaking. Acceleration is smooth when the driver is measured with the throttle, and the power delivery feels more willing than the modest engine capacity might suggest.

The six-speed automatic transmission suits the S-Cross’s relaxed character. It removes the effort from congested driving and generally responds predictably.

With a 0-62mph time of 10.1 seconds for the two-wheel-drive automatic, performance is adequate rather than exciting. Fuel economy and emissions are competitive for a petrol SUV, helped by the mild-hybrid system’s regenerative braking and stop-start functions.

## Reliable handling

The S-Cross handles in a dependable and predictable way. The steering is light, which makes town driving and parking easy, although it offers limited feedback on faster roads. The suspension is tuned more for comfort than agility, allowing the car to deal effectively with uneven surfaces.

There is some body movement through bends, and enthusiastic drivers will find little hidden entertainment in the chassis. However, the car remains stable and composed, with reliable grip and reassuring responses. That matches the impressions from testing: the S-Cross does not provide driving thrills, but it is easy to place on the road and inspires confidence.

Wind and road noise can become noticeable at motorway speeds, but the engine remains reasonably refined during normal driving. Adaptive cruise control works well on longer journeys, while the lane-keeping assistance provides useful support without constantly fighting the driver. The assistance systems can also be adjusted using physical controls, which is a welcome feature.

Ultra versions also benefit from Suzuki’s ALLGRIP Select four-wheel-drive system. It is not essential for most buyers, but it provides extra reassurance on slippery roads and makes the S-Cross more capable on gravel tracks, grass and light off-road surfaces.

## Well equipped and good value

The S-Cross’s strongest argument is the amount of equipment included for the money. Even Motion versions receive heated seats, dual-zone climate control, keyless entry and start, navigation, smartphone connectivity, parking sensors and extensive safety technology.

The Ultra adds the features most likely to improve daily use: leather upholstery, a panoramic roof and a 360-degree camera. Seven airbags, automatic emergency braking, adaptive cruise control, blind-spot monitoring, lane-departure prevention, traffic-sign recognition and rear cross-traffic alert are standard across the range.

## Verdict

The Suzuki S-Cross is a deliberately uncomplicated family SUV in a market increasingly dominated by oversized screens, complex powertrains and ever more elaborate styling. That approach leaves it less fashionable than some rivals, and the interior in particular feels conservative.

However, the S-Cross remains easy to understand, comfortable enough, sensibly sized and well equipped. The 1.4 Boosterjet automatic delivers the appropriate level of performance, while the handling is reliable rather than entertaining. For buyers who value practicality, safety and good value over technology overload or driving excitement, the S-Cross remains a convincing choice. Its greatest achievement is not doing anything spectacular, but making everyday motoring feel straightforward.
