A FATHER has completed the Great North Run in memory of his three-day-old daughter.

Lee Haigh, from Bradford, took on the world’s largest half marathon alongside best friend Aaron Barber, raising more than £3,000 for Bradford Hospitals Charity’s Home from Home Appeal.

The 13.1-mile challenge was completed as part of a team of five runners, who together raised £3,150.

Mr Haigh and Mr Barber ran as part of Alice’s Army, named in honour of Mr Haigh’s daughter Alice, who died in December 2018.

Mr Haigh said: "Taking on the Great North Run in Alice’s memory was always going to be emotional.

"We can’t change what happened to our family, but being able to do something positive in Alice’s name and hopefully make things a little easier for other families going through incredibly difficult times means a huge amount to us."

Alice died just three days after birth, and her family spent time with her on the neonatal unit at Bradford Royal Infirmary.

The care they received inspired Mr Haigh to support the charity’s Home from Home Appeal, which aims to fund dedicated accommodation for families with babies in the neonatal unit.

Mr Haigh said: "Having Aaron by my side made it even more special.

"He’s been a huge support to me and our family, and to cross that finish line together knowing why we were doing it is something I’ll never forget.

"We’ve been blown away by everyone who has donated and supported us.

"Alice will always be part of our family, and knowing her name is continuing to make a difference to other families means everything."

The pair originally set a fundraising target of £500 but exceeded it with the support of friends, family and the wider community.

They were joined in the Great North Run by Laura Kettlewell, Elise Townsend and Daniaal Rauf, who also raised funds for the appeal.

Laura Riach, director of Bradford Hospitals Charity, said: "For neonatal families, long and unexpected hospital stays can become part of daily life, with many travelling an average of 55 miles just to be by their baby’s side.

"It was so special to see our runners cross the finish line surrounded by their own support networks, with husbands, wives, daughters, nieces and friends cheering them on."
