Holiday pay calculations are causing confusion and concern for employers in Yorkshire.

Changes to employment law that came into force on April 6 require all UK businesses to keep detailed records of holiday entitlement and pay for six years, in line with National Minimum Wage documentation.

The rules apply to 2.73 million VAT and/or PAYE-registered businesses nationwide, including 191,000 in Yorkshire and the Humber.

Hannah-Jane Dobbie, head of HR consultancy at Azets, said: "I’m getting calls from bosses of well-run companies worried sick about falling foul of compliance through no fault of their own; they want to ensure staff are paid what they are owed, but the calculation method is so difficult for what appears to be the largest group of workers that it leaves them exposed, and as of now, we haven’t found two employers tackling this in the same way."

The new requirements mean employers must record annual holiday entitlement, leave taken, and pay for each period of leave, including pay in lieu of unused holiday upon termination.

While the principle is straightforward, the practical application has left many businesses struggling, particularly when calculating holiday pay for employees with fixed hours but variable pay due to overtime, bonuses, or allowances.

Ms Dobbie said: "What has come to light is that employers do not understand the calendar method or how to calculate holiday pay correctly."

She warned that errors could lead to investigations by the Fair Work Agency (FWA), employment tribunals, and compensation claims.

The FWA, launched in April, has the power to inspect premises, demand records, and impose unlimited fines or criminal sanctions for non-compliance.

Julie Gunnell, associate director – growth payroll at Azets, said: "Many businesses haven’t seen this coming and are not prepared – it’s been like a bolt out of the blue."
