Money raised in York from a proposed £1-a-night fee on overnight visitor stays would go into a pot to be spent across the region, the county’s mayor has said.

David Skaith told The Press that all money raised from the visitor levy, dubbed a “tourist tax”, would go into a “wider pot for York and North Yorkshire” to be distributed across the county.

It comes after the York Liberal Democrats argued that money from the overnight levy should be spent where it has been raised.

But Mr Skaith ruled that out, saying: “It will be a levy raised region-wide across York and North Yorkshire. And it will be spent region-wide across York and North Yorkshire.

“That’s the biggest opportunity we have to get the bigger investment and bigger opportunities we have across York and North Yorkshire.”

A report by York St John University, commissioned by the mayor, found that a £1-a-night visitor levy on overnight stays in York and North Yorkshire could raise up to £26 million-a-year.

Mr Skaith said the combined authority has been working with industry bosses to determine what money from the levy would be spent on, but this would include promoting York and North Yorkshire by staging more major events in the region.

The Labour mayor would not be drawn on specific projects where the money would be spent but did single out Parliament Street in York, saying: “We’ve known for years that’s needed investment to make sure that’s in a much better position.

“We’ve got the opportunity now to actually do that investment. Previously, we wouldn’t have been able to go anywhere near that project – it would have cost too much money.”

He also said the money could be used to improve public transport across York and North Yorkshire, calling it a “huge opportunity for our region”.

Mr Skaith said it was too early to say how money raised from the levy would be distributed, but it would “probably be a combination” of it being given to businesses, events and local authorities.

“I want to be clear that we are still speaking to the sector all the time, we want to be in a position where we can promote the region, promote the hospitality and tourism sector,” the mayor said. “We want to be able to invest into this skilled workforce and support that region as well.”

## Cash from levy would 'turbo charge' investment into county - mayor

Asked how he had arrived at the £1-a-night figure for the proposals, Mr Skaith said: “We just wanted, to begin with, an arbitrary figure just to get those findings … We wanted to understand the figure, understand the business numbers and what that kind of money could do for a region like ours.

“Comparatively speaking, compared to the money that I get as mayor [£18 million in government funding per year], it’s considerably more than that per year.

“And we know it’s every year, and it will be linked to inflation as well, so it will only go up as well.”

Mr Skaith insisted that money raised from the levy would not simply be used by local authorities to balance budgets. “I’ll rule that out,” he said. “It’s always been a mayoral power, and it will be coming to me as the mayor.

“We’re still waiting on government legislation to say what it can and can’t be used for, but I’m pretty certain it will be used for what we call being on the front foot with things – so investing in the sector, promoting the region, investing in our towns, our high streets, our public transport.”

Critics have argued that introducing the overnight levy could deter people from visiting York and North Yorkshire, but Mr Skaith said the report had not found that not to be the case.

The report pointed to visitor growth remaining steady in places that have already implemented a levy on overnight stays, such as Edinburgh, Manchester, Paris and Barcelona.

Meanwhile, Conservative MPs in North Yorkshire opposed the visitor levy, saying it would hit people taking family holidays in the county and the “family businesses that depend on them, at a time when both are already facing significant cost pressures”.

But Mr Skaith said: “We’ve got an opportunity here to raise much-needed capital for the region.”

He added: “I’m happy to work with any politician or anyone in the region to make sure this works.

“But we’ve got an opportunity to really shift the dial for York and North Yorkshire, and turbo charge that investment.”
