Lawmakers from New Mexico have urged Apple and Google not to rename the state on their mapping apps after President Donald Trump suggested restyling it as “New America.”

On Thursday, the state’s congressional delegation — five Democrats — sent letters to the executives of both tech companies, asking them to “publicly commit” to keeping New Mexico on Apple Maps and Google Maps.

“Let us be clear: the President has no legal authority to rename New Mexico,” the lawmakers wrote. “Our state’s name belongs to the people of New Mexico and is rooted in our history and our Constitution, which clearly states that ‘the name of this state is New Mexico.’”

Trump, who over the weekend posted at least seven maps labeling New Mexico as “New America,” has demonstrated that his renaming push is more than just a trolling attempt.

Last month, amid his trade war with Canada, he signed an executive order directing federal agencies to refer to Lake Ontario as “Lake America.” Last January, he signed a similar order rebranding the Gulf of Mexico as the “Gulf of America.”

![Lawmakers from New Mexico urged Apple and Google not to rename their state on their map services, following President Donald Trump's weekend posting spree indicating he would like the state to be called 'New America'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/10/18/25/89183b44d45973ad-cf4fb16f4800.jpeg)

While the president can direct how the federal government refers to geographic features, he cannot compel foreign nations, private entities or even U.S. states to follow his lead.

Yet, both Google and Apple have updated their maps so that users in the U.S. see the new Trump-given names for both the Gulf of Mexico and Lake Ontario.

“These updates follow our long-standing policy for bodies of water with names that vary from country to country, and are starting to roll out now,” Google said in a blog post.

The New Mexico lawmakers described these changes as “disappointing but unsurprising,” pointing out that both companies “have significant business interests before the federal government.”

![Senator Ben Ray Luján was one of five New Mexico Democrats to sign the letters sent to Apple and Google executives](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/10/18/28/GettyImages-2244873485.jpeg)

Since Trump’s return to office, the two companies have seemingly moved to cozy up with the administration. Both donated to Trump’s planned White House ballroom project, and executives from each attended his second inauguration last January. In a separate gesture last year, then-Apple CEO Tim Cook presented Trump with a 24-karat gold statue.

“Because of this history, we ask each of you to publicly commit that Apple Maps and Google Maps will continue to identify the State of New Mexico as New Mexico, regardless of what the President posts on social media, what executive order he attempts to issue, or what pressure he puts on your companies,” the lawmakers wrote.

The state’s delegation includes Senators Ben Ray Luján and Martin Heinrich, and Representatives Teresa Leger Fernández, Melanie Stansbury and Gabe Vasquez.

*The Independent* has contacted Google and Apple for comment.
