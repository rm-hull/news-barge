North Yorkshire Council has proposed improvements to its school travel policy following a wide-ranging review.

The changes aim to address concerns raised by families and improve clarity around eligibility for free school transport.

The review was launched after the council updated its policy in September 2024, shifting eligibility for free transport from catchment areas to the nearest school.

Councillor Annabel Wilkinson, executive member for education, learning and skills, said: "We know that school travel arrangements are incredibly important to families and that the changes introduced in 2024 have affected some households more than others.

"When the policy changed, we promised to review how it was working in practice and to listen to the experiences of families.

"That is exactly what we have done."

The review considered feedback from parents, carers, schools, and other stakeholders, as well as appeals, complaints, and operational data.

It found that the policy is generally achieving its goals of fairness and consistency, but also identified areas for improvement.

The council provides home-to-school transport for around 11,000 eligible pupils, many of whom live in rural parts of England’s largest county.

The service costs more than £52 million annually.

Cllr Wilkinson said: "The review shows the policy is meeting its objectives, but it has also highlighted areas where we can make improvements.

"We are committed to learning from people's experiences and ensuring families have clearer information and a better experience of the service."

The review recommends 24 changes to the current system, covering mapping, appeals, communications, and operational processes.

These include removing five routes and associated footpaths from the mapping system due to practical challenges.

The affected routes are Fleet Moss Road, Keld to Kirkby Stephen, Buttertubs Pass, The Stang, and Kidstone Pass.

The review also suggests improving route information, strengthening the appeals process, and providing clearer guidance for families.

Some recommendations would require public consultation and full council approval, while others could be implemented by the executive.

Any changes to the policy would not take effect before September 2027 and would apply to children starting Reception or Year 7 in September 2028.

The existing policy remains in place for those applying for a Reception or Year 7 school place in September 2027 or making in-year applications during the 2026/2027 academic year.

Cllr Wilkinson added: "Families should continue to make school preference decisions based on existing arrangements while the decision-making process is completed.

"To support families during the transition period, we will continue to use the appeals process and discretionary powers in exceptional cases.

"This means support can be considered where specific routes or circumstances cause difficulties, rather than waiting for any future policy changes to take effect."

The council’s children and families overview and scrutiny committee will review the report on September 16.
