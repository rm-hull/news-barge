A SUICIDE prevention partnership has been launched to help stop avoidable deaths and build a more supportive community.

Darlington Borough Council has joined forces with If U Care Share, a charity based in the region, to deliver two years of suicide awareness and prevention training across the borough.

It follows concerns about suicide rates in Darlington, which are among the highest in England.

Councillor Matthew Roche, cabinet member for health and housing, said: "Whilst suicide rates in Darlington are high, the numbers of lives lost to suicide remain relatively small.

"However, every suicide is a tragedy that has a devastating impact on families, friends, and communities.

"The council, and our partner organisations, are committed to addressing this issue, including creating more opportunities for early intervention."

The scheme aims to equip residents, businesses, and community groups with the confidence to talk about suicide, spot warning signs, and support those at risk.

If U Care Share will deliver targeted training to council employees and elected members, as well as bespoke sessions for local businesses, community groups and organisations.

Free community-based workshops will also be offered to anyone living or working in Darlington.

David Brown, head of training and development at If U Care Share, said: "We are proud to be working with Darlington Borough Council to help individuals, workplaces, and communities respond confidently to those in distress.

"Suicide prevention is everyone’s business and there is a growing need to strengthen our collective response to suicide and mental health crises."

Sessions are already planned for October and November at Firthmoor Community Centre.

Support officers from If U Care Share will also be on hand at the Cornmill Shopping Centre on Friday 10 September as part of planned World Suicide Prevention Day activities.

They will be available to speak with shoppers about mental health over a cuppa or while taking part in a craft activity.

Cllr Roche said: "This new partnership will make support more visible and equip more people with the knowledge, skills, and confidence to recognise warning signs and respond appropriately.

"Ultimately, we all want to support people to live well and stay healthy for longer."

The training sessions can be booked online via the If U Care Share website.
