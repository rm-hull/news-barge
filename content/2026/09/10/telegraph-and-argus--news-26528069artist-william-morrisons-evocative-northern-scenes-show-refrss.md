A NEW Exhibition at Bingley Gallery, The Veil of the Land, features ethereal Yorkshire landscapes by Halifax-based master painter William Morrison.

William Morrison is a landscape painter whose oil paintings are deeply informed by the moorlands of West Yorkshire, with their shifting mists, changing weather and expansive horizons. Morrison explores the relationship between land, atmosphere and memory, creating works that reflect both the physical character of a place and its emotional resonance.

Born in Scotland, his first art tuition was a part-time post at the renowned Glasgow School of Art, but he has lived most of his life in Yorkshire, where he continued his art studies at Bretton Hall College near Wakefield. His talent as an artist was, perhaps surprisingly balanced by a working as a bricklayer in the building trade. Now having retired from that, and based in Halifax, he is able to indulge himself in his twin passions as a painter and a musician.

William, despite his unquestionable talent, is not a person to push himself into the glare of publicity and is sparing in explaining his methods and motivation. The former is unconventional - using a mixture of oil paint and home-mixed Damar varnish over a white ground that provides a remarkable degree of luminosity. Morrison explains a little of his aims: “Through my paintings I try to capture the peaceful stillness and serene energy of the Yorkshire landscape, using a muted colour palette to evoke a sense of tranquillity. The weather plays a major role in my work, as I love exploring the different moods and atmospheres it creates. I hope that my paintings transport viewers to a familiar yet dreamy world, where they can escape and find solace in the calming beauty of nature.”

As gallery owner, I am a particular fan of Morrison’s work and its Turner-esque qualities. His landscape paintings do not rigidly depict the topography of the hills, valleys, moors and water features. Instead, through the subtle blending of all the elements, they create a painting that powerfully captures the spirit of a place, whether at its most dramatic with a thunderstorm approaching or in the calm of a late evening as the light slips away. There’s so much emotion attached to each work.

Working primarily in oils, Morrison uses a restrained palette to evoke the subdued tones of the moors and the subtle transitions of light and weather. The stillness before rain, the softness of mist and the drama of approaching storms are recurring elements in his work. Rather than treating the landscape as static, he captures its constant transformation through shifts in light, colour and mood.

In preparing any exhibition, there is always an element of uncertainty about how an artist’s work will develop prior to the show. For The Veil of the Land, Morrison has also been experimenting with industrial landscapes. The mills and chimneys of the northern mill towns offer a very different subject from the open moors, but Morrison finds a similar sense of atmosphere in them. Their imposing forms have a grandeur of their own, while their muted tones evoke a haunting melancholy for the industrial landscape.

Another work stands out for its detailed realism: In 'Dropgate, Brockle Beck Keswick' each leaf of the tree is sharply depicted against the deep shadows, showing the extent of the artist’s versatility.

The Veil of the Land presents Morrison’s evolving response to the places that have shaped him, moving between open moorland and the industrial landscapes of West Yorkshire. Together, the works explore how landscape can carry memory and emotion, inviting viewers to look beyond the physical scene to the character and feeling of a place.

The exhibition runs from now until Sunday October 11 2026. Further information can be found on the Bingley Gallery website davidstarleyartist.com/bingley

Gallery opening hours : Thursday to Sunday 10am to 5pm.
