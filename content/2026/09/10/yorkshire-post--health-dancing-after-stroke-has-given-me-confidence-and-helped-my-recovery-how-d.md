# 'Dancing after stroke has given me confidence and helped my recovery' - How Dancing for Health programme is changing lives in Sheffield

## It was just another ordinary day for Janet Pepler, but one that would change her life forever. She was at home preparing dinner when she experienced the devastating impact of a stroke, only surviving after being in a coma for two months. Now, 17 years on, she is inspiring people with a new love of dance.

When Janet joined Stepping Forward, a wellbeing programme delivered by Dancing for Health CIC in Sheffield, she was looking for friendship, confidence and somewhere she felt understood. What she found in the sessions was a community that not only supported her recovery but celebrated her unique journey.

Instructor Tracey Barnes designed a dance routine around Janet’s level of ability, creating movements that reflected the physical challenges she experiences every day as a result of her stroke while embracing everything she can achieve. When the whole group learnt and performed Janet's Dance - based primarily around her ability to move her left arm - it became one of the programme's most moving moments.

“It is so uplifting for someone who has had a stroke to be with other stroke survivors and we are really close knit as a group,” says Janet. “The group is wonderful - we have a fantastic set of people teaching us the moves and I can now do a lot more than I could when I first started.”

![Janet Pepler has been taking part in Stepping Forward, the wellbeing programme delivered by Dancing for Health CIC in Sheffield.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjY0ZjZkMzE2LTVhNGQtNDBjOC05MjA3LWZiYTFlODZkNDM2NjplM2IxZTRmZC1jZjNmLTQ2MjItYjgyZi02MDRlMDllNmJiOTI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)



"Janet has taught us as much as we've supported her,” says Tracey, the Managing Director of Dancing for Health CIC.

“Creating her dance routine wasn't just about movement, it was about recognising her strengths, acknowledging her daily challenges and helping everyone understand what life after stroke can really look like.

“It was a wonderful example of how dance brings people together, builds empathy and reminds us that every achievement deserves celebrating.”

Originally developed to help stroke survivors continue their recovery after NHS rehabilitation ended, Dancing for Health’s Stepping Forward programme is now expanding to support a much wider community of people living with the impact of life-changing illness.

Weekly sessions of Stepping Forward: Wellbeing Through Movement at the Salvation Army Citadel now welcome people affected by stroke, cancer, diabetes, arthritis, neurological conditions, trauma, bereavement, injury and other major life changes, together with carers and loved ones.

They combine gentle adaptive dance and movement, particularly good for brain health, with healthy lifestyle advice and peer support to help people regain confidence, improve mobility and rebuild social connections. Every activity can be adapted to suit individual abilities, whether participants are seated or standing.

Tracey says: “We realised people recovering from many different life-changing experiences all share similar challenges—loss of confidence, loneliness and uncertainty about moving forward. Stepping Forward gives them somewhere they feel understood, accepted and supported.”
