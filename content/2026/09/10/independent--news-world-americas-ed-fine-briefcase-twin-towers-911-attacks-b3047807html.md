A man captured in an iconic photograph walking away from the Twin Towers with a black briefcase on 9/11 has spoken of how missing an elevator and taking the stairs saved his life.

Ed Fine was unaware that his image had become famous around the world after it was plastered on the front cover of *Fortune* magazine. It shows him covered head-to-toe in white dust, still in his work attire, walking through the rubble of the deadliest terrorist attack in modern times. 

Speaking to The Mirror, Mr Fine says he still uses his briefcase and sees it as a “message to those who committed the attacks that they won’t win”.

Mr Fine, now 83, said: "I’ve kept the briefcase and the suit I wore that day. They’ve both been cleaned but I felt it was very important that I kept them.

![A dust-covered Edward Fine walks away from the Twin Towers with his black briefcase](https://static.independent.co.uk/2021/09/07/16/AFP_9EX6BR.jpg)

"We moved from a house into an apartment but there was no way we were getting rid of them. They remind me of a day when I was very lucky. I returned home from 9/11 - many people didn’t. I never forget that."

Mr Fine was one of the survivors of the September 11 attacks, which resulted in the deaths of 2,996 people, including the 19 plane hijackers and hundreds of fire service personnel. Both the World Trade Center’s Twin Towers and the Pentagon were attacked.

It was a “bizarre coincidence” that he was on the 79th floor of the North Tower of the World Trade Center when the Boeing 767 plane struck between the 93rd and 99th floors, Mr Fine said.

“We had just recently started an investment banking business and I had set up an appointment for my son with a firm in WTC,” he told the outlet.

![Mr Fine was on the 79th floor of the North Tower when the plane hit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/03/11/40/stefan-schweinfest-911.jpg)

"But at 10.30pm on September 10, he called to say he was sick and asked if I could go instead. The next morning I woke up early - it was a gorgeous day. I checked in at 7.52am for my appointment on the 87th floor.”

Mr Fine took the elevator to the 79th floor, where he said people had to transfer elevator. “I ran to catch the next elevator to the 87th, but I missed it by seconds,” he said.

"If I'd been on that elevator we wouldn't have been having this conversation today."

Mr Fine’s 84-year-old wife, Ingrid, said she “didn’t hear from Ed for a couple of hours” and “didn’t know if he was dead or alive”.

The couple have two children, 60-year-old Stuart and 57-year-old Heidi.
