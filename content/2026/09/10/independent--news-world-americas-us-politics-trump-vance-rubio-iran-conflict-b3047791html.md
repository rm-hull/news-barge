Top White House advisers, including Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, have privately warned President Donald Trump that the conflict with Iran could extend through the remainder of his presidency, the *Wall Street Journal* reported on Wednesday, citing U.S. officials.

The private warnings were delivered during internal strategy discussions held in the Oval Office and the Situation Room. During these confidential meetings, senior officials informed the president that Tehran could continue to resist U.S. pressure, potentially prolonging the conflict beyond Inauguration Day in January 2029.

According to details reported by the *Journal*, the assessment from key aides highlights concerns within the administration that Iranian resistance may not be easily broken, suggesting military engagement could become a defining foreign policy issue for Trump's entire term in office.

These private warnings stand in sharp contrast to Trump's own public remarks regarding the war. Speaking on Wednesday, the president offered a much shorter timeline for the conflict, telling reporters and the public he expected the fighting to end far sooner than his top advisers projected in private.

Specifically, the president said on Wednesday he expected the war to end "immediately after" the U.S. midterm elections in November. Trump argued that Iran was trying to influence the vote through its ongoing resistance, but asserted that the country would be unable to sustain its opposition much longer.

Trump also acknowledged that diplomatic talks remain a possibility for resolving the dispute, though he clarified that his administration did not prefer negotiations at this stage.

The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment regarding the *Wall Street Journal* report or the internal briefings provided by Vance, Secretary of State Rubio, and other advisers.

*The Journal's* disclosures underscore a clear divergence between internal assessments and public messaging. While advisers in the Oval Office and the Situation Room cautioned that Tehran’s resistance could carry the conflict past Inauguration Day in January 2029, Trump's public statements focused on a resolution tied closely to the November midterm elections.

In his public statements, Trump reiterated his belief that Iranian efforts to influence the elections would fail due to an inability to endure sustained U.S. pressure.

Although Trump noted that diplomatic talks remain a theoretical option, his preference against negotiation leaves open the question of how the administration will handle a prolonged confrontation. For now, private warnings from White House officials suggest that the conflict with Iran may continue well beyond the midterms and through the remainder of Trump's presidency.
