A former soldier dragged his partner by the hair and choked a boy to the point he couldn't breathe as he tried to stop a second attack.

Carl Fitzgerald left them “walking on eggshells”, fearing his reaction at the slightest thing, usually when he had been drinking.

Teesside Crown Court heard that following his arrest for a "vicious" domestic assault, in August last year, Fitzgerald was bailed on condition he did not attend his former partner’s home.

But he ignored that condition and went there anyway, turning violent again, later last year, resulting in his further arrest, since when he has remained in custody.

The court was told his actions included him dragging his now former partner by the hair, removing clumps of hair in the process, and choking a boy to the point of breathlessness, when he tried to intervene to prevent a second assault by Fitgerald on his partner, at the time.

Fitzgerald, 34, of Water Lily Drive, Darlington, appeared before the court for sentence having admitted controlling and coercive behaviour in a relationship, two counts of assault causing actual bodily harm, one of assaulting an emergency worker, and criminal damage.

The court heard some of those admissions were made at his plea hearing, but others on the day of his recent trial.

  Former soldier Carl Fitzgerald given a 50-month prison sentence and made subject of a 15-year restraining order for domestic violence offences 

*(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

A pre-sentence report prepared by the Probation Service considered him a, “high risk” of causing harm to his former partner and “medium risk” to other members of the public, generally.

Albany Kidd, in mitigation, said despite those assessments, the defendant has no previous convictions and served in the Army until he was medically discharged in May 2023.

Miss Kidd said despite the Probation Service referencing his lack of remorse and insight, due to his “minimisation” of his actions, the defendant has expressed to her his “shame” in committing the offences.

She said that while in custody her has also worked on, "the triggers behind his offending”, managing his emotions and the effects of his PTSD, from his time in the Army.

He also now works with other injured veterans who find themselves in custody.

Miss Kidd added that the defendant has engaged in counselling and plans to continue to work with services available to inmates to reduce the risk he poses in future.

Judge Jonathan Carroll told Fitzgerald his "domineering" behaviour in the relationship was indicative of, “entrenched, negative, toxic, hostile attitudes and disturbed thinking towards women and feelings of inadequacy in your own position.”

The judge described the hair-pulling assault as “vicious", committed while in drink, leaving the defendant’s partner bruised, with swellings to the face and with clumps of hair missing.

Judge Carroll said grabbing the boy around the neck to the point of losing breath was, “just about as bad as it gets” and added that it, “beggars’ belief” he was not charged with a more serious offence than assault.

Imposing a total 50-month prison sentence, the judge also put in place a 15-year restraining order, relating to the victim and her family.
