A LARGE Bradford secondary school where staff don't feel safe as pupils fight and throw furniture has been placed into special measures by Ofsted.

Beckfoot Thornton, located on Leaventhorpe Lane, has been told it is "failing to provide an acceptable standard of education" and those in charge are "not demonstrating the capacity to secure the necessary improvement in the school".

The school, which caters for 1,272 pupils, requires 'Urgent Improvement' in two areas and 'Needs Attention' in the remaining four categories following an inspection in April.

The Beckfoot Trust says it is "consider(ing) a transfer of Beckfoot Thornton to Delta Academies Trust" but "no final decision has been made".

It also adds that in a recent survey, "97 per cent of staff confirmed that behaviour has improved in recent months".

## What does the report say?

The report said leaders had “not ensured that there is an open and positive culture around safeguarding that adequately protects children from harm.”

Inspectors said the school failed to address child-on-child violence and bullying effectively, with some pupils and members of staff reporting that they do not feel safe on site.

The report said: “The behaviour of some pupils around the school site is dangerous and disrespectful.

“It is sometimes aggressive.

"Lessons are often disturbed by pupils in corridors, making staff and other pupils feel uncomfortable.

"Pupils and members of staff alike report not feeling safe.

"On occasion, some pupils move around in groups and intimidate others."

Inspectors found that trustees had not ensured the school was safe and secure, and said those responsible for governance were not meeting their duties.

They said: “Leaders do not manage the behaviour of some pupils well.

“Staff and leaders care for pupils, but systems to ensure that all pupils behave and treat each other with respect are not effective.”

## Reports of damage, fighting and furniture thrown

The report described a culture in which damage to the building is a regular occurrence, fighting is reported, and staff say furniture has been thrown.

Inspectors said: “There is often damage to the building, specifically the doors.

"Pupils report fighting at the school, and staff report furniture being thrown.”

## Swearing and derogatory language commonplace

Swearing, derogatory language and discriminatory language were also frequently heard, according to the report.

Inspectors said leaders did not have the expertise needed to manage behaviour effectively.

They said: “Some pupils swear, use derogatory language and display aggressive and intimidatory behaviour.

"This makes some pupils and staff feel uncomfortable.”

The report also said bullying was not dealt with effectively.

It added that concerns about pupil safety were shared by staff, pupils and parents.

## 'Significant minority' of pupils not managed well

Inspectors said most pupils were “friendly, polite and want to do well”, but a “significant minority” were not managed effectively.

Behaviour in corridors was described as poor, while movement between lessons was chaotic.

Inspectors said: “Behaviour in corridors is poor.

"Movement between lessons is chaotic and sometimes pupils leave lessons and disturb others’ learning.”

Attendance was also identified as a concern.

## Improvements in academic achievement

The report did note improvements in some areas, including academic achievement.

Pupils’ performance in national tests, including GCSEs, improved in 2025.

Attainment in science was similar to the national average.

Disadvantaged pupils achieved results similar to those of disadvantaged pupils nationally.

However, inspectors said overall standards remained low and that behaviour problems were affecting pupils’ progress.

## 'Well-organised' curriculum

The report also identified strengths in the curriculum.

Inspectors said it was increasingly well organised and that pupils were being better prepared for their next steps after school.

They said: “Pupils study a curriculum that is increasingly well organised.

"The quality of teaching they experience is variable, but improving.

"Work is ongoing to ensure that those pupils who need extra support receive it.”

## What did the school say?

A spokesperson for Beckfoot Thornton said: "In recent months, Beckfoot Trust has formed a partnership with Delta Academies Trust, a large Yorkshire-based trust with schools in Bradford.

"The Delta team will work closely with Beckfoot Trust from the start of this term to further boost leadership capacity.

"Given the level of capacity and resource needed to deliver sustained improvement, the trust Board is working with the Department for Education to consider a transfer of Beckfoot Thornton to Delta Academies Trust.

"The process remains at an early stage, and at this point, no final decision has been made.

"Should the trust decide to move to the next stage of transferring the school, a consultation process will take place with the school community to ensure they have every opportunity to have their voices heard.

"The update comes as Ofsted publishes the outcome of an inspection of Beckfoot Thornton which took place in April last academic year, concluding that the school requires special measures.

"Partnership conversations with Delta have been underway for some time and prior to the publication of the report, with the aim of accelerating improvement in the school in advance of any legal process.

"The report recognises positive elements including improving pupil achievement, developments to the curriculum, support for some of the school’s most vulnerable pupils and careers education.

"It also notes that students know staff care and that a variety of pastoral support is available.

"However, the school also recognised that the behaviour of a significant minority of students has declined, particularly outside of the classroom."

## Staff say behaviour has improved

The spokesperson added: "Inspectors identified areas where further work is needed, with attendance and behaviour, and leadership and governance, judged to require urgent improvement.

"Since the inspection, a number of changes have already been put in place, including:

- Reorganising the senior leadership, pastoral and safeguarding teams
- Strengthening systems around behaviour and bullying
- Providing additional safeguarding training for staff
- Changing the structure of the school day to give students greater access to their form tutors
- Appointing new SEND leadership.

"The impact of these changes is already being seen, creating more effective systems and processes at the school.

"In a recent staff survey, 97 per cent of staff confirmed that behaviour has improved in recent months.

"The trust recognises, however, that significant work remains to return the school to a consistently calm and positive environment."

### What did the trust say?

Shirley Watson, CEO of Beckfoot Trust, said: “Our focus is on ensuring Beckfoot Thornton has the support, resources and leadership capacity it needs to deliver sustained improvement and the environment we know students at the school deserve.

“We are encouraged by the progress already being made, but we know there is significant work still to do.

"Our partnership with Delta is an important part of strengthening the capacity available to the school, and we are carefully considering what will be in the best long-term interests of its students, families and staff."

“Discussions about a potential transfer remain at an early stage and no decision has been made.

"We want to be transparent about the option being considered and, should we decide to move to the next stage, we will ensure our community has every opportunity to have its voice heard.”

The Telegraph & Argus has contacted Delta Academies Trust for a comment.
