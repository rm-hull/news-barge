A seven-year-old is set to take on the Mini Great North Run to raise funds for the team that has supported her since she was a baby.

Rose Durant, from Billingham, was inspired by her mum’s efforts in the main race last year and will run this weekend in support of the cystic fibrosis team at James Cook University Hospital.

She was diagnosed with cystic fibrosis when she was around a week old, following emergency surgery for a perforated bowel just 12 hours after birth.

Kim Cleaver, Rose’s mum, said: "This was very traumatic for us, especially as we didn’t know Rose had any complications until shortly after she was born.

"We had no knowledge of cystic fibrosis and were very scared of what the future held."

Though Rose has faced a range of treatments and hospital admissions, her family says the team at James Cook has been a constant source of support.

Ms Cleaver said: "Rose is thankfully benefiting from new medication and is very well at present.

"The cystic fibrosis team knows how best to work and reassure her and us.

"Last year when I ran the half marathon, Rose came to watch, she enjoyed it and said she wanted to do it this year."

Rose will be joined on the day by her five-year-old cousin, Harry, with both planning to dress up as superheroes for the race.

The money raised will support the cystic fibrosis team in caring for other children living with the condition.

Claire Lord, a physiotherapist at the hospital, said: "We couldn’t be prouder of what you are doing.

"You are showing other children with CF that they can achieve anything they put their minds to, and it’s great physio."

Donations can be made through Rose’s JustGiving page.
