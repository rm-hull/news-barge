Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky traveled to Canada on Thursday for bilateral talks with Prime Minister Mark Carney, marking his most recent international journey aimed at securing ongoing assistance in resisting Russia's invasion.

Kyiv remains under severe pressure from Moscow's aerial campaign, which deploys ballistic missiles alongside jet-powered drones to penetrate defenses.

Ukrainian officials state that Russian strikes have repeatedly focused on the nation's energy grid ahead of winter to undermine civilian morale.

"The main focus will be on supporting Ukraine in protecting our skies and preparing for the winter," Zelensky said of his Canada trip in a social media post. "We will discuss how to strengthen our resilience, support our people and ensure that Ukraine has the capabilities it needs to defend itself."

Canada and various European states are supplying their own military equipment to Kyiv while purchasing American systems through the Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List, or PURL.

Meanwhile, the United States under President Donald Trump has halted direct weapons donations to Ukraine.

"Canada has consistently provided Ukraine with concrete support, and this visit is about taking that cooperation further," Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha, who was also on the trip, said on X.

Speaking at a photo opportunity prior to meeting Carney, Zelensky noted that Canada had taken a major new step regarding air defense for Ukraine, describing it as "a decision for our lives" while declining to "spoil" the official announcement before full details emerge.

He stressed that air-defense hardware is globally scarce and categorized the promised Canadian aid as vital.

Carney confirmed that Canada will deliver supplementary assistance for Ukraine’s energy network while reiterating firm backing for Kyiv’s prospective integration into NATO and the European Union.

Additionally, he guaranteed Canadian commitment toward Ukraine after hostilities end.

"Ukrainians helped build this country," Carney said. "And Canada will help rebuild Ukraine when peace comes."

Zelensky remarked that talks with Moscow would eventually take place, though Ukraine must approach them from position of strength. "We need to be strong, not to be weak," he stated, emphasizing the requirement for a "strong and lasting peace."

Recent diplomatic endeavors by Washington to resolve the ongoing war spanning more than four years have produced no major breakthroughs, though negotiations involving both capitals will proceed.

Zelensky's arrival late Wednesday in Calgary, Alberta, occurred one day after his flight from Moldova to Norway faced delays when officials closed Moldovan airspace following a drone incursion.

The aerial drone conflict between Moscow and Kyiv has intensified dramatically following Russia’s full-scale invasion in February 2022. Neighboring states such as Romania and Moldova, alongside other European nations, report frequent airspace violations.

Ukrainian drones now reach targets in Siberia, officials say

A Ukrainian sea drone injured 28 people, including two children, along the waterfront in the Russian city of Sochi, according to local officials. Authorities confirmed that beach infrastructure sustained damage, though further specifics were withheld.

Russia’s Ministry of Defense claimed Thursday that its defensive forces downed 448 Ukrainian drones overnight. The unmanned aircraft were destroyed over 13 Russian regions, the annexed Ukrainian territory of Crimea, as well as across the Black and Caspian seas, according to the ministry.

Falling debris from an intercepted Ukrainian drone sparked a house fire in Russia’s southwestern Voronezh region, killing one woman and injuring two others, local Gov. Alexander Gusev reported Thursday.

Ukrainian forces targeted eight Russian infrastructure facilities supporting Moscow's military campaign over the preceding 24 hours, including a port in Dagestan and a refinery in Yamalo-Nenets, Zelensky announced Thursday.

Facilities across the Moscow, Voronezh, Astrakhan and Bryansk regions were also targeted, he noted.

Those locations were separated by hundreds of kilometers (miles), illustrating the broad scope of Kyiv's domestically produced long-range drone technology currently troubling Russia.

A day prior, Ukrainian units hit an oil refinery positioned over 3,000 kilometers (1,800 miles) away in Siberia, Ukraine’s General Staff confirmed in a statement.

Meanwhile, Russian forces deployed 149 drones, including jet-powered models, against Ukrainian targets overnight, Ukraine’s air force said Thursday.

A Russian drone attack on the Kyiv region early Thursday morning killed one person and wounded two others, according to regional administrator Tymur Tkachenko.

At least 16 additional civilians sustained injuries across other Ukrainian districts, local officials reported.
