Yorkshire’s first ever joint trade mission will head to India this October.

The delegation, led by Mayor David Skaith of York and North Yorkshire, will include the mayors of South and West Yorkshire and will begin in New Delhi as part of the wider Great North trade mission.

This marks the first time that Yorkshire has come together as a unified delegation to pursue international opportunities.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: "Yorkshire is recognised throughout India for its cricketing heritage.

"But following the UK and India's recent trade agreement, there is the potential to be known for new partnerships with God's own county.

"York and North Yorkshire is a trailblazing city region, rural powerhouse."

The group will then continue with a dedicated programme in Hyderabad and Mumbai, designed to highlight Yorkshire’s economic strengths and secure investment in key growth sectors.

The mission also serves as the region’s first major response to the UK-India Free Trade Agreement, which took effect on July 15.

The UK Government estimates that the agreement will boost annual bilateral trade by £25.5bn by 2040 and contribute an additional £4.8bn to the UK economy each year.

York and North Yorkshire’s delegation will focus on the bioeconomy and creative industries.

York and North Yorkshire is a national leader in agri-tech, bio-based materials and sustainable food production, supported by research at the University of York.

The University of York Mumbai will be welcoming its first cohort of students this month.

Mark Corbett, director of The Biorenewables Development Centre, said: "Cooperation between the UK and India brings together two leading nations driving innovation and growth in a global bioeconomy set to grow to $30tn by 2050.

"I look forward to the new partnerships that will grow from this mission."

York and North Yorkshire’s creative sector, employing more than 20,000 people, will also be represented.

The city is one of only 27 worldwide—and the only one in the UK—to be designated as a UNESCO City of Media Arts.

Tom Higham, creative director at Mediale, said: "India has a fast-growing digital arts scene with a huge appetite for international exchange, so we are extremely excited about building connections and finding future collaborators."

Matt Richards, managing director of Air TV, said: "This mission gives us a fantastic opportunity to build relationships, share ideas and take our distinctive brand of high-quality factual storytelling to new audiences."

The trade mission, backed by universities and businesses across the region, aims to build long-term commercial, educational and cultural links with India, one of the world’s fastest-growing economies.
