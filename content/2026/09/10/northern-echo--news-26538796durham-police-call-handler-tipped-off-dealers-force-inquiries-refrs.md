A police call handler with a drug addiction kept his dealers “onside” by passing on confidential information.

Cameron Clifford, who had ran up large drug debts, abused his position with Durham Police by telling dealers whether they were on the force’s “radar”, between January 2021 and January 2022.

As police suspicions about Clifford grew, an undercover officer, posing as a member of the public, made a false “tip-off” about two of his suppliers’ dealing.

The call was deliberately aimed at Clifford, who then went “into over-drive” to alert his suppliers to police suspicions.

Clifford was desperate to contact the dealers to, in his words, “keep them out of jail”, Teesside Crown Court heard.

But a judge said he, too, was also trying to avoid police scrutiny.

Prosecutor Liam O’Brien said police watched Clifford leave home after work and head to a nearby pub car park, where he was picked up in a Volkswagen Golf.

Mr O’Brien said police “struck” 15 minutes later, arresting Clifford, Brandon Wales and Kieren Smith in the car.

  Drug dealer Kieren Smith, also sentenced to 40-months imprisonment


James Chappell, a third member of the gang, was arrested later.

  Drug dealer James Chappell, received a 48-month prison sentence


Police seized phones from the car, including a “burner” shared by the dealers to run their operation, plus more than £1,000 worth of cocaine belonging to Clifford.

Mr O’Brien said Clifford had a “close relationship” with the dealers and kept in regular contact with them throughout 2021 and into January 2022.

He told them to be careful and to store drugs at separate addresses, but later claimed much of his advice was “bull” designed to make them think he knew more than he did.

Clifford, 28, of Strangford Avenue, Chester-le-Street, admitted two counts of misconduct in public office, one only at the start of a scheduled trial, and possessing cocaine.

Smith, of Main Street, Crookhall, and Wales, of Holly Gardens, both 25, plus 28-year-old Chappell, of Taylor Street, all Consett, each admitted conspiring to supply class A drugs.

Wales also admitted refusing to give police the passcode to his mobile phone.

David Callan, in mitigation for Clifford, said he came from a good family and attended university before joining his “desired” job with Durham Police in March 2020.

But Mr Callan said Clifford’s downfall was increasing drug misuse and cocaine addiction from his late teens, which worried his parents.

He said Clifford was of previous good character and helped his community by coaching a girls’ football team.

“Had it not been for his drug habit, things would have worked out very differently for him,” Mr Callan told the court.

He said Clifford mostly told the dealers what police did not know about them to reassure them they were, “not on the radar”.

Counsel for Smith, Wales and Chappell, each also of previous good character, told the court how the men had turned their lives around since the offences.

Smith has a steady job in groundworking and Wales has worked as a joiner for several years.

Chappell, however, put his life on hold while awaiting the case’s conclusion but hopes to find work in future.

All three had stayed out of trouble since the offences and matured, the court heard.

Judge Nathan Adams told the three dealers they played a “significant role” in the street-level operation for a year.

He said Clifford abused his position by feeding them confidential information from police computer systems to which he had access.

The judge told Clifford: “To some extent you were trying to keep them (the dealers) out of trouble, but it was also to keep you out of trouble”.

Judge Adams accepted the information may have helped the trio but said: “There’s no evidence any police action was directly compromised by your actions.”

He sentenced Chappell to 48 months in prison and the other two dealers to 40 months, reflecting their earlier guilty pleas.

Clifford was jailed for 22 months. The judge said only an immediate sentence was justified because of the “public and serious nature” of his offending.

All were told they would serve no more than half their sentences in prison before release on licence, and could serve less under new sentencing rules.

The judge also ordered the drugs, phones and cash seized from two defendants at their arrest to be forfeited and destroyed.

Durham Police confirmed Clifford has not been employed by the force since February 2023
