A major UK restaurant chain is shutting all of its sites across the country after more than five decades in business.

All 106 Beefeater restaurants will close this week as parent company Whitbread restructures its business.

Whitbread announced the closures in April, including Bar + Block, Table Table and Cookhouse + Pub sites, which have already closed this month.

All Brewers Fayre restaurants closed on Monday, September 7.

## Why are all the Beefeater restaurants closing down?

Whitbread is shutting the chain under a five-year plan to focus on hotels.

The changes put about 3,800 jobs at risk across Whitbread’s UK and Ireland business, which employs around 30,000 people.

Whitbread says the restructure will save £250 million. Some restaurant sites will become extra Premier Inn rooms, while about £1.5 billion of freehold property will be sold.

The money will fund further hotel expansion. Whitbread owns about 86,600 rooms and aims to increase that to 96,000 by the 2031 financial year.

Dominic Paul, chief executive of Whitbread, said: "We always challenge ourselves to improve and, in light of significant cost increases in the form of business rates and national insurance, as well as the implied market discount to our inherent value, we’ve looked hard at the options open to us to maximise value creation over the medium and long-term.

"This has been a rigorous process and we’ve approached all options with an open mind.

"Our new five-year plan builds on our strengths and drives a significant acceleration of our strategy.

"This plan will transform Whitbread into a higher-margin, higher-returning pure-play hotel business.

"We’re going to go further and faster to deliver a great experience for our guests and high-quality growth and returns for our shareholders."

Whitbread expects to redeploy many affected workers but says the changes will still mean job losses across its restaurant business.

A Whitbread statement said: "We recognise the impact of this proposal on colleagues who work at the affected sites.

"As a business which recruits around 15,000 people every year, we expect to be able to retain a significant proportion of those affected and will be looking to redeploy as many of our impacted colleagues as possible.

"However, we do anticipate that the proposed changes, which are subject to consultation, would result in a reduction of around 3,800 roles of a total UK and Ireland workforce of around 30,000.

"We will do all we can to support those colleagues affected."


## Can you still use loyalty points at Beefeater?

You can no longer use reward points at Beefeater before it closes.

Diners had until Monday, August 24, to earn extra points through the loyalty scheme.

They then had one week to redeem vouchers before they expired on Monday, August 31.

The Beefeater Grill Reward Club gave customers five points for every £1 they spent on food and drink.

Rewards could be claimed from 500 points, including £5 food vouchers, free starters, desserts or bottles of wine.

## When are all the Beefeater restaurants closing?

Beefeater will close its doors for the final time at the end of Thursday, September 10.

That will end Beefeater’s 52-year run, which began in 1974.

Beefeater expanded in 1995 after Whitbread bought and rebranded the Berni Inn chain.

By the late 1990s, Beefeater had more than 250 restaurants.


Whitbread launched Brewers Fayre in 1981, and it had more than 100 locations nationwide by the end of the decade.

The smaller chains Bar + Block, Table Table and Cookhouse + Pub launched more recently, with Table Table closing after 18 years.

Cookhouse + Pub launched in 2017, while Bar + Block launched in 2016.

All three chains shut on Thursday, September 3.

## Some Beefeater sites could be saved

Some of the closed restaurants could reopen under different names.

Queensway Inns has conditionally agreed to buy 53 of the 261 Whitbread restaurant sites marked for closure.

A spokesman for Whitbread told Newsquest previously: "Whitbread has reached an agreement for the conditional sale of 53 branded restaurant sites to Queensway Inns, subject to a number of final conditions being met.

"Queensway Inns has extensive experience operating hospitality businesses across the UK and intends to operate the sites as going concerns.

"Subject to completion of the sale and following the relevant TUPE information and consultation processes, around 900 team members could transfer and will be supported through the process."
