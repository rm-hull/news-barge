**Harry Styles is extending his record-breaking Together, Together tour in 2027, with 25 more shows, including a return to Wembley Stadium next August.**

The singer, who is currently in the middle of a 30-show residency at New York's Madison Square Garden, is adding dates in Ireland, Spain, Italy, Germany and France - who all missed out on the tour's first leg this summer.

Every city will see a different support act, with 80s pop icons Tears For Fears playing at Wembley, and pop legend Kylie Minogue on the bill in Los Angeles.

Although Minogue is a festival headliner in Europe, these shows will represent her first ever stadium concerts in the US.

She celebrated the announcement on her Instagram stories, writing: "Together, together. Harry. Me. Can't wait to see you in April, LA."

![Harry Styles on stage in Amsterdam](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1024/cpsprodpb/74a1/live/626eb980-ad18-11f1-826f-933990eae6f0.jpg)

The tour opened in Amsterdam in May

Unlike the first half of the tour, which concentrated on long residencies in cities like London and New York, the 2027 dates represent a more traditional rolling stage show.

Styles will also play US stadiums for the first time as a solo artist - with dates booked in Phoenix, Dallas, Atlanta and Chicago.

After confusion and outcry over ticket prices for the 2026 shows, the singer's team have made the pricing structure clear for his upcoming dates, specifying that prices will range from $60 to $300 (£44 to £221).

The 2027 dates will also introduce "Unpredictable Fun Tickets," an initiative to make concerts more accessible to people on a limited income.

The programme will make a limited number of tickets available for £20 in the UK, $20 in the US and €20 in Europe. Details on availability and how to apply will be announced soon.

The cost of regular tickets will vary by city. Details, including on-sale dates, are available on the star's website., external

Almost one million people attended the Wembley shows, travelling from all over the world to see the former One Direction star

Fans were already aware that a second leg of Styles' tour was on the way, after the 32-year-old posted a note on Wednesday teasing what he called the "final" shows of the hugely-successful run.

"When we decided to do the 2026 tour I knew it would be special but what you turned it into was far beyond what I could have imagined," he wrote.

"I love playing music, I love being with all of you and being a part of the love and joy you all create together every night. That's why I wanted you to be the first to know we will be adding 2027 dates, which honestly, at first I found daunting.

"Being an artist is the only life I've ever known really, I have been doing this since I was 16 years old, and I feel like the luckiest person in the world to still be sharing these moments with you.

"Tomorrow's announcement will be the final cities of Together, Together," he continued.

"After that, I don't know what comes next for me, but I know that I'm excited to find out."

![Harry Styles on stage](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1920/cpsprodpb/e802/live/fe559d00-ad18-11f1-826f-933990eae6f0.jpg)

The singer is donating money from his UK shows to support grassroots venues

Reviews for the Together, Together tour have been largely positive.

Rolling Stone magazine called Styles "effortlessly charismatic" , externaland noted there was "real diversity" in a setlist that covered all four of his solo albums to date.

Variety magazine was also impressed by his stage presence, external, writing: "Styles is a one-in-millions performer, with a hurricane-force charm that's dashing and sexy and goofy at the same time."

The BBC's own review noted the unique stage design, which carved Wembley into smaller fan zones, making the event "more like a club show than a stadium concert - with Styles zipping around the borders, blowing kisses and posing for cameras throughout the night".

Styles' 12-night stint at the venue earned him a Guinness World Record for the longest residency by a musician at Wembley Stadium during a single run.

He will wrap up the tour with three further nights at the stadium next August. £1 from every ticket sold in London will be donated to the Live Trust, which aims to support and protect grassroots music in the UK.
