Ed Husic has urged the Albanese government to take concrete action against illegal Israeli settlements in the West Bank, warning Palestine cannot be “sticky-taped” back together with “yet another statement”.

It comes as Labor party members criticised the federal government for not following the UK, Canada and France in banning trade with West Bank settlements, also urging the foreign affairs minister, Penny Wong, to mount a stronger response to violence against Palestinians.

The western Sydney MP said he was disappointed in his party’s decision not to join the coordinated approach.

Wong said the government was concerned about a blanket ban having “unintended consequences” for Australian businesses, for Palestinians and for Israelis.

Husic, who has been consistently outspoken on the issue, pointed to a potential double standard in how Australia had treated Israel compared with Russia.

“It would sound quite odd for us to say we wouldn’t impose sanctions on Russia because of the impact on Australian businesses, or Ukrainians, or Russians. We took action knowing it would have an impact, precisely because we wanted it to,” he told parliament on Thursday.

Husic said the Albanese government could be justifiably proud of being the first in the country’s history to recognise Palestine. But it needed to back it up with “concrete action”.

“Having stated the ambition, we must act to protect it because settler terrorism is tearing to shreds our ambition to see a state of Palestine, and we cannot sticky-tape that back together with yet another statement,” he said.

The Labor Friends of Palestine group also wrote to the foreign affairs minister on Wednesday, calling for Australia to join a coordinated approach with allied countries, in line with changes to the party’s national platform agreed in July.

A campaign group within the party’s rank and file membership, Labor Friends of Palestine called for careful policy design, rather than “inaction”, including on how targeted trade restrictions could be implemented while protecting Palestinian workers and avoiding unintended consequences.

This week, Wong confirmed Australia was preparing another round of sanctions against Israel, but stopped short of joining the UK in banning trade with Israeli settlements in the West Bank.

The UK foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, called efforts by Israel to control the territory unlawful and agreed there was “ethnic cleansing of Palestinians in areas of the West Bank, perpetrated by settler terrorists”.

Conveners of the group told Wong joining the new push led by the UK would be consistent with the principles endorsed by Labor members at the party’s national conference in Adelaide.

Labor’s platform, which is used to guide policy development, opposes the annexation of Palestinian territory and calls for Israel to bring illegal settlement activity and the occupation to an end.

It calls on the government to end any support being given to illegal settlement activity or extremist violence, and to implement further action and sanctions as necessary.

“The announcement by Canada, France and the United Kingdom provides Australia with an opportunity to give practical effect to those commitments,” the group said in a letter to Wong and provided to Guardian Australia.

“Australia has already worked alongside these countries in responding to escalating settler violence. In June, Australia joined Canada, France, Norway and the United Kingdom in coordinated sanctions and other measures against extremist settlers and those supporting violence against Palestinian civilians.”

The UK will ban goods from illegal settlements in the occupied territories, and introduce a new ban on arms licences and other exports. Sanctions against individuals are also planned but the plan could take six to nine months to be implemented.

In a joint statement, the UK prime minister, Andy Burnham, the French president, Emmanuel Macron, and Canada’s prime minister, Mark Carney, said they were taking further action to push for progress on a two-state solution.

Nine European countries also said they were considering similar measures.

While Australia has previously acted in concert with its international partners, Wong said this week Australia was not pushing for a broad ban on goods produced in Israeli settlements.

She told the Senate on Thursday claims by the Greens that Australia was trying to placate the Netanyahu government were wrong.

“I think that prime minister Netanyahu’s government has made very, very clear their displeasure at the actions that the Albanese government has taken,” she said.

The Greens senator Mehreen Faruqi challenged Wong to say if she agreed with Miliband that ethnic cleansing of Palestinians was taking place in the West Bank.

Instead Wong said Australia had “sanctioned Israeli extremists for the abhorrent settler violence terrorising Palestinian communities”.

Wong has been working with the family of the killed Australian aid worker Zomi Frankcom on a response to the Israel Defense Forces’ decision not to open a criminal investigation into Gaza strikes that killed her and six of her colleagues.
