Work is underway to reduce sewage discharges from a storm overflow into a tributary of the River Derwent.

Yorkshire Water’s project in Crambe aims to lower the frequency and volume of releases from a combined storm overflow (CSO) as part of its wider £1.5 billion investment to reduce discharges across the county.

A new surface water sewer will be constructed, along with four gullies to improve drainage into it.

Qasim Elahi, project manager at Yorkshire Water, said: "We're making good progress with the Crambe project, which, when complete, will reduce the number of discharges into the receiving watercourse – a tributary of the River Derwent. Our storm overflows are operating more than we, and our customers, would like, so we're making improvements across 450 overflows by 2030."

The work, expected to finish in October, involves separating surface water from the combined sewer network to reduce system flow during heavy rainfall.

Three further gullies will be diverted into the new sewer.

The Crambe project is one of eight similar schemes underway in North Yorkshire.

Others are taking place in Malham, Hebden, Wass, Snape, Myton-on-Swale, Great Barugh, and Weeton.

Over the past two years, Yorkshire Water has invested £180 million to upgrade more than 100 storm overflows.
