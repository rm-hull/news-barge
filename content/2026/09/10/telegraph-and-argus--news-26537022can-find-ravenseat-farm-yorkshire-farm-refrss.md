Ravenseat Farm, made famous by television, stands in one of North Yorkshire’s most remote valleys.

The farm is the setting for Channel 5’s Our Yorkshire Farm and its follow-up, Our Farm Next Door.

But many viewers don't realise that it is a real farm in Upper Swaledale, at the head of Whitsundale, within the Yorkshire Dales National Park.

Ravenseat covers about 2,000 acres of upland land, equal to around 1,000 football pitches. It is a working sheep farm and has been farmed for generations.

The Owen family’s life there became the focus of the original television series, which showed the work and difficulties involved in running a farm in an isolated part of the county.

Its location is a major part of the programme. The farm is surrounded by hills and lies away from towns and major roads. Weather conditions can change quickly, adding to the demands of farming in the area.

The nearest village is Keld, about three and a half miles away. Keld has fewer than 100 residents and includes cottages, a bed and breakfast business and a hotel used by walkers. It does not have a shop, meaning residents cannot simply travel a short distance for basic supplies.

Muker is further from Ravenseat and is another village in Swaledale. It has a pub used by people walking in the valley.

For a period, members of the public could visit Ravenseat for a cream tea or stay overnight. Public access ended in 2022. The farm is now private, and people visiting the area are asked to respect it as the Owen family’s home and workplace.

Public roads and footpaths around the area remain available. Visitors can travel through Swaledale and visit places including Keld and Muker. However, there are few shops and petrol stations in the upper dales. People planning a visit are advised to take food and drink and fill their vehicle with fuel before travelling into the valley.

Keld is also on the route of the Coast-to-Coast Walk. Another local site is Crackpot Hall, the remains of a 16th-century building. Its name refers to a ravine where crows gather.

The television story changed after Amanda Owen and Clive Owen separated. Our Farm Next Door was launched on More4 and continued to follow the family’s life in the Yorkshire Dales.

The programme has a different focus from Our Yorkshire Farm. It follows the Owen family as they work on the rebuilding of Anty Johns, a derelict building known locally by that name. The project provides the main story for the new series and links the family’s present life with the area’s past.

Our Yorkshire Farm followed the family during an earlier period. Our Farm Next Door shows how their circumstances have changed while farming remains at the centre of their lives. Both programmes are based around the work needed to maintain a farm and a home in the dales.

Ravenseat itself is not a visitor attraction. Although many people have become interested in the farm through television, the buildings and land are private. The surrounding landscape can be seen from public routes, but visitors should not enter the farm or approach the family.

A visit to the Yorkshire Dales can still provide a view of the places shown in the programmes. Roads and walking routes pass through the valley and connect villages, farms and historic sites. Conditions can be demanding, so visitors need to prepare for limited services and changing weather.

The area remains a working farming community rather than a television set. Sheep farming, walking routes and local buildings form part of everyday life in Upper Swaledale. Ravenseat’s television role has brought attention to the valley, but its main purpose remains the Owen family’s farm.
