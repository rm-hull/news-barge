A RARE bicycle used by an adventurer from West Yorkshire has gone on display at Bradford Industrial Museum.

The bike belonged to the late Neil Crabtree, a lifelong cycling enthusiast who bought it from Ellis Briggs Cycles in Shipley in 1989 for just under £300.

Originally from Halifax, Mr Crabtree developed a passion for cycling as a young boy.

His Ellis Briggs bike accompanied him on countless journeys, including cycling holidays across the UK and overseas.


In the summer of 1990, just months after the fall of the Berlin Wall, he used it for his fifth solo cycling tour of Germany.

Mr Crabtree continued riding throughout his life, using the bike regularly well into his 80s, including for local shopping trips.

After he died aged 89 last year, his children, Jane Nugent and Jonathan Crabtree, approached Bradford District Museums and Galleries to donate the treasured family object.

It is now on show as part of Bradford Industrial Museum’s Made by Many Hands exhibition, which explores traditional and threatened crafting skills from across the Bradford district.

  Neil Crabtree's beloved bike 

*(Image: Newsquest/Mike Simmonds)*

Founded in Shipley in 1933, Ellis Briggs is recognised as the UK’s oldest custom steel bicycle frame-builder.

Mr Crabtree’s children, Jane and Jonathan, said: “We are both delighted that Bradford Industrial Museum has accepted our donation.

“The Ellis Briggs bicycle opened up a world of adventure for dad, both at home and abroad.

“He would be thrilled that it has now made its way full circle ‘back home’ to Yorkshire.”

  Mr Crabtree with his bicycle 

*(Image: Submitted)*

Heather Millard, community curator at Bradford District Museums and Galleries, described the bike as a “perfect addition” to the exhibition.

She added: “In museums, we always talk about stories – after all, those stories make the objects so special.

“To get this bike with such a wonderful story behind it – a Yorkshireman on his adventures – is brilliant.

“I’m so pleased.”

  Jane and her brother Jonathan with their late father's bike 

*(Image: Submitted)*

Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council’s executive member for neighbourhoods and communities, said: “The acquisition of this Ellis Briggs bike could not have come at a better time.

“As Made by Many Hands explores some of the traditional skills at risk of disappearing, this bicycle provides a tangible connection to the district’s internationally respected frame-building heritage.

“It is a fantastic addition to the collection and highlights the important role our museums play in preserving and sharing the stories that make our district unique.”

The Made by Many Hands exhibition runs at Bradford Industrial Museum until November 15.
