It is a new era, and I want you to be part of it.

Over the last decade, I have found myself in Parliament confronted with barriers and walls, and having to contemplate how to scale them with your grievances and concerns, your ideas and aspirations.

I have never given up and never considered the cost. I have simply been tenacious and relentless in trying to bend the curve of history no matter what has got in the way.

Returning to Parliament after a summer on the streets of York talking to residents, hearing the challenges you face in my surgeries and reading and writing to deepen my engagement on the things that concern you most, I was ready to delve deep into my resources to fight again.

This is the difference that Andy Burnham’s Labour Government makes.

On his first day in the House of Commons, as Prime Minister, he invited me to meet with him to discuss policy and the challenges you have faced. Within a week, I had my first meeting.

We are now at work to find resolution and progress change. Never have I found a Prime Minister so concerned, so engaged, so wanting to go further. No more walls, but an open door, an open mind and an eagerness to serve you.

In particular, I raised my 11-year campaign for better mental health services. How 10 months ago, following this fight, we were promised a public inquiry after significant failures occurred but how nothing had happened since.

Already, this is being actioned.

I discussed how we ended up in this situation, following my research and writings over the summer, that power in institutions like the NHS and layers of governance were unaccountable to the public and politicians.

I raised how in the NHS, when mistakes occur, it can take years to find restitution.

The Prime Minister wants me to work on this, so we can address the failures of institutions.

I highlighted how our water companies can dump sewage in our rivers without recourse and how multi-academy trusts have no oversight from the very families they are there to serve.

It is the same problem with the justice system, housing associations and even decision makers.

From my work so far, the Prime Minister is keen to work together for better scrutiny and accountability.

Over the summer, I have been on the doorsteps of York, listening to your concerns and how you feel about the state of your community, the country and how your hopes are just not being realised.

Life is tough, the cost of living real, and services not as they should be.

When your street is left with litter and vegetation has grown out of control or antisocial behaviour is a blight on the neighbourhood, you feel that no one respects your space; respects you.

I understand the cynicism, and that is why we are going to work together, in this new era of “can do” politics, where we can walk through doors, not having to scale walls, to get things done.

A different approach in, working together, building bridges and what I call, flipping politics, where your aspirations determine the action; and agencies and politicians serve.

Rebuilding a sense of place and respecting community is so important if we are to address the wider challenges we see.

It is the foundational as to how our political system should respond.

With this very different government, we have a moment to rebuild our society to the one we aspire.

It is one where we need no longer shout in our social media silos, which only distorts and divides, but instead get things done.

It was such an honour to welcome former Prime Minister, Gordon Brown, to York, where his encyclopaedic analysis of the injustices of our time, identified the urgent need for a new model of politics, one of collaboration and co-operation.

We are doing this on the ground, and Andy Burnham is leading this in our country.

I know that the walls you face are high and challenging, but for the first time in being your MP, the door has been opened, and now I am determined to walk through with every challenge you have.

It is no longer a hope that times are changing, they are, and I want you to be part of this too.
