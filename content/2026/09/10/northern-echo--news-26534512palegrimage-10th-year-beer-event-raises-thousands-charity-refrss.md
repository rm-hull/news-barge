Beer lovers across Teesside have helped raise thousands of pounds for the Great North Air Ambulance Service by pushing barrels more than 50 miles.

The annual pALEgrimage, organised by Stockton micropub The Golden Smog, marked its 10th year as breweries and pubs donated casks and kegs for teams to transport back.

Dave Mockler from Hartburn led a team that started at Wensleydale Brewery in Leyburn before travelling to Bayonet Brewing in Northallerton for a second keg.

Over three days, the team took turns pushing the barrels on a trolley from The Golden Smog in Stockton to The Chapel in Middlesbrough, covering more than 50 miles.

  Teesside pALEgrims raises thousands for the Great North Air Ambulance Service


Dave, who has taken part in the event since 2017, said: “It’s become a really important part of my year.

“Knowing that one of the Smog's regulars had his life saved by the Great North Air Ambulance in 2016, enabling him to take part in the event in 2017, makes the cause particularly close to home.

“You know the money that you raise could genuinely help save someone's life, and that makes every mile worthwhile.”

The donated beer was served at a special festival at The Chapel on Sunday, August 30, with all proceeds going to GNAAS.

  Teesside pALEgrims raises thousands for the Great North Air Ambulance Service


Donations can be made through the pALEgrimage26 JustGiving page.

The pALEgrimage has raised more than £122,000 since 2016, with organisers hoping this year’s event will add £25,000 and take the total close to £150,000.

  Teesside pALEgrims raises thousands for the Great North Air Ambulance Service


John Christie of The Golden Smog said the fundraiser’s growth prompted organisers to move this year’s beer festival to the larger Chapel venue.

He said: “I’m so grateful to all the breweries and businesses who donate every year, and to the incredible pALEgrims who give up their time to walk or cycle these routes so we can raise money for the Great North Air Ambulance.

“The event has grown so much that we moved to The Chapel this year, and everyone agrees it was a roaring success.

“Once all donations are counted, we’re hoping to top £25,000 again, taking us close to £150,000 raised over ten years.”

  Teesside pALEgrims raises thousands for the Great North Air Ambulance Service


pALEgrimage teams collect donated beer from breweries across the region and transport it back under their own steam.

Most teams travel on foot or by bike, but previous participants have also taken to the water.

Every penny from the challenge and donated beer goes directly to GNAAS.

The charity’s doctor-led teams provide critical care at serious emergencies across the North East, North Yorkshire and Cumbria by helicopter and rapid-response vehicle.

The service responded to 2,083 callouts in 2024.

Last year's pALEgrimage raised £21,212.12 for GNAAS, with participants later visiting the charity's base to present the cheque.
