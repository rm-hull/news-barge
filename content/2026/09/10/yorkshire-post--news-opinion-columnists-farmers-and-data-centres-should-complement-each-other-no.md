# Farmers and data centres should complement each other, not compete for resources - Ben Pluke

## It has been a hard year for British farmers. Energy spikes, floods and droughts have all conspired to produce one of the worst harvests on record. Now farmers’ face a fresh threat for their resources; data centres.

However, we needn’t pit crops against computers. Data centres could cut energy bills, absorb farming waste and recycle farmyard waste water.

We need farms for food security. We need data centres for national security. This is not a zero-sum trade-off; smart, forward-thinking policy could marry the needs of both. We might take some hard-won lessons from America. In a bid to buoy the economy and boost national security, the American data centre frenzy has begun. Many rural communities have pushed back. The schemes that have won public support are the ones that give something back: their heat, their revenue and their infrastructure. When communities are brought in early, the build-out moves faster. We’re starting to see the same wave of investment arriving in the UK countryside. Plans for £13.8bn artificial intelligence data campus and battery storage facility show the scale of capital heading for rural Britain. This is not isolated; projects are coming in Leeds, Havering and Kent. Each one is a chance for sustainable and efficient use of resources. Data centres have been designated ‘Critical, National Infrastructure’, on the same footing as energy, water and emergency services. This is understandable; domestic computing capacity reduces foreign dependency, grows our economy, and keeps us competitive.

Farmland is critical infrastructure too. We must protect our capacity to produce our own food; without it we become even more precariously dependent on imports. Productive agricultural land must be used for nothing other than feeding the nation.

Nearby brownfield sites, however, are ideal candidates. A large source of a data centres’ energy usage is devoted to cooling server racks. Much of this energy is then expelled as excess heat, much like the warmth one feels out the back of a fridge. That heat is an asset, and farms are the perfect recipient of it. It could warm poultry and dairy sheds, greenhouses, polytunnels, grain stores and drying floors. Heated greenhouses next door to a data centre could grow salad, fruit and vegetables through the winter.

This doesn’t just reduce energy bills for farmers. It could boost animal welfare and air quality too. Warmer, drier livestock sheds produce less ammonia, protecting the respiratory health of both people and livestock.

Perhaps, a share of the £1.1bn funding devoted to urban heat networks in the Warm Homes Plan could be devoted to rural heat networks connecting data centres to local farms? Waste water from farms can be recycled, and used to cool data centres, easing competition for local water sources. Waste water recycling should become a pre-condition for any data centre operating in a rural area. Equally, non-potable water use for cooling must be mandatory, not merely ‘encouraged’, as it currently is. Equally, the slurry waste produced by farms can be processed into biogas, which could be fed back into a data centre’s energy mix as clean, renewable energy.

The data centre build out is necessary. However, it will only succeed when farming communities benefit directly.

Modern civilisations are built on the back of farming. Future civilisations will be built on the back of new technologies. Today’s societies need both crops and computers. When married with the right tools and policies, they should bear fruit alongside one another.

Ben Pluke is founder of Raft Energy.
