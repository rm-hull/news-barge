**Mayor David Skaith will lead a delegation of North Yorkshire businesses** to India this October in a historic, first-ever joint trade mission alongside the mayors of South and West Yorkshire. The “White Rose” collective aims to secure new jobs, exports, and major investment for the region by showcasing its unique economic strengths on the international stage.

The trip represents the first major regional response to the newly active UK-India Free Trade Agreement (FTA), which came into force on 15 July. The trade deal is projected to boost annual bilateral trade by £25.5 billion by 2040.

The unified delegation gathered for the first time yesterday (Wednesday 9 September) at a pre-visit reception in Leeds, hosted by Grant Thornton. The event was attended by the Indian High Commissioner to the UK, Periasamy Kumaran, and India’s Consul General for the North of England, Dr Vishakha Yaduvanshi.

The trade mission will begin in New Delhi as part of a wider Great North trade mission, before the Yorkshire delegation breaks away for targeted programmes in Hyderabad and Mumbai.

Mayor David Skaith emphasised that the mission is a major opportunity to put York and North Yorkshire’s distinct local economy on the global map.


**Mayor David Skaith said:**

Yorkshire is recognised throughout India for its cricketing heritage. But following the UK and India’s recent trade agreement, there is the potential to be known for new partnerships with God’s own county.

York and North Yorkshire is a trailblazing city region and a rural powerhouse. Along with business leaders, I will be presenting a York and North Yorkshire that is full of talent and ready for growth, from our world-class strengths and leading-edge research in the bioeconomy to a creative sector that is driving growth in one of the UK’s most successful export industries.



For York and North Yorkshire, the mission will focus heavily on two locally dominant, high-growth sectors:

- **The Bioeconomy:** As a national leader in agri-tech, bio-based materials, and sustainable food production, the region boasts pioneering research via the University of York—which is coincidentally welcoming its first cohort of students to its Mumbai campus this month. Representatives from local organisations Agrisound, Anglo American, and the Biorenewables Development Centre will join the trip.
- **Creative Industries:** The local creative economy employs over 20,000 people across digital arts, film, interactive media, and television. Highlighting this sector’s prominence is York’s status as the UK’s only UNESCO City of Media Arts. Local stakeholders joining the mission include Air TV, Peel X, Viridian FX, and Mediale.

West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin and South Yorkshire Mayor Oliver Coppard will join Mayor Skaith to market the combined scale of the county, arguing that going as a united Yorkshire makes the region impossible to ignore. The unified push has also received backing from Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds, who praised the regions for working together to unlock international trade.
