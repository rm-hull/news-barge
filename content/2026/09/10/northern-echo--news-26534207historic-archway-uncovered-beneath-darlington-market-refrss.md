Conspiracy theories, secret tunnels and long-forgotten passageways have all been suggested after an ornate archway was uncovered during works outside Darlington Indoor Market.

The discovery, revealed as contractors carried out excavation work in front of the historic building, quickly attracted attention from curious residents, with some speculating the structure could be linked to long-standing rumours of hidden tunnels.

But according to the markets manager, the truth is far less dramatic.


Darlington Market's manager Michael Harvey said the work involves equipment upgrades at the substation, while the exposed features are historic details from an earlier phase of construction.

He said: “Unfortunately, it is nothing as exciting as what the comments suggest.

“The ornate archways uncovered are simply from a time when more care and attention was given to construction – I believe they were previously just vents to provide air circulation in our cellar rooms before the streets were built up.”

Mr Harvey, who said he expects work on the site to be wrapped by the end of this week, or early next week added that he was aware of long-running theories about a network of underground tunnels running throughout Darlington.

Rumours of subterranean routes beneath Darlington have circulated for generations, with one of the town’s most enduring theories focusing on a supposed tunnel connected to the former Pierremont estate.

It is rumoured that the supposed tunnel may have been built by the Pease family as an emergency escape route and could have extended towards Cocker Beck and Brinkburn.

Others say many supposed “tunnels” are actually old culverts, drains or sewers.

Mr Harvey would only provide the following tongue-in-cheek comment: "The secret tunnels are another level below our cellar rooms... and I couldn't possibly comment on them."

For now, the discovery offers a glimpse of Darlington’s Victorian craftsmanship rather than definite evidence of a hidden passageway beneath the market.
