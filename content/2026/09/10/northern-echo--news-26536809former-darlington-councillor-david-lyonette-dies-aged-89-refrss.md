Family, friends and former colleagues have paid tribute to former Darlington Mayor David Lyonette as being “Darlington through and through”.

Mr Lyonette, who represented Haughton during a 46-year stint on Darlington Borough Council, died on August 31, aged 89.

Born and raised in Darlington, he was first elected to the council in the 1970s and remained a familiar and respected figure in the town’s politics until his retirement in 2019.

  Former Darlington councillor David Lyonette, remembered by loved ones as “Darlington through and through”.


He served as mayor in 1992 and led the council’s work on regeneration and planning for two decades before taking responsibility for transport.

Among his proudest achievements were the restoration of former industrial areas beside the River Skerne and the creation of a walkway along the river into Darlington town centre, an ambition he had held for more than 40 years.

In recognition of his decades of public service, Mr Lyonette was made an honorary alderman following his retirement.

His daughter-in-law, Ruth Lyonette, 60, said he remained deeply proud of what he had achieved through the council and of the difference he had made to his hometown.

She said: “He was very proud of everything he had achieved through the council. That was a real love of his.

  Former Darlington councillor David Lyonette, remembered by loved ones as “Darlington through and through”.


“He was born and raised in Darlington and was Darlington through and through.

"He was a councillor for Haughton for all those years and was involved in transport, planning and regeneration, so he was involved in a lot of important things.”

Mrs Lyonette described him as a quiet and thoughtful man who listened carefully to others before putting forward a strong, well-considered argument.

She added: “He came across as being quite quiet initially, but he was very thoughtful and took in everything people were saying.

“He was also very strong in his argument. When he spoke in the town hall, he would have a very well thought-out argument and people respected him for that.”

  David Lyonette pictured in 2000. 

*(Image: ARCHIVE.)*

She said Mr Lyonette came from an era when political disagreements did not stop councillors from respecting one another outside the chamber.

“They would discuss things and, yes, there were big arguments, but they would still go and have a drink afterwards, regardless of which party they were from,”

“He was very well respected by a lot of people. He was very tolerant of all sorts of people and didn’t judge them. He just took people as they were,” she said.

Away from the council, Mr Lyonette was a keen football fan who followed Darlington FC and other North East teams.

  David Lyonette with his grandchildren Ellie and Theo when he was made an alderman in 2019.


Mrs Lyonette said: “He loved football and would always look at what all the local teams had done every week.

“He was interested in Darlington and all the North East teams, but he would watch any football. On a Saturday he would go to the pub to watch the scores.”

Mr Lyonette was also remembered as the centre of his family, bringing relatives together for regular visits and meals.

His son, Joe, who was also a Labour councillor, died in 2014.

Mr Lyonette was a grandfather to Theo and Ellie, as well as a great-grandfather.

Mrs Lyonette said: “He brought everybody together and was the unifying factor between us all. We all helped to look after him during the last few years and made sure he was okay on a weekly basis.

“We saw him regularly and would go out for meals together as a family. He was very much at the centre of the family, so he will be greatly missed.”

Darlington Borough councillor Nick Wallis, who served with David as ward councillor for Haughton West between 1991 and 2015, said: “David was my mentor when I first became a councillor, and I always listened to his gentle, wise counsel.

“In his understated way, he achieved so much both for Haughton and Springfield, and for Darlington more generally.

"He will be very sorely missed.”

A civic funeral service will be held for Mr Lyonette at St Augustine's Church, Darlington, on Friday, October 9, at 2pm.

A celebration of his life will follow at Maxine's. Family flowers only have been requested, with donations welcomed in support of St Teresa's Hospice.

His son Joe, who also served as a Darlington councillor representing Park East, was remembered for raising thousands of pounds for charity through Darlington Carnival.
