It took years, but Apple finally gave the world its first foldable phone — the iPhone Duo — at its Surprise and Shine event earlier this week. The Duo steps into a busy foldables market, with one particular model primed to give it a run for its money: the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8.

The Z Fold 8 is the latest in a long line of Galaxy foldables, and Apple's first foldable bears a number of similarities to Samsung's passport-shaped device. But for anyone considering investing in either the Z Fold 8 or iPhone Duo, there are a number of key differences to be aware of.

There’s still a month to go until iPhone Duo preorders open, and even longer until the device begins shipping (October 23). But still, there’s no time like the present to check out seven major areas in which the iPhone Duo and the Galaxy Z Fold 8 differ.

![Galaxy Z Fold 8](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/T87Q8rM8cfsFhP7zBPEqTW-1280-80.jpg) 

The first notable difference between the iPhone Duo and Galaxy Z Fold 8 is how much they cost.

The iPhone Duo retails for $1,999 /£1,999 / AU$3,499, which is significantly more than the Galaxy Z Fold 8's $1,899 / £1,699 / AU$2,699 (at least in certain regions), with both phones starting with the same 256GB of storage. If you want the best deal on a wide foldable, Samsung has the clear advantage.

### Diverging displays

![Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 product shots](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/urGVc25XhSYAus78WxKbUU-1200-80.jpg) 

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7pMKFZuPuVrLUV8fb8M7jH-1280-80.jpg) 

![Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 product shots](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/urGVc25XhSYAus78WxKbUU-1280-80.jpg) 

Both Samsung and Apple have gone for roughly the same shapes for their respective foldable displays, but with a small size difference. The iPhone Duo uses a 7.6-inch main inner display and a 5.4-inch outer display. The Galaxy Z Fold 8’s inner display also measures 7.6 inches across, but its outer screen is marginally larger at 5.5 inches.

There’s also an interesting split in how Samsung and Apple have treated the material in their phones' screens. The iPhone Duo uses a matte-like nano-texture display instead of a glossier, anti-glare coating like the Galaxy Z Fold 8. Both materials serve the same purpose — to make the screen easier to read under bright light. But which one is better remains to be seen at this point.

Apple has also given the iPhone Duo stylus support on both displays, via the Apple Pencil USB-C. Samsung used to offer stylus support on its foldables, but unfortunately for wannabe doodlers, the Galaxy Z Fold 8 does not work with any of Samsung’s S Pens.

### Different designs and mismatching materials

![Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 back on tree stump](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/QhsQ5Kk8Qb45mvWQkYvpn6-1200-80.jpg) 

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/SGqVQLHtN4AMzybTNa5KfH-1280-80.jpg) 

![Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 back on tree stump](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/QhsQ5Kk8Qb45mvWQkYvpn6-1280-80.jpg) 

Despite their similar passport shapes, the iPhone Duo and Galaxy Z Fold 8 do differ quite significantly in terms of design.

The iPhone Duo, for instance, has four rounded corners when open, but when closed, it has two right-angled corners on the hinge side. Samsung, by contrast, has gone with pointed corners all around on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, which arguably gives it a more cohesive look.

For side rail materials, it’s a case of polished titanium on the iPhone Duo versus matte aluminum on the Galaxy Z Fold 8. Both look great on their respective phones, but which finish is better will be up to the viewer.

These two phones also use different display materials to shore up their durability. Apple has equipped the Duo with its own Ceramic Shield 2 toughened glass on the front, and the original Ceramic Shield on the back. Samsung instead uses Gorilla Glass Ceramic 3 and Gorilla Glass Victus 2 to protect the Galaxy Z Fold 8’s front and back, respectively.

Apple has also equipped the iPhone Duo with the same Camera Control key that you find on other iPhones, making the edges of the phone surprisingly busy. Samsung lacks equivalents for these buttons on the Galaxy Z Fold 8, which simply offers a power button and volume controls.

Interestingly, the iPhone Duo is some 53g heavier than the Z Fold 8 (254g vs 201g), but it does offer IP68 water and dust resistance, while the Z Fold 8 only offers IP48-level protection.

The last thing to mention is color options. The iPhone Duo comes in either Star White or Night Sky (deep blue), while the Galaxy Z Fold 8 has a wider selection of Lavender, Graphite, Cream, and Pistachio.

![Galaxy Z Fold 8](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/iUqhxBY8smNosTpxDQJ9uV-1280-80.jpg) 

When it comes to actually adding your phone number to one of these phones, there’s an important difference to consider here too. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 offers a physical SIM slot as well as eSIM support for virtual numbers. However, the iPhone Duo is eSIM-only worldwide.

You can easily convert a regular SIM into an eSIM during phone set-up. But unless you re-request a SIM card from your carrier, you’ll be locked into the eSIM way of life from then on.

### Branching battery tech

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/x7JBPWF4wuXt9znCf3k3hH-1200-80.jpg) 

![Galaxy Z Fold 8](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/YsteNnwzrj3TD8UfTeH5TQ-1200-80.jpg) 

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/x7JBPWF4wuXt9znCf3k3hH-1280-80.jpg) 

![Galaxy Z Fold 8](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/YsteNnwzrj3TD8UfTeH5TQ-1280-80.jpg) 

Since Apple doesn’t publish its battery sizes itself, we don’t know right now what the capacity of the iPhone Duo is. Instead, Apple says its internal testing reveals the iPhone Duo is capable of 31 hours of video playback when using the inner display, 44 hours when just using the outer display, or “all-day” battery life when it comes to general use.

Samsung’s testing promise for the Galaxy Z Fold 8 is 26 hours of video playback, which sounds like it’s inferior to Apple's on paper. However, without doing consistent battery testing on both phones, we don’t yet know for sure which phone will last longer in the real world.

For refuelling, Apple claims the iPhone Duo can charge up to 50% full in 20 mins with a compatible wired charger, or 30 minutes with a MagSafe or Qi2 wireless charger. Samsung’s claim is a 63% wired charge in 30 minutes, which doesn’t quite map onto Apple’s information either. This will also require some of our own testing to figure out which device charges faster.

### Contrasting cameras

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/pTKymDmCnbowMMzEn7YbPH-1200-80.jpg) 

![The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8, on top of a towel.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/x5LKqW3nX7K4nZqYjAyFDa-1200-80.jpg) 

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/pTKymDmCnbowMMzEn7YbPH-1280-80.jpg) 

![The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8, on top of a towel.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/x5LKqW3nX7K4nZqYjAyFDa-1280-80.jpg) 

The iPhone Duo and Galaxy Z Fold 8 each have four cameras, serving the same purposes. But there are some technical differences to pay attention to.

On the back of the iPhone Duo, Apple has stuck with its typical 48MP main and 48MP ultra-wide combination. That puts the Duo at a small resolution disadvantage compared to the Galaxy Z Fold 8, which boasts two rear 50MP cameras.

However, things change for front-facing cameras. Samsung uses 10MP front cameras on the inside and outside of the Z Fold 8, but Apple uses one higher-res 12MP front camera on the outer display, and an under-display inner camera of currently unknown resolution.

Samsung *used* to use this kind of camera on its older Galaxy Z Folds, but the Galaxy Z Fold 8 has a more typical punch-hole cutout, removing a bit of the display but guaranteeing higher-quality shots. Maybe Apple has worked out how to take clear, attractive photos through display layers, but it’s not something we’ve seen any other company manage, even after the technology has existed for several years.

### Potentially polarized performance

![Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 game on inner display](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/3D4kZWcyijbRZBC3nF5Zy6-1200-80.jpg) 

![iPhone Duo Hands On](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/9hJYFpM35VTKtuyUyEprhH-1280-80.jpg) 

![Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 game on inner display](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/3D4kZWcyijbRZBC3nF5Zy6-1280-80.jpg) 

The silicon inside the Galaxy Z Fold 8 is a known quantity. The Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy has appeared in other premium Samsung phones this year, including the Galaxy S26 Ultra, and it’s a monstrously powerful chipset.

The iPhone Duo contains Apple’s brand-new A20 Pro chip. Since it’s fresh out of the foundry, we will have to wait for test results to know if it’s more powerful, less powerful, or about the same as the Snapdragon silicon in the Z Fold 8.

We don’t know if there’s any RAM capacity difference, but for storage, you can get 256GB, 512GB, and 1TB versions of both the iPhone Duo and the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8. However, Apple also offers a 2TB edition of the iPhone Duo if you want even more space for your files.
