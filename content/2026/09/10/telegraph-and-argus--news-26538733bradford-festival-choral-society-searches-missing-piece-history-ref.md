BRADFORD Festival Choral Society is asking people across the district to search old family albums, scrapbooks and boxes of photographs for missing pieces of the choir’s 170-year history.

The Society, founded in 1856, is particularly hoping to trace photographs from an 1880s garden party of one of its most important early supporters and presidents, Hermann Averdieck.

Research suggests that a photograph of the event was given to choir members who attended - raising the possibility that copies may still survive in Bradford family collections.

The appeal comes as the historic choir prepares a special Heritage Open Days exhibition at Bradford Mechanics’ Institute Library on Saturday, exploring the everyday lives behind its 170 years of music-making in the city.

  Bradford Festival Choral Society at St George's Hall in 1956. The choir is celebrating its 170th anniversary 

*(Image: Bradford Festival Choral Society)*

The event is part of Bradford’s Heritage Open Days, running from September 11-20 and featuring more than 60 free events exploring the district’s rich heritage.

Visitors to Bradford Festival Choral Society’s event are encouraged to bring along their own BFCS photographs, programmes, newspaper cuttings, tickets, scrapbooks and memories, whether from their own time with the choir or from parents, grandparents or earlier ancestors.

  Jane Clark researching the Choral Society's archive. 

*(Image: Bradford Festival Choral Society)*

Says Sara Daniel, BFCS trustee: “We’re not simply looking backwards. What we’re really discovering is why this choir has kept going for 170 years - and so much of that story belongs to the ordinary people who sang in it, supported it and made friendships through it.

“Formal archives can only ever hold part of that history. There will be photographs, programmes and stories still sitting in family albums, drawers and boxes around Bradford, and we would love people to help us find them.

  Hermann Averdieck, centre, in the choir's archive. 

*(Image: Bradford Festival Choral Society)*

“The Averdieck garden party photograph is a perfect example. We understand that copies were given to choir members, so there is every chance that one may still exist somewhere without the present owner realising what it is.”

Born in Hamburg in 1822, Hermann Averdieck came to Bradford in the 1860s through his business interests and quickly became a prominent figure in the city’s musical life.

  Choir members at an organ recital in Bingley with organist Will Campbell.


Artist John Sowden, whose notebooks preserve a sketch and biographical notes about Averdieck, described his musical enthusiasm, artistic temperament and devotion to the city’s cultural life.

Averdieck became vice president of Bradford Festival Choral Society and, following the death of its first president Samuel Smith in 1873, became president.

Sowden described him as an ‘ideal president’, praising his musical judgement, enthusiasm and devotion to the Society, which he supported for more than 40 years.

He was also closely involved with Bradford Subscription Concerts and was a lifelong friend of celebrated conductor and pianist Sir Charles Hallé.

His contribution extended beyond music. Averdieck supported Bradford Royal Infirmary and the Eye and Ear Hospital, where Sowden recorded that he furnished and maintained two wards at his own expense. He died in 1900.

The Heritage Open Day reflects this year’s national theme, Everyday Lives.

From its earliest years, the choir drew people from across Bradford society. Anniversary research has uncovered stories of performers, supporters and families alongside famous conductors and landmark concerts.

One 19th century newspaper account even records choir members returning from a President’s garden party and singing Hail Smiling Morn beneath the arches of Bradford Midland Station - a piece BFCS still sings at Christmas today.

The exhibition will bring together historic photographs, programmes, newspaper reports and other discoveries showing how generations of Bradford people have made music together while the city changed around them.

The free event takes place at Bradford Mechanics’ Institute Library on Saturday, with the exhibition open from 9.30am to 1pm and an illustrated talk at 10.30am.

* BFCS’s 170th anniversary season continues with Eternal Light at Price Hall on November 14 and A Bradford Festival Christmas on December 19, followed next year by Verdi’s Requiem at St George’s Hall on March 20 and the Society’s 170th Anniversary Gala Concert there on June 26.

Full season details are available on the Bradford Festival Choral Society website, where people can also register to receive the Society’s 170th anniversary brochure, containing details of the season and more stories from its history.
