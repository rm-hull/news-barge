The headteacher of a Darlington school has paid tribute to former teacher and "wonderful role model" PC Matthew Blades, who was tragically killed in the A66 crash.

PC Blades died alongside PC Tom Clought when their marked Cleveland Police car was hit by a VW going the wrong way up the A66 at South Bank at 3.39am on Saturday, August 22.

Just after 11am on Wednesday (September 9), hundreds lined the streets on Hutton Avenue in Hartlepool to bid PC Matthew Blades a final farewell.

  The funeral of PC Matthew Blades was held at St Joseph's Roman Catholic Church in Hartlepool.


Before becoming a police officer, PC Blades worked as a teacher at Carmel College.

After his funeral, Carmel College headteacher Melanie Kane described PC Blades as an “excellent teacher” who “deeply cared about his students”.

She said: “The whole Carmel College community joins together in remembering Matty and in holding his wife, his two sons, his family, friends and colleagues in our thoughts and prayers.

“We remember Matty with great affection as an excellent classroom teacher, a wonderful role model and a colleague with a genuine passion for his subject and for the young people he taught.

“Matty cared deeply about his students and worked particularly closely with some of the most vulnerable young people in our community. His kindness, patience and ability to see the potential in every student meant that he made a lasting difference to many lives.”

The headteacher said former students had since flooded the school with messages remembering his kindness as their form tutor and teacher.

She added: “They have remembered the support he gave them during their own times of difficulty and sadness, the way he listened and the genuine concern he showed for them.

“Their words are a powerful reminder that the impact of a great teacher reaches far beyond the classroom and can remain with a young person long after they have left school.

“We were very sad when Matty decided to leave teaching to pursue a career with the police, but those who knew him understood why he had made that decision.

A fundraising page launched in memory of the two officers for the families raised more than £1.2 million.

The funeral took place on Emergency Services Day, a national day of remembrance for those who have lost their lives in the line of duty.
