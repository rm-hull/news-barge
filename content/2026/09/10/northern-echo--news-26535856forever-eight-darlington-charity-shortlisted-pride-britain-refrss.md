A Darlington charity has said it is an 'honour' to be shortlisted for the Tyne and Tees Pride of Britain Awards.

The Forever Eight charity was set up in memory of eight-year-old Amelia Davies, who tragically died from cancer during the Covid-19 pandemic.

Amelia was diagnosed with stage four neuroblastoma in October 2020 after initially struggling to eat her favourite poached egg sandwich.

   

*(Image: FOREVER EIGHT)*

Doctors discovered a 13cm tumour in her stomach which had spread to her bone marrow.

She died in January 2021 at the Great North Children’s Hospital.

Now her family’s charity, Forever Eight, has been recognised for its work bringing comfort and happiness to children facing hospital stays.

  Heather and her granddaughter Amelia 

*(Image: PROVIDED)*

Heather Hill, Amelia’s grandmother and a trustee of Forever Eight, said the family had no idea they had been shortlisted until they received a call from ITV.

She said: "Of course we do this for the children and in Amelia's legacy and that is the most important part but it is always nice to be recognised with something like this.

"We are a close charity with all the trustees having a close connection with Amelia and we are passionate in what we do."

  Heather and her granddaughter Amelia 

*(Image: PROVIDED)*

The charity provides bags packed with arts, crafts and toys to children in hospital, inspired by something that brought Amelia comfort during her own treatment.

Heather said: "We give the bags away in memory of Amelia as when she was in hospital one of her little friends made a box for her and it just lit her up and kept her occupied.

"Its about bringing them some joy in scary times and letting them be children and enjoy playing with slime or fidget toys -whatever they have in the bags."

Forever Eight has three different types of bags, depending on how long a child is expected to remain in hospital.

   

*(Image: FOREVER EIGHT)*

Heather said: "We have three levels of bags, we have a small one which is designed for children in A&E that may be there for a few hours to help occupy them but not there for a long time. We then have medium bags designed for children who may be admitted to a ward overnight or for the short term and this includes a bit more for them.

"Then we have the large bags with around or over £50 worth of things and 95 per cent of these bags go to the RVI for children with cancer usually who have extended periods of time in hospital. Just to try and brighten up their stay."

  The trustees behind the charity Forever Eight 

*(Image: PROVIDED)*

The charity also supports children at Darlington Memorial Hospital, while part of its donations fund cancer research and other organisations helping children with long-term illnesses.

Heather said the bags were particularly important for children who are too sick to leave their beds or take part in activities on hospital wards.

She said: "We know hospitals have the play areas but sometimes a child might be too sick to leave their bed or not able to socialise so this is making sure that every child can get that some sort of enjoyment when in hospital - all in Amelia's name."

  The charity was set up in memory of Amelia Davies from Darlington 

*(Image: Contributor)*

The family knows all too well the impact a long hospital stay can have on a child.

Amelia was treated during the Covid-19 pandemic, when strict restrictions meant her family could not visit her in the way they wanted.

Her auntie Laura Bailey previously described the heartbreak of watching Amelia battle cancer under the restrictions.

Laura said: “Throughout her full diagnosis, we were under Covid rules. In her last few months, we couldn’t see her the way we wanted. She was so poorly, and we had to protect her."

Amelia spent long periods in hospital seeing only one visitor at a time.

   

*(Image: FOREVER EIGHT)*

Her family said she had been a vibrant and happy eight-year-old who loved nails, hair, makeup and fashion.

Forever Eight was created to ensure her legacy could bring joy to other children going through difficult times.

The charity now relies on regular fundraising to continue its work.

   

*(Image: FOREVER EIGHT)*

One upcoming fundraiser will see a group of children climb Roseberry Topping after turning eight, the same age Amelia was when she died.

The children are taking on the challenge to raise money for Forever Eight and help the charity continue providing its bags to young patients.

Another fundraiser is being held at the Quoits Club on Saturday, September 12, from 4pm until late.

Tickets cost £10 for adults and £5 for children, with a raffle, live entertainment and a quiz planned.

The winner of the Tyne and Tees Pride of Britain award will be announced on Friday, with the successful finalist going to London.

Winners and finalists for the Pride of Britain Awards are chosen by a dedicated judging panel from a comprehensive shortlist prepared by the Pride of Britain research team.
