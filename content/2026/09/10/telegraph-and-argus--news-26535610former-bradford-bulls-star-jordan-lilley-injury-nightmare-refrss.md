Former Bulls ace Jordan Lilley has been out for several months with a dreadful knee injury, but he fears he could be on the sidelines for three years unless he can find the money for private surgery.

Having spent nearly a decade at Bradford, the little half back joined Sheffield ahead of the 2026 season, only to get badly hurt in a defeat to Doncaster in April.

But with Sheffield unable to cover the costs of an operation, Lilley has been forced to rely on a GoFundMe page to help out.

Lilley spoke to the T&A yesterday and told us: “Mentally it’s been tough.

“Being injured and not being able to play the game you’ve lived and breathed for more than 10 years is hard.

“Sitting and watching your team without being able to contribute is really difficult.

“Over the last few months that’s taken a toll, and I’m grateful for the people around me who have helped me through it.”

Reflecting on the injury itself, Lilley said: “It was a strange one, just a right-foot step.

“As soon as I planted it I heard it pop and went down, which anyone who knows me will tell you isn’t like me.”

That was scary enough, but what has followed has only added to Lilley’s pain.

He said: “The uncertainty has been the hardest part.

“I’m someone who needs something to work towards, and not having a clear timeline has been challenging.

“If it has to go through the NHS route the wait could be a long one, which is a daunting prospect.”

  Jordan Lilley was key for Sheffield in their early-season games, but he has been on the sidelines for the last five months with no end to his injury hell in sight. 

*(Image: Alex Whitehead/SWpix.com)*

Lilley admitted on his personal Facebook page to “feeling lost” for the first time in his life, and this situation is the scary reality for many players at semi-professional level, who often cannot access the same level of treatment as in Super League.

The half back did not criticise Sheffield when asked if he had been hurt by how this situation has unfolded, but he did tell us: “Player welfare is so important at this level.

“We put our bodies on the line every week for the club, so getting the right support and a clear path back is everything.”

And that support is now coming in an unconventional manner, with a GoFundMe page attempting to raise £18,700 to help Lilley access private surgery and get his career going again.

The player said: “The support from my network has been amazing.

“To see people at Bradford and further afield backing me and wanting to get me back playing means a lot.

“It’s keeping me going.”

More than £3,000 had been raised when this was written, including £1,000 from Richard Bloodworth of Bulls sponsors MNA Group and £500 from club director Paul Wild.

Lilley will always hold Bradford dear and still takes his children to watch the club’s games, so it is no surprise to see him get that kind of support.

He said: “Richard and Paul have been brilliant.

“They’re great blokes and we’re still close. Them standing by me means everything.”

“They’re friends for life.”
