A SKIPTON family is visiting all lifeboat stations in the UK in memory of a much-loved teacher.

Skipton Parish School teacher, Megan Dryland, 35, died from a brain tumour earlier this year.

At a memorial service in July for her pupils, it was revealed she was a keen supporter of the RNLI and had wanted to visit all the lifeboat stations.

Now, 10-year-old Thomas Mallinder, who was taught by Mrs Dryland for two years, along with his 13-year-old sister, Alice, and mum, Rebecca Shaw, are two months into visiting all 238 lifeboat stations.

  Thomas, his mum and sister have so far visited around 30 lifeboat stations 

*(Image: Rebecca Shaw)*

The family hope to have visited all stations by the end of Thomas's time at Parish Church School in September next year and they have also set a target of raising £1,000 for the RNLI.

Mum, Rebecca, said: “Mrs Dryland was far too young to leave this world and ever since her parting, Thomas has been sad. I wanted to do something.

“After her memorial service when we learned Mrs Dryland had wanted to visit all the RNLI stations, I suggested to Thomas that we could do this.”

So far, the family has visited around 30 lifeboat stations and at each one has left behind a pot of cornflowers, Mrs Dryland's favourites.

  Thomas, his mum and sister have so far visited around 30 lifeboat stations 

*(Image: Rebecca Shaw)*

The pre-planted cornflowers, with seeds and pots donated by Twin Locks Garden Centre in Gargrave, are wrapped in a yellow ribbon, a colour that meant something to Mrs Dryland.

Rebecca, who describes Mrs Dryland as 'amazing' and 'simply lovely', said: “Mrs Dryland meant a whole heap of everything to Thomas, so in turn, I will do everything in my power to facilitate this challenge.”

Rebecca says planning for the RNLI Challenge has – and is – a mammoth effort which she expects will get more difficult with the coming of autumn and winter and with the end of the school holidays.

  Thomas, his mum and sister have so far visited around 30 lifeboat stations 

*(Image: Rebecca Shaw)*

With so many lifeboat stations to get round, they aim to visit at least seven each trip with an overnight stay.

It has meant some very early starts, and at the beginning, sleeping in Rebecca's car, which being as it's a VW Up – was far from uncomfortable, especially with the family's dog, Mabel, joining the trip.

“We've been getting up at silly O'clock to beat the traffic, but it's been great, we work together on the routes and have lists of all the lifeboat stations," says Rebecca.

So far, they have not travelled up to Scotland, and plan to leave lifeboat stations there and in Ireland until the end – when they hope to have enough time.

They have so far visited lifeboat stations up and down the country, including Craster, on the Northumberland coast, Bridlington, Flamborough and Staithes and Runswick.

People can follow the family's challenge on Facebook and Instagram.

So far, they have raised £800 of their £1,000 target.

Donations can be made via their fundraising page at: [https://www.justgiving.com/page/rebecca-shaw-1602?utm_medium=FA&utm_source=CL](https://www.justgiving.com/page/rebecca-shaw-1602?utm_medium=FA&utm_source=CL)

During the October half-term holidays, they plan to visit 30 lifeboat stations in Cornwall and Norfolk, and there will also be weekend trips.

On one trip to the Yorkshire Coast, they later discovered, they had missed Redcar, and plan to make that their last lifeboat station.

Rebecca adds what she could really do with is a campervan, so the family could sleep overnight 'on the road'.

Meanwhile, she hopes their efforts will be a boost to the RNLI.

“Please wish us luck on our quest and please give as many pennies as you feel you might be able, all in aid of the memory of Mrs Dryland and the wonderful service the RNLI do for the country.”
