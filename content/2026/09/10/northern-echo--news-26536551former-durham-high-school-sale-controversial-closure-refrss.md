The former Durham High School site has been put up for sale by administrators just weeks after the independent school's controversial closure.

The independent school closed its doors on July 2, less than 10 weeks ago, leaving 281 pupils stranded searching for new schools and 100 staff members without jobs.

It has prompted fresh concerns about the site's future, with calls for the buildings to be used for education rather than be knocked down.

Marketing material for the South Road site highlights opportunities for continued educational use but also points to the "potential for redevelopment for a variety of alternative uses", subject to planning permission.

Neil Bestwick, a partner in Sanderson Weatherall's asset advisory and recovery team, said the site presented opportunities for education-related uses "across all buildings or in a more condensed form" as well as redevelopment possibilities.

  The 142-year-old independent school closed in July. 

*(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

The school’s Chinese owners Galaxy Global Education (GGE) blamed the closure on a combination of falling numbers, soaring employment costs and the financial impact of VAT on private school fees.

They said the school had become "no longer financially sustainable" - with Durham's MP Mary Kelly Foy branding the decision “utterly abhorrent” and parents reacting furiously.

  Staff, pupils and parents gathered outside the school on the day of its closure. 

*(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

City of Durham MP Mary Kelly Foy said the closure and subsequent sale had reinforced concerns raised when the school shut.

She said: "The closure of Durham High School, and the way that families, pupils and staff have been treated is an absolute travesty.

"Now the site of the school is up for sale, with potential buyers being told the site has potential for redevelopment for a variety of uses."

  Mary Kelly Foy. 

*(Image: UK PARLIAMENT)*

Ms Foy said many parents wanted the site to remain in educational use and warned there was a pressing need for school places, particularly for children with special educational needs and disabilities.

Founded in 1884, Durham High School had operated for 142 years and was regarded as one of the North East's leading independent schools.
