A judge has told a young man to throw away his internet devices and live in the real world as he sentenced him for 15 online sexual offences involving children.

Recorder Christopher Knox told Matthew Callon, now 22, he had been living in a “fantasy world” through “these very, very nasty devices”.

“They exploit in the nastiest way young people like you who are addicted to living online,” he said.

Michael Cahill, prosecuting, said Callon had had sexual chats with five girls and boys who appeared to be aged 13 to 15 through social media and had encouraged them to send him sexual pictures and videos of himself.

One of the contacts was identified with the help of a US law enforcement agency to be a real 15-year-old boy. The prosecution had been unable to trace the others, so could not say if they were genuine children, but Callon had been aware they were apparently underage.

Callon also had 63 sexual videos and 254 sexual pictures of children aged six to 16 on two mobile phones. At the time of his crimes, he himself was 18 and 19 years old.

“He has got a completely distorted view of what sexual activity is,” the judge said at York Crown Court. “I hope he is going to throw away all his devices and never look at another device again.”

Callon, of Millcroft, Brayton, pleaded guilty to one charge of sexual communication with a child, one of causing or inciting a child to engage in sexual activity, three of attempted sexual communication with a child, four of attempted incitement or encouragement of a child to engage in sexual activity and six of having indecent images of children and one of extreme pornography relating to an image downloaded from the internet when he was probably 15 years old.

He was given a two-year prison sentence suspended for two years on condition he does a 26-session rehabilitative programme and 20 days’ rehabilitative activities.

He was put on the sex offenders’ register for 10 years and made subject to a sexual harm prevention order under which his online activities will be restricted, and police will monitor his actions, also for 10 years.

For Callon, Zarreen Alam-Cheetham read out part of a letter he had written, in which he wrote; “I cannot express how ashamed I am of these actions.”

He wrote that they had gone against his personal morale code and he found it hard to believe he had done the sexual offences. He wrote he would never harm anyone, especially a child.

She handed in references on Callon’s behalf including from his employer.

The judge said Callon should keep his current job as it was an anchor keeping him in the real world.
