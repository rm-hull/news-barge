## Row as transport boss caught in hour-long traffic jam due to York roadworks

Works on the Monks Cross Link Road, in York *(Image: Newsquest)*

CITY of York Council's transport spokesperson was stuck in traffic for more than an hour after being caught out by roadworks linked to a housing development.

Cllr Kate Ravilious said she shared the frustration of those affected by the congestion caused by the Monks Cross Link Road works which began on the first weekend of September.

But Liberal Democrat Huntington and New Earswick ward's Cllr Keith Orrell said residents and councillors should not find out about roadworks by getting stuck in the resulting traffic.

It comes as works on the roundabout and utilities in the road happening as part of the building of a 970-home development are set to continue until November.

A council spokesperson said they had permitted the one-way northbound lane closure for the works until Friday, November 6 following delays after they began.

Plans for 314 homes as part of the first phase of the Monks Cross Garden Village were approved in October 2025.

Developer Redrow is behind the scheme and the current road closures.

Huntington and New Earswick's Cllr Orrell asked Transport Executive Member Cllr Ravilious why the local knowledge of he and other ward councillors had not been sought prior to the works.

Cllr Ravilious said she had been briefed about the works but did not remember that they were going ahead on the day she got stuck in traffic.

Speaking after the exchange at the full council meeting on Thursday, September 17, Liberal Democrat Cllr Orrell said: "You couldn’t make it up, the executive member responsible for transport was stuck in the very traffic residents have been complaining about for more than an hour.

![]()(From left to right) Huntington and New Earswick ward councillors Carol Runciman, Chris Cullwick and Keith Orrell *(Image: Pic supplied)*

"Residents and councillors deserve proper notice and a Council that knows what is happening on its roads before the disruption starts."

Liberal Democrat Cllr Chris Cullwick, also of the ward, said people had been left facing major disruption without being properly notified, which the Labour administration had to share some responsibility for.

Huntington and New Earswick's Cllr Carol Runciman said Cllr Ravilious getting caught in the traffic was embarrassing.

She added permission for Redrow to do the works should be withdrawn until proper communication has taken place.

Labour's Cllr Ravilious told Thursday's meeting council officials had looked at various different options for the works but current arrangements were the best option.

She added work with the developer had been ongoing to ensure they communicate properly in the future following the severe disruption.

![]()Cllr Kate Ravilious *(Image: Supplied)*

Speaking following the meeting, Cllr Ravilious said: "I share the same frustrations as fellow residents having been caught in the congestion around Monks Cross recently myself.

"I asked council officers to urgently contact Redrow to do something about its lack of communications to local residents and businesses.

"The developer has now acted on this and people should now be much more aware of potential disruption to their journeys, especially when matches are being played at the LNER Community Stadium.

"And council officers are doing everything they can to adjust traffic signals to alleviate delays.

"As far as local councillors are concerned, I would expect them to stay informed on what is happening in their ward so they can help keep residents up-to-date, especially if a developer is falling short."
