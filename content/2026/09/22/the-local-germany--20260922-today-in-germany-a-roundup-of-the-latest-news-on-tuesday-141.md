**Tuesday's top story:Merz moves to block housing expropriation after Berlin election results**

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has pledged to fast-track federal legislation banning the expropriation of residential property following the Left Party’s landmark election victory in Berlin and its renewed push to nationalise large housing companies.

Speaking in Berlin, Merz said the proposed law was intended not only to prevent such measures in the capital but also to reassure international investors that private property rights remain protected in Germany. He argued that clarity on the issue was vital for Germany’s attractiveness as an investment destination.

The chancellor criticised the Left Party’s expropriation proposals, claiming they had already caused significant concern both domestically and abroad. He also questioned the financial viability of any large-scale nationalisation programme, arguing that the costs would be impossible to meet in practice.

According to Merz, the governing coalition intends to move quickly, with draft legislation already being coordinated between the Justice and Interior ministries. He indicated that a formal decision could be reached in the near future.

The move comes just days after the Left Party secured a historic victory in the Berlin state election, fuelling debate over housing affordability, rent controls and the role of public intervention in Germany’s property market.

**Merz warns of rising antisemitism as Berlin’s Left rejects allegations**

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz has voiced strong concern over what he described as a growing tide of antisemitism in Berlin, following the Left Party’s historic victory in the city-state election and renewed scrutiny of elements within its support base.

Speaking in Berlin, Merz said he was deeply troubled by what he called increasingly radical and openly expressed antisemitic sentiment, arguing that such behaviour was too often met with indifference or approval.

He pledged that both the CDU and the federal government would do everything possible to ensure the safety of Jewish communities in Germany, particularly in the capital.

The comments came as the Left Party faced criticism over the presence of controversial figures at its election victory celebrations, including an activist alleged to have supported armed Palestinian groups and a member of a family frequently linked in media reports to organised crime.

The party strongly rejected accusations of antisemitism. Lead candidate Elif Eralp condemned violence and terrorism as “absolutely unacceptable” and insisted that Jewish residents, alongside other communities, should feel safe in Berlin.

The dispute adds a new layer of tension to coalition discussions, with the SPD warning that cooperation could prove difficult if concerns over antisemitism are not adequately addressed.

**Oktoberfest ride worker dies after serious accident**

A 52-year-old employee at Munich’s Oktoberfest has died after suffering critical injuries in an accident at one of the festival’s fairground rides, prompting a police investigation into the circumstances surrounding the incident.

The accident occurred on Monday evening at a free-fall tower ride, where passengers are dropped rapidly from a significant height. According to police, the worker was reportedly in an area he should not have been and was struck by moving equipment before possibly being crushed. He was resuscitated at the scene and taken to hospital in a life-threatening condition but later died from his injuries.

Police said festival-goers were never in danger, and a crisis intervention team was deployed to support witnesses to the incident. The area around the attraction was cordoned off while emergency services carried out a large-scale rescue operation.

Investigators are appealing for witnesses to come forward as they seek to establish exactly how the suspected workplace accident occurred.

The tragedy comes during the 191st Oktoberfest, which opened on Saturday and attracts more than six million visitors each year. Hundreds of doctors, paramedics and emergency personnel remain on duty throughout the festival to respond to accidents and medical emergencies.

**Thousands of auto workers join nationwide protests over job cuts**

Around 175,000 workers took part in nationwide demonstrations organised by Germany’s powerful IG Metall union on Monday, protesting against job losses, factory closures and planned reductions within the country’s automotive sector.

The union said employees participated in approximately 280 actions across Germany as part of its “Automotive Industry Day of Action”, held under the slogan “A Future, Not Mass Layoffs”.

Workers from major car manufacturers and suppliers joined factory gate rallies, workplace meetings and public demonstrations.

IG Metall chair Christiane Benner said employees were no longer willing to “pay for a crisis they did not cause”, arguing that job cuts were not the answer to the challenges facing the industry. She urged companies to focus instead on innovation, investment in new technologies and strengthening production sites in Germany.

The union also criticised attempts to weaken labour protections, including collective bargaining agreements, co-determination rights and the 35-hour working week.

Alongside its message to employers, IG Metall called on the federal government to pursue a more active industrial strategy, including targeted support for manufacturers, stronger trade protections and local-content requirements.

An extraordinary meeting of the German Olympic Sports Confederation (DOSB) will take place in Baden-Baden this weekend, at which delegates will vote on the country’s preferred candidate for the 2036, 2040 or 2044 Summer Olympics.

The decision will determine whether Berlin, Munich or the Cologne/Rhine-Ruhr region becomes Germany’s official contender in the race for the Games. The chosen bid will then enter an increasingly competitive international process, with cities including Ahmedabad, Doha, Riyadh and Istanbul expected to be among the leading rivals.

Ahead of the vote, sports historian Stephan Wassong of the German Sport University Cologne said Germany’s chances would depend on presenting a credible financial plan, demonstrating flexibility in response to changing economic conditions and securing broad public backing.

Berlin’s prospects of becoming the preferred candidate appeared to take a hit this week when Elif Eralp, leader of the election-winning Left Party in the capital, argued that public funds should instead be focused on addressing Berlin’s housing crisis.

But Munich and Cologne/Rhein-Ruhr were considered favourites even before Eralp’s intervention, largely because their bids received strong public support in referendums. Hamburg withdrew from the race after a referendum defeat earlier this year, while Berlin chose not to hold a referendum.

The International Olympic Committee is due to select a host city in 2029.
