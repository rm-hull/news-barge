A NEW equipment sharing scheme designed to support community events is being showcased at this year’s York Food Festival.

David Skaith, Mayor of York and North Yorkshire, will visit the festival on Tuesday, September 22, 2026, to see the scheme in action and learn how it is helping community groups deliver events in the city.

The scheme, developed by York Food Festival, provides not-for-profit organisations with free access to event equipment such as marquees, tables, chairs, cooking gear, and display materials.

It is funded through the Vibrant and Sustainable High Streets Fund, part of the £27 million Mayoral Challenge Fund, and aims to reduce the financial and logistical barriers for groups organising events across York and North Yorkshire.

Michael Hjort, director of York Food Festival, said: "The Festival gives us a good opportunity to demonstrate what the equipment sharing scheme can do in practice.

"The new equipment is being used here during the Festival, but its purpose is to support a much wider range of organisations and events throughout the year."

During his visit, Mr Skaith will meet Mr Hjort and observe a free children’s school workshop that forms part of the festival’s community programme.

Mr Skaith, mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: "Our high streets are where our communities come together.

"That’s why I’m backing creative ideas from the people who know their area best with my High Street Fund.

"By supporting the events equipment sharing scheme in York, we are creating a pool of equipment for events and community activities on the York high street, reducing costs, transport needs, and environmental impact.

"This is one of 66 projects we’ve supported across York and North Yorkshire helping to transform our high streets into vibrant destinations that residents and visitors will want to return to again and again."

The equipment, currently in use at York Food Festival, will become available to other organisations following the event.

The aim is to maximise the use of the equipment year-round and enable more events to take place on high streets and in community spaces across the region.

The scheme has received £46,000 in funding and is supported by the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority.

Organisations interested in using the equipment can find more information and submit enquiries through the York Food Festival website.

Mr Skaith’s visit will also help promote the scheme to other groups that may benefit from the shared resources.
