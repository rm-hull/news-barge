!["Air traffic out of control" reads the headline on the front page of Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/828/cpsprodpb/7b90/live/d73ee850-b602-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

British air traffic is "out of control", writes the Metro, after a second glitch to the control system this month grounded "thousands" of flights. Now, it says airline chiefs are calling for the boss of the National Air Traffic Services (Nats) to be sacked. Transport secretary Heidi Alexander has called the outage "deeply frustrating". Also on the Metro's front page, "Cindy's agony" as Presley Gerber, son of supermodel Cindy Crawford, has died aged 27.

!["Burnham sends RAF to help Saudis" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1040/cpsprodpb/2f7a/live/5f03c3d0-b605-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

The lead story for the Telegraph is Prime Minister Andy Burnham's decision to send "RAF to help Saudis" against the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen. The PM said he had agreed to give refuelling support to Saudi jets on advice from Defence Secretary Wes Streeting and Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband.

!["PM agrees military aid for Saudis over Yemen" reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/809/cpsprodpb/3271/live/433fb8d0-b604-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

The Times also leads with "military aid for Saudis", quoting Burnham as saying that getting oil flowing will help the cost of living. This is his "first big foreign policy intervention", the paper notes. Also on its front page, The Times reports that most inmates freed as part of the early release scheme will not be given a tag before their release. The paper says the Probation Service does not have capacity to fit monitoring tags before releases begin on 1 October and that released inmates will instead be tagged "once in the community".

!["UK to give support to Saudi jets in bid to counter Houthi fighters" reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/782/cpsprodpb/5632/live/636952c0-b603-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

The Guardian too has British support for Saudi Arabia as its top story. It also notes the PM has said he will use his first meeting with US President Donald Trump to urge him to end the conflict in Iran. In domestic news, Martha's Rule, which allows patients, staff and relatives to request a review of their care, is being expanded across all A&Es in England.

!["Burnham agrees UK military help for Saudi Arabia" reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1536/cpsprodpb/437b/live/2d78d970-b611-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

The Independent echoes several other fronts with Burnham's announcement about aid to Saudi Arabia, which comes as he prepares to meet Trump at the UN in New York City. The paper also carries a striking image of a burning building following a Russian attack on Zaporizhzhia on Monday.

!["NHS needs 55,000 more nurses" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/3863/live/214a4a70-b603-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

In other NHS news, the Daily Mirror reports that the health service "needs 55,000 more nurses". A "damning" report found that the shortfall is double the advertised vacancies, "putting patients at risk". Like several of Tuesday's papers, it also previews the upcoming season of the Celebrity Traitors, due to start on 1 October.

!["Letby doc: We need a retrial" reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/553/cpsprodpb/9038/live/a58bc860-b601-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.png)

On the front page of the Sun is a call for a retrial for nurse Lucy Letby by a doctor who originally accused her. Now, Dr John Gibbs tells the paper he is "not sure of guilt". Letby is serving 15 whole life sentences after she was convicted of the murder of seven babies and the attempted murder of seven others.

!["New small boat farce" reads the headline on the front page of Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/810/cpsprodpb/ab49/live/3960b6c0-b604-11f1-bc1f-3f186ca4140c.jpg)

The Daily Mail says there is a "new small boat farce" after it reports a dinghy carrying "up to seven migrants" landed unintercepted near Folkestone, Kent. It reports five of the occupants were apprehended "only after a GB News producer alerted the authorities".

!["UK fires torpedo from undersea drone in new warning to Putin" reads the headline on the front page of The i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/830/cpsprodpb/b4eb/live/e0b25750-b602-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

The UK has fired a torpedo from an undersea drone in what The i Paper says is a "new warning to Putin". This was the first test of the "XV Excalibur" drone, it adds.

!["Kremlin-aided forgers moved $7bn through global banks" reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/1056/cpsprodpb/14ce/live/c319e500-b602-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

The Financial Times reports that "Kremlin-aided forgers moved $7bn through global banks". The Kremlin-backed fintech company "tricked its way" into Standard Chartered, Citigroup and other international banks in a case of "old-fashioned money laundering".

!["More freedom handed to new arrivals than British citizens" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/98ed/live/0258b0c0-b603-11f1-a430-4d16ee157c41.jpg)

In an exclusive for the Daily Express, leader of the Conservatives Kemi Badenoch tells the paper "more freedom [is] given to new arrivals in Britain than British citizens." She was pictured with residents of Piddington, who have voted to leave the UK in a symbolic referendum to protest the government housing migrants nearby.

!["Racing done for!" reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/802/cpsprodpb/de3e/live/41f972a0-b603-11f1-b1d1-571ed4d7ff2c.jpg)

"Racing done for!" laments the Daily Star as the head of Betfred, Fred Done, says he "won't sponsor the Derby if bet shop tax rises". The Star says his sponsorship warning applies to racing's five Classics.

**The UK's support for Saudi Arabia in its war against the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen makes the front pages of the Times**, external**, the Daily Telegraph**, external** and the Guardian**, external**, which calls it a significant military intervention.**

The i Paper reports that the UK has fired a torpedo, external from an uncrewed, underwater drone for the first time. According to the paper, the test was carried out last week in British waters and the weapon is part of the deterrent designed to protect UK ships and seabed pipelines from attacks by hostile powers.

The Financial Times says a financial technology company backed by the Kremlin managed to trick its way into the global financial system to launder nearly $7bn (£5bn) using what it describes as a "vast document forgery, external operation". The paper says it allowed Russian companies to settle bills abroad, even though its banks were cut off from the system because of the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The majority of prisoners being freed under the government's early release scheme next month will not be fitted with monitoring tags, external before they leave prison, according to The Times. The paper says 1,350 prisoners will be freed over a two-week period from 1 October, but it understands the Probation Service does not have the capacity to fit tags before release to all but the most dangerous offenders, with the remainder being fitted in the community. A Ministry of Justice spokesperson is quoted as saying that the government's investing £100m in the "biggest expansion of tagging in British history, introducing a presumption to tag offenders before release for the first time".

A University of Cambridge study suggesting that girls and boys exhibit differences, external depending on their gender within just a few hours of being born is picked up by a number of the papers. The Telegraph says the Cambridge scientists have shown baby girls tend to prefer looking at human faces, while boys choose to gaze at objects. Their conclusion in a nutshell: that both nature and nurture play roles in shaping differences between the sexes, not nature or nurture.
