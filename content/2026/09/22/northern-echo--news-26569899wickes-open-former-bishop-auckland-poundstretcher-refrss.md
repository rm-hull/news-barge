Wickes is set to take over the former Poundstretcher store in Bishop Auckland.

The home improvement retailer is expected to move into the shuttered Fylands Drive shop, just off the A688, after plans were lodged with Durham County Council for new signs.

The store has remained closed since Poundstretcher closed for good on August 7, just months after the firm announced a restructuring plan to restore financial stability.

Wickes has long been expected to open a store in Bishop, with the firm initially striking a deal to open in the first phase of Bishop Auckland Retail Park.

However, while the likes of The Range, Home Bargains, and Nando’s have opened at the newly developed complex, this never came to fruition.

Plans have been submitted for seven signs at the site, minutes from the retail park, including two blue Wickes logos on the building and a tall sign by the car park entrance.

Planning documents lodged in late August also show a logo at the rear of the building and two signs beside the main doors: ‘kitchen and bathrooms design and installation’ and ‘home improvement centre’.

Wickes is known for selling DIY products and home improvement building materials and tools for decorating everything from kitchens and bathrooms to gardens.

The brand was born in Michigan, in the US, in 1854 when Henry Dunn Wickes and his brother started a local lumber business before coming over to the UK in 1972.

Wickes has one store in County Durham, at St Andrews Park in Durham, plus sites at Pennywell Industrial Estate in Sunderland and Portrack Lane in Stockton.

Poundstretcher shut during a turbulent period for the brand, just months after the High Court heard it would “likely have no choice” but to file for administration.

The company then presented a restructuring plan to restore Poundstretcher to “financial stability” and enable it to “implement the turnaround business plan”.

The firm said this was focused on “strengthening Poundstretcher’s long-term position and creating a company that can grow in the years ahead.
