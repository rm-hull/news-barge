## Police 'need an accurate picture' of anti-social behaviour in York town

Haxby Town Council's call to residents to report antisocial behaviour has been supported unanimously by its partners in tackling the problem *(Image: Contributed / File image from Newsquest)*

EFFORTS by a York town council urging residents to report anti-social behaviour (ASB) directly to police have received unanimous backing from officials it is working with to tackle the long-term problem.

Haxby Town Council's appeal, reported by The Press last week, came amid concerns that it was among towns included in recent national anti-social behaviour ‘spikes’.

Its spokesperson said it was working with North Yorkshire Police, ward councillors, the deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime for York and North Yorkshire, and York Outer MP Luke Charters to cut crime and anti-social behaviour.




A council spokesperson said police would not capture reports posted on social media, so residents “cannot expect a police response”.

Jo Coles, York and North Yorkshire's deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime, said: "Anti-social behaviour damages communities and ruins people's lives.

"I welcome Haxby Town Council's efforts to encourage residents to report incidents of anti-social behaviour and crime to North Yorkshire Police.

“While social media can help raise awareness of issues, reports made through official channels give police the information they need to respond effectively and help build an accurate picture of what is happening across the county.

"The Mayor of York and North Yorkshire and I are determined to ensure everyone in our region feels safe."

![]()Jo Coles, York and North Yorkshire's deputy mayor for policing, fire and crime *(Image: Supplied)*

### 'Residents should be able to enjoy our parks, streets and public spaces without intimidation or nuisance'

Haxby and Wigginton Liberal Democrat ward councillors said sharing experiences on social media can alert neighbours to problems, but it does not replace making a report.

The group's spokesperson added: “Equally, residents need to know that reporting incidents leads to something.

"It is understandable that people become frustrated if they report problems but see little change.

"Visible policing, clear communication and follow-up are essential to maintaining confidence and encouraging people to keep reporting.

“Residents should be able to enjoy our parks, streets and public spaces without intimidation or nuisance.

"Tackling persistent problems needs a coordinated response from the police, councils and other local organisations.

“We also welcome the Town Council’s continued support for youth work and investment in local facilities.

"The vast majority of young people are not involved in anti-social behaviour, and their need for places to meet and things to do should not be overlooked.

![]()Haxby and Wigginton Liberal Democrat councillors Ian Cuthbertson, Andrew Hollyer and Richard Watson *(Image: York Liberal Democrats)*

York Outer MP Luke Charters filmed in Haxby in 2025 as part of a Channel Four documentary on Government plans to reduce ASB and said: “I know there are real concerns about anti-social behaviour in Haxby.

"It’s something I’ve spoken to residents about a lot, including at the town hall I held there with the police.

"Facebook groups are useful for letting neighbours know what’s going on, but please make sure you report it to the police as well.

"We’ve seen more neighbourhood police officers and PCSOs coming into York, which is really welcome.

"But to make sure those officers are in the right places, the police need an accurate picture of what’s actually happening."

"If something isn’t reported, they simply don’t have that full picture.

"So please keep reporting incidents. It genuinely helps the police decide where officers and resources need to go.”

![]()York Outer MP Luke Charters *(Image: Supplied)*

A spokesperson for North Yorkshire Police said: "It's really important to report crime and antisocial behaviour to the police.

"It helps us to bring offenders to justice and stop more incidents from happening."
