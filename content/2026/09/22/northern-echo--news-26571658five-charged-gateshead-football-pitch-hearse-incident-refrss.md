Five men have been charged after a hearse was driven onto a Gateshead football pitch sparking a probe into organised crime.

An investigation was launched in summer 2023 after a hearse and a car, carrying a group of balaclava-clad men, were driven onto the Dunston Football Club field on July 21.

The ordeal, which saw the vehicles do donuts during half-time of the Dunston UTS v Gateshead friendly, was the first of a string of connected incidents across that summer.

Others include reports of criminal damage across addresses in the Dunston, in Gateshead, and Byker, in Newcastle, areas as well as a report of a stolen vehicle from the Durham area.

Following a complex investigation into the suspected conspiracy behind the incident, Northumbria Police has confirmed that five men have been charged.

Carl Barron, 43, of Grange Villa, has been charged with two counts of conspiracy to commit criminal damage, conspiracy to make threats to kill and conspiracy to steal.

Ian Boddy, 44, previously of Bishop Auckland, has been charged with conspiracy to steal and participating in the activities of an organised crime group.

Darren Kay, 31, of Chester-Le-Street, has been charged with conspiracy to make threats to kill.

Graeme Rutherford, 54, of Birtley, Gateshead, has been charged with two counts of conspiracy to commit criminal damage and conspiracy to make threats to kill.

John Wright, 42, of Tow Law, has been charged with two counts of conspiracy to commit criminal damage, conspiracy to make threats to kill and conspiracy to steal.

All five men are due to appear at Sunderland or South Tyneside Magistrates’ Court this week.

The force said: “As ever, we'd ask the public to refrain from any kind of speculation – both in the community and online – which could negatively impact the proceedings.

“We’ve been working through a long-spanning and complex investigation since we were first aware of this activity, which we have always treated as being linked.

“Operation Sentinel is our regional response to reports of serious and organised crime – and our teams have worked tirelessly alongside our colleagues from the Crown Prosecution Service to have now reached these charges.

“If you've got information about anything suspicious in your communities, or you want to tell us something, please send us a direct message on social media or using live chat and report forms on our website.

The incident at Dunston Football club meant the game was unable to continue after the hearse was abandoned on the pitch. Leaflets were also thrown onto the grass.

UTS Dunston said at the time that it was "deeply saddened" by the game being abandoned following the bizarre events as some team members launched a fundraiser towards repairing the damaged pitch and barrier.
