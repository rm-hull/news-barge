The Pentagon updated its official casualty database to list 19 total U.S. military deaths related to the war with Iran after quietly adding a new fatality.

The change occurred following the death of a female Army officer who suffered a fatal medical emergency Friday while traveling to the Middle East, as reported by *The Washington Post*.

A U.S. defense official speaking on the condition of anonymity told the outlet that the casualty was logged into the database sometime after Monday afternoon. The death was reported earlier by Task & Purpose. Her identity has not been released.

The addition brings the total number of documented fatalities in the Defense Casualty Analysis System, or DCAS, to 19, up from 18 recorded earlier in the week. The database also lists more than 820 service members wounded in action.

However, current and former officials indicate the public count remains incomplete. Five U.S. officials told the publication last week that at least 22 service members have died in the region since military action began on February 28, while a sixth official placed the figure at 23.

![Pentagon records now reflect 19 US military fatalities connected to the Iran war following the unannounced addition of another service member’s death](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/22/20/36/GettyImages-2265308261.jpeg)

Some of those unrecorded deaths involve military personnel deployed to the region during active hostilities, even if not directly killed in combat.

In response to reports regarding the accounting discrepancies, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth rejected claims of intentional omissions.

“Even if we wanted to attempt to hide a combat death, we never would and we never could,” Hegseth said.

The accounting methods used by the Defense Department have drawn scrutiny throughout the conflict. Earlier this year, four service members killed in action were temporarily left off the official count.

Discrepancies in casualty counts carry logistical consequences beyond public record-keeping. The military conducts a formal “line of duty” inquiry for every death to determine whether it occurred during active duty, a designation that directly dictates the financial survivor benefits allocated to military families.

![Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth rejected claims that the military was masking fatalities, asserting that the Pentagon could not hide combat deaths even if it attempted to do so](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/19/14/2026-09-18T155041Z_1888227381_RC2RLNA0ZT7Y_RTRMADP_3_USA-DEFENSE-POWS.JPG)

Within the DCAS system, 14 of the listed deaths are categorized under Operation Epic Fury, the official administration title for the military campaign against Iran.

An additional five deaths — including the female officer added this week — are listed under a separate “Overseas Operations” category, which includes casualties that occurred after a brief July ceasefire collapsed.

In addition, the Pentagon issued press releases earlier in the war acknowledging the deaths of two service members in the Middle East at bases targeted by Iranian strikes, though neither name appears in the DCAS registry.

The conflict, now in its seventh month, continues to face widespread skepticism among the public and lawmakers.

“The Washington war machine is trying to turn Iran into another Iraq,” Representative Nancy Mace, a South Carolina Republican, said in March. “Our troops are not to be used for another endless war.”

A recent Fox News poll found 60 percent of voters believed the military action in Iran was a wrong decision, while 71 percent indicated they did not believe the administration had a clear plan to end the hostilities.

![President Donald Trump attended a July ceremony at Delaware’s Dover Air Force Base for four fallen service members, calling the ritual ‘one of the hardest things to do as a president’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/01/21/44/GettyImages-2286569858.jpeg)

In July, President Donald Trump attended a dignified transfer ceremony at Dover Air Force Base in Delaware for four service members killed during operations tied to the war.

The soldiers honored included Army 1st Lieutenant Tyler James Feehan, 25, of Hawaii, Army Private Isabella Gonzales, 19, of Texas, Army Sergeant Angel S. Rampersad, 28, of New York, and Army Sergeant Michael Emmanuel Swinton, 30, of North Carolina.

Feehan, Gonzales and Rampersad died following an attack on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, while Swinton was killed during a controlled detonation of a downed drone in Iraq.

Speaking to reporters on his way to the ceremony, Trump called the event “one of the hardest things to do as a president.”

“There’s no games, there’s no nothing — that’s their child,” Trump said. “And all you can do is throw out your heart.”

*The Independent* has contacted the Pentagon for comment.
