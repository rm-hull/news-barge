Twenty people were arrested in a crackdown on suspected organised crime groups.

More than 130 officers carried out raids across Redcar and Middlesbrough on Monday (September 21).

Cleveland Police were joined by the National Crime Agency and the North East Regional Organised Crime Unit for the day-long Operation Artemis raids.

The force has released details of the arrests.

* 20 arrests in total for the day.

* 17 men and three women were arrested for a range of offences including organised crime activity, possession of firearms, supplying class A drugs, violence, rape and dangerous driving.

* Four of those arrested were connected to precursor events to the tragic incident on the A66.

* Seven have since been charged and were remanded to appear before Magistrates today, (Tuesday, September 22).

* Suspected class A and class C drugs, cash, a knife, a knuckle duster, a baton, an illegal e-bike, an electric scooter, an electric assisted pedal cycle (revvi) and a suspected stolen wacker plate have been seized. After assessment, a dog suspected to be a banned breed was not seized.

* Throughout the day, officers and PCSOs from neighbourhood teams across South Bank, Grangetown, Eston and Redcar continued the high visibility presence with engagement in retail parks and Dormanstown Primary School, along with delivering leaflets to neighbouring properties where arrests had taken place.

* Specialist officers from South Yorkshire Police were drafted in to assist in the operation on off road bikes and a quad, to tackle illegal bikes in the area.

* Matrix officers stopped 27 cars during the day of action, issuing fixed penalty notices for driving offences and a vehicle was seized due to the driver having no licence and no insurance.
* Two were arrested for theft of a motor vehicle after a Surron was spotted driving around Grangetown in an antisocial manner. The drone followed the riders to an address where officers surprised them with a visit. The Surron was seized along with another high value electric bike and a wacker plate, both suspected to be stolen.

The raids were carried out in the wake of a fatal crash resulting in the death of two police officers.

PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38, died following a serious collision on the A66 in South Bank in the early hours of Saturday, August 22.

The crash also sparked grief and anger because Jakub Matusiak, Makai Saddington, Theo Rae, Michael Robert Cahill and Cole Robert Worthy died.

An inquest heard that a police pursuit had been aborted before the Volkswagen Passat entered the A66 in the wrong direction and collided with the officers' vehicle.
