A Reform UK councillor has rejected claims Durham County Council is in "chaos" after three members quit the party in a single day, including one of his ward colleagues.

The departures left Reform UK with a majority of just three on its flagship local authority, which it won in a landslide last May.

However, Cllr John Kinvig said he did not believe they reflected wider problems within the council leadership.

"It's perception," he said. "People say it's chaos - it's not chaos.

"We are progressing forward with our plan, and it's not chaos."

![]()Cllr Kinvig in blue on a Multi-agency walkabout around Bishop Auckland. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Cllr Kinvig also expressed surprise at the decision by Bishop Auckland councillor Lyndsey Fox, who represents the same division.

"No, I haven't spoken to her since she made that decision," he said.

"I spoke briefly some months ago when we were at Kynren, but it's a bit of a surprise to myself.

"She jumped from Conservative to Reform, she didn't like what the Conservatives were doing, and now she's left Reform.

"But she's still a representative of the community, and that's what she wants to focus on next, is more of that.

"My phone's always open for her, she's got my WhatsApp, she can text anytime."

![]()Lyndsey Fox. *(Image: Durham County Council)*

Cllr Fox said she had left Reform because of "growing concerns about the culture I have experienced" and stressed her resignation was not linked to upcoming budget discussions.

She said she had raised a "serious safeguarding concern" with party leadership in July, which she subsequently reported to police and the Police and Crime Commissioner, but felt there had been no meaningful response.

Cllr Fox said she had become increasingly uncomfortable with the direction of the group since taking office and believed too much focus was being placed on internal politics and future elections.

![]() *(Image: SKY)*

Despite the latest departures, Cllr Kinvig said those remaining in Reform UK were united.

"We have a handful of individuals who had a perception of what they wanted to do and for whatever reason, they feel that they don't want to be part of Reform and they want to leave, fine," he said.

"We have our agenda, we know what we're doing, we're happy with what we're doing, and the people that are remaining, we're all one team.

"We're here for the residents of County Durham and we're trying to achieve what it is we set out to achieve."

Responding to the resignations, council leader Andrew Husband accused the departing councillors of letting voters down.

He said: "These people have let their residents down.

"They have conned them using Reform colours and betrayed the many members who spent their time and money to help get them elected."
