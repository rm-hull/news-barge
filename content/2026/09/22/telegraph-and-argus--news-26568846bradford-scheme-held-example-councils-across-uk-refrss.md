Bradford's City Centre Transformation has been highlighted as a leading example of how investment in walking and cycling infrastructure can benefit communities.

National charity Cycling UK featured the project in a new report showcasing successful schemes from across the UK and encouraging councils to continue backing active travel projects.

The report includes Bradford alongside other major schemes, pointing to the district's city centre revamp as an example of how prioritising pedestrians, cyclists and public transport can deliver wide-ranging benefits.

Bradford resident Norah McWilliam, whose experience is featured in the report, praised the changes made to the city centre.

She said: "The city centre is so much nicer now. All the relandscaping is really attractive, and the wildflower planting is lovely.

"You can now walk or cycle right through the city centre without worrying about traffic, so that's a really big improvement."

## What did the scheme achieve?

Completed in 2025, the Bradford City Centre Transformation reshaped the heart of the city through a partnership between Bradford Council and the West Yorkshire Combined Authority, with funding largely provided through the Government's Transforming Cities Fund.

The project introduced around 2.4km of cycle lanes, 1.6km of pedestrianised streets and 12,000 square metres of new green space, while reducing the dominance of private vehicles in the city centre.

The improvements were delivered alongside Bradford's year as UK City of Culture 2025, helping residents and visitors move more easily between venues and attractions.

![]()Ongoing work on Thornton Road *(Image: Bradford Council)*

According to Cycling UK, the scheme has helped drive a 25 per cent increase in footfall in the city centre, supporting local businesses and cultural tourism.

The charity said the transformation has also delivered environmental benefits, including a 24 to 27 per cent increase in biodiversity through new planting, trees and sustainable drainage features.

The report further estimates the project has generated more than £9.8 million in social value through volunteering, donations and engagement with schools.

Duncan Dollimore, associate director at Cycling UK, said: "We decided to collect some of the strongest success stories into our new report, to make it really clear that people want this, and will thank councils for delivering safer streets for walking and cycling."

## Further regeneration projects ongoing

The recognition comes as work continues on another major transport scheme on Thornton Road in Bradford.

![]()Works have begun on Thornton Road *(Image: Mike Simmonds)*

Earlier this month, Bradford Council confirmed that works are 'progressing well,' with the first phase of improvements completed, and the next stage underway.

The 18-month project aims to improve links between west Bradford and the city centre and is expected to make walking, cycling and public transport journeys safer and more attractive.

It is expected to be completed in 2028.
