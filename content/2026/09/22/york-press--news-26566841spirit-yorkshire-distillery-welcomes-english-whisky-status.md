Yorkshire’s first single-malt whisky distillery has welcomed protected geographical indication (GI) status for English whisky.

Spirit of Yorkshire Distillery in Hunmanby, a founding member of the English Whisky Guild, says the status requires English whisky to use UK grain and English water and to be distilled and matured in England.

Jenni Ashwood, marketing director at Spirit of Yorkshire, said: "This GI has been painstakingly developed over a number of years, to protect the quality, integrity and focus on provenance and flavour that has defined English Whisky over the past two decades.

"It provides a robust framework from which the category can flourish and thrive long into the future.

"As farmers, as well as distillers, we're particularly pleased that the GI stipulates that to be labelled an ‘English whisky’ it must be made from UK cereal grain, thereby directly supporting British agriculture."

Managing director David Thompson said: "English whisky is the fastest-growing UK spirit, with a market worth £1 billion, and makes a valuable contribution to both the economy and tourism, so the protection afforded by GI status is very welcome."
