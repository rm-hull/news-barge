Staff at a Bishop Auckland cycle shop have warned bike thefts in the town are on the rise amid fears of increased crime and anti-social behaviour in the town.

Workers at Inspiral Cycles, on Newgate Street, said more and more people are buying locks for their bikes and it has been "really sad" to see so many reporting thefts.

The warning comes as fed-up Bishop Auckland residents say break-ins, vandalism, nuisance bikes and litter louts are plaguing their town.

Despite millions of pounds being poured into regenerating the area, residents have described feeling unsafe in a "deteriorated" town with many fearful of even reporting crime.

Lou Hamilton, an employee at the shop, said: “We’ve noticed a real increase in people buying locks and coming in with bikes having been stolen.”

“We’ve started recording frame numbers so we can help people as much as we can.”

“It is really sad to see because so many people spend a lot of money on a bike and then it gets stolen.”

![]() *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

The concerns come as more than 130 people have joined the newly formed Bishop Auckland Community Action Group (BACAG), which is calling for action over crime, antisocial behaviour across the town.

The group has raised concerns about theft and vandalism, as well as illegal e-bikes and motorbikes being ridden at speed around Newgate Street.

For Ms Hamilton, however, the issue is not only what happens to bikes once they are left unattended, but whether cyclists have somewhere secure to leave them.

She said: “I personally wouldn’t ride my bike down Newgate Street because there aren’t many places to safely lock your bike away.”

The shop is also concerned that legitimate e-bike users risk being caught up in the backlash over illegally modified machines.

Ms Hamilton said: “None of the ones we sell go above the legal limit. We won’t sell one or change one that is.

“But there is this idea that all electric bikes are awful.

"Someone who genuinely needs it to enjoy a bike ride feels so insecure and self-conscious because of the stigma.”

One of those calling for change is Emily Blackmore, 23, who has founded BACAG.

“Over the past decade, we have watched the decline of our high street. Bishop Auckland was once viewed as a historic landmark that attracted visitors from across the North East," she said.

"Today, many residents feel that our town centre has been allowed to deteriorate into a place where crime, vandalism and urban decay have become increasingly visible.

“We have raised serious concerns about antisocial behaviour and urban decay within the town centre itself. It is not unusual to see illegal e-bikes and motorbikes travelling at high speeds around Newgate Street, creating a danger to pedestrians.

"There have also been multiple incidents involving shops being damaged or broken into over recent months.”

BACAG is campaigning for improved CCTV and security measures, while police have encouraged residents to report incidents so officers can identify patterns and target resources.

Durham County Council has previously said neighbourhood wardens regularly patrol the town and agencies work together to tackle antisocial behaviour.

Durham Police said reports from the public are vital in identifying patterns and deciding where resources are needed and encouraged witnesses to come forward.

Neighbourhood Inspector Mark Edwards said officers regularly carry out high-visibility patrols and work with residents and businesses in the town centre.

He urged people not to assume somebody else had already reported an incident, adding: “Make the call and help us tackle the issues where you live.”

Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen has also addressed the concerns, saying recent incidents have had a “real impact on community confidence.”

She has commissioned a review of CCTV provision across the force area – one of BACAG’s biggest concerns – while stressing that reports from residents help police identify patterns and target resources.
