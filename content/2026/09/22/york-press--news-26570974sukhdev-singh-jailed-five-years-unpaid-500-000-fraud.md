A LYING former accountant has been jailed for an additional five years after failing to pay a confiscation order linked to a fraud conviction that saw him gain over a million pounds from a vulnerable victim.

Sukhdev Singh, 78, of Chelwood Drive, Leeds, was originally sentenced in 2022 to more than five years in prison for defrauding a vulnerable man in Harrogate.

The victim, who had significant disabilities and a learning age of 12, was systematically exploited by Singh, who fraudulently acquired his mortgage-free home, valued at £500,000.

Singh also transferred more than £34,000 from a Gibraltar savings account and attempted to take control of a property the victim owned in Spain.

As reported by The Press during Singh's sentencing in 2022, Judge Sean Morris, said: “I have never come across a worse liar than you. When faced with irrefutable evidence your default position is to lie, lie, lie, no matter how ridiculous the lie is. You should hang your head in shame.”

The jury heard how before the death of the victim’s father, a business associate of Singh, the accountant had promised he would look after the son.

Instead, he had taken as much of the son’s assets he could get his hands on, duping honest people into witnessing fake documents he had prepared and claiming the son wanted him to have the assets.

He took so much that by the time a Harrogate Borough Council worker realised what was going on and sounded the alert, the victim was living in squalor and renting his own house from Singh.

A Proceeds of Crime Act investigation by North Yorkshire Police found Singh had benefited from criminal conduct totalling £1.19 million and held assets worth £490,284.98.

On August 25, 2025, York Crown Court ordered Singh to pay the available amount within three months or face a further five years in prison.

Despite unsuccessful appeals and repeated extensions, Singh made no payments - saying that he planned to appeal against the order.

At Leeds Magistrates’ Court on August 27, 2026, he again refused to accept responsibility or pay the confiscation order.

The court activated the five-year default sentence, and Singh was returned to prison.

Ian Sharp, from North Yorkshire Police’s Economic Crime Unit, said: “We will relentlessly pursue individuals in relation to the proceeds of their crimes. In this case, enforcement action to recover the outstanding debt will continue.”

The victim died earlier this year before receiving compensation from the confiscation proceedings, however his estate is expected to receive the funds once they are recovered.
