The plan to house Berlin’s Central and Regional Library (ZLB) in the Galeria building at Alexanderplatz has taken another step forward. The State of Berlin and property owner Commerz Real have signed a letter of intent for the project, the Senate Department for Culture announced. However, further negotiations are still to come.

“Both parties intend to hold negotiations on key economic terms as early as October 2026,” the statement said. Under the current plans, around 42,680 square metres of space would be allocated to the ZLB, including around 28,133 square metres of space open to the public. Part of the building is to continue operating as a department store (around 14,220 square metres).

“Having the ZLB at Alexanderplatz offers the opportunity to bring Berliners together in a central, highly accessible location while also giving Alexanderplatz in the heart of Berlin a much-needed boost,” said Senator for Finance and Culture Stefan Evers (CDU).

### Central Location Sought for the Library

Berlin’s Central and Regional Library has been in need of a new home for years. The library, which is in urgent need of renovation, is currently split between two sites, at Blücherplatz and on Breite Straße.

At one point, a move into the former Lafayette building on Friedrichstraße was also discussed. Former Senator for Culture Joe Chialo (CDU) strongly advocated the idea, but it ultimately fell through.

A move into the Galeria department store at Alexanderplatz has also been discussed for some time. Commerz Real has supported the idea, and the ZLB also sees a potential move as a major opportunity. However, issues such as financing for the project still need to be resolved. The deal would then probably have to be finalised by the new state government, following Sunday’s election.

“The letter of intent gives us hope,” said ZLB director Jonas Fansa. A public library would revitalise Alexanderplatz. Around 2,000 workstations, spaces for families to spend time, creative spaces and learning areas for young people could be created there. “You could hardly hand a new government a better project,” Fansa said.
