Lawyers pursuing legal action against two companies involved in an illegal tyre storage site in Bradford that caught fire and burned for almost three weeks say they have tried and failed to make contact with them.

One director is believed to be in Spain.

Another died and his replacement has not responded to letters from the Environment Agency.

Husband and wife Stuart and Vicky Bedford were the directors of Equalityre Limited, which operated a tyre storage facility on land off Spring Mill Street on the edge of Bradford city centre.

When that site caught fire in 2020 it impacted more than 14,000 pupils in 25 schools, affected housing, businesses, medical facilities, and care homes, and disrupted road and rail travel in and out of Bradford.

The cost of cleaning up the former go-kart track, which was left covered in black sludge and partially burned tyres, was estimated to be more than £1.1 million.

![]()Fire fighters try to put out a tyre fire in Spring Mill Street off Manchester Road. *(Image: .)*

Equalityre also operated a second site at Wright Business Park, Doncaster.

Stuart Bedford, 64, formerly of Fairfax Avenue, Harrogate, described as “the controlling mind” behind the Bradford site, which had almost 570,000 waste tyres stacked on it, was jailed for 12 months in 2024.

His wife, Vicky Bedford, 53, of Fairgrey Close, Ripon, described as “a straw director” of Shipley-based Equalityre, was handed a 12-month community order.

Last December the couple were ordered to sell their property in Spain.

However, Equalityre was ordered to pay a nominal sum of just £1 despite making £1.8 million from the illegal operation.

Both Equalityre and a second company, Licenced Tyre Disposals, face being sentenced at Bradford Crown Court under proceedings brought by the Environment Agency.

Prosecuting barrister Joseph Millington, for the Environment Agency, told the court: “Equalityre remains to be sentenced.

“Licenced Tyre Disposals’s involvement in the underlying offending was somewhat different [as it] paid Equalityre to remove a large quantity of their waste tyres [which] ultimately ended up being illegally deposited at the waste sites.”

He said Companies House records showed that neither company was up to date with the filing of records and both were subject to active applications to strike them off the register.

Mr Millington explained: “There is no indication that these companies continue to trade. There’s been nothing heard from them.

“Speaking candidly, whilst it may feel that this may be a fruitless exercise - because if a fine is imposed these companies are very much likely to just simply be liquidated or dissolved because they no longer trade - these proceedings have to be concluded following the guilty pleas of both companies.”

He added: “Mr Bedford wasn’t interested to turn up at the confiscation hearing.

“It’s not anticipated that either of these companies is interested in seeing these proceedings through.

“These are companies that are only liable to a fine rather than natural persons [such as Stuart and Vicky Bedford] who are at risk of imprisonment.

“They were sentenced so long ago that Mr Bedford has been released from prison and is understood to reside in Spain.

“The Environment Agency are extremely keen for these matters to be resolved because costs are being incurred.

“I’ve explained the blunt reality of where we are: this is unlikely, I’m afraid, to levy a fine that is going to be recovered.”

His Honour Judge Ahmed Nadim adjourned sentencing to December 8.
