A family has paid tribute to a "ray of sunshine" who spent years helping others after being diagnosed with terminal cancer.

Kylie Lang, from Bailiff Bridge, near Brighouse, died on September 19 aged 44 following a seven-year battle with stage four cancer, having far exceeded the 18 months doctors initially gave her to live.

Her husband, Daniel Lang, described Kylie as a brave, selfless and positive woman who refused to let her illness define her, instead spending years raising thousands of pounds for Overgate Hospice.

Daniel said: "She was a ray of sunshine.

![]()Kylie Lang and her family. *(Image: Daniel Lang)*

"All the way from diagnosis, obviously it was horrendous, but she never felt sorry for herself.

"She always had a big smile on her face. Always had time for others, to help people."

## 'We spent every spare minute we had together'

The couple's story began a decade ago after meeting online.

"We met for a coffee and from there on in we were best friends, more or less instantly," Daniel said.

"We spent every spare minute we had together."

At the time, Daniel was already father to his son, Erwin, who was two years old when Kylie became part of the family.

The couple later welcomed daughter Elsie, who is now eight.

## Diagnosis 'wiped out' future

Daniel said their lives changed forever when Kylie received her diagnosis shortly after Elsie's first birthday.

"We were a young couple with a baby, with everything in front of us, and then your complete future's been wiped out," he said.

![]()Kylie Lang and her family. *(Image: Daniel Lang)*

The cancer had already spread to both of Kylie's lungs and doctors told her she had around 18 months to live.

Instead, she battled on for seven years.

"It's a testament to her fighting spirit," Daniel said.

"I think the majority of people probably would've given up, whereas she saw it as needing to be there for our daughter.

"Because she was a baby, she'd only just started walking at that time.

"Kylie pulled out the stops to be there for her."

## Helping others

That determination became a defining feature of the remainder of her life.

Drawing on her own experiences, Kylie worked closely with Overgate Hospice in Elland, where she found support following her diagnosis, and spent her final days.

As well as raising thousands of pounds for charity, Kylie used her own experiences to support other families facing difficult circumstances.

After Elsie, who is non-verbal autistic, started school, Kylie realised there was little support available to help children with special educational needs understand grief and bereavement.

She subsequently worked with the school to create a book aimed at addressing that gap.

She also became a familiar face at fundraising events, particularly Overgate's Colour Run in Halifax.

![]()Kylie Lang and her family. *(Image: Daniel Lang)*

Earlier this year, Kylie learned the cancer had spread further and that her prognosis had worsened.

Even so, she remained determined to take part in the Colour Run one final time in July, walking the three-and-a-half kilometre route despite her deteriorating health.

"She was a trooper," Daniel said.

## Fundraiser launched

Family friend Dan Ramsden recently launched a GoFundMe page to support Daniel and help cover funeral expenses.

Dan and Daniel have been friends since childhood and he said Kylie's impact extended far beyond those who knew her personally.

"She was just unbelievably courageous and strong," Dan said.

"She didn't put herself first in that sense. She was always still thinking about others. She was thinking about raising funds for charity."

![]()Kylie Lang and her family. *(Image: Daniel Lang)*

Dan added: "There's a lot of people reaching out to Daniel, saying how much Kylie helped them, even though she never met them."

The fundraiser has already attracted significant support, something Daniel said reflects the kindness Kylie showed throughout her life.

"It restores your faith in humanity a little bit," Daniel said.

"There's a lot of people that do want to help.

"I suppose it's paying back the kindness that Kylie showed to other people."

## Overgate Hospice were 'unbelievable'

Daniel also paid tribute to the staff at Overgate Hospice, who cared for Kylie and supported her family during her final weeks.

"It was unbelievable," he said.

"Her parents were more or less living there for the last two weeks. They were feeding them, looking after them, offering them emotional support, offering me emotional support.

![]()Kylie Lang and her family. *(Image: Daniel Lang)*

"Nothing was too much for them."

Asked how he hopes Kylie will be remembered, Daniel said: "Just remember how happy she was.

"She was a happy-go-lucky, really friendly person that would do anything for you.

"I don't want to remember her for the disease and being in the hospital.

"I want to remember her for being my best mate."
