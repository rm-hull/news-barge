A 19-year-old mechanic from Fairweather Green has turned a lifelong passion for cars into a growing mobile vehicle repair business.

Kamron Ali, who runs Kammy Diagnostics, recently completed a three-year mechanics course and became fully qualified in July.

Since launching his business around a year ago, he says demand has steadily increased as he expands the range of services he offers.

"I've only just started my business, I'd say it's been about a year now and it's grown quite a bit," he said.

![]()Kamron Ali at work *(Image: T&A)*

Operating as a mobile mechanic, Kamron travels to customers' homes to carry out repairs and maintenance, offering everything from diagnostics and servicing to brake work, welding and pre-MOT repairs.

## 'Whatever it is, it's always cars, me'

Kamron's journey began with vehicle diagnostics and coding work before branching out into more complex mechanical jobs as his qualifications progressed.

He said: "Now I do servicing, oil, brake pads, brake discs, engine work, pre-MOT work, welding.

"Now that I'm fully qualified, I've got the qualifications and I thought, well, I'll use the qualifications and I'll start a business."

His interest in cars stretches back as far as he can remember, having grown up in a family of car enthusiasts.

![]()Kamron Ali at work *(Image: T&A)*

The teenager said his passion for mechanics was sparked when he was around 13 years old and helped his father repair a Mercedes.

"Whatever it is, it's always cars, me," he said.

"Even when I do my customer's car, I don't think like it's a customer's car.

"I'll make sure it's done to standard and make sure it's like I do my own car."

Social media has played a key role in helping Kamron attract customers, alongside recommendations from previous clients.

"At the moment I've got success on the TikTok page, and Google," he said. "I get a lot of word of mouth as well."

## Helping the community while building a business

One of his proudest moments came earlier this month when he completed his first full vehicle service after qualifying.

"About a week ago, that's when I started doing servicing," he said.

"When I did that first car I was proud. Look where I've came from, look where I started.

"I was just proud of myself."

Despite his young age, Kamron says he is comfortable taking on a wide variety of mechanical work and prides himself on treating customers fairly.

![]()Kamron Ali at work *(Image: T&A)*

"I'm not all about the money. I always like helping people," he said.

"Sometimes people come to me, they want diagnostics, sometimes I don't charge them when it's obviously hard for them."

Looking ahead, Kamron hopes his business can continue to expand, with ambitions of opening his own garage and eventually operating from multiple cities.

For now, however, the young entrepreneur is focused on building his reputation one customer at a time.

He said: "I treat everyone like family and I'll treat their car like mine."
