The loss of more than 150 jobs at a County Durham factory has been met with "devastation".

Today (Tuesday, September 22), administrators confirmed hundreds of jobs have been lost across three Clayton Glass sites in Stanley, Lancashire and Scotland.

James Lumb, managing director at Interpath and joint administrator of Clayton Glass said the economics of the industry are "incredibly challenging" and directors fought hard to find a solvent solution - they said regrettably not all of the business could be rescued in this market.

It comes after workers at its Stanley site were told on payday that they would not receive their wages and the company had filed a notice of intention to appoint an administrator.

The firm, was slammed as "heartless" as staff only learnt of the news after turning up for work.

Annfield Plain and Tanfield councillor Darren Grimes previously said the redundancy announcement was a devastating blow for the area.

In a new statement, he said: "Clayton Glass. Founded 1956. Survived everything for 70 years. Couldn't survive Britain's energy bills.

"The administrators said it plainly: high energy costs helped finish it off. 290 redundancies, and County Durham is one of the sites closing.

"Ed Miliband gets his green halo. Families in my ward get the dole queue.

"I am genuinely disgusted by those who purport to be the 'party of the working class'."

Luke Akehurst, MP for North Durham, has also spoken out.

He said: "I'm devastated for the employees of Clayton Glass who have lost their jobs.

"If anyone affected needs my help I would urge them to contact me on [luke.akehurst@parliament.uk.](mailto:luke.akehurst@parliament.uk.)"

![]()The Clayton Glass factory *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

A County Durham Liberal Democrat spokesperson said: “For many years, Durham County Council's employability services, including Durham Works and Employability Durham, have helped residents affected by unemployment and redundancy to get back on their feet by providing practical support with skills, training, confidence-building, job searching, applications and connections to local employers.

“No worker should discover on payday that their wages are not coming. That is frightening for any family and the council is right to help the Clayton Glass workforce.

“But this cannot be support by postcode. Businesses in Bishop Auckland, Durham and elsewhere have faced administration since the current administration took control in May 2025. Their workers faced the same fear and uncertainty.

“The administration must now answer a straightforward question: will Durham County Council provide the same rapid, practical support to every County Durham business and workforce at risk of administration, regardless of ward or political representation?

“We want the council to publish a clear countywide offer, including a named contact for affected employers and workers, urgent benefits and debt advice, employment and skills support, and early coordination with administrators and trade unions. When jobs are at risk, people need certainty and action.”

The Liberal Democrat Group is calling on the council to confirm whether its response to Clayton Glass represents a new countywide policy, what criteria will trigger support, and how businesses and workers can access help before missed wages or redundancies leave families in crisis.
