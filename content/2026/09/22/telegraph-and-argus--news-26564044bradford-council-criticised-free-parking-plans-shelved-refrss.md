Councillors have criticised Bradford Council for failing to deliver on a promised hour of free parking.

The pledge, made earlier this year by the Reform-led administration, would have included an hour of free parking at council-owned car parks, free evening parking from 6pm, and free parking at leisure centres and some attractions during school holidays.

But earlier this month, councillors revealed the plans were not viable due to financial pressures.

![]()Councillor Alun Griffiths *(Image: newsquest)*

### 'We don't accept excuses'

Councillor Alun Griffiths, (Liberal Democrats, Idle and Thackley), said: "We don't accept the excuses from the Reform leadership.

"They made repeated promises to address car parking, including after they had taken control and were fully aware of the council's financial situation.

"If they are unable to fulfil their commitments, it raises the question of who is actually in control of the council.

"It doesn't appear to be them.

"We are writing again to the leader and portfolio holder asking them to confirm that at the very least some car parking charges will be removed as part of the next budget."

Conservative Councillors for Ilkley and Addingham also expressed frustration, particularly in light of changes to on-street parking charges introduced earlier this year.

### 'Deeply disappointing'

Councillors Jane Sellers, Richard Downey and Andrew Loy said: "We have been fighting on this critical issue since the previous Labour administration’s damaging changes to Ilkley’s on-street parking charges were implemented earlier this year

![]()Councillors Richard Downey, Jane Sellers, and Andrew Loy at the proposed housing development site in Addingham *(Image: UGC)*

"After meeting with the Reform leadership, we were optimistic that common sense would prevail and that the changes would be reversed in their entirety during the summer months, helping local businesses well in advance of the Christmas trading period.

"The announcement of changes to council-owned car parks, whilst not directly addressing the problem of on-street charges in Ilkley, was a welcome step in the right direction and would have provided a lifeline for local businesses.

"It is therefore deeply disappointing that their proposal hasn’t come to fruition as promised.

![]()Councillor Ian Walker Reform UK for the Wyke ward *(Image: T&A)*

"It is our view that the changes could be reversed in their entirety if there is the right political will, and we’ll continue fighting for the people of Ilkley until that happens."

Bradford Council leader Stephen Place had said in July that the proposals would be considered at a meeting of the executive in September.

Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council's Cabinet Member for Neighbourhoods and Communities, said the council was currently unable to implement the improved parking measures and that the proposals were not ready to be presented for approval at the September Executive meeting.
