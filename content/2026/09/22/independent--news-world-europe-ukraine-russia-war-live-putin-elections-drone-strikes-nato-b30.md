Donald Trump has warned that Russia has “unfortunately lost control” of its oil industry after again urging Ukraine to halt long-range strike on refineries.

“A large number of their diesel refineries have been blown up and are, at least temporarily, out of commission,” the US President wrote on Truth Social, following a phone call with Volodymyr Zelensky.

“This ridiculous and never ending War with Ukraine must be ended. The whole World suffers as 25,000 people, mostly soldiers, are being killed each month. What a shame! President DJT.”

A senior White House official familiar with the conversation between the two leaders said Trump was worried that the Ukrainian attacks are exacerbating a global diesel shortage and pushing up prices, the FT reported.

The two presidents will meet later this week on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York.

Responding to Trump’s post on Truth Social, Zelensky said that stopping attacks on Ukraine’s energy sector, critical infrastructure and food exports could lead to “matching de-escalation steps on our part.”

## Zelensky arrives in New York for UN General Assembly meeting

Volodymyr Zelensky arrived in New York for the annual high-level gathering at the UN General Assembly as wars in Ukraine and the Middle East are expected to dominate the talks.

Zelensky said around 20 meetings are planned, including with Donald Trump, European leaders, US senators and members of the House of Representatives, heads of international organisations, leading American companies, and think tanks.

“A new Drone Deal is also ready to be signed. Of course, Ukraine’s number one priority is bringing peace closer and protecting the lives of our people,” he said.

## Pro-peace Russian candidate says she’s home after going missing

A pro-peace candidate in Russia’s State Duma has finally spoken out after disappearing for three days.

Yulia Kuznetsova and her husband went missing on the eve of the parliamentary elections and stopped responding to messages and calls.

In a video released on Telegram the following day, she said she had needed psychological support and medical help after a “difficult conversation,” but shared not further details.

“I am back home, and my husband and I are fine. I was also able to vote at my polling station. Thank you all for your support,” read a message posted to Kuznetsova’s channel, along with a photo of her at a polling station.

Her team have still not explained why the couple went missing.

“They are free, and that’s what matters,” her support team said.

## Fighting conditions becoming ‘more atrocious’, say Ukrainian war veterans

James Fensom, who fought on several battle fronts in Ukraine, says war conditions are getting “more atrocious” as the skies are nearly saturated with armed drones stalking soldiers round the clock.

The war veteran, who fought in the brutal battle for the city of Bakhmut in 2023, locally called “the meat grinder”, told *The Telegraph* that he prefers that over the ongoing assault.

“The war fighting conditions are becoming ever more atrocious,” says Fensom.

“Those of us who’ve been here a while sometimes reminisce about Bakhmut, and think: ‘God, if only we could go back to those days’.”

He blames it on the ever-increasing range of new-age drones that have forced troops to spend nearly all their time in foxholes and bunkers.

“The Russians just kept hitting it non-stop, until it almost collapsed,” he says, recalling being in one blindage that was attacked by 42 First Person View drones in 24 hours.

“There were only three of us there, and we weren’t even on the zero line – that shows you the amount of firepower the Russians can expend on us.”
