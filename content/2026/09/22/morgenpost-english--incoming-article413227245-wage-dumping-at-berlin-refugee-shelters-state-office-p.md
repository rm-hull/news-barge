Shortly before the election, trade unions and social service providers are raising concerns about wage dumping at refugee accommodation facilities in Berlin. Contracts to operate the facilities always go to the cheapest bidder, according to a press release issued on Wednesday.

Those involved have been complaining about this problem for some time – but until now, mostly behind closed doors. Shortly before the election, the Verdi trade union and the German Trade Union Confederation (DGB), together with two social service providers, AWO Mitte and Berliner Stadtmission, have now decided to speak out publicly.

### “The Lowest Price Alone Determines the Success of Integration Measures”

“It is a scandal that the Senate is encouraging wage dumping in an area as socially sensitive as refugee accommodation,” says Claus Foerster, works council representative at AWO Mitte. According to the social service provider, it has bid in 24 tenders issued by the State Office for Refugee Affairs and Accommodation (LFU) over the past three years, but has never been awarded a contract. “Because of our collective agreement and permanent employment contracts, we have no chance against providers that undercut us on price by using low-paid workers on temporary contracts,” adds Timm Meike, managing director of AWO Mitte.

The fact that adherence to collective agreements plays no role in tenders for refugee accommodation is “unacceptable”, says Katja Karger, chair of the DGB in Berlin-Brandenburg. “Berlin wants to be a city of good working conditions – that must also apply to accommodation facilities.” A new Senate coalition would have to change course, according to Karger.

Mia Michels of Berliner Stadtmission makes clear that awarding the contract to the cheapest bidder is also a problem for refugees. “It is short-sighted and risky if the lowest price alone determines who is awarded the contract and, with it, the success of integration measures,” Michels says. “This fundamentally raises the question of whether public funding can still fulfil its actual purpose – namely, ensuring a service of appropriate quality that is also fit for the future.” According to Berliner Stadtmission, it has bid in 16 tenders over the past two years. It, too, was not awarded a single contract.

### Refugees: State Office Defends Itself Against Criticism from Service Providers and Trade Unions

“It is true that the most economically advantageous offer is selected, which means price then takes priority,” the State Office for Refugee Affairs said in response to an inquiry. However, quality was ensured through the stringent requirements set out in the tender. With responsibility for 130 accommodation facilities, the authority also had sufficient experience to assess whether bids offering low costs could actually meet their commitments. Facilities were regularly inspected without prior notice to ensure compliance with contractual standards. Unsuccessful bidders always had the option of lodging an objection and requesting a review.

The LFU argues that there is currently no legal basis for the trade unions’ demand to make adherence to collective agreements mandatory in the procurement process. There is no collective agreement in Berlin covering the operation of accommodation facilities for refugees and other homeless people. Individual organisations do have company-level collective agreements. A generally applicable regulation would require an industry-wide agreement. This would have to be negotiated between trade unions and employers.
