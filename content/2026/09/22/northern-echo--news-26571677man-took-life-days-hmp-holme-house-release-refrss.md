A 36-year-old man died after he was found unresponsive in his friend's garden just days after his unexpected prison release, a watchdog has found.

Aaron Rowley was found unresponsive on February 22, this year, he died three days later in hospital.

The Prisons and Probation Ombudsman (PPO) report said he suffered brain damage following a cardiac arrest, an inquest ruled his death by method of suicide with substance misuse as a contributing factor.

![]()HMP Holme House in Stockton *(Image: pr)*

Rowley was first jailed in October 2025 after admitting possession of a bladed article, assaulting an emergency worker and drugs offences.

He was released on licence but arrested again in December on suspicion of grievous bodily harm and cocaine possession.

The assault case was dropped but he was charged with possessing a class A drug and recalled to prison for breaching licence conditions.

He was not arrested until February 12 and was sent straight to HMP Holme House. He was released unexpectedly on February 16 after completing his sentence, accounting for time already served on remand.

![]()HMP Holme House in Stockton *(Image: pr)*

The report found no evidence Rowley had expressed suicidal thoughts or self-harm intentions while at Holme House or after his release.

He told a prison nurse he had a history of anxiety and depression but denied any suicidal thoughts. He also refused a referral to the prison's mental health team.

A substance misuse worker met Rowley on February 14 and noted he wanted to cut his drug use. The worker warned him that drug tolerance falls in custody and that using illicit substances could be dangerous.

The report states Rowley "did not express any thoughts, intentions, or plans relating to suicide or self-harm, and there was no indication of risk."

The report states Rowley "was released unexpectedly" at about 8.50pm on February 16, giving prison and probation staff little time to prepare.

The PPO said staff did what could reasonably be expected to identify and manage Rowley's needs.

![]()HMP Holme House in Stockton *(Image: Supplied)*

Before leaving, a nurse gave him a release pack warning about overdoses, reduced drug tolerance and mixing substances. Rowley declined a community support appointment but was told he could seek help at any time.

Rowley stayed at a friend's home after release. On February 17 he told his community offender manager he could not attend a probation appointment because he had no money for transport. The appointment was rearranged for February 18 but he did not attend.

His offender manager called him and said his speech sounded "slurred and incoherent". Rowley denied being under the influence.

The officer and a manager agreed to issue a warning, arrange another appointment and make a welfare call in the meantime. He was also due to have a drug test at his next appointment.

The PPO said staff did what could reasonably be expected to identify and manage Rowley's needs. The report states: "We found no issues with Mr Rowley's pre-release planning or post-release supervision." No recommendations were made after the investigation.

![]()HMP Holme House in Stockton *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Rowley was one of nine men whose deaths within 14 days of release from Holme House were investigated by the watchdog between February 2023 and February 2026. Two deaths, including Rowley's, were self-inflicted, five were drug-related and one was accidental.

The PPO said deaths after release highlight prison leavers' "acute vulnerability", particularly in their first few days outside custody. It said prison leavers often face several risks, including mental health problems, substance use and homelessness, which can raise their risk of death.

The Northern Echo contacted Spectrum Community Health CIC, the prison's main healthcare provider, but it did not respond before publication.
