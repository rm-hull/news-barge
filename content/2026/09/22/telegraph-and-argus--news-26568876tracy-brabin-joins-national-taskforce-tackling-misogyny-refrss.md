West Yorkshire mayor Tracy Brabin has been appointed to a new national taskforce aimed at tackling misogyny across society.

Mayor Brabin will sit on the Government-led group, which brings together ministers, experts, and community leaders to address harmful attitudes and help shape policy.

### Mayor to help tackle misogyny across society

Chaired by the Minister for Women and Equalities, the taskforce will explore how the Government can take further action to address misogyny in workplaces, communities, and online spaces.

![]()Tracy Brabin *(Image: Newsquest)*

Bridget Phillipson, MP and Minister for Women and Equalities, said: "Before I entered politics, I ran a refuge for women fleeing domestic abuse.

"I’ve seen first-hand the devastating impact on women and girls when harmful attitudes are tolerated and left unchallenged.

"No woman should have to make her world smaller or curb her ambitions out of fear – and making that a reality is everyone’s business.

"As a whole society, we can and must be better.

"With political leadership and the best experts from across the country, we will shift the dial on the poisonous attitudes that blight women's lives, turn evidence into action, and send misogynistic attitudes back to the dark ages where they belong."

### Group to tackle misogyny in workplaces and online

The taskforce will include representatives from women’s organisations, academia, and local leaders.

![]()Tracy Brabin, Mayor of West Yorkshire *(Image: West Yorkshire Combined Authority)*

Its work will complement existing cross-government efforts to halve violence against women and girls.

While the first meeting will focus on young people, future sessions will address issues in workplaces, digital spaces, and social cohesion.

Felicity Oswald, chief executive of Girlguiding, said: "Girlguiding is honoured to join the Minister for Women and Equalities’ new taskforce to tackle misogyny.

"Girls tell us they are facing rising levels of sexism and misogyny online, in public and in schools.

"That’s why we are calling for more education around healthy relationships, gender stereotypes, early warning signs and consent.

"We welcome the opportunity to bring girls’ experiences and perspectives directly into discussions with government.

![]()Tracy Brabin *(Image: Submitted)*

"Girls are experts in their own lives, and it is vital that their voices are heard when decisions are made that impact them."

Nearly one million adults experience sexual assault each year, 1.4 million are subjected to stalking, and nearly four million endure domestic abuse.

### 'Sixteen million pound package to address harmful attitudes'

The Government has recently introduced a £16m package to address harmful attitudes early.

This funding will support education on healthy relationships and train staff to address misogyny in schools.

An additional £10 million will fund intervention programmes for children displaying harmful behaviours.

The government also published statutory relationships, sex and health education guidance for schools, challenging myths about women and relationships that are spread online in the ‘manosphere’.
