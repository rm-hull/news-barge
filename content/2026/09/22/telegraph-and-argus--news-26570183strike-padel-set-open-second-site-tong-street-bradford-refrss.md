A Bradford-born padel company is expanding its presence in the city with plans for a second venue at Weaverthorpe Retail Park.

Strike Padel has unveiled plans for a new venue on Tong Street, building on the recent expansion of its Beckside Business Park facility from five courts to 12.

The company, launched by local healthcare professionals, says the new venue will allow it to bring the fast-growing sport to more communities across the district.

Managing director Fahad Qureshi said: "We are extremely proud to be a Bradford-born brand, founded by local people who are passionate about investing in our city."

"Receiving planning permission for our second Strike Padel site at Weaverthorpe Retail Park is another exciting milestone for us.

![]()Computer-generated image showing the proposed site *(Image: Strike Padel)*

"The support we have received from the people of Bradford since opening our first venue has been incredible, and this expansion is a direct result of that support."

## Plans for new site to operate 24/7

The announcement represents another milestone for the Bradford business, which has rapidly grown its presence in the city as interest in padel continues to rise nationwide.

Strike Padel hopes the new Tong Street venue will operate 24 hours a day, seven days a week, giving players greater flexibility while helping introduce more people to the sport.

Mr Qureshi said the company's focus had always been on making padel accessible to local communities.

"We have provided more than 160 free padel sessions and allowed local schools to use our courts completely free of charge," he said.

"We are proud to have led the way in making padel accessible and inclusive in Bradford."

According to the company, thousands of people have been introduced to padel through its activities over the past year.

![]()Strike Padel coach Cezar Gniadek at the expanded Beckside Business Park site. *(Image: T&A)*

Alongside free taster sessions, its community programme includes women-only events, junior coaching, over-50s social sessions and opportunities for schools, charities and community groups to use its facilities.

## 'Bradford is our home'

The additional venue is expected to expand those initiatives and increase capacity as demand for court time continues to grow.

Mr Qureshi said: "Our ambition is for the Weaverthorpe facility to operate 24 hours a day, giving members greater flexibility while creating more opportunities for schools, young people, families and community organisations to get involved."

While details of the facility, including its opening date and membership packages, have yet to be confirmed, the company says the development forms part of wider plans for growth while remaining rooted in Bradford.

"Bradford is our home," Mr Qureshi added.

"It is where Strike Padel was created, where our journey began and where we will continue to invest, create employment and provide outstanding sporting opportunities for local people."
