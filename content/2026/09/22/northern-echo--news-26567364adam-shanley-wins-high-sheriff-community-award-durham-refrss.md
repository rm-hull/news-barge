A council official hailed as a true “Mr Durham City”, has won a prestigious award for his outstanding service to the community.

Adam Shanley, clerk to the City of Durham Parish Council, received the High Sheriff Community Award at a surprise ceremony attended by current and former mayors of Durham, officials, councillors, business leaders and Durham University’s Vice-Chancellor.

Dr Arnab Basu MBE DL, High Sheriff of the County Palatine of Durham, presented the award for Mr Shanley’s long-standing public service.

Dr Basu said: "Again and again, when something important to the community has needed protecting, organising or simply making happen, Adam has been there.

![]()Adam Shanley speaking to the then Home Secretary Yvette Cooper about policing *(Image: Supplied)*

"Adam has made an extraordinary contribution to Durham, often working quietly behind the scenes to bring people and organisations together and make things happen for the city.

"His commitment to public service and the breadth of what he has helped achieve have made a lasting difference, and I was delighted to recognise that contribution with the High Sheriff’s Community Award."

Under Mr Shanley’s leadership, the parish council gained national Quality Award status and was named one of the UK’s top 100 councils.

He also won the 2025 County Durham Together Public Service Award and finished among the top five nationally in NALC’s Clerk of the Year awards.

Mr Shanley said: "I am really quite overwhelmed by this.

"I roll up my sleeves and get on with the job that’s in front of me and if I can help anyone with their local issues or let them know that they are heard, then that’s what getting up in the morning is all about for me."

Mr Shanley also paid tribute to his fellow parish councillors, describing their voluntary work as the greatest public service imaginable.

Mr Shanley said: "It is astonishing to see what the parish council has achieved since we started eight years ago.

"The parish council is making a huge difference in our community, and we want to do even more."

He said he could not do his job without the support of his 15 fellow parish councillors, past and present.

Mr Shanley’s hands-on approach has delivered "tangible improvements" across the city.

He helped establish the Durham City Safety Hub and led Operation Lentil, a joint initiative with Durham Constabulary to fund extra police officers.

He also successfully campaigned to save an NHS dental practice from being converted into student accommodation.

Mr Shanley led the parish council’s campaign with government ministers to prevent Durham Railway Station’s ticket office from closing.

He also secured funding to restore the City Theatre, one of Durham’s few remaining independent theatre venues.

Mr Shanley launched the Durham Heritage Blue Plaque scheme, with a special focus on highlighting women’s history.

He also helped save the Durham City Remembrance Parade by persuading the parish council to take legal and organisational responsibility, growing it into one of the region’s largest events of its kind.

His work extends beyond his official duties.

In December 2020, Mr Shanley made headlines after helping stop a mugging during a walkabout with residents.

Former Durham City police inspector Dave Clarke called him a "trailblazer".

Mr Clarke said: "I am absolutely thrilled that Adam has received this award, which is so thoroughly deserved.

"Adam’s list of successes is remarkable.

"He has a genuine flair for the audacious when it comes to tackling huge challenges and getting things done for local people.

"Quite simply, Adam Shanley is a true 'Mr Durham City'—the ultimate backbone of public service who brings everyone together for the good of our city."

Mr Shanley has also chaired the Golden Orb Working Group, organised major national events, launched the Riverscape litter-picking group and secured funding for community centres.
