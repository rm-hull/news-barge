A Roman altar dedicated by a soldier grateful to have returned home safely will go on display in Bishop Auckland this autumn, offering a rare glimpse of life nearly 1,750 years ago.

Found during 2013 excavations at Binchester Roman Fort, the altar will go on display at the Faith Museum, part of The Auckland Project.

Eltaominus, a retired military architect who served with the Ala Vettonum cavalry at Binchester, dedicated it to Fortuna Redux, the goddess of safe homecoming.

![]()Eltaominus Altar being excavated *(Image: Durham University)*

Dr John Castling, archaeology curator at The Auckland Project, said: "What makes this altar so fascinating is that it gives us a rare insight into the life and beliefs of an individual who lived at Binchester around 1,750 years ago.

"We know his name, Eltaominus; we know something about his military career, and through this altar we can begin to understand what mattered to him personally.

"Dedicating an altar to Fortuna Redux was a way of giving thanks for a safe homecoming, so behind the archaeology is a very relatable human story.

![]()Eltaominus Altar being excavated *(Image: Durham University)*

"That is what makes objects like this so powerful.

"Archaeology may sometimes feel like it is about buildings, dates and distant events.

"But it is ultimately about people who were not so dissimilar to us.

"Discoveries like this allow us to get much closer to an individual and recognise that although there are many differences, there’s also much common ground between their lives and our own, even across 18 centuries."

![]()Eltaominus Altar *(Image: Supplied)*

Dating to around AD 275, the altar stood in the fort’s bathhouse changing room, where other bathers would have seen it.

A hollow in the top suggests people poured offerings such as wine, spices or grain into it. One side bears a carving of a bull.

The altar was later discarded and rediscovered during recent excavations led by Durham University’s Department of Archaeology.

![]()Eltaominus Altar *(Image: Supplied)*

It will go on display from Wednesday, September 23, as The Auckland Project takes over day-to-day management of Binchester Roman Fort from Durham County Council.

Binchester, known to the Romans as Vinovia, was founded around AD 80 and grew into one of northern Britain’s largest military sites.

Its well-preserved remains include the commanding officer’s house and two bathhouses with intact underfloor heating.

The site stood on Dere Street, a key Roman road, and supported a busy civilian settlement until the end of the Roman era.

The Auckland Project has improved Binchester with new education and family facilities, a bigger food and drink offer and an upgraded visitor centre.

The site will also host the charity’s Learning and Skills programme for students of all ages across the North East.

Plans are under way for more archaeological excavations from 2028 to uncover more about the site’s history.
