A teenager arrested after the A66 crash that killed two police officers has admitted weapons offences linked to a shooting.

Kai Beattie, 19, pleaded guilty at Teesside Crown Court to possessing a firearm with intent to endanger life and possessing a machete.

Beattie’s barrister, Gary Wood, said the teenager admitted being in Grangetown when a gun was fired on July 22 but denied being the shooter.

He said: "The defendant says he was present when the firearm was discharged and he was part of a joint group but was not the person who discharged the firearm."

Prosecutor Richard Herrmann said the Crown Prosecution Service would consider Beattie’s basis of plea, but currently says he fired the gun.

Judge Francis Laird KC, the Recorder of Middlesbrough, ordered a pre-sentence report for the teenager.

The defendant was told that a trial of issue may need to be held to determine the issue of who fired the weapon as it could affect his eventual sentence, the court heard.

Beattie, of Foxrush Close, Redcar, was remanded in custody and will be sentenced on November 20.

Beattie was arrested during police operations after the August 22 crash on the A66 near Middlesbrough, in which seven people died.

PC Tom Clough and PC Matthew Blades died when a VW Passat travelling the wrong way along the dual carriageway hit their response car head-on.

Michael Robert Cahill, 23, Theo Rae, 17, Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Jacob Matusiak, 23, and Makai Saddington, 18, also died in the collision.

By August 29, 24 people had been arrested and three charged in an organised crime crackdown following the A66 crash.
