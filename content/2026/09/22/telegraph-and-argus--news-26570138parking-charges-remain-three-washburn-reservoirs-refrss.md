PARKING charges are here to stay at three popular reservoirs in the Washburn Valley.

Yorkshire Water has confirmed that fees at Swinsty, Fewston, and Thruscross reservoirs will remain in place following an extended trial.

The scheme, introduced in 2024, raises funds to support nature conservation, countryside management, and the company’s first dedicated ranger team, says Yorkshire Water.

![]()Parking charges will remain at Washburn Valley reservoirs *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

The charges, £1 for an hour, £3 for six hours and £5 for all day, have led to some people choosing to park on the roads close to the reservoir and at Swinsty and Fewston at the start of charging, ticket machines were vandalised, prompting police patrols in the area.

Lee Otter, head of land and property at Yorkshire Water, said: "Our reservoirs are important outdoor spaces for local communities and visitors, and it’s vital that we care for them and make them as enjoyable and safe as possible for people and wildlife alike.

"By introducing a small parking fee, we, and especially our rangers, have been able to carry out a range of projects to that effect."

Improvements funded by the charges during the trial have included upgrades to the circular route around Fewston Reservoir, with a focus on disabled access.

More frequent inspections at the sites have also helped to identify and resolve maintenance issues more quickly.

Yorkshire Water’s 10-strong ranger team, funded by the charges, works with volunteers and partner organisations to manage habitats, control invasive species, plant wildflowers, repair paths, and conduct wildlife surveys.

The rangers also run educational events for schools and families, including wellbeing walks and nature-based craft sessions.

David Spencer, lead ranger at Yorkshire Water, said: "Developing and growing our team over the past three years has allowed us to step up our approach to maintaining the land around our reservoirs, and importantly, to encouraging local communities to enjoy and engage with the natural world around them."

Funds raised through future parking charges will continue to support the ranger team’s work and other conservation and maintenance efforts at the reservoirs.

![]()Thruscross Reservoir, September, 2026 *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

Mr Otter said: "We hope that visitors to our reservoirs have noticed some of the positive changes that we’ve been able to make since the charges were introduced.

"We’ll continue to work hard to improve access, habitats, and overall community enjoyment of the facilities."

![]()Money from the charging goes towards the work of rangers, *(Image: Lesley Tate)*

The parking operation will continue to be managed by Bransby Wilson, with tariffs remaining the same, and annual permits remaining available.

Parking charges are £1 for an hour; £2 for two hours, £3 for six hours and £5 for all day. An annual permit costs £30 and Blue Badge holders park for free.
