New photographs have revealed the devastating condition of a historic County Durham church that has become a growing source of concern for residents

The images were taken inside St Peter's Church in Bishop Auckland during a multi-agency walkaround involving the town's MP Sam Rushworth, Durham Police and Durham County Council officers, and show the scale of damage and neglect inside the Grade II-listed building.

Rows of wooden pews remain standing in parts of the church, on Princes Street, but beside them lie piles of charred debris, burnt-out metal frames and blackened remains of items destroyed in a blaze earlier this month.

Speaking during the visit, Mr Rushworth said it was "incredibly upsetting" to see the state of the church.

He said: "For the people who live around here, there's fear of it being set on fire. It's the anti-social behaviour that it attracts.

"But also, this is one of Bishop's really historic buildings. Elgar played the organ here. It's got connections to Stan Laurel. It's a listed building. It's part of our history.

"We really need to do more to bring buildings like this back into use."

It comes after a more than 130-strong group called for more to be done to tackle anti-social behaviour in the town, saying: "Our town centre has been allowed to deteriorate into a place where crime, vandalism and urban decay have become increasingly visible."

**A Northern Echo special report** highlighted that residents have become fearful of what could happen if they report incidents.

Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen has also addressed the concerns, saying recent incidents, including extensive damage to a shop after a car crashed into the The Brow Lounge on Newgate Street, have had a “real impact on community confidence.”

Labour MP Mr Rushworth said vacant buildings could become a 'magnet' for crime and anti-social behaviour and called for efforts to restore the church.

Looking around the interior, he described seeing evidence of fires being lit and damage to pews and furnishings.

Other photographs show smoke-stained walls, shattered glass scattered across the floor and damage around the altar area, while sections of flooring have been exposed and debris is strewn throughout the church.

Despite the destruction, some of the building's historic features remain visible, including stained-glass windows above the altar which survived the fire.

Mr Rushworth said: "It's going to take a big clean-up.

"I think in the first instance, actually, it just needs someone to do a proper clear out, get a couple of skips, clean it out.

"Then we need to look at, could lottery heritage money, could Historic England, is there somebody that wants to work together with local people to save a building like this?"

St Peter's, which closed in 2013, suffered damage in a blaze on Sunday, September 13.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service mounted a large response, with firefighters spending several hours at the scene as flames spread through parts of the building and smoke billowed across the town.

The condition of the site has been a longstanding concern for local residents.

At a PACT meeting held days after the fire, residents asked who should be contacted about ongoing problems at the church, saying they were unsure whether reports should be made to Durham Police or Durham County Council.

A police representative told the meeting: "The main door has been screwed down and windows boarded up.

"We are aware of the issue with the church and the owner has been contacted multiple times."

Owen Cleugh, Durham County Council’s safer places manager, said: “We appreciate the concerns of residents regarding St Peter’s Church and anti-social behaviour.

"We will be serving a legal notice on the owner requiring them to ensure the building is secure in order to prevent unauthorised access.

"If this work is not undertaken within a reasonable amount of time, we will look to secure the building ourselves and recover the costs from the owner.

“We will always look to work with owners to make sure buildings are safe and do not become a cause for wider community concern.”
