North East businesses are being urged to bid for a share of £1.4 billion in contracts to supply Rolls-Royce SMR’s new generation of small nuclear reactors.

The opportunities are part of a drive to increase UK involvement in clean-energy projects, with one key project targeting more than 70 per cent domestic content.

Rolls-Royce SMR’s new Supplier Programme aims to strengthen the UK nuclear supply chain and boost exports.

Chris Cholerton, chief executive of Rolls-Royce SMR, said: "The scale of the opportunity available to UK suppliers from the Rolls-Royce SMR programme is very significant.

"Signing the contract with Great British Energy-Nuclear in April 2026 has unlocked a forecast £1.4bn opportunity for UK business in the first year alone.

"As the only SMR provider with multiple contractual commitments in Europe, we want to partner with the best of UK industry, to maximise their opportunity to contribute to our growing European fleet."

Rolls-Royce SMR has spent more than £300 million with UK suppliers since it was formed in 2021 — 88 per cent of its total spending so far.

The programme has already worked with more than 650 UK businesses and held three supplier events.

The programme is especially significant for the North East, where plans for new nuclear projects are gathering pace.

A planned nuclear project in Hartlepool could power factories and AI data centres while creating an estimated 2,500 jobs.

US nuclear firm X-Energy and British energy company Centrica plan to build 12 advanced modular reactors at the project.

Elsewhere, Siemens Energy’s Newcastle facility, formerly CA Parsons Works, will make steam-turbine parts for the first three Rolls-Royce SMR reactors, starting with the Gwyndod project in Anglesey, North Wales.

![]()Business Secretary, Jonathan Reynolds *(Image: Supplied)*

Simon Roddy, chief executive of Great British Energy-Nuclear, said: "This is an important milestone in strengthening the UK’s nuclear supply chain and creating opportunities for businesses across the country.

"Working with Rolls-Royce SMR, we are supporting delivery of more than 70% UK content and helping to ensure the benefits of the programme are felt here in the UK.

"The Supplier Programme will give companies the tools and confidence to compete for work at home and abroad."

The programme will help businesses enter the nuclear sector, expand their capabilities and showcase new ideas.

Business secretary Jonathan Reynolds MP said: "We have world-class nuclear expertise in this country and some of the best manufacturers anywhere.

"As demand for nuclear grows around the world, there's no reason Britain shouldn't be winning more of that work.

"This programme is about helping British firms get a bigger slice of that opportunity.

"Through our Modern Industrial Strategy, we're backing businesses to win more contracts, create more skilled jobs and make sure the benefits are felt in every postcode."

Workshops, industry events and expert advice will help businesses understand the nuclear sector’s rules and commercial demands.

Suppliers will see future demand more clearly and meet Rolls-Royce SMR procurement teams at networking and meet-the-buyer events.

Businesses with key capabilities will be able to work more closely with SMR supply-chain teams.

That could lead to joint planning and long-term projects to help deliver the SMR fleet.

The programme could put the North East and other regions at the heart of building clean, secure energy infrastructure, creating skilled jobs and driving industrial growth.
