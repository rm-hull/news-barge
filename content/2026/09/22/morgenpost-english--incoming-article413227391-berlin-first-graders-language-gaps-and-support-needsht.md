How ready are Berlin’s children for school? The answer – at least in some districts – is rather sobering. In Marzahn-Hellersdorf, for example, two thirds of preschool children need additional support at school. This is according to an analysis of as yet unpublished district data from the 2023 school-entry health examinations.

In Marzahn-Hellersdorf, the proportion of children requiring support at school rose from 59.7 percent in 2019 to 66 percent in 2023. What stands out is the uneven distribution of support needs within the district. Of the nine district regions, Hellersdorf Ost has the highest proportion of children requiring support, at 80.4 percent. As in previous years, Hellersdorf Nord ranks highest for language deficits and low parental social status (63.7 percent with language deficits; 25.5 percent from households in the lower social-status group).

### Major Regional Differences Within the District

In the low-rise residential areas, support needs were significantly lower than in the large housing estates, as they had been the previous year. This reinforces the finding that children’s opportunities at the start of school are determined above all by their family background. Another important factor is media use. In the school-entry examinations, 32.6 percent of parents said their children used digital media for two hours a day.

To provide a more precise picture of the differences within the district, the district office analysed the data by planning area. The results show that children from the Ahrensfelder Berge area in Marzahn-Mitte have the highest need for support, at 86.5 percent, while Mahlsdorf-Nord has the lowest, at 24.2 percent.

“The persistently high numbers of children requiring support are alarming,” said Gordon Lemm (SPD), District Councillor for Youth, Health and Family. Schools were overwhelmed by the enormous level of support required and were not adequately equipped.

“I see the key areas for improvement as support at home and children’s use of digital media,” Lemm said. He said he had heard from daycare centre managers that there were increasing numbers of very young children under the age of three with limited social, cognitive and language development but remarkable skills when it came to unlocking and using tablets or smartphones. These developments showed that “smartphone-free schools must be one way of strengthening skills that have deteriorated”.

### Young Children’s Media Use Is a Major Problem

The extensive use of digital media by young children in daycare is also viewed very critically in the Reinickendorf district. Public health officer Patrick Larscheid speaks of a veritable “dumbing down” of children. “There are parents who attach a smartphone to their child’s buggy,” he said. This is disastrous, he said, because it means the children barely perceive their surroundings and develop far fewer skills. “We then see the result every year in the school-entry examinations,” Larscheid said. “We find that one in three children requires additional support, and the figure is rising.”

In Neukölln, the proportion of children for whom language support at school was recommended rose to 44.6 percent in the 2023 school-entry examinations. From 2019 to 2022, the figure stood at 40 percent. According to Hannes Rehfeldt (CDU), Neukölln District Councillor for Social Affairs and Health, however, these figures are still provisional: the data analysis has been completed, but the report itself has not yet been finalised.

### There Is No Citywide Overview of School-Entry Examinations

In Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg, too, the data from the 2023 school-entry examinations are available, but “a more in-depth expert analysis of the available data has not yet been possible”, the district press office said in response to an inquiry. The reason is that the position responsible for health reporting in the Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg district has been vacant for several years.

Public health officer Larscheid finds the lack of an annual citywide overview of school-entry examination results “extremely frustrating”. It was unacceptable, he said, that the districts collected the data every year but then nothing further happened.

“A Berlin-wide analysis of the results would be incredibly important in order to identify differences and take preventive and health-promoting measures,” Larscheid said. He believes responsibility clearly lies with the Senate Department for Health. “Until the pandemic, it published these data every year and then stopped doing so from one day to the next,” Larscheid said. “That is a huge problem. We need to know the situation across Berlin in order to formulate policy demands such as a ban on mobile phones.”
