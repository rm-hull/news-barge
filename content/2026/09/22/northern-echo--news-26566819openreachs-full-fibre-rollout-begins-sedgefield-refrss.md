Homes and businesses in Sedgefield are set to get some of the UK’s fastest internet speeds as work begins on a new full fibre network.

Openreach is rolling out the Sedgefield network as part of a UK-wide plan to reach 25 million premises by the end of 2026.

Fibre-optic cables will deliver gigabit-capable speeds and a more reliable service to residents and businesses.

Kasam Hussain, partnership director at Openreach, said: "We’re bringing full fibre broadband to Sedgefield and letting local people know what to expect.

![]()Openreach begins work on a full fibre broadband network in Sedgefield, bringing gigabit-capable speeds and more reliable internet to homes and businesses *(Image: Martin Avery)*

"This is a major infrastructure upgrade, so there will be more engineering teams, equipment and vans around town, and we're working hard to keep disruption to a minimum.

"Wherever possible, we’ll use our existing network of ducts and poles to avoid roadworks, new street furniture and disturbance.

"But there may be places where we need to install new poles, underground ducts and fibre cables because it’s the only way to make sure households get included in the upgrade."

Full fibre promises fewer faults, steadier speeds and enough capacity for growing data use.

It should keep streaming, gaming, video calls, online banking and customer interactions running smoothly, even at peak times.

Residents can register for updates and check availability as the work progresses at openreach.co.uk/ultrafastfullfibre.

Openreach is investing £15 billion in its national full fibre rollout and has reached more than 23 million premises.

It aims to reach up to 30 million homes and businesses by the end of the decade, if investment conditions allow.

Mr Hussain said: "Openreach is committed to building the best full fibre network, and doing it sustainably is crucial for our business, the communities we serve, and the environment we all share.

"As part of our ‘Let’s Reach Zero’ strategy, we aim to lower our carbon emissions, use less and waste less material, and protect nature wherever we operate."

Openreach is also switching its commercial vehicle fleet, the UK’s second-largest, to zero-emission vehicles by 2031.

Research suggests nationwide full fibre could prevent 400 million commuter trips a year, helping cut emissions.

Openreach is a wholly owned and independent subsidiary of BT Group, generating revenues of £6.19 billion in the year ending March 2026.

Full fibre is more reliable, faster and has lower latency than copper broadband.

It is also less likely to fail in bad weather or suffer electromagnetic interference.

At home, it supports 4K streaming, online gaming and remote working without buffering or slowdowns.

For businesses, it supports cloud computing, e-commerce and uninterrupted video conferencing.

Openreach will keep residents and businesses updated and minimise disruption as the rollout continues.

The project is part of a wider push to strengthen the UK’s digital infrastructure and support growth in communities nationwide.
