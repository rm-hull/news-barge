OUR hearts go out to the 150 workers, and their families, who have abruptly lost their jobs as a 70-year-old glassmaking company, the biggest of its kind in the country, has gone into administration.

Many of them will have put their heart and soul into Clayton Glass in Stanley, working there for decades and, in recent tough times, accepting pay cuts to try and get the company through.

For much of the last decade, there has been nothing but good news stories coming out of Clayton Glass in Stanley as, through acquisitions, it grew to own plants in Scotland, France and Ireland, making glass units for residential properties.

In times when the country is supposed to be building 300,000 new homes every year and when we have a new prime minister who, correctly, is especially keen on reindustrialising the north, Clayton Glass should have had everything on its side.

Yet it is closing.

Administrator James Lumb said: “Unfortunately, we are seeing a similarly story across a range of businesses supplying the housing market.

“The economics of the industry are incredibly challenging with high and rising fixed costs and soft demand which makes good businesses like Clayton unsustainable on a normal operating base.”

It is rarely one thing that brings a company down but high and rising energy costs will have contributed to Clayton’s difficulties.

They are caused primarily by Donald Trump’s ill-judged war in Iran and Ukraine’s understandable attempts to hit back at Russia, but our own government has to look at how it can ease the crisis that is hitting ‘good’ companies like Clayton as well as ordinary people so very hard.
