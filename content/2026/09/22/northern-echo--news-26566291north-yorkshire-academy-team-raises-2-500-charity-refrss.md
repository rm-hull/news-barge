Staff and a sixth-form student from a County Durham academy raised more than £2,500 for charity by tackling the Great North Run.

The team from King James I Academy in Bishop Auckland completed the half marathon and raised money for four charities.

Headteacher Simon Whitehead ran for St Teresa’s Hospice in Darlington, while assistant headteacher Lucy Mulryan supported Macmillan Cancer Support.

History teacher Lois Best chose to fundraise for Teenage Cancer Trust, and Year 13 student Codey Austin supported the If U Care Share Foundation.

Mr Whitehead said: "Fundraising is a great way to promote empathy in young people and to give them a sense of belonging in the world and personal achievement.

"It is an important part of our academy calendar and I hope that as a group, we have set a good example to our students and proved that with determination, you can achieve anything."

Science teacher Lucy Wilson and head of computing Jade Franklin also completed the race as a personal challenge.

Student runner Codey Austin said: "I have grown up watching the Great North Run and always wanted to take part.
