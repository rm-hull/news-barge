More than 150 staff at a County Durham factory have been made redundant after the company fell into administration.

Workers at Clayton Glass' Stanley site were told on payday last Friday (September 18) that they would not receive their wages and the company had filed a notice of intention to appoint an administrator.

The firm, which employed around 160 people at Harelaw Industrial Estate, was slammed as "heartless" as staff only learnt of the news after turning up for work.

Today (Tuesday, September 22), administrators confirmed 290 jobs have been lost across three sites in County Durham, Lancashire and Scotland.

James Lumb, managing director at Interpath and joint administrator of Clayton Glass said: “Clayton Glass has a proud history, having established itself at the heart of the British glass industry, and this transaction gives the business a platform to continue, secures some of its operations and, crucially, preserves a significant number of jobs.

"Unfortunately, we are seeing a similarly story across a range of businesses supplying the housing market.

"The economics of the industry are incredibly challenging with high and rising fixed costs and soft demand which makes good businesses like Clayton unsustainable on a normal operating base.

![]() *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT/NORTHERN ECHO)*

"The directors have fought hard to find a solvent solution, but regrettably not all of the business could be rescued in this market. We have a dedicated team on site supporting impacted staff through this difficult time with making representations to the Redundancy Payments Service.”

The business said it was unable to meet its financial obligations, partly due to high energy and raw material costs and an extended period of low demand.

Employees previously told The Northern Echo they arrived at work on Friday expecting answers - but instead learned they would not be paid.

One worker, who asked not to be named, described the mood as "horrible".

He said: "Everybody's frustrated. They feel hard done to.

"People have got families, they've got bills to pay and some people work Friday to Friday. Not everybody's got savings.

"It's going to be a hard time for them."

The employee said staff remained in the dark for much of the day, with many uncertain whether the business would continue operating under administrators or whether jobs would be lost.

He also said many employees had spent decades at the factory and had previously accepted pay cuts in an effort to keep the business going.

"We took a seven per cent pay cut, and office staff took a 10 per cent cut, to help keep the business open," he said.

Annfield Plain and Tanfield councillor Darren Grimes said the redundancy announcement was a devastating blow for the area.

He said: "Clayton Glass. Founded 1956. Survived everything for 70 years. Couldn't survive Britain's energy bills.

![]() *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"The administrators said it plainly: high energy costs helped finish it off. 290 redundancies, and County Durham is one of the sites closing.

"Ed Miliband gets his green halo. Families in my ward get the dole queue.

"I am genuinely disgusted by those who purport to be the 'party of the working class'."

Luke Akehurst, MP for North Durham, said: "I'm devastated for the employees of Clayton Glass who have lost their jobs.

"If anyone affected needs my help I would urge them to contact me on [luke.akehurst@parliament.uk.](mailto:luke.akehurst@parliament.uk.)"

![]() *(Image: PARLIAMENT)*

Cllr Grimes previously criticised the way staff had learned of the firm's difficulties, describing the treatment of workers as "absolutely disgusting".

He said workers had arrived expecting to start their shifts only to discover they would not be paid.

Ward colleague Karen Allison also expressed concern for employees and the wider economic impact on the area.

She said: "Obviously our thoughts go out to the families.

"It doesn't just affect those employees, it also affects the supply chain.

"You probably have companies now that are going to be struggling because of what's happened here.

"It's going to have a devastating effect on the area as a whole."

Mr Akehurst previously said he was "disgusted" by the way staff had been treated.

He said: "While I am very appreciative of the pressures businesses are under, for example due to energy costs, there is no excuse for springing this kind of announcement on staff.

"This has been badly mismanaged."

Durham County Council said support may be available for affected staff through its Crisis and Resilience Fund for those facing immediate financial hardship.
