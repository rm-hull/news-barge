A restored oyster reef off the North East coast is thriving after thousands of shellfish were added this month.

It forms part of the five-year Wild Oyster Project: Tyne and Wear to revive native oyster populations and boost marine biodiversity in the region, following the deployment of innovative ‘Oyster Reef Cubes’ in the North Sea last year.

![]()Cultch on the seabed *(Image: Project Baseline UK)*

On September 10, the team deployed a further 1,000 mature native oysters and 15,000 spat-on-shell (juvenile oysters) to boost the restoration site.

Marine conservationists from the Zoological Society of London (ZSL) and Groundwork North East & Cumbria have welcomed the results of recent dive and underwater video surveys of the site.

![]()Divers preparing to survey the reef *(Image: Groundwork North East)*

The surveys show that both adult and juvenile native oysters are surviving on the reef, alongside native fish and crustaceans.

Dr Ashleigh Tinlin-Mackenzie, marine ecology technical lead at Groundwork NE & Cumbria, said: "We are delighted by the results of the latest dive survey and are extremely grateful to Project Baseline’s UK volunteers for their dedication and hard work gathering data at 25 metres depth in the cold North Sea.

"The Oyster Cubes have done a great job."

"Seeing the artificial reef and the oysters surviving their first winter and seeing fish, crabs, sea urchins, shrimps and squat lobsters already using different parts of the restoration site gives us confidence that this approach will help restore native oyster reefs and support wider marine ecosystems in the North Sea."

![]()Oyster Reef Cube (18 June 2026) *(Image: Project Baseline UK)*

Native oyster reefs disappeared from the North East more than a century ago.

The site, covering one hectare, was established in October 2025 as part of a first-of-its-kind project to restore ‘superstar’ water filters to UK waters.

The scheme is led by South Tyneside Council and funded by Defra as part of the £200 million Flood and Coastal Innovation Programmes, managed by the Environment Agency.

A team of seven volunteer divers from Project Baseline UK carried out a survey in July to assess progress nine months after the reef’s installation.

The site includes 20 Oyster Reef Cubes, 60,000 juvenile oysters, and 100 tonnes of scallop shell cultch.

![]()A 'spat on shell' *(Image: Groundwork North East)*

The cubes are designed to mimic natural reef structures and provide a stable environment for oysters and other marine life.

Results from the survey showed around 99 per cent of adult oysters attached to the cubes were alive and healthy.

Loose oysters on the seabed within the area were also surviving, and young oysters (spat) had successfully overwintered despite cold temperatures and limited food.

The project aims to continue expanding the reef and testing new restoration techniques.
