A County Durham heritage railway has won a top award at one of the North East’s leading horticultural competitions for its work with the environment and community.

Tanfield Railway won Gold and Best in Class in the Bus, Metro and Railway Stations category at the Northumbria in Bloom awards.

Angela Pickering, secretary of the Tanfield Railway Trust who led the award application, said: "We were over the moon to win Gold and even more so Best in Class.

"There is so much work that goes into maintaining the railway, and this is a true credit to everyone involved.

"Countless hours go into making our stations look great, and we owe a special thanks to all of the groups who have helped us, in particular Stanley Town Council, who have supported us for a number of years."

Judges praised the railway’s volunteers, its partnerships with schools and organisations, and its vibrant floral displays, which complement its 1920s–1950 heritage theme.

The award adds to a successful year for the railway, which also won Railway of the Year and Young Volunteer of the Year at the Heritage Railway Association awards.

Volunteer gardener Geoff Sharp said: "I'm still beaming now.

"We have won silver gilt for the last few years, and to get Gold this year after so much hard work is the cherry on the top.

"We are just a small team who look after the gardens and estates, so this is credit to everyone involved."

Mr Sharp was also delighted by the public response to the team’s work.

He said: "We've worked with so many people and groups along the way, and it's great to see visitors comment on the lovely hanging baskets, floral displays, and well-kept stations and grounds.

"We're not going to rest on our laurels though – we have to maintain our winning streak for next year."

Visitors can still enjoy the award-winning surroundings while Tanfield Railway runs heritage steam trains every Sunday until October 18.

The railway is also seeking volunteers to help with gardening, grounds maintenance and other support roles.
