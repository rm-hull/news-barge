A North East entrepreneur has opened a wellness café after leaving his job managing community facilities and school lettings to pursue his passion for coffee, fitness and nutrition.

Mark Graham, 40, founded KULT, a Gosforth High Street café serving speciality coffee, health-focused drinks and snacks.

KULT is at 161-163 Gosforth High Street, opposite The Brandling Arms and next to The Gym Group, serving customers seeking a nutritious breakfast or post-workout refuel.

![]()Mark Graham at the new KULT, a wellness café on Gosforth High Street *(Image: LAUREN PETERS)*

Mr Graham said: "I’d always wanted to do something totally different and follow a passion.

"I’ve always enjoyed coffee and I love it, but I’ve also got my Level 3 personal training qualification and have always been interested in fitness and nutrition.

"I thought there are a million different coffee places you can go to, but I wanted to create somewhere that fitted with me.

![]()Brew coming up *(Image: LAUREN PETERS)*

"I like going for a coffee, but I also like to train and make sure I’m eating properly afterwards.

"When the opportunity came up in Gosforth, with lots of fitness businesses around the area, it just seemed to make sense from a business perspective."

Mr Graham had no barista experience before completing intensive training and spending nine months refining the café.

KULT serves coffee, protein shakes, macro-friendly snacks, matcha drinks and protein-packed overnight oats.

Biscoff protein overnight oats are among KULT’s bestsellers, with Mr Graham saying they are "absolutely flying."

He said: "You get more than 30 grams of protein and they’re like a full meal, but they taste like a dessert.

"People come in and buy three or four at a time."

The peanut butter and banana protein shake is another favourite, but iced matcha has unexpectedly become a top seller.

Mr Graham said: "I didn’t expect the matcha to be as popular as it is.

"We’re actually selling more iced matchas than cappuccinos, which has surprised us, but it’s a huge trend at the moment."

Mr Graham expected mainly fitness fans, but has been pleased to attract a much broader crowd.

He said: "We’ve got everything from retired people who come in because they’re curious, through to people who are really into fitness and nutrition.

"For me, customer service is hugely important.

"I love getting to know people, finding out a little bit about their background and what they do.

"I wanted Kult to be somewhere where people feel comfortable coming in, whatever their reason for being there."

Graham chose the name KULT because it was short, memorable and easy to build into a brand.

Mr Graham said: "I don’t see KULT as just a coffee shop.

"There are lots of different directions we can take it in, including events and different wellness activities."

He employs five part-time staff and hired for personality rather than hospitality experience alone.

Mr Graham said: "It’s all been a bit mad and crazy.
