The artist behind the UK’s first Flower Carpet has visited Bradford Industrial Archive.

Shiraaz Ali, the artist behind the project, examined archive material, seeking ideas for the installation.

A social media post shared by Bradford Culture Company on September 16 said he had taken inspiration from Bradford’s textiles, machinery and artisans.

### 'A testament to Bradford'

Mr Ali said: “All the flowers are going to be packed together and slowly, this beautiful image will be created and it all will be a testament to Bradford.”

He said the books within the archive were beautifully made, with many designs and textiles.

Mr Ali said: “There’s endless inspiration with some of these pieces, but for me, the design as a whole for the Flower Carpet was meant to be accessible.

"It’s a representation of Bradford as a whole and what it means to be a Bradfordian, the vibrancy of Bradford and its people and its communities.”

### 'This is what built Bradford'

The Bradford Industrial Museum's Collections Manager Elizabeth Llabres said: “This is what built Bradford, this is Bradford, the textile trade is what made the city what it is today, it’s what’s attracted such an amazing and diverse community.”

She also pointed to the skills and knowledge held by local people, saying the archive material could inspire new artists.

Mr Ali said: “It was a beautiful stepping stone to see these fabrics and to actually feel their craftmanship and their detail and I think hopefully this is the same way the flower carpet will emerge.”

![]()The Flower Carpet installation attracts crowds in Belgium, where the event has been a tradition since the 1970s and will make its UK debut in Bradford this September. *(Image: Bradford Culture Company)*

He added: “This has a lovely connection with these fabrics where the fabric is a finished piece but actually there’s thousands and thousands of fibres that have come together through skill and craftmanship, time, energy and effort to create this and I think that’s beautiful.”

### On display from Thursday 24 September

The 29-meter Flower Carpet will go on display from Thursday, September 24 to Sunday, September 27, 2026 in Centenary Square.

The project is part of Nature Season, a programme of nature-themed cultural activity across five West Yorkshire districts.

The programme includes large-scale installations, open-air concerts, woodland art trails and community gardening projects.

It was created in collaboration with Tapis de fleurs de Bruxelles, the non-profit association that organises the Flower Carpet event at Grand-Place in Brussels.

Nature Season is supported by the West Yorkshire Combined Authority and West Yorkshire Local Visitor Economy Partnership.

Partners include Bradford City Council and Bradford Culture Company, Calderdale Council, Kirklees Council, Leeds City Council and Wakefield Council.
