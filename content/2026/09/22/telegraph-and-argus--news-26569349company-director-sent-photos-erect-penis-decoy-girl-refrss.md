A man who sent photos of his erect penis to a 12-year-old girl during online chat was actually communicating with an undercover police officer.

Nicholas Gray is now beginning two years and three months in prison and must register as a sex offender for the next decade.

The 38-year-old, formerly of Thornton, Bradford, was described in Bradford Crown Court as a company director and a man of previous positive good character.

However, he was convicted by a jury after a trial of attempting to incite a child under 13 to engage in sexual activity and attempting to engage in sexual communication with a child between April 24 and May 18, 2024.

Prosecutor Olivia Fraser told the court how Gray began messaging the girl, who said she was 12, and encouraged her to take pictures of her body under the desk at school.

He asked her “what mischief she was open to”, estimated how far he was from her school, and stated he could be there in 35 minutes.

During one of their discussions, he told her that he “wanted all he could get his hands on.”

He encouraged her to take pictures and sent her photographs of his erect penis.

When he was arrested and interviewed by police, Gray claimed he did not believe that the person he was communicating with was 12 and that he was engaging in fantasy.

He maintained he was only attracted to adult women.

In mitigation, barrister Phil Gibbs said Gray’s offending amounted to “a serious but isolated aberration” by a man who was otherwise of positive good character.

He said Gray had lost his job and there had been difficulties at home as his wife was suffering from post-natal depression and mental health issues.

He added: “He has succumbed to offending that he has struggled to acknowledge in some ways.

“He describes himself as foolish and regretful and sorry. It is accepted that he is remorseful.”

He said there was every prospect of rehabilitation in Gray’s case and that there was a low risk of re-offending.

He said Gray had “aged significantly” and had been in A&E with “suicidal thoughts”.

He said Gray was staying in a hotel during the week to be able to perform his duties as a company director in Brighouse and lived with his parents in Leicestershire at weekends.

He suggested that any prison sentence could be suspended.

Sentencing Gray, Her Honour Judge Sophie McKone said: “For about three weeks you engaged in sexual talk with somebody you hoped and believed was a 12-year-old child.

“It of course wasn’t a child, but you wanted it to be.

“The jury rejected your account that you knew the person was an adult and were wanting, in effect, to bring them out into the open so that you could report them to the police.

“Had that been a real 12-year-old child the damage to that child would have been substantial not just at the time but no doubt for the rest of their life.”

She added: “You continue to deny to your offending … although you do regret your actions.”

For attempted incitement Gray received two years and three months’ imprisonment. For attempted sexual communication he was handed 18 months, to be served concurrently.

Judge McKone ordered Gray to register as a sex offender for the next ten years and imposed a Sexual Harm Prevention Order for the same period.

She told Gray he would serve half of the custodial sentence before being eligible for release.
