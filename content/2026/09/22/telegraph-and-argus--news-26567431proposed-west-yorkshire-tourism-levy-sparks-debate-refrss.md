A proposed tourism levy that would see visitors charged a small fee for overnight stays in West Yorkshire has sparked debate among businesses and cultural leaders across the region.

Rebecca Yorke, director of the Brontë Parsonage Museum in Haworth, said: "We have not consulted our local accommodation partners or our visitors about the possible introduction of an overnight visitor levy, but understand why there might be concerns.

### Museum director urges transparency

"The current economic climate continues to put pressure on the budgets of businesses and visitors alike and many tourist destinations have already been impacted by increased costs, e.g. the price of fuel and car parking charges."

![]()The Bronte Parsonage Museum at Haworth *(Image: submitted)*

She stressed the need for transparency if a levy is introduced.

Ms Yorke said: "While the introduction of a tourism levy will bring us in line with many European destinations, we believe it's important that its purpose is clearly communicated and articulates how the income generated by it will be used."

A local business has expressed support for the idea, provided the funds are used to benefit the community.

### Local business backs community investment

A spokesperson for Lighthouse Lane, a business on the main street in Haworth, said: "We so appreciate those who travel to visit our beautiful village.

![]() *(Image: Getty)*

"I would support the introduction of a modest tourist levy in Haworth if that money was used to support the villages events and infrastructure and to keep it a safe, fun and attractive place to visit."

Tracy Brabin, mayor of West Yorkshire, has publicly backed the scheme.

![]() *(Image: Newsquest)*

She highlighted the importance for Yorkshire specifically, stating “Our beautiful region has so much to offer; from Brontë Country to the Hepworth Gallery, the rolling moors to our bustling city nightlife, arts and theatre to beautiful architecture.

### 'Invest in our culture and grow our economy'

She said: "By asking people to make a small contribution, like in many other European countries, we can continue to invest in culture, grow our economy and ensure people want to visit for generations to come."

The government argues more money could be invested into high streets, public transport, and events to boost tourism and drive good growth thanks to the latest shift of power from Whitehall and back into communities.
