**Today's Object of the Week is a remarkable collection of photographs that captures everyday moments, familiar faces and a way of life now largely disappeared, preserving a unique snapshot of North Yorkshire during a time of great change.**

A collection of photographs offering a vivid glimpse into North Yorkshire life during the 1940s is set to bring the past into sharp focus once again.

Now on display in a new exhibition at The Garden Rooms at Tennants, in Leyburn, the images were taken by Leeds photographer Harold Bryce Thomson and capture everything from family life and village scenes to farming traditions, local characters and well-known landmarks.

What makes the photographs particularly compelling is their sense of authenticity.

Rather than documenting grand events or historic occasions, Thomson turned his attention to ordinary people and everyday moments.

![]()Capturing an everyday moment, a child poses in front of a wooden cart in this black-and-white image from Harold Bryce Thomson's archive *(Image: THE GARDEN ROOMS, TENNANTS)*

Children at play, farmers at work and residents going about their daily lives all became subjects for a photographer with a keen eye for detail and a deep affection for the communities he visited.

Many of the photographs were taken in and around Wensleydale during and after the Second World War.

Thomson developed a strong connection with the area after three of his children were evacuated there during the conflict, and he and his family went on to become regular visitors.

That affection is evident throughout the collection.

The photographs reveal a landscape shaped by tradition and hard work while documenting the people who gave the Dales its distinctive character.

![]()Showing The Cow, Castle Bolton: A man and woman display a prize animal in one of Thomson's photographs of farming life in the Yorkshire Dales *(Image: THE GARDEN ROOMS, TENNANTS)*

Looking through them today offers a chance to step back into a period when rural life followed a very different rhythm.

For Thomson, a trichologist by trade – a specialist who studies and treats problems related to the human hair and scalp – photography was far more than a pastime.

It brought together many of his interests, from history and art to his love of Yorkshire and its people.

His friendships with artists, including Fred Lawson and members of the Castle Bolton artists' circle, further strengthened his ties to the region.

His dedication to photography earned wider recognition.

Thomson served as president of Leeds Camera Club, exhibited and lectured on photography, and became a member of the Royal Photographic Society.

The photographs featured in the exhibition have been reproduced from a much larger archive of around 480 glass slides.

Together they form an extraordinary record of a North Yorkshire landscape and way of life that has largely disappeared, preserving moments that might otherwise have been lost to history.

Visitors to the exhibition will find portraits, family scenes, farming images and views of local landmarks, each helping to tell the story of the Dales and its communities during a period of enormous change.

The exhibition is free to enter and open daily from October 6 to November 1 at The Garden Rooms at Tennants.

The archive from which the photographs have been drawn will also go under the hammer in Tennants Auctioneers' Books, Maps & Manuscripts Sale on November 25, when the collection of around 480 glass slides is expected to sell for £300 to £500, plus buyer's premium.

Further details about the exhibition can be found on the Tennants Garden Rooms website.
