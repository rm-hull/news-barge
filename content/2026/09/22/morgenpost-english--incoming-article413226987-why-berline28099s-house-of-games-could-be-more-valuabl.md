The first sections of the House of Games in Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg are due to be completed in December, with the first tenants also expected to move in. The buildings around the historic Lux Tower are to become a shared hub for Germany’s games industry, with office and event space.

One important tenant is French game developer Ubisoft (“Assassin’s Creed”, “Far Cry”). The state-owned Wista Management GmbH announced the company as an “anchor tenant”. The contract was signed at the end of August at Gamescom in Cologne, the world’s largest computer games fair.

Speaking to the Morgenpost, Felix Falk, Managing Director of the German Games Industry Association, explains why the planned House of Games is much more valuable to Berlin than Gamescom. “There can only be one Gamescom as the world’s largest gaming event. But it only takes place for one week a year. A House of Games will shine 52 weeks a year as an international beacon,” Falk said. “There is no project like this anywhere else in the world,” he enthused. “Berlin is playing to its strengths.” His association, Game, will also be based there.

### Gamescom Is Unique and Will Remain in Cologne

Gamescom, on the other hand, will remain in Cologne. The association has a contract with the City of Cologne and Koelnmesse, and there are no plans to move the event to Berlin, Falk stressed. The trade fair is developing rapidly, as is the billion-euro games industry. The power and importance of games are being recognised, Falk said, including by politicians. What is still missing, however, is “an understanding of how the potential of games can be put to even better use for society and the economy”.

According to a recent study by the German Games Industry Association, Germany is one of the world’s five largest markets for computer games. In 2024, players in Germany spent around €5.5 billion on them, and the figure is rising. However, only a small proportion of this – €300 million – is spent on German games, giving them a market share of just 5.5 percent. Germany’s games industry comprises around 900 development and publishing companies and around 300 service providers. The number of German games studios has almost doubled compared with 2018. Berlin is home to the largest share, at 22 percent, followed by Bavaria (19 percent) and North Rhine-Westphalia (17 percent).

### Artificial Intelligence Has Made Game Development Faster

According to Falk, the 12,000-square-metre House of Games is “a huge opportunity for Germany as a business location and for Berlin, because there is no project of this scale anywhere else in the world. Investors looking at the German market will go to the House of Games.” The centre is intended to help “make Germany the heart of the games world and enable the industry to compete globally”.

The different facets of games are to be showcased here: “Games as an important cultural asset, as the heart of pop culture, as an economic force and as a technology.” Many technologies of the future, such as cloud computing, AI and 3D simulation, were developed in the games industry. Artificial intelligence has accelerated game development. Falk said: “With AI, a four-year development period might be cut to just two and a half years. That is a huge advantage for companies.” AI, however, cannot create good, complex games with a storyline.

Games such as Rockstar Games’ announced “Grand Theft Auto 6” are becoming increasingly sophisticated, both technically and visually.

### The Retro Trend in Gaming Continues

Six out of ten Germans play video games today, according to Falk. Popular gaming platforms include computers, consoles, smartphones and tablets, as well as retro gaming systems. The retro trend remains strong. “Many 40-year-olds grew up with games and fondly remember the games they used to play,” Falk said.

Speaking about Gamescom, Falk said: “As the games industry, we clearly stand for values such as democracy, openness and diversity. The AfD and its elected representatives, who oppose these values even though political parties should be particularly committed to democracy, are therefore not partners for us and are not welcome at Gamescom.” Falk clarified that he was referring to AfD elected representatives, not voters, and explained: “The AfD’s positions – against the EU, against the euro, against cultural diversity, its pro-Russian stance, its nationalism, its anti-democratic positions – are not only contrary to our values, they also endanger the success of our industry and Germany’s success as a business and cultural hub overall.”

### The German Games Industry Is Highly International

Following his statement at Gamescom, Falk received many reactions. There was also “a small shitstorm”. But most of the responses from gamers and the industry were positive. The industry is highly international: one in three people working in Germany’s games industry does not have German citizenship. Berlin studios such as Wooga have employees from 30 different countries. English is therefore spoken in many games studios.
