A nature reserve on the outskirts of York linked to one of England’s biggest historic battles is expanding after a deal secured more land for wildlife and flood protection.

The Church Commissioners for England have agreed a 30-year lease with the Carstairs Countryside Trust, adding five acres to Fulford Ings and taking the wetland site to 95 acres.

Fulford Ings, south of York, is a Site of Special Scientific Interest known for its floodplain meadows, rare species and role as a natural flood store for the River Ouse.

Robbie Brett, environmental asset manager at the Church Commissioners for England, said: "With a history that dates back over nine centuries, Fulford Ings is an important riverside location for nationally and regionally significant species as well as for the public at large.

"Our agreement with the Carstairs Countryside Trust adds to a successful nature conservation effort of several years.

"We look forward to working collaboratively to ensure the site’s wildlife continues to flourish."

The announcement coincides with the 960th anniversary of the Battle of Fulford, fought on September 20, 1066, between Norway’s King Harald III and northern earls.

Stephen Cottrell, Archbishop of York, said: "The care of creation is a vital part of our Christian calling.

"Places like Fulford Ings remind us that when we work together to restore and protect the natural world, we are investing in the flourishing of both people and wildlife.

"I welcome this partnership, which will help ensure this remarkable landscape continues to be a place of beauty, biodiversity, and hope for generations to come."

Fulford Ings supports a pair of curlews listed on the IUCN Red List, as well as kingfishers protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act.

The site is also home to the endangered tansy beetle and rare Agabus undulatus beetles.

A recent Natural England survey found the site in favourable condition and credited the Trust’s management since 2010.

David Hargreaves, CCT’s operations manager, said: "Co-operation with the Church Commissioners for England is a milestone in helping the Trust to drive forward local partnerships for wider nature recovery."
