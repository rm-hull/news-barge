An Indian street food outlet in a West Yorkshire University Student's Union has responded to its one-star food hygiene rating it received last year.

Tikka Nation, located on the lower ground floor of Leeds University Union on Lifton Place, received a 1-star rating following an inspection in October 2025.

The food vendor, which markets its as a "gateway to India’s culinary treasures," serves curries, wraps, loaded fries and other Indian street food.

It has multiple branches all over the UK.

## Site is now under new management

Sumit Jain, founder of Tikka Nation, said: "We take food hygiene and the safety of our customers extremely seriously.

"The 1-star rating issued in October 2025 related to issues identified at the site at the time of that inspection.

"Since then, the site has come under new management, and significant work has been carried out to address the matters raised."

Mr Jain said the new management team had implemented a range of improvements to ensure compliance with food safety standards.

He continued: "The new management team has reviewed and strengthened food safety procedures, staff training, record keeping, cleaning and compliance processes, together with increased management oversight to ensure the required standards are consistently maintained.

"We recognise that a 1-star rating is not the standard our customers should expect from Tikka Nation, nor is it the standard we expect of ourselves.

"The issues highlighted during the previous inspection have been worked on, and we are currently awaiting a further inspection so that the improvements made under the new management can be formally assessed."

## What the report said

Tikka Nation in Leeds was awarded a 'good' rating both for its hygienic food handling and for the cleanliness and condition of facilities and the building.

Where it felt short was with regards to it food safety management for which "major improvement" was deemed necessary.

This includes system or checks in place to ensure that food sold or served is safe to eat, evidence that staff know about food safety, and the food safety officer has confidence that standards will be maintained in future.

Mr Jain said the company is committed to maintaining high food hygiene standards and will continue to work with environmental health officers to ensure full compliance.

The University of Leeds was also contacted for comment.
