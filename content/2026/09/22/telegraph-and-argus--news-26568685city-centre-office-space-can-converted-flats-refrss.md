A CITY centre building that was recently earmarked as offices for a union can now be converted into flats – planners have agreed.

13 - 15 Sunbridge Road has previously been a bank and a convenience store, with office space above.

Earlier this year, property developer Mab Hussain applied to Bradford Council for permission to convert the upper floors of the Conservation Area building into ten flats.

It was not a planning application, but a request for “prior approval.” Under current planning rules developers can convert empty office space into residential use without the need for planning permission.

But Council planners need to agree that the development meets certain criteria.

Bradford Council has now granted prior approval for this residential conversion – which will retain the retail space on the ground floor.

In 2019 the Unite Union was granted planning permission to convert the ground floor unit into office space – part of a plan to convert the entire building into a new base for the Union.

At the time the organisation said: “Unite is planning to have new offices in Bradford as a springboard to stand up for working people in a vibrant and youthful city at what are very challenging times.”

The Telegraph & Argus had contacted Unite several times since then for an update, but have never received a response.
