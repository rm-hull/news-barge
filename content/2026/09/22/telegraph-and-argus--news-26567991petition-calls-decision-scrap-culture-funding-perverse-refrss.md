A PETITION has been set up calling for Bradford Council bosses to reconsider pulling £400,000 for Culture in the District to re-open a rural tip.

It describes the decision as “perverse and short-sighted.”

Earlier this month, Bradford Council’s Executive voted to re-open the Sugden End tip at Cross Roads, near Keighley, by the end of the year.

The tip was one of three to have been closed in 2024 as part of cost cutting measures by the then Labour run Bradford Council.

Although the re-opening was welcomed, concerns had been raised over how the Council intended to fund it.

To pay for the re-opening and the tips continual operation, the Council has “deprioritised” £400,000 that had been earmarked for cultural activities.

The funding had been approved earlier this year, and was intended to “support the delivery of events across the district to promote local heritage, culture and arts and drive increased footfall in district centres.”

The decision has been called in by Labour Councillor Susan Fricker (Bingley East) and will be debated again at a meeting of the Regeneration and Environment Scrutiny Committee tonight (Tuesday evening).

A petition calling for the Council to re-consider the culture funding was set up last week, and has so far been signed by 360 people.

It says: “After the spectacular success of City of Culture 2025, funds were approved by the council just six months ago to nurture the green shoots of the regeneration experienced across the district.”

Referring to Bradford 2025, it adds: “Bradford’s light shone brightly. The local economy benefitted as did the wellbeing of communities and individuals, a fact acknowledged by the council itself.

“This fund of £400,000 was earmarked to build on all of those these elements, and thereby help Bradford reinvent itself and emerge from decades of division and decline.

“To instead use it primarily to reopen and manage one dump appears perverse, short-sighted and possibly a massive false economy.

“The world has woken up to Bradford as a result of its cultural offer and this is a precious opportunity to maintain that momentum and build a robust and confident new version of our city and districts.

“We are not going to achieve that by reopening one tip.

“We do, of course, need proper provision re waste and recycling management but this proposed reassigning of funds would have a profound impact on Bradfords continuing growth, which is of far greater importance at this point in time.

“We request that a considered and transparent consultation process is undertaken, engaging with individuals and organisations that can best identify the impacts of this proposed withdrawal of funds, before the council votes to move forwards with this proposal.”

The petition runs until October 31.
