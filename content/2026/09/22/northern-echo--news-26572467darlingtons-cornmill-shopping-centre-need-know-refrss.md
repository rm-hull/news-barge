From everyday services, plenty of shops and picking up a bite to eat, the Cornmill Shopping Centre has been a familiar destination for generations of Darlington locals and visitors.

Since opening in 1992 it has become a key part of the town centre, boasting 53 shops and services.

Whether its stopping for a Greggs sausage roll, browsing the racks in New Look, or smelling all the goodness inside Yankee Candle, there is something for everyone.

Luckily, it is in a convenient location – close to major bus routes, as well as Darlington Train Station and a car park.

Whether you're planning your first visit or simply want to know what is open and when on your next trip, here's everything you need to know about the Cornmill.

### Is it open on Bank Holidays?

On Bank Holidays the Cornmill is open from 9am until 5pm.

The shopping centre is closed on Easter Sunday, Christmas Day and New Years Day.

### Where to eat in the Cornmill

You can stop for a coffee at the Waterstones café or The Savoury Cafe.

There is also a Greggs, Costa Coffee, Enquires Coffee, plus Pepe's.

### What shops are inside the Cornmill?

Shops include big brands such as Primark, New Look, HMV and JD.

There is also H Samuel, Footasylum, Next, Blush, and Bonmarche.

Book fans can browse in Waterstones.

There is also a Tesco Express to top up on food supplies, a Vision Express, plus a Superdrug.

The Cornmill is also home to a vape shop, phone repair shop, and toy stores.

You can pick up special occasion essentials at Cardzone and browse Amiyah Children's Boutique for sweet kids' outfits.

There is also a Eurochange money exchange shop.

### Closure of Cornmill's Post Office and TG Jones

TG Jones is closing down in Darlington - along with the town centre’s only Post Office.

The national chain, formerly WHSmith, will be pulling down the shutters on its store at the Cornmill Shopping Centre later this year, it has been confirmed.

The closure means that Darlington Post Office will also shut.

Bosses say a closing date is still being finalised but is likely to be around the end of November.

However, a spokesperson for the postal service confirmed that it is looking to open a replacement branch as soon as possible, and has already heard from interested retailers.

It could take around six months to set up, meaning customers will have to use branches on Yarm Road, Skerne Park, and Haughton-Le-Skerne for a period of time.
