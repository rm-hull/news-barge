A pothole has appeared at Bishop Auckland's new £11.8m bus station just months after it opened.

The bus station on Saddler Street officially opened in May after more than two years.

Work on the site began in February 2024 as part of wider regeneration plans for the town centre, funded through the government's Future High Streets Fund.

Durham County Council has now confirmed it is investigating a failure in the road surface after the defect emerged.

Rachael Smith, the council's section manager for transport and infrastructure, said the authority was aware of the issue and had already begun looking into the cause.

She said: "We are aware of the defect to the road surface at Bishop Auckland Bus Station.

![]()The pothole appeared just three months after the £11.8m bus station opened. *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

"Inspections by our external contractors confirmed that the material used for the road was more than adequate to withstand the heavy vehicle use it would be subjected to.

"Therefore, further investigations are now underway to identify the source of the failure, and we will continue to work closely with our partners to repair it as quickly as possible."

The council said investigations had ruled out concerns about the quality of the road-building materials and were now focused on identifying what caused the surface to fail.

Ms Smith added: "The building is still under a construction warranty period, so any costs of repair would fall to the contractor responsible for the defect."

The appearance of the defect comes only months after the long-awaited transport hub welcomed its first passengers.

No timescale has yet been given for the completion of repairs, with investigations into the cause continuing.
