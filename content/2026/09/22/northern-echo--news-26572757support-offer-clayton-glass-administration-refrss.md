Businesses affected by the collapse of Clayton Glass are being offered support after administrators laid off more than 150 staff at the County Durham factory.

All staff at the glass manufacturer's factory on the Harelaw Industrial Estate in Stanley were made redundant after administrators announced the closure of sites in County Durham, Lancashire and Scotland.

The move came days after workers were told they would not receive their wages and that the company had filed a notice of intention to appoint an administrator.

Now, Durham County Council has announced measures aimed not only at former Clayton Glass employees but also companies in the manufacturer's supply chain.

A council spokesperson said: "When a large firm goes into administration, it's not just the employees that work for the company that are affected.

"That's why, along with the support we are offering to Clayton Glass employees through our Employability Durham service, we are also reaching out to firms in the company's wider supply chain."

County Durham businesses that supplied or worked with Clayton Glass have been urged to contact Business Durham for assistance.

The support available includes help exploring new contracts, markets and supply chain opportunities, access to technical advice such as digital health checks, energy audits and financial guidance, and a range of free workshops and webinars.

These cover topics including business strategy, costing and pricing, marketing, winning new customers and growing sales.

Businesses seeking support can contact Business Durham on 03000 261261 or by emailing [enquiries@businessdurham.co.uk.](mailto:enquiries@businessdurham.co.uk.)

## Support sessions for workers

The council has also announced dedicated support sessions for workers who lost their jobs following the closure.

Employability Durham will run free drop-in sessions in Stanley offering advice on finding work, training opportunities and available support.

The first session will take place at The Venue, Stanley, on Wednesday, September 23, from 10am until 2.30pm.

A second event will be held at Elevate Hub, Louisa Centre, Stanley, on Thursday, September 24, also from 10am until 2.30pm.

Support will include help with CVs and job applications, training and skills development, finding new employment, confidence building and planning future career options.

People unable to attend can contact Employability Durham on 03000 262009.

## Factory closure

The support package follows the collapse of Clayton Glass, which had operated since 1956.

One employee, who asked not to be named, said around 160 workers were employed at the Stanley factory and described the mood among staff as "horrible".

He said employees felt "hard done to" and were worried about paying bills and supporting their families.

Councillor Karen Allison, who represents Annfield Plain and Tanfield, had earlier warned that the impact would extend beyond the workforce.

She said: "It doesn't just affect those employees, it also affects the supply chain.

"You probably have companies now that are going to be struggling because of what's happened here.

"It's going to have a devastating effect on the area as a whole."
