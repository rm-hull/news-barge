A dedicated community action group has issued a stark warning to the Home Office, claiming that an ongoing feasibility study into utilising the former **RAF Linton-on-Ouse base** risks repeating a multi-million-pound taxpayer disaster due to insurmountable rural recruitment barriers.

The intervention from the **Linton-on-Ouse Action Group** highlights an identical staffing crisis currently unfolding at the nearby**£400 million HMP Millsike “super-prison”** near York. Due to acute officer shortages, the recently opened facility has been forced to leave roughly**350 cell spaces entirely empty and closed** because it cannot attract enough personnel to run the wings safely.

The group warns that because the Linton-on-Ouse site is even more rural and suffers from severely restricted public transport infrastructure, any attempt to revive it will trigger a worse recruitment failure, potentially escalating the regional prison staffing crisis by draining the exact same local labour pool.

> **Olga Matthias, Spokesperson for the Action Group, said:**
>
> The Home Office must explain why lessons from 2022 have been ignored and they continue with their feasibility study wasting millions more taxpayer funds. With HMP Millsike already unable to staff its cells and Linton being more rural, recruitment barriers are the same as they ever were, if not worse – just insanity!

## The Legacy of Sunk Taxpayer Costs

The current friction marks a tense escalation for the small North Yorkshire village, which successfully fought off initial Home Office plans in 2022 to turn the former airbase into a major holding centre. A look back at the financial ledger of that aborted project reveals a significant history of state waste:

* **The Original Mobilisation Sunk Cost:** In 2022, the Home Office paid private contractor Serco approximately**£1.5 million** purely for initial site mobilisation, which covered rapid recruitment drives, training protocols, staff salaries, and baseline project management before the scheme was abruptly cancelled.
* **The Total Aborted Bill:** When asset management, structural adjustments, and legal wind-downs were finalised, the total sunk taxpayer loss for the aborted 2022 site approached**£2.9 million**.
* **The Full Aborted Liability:** The original commercial frame agreement handed to Serco had been initially valued at up to**£32.8 million** over a two-year operational window before local public resistance forced the government to drop the gates.

## Insurmountable Rural Labour Shortages

The primary operational bottleneck facing the Home Office’s renewed feasibility plans is the highly restricted nature of the North Yorkshire labour market. Data compiled by regional economic analysts highlights a tight employment landscape that makes mass recruitment for low-wage, high-stress roles mathematically improbable:

* **Extreme Low Unemployment:** General unemployment across North Yorkshire sits at a record low of roughly**2.2%**, floating significantly below the wider UK national average of** 3.4% to 4.1%**.
* **The Claimant Deficit:** The regional out-of-work claimant rate is locked at a minor**2.0% to 2.15%**, leaving businesses with virtually zero local labour reserve to draw from.
* **Youth Labour Constraints:** Local youth unemployment (ages 18–24) is squeezed at**3.87%**, compared to a much wider national youth baseline of** 5.95%**.

Compounding the problem are the historical parameters of the roles themselves. Past recruitment drives for the Linton-on-Ouse installation described demanding frontline positions involving urgent medical responses, suicide risk management, anti-social behaviour mitigation, and potential deaths. Despite these severe operational pressures, the roles were ultimately re-advertised at basic minimum wage.

Because the site features zero local amenities and very limited public transport networks, applicants are legally required to own a private vehicle simply to commute to the base. The Action Group notes that Mitie—the private operator running HMP Millsike—is also one of the primary accommodation providers positioned to bid for the Linton base contract. If the base moves forward, the two massive facilities will actively cannibalise the same regional pool of car-owning, minimum-wage applicants, leaving local prison wings empty and base staffing lines dangerously compromised.
