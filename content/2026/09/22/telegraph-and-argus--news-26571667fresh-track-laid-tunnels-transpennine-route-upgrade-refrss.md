Fresh tracks are being laid through a pair of historic railway tunnels in West Yorkshire as part of a huge railway upgrade.

The work in the Grade II-listed Gledholt Tunnels, just west of Huddersfield Station, is part of the multi-billion pound Transpennine Route Upgrade (TRU).

Historic England said the north portal was built in 1845-1849 during the "heroic age of railway building" to the design of engineer Alfred Stanistreet Jee, with the south portal added in 1882-1886 under engineer J J Lee.

A 16-day closure of the railway line through Huddersfield is due to end on Sunday, October 4, with rail replacement buses running in the meantime.

A TRU spokesperson said: "Over the weekend, TRU teams were busy renewing track through Huddersfield and Gledholt tunnels, which will support more frequent, faster and more reliable journeys for customers."

![]()New tracks being installed in Gledholt Tunnel *(Image: TRU)*

"These upgrades will help keep trains running reliably and improve journeys for years to come. Thank you for your patience and understanding during this period while we invest in a better railway."

The current phase of works also involves further construction at Huddersfield Station, where new canopies are being installed to shelter passengers using the extra platforms that are being built. The station's historic Euston-style roof is also undergoing a painstaking restoration, as reported previously by the Telegraph & Argus.

![]()The work taking place at Gledholt Tunnel *(Image: Sarah Bell)*

Further east, major work is ongoing to reconfigure Deighton Station to accommodate the new four-track layout through the area. Whitacre Street, near the station, remains closed while the remaining work on a replacement bridge is completed.

The current line closure is presenting the chance for further worj to be carried out on the new footbridge at Deighton Station, which will include lifts to provide step-free platform access.

![]()Work taking place beneath Huddersfield Station's listed Euston-style roof canopy *(Image: TRU)*

On the A62 Leeds Road, between Deighton and Bradley, the old overbridge is set to be demolished as it was too low for overhead line equipment needed for electrification to pass beneath. Traffic has already been diverted onto a section of new bridge in a bid to minimise disruption.

The TRU has already delivered significant improvements, with reconstructed stations at Morley and Mirfield being among the most significant changes.

The work at Ravensthorpe, near Dewsbury, has been particularly noteworthy, with the new Baker Viaduct under construction. A new grade-separated fly-over junction is also being built near the site of the now-closed station, which is being reconstructed about 200 metres to the west of its former location.
