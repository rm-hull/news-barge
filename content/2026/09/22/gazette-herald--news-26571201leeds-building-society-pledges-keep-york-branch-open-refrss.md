A major building society has pledged to keep its York branch open for at least the next five years.

Leeds Building Society has announced that the branch at 49 York Road, Acomb, will remain open until at least September 2031.

A similar commitment has been made concerning the Leeds branch at 12 Oxford Street, Harrogate

The commitment is part of a new set of promises aimed at providing long-term certainty to members.

Graham Binns, head of Leeds Building Society’s branch network, said: "We know that for many people in York having access to a local branch still matters.

"Whether it's opening a savings account, getting help to protect themselves from fraud, or simply speaking to someone face-to-face, our branches provide a service that many members value.

"That's why we're making a clear commitment that our York branch will remain open until at least September 2031.

"Alongside that, we're making a series of wider promises that reward loyalty, provide greater certainty and ensure members can continue to access our services in the way that suits them best."

The promise is part of a broader pledge to keep all 51 of the society’s branches open across the UK until at least September 2031.

Branches will continue to operate every weekday from 9.30am to 4.45pm without the need for an appointment.

Additional customer commitments include ensuring members can always speak to someone in person or over the phone, and not offering savings accounts exclusively to new customers.

Existing savers will continue to have access to exclusive opportunities, such as the newly launched Branch Regular Saver account, which offers an interest rate of 6.5 per cent.

Leeds Building Society has also committed to aim to pay more than the market average savings rate.

Over the past decade, it has delivered an average rate of 2.26 per cent compared to the market average of 1.49 per cent.

Members will be offered the best available rate on their chosen account at maturity.

If instructed, the society will lock in the rate or switch to a new issue of the same account type if the rate increases before the new account begins.

The society said these promises reflect its long-term commitment to high streets and local communities.

Despite the shift towards digital banking, its data shows that branches remain important to members of all ages.

In the past year, 29 per cent of savings accounts were opened in-branch, and nearly one in ten branch users were aged 30 or under.

The branch network also delivers community support through services such as free fraud clinics.

Leeds Building Society plans to review its savings promises in 12 months, but the branch commitments will stay in place until at least September 2031.
