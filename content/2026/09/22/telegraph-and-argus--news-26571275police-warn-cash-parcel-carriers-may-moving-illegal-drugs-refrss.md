GULLIBLE students are being recruited to act as unwitting drug mules, West Yorkshire Police has warned.

Criminals are recruiting people to move illegal goods without attracting attention. Students and vulnerable people who need extra money may be targeted and offered payment for taking packages on someone else’s behalf.

The parcels could contain illegal drugs, offensive weapons or stolen goods, police said. Those involved could face criminal offences including possessing controlled drugs, handing stolen goods or money laundering.

Chief Inspector Michelle Swift said: "What may seem like an easy favour or a way to get quick cash, it could leave you facing a police investigation and a criminal record."

Recruitment often takes place through social media platforms including Snapchat, Instagram, TikTok and Facebook.

Police urged anyone who feels uncomfortable about an offer to trust their instincts and report it. The force encouraged parents to discuss the trend and its risks with university-age children.

Chief Inspector Swift said: "We hope by raising awareness of this type of crime, people will be alert to the risks and tactics used by organised criminals. We would particularly encourage parents to have this conversation with their university age children to make them understand this type of trend and the dangers that are associated with it."

People who believe they have been targeted can contact police through Live Chat at www.westyorkshire.police.uk/livechat or by calling 101.

Anyone reporting a crime in progress or danger to life should call 999. Crimestoppers can be contacted anonymously on 0800 555 111.
