A drug dealer caught with heroin and crack in his pants and more than £1,000 in his car has been jailed for almost three years.

Bradford Crown Court heard how police spotted Sohiel Shabir with a passenger in a Seat Leon parked up on Kirkwall Drive on April 3 last year.

As they watched, they saw a woman, who they knew to be a Class A drug user, lean in to the driver's window, which indicated to them that a drug deal was taking place.

Shabir and his passenger were detained for a stop and search.

In the car police found £1,115 in a wallet in the driver's door pocket.

Shabir, who was at the wheel, also had two mobile phones: an iPhone and a Nokia.

Both he and his passenger were arrested on suspicion of being concerned in the supply of crack cocaine.

Both were later strip-searched, and Shabir was found with a clear plastic bag in his scrotum containing two wraps of crack cocaine and heroin.

In an interview he answered ‘no comment’ to questions except to confirm that the car was his and that drugs he had on him were for his own personal use.

He said the cash found in the car was from selling mobile phones and nothing to do with drugs sales.

Police discovered that both mobile phones contained messages relating to the sale of drugs.

The Nokia appeared to be operating a drugs line.

Charges were dropped against Shabir’s passenger, but he was described in court as ‘a trusted holder of the drugs line.’

Shabir, 42, of Great Horton Road, Bradford, later pleaded guilty to being concerned in the supply of cocaine, and possession of both heroin and crack cocaine with intent to supply.

He had no previous convictions.

In mitigation the court heard that he had been a man with a solid work ethic who had formed a relationship with a drug addict and had become an addict himself, which resulted in his owing a drug debt.

As a result, he had lost his business, his reputation, and his relationship with his children.

The court heard: ‘He plainly needs help with his addiction. He can be a productive member of society.’

Giving evidence to the court, Shabir said the drugs found in his pants were for his own use and that he ‘just put them down my pants’ when he was caught by police.

He described the wraps as ‘one of each for myself’ and added: ‘I didn’t want them to take them off me.’

He said the cash found in the car had been earned by selling phones through his mobile phone and vape shop, which he was planning to use to buy stock.

He said he had been selling drugs for ‘a week, tops – just to pay my debt off.’

‘I was getting threatened to pay my bill off. I got battered once. I just had to do it [deal drugs].’

He said he had earned around £80 on the day he was stopped by police.

Sentencing Shabir to a total of 32 months’ imprisonment, His Honour Judge Ahmed Nadim said the messages on his phones were consistent with drugs supply and bulk advertising.

He told Shabir: ‘Class A drugs cause immense harm not only to those who become dependent on them.

‘The supply of crack cocaine and heroin fuels addiction, ill health, and social deprivation.’

He added: ‘Your offending is so serious that only a custodial sentence is justified.

‘The offending you were engaged in inflicts immense harm on individuals and communities and the message must go out that those that offend in this manner will be met with condign punishment.’

He said Shabir would serve up to half of the sentence in custody before being released on licence.

Judge Nadim ordered the forfeiture and destruction of the drugs, mobiles, and drugs paraphernalia seized by police.
