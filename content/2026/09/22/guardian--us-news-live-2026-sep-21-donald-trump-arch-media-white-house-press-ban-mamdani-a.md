Trump braces for loss in lawsuit brought by media organizations, expects to 'go for appeal'

Donald Trump is bracing for a loss in the lawsuit brought by three media organizations, as he considers that the judge presiding over the case – his own appointee – previously ordered the White House to restore credentials for journalist Jim Acosta.

“They have drawn a great Judge, for them, a man who ruled in favor of Jim Acosta, who now seems to have disappeared from the Planet,” Trump wrote on Truth Social, referring to a lawsuit filed by CNN in 2018. “The Judge’s name is Tim Kelly, and he was, sadly, appointed by ‘TRUMP.’ In other words, almost without question and, as usual, we’ll go for appeal because Fake News people and publications that only write negatively, and who violate our National Security by writing false and defamatory stories with unknown ‘sources,’ shouldn’t be allowed access to the most important Office anywhere in the World.”

A long-running legal fight over Missouri’s congressional map took another turn Monday when a federal appeals panel ruled in favor of new districts backed by Donald Trump, even though the US supreme court has blocked those districts from being used in the November election, the AP reports.

A three-judge panel of the eighth US circuit court of appeals said Missouri should use the new Republican-drawn US House districts in the November election, just as the state did in its August primary. To revert to its prior districts for the general election would violate the US constitution, the appeals judges said.

However, the ruling has no immediate impact. That’s because the federal appellate panel delayed the effect of its order for a week to allow time for an appeal. The US supreme court already has twice sided against the Trump-backed map and had blocked enforcement of a lower court order favoring the map pending a potential appeal and eventual decision by the high court.

Missouri is currently set to run its November election with the same congressional districts it used in 2024 and 2022, following prior decisions by both the Missouri supreme court and the US supreme court.

US Senators Alex Padilla and Adam Schiff are asking California attorney general Rob Bonta to look into the claims of a whistleblower alleging the Department of Homeland Security isordering officers to impersonate voters to access online voter data.

According to the senators, officers in a unit of the US Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS), an agency within the department, are being tasked with investigating 40 subjects per day.

The whistleblower claims that officers were instructed to pull citizens’ personal data from federal databases and enter it into the state website to gain access.

“The Trump Administration appears to be undertaking this rushed effort to find – or manufacture – evidence for its baseless election conspiracy theories of widespread voter fraud ahead of the 2026 midterm elections,” reads a letter by the senators. “Inaccurate claims of ‘unlawful voters’ and erroneous federal law enforcement records can result in serious consequences for Californians, including many naturalized citizens.”

“The Trump Administration may also use these claims to further threaten state and local election officials in California and undermine or challenge the election results themselves,” the senators added in the letter.

The US is planning to reopen a former cold war-era ⁠military base in southern Greenland and set up a military presence at an east coast base, Reuters reports.

Sources told the wire that an agreement is expected to be signed by the US, Denmark and Greenland on Tuesday at the United Nations general assembly in New ‌York City.

One of these military bases, Mestersvig, ⁠is currently used by the Sirius Dog Sled Patrol. The other post is Narsarsuaq, where the population has dwindled to a few dozen people since the US closed its Bluie West One military base in the 1950s.

The agreement follows the announcement of an Arctic security pact between the US and the leaders of Denmark and Greenland, following pressure from Trump to annex the semi-autonomous Danish territory by force.

In his remarks today, New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani said he shared “very strong opinions” on “how we can keep immigrant New Yorkers safe” during his meeting with Donald Trump, especially regarding temporary protected status (TPS) for immigrants in the United States.

Mamdani said he told the US president “how important immigrant New Yorkers, especially Haitian New Yorkers, are not only to the fabric of our city, but also to our city’s economy, to so many of the industries that build our city, and the stability that is in the president’s hands”.

In June, the US supreme court ruled in favor of the Trump administration’s effort to strip TPS from hundreds of thousands of Haitians and Syrians.

“It’s interesting because they said they were going to boycott me, but they never boycott me, so I wasn’t very worried about that,” Trump said.

In response to a question about whether the ban was put in place as a distraction from the war in Iran and high gas prices, Trump said: “I’m not.”

“I’m only doing it because I think the press also has an obligation,” Trump said. “I think that these outlets have an obligation to be fair. And if they’re fake news, I really think I have an obligation not to allow them into another very special house. This is a special house, Gracie Mansion. Well, the White House is the most special of all houses.”

New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani and President Donald Trump spoke ahead of the UN General Assembly this week following a closed-door meeting at Gracie Mansion on Monday.

“One of the focuses of the conversation the president and I have been having is about the possibility of building more housing here in New York City,” Mamdani said.

“We’ve spoken about Sunnyside Yard,” he added, referring to the 180-acre railyard in Queens, “and it’s a real pleasure that just today we’ve agreed to set up a new level of that conversation between our housing and operations team at City Hall and the White House team.”

“I can tell you that Sunnyside’s a big job,” Trump said. “I’ve been watching that for 35 years. Everybody’s wanted to do something with Sunnyside Yards.”

“You’re going to need a lot of federal approvals and probably some federal money to do it. But it’s always been a great location,” Trump added.

The New York Timessaid it would hold off on publishing or distributing still photographs taken as part of the White House pool coverage on Monday until midnight, joining a collective effort with photographers in other newsrooms in support of the media organizations that were banned from the White House.

“The New York Times will not publish or distribute any images taken as part of the White House still photography pool today, in conjunction with the other members of that pool and in a show of support for the news organizations being unfairly targeted by the president’s barring of journalists from the White House,” said Charlie Stadtlander, a spokesperson for the news outlet, in a statement.

“We remain committed to covering the president’s travel to New York City today,” Stadtlander added. “Americans deserve a free and independent media covering their government without intimidation, as well as comprehensive coverage of newsworthy events.”

Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez, the Venezuelan man who was shot by an ICE officer in Austin, spoke over the phone at a news conference, offering further details on his condition.

The 28-year-old was taken to a hospital in Austin, Texas, in serious but stable condition before being taken into custody.

The Associated Press reports that, speaking by phone during a news conference, Garcés Pérez said doctors didn’t remove the bullet because it’s near his spine.

He said ICE pressed for him to be released quickly from the hospital so officers could move him to a detention center. He said he had received no pain medication or treatment there, and had slept on the floor before eventually being given a “pill” by a nurse on Monday.

“I am not doing well due to the gunshot wound, and I have not received the medical attention that I require,” Perez said, speaking in Spanish over the phone as his wife translated during the news conference.
