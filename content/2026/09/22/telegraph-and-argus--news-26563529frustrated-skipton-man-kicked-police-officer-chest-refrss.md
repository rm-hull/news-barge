A SKIPTON man who kicked a police officer in the chest has escaped a community sentence but will have to pay £1477 in a fine, costs and compensation.

Christopher Johnson, 36, had been arrested on an un-related matter in Skipton on August 22 after which he had become obstructive, heard Skipton Magistrates Court on Friday.

He started shouting as he was taken by officers to the police van, saying he would 'kick the s\*\*\*' out of them' before kicking a police sergeant.

He also kicked at the cage in the van, causing the door to fly open, the court was told.

Johnson, of Willow Way, Skipton pleaded guilty to the assault by beating of a police officer acting in the exercise of his functions of an emergency worker.

The court heard in mitigation that Johnson was full of remorse for what he had done.

He had called the police about a domestic incident that was 'in the making', but it had all been a misunderstanding, the court was told by his advocate.

Johnson, who had been in drink, had become frustrated and had kicked the cage in the van, but kicking the police officer had not been intentional.

A self employed renderer, Johnson was expecting his first child with his partner, the court was told.

The Magistrates Bench chairman told Johnson assaulting someone whose job it was to protect the public was not acceptable.

But, the bench did not think his was a case that needed the intervention of the probation service.

Instead, he was fined £923 and ordered to pay a surcharge of £369 and costs of £85. He will also have to pay compensation of £100 to the police officer.
