Residents living near the closing Clayton Glass factory have spoken of their shock and sympathy for workers after the company collapsed, leaving hundreds jobless.

Administrators confirmed on Tuesday (September 22) that 290 jobs had been lost at Clayton Glass sites in County Durham, Lancashire and Scotland after the firm filed a notice of intention to appoint administrators the previous week.

The Stanley site on Harelaw Industrial Estate employed about 160 people, who were told on payday, Friday, September 18, that they would not be paid.

![]() *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Administrators have moved into the site to wind down the business.

The closures have alarmed residents, who said the factory had long been a major local employer.

![]() *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

A man who lives opposite the site said he was stunned by the scale of the job losses.

He said: "That's mental because so many people work there. It's always so busy."

A neighbour added: "I feel bad for them. I didn't realise they were told when they turned up. That's terrible."

![]() *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

A third resident highlighted concerns about the challenges facing those now searching for work.

She said: "That's such a shame. It's so hard for people to find a job at the moment as well."

Another local added: "It's awful. The place is always so busy. I just hope they can all find jobs now."

Politicians warned that the collapse would hit Stanley and surrounding communities.

![]() *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Annfield Plain and Tanfield councillor Darren Grimes called the redundancies a devastating blow.

He said: "Clayton Glass. Founded 1956. Survived everything for 70 years. Couldn't survive Britain's energy bills.

"The administrators said it plainly: high energy costs helped finish it off. 290 redundancies, and County Durham is one of the sites closing.

"Ed Miliband gets his green halo. Families in my ward get the dole queue.

"I am genuinely disgusted by those who purport to be the 'party of the working class'."

Ward colleague Karen Allison offered support to affected families and warned that local businesses could also suffer.

She said: "Obviously our thoughts go out to the families.

"It doesn't just affect those employees, it also affects the supply chain.

"You probably have companies now that are going to be struggling because of what's happened here.

"It's going to have a devastating effect on the area as a whole."

North Durham MP Luke Akehurst said he was “devastated” by the job losses and urged affected workers to contact him if they needed help.

The company said high energy and raw material costs, combined with prolonged low demand, had left it unable to meet its bills.

James Lumb, Interpath managing director and the company’s joint administrator, said Clayton Glass had a “proud history” but faced the same economic pressures as many housing-market businesses.

Employees previously told The Northern Echo they arrived at work expecting answers about the firm's future, only to discover they would not be paid.

One worker described the mood as “horrible”, saying many staff had families and bills to support while others had spent decades at the factory.

He said workers had accepted pay cuts to keep the company going, with factory staff taking seven per cent and office staff ten per cent.

Durham County Council said affected workers facing immediate hardship may be able to get help through its Crisis and Resilience Fund.
