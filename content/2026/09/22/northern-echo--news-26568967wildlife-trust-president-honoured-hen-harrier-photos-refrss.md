A North East wildlife charity is celebrating after its president won a major international photography prize for a powerful series highlighting one of the UK's most persecuted birds of prey.

Conrad Dickinson, president of Northumberland Wildlife Trust, won the Conservation Award in the 2026 Bird Photography the Year competition for his Hen Harrier Story, a documentary-style photo series focused on the hen harrier.

The series, captured in England, tells the story of one of the UK's most persecuted birds of prey and the ongoing conservation efforts to protect it.

![]()Sophisticated monitoring devices, cameras and night vision binoculars have been developed in this fight against persecution. This image shows one of the satellite ariels on one of the hen harrier chicks *(Image: Conrad Dickinson)*

Mr Dickinson said: "I'm delighted to have won the award, although first and foremost my photographs are all about promoting nature conservation and encouraging people to take action, rather than winning prize money and trophies."

The competition received more than 24,000 entries from photographers around the world.

Born and raised in Northumberland, Mr Dickinson is a retired polar explorer who has spent the last 20 years documenting his travels through photography.

In recent years, he has focused increasingly on wildlife photography.

![]()As part of the protection of hen harriers, when they are just about to leave the nest, the chicks have a DNA sample taken that both helps to record the breeding linage of the bird, but also helps in case there is any future persecution *(Image: Conrad Dickinson)*

He has previously received commended and highly commended honours in the International Bird Photographer of the Year competition, and in 2021 won first prize in the Share the View International Photography Competition for Diving Gannet, an image taken in Shetland in 2019.

Mr Dickinson now dedicates much of his time to supporting Northumberland Wildlife Trust’s campaign to raise £30 million to purchase the Rothbury Estate.

Earlier this year, he also narrated a film showcasing the natural beauty and ecological value of the area.

![]()Conrad Dickinson *(Image: The Wildlife Trusts)*
