PRIDE IN PLACE is a really interesting government initiative to get investment into the grassroots of our communities.

Rather than creating big, shiny buildings, which are often a very visible, headline-grabbing legacy of government regeneration programmes, this scheme is about bolstering initiatives, ideas and events at a community level.

Darlington is the latest town in the North East to benefit from £20m over 10 years.

Spennymoor, Peterlee, Stanley, Crook, Middlesbrough, Redcar and Stockton are also getting the funding which is, rightly, being focused on the poorer areas.

In Darlington, where the gap in life expectancy between the better-off and the poorer communities is as big as anywhere in the country, that means Skerne Park, Bank Top, Northgate, Branksome and Red Hall will see the money – and these areas are small enough for the money to really be seen.

There are to be youth projects as well as local transport fixes, and because Pride in Place is locally orientated, people will have a say in how the money is spent.

Pride in Place also needs to go some way to changing the way we see ourselves.

There is a narrative at the moment that Britain is broken, but that is not true of so many of our communities where there are brilliant initiatives going on that are unheralded and unnoticed.

These initiatives are arranged by local people for the benefit of local people and they bring the local community together – and there would be more of them if the communities got more support.

We look forward to seeing pride put back into our towns because we know, for all their difficulties, our local communities are, at heart, great places to live and work.
