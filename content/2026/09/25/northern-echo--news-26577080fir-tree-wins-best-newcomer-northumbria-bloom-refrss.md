Residents are celebrating after their County Durham community won Best Newcomer at a regional gardening competition, following a year spent transforming the area.

Fir Tree won the award at the Northumbria in Bloom 2026 ceremony at The Fed in Gateshead on September 16.

The village impressed judges on its first attempt and picked up a string of other awards.

Sonya Bree, chair of the Fir Tree and Harperley Residents Association, said: "We are absolutely chuffed to bits with the awards.

"We had a vision, the community came together and look what we achieved.

"Anyone that knows us knows we don’t do things by halves.

"Thank you so much to all the volunteers who took time out of their day to tend to the flowers.

"It really did pay off."

The village also received a glass trophy and a £60 cheque.

Fir Tree was represented at the ceremony by Sonya Bree, association secretary Karen Fatkin, resident and volunteer Steve Hodgson, and Duke of York landlady Imogen Bowater.

The win followed months of teamwork and dedication.

Residents and volunteers improved the village by picking litter, caring for verges and pavements, adopting flower tubs, and supporting events including the Village Fair and Easter Eggstravaganza.

Their efforts earned Fir Tree Silver Gilt in the Village category, Silver for the Duke of York in the Pubs category, Bronze for Marie’s Garden in Private Gardens, and Silver for Pea Hill in Parks.

Fir Tree also won Silver Gilt for Elliot’s Conservation Project and Gold for the Fir Tree Cenotaph in the War Memorials category.

Steve Hodgson, who led the planting and watering and designed the award-winning cenotaph layout, attended without expecting to win but was blown away by the results.

He said he was immensely proud of what the community had achieved through its hard work and dedication.

Ms Bree said the awards meant more than recognition for floral displays.

She said the competition united the community behind a shared goal and gave residents the chance to improve and take pride in their surroundings.

The Northumbria in Bloom entry was part of a wider effort to improve life in Fir Tree, with other projects under way to bring residents together.
