A North East victim of sexual assault has spoken of the devastating impact an attack has had on her life as the man responsible begins a prison sentence.

Harry Gray, 24, of Dawes Street, Gillingham, Rochester, was jailed for five years at Newcastle Crown Court on Thursday after being found guilty of sexually assaulting a secondary-school-aged girl, in South Tyneside.

The victim, who was assaulted in her sleep before being physically overpowered, has shared the lasting trauma caused by Gray’s actions.

She said: "I have had entire days when I don’t even feel real and past depressive episodes so severe that I’ve had suicidal thoughts and even made plans on so many occasions.

"I have had an intense fear of intimacy since my brain now sees it as a violent act and not a loving one.

"I’ve held such high standards for who can be in my life that it is just easier to be alone. I have been so scared to be taken advantage of again or being reduced to an object for someone else’s use, rather than being seen as a person."

She described the toll the assault has taken on her ambitions and daily life.

She said: "Taking it one day at a time has been my way of dealing and having to live with this, but this way of coping doesn’t work in any other factors of life.

"I am a real person with a soul and dreams, but both of these have been permanently altered because of what happened.

"I had so many plans for my life and so many dreams I could have achieved had I not been so depressed.

"I’ve lost all enjoyment in most things I used to do or those I’m expected to enjoy because I’m too afraid."

The victim also described ongoing anxiety and fear.

She said: "The anxiety I face every day is overwhelming.

"I’m too scared to grow as a person and leave my comfort zone, questioning whether this will happen to me again, runs through my mind time after time.

"I’ve held myself back from opportunities and cost myself so many new experiences, friends and relationships."

She said even basic interactions and activities are now shadowed by fear.

She said: "I hold back from going to new places and speaking to new people, as not knowing the people around me and what they are capable of is always on my mind.

"The energy I am using worrying about what someone could do to me takes so much life out of me.

"The excitement I had to learn and experience new things has gone."

She said the pain is constant, even on better days.

She said: "The constant feeling of sadness and shame is something that has not got any easier and I have just found better ways to mask it.

"I have good days every now and then where I go hours or even the whole day without thinking about it.

"I start to think maybe I’m getting better and it’s over, but then there is always an end to that day, and I am back in my bedroom, alone, wishing that he had just killed me, instead of making this a lifelong ordeal I must deal with."

Gray denied wrongdoing in police interviews but was charged after a complex investigation by Northumbria Police’s Safeguarding Department.

A jury found him guilty of one count of sexual assault at trial.

Detective Constable Kerry Kane, who led the investigation, said: "It’s clear from the words shared by the victim-survivor that Harry Gray’s crimes have impacted her enormously.

As part of his sentence, Gray will remain on the Sex Offenders’ Register for life.
