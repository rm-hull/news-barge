Diesel prices are nearing record highs, sparking fresh calls for an extension and increase in the fuel duty cut to ease pressure on Durham drivers.

The average price of diesel rose to 196.1p per litre today, up from 195.2p before the weekend and just below the all-time high of 199.1p recorded in July 2022.

Petrol prices also climbed, reaching 172.4p per litre, up from 171.9p.

## Diesel prices rising to near all-time high

Luke Bosdet, road fuel price spokesman for the AA, said: "Every 6p increase in the pump price of fuel includes an extra penny in VAT for the Treasury.

"Petrol currently averages around 172p a litre, with diesel above 196p.

"This time last year, they averaged 135p and 142p respectively.

"The extra 37p on petrol therefore includes a 6p-a-litre boost to VAT receipts, while diesel adds 9p a litre to the tax haul."

The AA has renewed calls for the Government to extend and increase the current fuel duty cut, warning that small businesses and rural diesel users are feeling the most pressure.

Mr Bosdet said: "There would be a good argument for diesel getting further relief as it nears the July 2022 all-time record of 199.1p a litre.

"While many big businesses pass the extra cost of diesel onto their customers, often via a sliding scale of fuel surcharges, smaller businesses don’t automatically.

"These include taxi drivers, craftspeople, domestic repairs, vets, farriers, and many who rely on vehicles to look after their customers."

He also highlighted the impact on rural communities.

Mr Bosdet said: "Diesel is heavily used by rural communities who already face big road fuel bills because of the longer distances they have to drive.

"Often with lower wages, they need all the help they can get with road fuel costs."
