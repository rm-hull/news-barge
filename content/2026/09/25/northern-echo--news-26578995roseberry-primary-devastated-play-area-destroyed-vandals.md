A County Durham headteacher has spoken of her devastation after vandals destroyed an award-winning play area which staff and families had spent years creating.

Outdoor equipment at Roseberry Primary and Nursery School, near Chester-le-Street, was smashed beyond use after people gained access to the school field.

The damage included the destruction of a mud kitchen, mini golf course, play baths, fire pit, storage box and toys used in the digging area.

Staff believe golf clubs were taken from a smashed storage box and then used to destroy other equipment. One club was found on the school field, while another was later discovered bent in the village by the school’s litter picking group.

Headteacher Emma Dunn said staff were “absolutely devastated” when they discovered the damage.

Durham Police confirmed an investigation is ongoing.

![]()Damage of equipment at Roseberry Primary and Nursery School

Mrs Dunn said: “We were just absolutely devastated on behalf of everyone who has put so much hard work into developing the play area.

“Our play team has worked on it during school holidays, half terms and after school. A lot of the equipment was donated by families and members of the community, so many people have played a part in creating it.

“The children absolutely love the field and all the opportunities they have to play outside in a productive way. To see everything smashed up after so much work has gone into it has just devastated us.”

The school began developing its outdoor spaces around three years ago.

It achieved the outdoor play and learning programme’s highest Platinum award.

The field is used several times each week and allows pupils from Reception to Year 6 to play together. Mrs Dunn said it had helped children build friendships across different age groups, with older pupils often teaching younger children how to play.

![]()Damage of equipment at Roseberry Primary and Nursery School

She said: “Children spend so little time outside now because, when they are at home, they can be behind a screen much of the time.

“We wanted to encourage them to be outside as much as possible and make the best of our extensive school field by creating opportunities that would make them want to play outdoors.

“All of our children play together. The older ones teach the younger ones, brothers and sisters can spend time together and children who might not otherwise have played with each other build relationships.

“Those relationships then continue outside school in the community. It has had such a positive impact on our pupils, which makes what has happened even more upsetting.

“At the end of the day, it is our children who are suffering and missing out.

![]()Damage of equipment at Roseberry Primary and Nursery School

“That is ultimately why we go to work every day, to make our school the best place it can be for them. It is really sad that all the hard work which was put in has been reduced to this.”

A fundraiser launched on September 23 to rebuild the play areas has already raised £4,080 towards a £4,500 target. You can donate by visiting gofundme.com/f/roseberry-opal.

Mrs Dunn said the response had shown the school it was not facing the damage alone.

She said: “Everyone has been really saddened that such mindless vandalism has taken place at a primary school. It is completely senseless and nobody gains anything from smashing a mud kitchen to pieces.

“The support makes us feel that we are not on our own and that everyone who is part of our community wants to help rebuild it.

“School budgets are stretched so tightly and we do not have spare funding available to replace everything. Without the fundraiser, we would not have had a budget to rebuild what has been lost.

“We have also received messages from people whose children previously attended the school and who remember how beneficial these opportunities were. It means a lot to know how deeply people care.”

Mrs Dunn, who has worked at the school since 1998 and has been headteacher for five years, said she hoped the area could return even better than before.

A Durham Police spokesperson said: “Police have received a report of damage being caused to outdoor play equipment at Roseberry Primary School, in Chester-le-Street.

“It is believed to have happened between Sunday, September 20 and Tuesday, September 22. Enquiries are ongoing."

Anyone with any information is asked to call Durham Police on 101 quoting incident reference number 511.
