Fresh concerns have been raised over proposed changes to night flight rules.

Leeds Bradford Airport (LBA) is seeking to update planning conditions that limit night-time operations, aiming to modernise its approach by introducing a quota count system (QCS) that would place a cap on noise rather than flight numbers.

The airport says the system would encourage airlines to use quieter, more efficient aircraft.

![]()Leeds Bradford Airport at night *(Image: UGC)*

It is also proposing to shift night-time hours to run from 11.30pm to 5.59am and introduce a noise insulation scheme to support nearby residents.

Vincent Hodder, CEO of Leeds Bradford Airport, said: "Leeds Bradford Airport is transforming into a modern gateway for Yorkshire and a catalyst for regional growth.

"Through significant private investment, industry-leading sustainability initiatives and a commitment to supporting local communities, we are building an airport that is fit for the future.

"This is about creating a stronger, more sustainable and better-connected Yorkshire for the decades ahead. This is not the 'thousands more night flights' some would claim.

"The QCS LBA is applying for would match the existing noise footprint.

"The same noise limits, allocated differently."

However, environmental campaigners and local councillors have voiced strong objections.

Nick Hodgkinson, chair of the Group for Action on Leeds Bradford Airport (GALBA), said the changes could lead to a substantial rise in night flights, to the detriment of residents.

Mr Hodgkinson said: "To be clear, GALBA is not saying the airport should be closed down. We simply say it should not be allowed another big increase in night flights.

"In May, LBA was allowed 941 more night flights – that’s a 77 per cent increase over the peak summer season – and now they want another 75 per cent increase of summer season night flights.

"That’s not fair to local people."

He rejected the airport’s claim that the changes are needed for economic growth.

Mr Hodgkinson said: "LBA’s PR material talks about economic growth.

"However, their planning application very clearly claims that growth will occur anyway, regardless of this planning application.

"They say there will be seven million passengers a year in 2030, with or without changing the night flight rules.

"The jobs figures used in the application come from LBA’s own Vision 2030 business plan, which makes no mention of the need to change the night flight rules.

"So, this is not some sort of 'competition' between the economy and the health of local people.

"LBA claims that its economic benefits will happen without changing the night flight rules."

He also warned of health risks.

Mr Hodgkinson said: "There’s a wealth of medical research linking frequent night-time noise to heart disease.

"The key point about LBA’s application is that it wants permission for far more frequent flights at night and between 6 and 7 in the morning.

"The health impacts would be serious."

Horsforth Town Council also raised concerns while acknowledging the airport’s economic importance.

The council’s planning, licensing and transport committee has recommended a response to Leeds City Council that highlights the need for improved noise insulation for residents, better infrastructure to support increased passenger numbers, and a greater use of sustainable aviation fuel.

But the council said it had "significant concerns" about the night-flight changes and questioned whether the QCS would deliver quieter aircraft.

The council said the current night-flight period should stay unchanged to protect residents from further disruption.

It neither supports nor opposes the application, instead calling for stronger safeguards and protections.

The airport has proposed noise insulation for up to 750 households, but GALBA argues this is insufficient and would leave many residents without support.

GALBA also said LBA would contribute only "towards" insulation costs and that about 100 homes would benefit each year, leaving many residents waiting years for protection.

GALBA said public opposition to the proposals had been overwhelming.

Leeds City Council’s planning website lists 1,182 objections and just 168 comments in support.

The deadline for public views on the airport’s proposal has been extended.

Residents now have until October 15, 2026, to submit their views.
