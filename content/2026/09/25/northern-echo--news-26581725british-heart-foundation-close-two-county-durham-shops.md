Two County Durham charity shops are among stores facing closure as the charity prepares to shut 150 outlets across the UK.

The British Heart Foundation plans to close 150 shops by March 2028, with 14 already shut.

The Consett store at 6 Middle Street and the Newton Aycliffe shop at 40 Beveridge Way are among the County Durham outlets operated by the charity.

Other North East stores include the BHF shops in Hartlepool, Stockton, South Shields and Sunderland.

The charity’s 651 shops made a net loss of £3.3m last year, according to its accounts.

The BHF had 470 clothing shops and 181 home stores in the year to March 2026.

Retail costs rose five per cent to £240m, while turnover climbed 1.5 per cent to £236m.

The charity said its shops’ financial return in 2025-26 was ‘not sustainable’.

It blamed the planned closures on ‘rising operating costs and changing customer habits’.

The charity told Civil Society: “The changes are designed to ensure our retail network remains strong and sustainable,” it said. “On the basis the proposals proceed, implementation will likely result in limited recovery.”

The charity spent £441m against income of £416m in 2025-26.

But the BHF made a net £17.4m gain on investments during the year.

Despite the retail losses, the charity raised a record £62m from fundraising, including event fees and merchandise sales.

It received another £106m in legacies.

The BHF awarded £103m in new research grants, taking its research portfolio to £485m — nearly £23m higher than the previous year.

The charity spent £45.2m raising funds and £145m on staff, including £1.1m in redundancy payments linked to restructuring.

BHF chief executive Charmaine Griffiths said: “Our annual report shows BHF at its best: protecting hearts today, backing world-class science and building the foundations for even bigger breakthroughs tomorrow.

“Thanks to our incredible supporters, we expanded our research portfolio to the highest level since the pandemic, reached millions of people with trusted health information and support and delivered a record year for fundraising despite significant external pressures.

“These pressures mean we must also now make difficult decisions, including proposed shop closures, to protect sustainable income for lifesaving research.”
