**Harrogate**— A major**£5 million environmental infrastructure project** has officially launched at the Harrogate North wastewater treatment works to clean up the**River Nidd**.

The extensive upgrade scheme, managed by **Yorkshire Water** and its contract partners Ward & Burke, will introduce advanced chemical and biological systems at the Bachelor Gardens facility to drastically strip out phosphorus from treated wastewater before it enters the local environment.

Phosphorus commonly enters the sewer network via everyday domestic products like shampoos and detergents, alongside agricultural fertiliser run-off. While low levels are harmless, high concentrations trigger toxic algal blooms that choke out sunlight, destroy native river plants, and threaten regional fish populations.

> **Lizzie Robinson, Project Manager at Yorkshire Water:**
>
> This project will result in significant reductions in phosphorus levels going into the River Nidd, improving overall water quality for 4.8km downstream of the works. Improving the health of our rivers is vitally important, and this is one way we can contribute.

The Bachelor Gardens initiative forms part of a broader **£350 million regional investment** aimed at completing 85 separate river health projects across Yorkshire by 2030. Alongside the main chemical stripping installation, engineering specialists Hydro International will refurbish the existing Harrogate infrastructure to boost processing capacity and extend the site’s working lifespan.

Construction at the Harrogate North facility is scheduled to run until **late 2029**.

The scheme follows an independent **£20 million investigation fund** recently launched by the utility firm in central Harrogate, which is actively targeting structural solutions to reduce storm overflow discharges along the River Nidd corridor.
