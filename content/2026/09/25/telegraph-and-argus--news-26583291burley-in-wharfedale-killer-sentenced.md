A 30-year-old man who stabbed a stranger has been jailed for 17 years after being convicted of manslaughter.

John Robinson admitted stabbing Mason Jones during an altercation off Main Street in Burley-in-Wharfedale shortly before 10am on March 1 this year.

He said the stabbing was an accident, and that he slipped as he ran to confront Mr Jones.

![]()Mason Jones, 30 *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

At the time Robinson was clutching a specialist chef’s knife.

Mr Jones, 30, suffered a severe abdominal injury and was pronounced dead in hospital three hours later.

At his trial at Bradford Crown Court Robinson denied murder, manslaughter, and having a knife in public.

He was convicted by a jury of manslaughter by a 10:1 majority, and of having a knife in a unanimous verdict.

He was found not guilty of murder.

![]()Some of the horror unfolded on Main Street in Burley-in-Wharfedale, pictured *(Image: Google Maps)*

During the two-week trial the jury heard that Will Clarke and his friend Mr Jones had travelled to Burley-in-Wharfedale after being invited to her flat by Annabel Ramsay.

She was described as Robinson’s on/off girlfriend, but that she had broken off their relationship a couple of days earlier.

When Mr Jones and Mr Clarke arrived in the early hours of March 1 from Bury, where they had been out drinking and had taken cocaine, they were allowed into the flat where Miss Ramsay had been drinking with her friend.

However, following an argument, they were thrown out and spent some time wandering around the village before returning twice to the flat where Mr Jones hoped he could charge his mobile phone.

![]()Robinson was convicted of mansalughter *(Image: West Yorkshire Police)*

During that morning Miss Ramsay called Robinson, who was at home in Derry Hill, Menston, and urged him to join her as the two men would not leave.

Robinson arrived by taxi.

He had with him an Asda bag containing various items including a 17cm-long Katto chef’s knife, which had been bought for him as a Christmas gift by Miss Ramsay when Robinson had been working as a chef.

He left the bag by the door to the flat.

He had been in the flat for around 90 minutes when Mr Jones and Mr Clarke returned.

Witnesses told the trial that there was banging, shouting, and swearing outside the flat.

Robinson, who was at an upstairs window, ran downstairs to confront Mr Jones - who he did not know - after he said he saw him grab Miss Ramsay by the throat.

As he did so, Robinson took his chef’s knife from the overnight bag he had arrived with.

He was holding it in his hand when he exited the door at speed and collided with Mr Jones.

He told Miss Ramsay: “F\*\*\* ing hell, I think I’ve stabbed him. I didn’t mean to do it.”

Video footage of the immediate aftermath of the incident showed Mr Jones, holding his side, staggering into Main Street where he collapsed into the road as Mr Clarke begged passers-by to call an ambulance.

Minutes later, Robinson and Miss Ramsay called a taxi to take them to his home.

He was arrested there 90 minutes later.

Sentencing Robinson to 17 years’ imprisonment with a further three years, to be served concurrently, for having a knife in public, His Honour Judge Jonathan Carroll said he had responded to Miss Ramsay’s call because he was “anxious to do what she asked” in his attempts to preserve their relationship.

He added: “She told you in clear and robust language that she wanted you to come to her flat to get these others out.

“You set off to Annabel Ramsay’s flat expecting a confrontation and being ready and willing to engage in it in your determination to impress Annabel.

“I am satisfied so that I am sure that you brought the knife to the scene intending to use it either simply to threaten, if that were enough, but willing to use it as a weapon if needs be.

“In the event, when the physical confrontation between Mason Jones and Annabel Ramsay erupted again, you did immediately pick up the knife.

“It did not simply fall out of its sheath. You removed it and, within moments, used it, stabbing Mason Jones in a lethal stab wound to his abdomen some 16cm in depth.”

He described the incident as “a deliberate, conscious act of thrusting that knife” that caused injury “that was lethal from that moment.

“The knife was especially dangerous, being a specialist chef’s knife.

“You took this highly dangerous weapon intending to use it though I accept from the jury’s verdict that you did not do so with an intention to kill or to cause really serious harm.

“You intended some harm and it would be obvious to anybody, including you, that to use such a knife in that way carried a high risk of death or grievous bodily harm, and I am satisfied that your intention fell only just short of that required for murder.”

Judge Carroll told Robinson that he will serve two-thirds of his sentence before being eligible for release.

He ordered the forfeiture and destruction of the knife.
