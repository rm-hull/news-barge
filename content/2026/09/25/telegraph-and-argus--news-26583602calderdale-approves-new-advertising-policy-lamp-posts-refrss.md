What can or can’t be advertised on Calderdale Council-controlled spaces – particularly its lamp posts – is now set down in policy, with the hope it will also bring in extra cash.

The issue gained prominence in the summer when the council, controlled by Reform UK since May’s local elections, ordered rainbow banners hung from some lamp post advertising spots to be removed and the money refunded on grounds they had not got the “appropriate consent.”

Public fundraising had paid for the banners and Coun Sarah Courtney (Lab, Hebden Bridge and Todmorden East), who had organised this, said the space had been booked through the usual channels.

The new policy was then introduced at the authority’s Governance and Business Committee and Coun Phil Pringle urged members of the full Calderdale Council to approve it.

Coun Pringle (Reform UK, Greetland), who chairs the committee, said it would plug the policy gap.

“Previously there was no policy in place for advertising and sponsorship of council-controlled lamp posts,” he said.

The policy would ensure the suitability of an advertisement, which would be checked before it goes up.

It also had the potential to create additional revenue for the council, said Coun Pringle.

Coun Martin Hey (Green, Northowram and Shelf) said he had some concerns about the policy.

The list of exclusions does not go far enough, for example fossil fuel advertising, he said.

The option the committee councillors supported – and now by the full council -includes what they hope is the best of both worlds, existing commercial agreements being retained but also using it for public service advertising.

Council space includes boundary signs, roundabouts, grass verges, roads and highways, lamp post banners, street furniture, for example information boards and planters, parks and car parks, council-owned buildings, council-owned or managed events, print or digital publications, the council’s fleet, any approved billboard sites, and parking meter tickets or other tickets and licences.

It sets out what is not to be permitted, including “support or promotion of a political party, candidate or lobbying groups” – there are a host of other “banned” categories and the policy allows for more to be added.

However, if deemed appropriate, local – registered as Calderdale-based – businesses in the food, drink and leisure businesses which might otherwise sit within the “not permitted” list could be considered, according to the policy.

If there is any doubt, the council’s communications team should refer the matter to the relevant member of the corporate leadership team for decision in agreement with the Cabinet portfolio holder, councillors were previously told.
