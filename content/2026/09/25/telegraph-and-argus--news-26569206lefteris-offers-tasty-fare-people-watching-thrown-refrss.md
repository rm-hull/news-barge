I LOVE people watching, and, with a window seat at a city centre café, I was able to indulge my passion.

It was raining lightly when, looking for a bite to eat in Bradford city centre, my husband and I settled upon Lefteris Coffee & Tearoom on Tyrrel Street. With floor-to-ceiling windows, well-spaced tables and what looked like comfy chairs, it fitted the bill perfectly.

So in we went. The café is light and airy, with many pot plants adorning shelves, adding a splash of greenery to the stylish décor. The chairs were indeed comfy, ranging from relaxed lounge-style to conventional café chairs - which we chose. It’s many window seats add to its appeal.

Lefteris - a word of Greek origin meaning ‘free’ - wraps around the corner of Tyrrel Street and Ivegate, it’s attractive frontage blending in with the surrounding York stone.

The building of which the café is part, was once home to Brown Muff’s department store, a popular shopping destination in Bradford which closed in 1995 under House of Fraser ownership. More recently the corner premises operated as a KFC. Lefteris moved in late 2018, brought to life by the team who operated Bradford Bakery, formerly in Rawson Place.

The cafe serves a range of sandwiches and toasties as well as a mouthwatering selection of cakes and pastries. A friendly member of staff told us that sourdough bread is made on the premises, with loaves on display that customers can buy.

Sandwiches included avocado, tomato and rocket, ham and gherkin and cheese and onion chutney, while toasties included pesto, cheese and mozzarella and ham, cheese and mustard.

I opted for a tuna and mayonnaise sandwich, served with chives and salad leaves (£6.50), while Andrew chose a chicken, bacon and cheese toastie (£7.50).

We couldn’t resist those cakes - choosing one wasn’t an easy decision, with so many on offer. We opted for carrot and walnut (£5) and a sweet cherry bun (£3).

The cakes were quickly brought to our table, and 15 minutes later, the sandwich and toastie appeared.

I’m very partial to a tuna mayo sandwich, and this one was, I can honestly say, among the best I’ve had. I am not a huge fan of wholemeal bread - I forget to ask if I could order white - but this was light in colour, soft and doughy. It was lovely, and tasted even better knowing it was made on the premises.

The tuna mayo was thick and tasty, with a tang of chive, and fresh rocket leaves on the side.

Andrew described his toastie as “a cut above”, piping hot, moist and flavoursome, with plenty of shredded roast chicken, melted cheese and bacon, seasoned lightly with salt and pepper, and a drizzle of balsamic vinegar adding to the mix. The toasted bread, he said, was lovely - crisp, with a good wholemeal flavour.

We drank tap water, available from a glass dispenser.

As lunchtime progressed, the café grew busier, with tables filling up.

We shared our cakes, giving us a chance to sample each. The carrot and walnut was delicious, with light, tasty buttercream within, while the sweet pastry cherry bun was just as it says on the tin.

Some of the cakes at the café, a friendly member of staff told us, are homemade, though most of the on-site baking is bread. A cake making service is also offered for private events.

We later ordered tea - decaffeinated (£3.70) for me - and an Americano (£3.90) for Andrew. My Ceylon tea came loose leaf, and was so refreshing.

Lefteris is perfect for a sit down and light lunch. And, of course, if you bag a window seat it offers a chance to observe the comings and goings of others.

\*Lefteris, 9 Tyrrel Street, Bradford, United Kingdom, BD11RU. The café has a Facebook page.
