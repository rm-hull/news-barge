The Great North Air Ambulance Service answered more than 550 emergency calls across the North East and beyond during one of its busiest summers.

Between June 21 and September 22, the charity’s critical care teams responded to 557 incidents across the North East and beyond.

Of those, 331 were in the North East, 173 in Cumbria, 24 in Northumberland, 10 on the Isle of Man, eight in Scotland, seven in Yorkshire and four in Lancashire.

![]()The Great North Air Ambulance in Saltburnj *(Image: GNAAS)*

Phil Lambert, chief pilot at GNAAS, said: "With only two helicopters we live on a knife-edge.

"If one is in maintenance and the other develops a fault, we’re grounded, and that means we can’t answer a 999 call by helicopter.

"A third aircraft changes everything.

"It gives us resilience and guarantees that when one is being serviced, two remain ready to fly."

![]()Crinkle Crags *(Image: GNAAS)*

The charity has secured funding for its first two aircraft but urgently needs public donations for a third helicopter’s deposit.

Last year, the charity launched its Operation SOS: Secure Our Service appeal to raise £2.5 million for a third helicopter’s deposit.

Just 33 per cent of the target has been raised, leaving more than £1.5 million to find by the end of 2026.

Summer is one of GNAAS’s busiest periods, with more people outdoors, travelling and using the roads.

![]()Moss Force Buttemere *(Image: GNAAS)*

Cardiac arrests and road crashes were the most common emergencies, followed by falls, medical incidents and assaults.

GNAAS crews operate from bases in Eaglescliffe and Penrith, delivering critical care across the North.

Donations to the Operation SOS: Secure Our Service appeal can be made through the charity’s website at gna.as/secure\_our\_servic.

The charity relies on public donations to keep its life-saving service flying and ready for emergencies.
