Two men who dragged a woman out of her car in an “absolutely vicious” car-jacking have together been locked up for more than 20 years.

Imran Altaf kicked the female victim to the chest and punched her in the head whilst Dawood Basharat pulled her from the vehicle as she tried desperately to cling to the steering wheel.

Today at Bradford Crown Court, His Honour Judge Jonathan Carroll jailed Altaf for a total of 12 years.

Basharat was handed eight years and one month.

He told the pair that they had attacked a lone female in her car on April 10 of last year.

He told Altaf that he had caused “significant fear” as he tried to drag the victim out of her car before using “excessive fear and violence”.

He told Basharat: “You were part and parcel of an absolutely vicious street robbery and car-jacking.”

Altaf was also sentenced for an earlier episode of dangerous driving in which he fled from police.

Prosecutor Rachel Webster told the court how the female driver was parked in her car in Keighley at 8pm on April 10 when Altaf and Basharat pulled up alongside her.

Basharat was acting “in a peculiar manner”, opening her door and touching her hands and arms before trying to get into her car.

She tried to close the door but was grabbed and pushed back by one of the men.

She then heard one of them shout: “Get her in the back.”

Mrs Webster said: “[The victim] had noticed that they had taken the keys and realised the severity of the situation.

“She started to push back and grabbed the steering wheel so that she couldn’t be moved.

“[She] was then assaulted. Mr Altaf kicked her to the chest twice and struck her to the head.

“Mr Basharat tried sitting on her to move her over, but she resisted. Mr Basharat was also touching [the victim] inappropriately.

“Still holding the wheel, the defendants continued this horrific attack by dragging her out of the driver’s seat of the car.

“They then jumped into the car and drove away.”

Altaf and Basharat left their victim at the scene, where she called 999.

She had also taken pictures of them.

Police later spotted the stolen car on Stanningley Bypass and there was a chase.

Altaf, who was driving, began harshly braking and performing U-turns.

The stolen car was stopped when a police officer made “tactical contact” with it.

Altaf ran off but was tackled to the ground and arrested along with Basharat, who was the passenger.

The victim identified both men in an identification parade shortly after.

In a statement read to the court on her behalf, the victim said she had been left in a state of shock by what had happened to her and said Altaf and Basharat behaved “as if they were not human”.

She added: “At one point I feared they were going to kill me.

I thought I was going to be kidnapped.

“They were laughing at me, which made me feel powerless.

“I am utterly terrified that they will come looking for me.”

The court heard that, a month prior to the car-jacking, Altaf was involved in a police pursuit in which he reversed at a police officer on foot, trying to hit him, and then reversed into a police car.

He drove away again before jumping out of the car and running off, during which he threw away a plastic bag.

He was caught by police who used Pava incapacitant spray on him, but Altaf responded by raising his fists.

When a taser was produced, Altaf moved back, ran off, and tried to get into an address before he was caught and arrested after first resisting.

He was found with £436 in cash and a Nokia mobile phone.

The plastic bag he dumped contained 36 bags of heroin and 17 wraps of cocaine worth £530.

The court heard that Altaf, 31, of Harewood Crescent, Oakworth, Keighley, had four convictions for six offences including assault occasioning actual bodily harm.

Basharat, 39, of Highfield Road, Keighley, had eight convictions for 15 offences including assaulting emergency workers and for driving matters.

In mitigation, the court heard that Altaf’s offending had been fuelled by alcohol and drugs but that he had since overcome his addictions.

He was said to have acted “in moronic terms” and had driven “idiotically”.

Basharat was described as “very much a junior partner” in the car-jacking but it was conceded that he should have interceded in the violence against the female victim.

In sentencing the pair, Judge Carroll banned Altaf from driving for two years plus a six-year extension to reflect his jail term and ordered him to pass an extended re-test before driving again.

He also ordered the forfeiture of the cash seized, and the forfeiture and destruction of the drugs.
