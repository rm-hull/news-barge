# The Government risks losing the public’s faith on law and order unless the courts crisis is dealt with

## The court backlog has reached an unacceptable level with a record number of victims waiting for justice to be done. There were 80,829 outstanding cases at the end of June, Ministry of Justice figures show, at crown court.

These are serious cases that in any democracy that values law and order would be dealt with in a timely manner.

Yet of these cases, the number which have been waiting to be dealt with for a year or more has hit a record high of 23,706, up 22 per cent from 12 months earlier. The number is clearly trending in the wrong direction.

Victims having their trauma unnecessarily prolonged is nothing short of a disgrace. This country has long abided by the principle of justice being done and being seen to be done, yet neither is being delivered in many cases.

![Leeds Combined Court Centre. PIC: James Hardisty](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBkNGIwMDQzLTIyZDctNDYyOC1iYTBkLTEwODZjNTU2YTgzMjo3ODNlODBmMy03MDZmLTRjZjUtYTk4ZC1iNWM1ODBhZTNlODU=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


If the Government does not get a grip of this then the public will quickly lose confidence with Labour. It has the potential to damage the party for generations to come. Aside from the obvious political ramifications of the growing backlog, there is the wider societal impact.

The courts crisis cannot be viewed in isolation. There is a chronic shortage of prison capacity and not enough police officers on patrol. As a result, scenes of lawlessness are becoming more and more commonplace.

The message it sends to criminals is that even if you are caught, there’s unlikely to be an urgency to see that justice is done in the courts and even if it is done the likelihood of serving the full sentence is remote.
