Shoppers across the UK are being urged to check their cupboards after a series of food recalls were issued by major supermarkets.

The Food Standards Agency has warned customers not to eat several affected products sold by retailers including Tesco and Waitrose.

The recalls were announced over safety concerns, with the products removed from shelves and customers advised to return them for a refund.

Anyone who has purchased the items is being urged to follow the guidance issued as the warnings remain in place.

## Waitrose recall

Waitrose has recalled a drink from sale in its UK supermarkets because it may pose a risk of microbiological contamination.

A breakdown in temperature control means that the products may now be unsafe to drink.

The supermarket has recalled its Waitrose Essential Unsweetened Oat Drink due to the rick of microbiological contamination and spoilage caused by the poor temperature control.

The affected products come in 1 litre bottles, were purchased between September 2 and September 21, and have best before dates between August 14 and October 29.

## Chocolate recall

A selection of chocolates have been recalled from shop shelves in England and Northern Ireland because of a salmonella contamination.

World of Sweets is recalling Dubai Style Crunchy Knafeh and Pistachio Milk Chocolate and Alyan Dubai Style Chocolate Truffles because Salmonella has been found in the products.

A spokesman for the Food Standards Agency warned: “The presence of salmonella in the products listed above.

“Symptoms caused by salmonella usually include fever, diarrhoea and abdominal cramps.

A popular beer sold UK supermarkets, including Tesco, has been recalled because it may explode.

Cans of Lucky Saint Hazy IPA have been recalled as some cans may become over-pressurised and rupture unexpectedly.

The affected products are sold in four packs of 330ml cans and have a best before date of August 2027.

## **What is a product recall?**

If there is a problem with a food product that means it should not be sold, then it might be 'withdrawn' (taken off the shelves) or 'recalled' (when customers are asked to return the product).

The FSA issues Product Withdrawal Information Notices and Product Recall Information Notices to let consumers and local authorities know about problems associated with food.

In some cases, a 'Food Alert for Action' is issued.

This provides local authorities with details of specific actions to be taken on behalf of consumers.
