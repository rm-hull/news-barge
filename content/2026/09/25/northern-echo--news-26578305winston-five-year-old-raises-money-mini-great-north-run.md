A County Durham youngster whose parents were told she may never walk has completed the Mini Great North Run to raise money for charities that have supported her.

Five-year-old Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month, walking the full 50 metres with assistance while raising hundreds of pounds for Gainford Primary School and children's charity Little SENDsations.

The achievement marked another major milestone for Millie, who was born at just 26 weeks weighing only two pounds and later diagnosed with cerebral palsy affecting her left side.

![]()Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month

Her mum, Anna England, said the day was emotional for the whole family.

She said: "It was really good. Obviously, it was a bit overwhelming at first because of her autism. The crowds were a little bit chaotic for us.

"However, once she got onto the route she absolutely loved it and smashed it.

"She walked the full 50 metres with assistance, and we could not be prouder of that. She is just absolutely amazing.

"It was lovely hearing everybody cheering her name because they had little flags with her name on them and they were all shouting, 'Go Millie'.

"It was very emotional for me and her dad Michael. At one point we were told that she may never walk and then here she is doing the Mini 50, raising money for charity and absolutely smashing it."

Anna said the proud moment was made even more special given everything Millie has overcome since birth.

![]()Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month

Doctors had previously warned the family to prepare for the worst after Anna suffered severe complications during pregnancy, while Millie spent 72 days in neonatal care following her premature birth.

Since then, the youngster has continued to defy expectations and recently started attending mainstream school.

![]()Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month

After crossing the finish line, the day's excitement soon caught up with her.

Anna said: "She was exhausted. By the time we got on the train to come home, she fell asleep. It had been such a big day for her."

The family chose to raise money for Gainford Primary School and Little SENDsations, organisations Anna says have played a vital role in Millie's development.

She said: "The primary school is just a little village school and they can always do with extra funding coming in. It helps them pay for buses for school trips, buy things that might be needed and provide extra opportunities for the children.

"They are so fantastic with Millie.

![]()Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month

"We just wanted to give something back because she has come on so much since she first started nursery and now she is in school. They have been absolutely brilliant.

"As for Little SENDsations, they are also a small charity. They put on trips and events, make story bags and story boxes, and everything they do helps children like Millie have a more inclusive life.

![]()Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month

"If we can help by raising funds, then we will do that wherever we can."

The fundraiser has raised around £620 for Gainford Primary School and £435 for Little SENDsations.

Anna said she had been overwhelmed by the response from supporters.

She said: "She has touched so many people's hearts and inspired people.

"In a world that is not always very inclusive, I always say 'be more Millie' because she inspires and motivates people.

"Ever since she was born she has touched a lot of hearts with her story and her determination. She is just incredible."

She added: "I just want to thank everybody who has sponsored her, supported her and sent kind messages.

![]()Millie England, from Winston, took part in the Mini 50 event earlier this month

"We are so appreciative of everyone, and it is truly going to make a difference to both charities."

Anna also hopes her daughter's story can offer encouragement to other families facing similar diagnoses.

She said: "When you hear the words that your child has cerebral palsy, it does hit you hard. We were terrified when Millie was diagnosed nearly four and a half years ago.

"But look where she is today.
