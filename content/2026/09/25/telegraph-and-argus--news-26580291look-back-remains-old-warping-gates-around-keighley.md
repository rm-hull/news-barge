By Robin Longbottom

A LONG forgotten process in the worsted industry was that of stretching and sizing the warp before it was put on the loom.

This had been traditionally done outdoors along a warping gate.

The gate (walkway) ran along a conveniently straight wall into which sticks were fitted over a distance of a 100 yards, or so, to support the warp as it was stretched and sized.

Size was used to strengthen the yarn to reduce breakages during weaving.

The remains of five warping gates have survived and can be seen in Oldfield, Haworth, Micklefield, Denholme and Steeton. Others may yet be discovered.
