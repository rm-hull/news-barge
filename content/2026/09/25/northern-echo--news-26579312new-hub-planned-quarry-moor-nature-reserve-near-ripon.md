A popular North Yorkshire nature reserve is set for a new chapter as plans move forward to restore education and outdoor activities at the site.

Demolition of the former Activity Centre at Quarry Moor, near Ripon, is paving the way for a new hub for learning, volunteering and community events.

The project is being overseen by the Alderman Tom F Spence Committee, which is also developing a temporary facility for school visits and volunteers on behalf of the charity whose sole trustee is Ripon City Council.

![]()Quarry Moor is entering a new chapter as demolition of the former Activity Centre makes way for a new hub focused on education, volunteering and community activities *(Image: Supplied)*

Councillor Julieann Martin-Long, chair of the Alderman Tom F Spence Committee, said: "Quarry Moor has long been a valued community asset.

"Councillor Duncan and I have many fond memories of visiting the site during our childhood, when the Activity Centre was a thriving and well-used facility.

"While we recognise that the removal of the former building will be a significant change, it also marks the beginning of an exciting new chapter for Quarry Moor.

"The new hub will provide an opportunity to re-establish school visits, support a programme of community activity and well-being services, and encourage local volunteers to help care for and enhance this site.

![]()Committee chair Councillor Julieann Martin-Long, centre,Councillor Tony Duncan right, and Ripon Mayor Councillor Andrew Williams *(Image: Supplied)*

"We are keen to ensure that the future development of Quarry Moor reflects the needs and aspirations of the local community.

"We will therefore continue to listen to residents, community groups, and other users of the site, while considering how the facilities are used and what further improvements may be required in the future to serve our community best."

The new centre will host education, wellbeing programmes and community activities.

It will also give volunteers a base to care for and improve Quarry Moor.

The wider Quarry Moor renewal programme will improve signs, gates, fences and information boards, making the reserve more welcoming to families and groups.

The plans also restore play areas and equipment.

Quarry Moor is a designated Local Nature Reserve containing two Sites of Special Scientific Interest, both known for species-rich limestone grassland.

The site will give children a place to learn outdoors, discover wildlife and learn how to protect it.

Working with partners including the Lower Ure Conservation Trust, the project will protect habitats, boost biodiversity and give volunteers a chance to help.

Councillor Andrew Williams, mayor of Ripon, said: "Quarry Moor holds a special place in the lives of many Ripon families.

"As sole trustee of the charity, Ripon City Council is committed to supporting the future of this special place.

"I’d like to thank members of the Alderman Tom F Spence Committee for progressing these plans after Full Council and the Chair’s motion to begin demolition.
