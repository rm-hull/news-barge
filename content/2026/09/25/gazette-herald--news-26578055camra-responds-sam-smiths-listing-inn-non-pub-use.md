CAMRA has called it “extremely disappointing” that Sam Smith’s Brewery is selling a North Yorkshire pub “for non-pub use only.”

The Tadcaster-based brewery has placed the restriction on its sale of the Wellington Inn at Knaresborough, as part of a sale of more than 20 pubs nationally, many of which have the same restriction.

The sale includes the Cross Keys Inn at Stillingfleet, which does not have the restriction.

The Wellington Inn at Knaresborough is on the market with Fleurets for £325,000.




Fleurets are marketing the Cross Keys Inn for £475,000.

However, it appears there is little or nothing that can be done to stop the sale of pubs for non-pub use only.

Allan Gauld, Branch Secretary of Harrogate and District CAMRA, said: “Camra always laments the loss of any pub, it is often said that a pub is not just a shop that sells beer but a place of community and memories.”

“It is especially disappointing as our understanding is that the Wellington is covered by a covenant restricting its future use as a public house.”

Allan says such a restriction may make it impossible to get the pub to receive Asset of Community Value (ACV) status, which can offer some protection against converting pubs for other uses.

He continued: “We also only tend to apply for those when there is sufficient support from the local community.

Overturning a covenant requires expensive legal action and so isn’t a likely course of action for any potential purchaser.”

“Our hope is that the new management at Sam Smiths will be able to reopen their other two pubs in Knaresborough and a number of others in the surrounding area.”

Nick Love, pubs protection officer for York CAMRA, agreed, confirming such restriction of the sale of a pub to non-pub use only is both legal and enforceable.

He continued: “It can be challenged but it is a lengthy and expensive process that few parties would want to go through unless they thought the pub could be opened as a goldmine.

"It could be argued that if that was the case - Sams would have kept it open to profit from it themselves!”

Nick added making the pub an Asset of Community Value (ACV) would just delay the sale and have no effect on removing the covenant at the end of the ACV process.

He also told The Press: “And Sam Smiths currently can choose to not sell to any group formed out of the ACV.”

As previously reported, Sam Smith’s brewery secretary Christian Horner told The Press he preferred not to comment on commercial matters like pub sales, but he added: “The brewery is continuing to look for new pub managers to reopen any closed pubs.”
