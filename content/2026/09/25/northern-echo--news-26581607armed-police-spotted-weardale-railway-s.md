Armed police officers were spotted at Weardale Railway, sparking questions at the popular County Durham attraction.

Police were sent to the railway as part of a specialist training exercise designed to prepare recruits for a range of potential incidents.

Northumbria Police used historic carriages as a realistic training environment for armed response recruits.

Instead of carrying visitors through the Durham Dales, the railway's carriages were transformed into the setting for a series of operational scenarios aimed at testing officers' training, decision-making and responses in an unfamiliar environment.

The exercise came after Northumbria Police approached Weardale Railway several months ago to explore whether the attraction could support its armed response training programme.

Following an assessment of the site by course instructors, the railway worked with police to identify how a variety of training scenarios could be safely carried out at the Stanhope site.

Before the exercise began, recruits received a full site safety briefing and orientation from the Weardale Railway team.

Claire Gibbons, general manager at Weardale Railway, said: "It was certainly a little different to our usual day at Weardale Railway, and we were pleased to be able to support Northumbria Police with this important training.

"Our carriages provided the officers with a realistic environment in which to carry out a number of scenarios, while allowing the training to take place safely and away from the public.

"Weardale Railway is an important part of the local community, so it was great to see our facilities being put to use in a completely different way."

Chief Inspector Gemma Calvert, of Northumbria Police's Firearms Support Unit, said firearms officers regularly take part in training exercises to ensure they are prepared for a wide range of incidents.

She said: "Our firearms officers regularly undertake training exercises to ensure they are prepared to respond effectively to a wide range of incidents and keep our communities safe.

"Training in different and realistic environments is an important part of that preparation, and Weardale Railway provided an excellent setting for officers to put their skills into practice across a number of scenarios.

"We'd like to thank the team at Weardale Railway for their support and for allowing us to use their facilities."

Weardale Railway operates heritage services between Bishop Auckland West and Stanhope and was rescued from administration by The Auckland Project in 2020.

Since then, investment has been made in repairs and infrastructure improvements, with the railway now offering a range of heritage journeys and special events throughout the year.

The Weardale Railway is an 18-mile heritage line running through the Durham Dales.
