A police raid on a pub in Southowram uncovered a cannabis growing operation which could have produced up to 40 kilograms of the drug.

Bradford Crown Court heard yesterday (Thursday) how officers had to force entry through locked doors when they attended at the Pack Horse Inn on April 12 this year.

Prosecutor Olivia Fraser said during the search officers found 30-year-old Tran Van Hung hiding under a bed in the kitchen area.

When officers carried out a full search of the premises they found five growing rooms in the cellar area containing over 200 plants and cuttings.

More than 160 plants were found in three further growing rooms on the upper floor and Ms Fraser said there was also a “follow on crop” of 177 plants ready to re-stock the rooms.

She explained that the 412 plants could have produced 23.2 kilograms of cannabis and the “follow on crop” of 177 plants could have yielded a further 16.8 kilograms.

The defendant, of no fixed abode, pleaded guilty to a charge of producing cannabis and yesterday he was jailed for 23 months.

Barrister Safaraz Ahad, for Van Hung, said he had been tending the plants and had performed a limited function under the direction of others.

“He is eager to return home (to Vietnam) as soon as possible,” said Mr Ahad.

“He welcomes being deported. His father has cancer and he wants to return as soon as he can.”

Recorder Patrick Palmer said the officers had discovered a commercial cannabis farm when they forced entry into the Pack Horse pub.

“The farm was equipped with sophisticated ventilation equipment and extensive growing equipment,” said the judge.

“You were found hiding under a bed in the first floor kitchen.

“It would have been obvious to you that this was a highly sophisticated and commercial arrangement.”
