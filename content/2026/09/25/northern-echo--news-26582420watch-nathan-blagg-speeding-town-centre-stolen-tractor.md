Police have released footage of Nathan Blagg speeding through a town centre in a stolen tractor before ramming it into a house linked to his bitter rival.

A long-running feud between Blagg, 28, and Luke Lancaster erupted in violence, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The feud, linked to serious and organised crime, was escalating in the weeks leading up to the tractor being stolen and rammed into the house on Priory Road at 1.35am on May 9.

Lancaster was jailed earlier this year for attempting to run Blagg over when he saw him chatting in Middlesbrough town centre.

Lancaster also posted footage of himself desecrating the graves of Blagg’s brother and father, a judge heard.

![]()The tractor in Middlesbrough

Blagg retaliated by stealing the tractor from a Castlelevington farm shortly after midnight before speeding along Acklam Road towards the house.

He rammed the tractor into the front of the terraced house before fleeing the scene in a Land Rover Discovery bearing cloned number plates. The vehicle was later discovered burnt out in South Bank.

![]()Police are investigating after a tractor crashed into a house on Priory Road, Middlesbrough in the early hours of this morning (May 9)

Blagg, of Addington Drive, Middlesbrough, was jailed for seven years and two months and given an extended two-year licence period after being branded a dangerous offender.

Since the incident, Cleveland Police has investigated it and related organised crime under Operation Sentinel, working with regional partners including the North East Regional Organised Crime Unit.

Speaking after the sentencing hearing, Temporary Detective Chief Inspector Dan Cook, from Middlesbrough CID, said Blagg showed scant regard for anyone’s safety as he sought revenge.

“To steal a tractor from a farm, to then be driven at high speed towards Middlesbrough and rammed with extreme force into a house shows the lengths Blagg will go to instil fear and violence, all without a thought or care for residents and the wider public,” he said.

“While it is lucky no one was inside that house at the time, Blagg was not aware of this or if those inside neighbouring properties were home when the tractor was rammed.

“The danger and significant risk it posed to neighbouring properties meant occupants had to evacuate for their safety due to the structural damage caused.

“It also took a number of days before the tractor could be safely and securely removed however this is at a loss to the owner of the vehicle and the occupant.

“From the evidential material gathered, we were able to put Blagg at the farm through mobile data recovered during a separate investigation. Analysis of the device placed him at the location at that time and showed the use of a flashlight.

“Further examination identified use of his internet history to show his interest in the sentencing guidelines and media coverage of the tractor.

“This type of criminality is often linked to serious and organised crime, which we take extremely seriously. It may be through disputes or tit-for-tat behaviour that leads to violence being orchestrated and the use of stolen and cloned plated vehicles.

“Our disruption of this criminality is ongoing, and we are working closely with our regional partners under Operation Sentinel to continue to tackle organised crime across Cleveland and try to prevent these crimes from happening in the first place.”

Blagg, of Addington Road, Middlesbrough, pleaded guilty to charges from three separate incidents.

The 28-year-old admitted affray and motoring offences linked to the April 21 incident.

Blagg admitted criminal damage recklessly endangering life and aggravated vehicle taking linked to the May 9 incident.

He admitted aggravated vehicle taking, possessing a Class C controlled drug and motoring offences linked to May 14.

Blagg was banned from driving for seven years after his release from prison.

Lancaster pleaded guilty to attempting to cause grievous bodily harm with intent, possession of a knife and dangerous driving, and was sentenced to 46 months.
