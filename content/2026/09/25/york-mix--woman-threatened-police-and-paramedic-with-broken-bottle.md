A notorious Selby woman has been jailed for 12 months for threatening a police officer and a paramedic with a broken glass bottle during a two-hour-long incident.

Jessica Simms, 20, threatened one of the emergency workers with the broken bottle at her flat in The Crescent in what was the latest in a catalogue of drink-fuelled incidents in the recent past, York magistrates heard.

She also threatened a female emergency worker with the broken bottle in the street near her home in the town centre during the same incident on 17 September, said prosecutor Kathryn Walters.

In an incident lasting two hours, Simms made threats to the police officer and paramedic who had gone to her aid during a mental-health episode.

On the same day, she breached a community-protection notice (CPN) by going to Selby Station which she was barred from doing by the order.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/selby-station-front-gsv.webp)

She was arrested and taken to Fulford Road Police Station in York where she damaged the custody suite which required a clean-up costing just under £80.

Simms was charged with breaching the CPN order, two counts of threatening a person with an offensive weapon and damaging property belonging to North Yorkshire Police, namely the police custody suite.

She appeared in court over the weekend when she pleaded guilty to all matters and was remanded in custody.

She appeared for sentence via prison-to-court video link yesterday (Thursday).

Ms Walters said that Simms “clearly” had mental health issues.

Simms’ solicitor Adam Henry said the Selby woman’s criminal record began in June 2024 after her mental-health problems deteriorated.

He said that Simms had been diagnosed with a personality disorder but had yet to receive any therapy.

“She has a tendency to drink when she suffers intrusive thoughts…which leads to her offending behaviour, which is alcohol-related or related to the emergency services,” added Mr Henry.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/york-magistrates-court-tower.webp)

He said that Simms had been banned from Selby Station due to the sheer number of call-outs to the station “either by herself or members of the public concerned for her wellbeing”.

At the time of the latest offences, Simms was subject to a community order which included an alcohol-treatment programme.

For the two counts of threatening a person with an offensive weapon, Simms was jailed for 12 months.

Julian Fowell, chairman of the magistrates’ bench, said the offences were so serious that only an immediate jail sentence could be justified “for what you’ve done”.

Simms was ordered to pay £79 compensation for the damage to the police custody suite and a victim surcharge of £187.

There were no separate penalties for breaching the community-protection notice and damaging the police custody suite and Simms’ existing community order was revoked.

However, she was re-sentenced for the original offences for which the community order was imposed in July.

Those previous offences were assaulting a police constable, impersonating a police officer and causing the wasteful employment of police.

For assaulting a police officer, she was given a further three-month prison sentence, but this was to run concurrently with the substantive 12-month jail sentence for the weapon offences.

For impersonating a police officer, she was given a conditional discharge. For causing the wasteful employment of police time, there was no separate penalty.
