**HARROGATE** — North Yorkshire Police has refused to disclose how many people have actually been charged following its heavily publicised “biggest operation in history,” admitting it lacks the administrative resources to audit its own case files.

The force launched a massive media blitz following **Operation Super Tornado Two**, a 24-hour surge executed on** Wednesday 23 September 2026**. In a multi-page corporate press pack distributed to regional media, the constabulary loudly boasted of a headline figure of 64 total arrests, 14 search warrants, and a £1 million cannabis cultivation uncovered in Northallerton.

However, an investigation by *The Harrogate Informer* has exposed that the force is refusing to supply basic localised data or confirm if a single one of those 64 high-profile arrests has actually resulted in a formal judicial charge or a remand in custody.

When pressed directly to provide transparency for the Harrogate District, the force’s Corporate Communications desk blocked the query, citing “fairness” to other media outlets they had already turned away.

> **Will Smith, Corporate Communications Manager for North Yorkshire Police:**
>
> Re the Harrogate breakdown – we received the same request from another Harrogate media outlet yesterday and advised them that we couldn’t provide it. We need to be fair and consistent in how we respond to requests, so we wouldn’t provide this information to one outlet having declined the same request from another.

In a further admission, the corporate press officer confirmed that the force is entirely unable to verify the long-term legal success metrics of its own historic crackdown due to a lack of staff resources.

> **Will Smith, Corporate Communications Manager for North Yorkshire Police:**
>
> Re the charges – establishing how many of the 64 people arrested have since been charged would require us to review each individual case file, and we don’t have the resources available to undertake that work today.

The revelation raises major structural questions regarding the validity of high-profile public relations campaigns executed by the force. In the UK legal system, an arrest tally carries no weight without subsequent charges, meaning a significant proportion of the heralded “64 suspects” could potentially be released on bail or freed under investigation without charge.

Despite the press desk’s lack of localised data, the official corporate release prominently featured extensive quotes from **Chief Constable Tim Forber**, who claimed the surge was about “taking the fight directly to those who cause the most harm” and utilising “every tactic available.”

The initial script also detailed the **first deployment of Live Facial Recognition (LFR) technology** in York, alongside high street sweeps that resulted in the recovery of 13,000 contraband cigarettes and a nail bar raid yielding £1,280 under the Proceeds of Crime Act.

While the command desk has officially invited dissatisfied outlets to file bureaucratic complaints via their website, *The Harrogate Informer* will continue to pursue the formal localised data via statutory Freedom of Information (FOI) channels.
