An exhibition marking the 90th anniversary of the Jarrow Crusade will open this autumn, bringing a defining chapter of North East history to life.

The free exhibition will run at Jarrow Town Hall on Saturday, October 3, and Sunday, October 4, with archive photos and displays about the marchers.

In October 1936, 200 men marched from Jarrow to London in a peaceful protest demanding jobs and investment after mass unemployment and shipyard closures.

Councillor Stan Wildhirt, Mayor of South Tyneside, said: "The Jarrow March is a story of courage, community and hope, and it continues to hold a very special place in the hearts of people across South Tyneside.

"This exhibition is a chance for residents and visitors to come together, remember the remarkable men who made that journey, and celebrate the spirit of Jarrow that still inspires us today."

The marchers’ 26-day journey made headlines nationwide and became one of Britain’s best-known peaceful protests.

Councillor Susan Sybenga, Deputy Leader of South Tyneside Council, said: "The 90th anniversary is an important opportunity to honour the legacy of the Jarrow Crusade and to share its story with new generations.
