# Leeds police officers' 'heroic' efforts to bring man back from brink of death

## PCs Daniel Coleman, Olivia Mason, and Hannah Stoves ran a mile in just 10 minutes to reach the man.

Three police officers who sprinted a mile in just 10 minutes to bring a man back from the brink of death.

PCs Daniel Coleman, Olivia Mason, and Hannah Stoves were each handed Royal Humane Society Resuscitation Certificates after their heroic efforts. The trio ran a mile on a cold and wet morning, on October 28, 2025, at Roundhay Golf Club over challenging terrain to reach a man suffering a heart attack

The man had collapsed on a remote part of the course, and they had to run to help him. Once there, they began administering CPR, which they then kept up for 12 minutes until an air ambulance could arrive and he could be taken to hospital.

His pulse had restarted, but he sadly died later surrounded by his family.

The three officers were also praised by the Secretary of the Royal Humane Society, who said: "Sadly the man died in hospital but the three officers who ran to the scene in atrocious conditions undoubtedly gave him the best chance possible of survival.

"They played a major role in bringing him back from the brink of death, albeit briefly, and giving him the chance to have his family with him when he died. They did an incredible and exhausting job.

"They richly deserve the awards they are to receive. At the same time though there are many incidents where lives are saved and those involved never receive awards they deserve because their names have never been put forward.

"We appeal to members of the public if they hear of incidents in which lives have been saved, sometimes in circumstances where the life savers have put their own lives in danger, to bring them to our attention."
