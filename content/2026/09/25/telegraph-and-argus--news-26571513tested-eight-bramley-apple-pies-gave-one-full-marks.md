APPLE pie season is here, when the fruit comes into its own.

Whether pies or tarts, with pastry tops and bottoms, they can be eaten hot or cold, and are even more yummy with custard or cream.

First recorded in the 14th century in England, apple pie recipes are now a standard part of cuisines in many countries. They are generally made using shortcrust pastry.

Bramley apples are cooking apples with a high acidity and low sugar content, which balances out the added sweetness in a pie. They break down into a fluffy or smooth consistency when baked, creating a rich and juicy taste.

I tested packaged Bramley apple pies from leading supermarkets, as well as brand leader Mr Kipling. I ate them cold, fresh from the box. Here are my findings.

Box weights are given where available.

\*Village Bakery, Aldi, 6 Bramley Apple Pies, 315g, £1.25

In common with all but one of the pies, these had an apple design on top with fluted edges and a little sugar sprinkled on top. The pastry in my pie was quite thick and a bit claggy in consistency. There wasn’t a great deal of apple filling inside, and what there was very sweet. It did have a slight apple taste, but there wasn’t much to go on.

Pros: appearance.

Cons: texture (pastry), taste.

Mark: 2/5

\*The Bakery at Asda, 6 Bramley Apple Pies, £1.25

These looked appetising, with neat brown edges, but when I picked one up it was crumbly around the top. The pastry tasted a little cardboardy, and, to borrow a Mary Berry phrase, had a soggy bottom. There was a lot of sugar dusting on top of the pie to add sweetness, but there wasn’t a great deal of filling and what was there had an artificial taste.

Pros: presentation.

Cons: consistency, taste.

Mark: 1/5

\*Tesco 6 Bramley Apple Pies, £1.25

These had a nice appearance, with firm pastry, a nicely-defined pastry apple in the centre, brown fluted edges and sugar dusting. texture of the pastry was light and the pie was well-packed - there were a few decent pieces of diced apple among the filling, which had a pleasant taste with a tart edge. Not outstanding, but not bad, and very good for the price.

Pros: presentation, texture, taste, price.

Cons: none.

\*Mr Kipling 6 Bramley Apple Pies, £3.25 (bought at Tesco)

With neat fluted edges, nicely browned on the outside, and the apple design in the middle, these looked appetising, but appearances can be deceptive. The pastry had a decent texture and taste, but this pie had the biggest void between the fruit and the pastry lid, and the taste of the sparse filling was like travel sweets. And this one is more than twice the price of most others.

Pros: presentation.

Cons: taste (filling), price.

Mark: 1/5

\*Rowan Hill Bakery, Lidl, 6 Bramley Apple Pies, £1.25

These looked a bit tatty round the edges, however the pastry was light and quite crisp - the nicest pastry in this price range. There was a decent amount of filling with pieces of apple visible among it, but, bizarrely, the apple taste was barely there.

Pros: consistency, taste (pastry)

Cons: presentation, taste (filling)

Mark: 3/5

\*M&S Food 4 Bramley Apple Pies, 226g, £3

This one had a different presentation, and was shaped like an apple, which I liked. It was brown around the edges and was sprinkled with demerara sugar, which had burned on top. The pastry was light and buttery. Inside it was packed to the rafters with apple filing, which actually tasted like apple. These are more expensive, but they were the nicest and most natural tasting by a long way.

Pros: presentation, taste

Cons: more than twice the price of many others, for fewer pies.

Mark: 5/5

\*Morrisons 6 Bramley Apple Pies, £1.25

These were a good shape, with a prominent apple design. The pastry was light and nicely textured. There could have been more filling, but it was adequate, with lots of pieces of apple. I could taste the apple, which, while not on a par with M&S, was pleasant enough. A good buy for the price.

Pros: consistency, texture, taste, price.

Cons: none.

Mark: 4/5

\*Sainsbury’s 6 Bramley Apple Pies, 315g, £1.25

Nice-looking pies, with neatly crimped, browned edges and light sugar dusting. The pastry on top was tasty, but the base was a little cloying. Inside, the pie was well-filled and there was a taste of apple, albeit slight. Not bad, but not great either.

Pros: appearance, taste.

Cons: consistency (base).

Mark: 3/5
