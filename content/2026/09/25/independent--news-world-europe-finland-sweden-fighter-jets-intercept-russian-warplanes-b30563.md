Sweden and Finland scrambled fighter jets to intercept Russian military aircraft over the Gulf of Finland in a new first as the Baltic countries cooperate against a growing threat from Moscow.

A group of Russian aircraft, including a Tu-134 transport plane and several MiG-31 and Su-30 fighter jets, was tracked down in international airspace by the European fighter jets, in what Finland’s air force said was the first joint "identification mission" with Sweden.

Baltic countries have been on high alert after incidents of gas pipeline outages, drone sightings and airspace violations have grown rapidly since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

![The group of Russian warplanes could be seen in the distance](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/25/13/2026-09-25T085611Z_730935199_RC28QNAGXLQO_RTRMADP_3_FINLAND-SECURITY-RUSSIA.JPG)

"It is a concrete expression of the readiness and trust we have built together – and an expression of the strength in the Nordic, Nordic-Baltic, and broader NATO cooperation in our part of the world," Swedish prime minister Ulf Kristersson said on X.

The operation was the first as part of Finland and Sweden’s new Quick Reaction Alert cooperation agreement, which allows fighter aircraft to operate from each other’s bases, he added.

"The Baltic Sea has never been as vulnerable as it is now – but it has also never been better protected than it is right now," he added.

The Russian embassies in Stockholm and Helsinki did not immediately respond to requests for comment when contacted by Reuters.

![Russian Su-30 fighter jets fly in the international airspace over the Gulf of Finland, September 24](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/25/13/2026-09-25T085626Z_370175090_RC28QNA4ET6G_RTRMADP_3_FINLAND-SECURITY-RUSSIA.JPG)

Finland and Sweden both joined Nato following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, departing from a longstanding policy of non-alignment. Their Nordic neighbours Norway, Iceland and Denmark were already members of the Atlantic alliance, as were the Baltic republics of Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania.

The move came after Denmark's defence intelligence service said Russia is likely to intensify its hybrid war against the West in the coming months in a new threat assessment on Thursday.

Hybrid warfare and sabotage on European soil gained renewed attention after Germany recently blamed Russia for an attempted drone attack at the Leipzig/Halle ⁠Airport, prompting EU members to call for ​fresh ⁠sanctions against Russia.

Moscow has repeatedly denied any involvement in hybrid warfare, sabotage operations in Nato countries, or plotting any confrontation, and the Russian embassy in Denmark ⁠said Copenhagen’s was assessment as unsupported by credible evidence.

![The aircraft flew in international airspace over the Gulf of Finland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/25/13/2026-09-25T085623Z_297349027_RC28QNAF5D5A_RTRMADP_3_FINLAND-SECURITY-RUSSIA.JPG)

"Our persistent warnings about the destructiveness of such an approach are being ignored," it said in a statement.

The defence intelligence service said Russia would ‌carry out more frequent attacks against the West and ‌NATO "with greater consequences for the countries targeted than in the past", adding that there is "a low but growing risk" that Russia would launch a limited military attack against one or several NATO countries that border it.

"A limited military attack would be a desperate move on Russia’s part and, like the intensified hybrid attacks, could potentially take place in the coming months," it said.

This week, the CIA has also warned that Russia is preparing to carry out drone strikes in Europe targeting France, Spain and Italy from the sea, according to a report in Spanish newspaper *El Mundo*.

Several European governments have been passed information about the Kremlin’s supposed plans to escalate its interference on the continent, an unnamed source close to the Lithuanian ministry of defence told the outlet.

It comes as Russia ramps up its attacks on Ukraine’s data centres, with Ukrainian authorities stating that around 100,000 households in Kyiv cut off from the internet this week.

![Moscow is driving up attacks on Ukraine's data centres, says Kyiv](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/25/14/13/sybiha.jpg)

Attacks on Kyiv’s data centres on Wednesday and Thursday have driven up fears of Moscow’s widening war on Ukrainian infrastructure. Ukrainian officials told the Financial Times said the recent uplift in attacks is a deliberate and targeted escalation.

“Russia has targeted major Ukrainian data centers, seeking to disrupt the flow of information,” foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said on X.
