A company has been appointed to deliver £2.5 million of energy efficiency upgrades to homes across Gateshead.

Esh Construction will carry out the work on 82 properties as part of a six-month programme commissioned by Gateshead Council to improve housing stock and reduce energy use.

Seventy bungalows will be fitted with photovoltaic (PV) panels and ventilation upgrades, while 12 flats will receive insulated flat roof improvements.

Lisa Stafford, head of retrofit solutions at Esh Construction, said: "We are delighted to be partnering with Gateshead Council to deliver this important programme of energy efficiency improvements for residents across the borough.

"The detailed assessment and design work completed ahead of construction has enabled us to develop tailored retrofit solutions for each home.

"Through a combination of insulated flat roofs, solar PV systems and ventilation measures, the scheme will help improve energy performance, reduce heat loss and create warmer, more comfortable homes for residents."

Properties set to benefit from the upgrades are located in Penrith Grove, Florence Avenue, Hillside Place, Kells Lane, Causeway, Malton Green and Carlisle Place.

A Gateshead Council spokesperson said: "This programme will help improve the energy efficiency of 82 homes across Gateshead, making them warmer and more comfortable for residents while helping to reduce energy use and carbon emissions.

"Esh Construction was appointed through a competitive procurement process which identified the company as the highest-ranked supplier.

"The appointment reflects the requirements of the funding programme and the need to deliver the works within the available timescales.

"All properties will be brought to a minimum EPC C rating, improving their energy performance from their previous ratings, which were predominantly D.

"We will continue to work with Esh and its supply chain to ensure the works are delivered in line with the required retrofit standards and provide lasting benefits for residents."

The scheme was procured through the NEPO233 Retrofit and Decarbonisation Works framework and forms part of the Warm Homes: Social Housing Fund Wave 3 programme.

It supports Gateshead Council’s ambitions to improve the energy efficiency of its housing stock through a fabric-first approach.

Pre-construction work included retrofit assessments, updated Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) ratings, and technical surveys to inform the bespoke designs.

Esh Construction has appointed Storm Tempest to deliver assessment, design, and coordination services, ensuring compliance with PAS retrofit standards.

The Warmer Homes initiative was designed to help improve the comfort and energy efficiency of lower income homes and follows on from the previous Home Upgrade Grant (HUG) projects funded by the Department for Energy and Net Zero.

When allocating funding, the Government has asked local authorities to prioritise homes in more deprived areas, those with the lowest EPC ratings of E, F, and G, and households with incomes below £36,000 or in receipt of means-tested benefits.
