Fans have spoken out following Bradford City's decision to allow Graham Alexander to hold talks with Preston North End.

Alexander has been approached by Preston, his former club, regarding their managerial vacancy, and City have "reluctantly" given permission for discussions to take place.

Ian Taylor, secretary of the Bingley Bantams supporters' group, said: "We’d be devastated if he went, no doubt about that.

"He’s the best manager we’ve had in recent times.

"We’d also be a touch disappointed because we feel we’re really building something here.

"I understand his link to the club (Preston), so I do understand it from his point of view.

"This is his third year here and he has a contract until (the summer of) 2028.

"He’s been good for us, and we’d like for him to stay.

"But if that isn’t meant to be, then it isn’t meant to be."

Humayun Islam, chairman of the Baji Bantams, echoed the concerns of many fans.

![]()The Baji Bantams are one of a number of prominent fan groups for City. *(Image: Baji Bantams)*

Mr Islam said: "We’re with the consensus.

"It’s not positive news.

"We want to keep sustainability for the long-term.

"We’d be disappointed, because it’s not just the manager, it’s the team and staff.

"We all grow together."

Bradford City confirmed the approach in a statement yesterday, shortly after the T&A's initial stories on the matter, which came via an exclusive from John Percy of The Daily Telegraph.

A spokesperson for the club said: "Bradford City AFC can confirm we have been approached by Preston North End to speak to Graham Alexander, regarding the Sky Bet Championship side’s managerial vacancy.

"Reluctantly, Bradford City has granted permission for Graham to speak to Preston North End.

"The club will be making no further comment at this time."

Mr Alexander is a highly respected figure at Preston, having made more than 400 appearances for the club during two spells as a player.

Mr Islam was philosophical about the situation, regardless of the outcome.

He said: "When one door closes, another door opens.

"Sometimes the grass is greener on the other side.

"In the short term, we’ll have lost somebody but in the long term it could work out fine.

"We’ll take the rough with the smooth.

"We look at Bradford City as a club.

"Win or lose, we’re always united, no matter who the manager is.

"So, let’s stay optimistic."
