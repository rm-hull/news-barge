## Major York film festival reveals masterclasses with award-winning film experts

Brian Falconer *(Image: Pic supplied)*

A major film festival in York is inviting audiences behind the scenes of screen culture.

The Aesthetica Film Festival, running from November 4 to 8, will feature a series of masterclasses with leading industry speakers from film, television, photography, and publishing.

These sessions are designed to demystify the creative process, offering practical insights and professional experiences from the people shaping contemporary screen culture.

A spokesman for the festival said: "They offer practical knowledge, but also something broader: reassurance that creative careers are built through experimentation, persistence and collaboration as much as through formal expertise.




![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21660315.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mick Audsley *(Image: Pic supplied)*

"For anyone beginning their journey, developing a project or simply curious about how great work is made, these sessions provide a rare opportunity to learn from experience."

The programme includes a conversation with Mark Jenkin, director of Bait and Enys Men, who will discuss his distinctive approach to filmmaking.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21660316.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nina Hartstone *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Mr Jenkin often takes on multiple roles in his productions, including cinematography and editing, and is known for his use of small-gauge film as a core element of his storytelling.

The masterclass will examine how image, place, and narrative combine to create a personal cinematic language, and will be in conversation with Jessica Loveland of the BBC.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21660323.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Holly Newsom *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Sound design will also be in the spotlight, with a session led by Academy Award-winner Nina Hartstone and Emmy-winner Louise Burton.

Their combined experience spans major projects such as Bohemian Rhapsody, Saltburn, and Emerald Fennell’s Wuthering Heights.

The spokesman said: "Through their shared experience, the session will consider dialogue, effects, foley and the subtle layers of audio that make cinematic worlds feel tangible.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21660324.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Neil Forsyth *(Image: Pic supplied)*

"It demonstrates the wider philosophy of Aesthetica’s programme – that every aspect of creative production matters, and that understanding the detail can change the way we experience the whole."

Other highlights include a talk with Brian J Falconer on the challenges and opportunities of telling regional stories with global reach.

He will discuss how authenticity can be a creative asset rather than a limitation.

The festival will also explore the editing process, described as a "deeply creative act" by the spokesman, featuring BAFTA-winner Mick Audsley (Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire), BAFTA-nominee Victoria Boydell (Saltburn), and Justine Wright (The Last King of Scotland).

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21660324.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Neil Forsyth *(Image: Pic supplied)*

A masterclass with screenwriter Neil Forsyth, creator of Guilt and The Gold, will focus on the craft of writing for television and sustaining narrative over a series.

A festival spokesman said: "The value of bringing award-winning speakers to Aesthetica is not simply that audiences can hear from people whose names appear in credits and award ceremonies – it is that their knowledge becomes something that can be encountered directly."

Festival passes are available at asff.co.uk/tickets.
