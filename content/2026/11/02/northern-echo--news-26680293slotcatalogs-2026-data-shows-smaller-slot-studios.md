A slot does not become a business the day it launches. A game reaches no players until a casino integrates it. Even then, it can sit deep in a lobby where few people scroll, and without enough spins it never registers in market statistics. Smaller studios have to get through all of that against suppliers with larger distribution networks and wider catalogues.

SlotCatalog measured two parts of this problem at the SlotCatalog SBC Awards Europe 2026: how widely a studio's games appear in casino lobbies and how often players spin them. Two of the four data-driven categories, Game Studio of the Year: Small and Rookie of the Year, were set up for smaller and newer studios. The scoring method and the winners show where independent developers stand in 2026.

## SlotCatalog: the biggest database of casino games behind the 2026 award data

SlotCatalog is the biggest database of casino games on the internet. Players, developers, and operators use it as an analytical resource. The catalogue lists more than 50,000 slots and games, more than 1,200 game providers, and 2,700+ casino reviews. A game record covers RTP and volatility, the mechanics and features, and the release date.

The database also follows how games are placed in casino lobbies in each market. SlotRank scores single games by lobby position, and ProviderRank does the same for studios. The awards rely on lobby data of the same kind, collected from real casino sites.

## How SlotCatalog measures studios: lobby presence and spin volume

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644841.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SlotCatalog)*

The awards page describes the results as based on measurable performance, and two indicators carry the scoring. Lobby presence is a studio's percentage share of games across casino lobbies in the selected European markets. Spin quantity is the total number of spins a studio generates, normalised against the daily market-wide totals.

A studio's points are its normalised lobby presence plus its normalised spin quantity, each multiplied by a weight coefficient, with the result shown as a percentage. Every category has its own coefficients. Casino Rank Value, or CRV, gives larger casinos more weight, so a position in a busy lobby counts for more than a position in a small one.

The scoring period runs 14 months, from February 2025 to April 2026, across six European markets. Casino lobbies are checked daily at thousands of online casinos, and the awards use samples taken on the 1st, 7th, 15th, and 23rd of each month. Spin statistics come from data partners, a source SlotCatalog has used since mid-2023, and machine learning processes the market signals.

Eligibility rules differ by category, and each category ends in a shortlist. Points are calculated for every nominee, and a nominee's share of the total is checked against its share of spins within the shortlist. For 2026, the page notes a stronger focus on independent studios. Brands with traction under their own name were favoured over results that came mainly from a large supplier group.

## SlotCatalog awards split studios into large, medium, small and rookie

The four data-driven categories sort the market by size and by age, and each one looks at a different stage of a studio's growth.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644843.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SlotCatalog)*

In the Large category, subsidiary studios and exclusive development partners count toward a supplier's score. The page calls this the real market presence of the entire network. A small studio has no network like that. Its score comes from one team, one catalogue, and the lobbies that team has managed to reach.

The Medium category drew the most contenders. Its shortlist grew to 15, against 10 elsewhere, and the page calls it the most intensely contested group. Hacksaw Gaming won it for the second year in a row, and Pragmatic Play repeated its win in Large. Both upper categories went to the same winners as the year before, which is the bar the Small and Rookie categories sit below.

Four more SBC categories ran alongside the data-driven ones. SlotCatalog experts judged them separately and rated nominees from 1 to 10. The data categories count lobby share and spins. The expert categories assess things like innovation and player experience, which gives a small studio's game a chance there as well.

## The visibility wall in SlotCatalog's small studio category

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644839.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SlotCatalog)*

The Small category asks whether a studio's games can break through what the page calls the visibility wall and get real lobby exposure. A lobby has a limited number of positions, and the games placed first get the most attention. A new title from a small studio usually has neither the brand recognition nor the distribution agreements that secure those positions.

The category looks at structural scale, and a long catalogue does not disqualify anyone. A studio can release many titles and still be a compact organisation. The focus is on independent teams that reach players without the distribution and production resources of major iGaming brands.

Peter & Sons won in 2026. According to its SlotCatalog provider page, the studio was founded in 2019 and has more than 80 games. ProviderRank is calculated per country, and the same page shows how a studio's standing changes from market to market. In Denmark, Peter & Sons holds first place with an average lobby presence of 3.45%. In Germany, it is fourth, with an average lobby presence of 2.58%. Both figures come from the provider page and describe one studio in two different markets.

Peter & Sons also appeared among the 18 entrants for Game of the Year with Barbarossa Dragon Empire, next to releases from Pragmatic Play, Play'n GO, and Relax Gaming.

## SlotCatalog's rookie category: what a new studio needs to prove

Rookie of the Year goes to a studio that is new to the market and already has measurable traction. The page says a newcomer must secure distribution, lobby visibility, and player activity, especially as an independent brand. A good debut alone does not qualify.

Shady Lady won the 2026 award. The studio entered the market in 2024, and the page credits it with consistent visibility, sustained momentum, and strong early market penetration. Its SlotCatalog provider page lists 20 games released through 2025 and 2026, including Devil's Finger in February 2025, Brainwashed in April, Swoll in September, and Laced in November. Six slots came out in the last several months alone. Every game shown on the page is a high-volatility title with a maximum win of 20,000x, so the catalogue has a uniform profile.

A regular release schedule gives a new studio more chances to appear in lobbies. Lobby presence is a share of games, and spin quantity is a total, so a studio with a single title has little to show on either indicator. A studio that launches one game and goes quiet has a hard time building the numbers this category measures.

## Two hurdles behind SlotCatalog's small studio scores

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21644838.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: SlotCatalog)*

The scoring reflects two obstacles that a release has to clear before it gains market traction.

Distribution comes first. A game cannot appear in a lobby until a casino integrates it, and integration usually depends on a commercial agreement with the operator. Lobby presence counts how many of a studio's games made it in, and CRV adds a second layer, because presence in large casinos carries more weight. A studio that reaches only minor operators scores lower even when its game count is high.

Player activity is the second. Spin quantity suggests whether players pick a game once they see it. A studio with strong lobby presence and low spin volume has distribution without engagement. A studio with high spins in a few lobbies has engagement without reach. The formula counts both indicators, and a studio needs a solid showing on each to compete for a title.

Position inside the lobby is a separate matter. A game that reaches a casino may still end up deep in the lobby, but position is not part of the award scoring. SlotCatalog tracks it with SlotRank, which is recalculated daily from lobby crawls, so a studio can watch it move as new releases arrive.

ProviderRank and SlotRank are open to every studio. ProviderRank shows how a studio stands in each market, and the country view shows where its lobby presence is concentrated. A competitor's game record lists RTP, volatility, mechanics, and max win. Before a release, a studio can go through these records to see which games already sit in the lobbies of its target markets and find a place for its own. After launch, the volatility and max win of the new game can be compared with similar titles. Its SlotRank over the first weeks shows how it develops, and the list of countries shows where it starts to appear. All of that shows distribution. Player reaction is a different question, and only spin data answers it.
