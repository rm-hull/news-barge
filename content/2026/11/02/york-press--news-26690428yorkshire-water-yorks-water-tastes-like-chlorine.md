Yorkshire Water has said “small changes” to how it treats York’s supply network has resulted in some customers noticing a chlorine taste in their tap water.

People across the city have reported turning to bottled water due to the unusual taste, with one resident claiming their water had become “undrinkable" from the tap.

But Yorkshire Water insisted York’s tap water is still safe to drink.

“We’ve recently made some small changes to the way we treat York’s water supply network and, as a result, some customers may notice a slight difference in the taste or smell of their drinking water,” a Yorkshire Water spokesperson said.

“Chlorine is an essential part of the water treatment process and has always been used to disinfect drinking water supplied to York.

"Chlorine concentrations within the York supply area are carefully controlled, continuously monitored and remain within the normal range expected for a public water supply.

“We know that minor changes in the taste or odour of drinking water can be concerning for customers and we investigate concerns raised with us.

"However, the recent change has not altered the quality or safety of the drinking water supply, which continues to meet our high drinking water standards.”

A Tang Hall resident said the "chlorine smell is so strong" and had been an issue for several months.

Other York residents said they had bought water filters due to the unusual taste.

"We’ve been using a Britta water filter jug for drinking water for a while," one resident said.

"I had thought the chlorine smell had started to clear but it seems like it’s returned in the last week, so we’ll be staying using the filter."

The issue comes after Yorkshire Water’s chief executive, Nicola Shaw, resigned on Friday (October 30), the day after the company was given the lowest possible environmental rating following a rise in pollution incidents.

Yorkshire Water was given a one-star (poor) rating by the Environment Agency (EA), meaning it would automatically be blocked from giving out bonuses to bosses under new rules introduced last year.

It followed new EA figures revealing Yorkshire Water was responsible for 260 pollution incidents in 2025, up from 151 the year before. This is a rise of more than 70 per cent and the highest total since 2011.

A Yorkshire Water spokesperson said its environmental performance last year was “not good enough and we understand that customers will be disappointed by this result”.

“In particular, we need to reduce pollution incidents and improve the reliability of our environmental performance."

The spokesperson also said Ms Shaw decided earlier this year it “would not be appropriate to receive a bonus due to our performance in the previous financial year, which was not good enough – particularly in relation to our environmental impact”.

It followed Ms Shaw facing backlash from regional leaders after it was revealed she had received an extra £1.3 million from Yorkshire Water’s offshore parent company, Kelda Holdings, since 2023.
