A new multi-storey car park and access road have been approved at a Bradford district hospital.

The scheme, part of the wider redevelopment of the hospital site, was given the go-ahead by Bradford Council and marks the first major step in the creation of the new Airedale Hospital.

Airedale NHS Foundation Trust described the approval as a "significant milestone" in their plans.

Foluke Ajayi, chief executive of Airedale NHS Foundation Trust, said: "We’re delighted that our plans to build a new multi-storey car park and access road have been well received, and that we have been granted full planning permission.

"We understand how important parking is to our patients, visitors and staff.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21659791.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Indicative West Elevation View *(Image: Supplied)*

"The new multi-storey car park, together with other on-site spaces, will ensure there is sufficient parking for everyone and make parking a much easier process."

Construction of the access road from Skipton Road is expected to begin before the end of the year, with work on the car park itself due to start in spring 2027.

The car park will be located in the southwest corner of the hospital grounds.

The design of the car park has been shaped by feedback from the community and will include larger parking bays, electric vehicle charging points, bicycle spaces and a laser-cut visual design reflecting the surrounding natural landscape.

It will also feature on-site security, CCTV and automatic number plate recognition.

The new access road is intended to improve traffic flow to and from the hospital and will initially be used to separate construction traffic from daily hospital operations.

Once the new hospital is open, the road will be used by staff, patients and visitors.

The planning application for the car park was first submitted last year and updated over the summer.

While the design remains similar, the overall size of the car park has been reduced in line with the Trust's ten-year Health Plan, which aims to deliver more care in community settings.

This change will result in fewer patients travelling to the main hospital site and a reduced need for parking spaces.

Ms Ajayi said: "The plans have been shaped by input received from our patients, local people and staff, which has helped us ensure we are creating a modern car park that meets the needs of all its users and is designed to complement the local landscape.

"The multi-storey car park is a key enabler for our new hospital, allowing us to free up the space needed to build our new hospital."

The Trust will share further updates on the project through its website as work progresses.
