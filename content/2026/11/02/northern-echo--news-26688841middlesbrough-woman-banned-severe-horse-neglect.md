A woman whose horse died after its infected leg was discovered wrapped in baby nappies has been banned from keeping horses for three years.

Kirsty Oliver, 38, of Harlech Close, Eston, failed to get treatment for her horse Red, leaving his injury untreated for weeks.

Red was left permanently lame and, despite weeks of treatment after his rescue, his pain could not be controlled, and he was put to sleep.

Oliver was banned from keeping horses for three years and given a 12-month community order at Teesside Magistrates' Court on October 22.

The court heard an RSPCA animal rescue officer visited a yard in Saltburn on September 6 last year after reports of a horse with a badly injured leg.

Nobody was at the yard, but the officer was so concerned by Red’s condition that he called a vet immediately.

The officer removed the makeshift dressing from the horse’s left hind leg and found baby nappies covering the wound.

The bandage was so tight it restricted blood flow and caused more swelling above and below the injury. Parts of the wound were exposed and covered in flies, while the area was hot, painful and oozing blood.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21658047.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Red's leg. *(Image: RSPCA)*

The RSPCA officer said: "Upon examining the horse and removing the dressing, I could see baby nappies had been used to cover the wound. It smelt awful and showed signs of infection."

The vet confirmed Red was suffering, and police took him into their care before he was moved to private boarding.

The vet said the wound was likely to be "at least multiple weeks old" and the covering was unsuitable.

In her statement to the court, she said: "The materials covering the wound that were present on arrival were not an appropriate way to manage this wound and had been applied incorrectly, causing further swelling and ultimately preventing healing.

"Had intervention not occurred, it is likely that infection would have progressed and the horse would continue to suffer.

"A responsible owner should have sought veterinary assistance for this wound as it required antibiotics, analgesia and appropriate wound management and care."

RSPCA enquiries after the rescue established that Oliver owned Red.

When officers visited her home in Eston, Oliver confirmed she was responsible for Red and agreed to hand him to the charity. The RSPCA said Oliver refused to co-operate with its investigation despite repeated attempts to interview her over six weeks.

She pleaded guilty to an offence under Section 9 of the Animal Welfare Act 2006 in August.

The charge concerned Oliver’s failure to get proper veterinary treatment for Red’s leg injury between August 5 and September 7, 2025.

Oliver was also ordered to pay £500 in costs and a £114 victim surcharge.
