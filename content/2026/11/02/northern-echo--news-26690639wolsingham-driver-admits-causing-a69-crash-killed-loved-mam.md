A drugged-up County Durham driver has admitted causing a two-car smash which claimed the life of a "much-loved mam".

Wolsingham's Daniel Porter pleaded guilty to causing Doreen Flatman’s death by dangerous driving on the A69, in Northumberland, on Saturday September 26.

Porter, of Angate Street, was told the “the likelihood” is that he will receive, “a custodial sentence of some length”.

The 36-year-old was given the warning by Judge Tim Gittins at Newcastle Crown Court after after his guilty plea today (Monday November 2).

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21659994.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Daniel Porter has admitted causing death of 76-year-old woman and two counts of causing serious injury, all by dangerous driving *(Image: Northumbria Police)*![Police-issue image from fatal crash scene](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21659128.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Floral tributes to deceased Doreen Flatman, 76, at the roadside on the A69 where the fatal collision took place on September 26*(Image: Northumbria Police)*

Mrs Flatman was a rear-seat passenger in a Volkswagen Tiguan that collided with a Honda ENY1 driven by Porter, between Hayden Bridge and Hexham.

The Honda was travelling westbound when it collided with the Volkswagen, which was heading eastbound, shortly before 1.30pm.

Emergency services attended but 76-year-old Mrs Flatman, from Haydon Bridge, was pronounced dead at the scene.

The Volkswagen’s driver and another passenger, both also members of the Flatman family, suffered serious injuries.

Porter, who appeared by video link from Durham Prison for today's plea hearing, also pleaded guilty to two counts of causing serious injury by dangerous driving, possessing methamphetamine, a class A drug, and driving while unfit through drugs.

In an unrelated matter, he denied two counts of breaching an interim Sexual Risk Order, imposed at Newcastle Magistrates’ Court on September 22 this year.

The allegations concern his alleged failure to give police the PIN for an undeclared mobile phone.

A trial was fixed for March 2, next year, for that matter and is expected to last two days.

Porter will be sentenced for the driving offences after the Sexual Risk Order case is concluded.

But Judge Gittins told him: “Obviously, as you have pleaded guilty to the serious driving offences and drug offences you will receive significant credit in due course, but the likelihood is that there will be an immediate custodial sentence of some length.”

The judge remanded Porter to remain in custody but to appear for a further case management hearing on December 22 and a pre-trial review on February 4.

In a tribute to Mrs Flatman in the aftermath of her death, her family said: “Doreen was a much-loved mam, and the loveliest friend and woman anyone could ever hope to meet.

“She was truly the life and soul of every room she walked into, bringing warmth, laughter, and an unmistakable brightness to all our lives.

“Her kindness knew no bounds. She was the kind of person who would do anything for you without a second thought, always putting others first and giving her heart so generously.

“Her untimely passing is a tragic loss that leaves a deep and painful void.

“Words cannot fully express how sadly missed she will always be, but the boundless love she gave and the beautiful memories she left behind will stay with us forever.”

Sgt Greg Huntley, of Northumbria Police’s Serious Collision Investigation Unit (SCIU), said: “The thoughts of everyone within Northumbria Police remain firmly with Doreen’s family and friends, who we are continuing to support at this extremely difficult time.”
