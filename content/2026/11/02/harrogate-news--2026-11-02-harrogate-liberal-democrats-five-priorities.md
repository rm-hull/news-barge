The Harrogate Liberal Democrats have outlined five key priorities for the town, covering local services, congestion, housing, the town centre and accountability at North Yorkshire Council.

In their responses to questions about the future of Harrogate, the party has called for greater local control over services, increased investment in preventative services and changes to how development and infrastructure funding are managed.

The proposals also include improving transport connections, supporting independent businesses and giving local representatives greater influence over decisions affecting the town.

## Greater local control and tackling congestion

The Liberal Democrats identified the loss of local control over services as one of the biggest challenges facing Harrogate.

They said they would seek to devolve significant areas of responsibility from North Yorkshire Council where this was supported by Harrogate Town Council and would help retain or restore the town’s distinctive identity.

Congestion was another major concern.

The party said it would undertake a comprehensive review of active travel, with the aim of developing joined-up solutions that allow cars to sit alongside cycling, walking and public transport.

On the town centre, the Liberal Democrats said they would work with North Yorkshire Police, the community safety hub, Harrogate BID and the Harrogate Homeless Project to reduce anti-social behaviour.

They also proposed exploring the creation of an indoor Independent Retail Showcase venue within the town’s Spa complex.

## Council finances and protecting local services

The party was critical of North Yorkshire Council’s financial approach since the creation of the unitary authority.

It argued that the council had failed to invest the financial benefits of local government reorganisation in ways that would deliver long-term financial sustainability and protect core services.

The Liberal Democrats said the creation of the unitary authority had delivered more than £80 million in savings, but claimed there was little evidence of investment to address the growing demand for statutory services.

They believe the council should use some of its strategic reserves to invest in preventative services that could reduce future demand and deliver economic, social and environmental benefits.

The party said it was developing a financial strategy focused on prevention and longer-term sustainability.

## Housing development and infrastructure

Housing and development were also identified as major challenges, with the Liberal Democrats expressing concern about the pace and scale of housebuilding around Harrogate.

The party said it would accelerate the development of the new North Yorkshire Local Plan, arguing that the current situation amounted to a “free-for-all wild west of house building” that risked overwhelming local infrastructure.

It also wants to work with partners to improve transport infrastructure, water and sewerage capacity and electricity connections.

Affordable housing was another priority, particularly for younger people and working families who struggle to live locally.

The party said new homes should also be suitable or adaptable for an ageing population and meet high environmental standards, including the installation of solar panels.

It would also seek to overhaul how developers’ infrastructure contributions are secured and spent, with the aim of bringing forward investment in essential services.

A further proposal involves working with the Combined Authority Mayor to develop hop-on, hop-off community transport linking residential areas.

## Supporting Harrogate town centre and independent businesses

The Liberal Democrats said they would work with local stakeholders to make better use of council-owned buildings and assets, ensuring they contribute to the economic and social life of Harrogate.

They also proposed developing stronger links between the education sector and major local employers to create employment pathways for young people.

The party wants to enhance the town’s cultural and events strategy, placing greater emphasis on Harrogate’s spa heritage and green spaces.

Care providers were also identified as important local employers. The Liberal Democrats said they would work with the sector to improve the recruitment and retention of local staff, including links to affordable housing solutions.

The party said progress could be measured through reductions in vacancies for essential worker roles, increased visitor numbers and improvements in local pride.

## Greater accountability and local decision-making

The final priority focuses on giving local representatives and residents more influence over decisions made by North Yorkshire Council.

The Liberal Democrats said they would strengthen the powers of the Harrogate Area Committee, including allowing it to shape local spending of developers’ Community Infrastructure Levy contributions.

They would also use the committee to work with Harrogate Town Council on priorities within the emerging Town Investment Plan and support the town council if it wished to take control of significant local assets.

Other priorities include improving local special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) services for children and young people.

The party also wants to support young people to play a fuller part in Harrogate’s economic, social and political life through partnerships with businesses and the cultural sector.

It highlighted the importance of supporting people at significant life stages, including retirement and bereavement, to remain connected with their communities.

The Liberal Democrats said progress would be reported through the Area Committee, which they want to see take on a stronger consultative and engagement role.

They argued this would replace what they described as North Yorkshire Council’s current top-down and uniform approach to consultation.

The proposals form the party’s outline of what it would seek to achieve over a four-year term of a Liberal Democrat-led administration at North Yorkshire Council.
