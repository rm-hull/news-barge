A group of York and North Yorkshire business leaders have called for an increase in interest rates to help tackle inflation.

Members of the Yorkshire Shadow Monetary Policy Committee have unanimously backed an increase in interest rates, warning that inflation needs to be brought under control, despite fragile business confidence.

All five members taking part in November’s meeting voted for a modest rise, with concerns about rising costs outweighing the risks that higher borrowing costs could slow investment and growth.

It brings together business representatives to discuss economic conditions and vote on what they believe the Bank of England should do.

Meeting after the Budget and ahead of the Bank’s next interest rate decision, members described delayed investment decisions, a slowing property market and an uncertain outlook, alongside continued growth in some sectors.

Beckie Hart, Regional Director of the CBI, said businesses were showing "resilience but not confidence", with performance varying across Yorkshire and the Humber’s diverse economy.

"Opportunities are coming through, but customers are taking a lot longer to commit, and investment decisions are being delayed as a result," she said.

Despite those pressures, Beckie supported an increase because of the changing inflation outlook and the risk that rising costs would begin to influence behaviour.

"We’re not seeing a second-round effect in wages yet, but I think that we’re going to start to see people changing their behaviours as they start to see costs go up," she said.

Nick Johnson, Corporate Finance Partner at York-based Ryecroft Glenton (RG), offered a more positive assessment of the businesses his team advises, highlighting transaction activity in industrials and industrial services, as well as opportunities in technology, skills and training.

He said businesses were continuing to invest and grow, although the wider environment remained fragile.

Explaining his vote, Nick said: "I think it was a tough balance because you don’t want to choke off growth.

But I think we’ve held for long enough and you’ve got to guard against embedded inflation.

I think what businesses want is certainty over costs and wages and input costs."

Richard Peake, Managing Director of the Helmsley Group, reported slowing residential sales and reduced enquiries from smaller housing developers.

Institutional funders were also becoming more cautious, although York city centre’s commercial property market was holding up comparatively well.

"I’m not seeing a lot of confidence in the market.

I’m certainly seeing delays across the board," he said.

Richard acknowledged there was an argument from a property perspective for holding rates, but believed inflation made a small increase necessary.

"We’ve got to do this now to start to bring inflation down," he said.

Dave Broadbent, Partner at Begbies Traynor, said his firm was busy supporting businesses across the spectrum, from those in financial difficulty to successful companies considering their options following the Budget.

He also highlighted reluctance among businesses to commit to spending, but concluded that higher interest rates were needed.

"I think the only way in which we’re going to try and get the inflation rate down is by unfortunately increasing interest rates," he said.

Graham Robb, Senior Partner of Recognition PR, chaired the discussion and cast the fifth vote for an increase.

He described his own business as steady, with prospective clients taking longer to make decisions.

"Entrepreneurship is generally dented in confidence and I haven’t seen it like this for a long time and I’ve been in business 39 years," he said.

Graham argued that inflation threatened jobs and savings and said the interests of people relying on savings income should also be considered.

"How seriously is the Bank going to be taken if it can’t get inflation under control?" he said.

"We know there’s lots of headwinds, but it’s still got a job to do."
