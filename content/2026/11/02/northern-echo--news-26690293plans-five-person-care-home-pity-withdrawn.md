Plans for a care home for up to five adults have been withdrawn after concerns were raised by Durham County Council officers.

Yeshua Healthcare Limited had applied to use a building on Abbey Road, Pity Me, as a residential care home for adults aged 18 and over.

They would have included a sensory room and office in the rear garden.

But the plan was withdrawn on Friday, October 30.

It comes after Councillor Elizabeth Pears, who represents Framwellgate and Newton Hall, had asked for the application to go before councillors if planning officers recommended approval.

"The plans do not show how the grounds of the building will be adapted to meet the needs of the extra residents, including sufficient parking for staff, visitors, medical and social care professionals to access the home which is on a busy main road," she said.

It comes after Durham County Council's adult social care commissioning team objected to the plans, saying there was no need for another care home in the area.

There are nine care homes providing 382 registered beds in the DH1 area, a report confirmed.

"The application does not evidence unmet need, identify a gap in local provision, or explain how the proposed service would support the council's strategic commissioning intentions," an adult social care commissioning team member said.

Concerns were also raised about parking and the impact of staff, visitors, health professionals and service vehicles travelling to the property.

Council officers questioned whether a five-person care home would be financially sustainable.

They said the smallest existing care home for older people or people with physical disabilities in County Durham had 16 beds.
