A missing man who lives with dementia was found in undergrowth in County Durham after a 12-hour rescue mission by police and other emergency services.

On Saturday (October 31), the Durham Police control room received a call from a lady reporting her elderly dad missing.

He had set off on a short walk back to his house, a route that would normally take him around 20 minutes, but he hadn’t arrived.

Response officers were deployed to the area where he was last seen to carry out enquiries.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657524.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The missing man in the police helicopter *(Image: DURHAM CONSTABULARY)*

A specialist Initial Missing Person Search Advisor (IMPSA) and Police Search Advisor (PolSA) were brought in to coordinate the search, and support was also sought from a force drone pilot, dog section, Teesdale and Weardale Search and Mountain Rescue Team and the National Police Air Service HQ (NPAS).

Several members of the public contacted the control room to report possible sightings of the man.

Then, just before 12.20am on Sunday (November 1) morning, NPAS spotted a possible heat source coming from a large, overgrown wooded area, near where the man had last been seen around 12 hours earlier.

They directed officers on the ground to the location, where it was confirmed to be the missing man, who was unable to get out of thick undergrowth and was amongst thorn bushes.

Police say he was "fit and well, other than a few cuts and scrapes."

Officers helped him through the dense undergrowth and to the only safe way out of the area by police helicopter, where he got a lift to Aykley Heads in Durham before heading to University Hospital of North Durham to be checked over.

Following the search, Durham Police put it on its social media page, adding: "We wish him and his family well."
