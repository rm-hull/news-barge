A lifelong Tony Hadley fan who is battling an inoperable brain tumour has revealed that a fundraising concert featuring one of Britain's best-known pop voices has raised an incredible £50,000 for charity.

Mike Carr, 58, from Northallerton, organised the event with friends and family after doctors initially blamed his symptoms on a trapped nerve before diagnosing an inoperable brain tumour.

The concert, featuring Tony Hadley, was held at The Forum in Northallerton in September and raised far more than expected for charities and good causes.

Mike has now revealed how much money has been raised: “We've raised £50,000.

"We had the gig in September in Northallerton and it was a fantastic event.

“The feedback was absolutely amazing.

"The response from the town and everyone was brilliant.

"The support and the cooperation and the way that the town has pulled together is unbelievable.

"To generate that sort of money is beyond belief.

"It's 10 times what we were expecting to make.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21661529.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mike Carr at the fundraising event. *(Image: AF PHOTOGRAPHY)*

The money will be shared between charities including Friends of the Friarage and Queen's Medical Centre in Nottingham to support cancer research and healthcare services.

Tony Hadley’s involvement made the event’s success even more special for Mike, who has admired the singer for decades.

Mike has been a devoted fan since 1983, and the pair became friends after meeting following a 1998 concert at Tall Trees in Yarm.

Mike’s symptoms first appeared at home in December last year.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21661533.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: AF PHOTOGRAPHY)*

He said: "I went to make a cup of tea and my right elbow froze and my hand started shaking.

"I went to the doctor the next day and they sent me to physio. I had three sessions and they diagnosed it as a trapped nerve.

"In the new year, after we thought we'd fixed it, it happened again.

"I made another appointment to go to the physio but then I had a really violent attack when I visited my mum in her care home.

"My brother took me to hospital and three hours later I was told it was a brain tumour."

The diagnosis came as a devastating shock.

"It wasn't something I was expecting," he said. "It absolutely threw me.
