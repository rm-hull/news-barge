One of York's largest employers has reported "disappointing" profitability in its latest financial accounts.

The Pavers shoe company has reported a drop in pre-tax profits in the year to January 31 of £6.1million compared to £9.6million the previous year.

This was despite turnover increasing from £171.6million to £179.8million.

Managing director Stuart Paver said: "Revenue growth was insufficient to offset the significant increase in employment-related and operational costs sustained.

"The current commercial and regulatory environment is highly-cost inflationary and consumer confidence is tepid. The increase in national insurance costs is particularly burdensome."

Mr Paver continued: "Despite extensive efforts from all involved, efficiency savings were insufficiently prompt to deliver in-year cost savings sought.

"That said, several initiatives undertaken during the year are bearing fruit this year and performance YTD is encouraging.

"Our customers' preferences are evolving at pace but we continue to focus on sourcing quality product, offering good value, and investing in customer service.

"There is still a lot of work to do in relation to our systems and data, and more broadly on increasing overall productivity; however, opportunities for growth continue to exist across most of our channels."

He added: "In what was a very challenging year, we remain grateful for the hard work and loyalty of our people. They continue to be highly engaged with the business. Their commitment, combined with positive relationships with our suppliers and stakeholders, is at the heart of our success."

"We are delighted to have donated a further £183,000 to our Foundation in the year to support causes to our colleagues and shareholders."

The family-owned Pavers has its headquarters at Catherine House, Northminster Business Park, Poppleton. It has a store in the city centre and at Designer Outlet York as part of a chain of more than 100 retail outlets.

The annual results also reveal total staff numbers fell from 1,913 to 1,878.

But wages and salaries increased from £34,051,000 to £35,529,000.

Social security costs increased from £2,619,000 to £3,530,000.

Pension contributions increased from £790,000 to £856,000.

Directors' remuneration, including pension contributions, fell from £648,000 to £595,000.

The highest paid director received £356,000 in 2025, plus a £37,000 pension contribution.

This increased to £387,000 and £53,000 in the latest financial year.

The corporation tax paid on current profits fell from £2,545,000 in 2025 to £833,000.

After various adjustments and deferments, the total tax charge fell from £2,628,000 in 2025 to £360,000, the annual results also reported.
