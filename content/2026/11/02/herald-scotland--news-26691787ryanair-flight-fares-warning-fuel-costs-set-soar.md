Travellers should “brace for a sharp increase in the cost of flying”.

That is the warning from one analyst in the wake of a key update today from Ryanair.

The budget airline said its fuel bill could go up by around €2 billion in the year to March 2028, with this estimate based on “current forward prices”.

Ryanair highlighted its belief that European short-haul airfares “will increase materially to reflect these much higher jet-fuel prices and competitor capacity reductions”.

Dan Coatsworth, head of markets at stockbroker AJ Bell, said: “Travellers should brace themselves for a sharp increase in the cost of flying unless oil prices fall substantially from current levels.

“Many airlines have navigated this year’s oil price spike thanks to forward hedging, having locked in large swathes of fuel costs before the Iran war drove up energy prices. Those hedges will soon roll off, meaning the airline industry faces significant cost pain.”

He added: “The natural response will be to jack up prices and to cut back on capacity, but that might not be enough to offset the extra costs.

“It’s possible that baggage charges go up, onboard food and drink costs increase, and airlines seek to operate with fewer staff and freeze the wages of those who do get shifts.”

Ryanair today set out the impact of the Middle East conflict on fuel costs and its business as it announced a 15% fall in first-half profits after tax to €2.15bn, even though revenue rose 3% to €10.08bn.

In the three months to September, the second quarter of the airline's financial year, Ryanair’s after-tax profits were down 6% on the same period a year earlier at €1.61bn in spite of a 5% rise in passenger numbers to 64.5 million and a 4% increase in revenue to €5.7bn.

The budget airline said: “Unless there is a swift resolution of the Middle East conflict, we expect jet-fuel prices to remain elevated well into 2027. As low-cost oil hedges roll off ahead of [summer] 27, all EU airlines face a significantly higher fuel bill next year.”

Ryanair added: “While we believe European short-haul airfares will increase materially to reflect these much higher jet-fuel prices and competitor capacity reductions, this is still unlikely to provide a full offset for an estimated [circa] €2bn increase, based on current forward prices, in our FY28 fuel bill.

“To help address this, we’re reviewing all costs across group airlines with a particular focus on airport churn negotiations, as the fuel-efficient MAX-10s deliver from spring 2027; maturing labour agreements, which may require temporary pay freezes; lower crewing ratios and increased use of AI (artificial intelligence) to drive further operational and back-office/support functions productivity.”

Mr Coatsworth said: “Ryanair says its fuel bill could go up by €2 billion in its financial year to March 2028. It must dig deep to find cost savings, such as pleading with airports to reduce fees and taxes, and using more AI to improve office productivity.

“The scale of the potential cost hike casts a dark shadow over Ryanair’s second-quarter results which showed it flew more passengers and generated more revenue, but neither grew by as much as operating costs.”

He added: “Ryanair is no stranger to navigating difficult environments and while it may experience some pain near-term, the business has form in emerging triumphant from challenging situations. Customers are going to have to lump it when it comes to the extra cost of flying. It’s unlikely that they will be able to go to a rival and make significant savings as the entire industry is in the same boat.”
