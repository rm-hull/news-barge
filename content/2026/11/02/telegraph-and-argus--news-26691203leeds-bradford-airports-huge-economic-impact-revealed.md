LEEDS Bradford Airport injects £737 million into the regional economy each year and supports more than 9,100 jobs.

The figures come from a new independent economic assessment being launched today at a stakeholder event in Leeds.

The event was attended by West Yorkshire Mayor Tracy Brabin and senior figures from business, academia, and government.

Vincent Hodder, chief executive of Leeds Bradford Airport, said: "This report demonstrates that Leeds Bradford Airport is far more than a transport hub.

"It is a vital piece of economic infrastructure that supports thousands of jobs, connects Yorkshire businesses to global markets and helps attract investment, visitors and talent into our region."

The report, Driving West Yorkshire’s Economy, highlights the airport’s role in supporting business productivity, attracting investment, enabling international trade, and driving tourism growth across the region.

Leeds Bradford Airport handled nearly 4.5 million passengers in 2025 and aims to grow that number to around seven million by the early 2030s.

Ms Brabin said: "Leeds Bradford Airport is West Yorkshire’s gateway to the world, and this report demonstrates the important role it plays in supporting jobs, growing our economy and connecting our businesses and communities.

"Strengthening connectivity, both within our region and internationally, is essential as we compete on the global stage for trade and investment.

"We are working hard to realise ambitions for a parkway station to serve the airport and will continue working with all partners to find the best solution to improve access."

The report estimates that airport-enabled business productivity generates £129 million of economic value and supports around 750 jobs in West Yorkshire.

Tourism linked to the airport contributes around £75 million and supports 2,840 jobs.

It also calls for major investment in surface access, including the proposed LBA Parkway Station, which would connect the airport to the rail network.

The report claims this could unlock even greater economic benefits by improving sustainable travel options and strengthening links between towns, cities, and key employment hubs.

Phil Ward, chief operations officer at Jet2, said: "We have been proudly operating from Leeds Bradford Airport since 2003, flying millions of customers on their well-deserved holidays in that time, and our longstanding commitment to the airport and the wider region has never been stronger."

Mr Ward said the airport is a "critical economic asset" and that its ongoing success will continue to benefit West Yorkshire and beyond.

Leeds Bradford Airport is currently investing more than £200 million in facilities and infrastructure.
