Business leaders from across York came together last week for the latest The Press business networking event, held in partnership with York St John University and York College.

The evening brought businesses and education together for networking alongside a programme of speakers covering workplace culture, employee health, the charity sector and opportunities for employers to work more closely with education.

Speakers included Helen Burke, Dr Will Robertson, Andy Knowles and Sue Newton-Denton.

The event also challenged businesses to consider how easily prospective customers can find and choose them online.

Delegates were encouraged to look at their businesses from a customer’s perspective, considering their search visibility, Google Business Profile, reviews, mobile website experience and how easy it is for customers to take the next step.

Steve Lowe, sales director at The Press spoke about the need to have your website built for AI and that at LOCALiQ, he has specialists in helping SME’s navigate the word of AI and SEO.

Helen Burke, an Investors in People Practitioner, explored what employee behaviour can reveal about levels of trust within an organisation.

Drawing on Investors in People’s The Trust Paradox research, she explained that while trust is widely regarded as important, the way employees actually behave can tell a different story.

The research highlighted that 61 per cent regularly “cover their backs”, while 65 per cent avoid raising concerns or speaking up.

Ms Burke outlined practical approaches for businesses, including measuring rather than assuming trust, equipping managers to build it, involving employees earlier in decision-making and creating conditions where people feel able to speak openly.

Dr Will Robertson, Clinical Director at Stonegate Medical, turned the focus to the health of business owners and their teams.

His presentation highlighted the impact ill health can have on employers and explored how private GP provision, medicals and occupational health can complement existing healthcare arrangements.

He cited an estimated £85 billion annual cost of workforce ill health to UK employers and ONS figures showing 148.8 million working days were lost to sickness or injury in 2025.

Andy Knowles, Chief Executive of York Against Cancer, shared his journey from a long commercial career into the charity sector.

He spoke candidly about the rewards and challenges of the move, arguing that business can bring commercial discipline, strategic thinking and strong systems to charities, while charities can teach businesses valuable lessons about purpose, trust, engagement and community.

His message to delegates was to “follow purpose, not just progression” and to recognise how valuable commercial skills can be within the charity sector.

Completing the speaker programme, Sue Newton-Denton, Business Partnerships Manager at York College, outlined the range of opportunities for local employers to engage with the college, including apprenticeships, work placements, site visits, guest speaking, employer advisory groups and recruitment activity.

She also highlighted forthcoming employer events and opportunities for businesses to support York College’s 200-year celebrations.

The evening underlined the purpose of The Press business networking events, which is all about connecting local businesses, sharing practical ideas and strengthening relationships between York’s business and education communities.

The events will continue in 2027 and the next one is set to return in January.
