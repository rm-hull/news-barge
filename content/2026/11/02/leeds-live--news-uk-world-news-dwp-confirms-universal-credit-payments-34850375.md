# DWP confirms Universal Credit payments being 'recovered' with claimants warned

## The Department for Work and Pensions has confirmed it is recovering Universal Credit overpayments - including those caused by official error

The Department for Work and Pensions has provided an update following questions about measures being taken to guarantee Universal Credit guidance remains accurate. Liberal Democrats MP Tom Gordon raised the matter in the Commons last week.

Mr Gordon enquired: "to ask the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions, what steps his Department takes to make sure Universal Credit helpline advice on capital disregards for compensation awards is accurate; and whether he will waive overpayments where a claimant relied on incorrect advice from his Department?".

Labour Party minister and DWP representative Sir Stephen Timms delivered the response, reports Birmingham Live.

Sir Stephen explained: "The Department supports Universal Credit helpline staff through operational guidance, learning products and regular policy updates to help ensure advice is accurate and consistent. Staff have access to guidance covering the treatment of capital, including compensation payments and capital disregard provisions where the relevant conditions are met."

In a concerning development for DWP Universal Credit recipients, Sir Stephen - who is currently overseeing a Personal Independence Payment review - also promised the benefits department and welfare officers would be "recovering" such payments. The cabinet member went on to say: "Universal Credit overpayments are recoverable, including those arising from official error. The Department has an obligation to protect public funds and to ensure that, wherever possible, overpayment and penalty debt is recovered.

"Overpayment recovery is subject to various legislative limitations and safeguards. It is DWP policy to recover all debt where it is reasonable and cost effective to do so. Debts should be recovered as quickly and cost effectively as possible without causing undue financial hardship to debtors."

Timms added: "Any difficulties in repaying debt should be discussed directly with the Department, where individual circumstances can be." This comes amid mounting pressure on Andy Burnham to rein in the spiralling welfare budget, with Timms' review into PIP expected to play a pivotal role in addressing the issue.
