A global business leader and philanthropist has received an honorary degree from the University of Strathclyde in recognition of his achievements in international business and support for education.

Dr Charles Huang was awarded the title of Doctor of the University at a ceremony at Glasgow’s Barony Hall, presented by Professor Stephen McArthur, principal and vice-chancellor.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21661435.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Dr Stuart Fancey, University Secretary; Professor David Hillier, Executive Dean, Strathclyde Business School; Professor Stephen McArthur, Principal & Vice-Chancellor; Dr Charles Huang, Founder and Chairman, Pasaca Capital; David Clark, Convener of Court; and Professor Sir Jim McDonald, Chairman of Scottish Enterprise and Emeritus Principal. *(Image: Jason Fong)*

Dr Huang studied at Strathclyde after receiving a British Council scholarship, graduating with an MBA in 1989 and a PhD in marketing in 1994.

He later founded Pasaca Capital Inc., a California-based investment fund with a global portfolio spanning medical devices, pharmaceuticals, technology, industrial automation and entertainment.

In 2021, Dr Huang donated £50 million to Strathclyde through the Charles Huang Foundation, the largest single gift in the university’s history.

The donation supported the establishment of the Stephen Young Institute for International Business, the Stephen Young Global Leaders Scholarship Programme and the Stephen Young Entrepreneurship Awards.

It also funded the development of the Charles Huang Advanced Technology & Innovation Centre (CHATIC), which is being built in the former Strathclyde Students’ Union building on John Street.

Programmes supported by the donation have so far funded 78 entrepreneurship awards, eight PhD students, 15 MBA awardees and two MSc scholars.

During his visit to Glasgow, Dr Huang toured the CHATIC site and met scholars and entrepreneurs who have benefited from the programmes.

Businesses supported through the Stephen Young Entrepreneurship Awards include Biosers, which is developing food-safety technology and has secured a further £400,000 in funding.

Upper-limb prosthetics business Metacarpal has raised more than £1.3 million, while digital health company Seluna is undergoing NHS clinical validation for a paediatric sleep apnoea diagnostic tool.

CHATIC is intended to provide space for researchers, students, businesses and partners to collaborate on innovation and entrepreneurship.

Born in Hubei, China, Dr Huang also holds a bachelor’s degree in economics from Wuhan University.

Professor Stephen McArthur, principal and vice-chancellor of the University of Strathclyde, said: "Charles’s career has been marked by ambition, enterprise and a strong international outlook.

"From his time as a student at Strathclyde, he has gone on to achieve considerable success as a business leader and investor, operating across industries and markets around the world.

"Just as importantly, he has used that success to create opportunities for others.

"His support for students, entrepreneurs and innovators reflects his belief in the power of education, enterprise and useful learning to change lives.

"This honorary degree recognises the breadth of Charles’s achievements and his commitment to helping others fulfil their potential.

"We are proud to count him among our alumni and our honorary graduates."

Dr Charles Huang said: "Strathclyde gave me the education, global perspective and opportunities that helped shape my life and career, so it is a great honour to receive this degree.

"I am proud to see my support helping talented students and entrepreneurs, advancing innovation and enabling people to turn their ambitions into achievements that can benefit society."
