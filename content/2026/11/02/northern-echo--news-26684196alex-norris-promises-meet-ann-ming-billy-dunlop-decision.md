Justice Secretary Alex Norris has promised to meet Ann Ming after a decision is made on whether to free “evil” killer Billy Dunlop.

William Dunlop was jailed for life in 2006 after murdering his ex-girlfriend, young mother Julie Hogg, in Billingham in 1989.

Last week, Dunlop bid for full release at a parole hearing in London after spending almost three decades behind bars.

The now 63-year-old, who said his outlook on life has changed since the death of his son last year, argued he is no longer the “violent” person he once was and was ready for freedom.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21617973.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ann Ming in London following killer Billy Dunlop's parole hearing *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

A decision is yet to be made by the parole board, who have previously twice recommended Dunlop be moved to an open prison.

The decisions were blocked by two Justice Secretaries at the time, and Ann hopes that, should he be recommended for release, current Justice Secretary Alex Norris will do the same.

This week, Mr Norris promised to meet with Ann and her family, including grandson Kevin Hogg, once a decision by the parole board has been made.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21650446.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Justice Secretary Alex Norris *(Image: Aaron Chown/PA Wire)*

In a letter sent to Stockton West MP Matt Vickers, he said the decision being provisional for 21 days means he will have time to consider whether to ask the board to reconsider.

Mr Norris also has a relatively new power to refer the decision to the High Court, if he believes Dunlop’s freedom could undermine public confidence in the parole system.

Kevin, who was three at the time of his mum’s death, said he would be “constantly on edge, wondering what he is doing” if Dunlop is released.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/3899449.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: (c) NORTH NEWS & PICTURES)*

Ann admitted that it was “not looking very promising” following the hearing, arguing he “should be in prison for life” for public protection.

Kevin said that “no one is denying he can’t be rehabilitated” but reiterated how Dunlop lied for almost 20 years, having twice been tried for 1989 murder but being acquitted.

In the years that followed, Dunlop went on to laugh at the law when he boasted to a prison officer that he had killed Julie while in jail for another offence.

He only formally confessed after the double jeopardy law was overturned following Ann’s campaign, backed by the Echo, and was jailed for life with a minimum term of 15 years in 2006.

He said he had done a “lot of reflecting” following the death of his son and wanted Julie’s family to "just be able to get on with their life".

If released, Dunlop would be subject to a string of conditions including a lie detector test, 12 months in a specialist hostel, tagging, and a curfew.

His prison offender manager (POM), community offender manager (COM), a prison psychologist, and an independent psychologist all recommended him for release.
