SPARK's owners have warned that the venue could be forced to leave York after 2030 if it cannot secure a future at York Central or another site in the city.

The food and drink venue's co-founders Sam Leach and Tom McKenzie said they had to be “realistic” about SPARK’s proposed future within the York Central development – a £2 billion regeneration project close to York Railway Station.

Despite holding “positive” talks with the lead developers of the project, the pair insist that “nothing is guaranteed” in terms of a confirmed future location after 2030, when SPARK’s licence with City of York Council concludes.




The owners said: “We are realistic about York Central. We’re a small player on a very big site.

“If there isn’t a home for SPARK on York Central or elsewhere after 2030, we may have to leave York.

“We desperately don’t want that, but we have to plan for all eventualities, and the business will grow regardless.”

SPARK secured permission from the council to stay in Piccadilly until September 2030.

Sam added that this was “fantastic” for safeguarding jobs and adding footfall to the area.

### 'We want to ensure it thrives until 2030'

The owners said that the extension to their licence, which was originally meant for three years, has allowed them to lodge a planning application, to revamp SPARK and extend its first-floor area to add outdoor seating.

They said: “We’ve applied for planning permission to make general improvements to the venue including making the venue more accessible.

“We want to ensure it thrives until 2030.”

Sam added: “We’d love to stay in York for as long as we’re needed and keep adding value.

“We’ve spent eight years honing a model that works by giving independent businesses an affordable first step, a venue that pays its way without extracting profit, and a social enterprise that puts its surplus back into the community.”

Last year, SPARK attracted 430,000 visitors, with its independent businesses generating more than £5 million in revenue.

Since opening, the social enterprise has hosted 65 businesses, 26 of which have gone on to permanent premises, and provided free or subsidised space to more than 500 groups.

Sam said: “We need room to grow into as there is demand for more businesses to launch with us that we can’t currently fulfil.

“It is a tough climate and there is apprehension with businesses taking on longer-term permanent premises, but there is still demand for start-up spaces which we exist to provide.”

### 'York is where we started and where we’d most like to stay'

Sam and Tom said they have been in regular contact with City of York Council – a major collaborator on the York Central Partnership and “several” of the lead York Central developers.

They hoped to pick up conversations after the scheme’s phase one was approved in September this year.

They said: “We have no intention of SPARK as a business stopping in 2030, the question for us is whether that’s in York.

“We are from here and we love this city.

“Whatever happens in Piccadilly or York Central, SPARK will keep evolving and finding new ways to back small businesses and bring people together.

“York is where we started and where we’d most like to stay and we’ll continue to make the case for this being a long-term home, but 2030 won’t be the end of the road for us.”
