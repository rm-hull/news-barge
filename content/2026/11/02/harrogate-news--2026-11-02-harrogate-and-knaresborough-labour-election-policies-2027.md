Harrogate and Knaresborough Labour has outlined its priorities ahead of the 2027 local elections, identifying transport, affordable housing and environmental protection among the main challenges facing Harrogate and Knaresborough.

In responses to five questions from The Harrogate Informer, the party set out its positions on council spending, housing development, the town centre, local employment and accountability.

The response was submitted by Chris Watt, Chair of Harrogate & Knaresborough Labour Party.

## Transport, roads and public transport

Labour identified traffic, transport and roads as its first major priority.

The party said public transport needed to work better for local people, particularly young people, older residents, disabled people and those who cannot drive.

It pointed to the role of Labour Mayor of North Yorkshire David Skaith, saying he was using new powers from the Government to ensure buses serve communities that need them. Labour also welcomed greater public control of the railways.

The party said North Yorkshire Council should improve coordination when utility and broadband companies need to dig up roads, helping to minimise disruption to residents.

## Affordable housing and environmental protection

Housing was identified as the second major challenge. Labour welcomed the Government’s commitment to increasing the supply of genuinely affordable homes and building more council houses.

The party said local authorities should embrace the opportunity to increase council housing provision.

Protecting the local environment was identified as its third priority. Labour linked this to better public transport, cycling and walking provision, as well as greater public control of waterways.

The party specifically highlighted sewage discharges by Yorkshire Water into rivers such as the Nidd.

> **Chris Watt, Chair of Harrogate & Knaresborough Labour Party, said in the party’s response:**
>
> We also need greater public control of our waterways, to tackle the disgrace of Yorkshire Water’s increasing sewage discharges into rivers like the Nidd.

## Council spending and local services

Asked how Labour would balance North Yorkshire Council’s budget while maintaining local services, the party said improving roads and the environment should be priorities.

It highlighted funding available through the Mayor’s office to tackle potholes, arguing that councillors should work together to ensure the money is allocated locally.

The party also pointed to the contribution of community groups and volunteers, citing residents in Starbeck who had carried out improvements to a council-owned playground.

It also highlighted volunteers who had cleaned up the Greenway linking Starbeck, Harrogate town centre and Ripley.

Labour said local councillors should champion and encourage these groups while pressing the council to provide more support for their initiatives through partnership working.

## Housing development and infrastructure

On future development in and around Harrogate, Labour said more genuinely affordable and sustainable housing was needed, particularly for families and young people.

The party said brownfield land should be prioritised when considering new developments, with proper consideration given to the need for additional facilities, including health centres, public transport access and playgrounds.

Labour also criticised traffic management around previous major developments in the Kingsley area.

> **Chris Watt, Chair of Harrogate & Knaresborough Labour Party, said:**
>
> Proper traffic management needs to be put in place by the Council, something that was sadly lacking, for instance, when permissions were granted for major developments in the Kingsley area, which both existing residents, and those who have moved into the area, are now paying the price for.

## Harrogate town centre and the local economy

Labour was critical of the handling of the Harrogate Station Gateway project, describing the process as botched and saying it had cost local taxpayers significant sums of money while delaying what could have been a boost for the local economy.

The party said lessons needed to be learned and that partnerships should be rebuilt so local businesses and residents could benefit as the project progresses.

Labour also called for councils to make use of opportunities to support business and employment, including working with local companies to promote apprenticeships and vocational and technical qualifications.

The response referred to Andy Burnham’s drive for greater local powers to support business and employment, alongside the wider devolution agenda.

## Accountability and delivery

Asked what measurable commitments Labour would make ahead of the next local elections, the party criticised what it described as disagreements between the Liberal Democrat-led Harrogate Town Council and Conservative-led North Yorkshire Council.

Labour said the two authorities had blamed each other for a lack of visible improvements in the area. It also claimed that Liberal Democrat councillors on the town council had turned down an opportunity to take on additional powers.

The party said its councillors would take a different approach, focusing on representing residents and working constructively across the different levels of local government.

> **Chris Watt, Chair of Harrogate & Knaresborough Labour Party, said:**
>
> Labour councillors on North Yorkshire Council, as well as on Harrogate and Knaresborough Town Councils, would be proper community champions, standing up for their residents instead of focusing on petty party squabbles.”
>
> We would be able to work constructively with the Labour Mayor, David Skaith, other Labour councillors across North Yorkshire and collectively with Labour Government Ministers to deliver real improvements to our area.

Labour’s responses form part of The Harrogate Informer’s questions to political parties ahead of the next local elections, giving residents an opportunity to examine their priorities and proposals.
