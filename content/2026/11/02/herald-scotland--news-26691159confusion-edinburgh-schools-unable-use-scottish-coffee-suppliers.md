**A SNP councillor has demanded answers amid claims students enrolled in barista courses at Edinburgh schools have been “banned” from using Scottish-based suppliers.**

Speaking at a full City of Edinburgh Council meeting last week, Councillor Adam Nols-McVey said that some secondary schools in the capital had been told to use American suppliers instead of local companies.

He said: “Some schools in Edinburgh are running barista courses as part of their curriculum, teaching kids about vital employment skills, which will take them through education and beyond, also teaching them about supply and demand, and a whole host of other business factors.

“Can I ask if the council leader shares my dismay that schools have been banned from using Scottish-based suppliers, like We Love Coffee, and forced on to a contract for American suppliers which fundamentally teaches children the opposite lessons of what we should be teaching them, and which goes against our own policies around circular economy and local supply chains?”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/13447305.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SNP councillor Adam Nols-McVey. *(Image: PA)*

The Herald understands that the council’s education department restricted the use of local suppliers on the grounds that preference for such firms was not expressly outlined in the council’s procurement framework.

In response, council leader Jane Meagher said she would “be more than happy” to discuss the issue with Councillor Nols-McVey in greater detail.

She told councillors: “I would broadly support anything that enhances and supports the local economy and local supply chain.

“Some of our procurement practices lead to rather interesting outcomes. Perhaps that is part of the problem, I don’t know.

“I would be more than happy to discuss this further with Councillor Nols-McVey so we can collectively seek resolutions...

“Let’s have a look at it and see what can be done.”

Several Edinburgh secondary schools currently include barista training on their curricula.

For example, Queensferry High School offers the coffee-making training programme as a Level 5 qualification.

According to the school, the unit covers “how to operate specialist equipment to prepare and serve coffee and a variety of other drinks both hot and cold” and helps pupils prepare “future employment opportunities in the hospitality industry”, including part-time work during university or college.

Similarly, Boroughmuir High School offers the course with support from the King’s Trust.

The school says the programme teaches students about “employment skills, sustainable working, sustainable products”.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20390810.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Barista training has sparked a row over procurement rules. *(Image: Supplied)*

Speaking after the meeting, Councillor Nols-McVey told The Herald: “The council has policies about using local suppliers where possible and Scottish-based suppliers to support our own national economy.

“Forcing our schools to use American-based suppliers doesn’t save a penny in cost but does rob our young people of a vital lesson about choosing local and choosing quality.

“I welcome the council’s commitment to looking at this issue since I’ve raised it and hope we can brew a solution very quickly.”

The council’s Sustainable Procurement Strategy, authored by Councillor Mandy Watt, convener of the Finance and Resources Committee, highlights a desire to promote small businesses.

“By prioritising local suppliers wherever possible, we aim to ensure the council’s procurement helps Edinburgh businesses and the third sector to grow and thrive,” Councillor Watt wrote in 2025.

“[This is] an approach which will strengthen our local economy and build resilience and opportunities for contractors within our city.”

However, the strategy goes on to note: “The council has a statutory duty to deliver best value, meaning delivering continuous improvement and obtaining the best value in terms of the optimum balance between quality, cost and sustainability."

According to the local authority's most-recent annual procurement report, the council awarded 202 contracts worth a total of £647.9m in 2024/25.

More broadly, the report states: "The council’s third party spend included a total of 3,909 suppliers, of which 687 were classified as local, accounting for 35.9% of the total supplier spend (excluding spend through purchase card)."
