Linton-on-Ouse asylum accommodation – two Conservative shadow ministers visited Linton-on-Ouse on Saturday, 31 October, to meet residents and hear concerns about the Home Office proposal to accommodate up to 1,200 male asylum seekers at the former RAF site.

Joy Morrissey, Shadow Minister for Women and Equalities, and Chris Philp, Shadow Home Secretary, were welcomed to the village by the Linton-on-Ouse Action Group.

The visit comes amid continuing opposition to the **Linton-on-Ouse asylum accommodation plan**, with the action group saying the proposal has not been adequately discussed with the local community and that the site is unsuitable for the intended use.

The group says there are concerns about the capacity of local infrastructure, including water and electricity supplies, as well as the availability of healthcare and policing resources.

It also argues that the proposed arrangements would involve substantial public expenditure. The action group estimates that the Home Office plan could cost £403 million, equivalent to £853 per asylum seeker per night. These figures are the group’s own assessment of the proposed expenditure.

## Action group says cross-party opposition remains

The Linton-on-Ouse Action Group described opposition to the proposal as cross-party, thanking local representatives from different political backgrounds for their support. It specifically acknowledged its local Conservative councillor and MP, alongside the Labour Mayor and Deputy Mayor and the Labour MP for York.

The group said the visit gave the two Conservative shadow ministers an opportunity to see the site and hear directly from residents about their concerns.

> **Professor Olga Matthias, of the Linton-on-Ouse Action Group, said:**
>
> This is the wrong plan in the wrong place, and everyone can see it now. The only people who haven’t been here are Labour Ministers who have been invited thousands of times and the Prime Minister, who still will not visit despite his public statement.

The group also referred to comments made by Andy Burnham during an interview with the BBC’s Laura Kuenssberg on 27 September, when he said he would listen to concerns. It said Asylum Minister Anna Turley had also indicated that a senior Home Office official would meet the local MP.

However, the action group said it was still waiting for those commitments to be fulfilled and that key decision-makers had yet to visit the village.

## Home Office and Andy Burnham invited to visit

Following Saturday’s visit, the action group extended an invitation to Andy Burnham and Home Office ministers to come to Linton-on-Ouse, review the evidence presented by residents and discuss the practical concerns surrounding the proposal.

The group said it wanted decision-makers to see the site and listen to the community before any further decisions were taken.

The visit by Morrissey and Philp represents the involvement of Conservative opposition politicians in the dispute. The action group’s statement describes the wider opposition as cross-party, although the visit itself does not establish the position of the Government or the Home Office.

The Linton-on-Ouse Action Group is continuing to campaign against the proposed accommodation scheme.
