*HaysMac is a Business Reporter client*

Artificial intelligence is transforming business, but not in the way the headlines suggest. Too often, AI is framed as a threat – a force set to render human roles obsolete. Adopted with careful consideration, AI is a tool that amplifies human capability, reshapes strategy and unlocks growth.

The evidence is clear. McKinsey’s 2025 global survey shows that 78 per cent of organisations now use AI in at least one business function, with 71 per cent deploying generative AI tools to enhance operations. Gartner predicts that by late 2026, 40 per cent of business workloads will involve AI, signalling a shift from experimentation to integration. These technologies aren’t replacing humans but instead enabling people to do what they do best: think critically, innovate and build relationships.

At HaysMac, AI isn’t just a tool – it’s a strategic amplifier. Across departments, certain tasks that once consumed hours are now completed in a fraction of the time, freeing teams to focus on work that truly drives value for clients. From advising on complex accounting treatments to leading innovative problem-solving, employees can focus on where human judgment and creativity matter most. Our philosophy to onboarding new AI tools is focused on unlocking potential and accelerating impact.

This shift demands a fundamental rethink of skills. As AI reshapes workflows, roles will inevitably change, and without deliberate upskilling some may become redundant.

As leaders, we recognise that responsibility sits with us. AI capability cannot be assumed; it must be actively developed. At HaysMac, we are embedding AI literacy, critical thinking and data fluency into training, ensuring our people can interpret outputs, challenge assumptions and apply judgement. Their work will become more advisory, strategic and client-centric; an evolution essential to sustaining relevance and long-term value.

The human dimension remains irreplaceable. Emotional intelligence, collaborative thinking and strategic insight are critical in driving the decisions and relationships that define success. AI can process data faster than any human, but it cannot replicate trust, judgement or the nuanced understanding of a client’s sector, market or ambition.

Of course, AI adoption carries risk. Poor data, weak governance and ethical missteps can have serious consequences. However, HaysMac’s Risk Assurance & Advisory Services (RAAS) team plays a central role in helping clients navigate complexity. From internal audit and cyber-security assessments to enterprise risk management, the RAAS team ensures that companies can scale AI responsibly while maintaining robust controls, compliance and strategic clarity.

The implications for the workforce are equally profound. Research shows that by 2030, 70 per cent of the skills used in most jobs will have changed, with AI driving much of this evolution. But this doesn’t diminish the value of human skills – it accelerates their development. Staff can engage more with clients, network effectively and tackle creative, strategic challenges sooner than ever before.

AI is not an endpoint. It is a strategic lever which acts as a partner in innovation, decision-making and growth. The businesses that thrive will be those that embrace AI with intent, not fear; that see it as a tool to enhance, not replace, the talent, judgement and insight that define their competitive advantage.
