## The government has announced measures, including changing the university admissions process, ensuring more teachers and capping the number of pupils per class.

French students returning from autumn holidays blocked some high schools on Monday, determined to reinvigorate a protest movement despite government concessions during the break.

Just 1% of France's 3,700 high schools for 15 to 18-year-olds called lycées were blockaded, the education ministry said.

Pupils maintained their protest after hundreds of thousands of people, including students, took to the streets across France on Saturday for a third consecutive weekend of demonstrations.

The cost-of-living rallies emerged after high school students launched nationwide demonstrations in September over learning conditions and inequality.

The government has announced measures, including changing the university admissions process, ensuring more teachers and capping the number of pupils per class.

But some students say it is not enough.

In the southern city of Marseille, several rubbish bins were stacked in front of the entrance to one high school on Monday morning.

"We've resumed the movement because we were supposedly given answers, but... it's clearly not enough," 16-year-old Meriem told the AFP news agency, perched on one of the dumpsters.

Education Minister Édouard Geffray told the France Inter radio station students should return to the classroom to avoid failing exams.

"I have students who are sitting the baccalaureate at the end of the year and several hundred thousand of them haven't attended classes for four or five weeks," he said.

President Emmanuel Macron has called for classes to resume in a calm manner, arguing that "young people have been heard".

With the European Union's second-largest economy on the brink of recession and under pressure to rein in debt, the government has limited scope to appease student protesters through additional spending.
