It is the Scottish romantic fantasy show credited with sparking worldwide interest in Scottish history and boosting visitor numbers at dozens of historic attractions across Scotland.

Now the long-running time TV series Outlander is to be honoured for its impact on the Scottish screen industry over the past 13 years.

The creators and stars of the show will be recognised for making an “outstanding contribution to television” at the BAFTA Scotland Awards later this month.

**Read more:**

Outlander, which was produced by American entertainment giants Sony Pictures and British outfit Left Bank Pictures, ran for eight seasons, with the finale airing earlier this year.

Special awards are expected to be presented to Sam Heughan and Caitríona Balfe, who played the show’s main characters, Jamie and Claire, along with executive producers Ronald D Moore, who led the development of the TV series, and Matthew B Roberts, Outlander’s showrunner.

They will be following in the footsteps of some of Scotland’s biggest screen stars who have received BAFTA Scotland’s biggest honour, including Billy Connolly, Brian Cox, Robbie Coltrane, Richard Wilson, Bill Paterson, Stanley Baxter, Lorraine Kelly, Shirley Henderson and Ewan McGregor, who was honoured last year.

BAFTA Scotland has also announced that long-serving location manager Lloret MacKenna Dunn will also be recognised for her "outstanding contribution" to the screen industry.

Her credits include Still Game, Only an Excuse?, T2 Trainspotting 2, Outlaw/King, World War Z and Frankenstein.

Outlander was based continually at Wardpark Studios, in Cumbernauld, where filming got underway in 2013 after an empty factory complex was transformed to ensure the show was shot in Scotland, however locations across the country were also used.

A further two series have been made of a spin-off prequel show, Outlander: Blood of My Blood, with the most recently in September.

The national tourism agency VisitScotland, which promotes the country around the world as "the home of Outlander", has hailed the "extraordinary impact" the show has had, including helping to boost the value of "screen tourism" in Scotland to more than £160 million a year.

Among the attractions used for filming were Doune Castle, near Stirling, Blackness Castle and Linlithgow Palace in West Lothian, Newhailes House in East Lothian and Glasgow Cathedral.

An official announcement from BAFTA Scotland said: “Outlander premiered in 2014, captivating millions of viewers worldwide with its sweeping, time-traveling story and the enduring romance between Jamie and Claire.

“Viewers fell in love not only with Jamie and Claire’s romance, but also with the Scottish heritage and traditions depicted within the series.

“Research by VisitScotland highlights that Outlander has become one of the most significant drivers of screen tourism in the country, encouraging visitors to explore historic sites, castles, villages and landscapes across Scotland.

“Outlander’s success has not only had a lasting influence on Scotland’s outward perception world-wide. Its impact on Scotland’s screen industry is undeniable, providing opportunities for local talent to develop and flourish.

“The Outlander Training Programme, established ahead of Outlander’s first series, has supported more than 170 new entrants through intensive on-the-job training alongside some of the industry’s most experienced technicians. The programme continues with the prequel, Outlander: Blood of my Blood.”

Mr Roberts said: “When we first landed in Scotland, we were outsiders – sassenachs – strangers in a strange land telling a very Scottish story.

“What we found was a country that opened its doors and a crew that opened their hearts. They welcomed us, guided us when we needed it, and made us feel like we belonged. That welcome is the real heart of Outlander and Blood of My Blood.

“And it never ended. Thirteen years on and I don’t feel like a visitor when I step off the plane. I feel like I’m home.”

Mr Moore said: “Since we first came to Scotland many years ago and decided to shoot the show here, it has been a tremendous pleasure to work with our Scottish crew, to utilise the Scottish locations and to learn more about Scottish culture and history.

“It’s been a huge joy for all of us on the production, especially those of us from North America, who perhaps did not know as much about Scotland as we should have, but who learned so much over the years.”
