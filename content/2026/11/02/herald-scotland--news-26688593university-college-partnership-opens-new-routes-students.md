Location matters in education, and it often feels especially true in Scotland.

When New College Lanarkshire (NCL) and the University of the West of Scotland (UWS) launched the NCL Undergraduate School in 2024, it was, in many ways, a testament to location's importance and the barriers it can create.

Since its inception, the school has seen more than 1,000 enrol on technical and vocational degrees, from cyber security and computer networking to dental nursing, law and sound production.

In October, the Undergraduate School celebrated the opening of a dedicated learning hub on the UWS Lanarkshire campus, a space to house the University-College partnership pathways.

Those close to the project make clear this is no token step.

Lady Susan Haughey, Lord Lieutenant of Lanarkshire, called the new hub a “truly transformative moment for tertiary education” and for the regional economy.

Alyn Smith, Scottish Government Minister for Tertiary Education, said the new hub sets a “terrific example” for the sector about the potential for collaboration between universities and colleges in working to meet the needs of learners and the economy.

Professor James Miller, Principal and Vice-Chancellor of UWS, said the school is more than a partnership:

“It is a shared commitment to the future of Lanarkshire. By placing learners at the centre of everything we do, we are opening doors to opportunity, raising aspirations and ensuring that talent and potential can flourish, regardless of background or circumstance.”

NCL Principal Christopher Moore said that the UWS-NCL Undergraduate School is “bold in its ambition” and determined to make “career-relevant education more accessible to the people of Lanarkshire.”

![UWS/NCL Undergraduate School](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21657502.jpg?type=mds-article-620)University of the West of Scotland and New College Lanarkshire's Undergraduate School has given students opportunities to pursue degrees with college and university resources. *(Image: NCL)*

For Nicola Mulholland, the college’s associate principal and one of the major forces behind the Undergraduate School, that commitment to the people of Lanarkshire is an essential pillar of the project’s mission.

Roughly 40% of the Undergraduate School’s students come from Scotland’s most deprived postcodes.

As Ms Mulholland explained, those postcodes are significant.

“People who are in that percentile are coming with a lot of barriers. Sometimes they’ve got caring responsibilities. Sometimes they need jobs because they might be the only person in the family that’s bringing in a wage.

“These are individuals that are not without the talent, and they’re not without skills to get a degree. But what they do have is probably a poor experience of school, or barriers to their education, or poverty, or care experience.”

“The whole idea behind the undergraduate school was this shared ambition and passion that both institutions had for bringing education closer, so that people who live in Lanarkshire could get a degree without having to leave the region.”

![UWS/NCL Undergraduate School](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21657501.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nicola Mulholland, NCL associate principal, believes the combination of a college feel and university degrees will have a huge impact on local students. *(Image: NCL)*

She said the most recent step, opening the physical hub, is about “bringing our students an opportunity to have that university experience that complements their college life.”

The location is critical, she said.

“Coming into a college is less daunting. But then we can take them on a journey, and having the hub is our opportunity to open up that journey.”

More than just providing a dedicated space for students enrolled at the Undergraduate School, Ms Mulholland said the hub also gives other college students a chance to familiarise themselves not only with a university setting but also with the pathways and opportunities a degree can offer.

When it originally opened in 2024, the school offered degrees in health, social care, business, digital technologies, science and the arts.

Ms Mulholland said the portfolio has continued to evolve, in true college fashion, to remain responsive to markets, skills needs, and job growth.

“The degrees that we are offering are very much about creating that workforce that Lanarkshire needs and plugging the gaps in the skills, and are focused on helping people get on that career journey.”

The partnership combines university depth with college flexibility, creating opportunities previously unheard of in the area and melding the two institutions' resources, knowledge, and capabilities to build industry-focused pathways for students.

Again, location is crucial.

Before the Undergraduate School, Ms Mulholland said, studying for a university degree as someone from Lanarkshire likely meant travelling.

“They had to get the bus, they had to get the train, probably two, maybe three.”

The creation of the hub does not entirely remove the need to travel, but it significantly shortens the distance without demanding the same sacrifice in terms of degree or pathway choice.

Now, students can start a degree in Lanarkshire and finish it there, with a university campus on their doorstep that comes with the support and feel of their local college.
