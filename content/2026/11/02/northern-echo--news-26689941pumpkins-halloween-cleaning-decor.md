Halloween clear-outs could leave North East households facing more than just overflowing bins, as residents are being warned that common festive rubbish may not belong in the recycling.

The advice comes as thousands of households in the region begin their Halloween clean-ups.

Waste removal experts are highlighting essential checks to make when sorting recycling.

This weekend also marked the first Halloween since Simpler Recycling requirements came into effect across England, introducing new household recycling rules, including Durham County Council's weekly food-waste collections.

Founder of waste removal company Rainbow Rubbish Removals, Miroslav Radov, said sorting seasonal waste this year is as important as ever, adding: "An autumn clear-out can bring together broken decorations, packaging, garden cuttings and old electricals.

"Once everything is mixed in a bin bag, separating it becomes a much bigger job."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21317963.jpg?type=mds-article-620)As families across the North East take down Halloween decorations and clear out leftover pumpkins, residents are being urged to avoid common recycling mistakes that could see waste ending up in the wrong place. *(Image: PA MEDIA)*

Mr Radov advised homeowners to check an entire item before deciding if it is recyclable, adding that wires, battery compartments or plastic coatings can change where something goes.

While the simpler recycling requirements came into effect in March, these do not yet include plastic film and bags, meaning sweet wrappers and other flexible packaging may not be recyclable through household collections until next March.

Durham County Council cannot recycle soft plastics such as salad bags, crisp packets, and chocolate or biscuit wrappers.

Plastic sweet wrappers and multipack wrapping can often be recycled at certain supermarkets, but residents are advised to check first.

Other non-recyclable items include hard plastic containers, children's toys, plastic wrap and film and polystyrene or bubble wrap.

Mr Radov also urged households to check Halloween decorations for batteries before disposal.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21646311.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham County Council is urging residents not to place batteries or vapes in recycling or general waste bins after a fire broke out in a rubbish lorry in October. *(Image: Durham County Council)*

Batteries and electrical items require dedicated recycling because hidden batteries can ignite when mixed with general rubbish or recycling.

Mr Radov recommended removing batteries only if it is safe to do so, otherwise taking the entire item to an electrical recycling point.

This advice follows similar warnings from Durham County Council after a fire in a rubbish lorry that reignited concerns about hazardous waste, including batteries and disposable vapes, being placed in household bins.

The incident occurred during a routine collection round in Shildon and was believed to have been caused by lithium batteries or disposable vapes igniting after being crushed.

A spokesperson for Durham County Council said: "Batteries and vapes contain lithium and other materials that can ignite when crushed during collection, putting our crews and the public at serious risk."

Batteries and electrical items should be bagged and placed alongside food waste caddies for separate collection, taken to a Household Waste Recycling Centre, or dropped off at a community Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) collection point.

Households are also being advised to separate decorations from packaging before disposal.

A cardboard box and a plastic decoration it contains may need to be recycled through different channels, especially since plastic toys and hard, bulky plastic are often not recyclable.

Residents are advised to store reusable decorations for next year, or give them away to those who will use them.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20146219.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Residents are advised to store reusable decorations for next year, or give them away to those who will use them.  *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Paper and card decorations can often be recycled once any plastic attachments are removed, though residents should check for glitter, foil, or plastic coatings that may render them non-recyclable.

Pumpkin disposal should also follow council guidance.

Durham County Council said pumpkins can be placed on top of residents' black food waste caddies to be converted into renewable energy and used in farming.

Posting to social media, a spokesperson for the council wrote: "Usually, we ask that all food waste is placed inside your caddy but, as pumpkin carcasses can be large, you can place them on top.

"Don't forget you can pop your pumpkin pulp inside as well with your egg shells, tea bags and other food waste."

Candles, wax, and decorations should be removed before recycling pumpkins.

Radov also advised that garden waste from autumn tidy-ups should be separated, with soil, rubble, plant pots, and plastic bags kept out of garden waste bins.

Lastly, residents are reminded to check who is collecting any large items that cannot fit in household bins.

Those using private rubbish removal services should verify the company’s waste-carrier registration through the Environment Agency and keep a receipt as proof.

Mr Radov said: "Ask who is collecting the waste and check their registration before agreeing to the job.

"Keep the collection details and receipt so you have a record of who took it away."

Experts say small recycling mistakes post Halloween can have big consequences.

From sweet wrappers and decorations to batteries hidden inside novelty items, taking care with what goes in each bin could help keep collection crews safe and prevent recyclable materials from ending up as waste.
