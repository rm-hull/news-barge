A takeaway on a major road could be named the best curry house in Bradford.

Maya’s, of Tong Street, is in the running for the Telegraph & Argus' latest competition.

Owner Mohammad Arif, who has worked as a chef for more than 30 years, said the shortlist recognition reflected the team’s work.

Team members Shaban Hussain, Ahsan Warsi, Maroof Hussain and Habib Nawaz help run Maya’s day to day.

## 'Serving great food at all times'

Mr Arif said: “To be shortlisted means that all our hard work, effort and love that goes into our food has paid off.”

He added: “We stand out from others as we do not compromise on the quality of our food and we are dedicated to serving great food at all times.”

Mr Arif believes the nomination is also a recognition of their quality customer service.

He said: “They nominated us for the quality and care that goes into our food and the effort we put in to making sure our customers have a great experience when ordering from us.”

He said winning would show that customers valued both the food and service, adding: "That's what we strive for.”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21651375.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Food offered at Maya's *(Image: Mohammad Arif)*

Maya’s was established in 2013 and has built a loyal following. It has a 4.6-star rating from more than 240 Google reviews.

The takeaway’s special balti dishes are currently among its most popular menu items.

## Owner warns rising prices could impact eating out habits

Like many hospitality businesses, Maya’s has faced pressure from rising costs.

Mr Arif said the cost of ingredients and other items had increased, while the business still wanted to keep prices low.

He said: “The price of everything has increased significantly and we always want to keep prices as low as possible for others.”

He said customers were eating out less often, while set meals and other deals were proving popular.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21651376.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Food offered at Maya's *(Image: Mohammad Arif)*

Mr Arif warned that continued price rises could affect the wider industry.

He said: “If the cost of living crisis continues the way it is and things get more expensive, less people will be able to afford eating out and the industry will suffer significantly.”

Mr Arif added: “Supporting local independent businesses is important because we are all a community in Bradford and we all should look out for each other.”
