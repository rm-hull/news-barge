Kyiv has urged Ukrainians to “stay calm” after it said its air raid app had fired out nuclear alerts after being hacked by “enemy influence”.

“The air alert app ⁠appears to have been subjected to enemy influence,” the air force said on ‌Telegram. “There is no radiation ​threat! ‌Stay calm ⁠and follow ⁠our updates on ‌our channel!”

It comes after Ukraine hit a Russian missile manufacturing plant and oil production facilities in its latest strikes behind enemy lines on Sunday.

Even as a six-day local ceasefire held on its final day around the Russian-controlled Ukrainian town of Oleshky, there was no letup in fighting elsewhere along the front line, and both sides kept up long-range air attacks.

Zelensky said the Kamensky missile plant in Russia’s Rostov region was struck, with Kyiv also launching more than 100 drones at the industrial hub of Kstovo, east of Moscow, which processes five per cent of the country’s crude.

Russia’s defence ministry claimed its forces had downed 235 Ukrainian drones over Russian regions overnight, while Ukraine’s air force said it had shot down 371 of 433 long-range drones launched by Russia since Saturday evening.

## Russian drone strike kills 13-year-old girl at school dormitory, Kyiv says

A Russian drone strike near a vocational school dormitory housing displaced people in Ukraine’s Vinnytsia region has killed two people, including a 13-year-old girl, and injured several children, according to Ukrainian authorities.

The attack took place on 1 November on the grounds of the educational institution, the state emergency service of Ukraine reported.

The strike is the latest in a series of Russian attacks targeting Ukrainian civilian infrastructure and populated areas, Kyiv authorities said.

## Hundreds evacuated from Kherson region during temporary Oleshky ceasefire

Ukrainian forces evacuated 167 civilians trapped on the right bank of the Dnipro River in Kherson during a temporary ceasefire in the Russian-occupied town of Oleshky.

The evacuation included 22 children and 21 people with limited mobility, according to the Ukrainian military general staff.

The ceasefire also enabled hundreds of residents from Oleshky, located on the eastern bank of the Dnipro River, to cross into Ukrainian-controlled territory and reunite with their families.

Ukrainian officials described the evacuation as an important humanitarian achievement, although concerns remain about the security of civilians in the region following the end of the truce.

The Kherson region has remained a major frontline area since Ukrainian forces liberated the regional capital in November 2022. Russian forces continue to occupy territory on the eastern bank of the Dnipro River.

## Power cuts as Russia intensifies attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure

Ukraine has reported widespread electricity disruptions after Russian attacks damaged critical energy infrastructure across four regions, with president Volodymyr Zelensky warning that Moscow is intensifying its campaign against the country’s power supply.

Kyiv experienced blackouts lasting between eight and 10 hours on Sunday after a critical thermal power plant was damaged.

Hundreds of thousands of households were also left without electricity following overnight attacks elsewhere in the country.

Zelensky said Russian forces were taking advantage of Ukraine’s shortage of ballistic missile interceptors to intensify their strikes.

“The intensity of the strikes is very high, and the Russians are seeking to maximally exploit the deficit of ballistic missile interceptors,” he said in a statement posted on Telegram.

## Ukraine resumes attacks on Russian oil refineries despite Trump pressure

Ukraine has resumed attacks on Russian oil refineries despite reported pressure from US president Donald Trump to halt strikes on Moscow’s energy infrastructure.

Russian state media reported on Sunday that Ukrainian long-range drones had set fire to the Kstovo oil refinery in Russia’s Nizhny Novgorod region overnight. The attack followed a separate strike on a major refinery in the Samara region, owned by Russian state energy company Rosneft.

The renewed attacks came after *The Wall Street Journal* reported that Kyiv had agreed to suspend strikes on Russian energy facilities following pressure from the White House.

However, Ukrainian officials told *The Kyiv Independent* that Ukraine had never agreed to a 30-day pause in refinery attacks, rejecting an alleged demand made by Jared Kushner, Trump’s son-in-law.

Russian officials said Ukrainian attacks had resumed on Friday, accusing Kyiv of ignoring Washington’s wishes.

Trump has previously blamed Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky for rising global fuel prices. Last month, his administration also lifted US sanctions on Russian diesel.

Zelensky has not publicly acknowledged the latest refinery strikes, but said Ukraine had targeted a Russian defence industry facility in the Rostov region that manufactures missiles. He also claimed that a Pantsir air defence system had been destroyed.

Kyiv has described its long-range strikes against Russian military and energy infrastructure as a form of retaliation for Moscow's continued attacks on Ukrainian cities and energy facilities.

## Russia says alert system ‘operating as normal’ after cyberattack

Russia’s emergencies ministry said the public alert system is “operating as normal” after both Ukraine and Russia said that cyberattacks had hit their emergency warning systems.

However, Moscow said that citizens are informed of alerts through sirens and loudspeakers, text messages, and TV and radio broadcasts.

It said that the app is “only an additional, ancillary news service”.

“The hacking of the mobile app has not had, and cannot have, any impact on the operation of the public alert system,” Russia’s emergencies ministry said on its Telegram channel.

The ministry said that it had employed experts to restore normal operation of the mobile app but said that the situation was “under control”.

## Pope Leo XIV hopes Oleshky ceasefire will strengthen dialogue between Moscow and Kyiv

Pope Leo XIV said on Sunday that he hoped the ceasefire arrangement between Ukraine and Russia in the Ukrainian town of Oleshky would encourage the two countries to pursue broader dialogue and peace efforts.

“I hope that the cooperation between the parties, which made this necessary humanitarian gesture possible, will strengthen their willingness to engage in dialogue,” he said during his Sunday prayer.

The development comes as trilateral talks involving Russia, Ukraine and the United States are expected to take place later this month.

Kremlin officials have also said Russian president Vladimir Putin is willing to speak directly with Zelensky by phone, although they insisted that European leaders supporting Ukraine should not participate.

However, prospects for a broader peace agreement remain uncertain. Putin continues to demand that Ukraine surrender the entirety of the eastern Donetsk region, along with additional territory in Kherson and Zaporizhzhia – including areas that Russian forces have failed to capture but Moscow claims to have annexed.

## Ceasefire in occupied Oleshky ends after successful evacuation of residents

A six-day ceasefire in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian town of Oleshky has ended, bringing to a close a humanitarian operation that allowed hundreds of residents to cross into Ukrainian-controlled territory and reunite with their families.

The temporary truce, which was in effect from 27 October to 1 November, was brokered by US first lady Melania Trump to facilitate the evacuation of civilians from the occupied town.

Ukraine’s human rights ombudsman Dmytro Lubinets confirmed on Sunday that the ceasefire had ended, describing the evacuation as an important achievement in humanitarian cooperation between the two sides.

Hundreds of residents were able to cross into northern Ukraine over the weekend, where they reunited with relatives.

The town of Oleshky lies on the eastern bank of the Dnipro River, opposite the city of Kherson, which Ukrainian forces recaptured in November 2022.
