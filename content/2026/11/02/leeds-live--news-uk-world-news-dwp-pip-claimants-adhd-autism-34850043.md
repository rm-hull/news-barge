# DWP PIP claimants with ADHD, autism and depression told to contact MP

## The Benefits and Work forum has called on PIP and DLA claimants to contact their MPs

Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) Personal Independence Payment claimants are being encouraged to get in touch with their MP regarding the hidden expenses associated with ADHD, autism, anxiety and depression. Jean-André Prager, who sits on the Timms review steering group, co-authored a recent report advocating for substantial reductions to incapacity and disability benefits.

The Timms Review is examining potential changes to PIP under the Labour Party government, as the benefits expenditure continues to grow with 4 million claimants now receiving the payments. Prager's report specifically targeted ADHD, autism, anxiety and depression.

Prager suggested the "burden" of such conditions "may fall primarily in the form of parental time, vigilance and complexity rather than direct expenditure." However, Benefits and Work, a disability forum, is challenging this view, reports Birmingham Live. The organisation has urged PIP and Disability Living Allowance, or DLA, claimants to reach out to their MPs to explain the additional expenses they incur due to their hidden conditions. The breakdown covered travel expenses, food costs, and additional outlays.

It stated: "In the face of the evidence from our readers about the real financial cost of these conditions, it is hard not to conclude that the Timms review has failed catastrophically in its selection process if people holding views like Prager's were chosen to shape the future of PIP."

The forum has compiled a list of 21 additional expenses that PIP and DLA claimants might encounter, urging members to forward it to their MPs so the Department for Work and Pensions properly considers these costs.

The forum added: "Some readers also described equipment or help they need but cannot afford. Going without something does not mean the need or its cost has disappeared." Pat McFadden told MPs in June of this year that he had raised the issue of neurodevelopmental conditions with the Timms review steering group, who are tasked with redesigning PIP.
