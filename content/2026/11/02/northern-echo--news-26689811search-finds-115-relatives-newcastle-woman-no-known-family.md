A North East woman thought to have died without family sparked a search that uncovered 115 relatives across the region and beyond.

When Olga Baker-Hewitt, 81, died in a Newcastle nursing home in January 2023, she had no known relatives and no will.

A public health funeral was arranged, while Finders International was brought in to trace any relatives.

The search eventually uncovered 115 relatives who could claim Olga’s estate.

Janice Stephenson, 68, from Gateshead, was among them — but she had never met Olga or known they were related.

![Janice Stephenson.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21658508.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Janice Stephenson. *(Image: SUPPLIED)*

Janice, a devoted fan of the BBC programme Heir Hunters, was stunned to receive a letter from Finders International.

She said: "I got a letter from Finders International and I was just so excited.

"I knew all about them because I loved Heir Hunters on TV.

"I contacted my cousin and, yes, they had received a letter too.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21658507.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rose Jackson, Olga's first cousin and Janice's mum. *(Image: SUPPLIED)*

"What happened next was mind-blowing."

Researchers found that Olga had divorced and never had children.

But the apparently simple case soon became far more complicated.

Olga’s father, Francis, was one of 14 siblings, creating a vast family network across several generations, said Finders International analyst Danielle Yianni.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21658510.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Janice and her brother with their mum, Rose. *(Image: SUPPLIED)*

She said: "We quickly established that Olga was divorced and had never had any children.

"But what initially looked like a straightforward case soon snowballed.

"Olga's father, Francis, was one of 14 siblings, creating a huge network of relatives across several generations.

"We eventually identified 115 relatives with an interest in the estate."

Janice discovered that Olga’s father and her grandfather were brothers.

She said: "It was unbelievable.

"The letter mentioned Olga, but I had never heard of her."

"I had actually done one of those at-home DNA tests the previous year, so I was becoming aware that I had quite a lot of relatives I didn't know about, but I never imagined our family tree could be anything like this."

"Then Finders told me that Olga's father and my grandfather were brothers, making me a first cousin once removed of Olga's."

Olga married Kenneth Baker-Hewitt in 1969 when she was 27.

The couple divorced 14 years later and had no children.

A search of family records identified 114 beneficiaries entitled to share Olga’s estate, worth around £15,000.

Janice received just £42, but said the experience was never about the money.

She said: "I have found out so much about my family.

"Lots of things I never knew about and that is priceless."

"I am sorry that I didn't know Olga.

"It makes me wonder what she was like and what her life was like.

"I wish I could have met her.

"I never imagined that one day I would be one of the people that Finders would be looking for."
