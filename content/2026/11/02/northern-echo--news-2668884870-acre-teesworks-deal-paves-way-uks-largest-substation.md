A plan to build one of Europe's largest data centres at Teesworks is now a crucial step closer after a deal with National Grid for the purchase of 73.3 acres of land at Dorman Point.

The agreement marks a significant milestone in plans to deliver major new electricity infrastructure at Teesworks and support the Tees Valley’s growing industrial and clean energy ambitions.

Chris Musgrave OBE, chairman of Teesworks Ltd, said: “Agreeing this deal with National Grid is a significant milestone for Teesworks and reflects the scale of the opportunity here at the Teesworks site. Making this land available for what is expected to be the UK’s largest substation is an important step in putting the infrastructure in place to support the next phase of industrial development across the site.

“Our focus now is on delivering the remediation works and preparing the land for handover, helping to ensure Teesworks is ready to support the energy needs of major businesses and industries for years to come.”

Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen added: “Everyone who’s tried to build a data centre or large-scale advanced manufacturing, technological or energy-intensive industrial developments in this country knows the biggest problem is getting enough power to the site quickly.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657717.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tees Valley Mayor Ben Houchen *(Image: Sarah Caldecott)*

“Having a contracted 1.44GW capacity, with further expansion up to 3.2GW in the near future, means we can finally compete for those projects.

“If Britain is serious about having its own AI capability and other advanced manufacturing technologies, with sensitive data stored safely on British soil, it needs somewhere to physically put it, and I’d much rather that was Teesside than Texas or Abu Dhabi.

“Full credit to Steel River Power, Iberdrola, Teesworks and National Grid for getting it over the line, and I’ve no doubt it’ll mean thousands of good jobs for local people over the next few years.”

The site had already made headlines a year ago when energy giant BP changed plans for a huge H2Teesside hydrogen plant after part of the site it needed to grow into was instead earmarked for a vast data centre.

Matt Pearce, National Grid Head of Land, said: “Securing this land agreement is an important step forward in the development of the new substation.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657704.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The site at Teesworks *(Image: tvca)*

“It reflects the constructive work between all those involved to enable this vital infrastructure which is part of The Great Grid Upgrade, the largest upgrade of the electricity grid in generations, and will support the growth of AI and digital technologies and enable the region’s next phase of industrial and economic development.

“We are committed to working closely with our partners to progress the development as quickly as possible.”

The site will accommodate Teesside Node A, the major new substation that will be delivered by National Grid’s contractor, Balfour Beatty and from which National Grid will provide Steel River Power an additional 1.44GW (equivalent to the same amount of power as Greater Manchester and Glasgow combined). The land sits directly beneath both 275kV and 400kV overhead electricity transmission lines, providing a strategically positioned location for the new infrastructure.

The substation’s initial 1,440 MVA capacity is scheduled to come online by the end of 2029, providing a major boost to electricity infrastructure at Teesworks. The capacity is contracted to Steel River Power (SRP), which owns and operates the private electricity network across the estate.

SRP is a joint venture between Steel River Energy Limited and North West Electricity Networks, part of the ScottishPower/Iberdrola group.

David Smith, chair of the South Tees Development Corporation (STDC), said: “This agreement is an important step forward for the site.

“For potential investors, having confidence that the energy infrastructure will be there to support their plans is crucial.

“This agreement is a real boost to our ability to attract future investment and move towards the development corporation’s long-term aims: bringing land back into productive use, creating opportunities for local people and ensuring the whole area benefits from regeneration for decades to come.”
