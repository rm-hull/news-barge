The Good Food Guide has revealed its list of the Top 100 Pubs in Britain, with eight Scottish spots earning a place in the rankings.

Now in its second year, the list draws on more than 20,000 reader nominations across eight regions, with 36 new entries joining returning favourites in 2026.

Pubs are recognised not only for their food and drink, but for their hospitality and connection to their local communities.

The Good Food Guide said: “This year’s list is packed with pubs that go beyond great food and drink, village locals that double as the last shop in town, community-saved pubs kept alive by the people who drink in them, and kitchens that know exactly where every ingredient came from.

“These are the pubs and people doing more for their communities, serving honest, top-quality cooking in a warm, welcoming setting, places all around Britain that you might not know exist but should be on your radar.”

Read on to discover all eight Scottish entries in this year’s list:

## No. 14: The Kinneuchar Inn

*Fife*

The Kinneuchar Inn in Kilconquhar is the highest-ranking Scottish pub on this year’s list, coming in at number 14.

The whitewashed 17th-century inn is already no stranger to acclaim from The Good Food Guide. Last year it was ranked second in Britain, when the guide praised chef James Ferguson's daily-changing menus and use of local produce.

## No. 58: The Taybank

*Dunkeld*

Sitting on the banks of the River Tay, The Taybank has a large outdoor bar and kitchen serving local beers and spirits, while its rustic downstairs bar offers seasonal dishes using produce from the team's four-acre walled garden.

The pub also appeared in last year's rankings, when it took the number 59 spot.

## No. 67: Applecross Inn

*Wester Ross*

At the end of the "nail-bitingly vertiginous" Bealach na Bà, the Applecross Inn is known for its idyllic coastal setting, hearty portions and local seafood, including langoustines, hand-dived scallops and dressed crab.

## No. 69: The Keelson

*Edinburgh*

This new Leith venue from the team behind Dogstar and Nauticus is a self-described “proper pub”, with a food menu centred around organic rotisserie chicken.

## No. 75: The Clachan Inn

*St John’s Town of Dalry Dumfries & Galloway*

The Clachan Inn also returns to the list after appearing in last year's inaugural rankings.

Based in Dumfries and Galloway, the family-run pub is said to have “everything you could wish for”, including a traditional, cosy vibe, slate floors, fireside chairs, a fabulous choice of real ales and a “seriously terrific restaurant”.

## No. 83: Nobles by Bellfield

*Edinburgh*

“Reinvented Leith stalwart” Nobles is a new addition to the rankings following its takeover by Bellfield Brewery.

The Guide says: “With its stained-glass windows, a huge, dark-wood bar and soaring, frescoed ceilings that hark back to the area’s seafaring history, this Leith stalwart is easily one of the most beautiful pubs in Scotland.”

## No. 89: The Flying Stag

*Braemar*

Part of the Fife Arms hotel, The Flying Stag is described by the Guide as a “true village institution”, with twice-weekly live Scottish folk music helping make it a local “social hub”.

## No. 92: The Clarence

*Glasgow*

The Clarence is a cosy gastropub on Hyndland Road in Glasgow’s West End, combining a relaxed pub atmosphere with an elevated menu of comfort food. The venue was also awarded a Michelin Bib Gourmand earlier this year.
