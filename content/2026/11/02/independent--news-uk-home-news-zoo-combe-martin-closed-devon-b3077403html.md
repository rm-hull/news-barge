A popular wildlife park in Devon faces permanent closure after four decades of operation, as its owners struggle against growing financial difficulties.

Combe Martin Wildlife and Dinosaur Park confirmed that it is facing the prospect of shutting its gates for good following the failure of several potential rescue deals.

The family-run site houses a range of species, including sea lions, leopards, wolves and monkeys.

Amid the uncertainty, the owners have begun reaching out to zoos and wildlife collections across the UK to find suitable relocations for the animals.

The attraction said that its animal care staff are evaluating the specific needs of each creature to ensure their long-term welfare in any prospective location.

While discussions with interested buyers, investors and financial backers have been described as encouraging, the park has not yet secured a deal to guarantee its future.

![The owners of Combe Martin Wildlife & Dinosaur Park](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/02/10/54/Jam_Press_JMP930090_pr.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=32%2C0%2C32%2C0&auto=webp)

The owners said: "For almost 40 years, the park has remained in our family's hands, and to us, it's never just been a business.

"It's been our way of life.

"Our children have worked alongside us, and many of the wonderful people who've worked here over the years have become part of our extended family."

Following messages of support from visitors, the family launched an official appeal on 31 October.

It raised more than £1,000 towards its £50,000 target within the first hour.

The owners said that the money would help cover essential costs while they continued trying to save the park and, if necessary, arrange the animals' relocation.

Donations will go towards food and specialist diets, keeper wages, veterinary treatment, medication, heating, electricity and enclosure maintenance.

![The donations will go towards caring for the zoo's animals, including the leopards (pictured)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/02/10/12/Jam_Press_JMP930087_pr.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=0%2C2%2C0%2C1&auto=webp)

The family said that moving the animals safely could take weeks or even months, meaning they would need continued care while suitable placements were arranged.

They stressed that donations would be used specifically for animal welfare and would not go towards repaying the park's historical business debts.

The appeal says: "Our animals cannot simply be moved overnight.

"Your generosity will help give us the time and resources to make the right decisions for our animals without compromising their welfare."

The owners have also appealed for businesses, organisations and individuals to consider offering substantial financial support, sponsorship, investment or a purchase opportunity.

They added: "Our greatest wish is to see Combe Martin Wildlife & Dinosaur Park survive and continue welcoming families for generations to come.

"But whatever happens, the welfare of our animals must always come first."

At the time of writing £9,945 had been raised.
