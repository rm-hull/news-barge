Bob Mortimer and Paul Whitehouse left Gone Fishing fans in tears with a heartfelt tribute to their canine co-star Ted in the newest series of the popular BBC show.

The emotional scenes came as the programme returned for its ninth series, the first since Ted died in April, aged 13.

Ted, owned by producer Lisa Clark, made his final appearance in the first episode of series nine, as Mortimer and Whitehouse fished for barbel on Lancashire’s River Ribble.

The episode ends with a tribute that reads: "Ted. 2012-2026."

Lisa Clark wrote on Ted's social media that his final appearance was "classic Ted," adding he was "never to be forgotten" in a follow-up post.

Ted became a regular on the BBC Two show after making his debut in series three, travelling with Middlesbrough-born Bob Mortimer and Paul Whitehouse to fishing spots across the country.

During a 2025 visit to Devon and Cornwall, Ted received a “lifetime achievement award” in the Christmas special.

Mortimer and Whitehouse remembered the Patterdale Terrier mix during the second episode of series 9 as they visit Norfolk, which Mortimer remarked was the first place Ted had ever joined them.

Whitehouse added: "Well, it's fitting that we are back here now then, isn't it? We'll maybe raise a glass to Ted, because we will miss him."

The pair later raised a cup to Ted beside the riverbank and joked about whether he would go to heaven or hell. Mortimer asked: "Do you think he will go up or down?"

Whitehouse replied: "It's very hard to say, I think he will go up."

Mortimer, who is from Middlesbrough, joked: "But then be rejected."

Ted was credited as the author of two books, Pawtobiography and Pup Fiction, and regularly appeared in the show’s end-of-series awards, often beating Whitehouse and provoking mock frustration.

Clark rescued Ted from the Diana Brimblecombe Animal Rescue Centre in Berkshire in 2013.

Following his death, tributes and support poured in, with Clark setting up a GoFundMe appeal which has now raised more than £37,000 in donations.

Clark said she was “considering many ideas” for the money, including a permanent memorial and donations to animal welfare charities.

Heartbroken viewers have also paid tribute online since Ted died. Many were delighted to hear he would appear one last time after becoming a much-loved member of the cast.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20073409.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ted became a regular on the BBC Two show after making his debut in series three, travelling with Middlesbrough-born Bob Mortimer and Paul Whitehouse to fishing spots across the country. *(Image: BBC)*

One fan wrote: "Can’t wait to see these two muppets together again, miss Ted though, I have read the books again and they are so funny."

Another commented: "Unfortunately, I don’t think I will be able to watch that episode, or if I do then I will be a blubbering wreck.

"RIP Ted, you brought smiles and joy and laughter to the program and everyone on the series will miss you so much."

Mortimer called Ted the "greatest little chum" in a tribute to the canine co-star, adding he was "going to miss him so much."

Whitehouse echoed Mortimer's sentiment, saying: "He wasn't a dog, he was a species all of his own.

"He's gone to the great briefcase emporium in the sky. We will really miss you, mate."

The newest series of Gone Fishing is available on BBC iPlayer, and showcases County Durham's High Force waterfall in another North East fishing trip for the comedy duo.

They have previously fished the North Tyne in Northumberland and the rivers Tees and Esk in Teesside and North Yorkshire.
