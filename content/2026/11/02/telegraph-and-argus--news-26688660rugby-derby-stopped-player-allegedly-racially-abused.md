A rugby match was halted following allegation of racist abuse.

Bradford & Bingley RFC said one of its players was racially abused during Saturday's match against Baildon RUFC.

The incident occurred during the first half of the First XV fixture at Jenny Lane, Baildon, which was eventually won 40-18 by the home side.

In a statement issued following the game, Bradford & Bingley RFC said it "strongly condemns the racial abuse directed towards one of our players during the first half".

Baildon RUFC said it was aware of the incident that occurred during Saturday's fixture and stressed that it takes "any allegation of racial abuse extremely seriously".

## Referee comments on incident

Bradford & Bingley said the situation became serious enough for play to be halted.

"The incident resulted in the game being stopped, and our player having to be pulled away from the situation," the statement said.

Referee Stuart Turnbull, from Huddersfield, commented on the incident after the final whistle.

He said: “I was told that someone had racially abused a Bradford & Bingley player and spoke to both the Baildon captain and a member of their coaching staff, but couldn’t identify the alleged perpetrator as there were about 30-40 people standing in the vicinity.”

## Information passed to Yorkshire RFU for investigation

Bradford & Bingley added that the match had been recorded and that all relevant footage and information would be passed to Yorkshire RFU for investigation and any appropriate disciplinary action.

The club said rugby is built on "respect, inclusion and sportsmanship" and insisted that while rivalries are part of the game, "racism is not".

"We stand firmly behind our player and will not allow this incident to be brushed aside," the statement continued.

## Statement from Baildon RUFC

Baildon acknowledged the allegation and said it would fully co-operate with any investigation.

They added: "Racism and discrimination have absolutely no place in rugby or our club."

Baildon said Yorkshire RFU's disciplinary department had been informed of the incident and that the club would provide its full support throughout the investigation process.

The statement said: "We are committed to ensuring that everyone involved in our club, whether players, members, officials, volunteers or supporters, is treated with dignity and respect.

"We recognise the seriousness of the matter and will await the outcome of the appropriate process before making any further comment."

Yorkshire RFU declined to comment when approached by the T&A.
