**The Scottish Conservatives have accused John Swinney of wasting taxpayers' money after it emerged that the Scottish Government's new schoolbag scheme will cost £28 per pupil.**

The initiative will provide around 47,000 Primary 1 pupils with a backpack containing items including pencils, crayons, a notebook, a pencil case and a water bottle.

Last week, it was revealed that Edinburgh-based APS Group has secured a two-year contract worth £1.35 million annually to assemble and distribute the backpacks to schools across the country ahead of the 2027-28 academic year.

The Conservatives claim the contents could be purchased for less than half that price. Education spokeswoman Meghan Gallacher said she spent £13.14 on a backpack, notebook, pencil set, water bottle, folding ruler and packet of crayons during a visit to a retailer in Edinburgh.

Gallacher said the comparison raised questions about the value for money the government-backed scheme delivers.

She said: "My trip to a well-known retailer has exposed the astonishing waste of John Swinney's schoolbag gimmick.

"It beggars belief that he is happy to charge taxpayers £28 per bag, when we know you can purchase a backpack and the items Swinney is planning to give Primary One pupils for less than half the deal he struck.

"This raises further serious questions about the contract which was awarded to a group who have already benefitted from other SNP schemes."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20654091.jpg?type=mds-article-620)First Minister of Scotland John Swinney and Minister for Housing Mairi McAllan

The MSP said ministers should instead focus resources on addressing wider challenges facing Scottish education.

"Instead of these government-issued schoolbags, Swinney should be focusing money and resources on restoring Scotland's reputation for educational excellence, ending the poverty-related attainment gap and tackling soaring levels of violence in our schools," she said.

"A taxpayer-funded schoolbag will do nothing to tackle these issues.

"This is a distraction from Swinney to try and hide from SNP incompetence, and another shocking example of how this arrogant nationalist government have no regard for Scotland's taxpayers."

The Conservatives' receipt did not account for the Government's pledge that the bags will also include literacy and numeracy resources, hoped to encourage further education outside of school. The government has also included information for parents in the bags, including social security entitlements and online safety advice and said that the contract covers costs in addition to the bag and its contents – including storage, packing, distribution and staffing.

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: “We have engaged with families and teachers to ensure the Welcome to School Bags will help cut costs as children start school. To ensure maximum value for money, the bags will be assembled in Scotland. This is just one of the policies designed to ensure that children get the best possible start to their education and represents around 0.01% of the overall spend by Scottish and local government on education.

“Alongside this, delivered in partnership with local authorities, we invest £31.731 million on the School Clothing Grant, £130 million on Pupil Equity Funding, £209.8 million on free school meals, and £1 billion on ELC, helping to reduce the financial barriers families face and support children's participation.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21363567.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A prototype design of the SNP Government's pledged 'Welcome to School' bag *(Image: Scottish Government)*The plan shares similar traits from former**SNP** leader,**Nicola Sturgeon**, who introduced Scotland’s Baby Box scheme in 2017, also supported by APS.** Education** Secretary, Màiri McAllan said: “Building on the success of our Baby Box offer, we want to ensure children start school on an equal footing and with a feeling that they belong.”

The bags are customisable, and children will have the choice of five different colours: Blue, green, pink, red or yellow. “As well as being available in five colours, children will have the option of adding a drawstring to personalise their welcome to school bag, to help them start school excited and with all the essentials for their first steps into the classroom,” McAllan added.
