A man who drove his pickup truck at a pedestrian, leaving him with critical injuries, has been jailed for seven-and-a-half years.

Shay Holmes drove at the 44-year-old victim on Carr Lane, Spennymoor, shortly after 1am on Thursday, January 8.

The victim was left fighting for his life having suffered significant injuries.

Officers spent hours trawling CCTV footage and carrying out door-to-door enquiries to piece together Holmes' movements, Durham Police said. Detectives traced his three-and-a-half-mile route in the hours surrounding the attack.

Holmes stopped on Carr Lane and briefly spoke to a man and a woman before driving at the male, leaving him with serious injuries.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21660927.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shay Holmes *(Image: DURHAM CONSTABULARY)*

He then fled the scene and disposed of his vehicle.

Holmes, 24, of Spennymoor, pleaded guilty to wounding with intent and dangerous driving at Teesside Crown Court earlier this year.

He was sentenced to seven years and six months in prison on Friday, October 30.

A Newton Hearing, also known as a trial of issue, was held immediately before sentencing after Holmes disputed some of the circumstances surrounding the incident despite admitting the offences.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20399783.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Carr Lane in Spennymoor *(Image: ANNA BARNES)*

Carr Lane was taped off following the incident, with pictures showing police cars at the scene while a number of items lay in the road.

The victim was taken to the Royal Victoria Infirmary in Newcastle in a 'critical condition' after the incident.

Holmes and another man were initially arrested on suspicion of attempted murder as detectives launched an investigation into the hit-and-run.

Detective Chief Inspector Andrew Crowe, of Durham Constabulary, who led the investigation, said: “Holmes knew what he was doing in the early hours of that morning and did not care about the consequences. His actions have had a lasting impact on the victim, who suffered significant injuries as a result.

“This was a lengthy and complex investigation and I grateful to my team for their persistence and determination to uncover what happened that day.

“I would also like to thank the people of Spennymoor for their invaluable support and assistance throughout this investigation, which played a key role in identifying the route Holmes took.”
