Two registered sex offenders with an interest in boys took the sons of one of the pair's friends to Beamish Museum without their parents knowing about their convictions.

Paul Rhodes “inveigled” himself into the lives of a family with two boys, Durham Crown Court was told.

He took the boys to meet his friend Jim Smith, and their parents had no concerns about them travelling in Rhodes’ car to the open-air museum.

Police were alerted when the men’s criminal pasts came to light this summer, and both were arrested.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648805.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sex offenders Paul Rhodes and Jim Smith were jailed for breaching their Sexual Harm Prevention Orders

Prosecutor Martin Towers said both men were subject to Sexual Harm Prevention Orders (SHPOs) because of their criminal records.

The orders prohibit unsupervised contact with children unless their parents are aware of their offending history.

Mr Towers said 43-year-old Rhodes has 34 previous offences, many involving indecent images of children.

He said, "most significantly", he was jailed for three years in 2009 for communicating with a child after grooming.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648806.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sex offender Paul Rhodes deliberately 'inveigled' himself into the friendship of a family with two young sons, without them knowing of his past offending

He was made subject to the previous form of a SHPO “until further order”.

Rhodes was also jailed for five years in 2014 for three historic indecent assaults on a boy.

Mr Towers said 42-year-old Smith, also previously known as James Oliver, has 11 convictions for making indecent images of children and six for distributing them, for which he was jailed for 32 months in 2014.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21648807.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sex offender Jim Smith, who previously used the alias 'James Oliver', jailed for 20 months for breaching his Sexual Harm Prevention Order

He was also made subject to the previous form of a SHPO “until further order”.

Smith returned to court in 2016 and was jailed for 28 months for sexual offences involving children.

Mr Towers said a Facebook group published details of Smith’s convictions in July.

Several members of the public then told police that Rhodes had driven children to Smith’s former home in Archer Avenue, Bishop Auckland.

Mr Towers said not all of those children had been identified.

The boys’ parents mentioned other unsupervised outings with Rhodes, whom they trusted and considered “kind” and “generous”.

When Rhodes’s past came to light, the boys’ mother said she felt “sick”.

Both Smith and Rhodes were arrested in August and made no comment in police interview.

Police found a “Star of the Week” certificate bearing one boy’s name inside a tin containing condoms in Rhodes’ bedroom.

The family said the boys told them nothing had happened, but they were concerned due to the time the children had spent with Rhodes.

In a victim statement read to the court, the boys’ “distressed” mother said she was “devastated” to discover the history of someone the family considered a friend.

Rhodes, of Norwich Gardens, Willington, and Smith, now of no fixed abode, appeared by video link from Holme House and Durham prisons respectively.

Both previously admitted breaching their SHPOs.

Chris Morrison, for Rhodes, said the defendant had asked him to offer “a sincere apology” to the family.

Judge Jo Kidd rejected Mr Morrison’s suggestion that Rhodes’s friendship with the family was not motivated by them having young children.

She said he had “deliberately inveigled himself” into their lives.

Tony Davis, for Smith, said his client’s only contact with the boys was during the car trip to Beamish, while their parents travelled in a vehicle behind them.

Mr Davis said Smith lost his truck-driving job after four years when his past offending was exposed. His car and former home were then vandalised.

Judge Kidd told Rhodes he was “clearly grooming” the boys’ family to win their trust.

She described it as, “persistent, manipulative and highly targeted” behaviour.

Judge Kidd said the terms of both men’s SHPOs “could not have been more clear”.

Rhodes was jailed for 32 months and Smith for 20 months.

The judge imposed restraining orders on both men covering the boys’ entire family, “until further order”.

Judge Kidd also urged Durham Police to review the terms of both men’s SHPOs covering their future computer use and contact with under-16s.

The breaches occurred over a two-month period, during which the men had unsupervised contact with children under 16 despite their SHPOs.
