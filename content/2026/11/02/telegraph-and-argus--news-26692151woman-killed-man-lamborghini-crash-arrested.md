A woman who caused the death of her passenger in a horror crash involving a rented Lamborghini and failed to turn up to her sentencing has been arrested and brought to court.

Humaira Batool, of Bradford, will now be sentenced on Tuesday afternoon at Leeds Crown Court.

His Honour Judge Ray Singh told her that her absence had been “unforgivable”.

He added: “I’m afraid I am going to have to remand you in custody.

“I have come to the conclusion [that] if I grant you bail there’s now likely to be a significant risk you will not attend tomorrow bearing in mind certainly your behaviour over the last week and, indeed, not attending this morning.”

Batool, 26, was due to be sentenced on Monday morning for killing her friend Mohammed Shaan Hussain when the hired Lamborghini Urus hit a barrier on the M62 and caught fire.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21401186.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Shaan Hussain, who was 20 and from Bradford, died after the black Lamborghini Urus in which he was travelling overturned on the westbound carriageway at Outlane between junctions 22 and 23 *(Image: West Yorks Police)*

In what her barrister Richard Dawson described as “a regrettable turn of events” the court heard that Batool had been admitted to Bradford Royal Infirmary.

That was described as “a ruse” by prosecution barrister Christopher Dunn, which prompted Judge Singh to issue a warrant for her arrest.

He said: “When one looks at the progress of the case through the court, the defendant has lied from first to last throughout and has attempted on a number of occasions to thwart the prosecution.”

Several hours later Batool was brought before the judge who told her it was “fairly obvious” she had not intended to come to court to be sentenced.

He added: “I am trying my best to help you here, and for you – deliberately in my view – to have got to a position where you weren’t going to attend this morning is unforgivable.

“We have got the deceased’s family present, and everybody else interested in this particular case.”

Earlier in the day Judge Singh said Batool was “avoiding being dealt with by way of sentence” and issued a warrant for her arrest.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21658911.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Humaira Batool *(Image: SWNS)*

## Lamborghini reaches speeds of 143mph

During her trial, Batool, of Smiddles Lane, Bankfoot, Bradford, embarked on a “distasteful attempt” to blame Mr Hussain for the excessive speed of the car, suggesting he had meddled with the controls to make the vehicle “surge”.

Shortly before the crash, between junctions 22 and 23 of the M62 near Ripponden, the car had reached speeds of 143mph.

Leeds Crown Court previously heard how the Lamborghini was driven by Batool “at grossly excessive speeds of up to 143mph in wet conditions” in the early hours of December 21, 2022.

Batool, who was aged 22 at the time, was convicted of causing death by dangerous driving.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21429920.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Footage shown to the jury shows Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights, moving around the Lamborghini and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain *(Image: CPS / West Yorkshire Police / SWNS)*

Seconds after it passed Scott Hall Farm – known as “house in the middle of the motorway” – the Lamborghini smashed into a barrier, overturned, and burst into flames.

Drivers who had just been overtaken by the stricken supercar stopped and desperately tried to save Mr Hussain.

But their rescue efforts were thwarted by a fire and an explosion.

Batool was later found on a grass verge near the motorway by a second group of witnesses.

Batool initially told police she had been asleep in the passenger seat and awoke when the car rolled, the court heard.

However, video was shown to the jury of Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights in Bradford, moving around the vehicle, and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain.

The court was told that Batool kept “entirely shtum” about the change of driver until confronted with the video footage.

After conceding that she was in fact driving, Batool then put forward a different reason why she was not responsible for the fatal crash.

During the trial, she claimed Mr Hussain triggered a “boost” by tampering with the supercar's controls.

Mr Hussain had hired the Lamborghini to collect a friend from HMP Leeds and picked up Batool from her Bradford home at about 4am.
