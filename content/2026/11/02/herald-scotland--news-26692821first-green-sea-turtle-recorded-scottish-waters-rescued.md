**A green sea turtle believed to be the first ever recorded in Scottish waters has been rescued after being found stranded on a beach in North Uist.**

Marine Environmental Monitoring said British Divers Marine Life Rescue (BDMLR) contacted it on October 29 after a member of the public found the hard-shelled turtle on the island off Scotland's north-west coast.

A BDMLR medic based on North Uist attended the scene and rescued the animal, which experts later identified as a green sea turtle with assistance from Sea Life centres.

The species is normally found in tropical and subtropical seas worldwide and feeds primarily on seagrass. Green turtles take their name from the green-coloured fat found beneath their shell, which is linked to their diet.

Since records began in 1875, only six live green sea turtles have been reported around the coastlines of the UK and Ireland. The North Uist discovery is understood to be the first confirmed live recording of the species in Scotland.

## Overnight care

The turtle was found suffering from "cold stunning", a condition caused by prolonged exposure to cold water.

Experts say hard-shelled marine turtles cannot survive in Scottish waters for extended periods because of the cold.

With no immediate way of transporting the animal from the island, the turtle was cared for overnight by the attending marine medic before being taken by ferry to the mainland the following day.

Staff at SEA LIFE Loch Lomond carried out initial treatment and stabilisation work before the turtle was transferred to Blackpool Sea Life Centre for longer-term rehabilitation.

BDMLR said the animal is now receiving fluids, gradual feeding and specialist care, with the hope it can eventually be released back into suitable warmer waters overseas.

## Warning to public

The rescue charity has urged members of the public to seek expert help immediately if they discover a stranded turtle.

It warned that cold-stunned turtles can appear lifeless and that warming them too quickly with heaters or hot water can prove fatal.

People who encounter a turtle at sea are encouraged to report sightings through the UK strandings network, while those who find turtles on shore should report them immediately to rescuers.

A BDMLR spokesperson described the operation as "one incredible turtle rescue", adding that a team of volunteer medics helped transport the animal across Scotland for specialist treatment.

The charity said: "Found cold-stunned on North Uist, this beautiful little visitor was cared for overnight before a relay of BDMLR medics transported the little green turtle across Scotland to Loch Lomond for specialist care.

"An unexpected passenger for quite the road trip."
