Residents could take over responsibility for looking after grass verges in York as part of a new trial.

The scheme is set to see the maintenance of some verges, planting areas and other City of York Council-managed spaces handed to residents and community groups on request.

A council report stated the trial aimed to boost biodiversity, improve the appearance of streets and get communities more involved in caring for local green spaces.

Details of the trial come as part of an update on the council’s broader work to manage public spaces in York which is set to be heard tomorrow (Tuesday).

The scheme is set to see locations go through an approval process with input from council officers to ensure they are feasible and minimise the risk to those taking part.

Spaces will be assessed based on the planting scheme they are subject to, how close they are to roads and speed limits on them and land ownership.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/city-of-york-council-hq-may-2022.webp)

Agreements will be reached with the groups taking them on about how they will be managed and the council would advise them of any risks such as from underground utilities.

Groups would identify a verge, planter or other space they want to take on and contact the council which would then do an assessment.

Requirements for spaces to be eligible include them being council-owned, by roads which have speed limits of 30mph or less and at least 1.5m away from the carriageway.

Surveys of utilities underneath spaces, including underground ones if necessary, will also be done as part of the process.

Groups will be required to complete risk assessments and ensure that roads or footpaths are not blocked while working on the space they are managing.

Initial digging will have to be sympathetic and be no more than 200mm deep. Exploratory digging will be needed before any machinery is used.

If shallow cables or utilities are found groups will have to stop work immediately and inform the council.

The council is currently responsible for the management of all verges on adopted roads, except along the A64 and its roundabouts.

Grass in council housing estates, parks and open areas is also managed by the council which aims to do six cuts a year in urban areas.

Council maintenance teams aim to keep grass at around 15cm high to allow shorter plants like clovers the opportunity to flower and seed.

Grass in rural areas is cut twice a year, in part to encourage biodiversity.

A 1m-wide strip is cut on verges on the side of rural A and B roads and mowing is limited to priority locations where grass may obstruct the line of sight for traffic on others.

The council’s report stated maintaining heights of 15cm in urban areas proved challenging earlier this year due to rapid growth, though cutting schedules were maintained.

It added public realm resources were deployed elsewhere during dry spells when grass growth was slower.
