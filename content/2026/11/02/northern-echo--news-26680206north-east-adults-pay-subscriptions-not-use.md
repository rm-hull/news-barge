More than a third of adults in the North East have paid for a subscription or service they no longer use, new research has revealed.

The findings come from the Money and Pensions Service (MaPS) as part of its annual Talk Money Week campaign, highlighting the financial impact of forgotten or unused subscriptions.

Talk Money Week, running from November 2 to 6, encourages people across the UK to take small steps towards improving their financial wellbeing.

Kirsty Rudd, North East manager at the Money and Pensions Service, said: "Right now, many people across the North East are under financial pressure.

"Rising costs, unexpected expenses and wider economic uncertainty can make managing money feel overwhelming, but you are not alone and even small changes can make a big difference.

"Whether it’s making a budget, shopping around for a better deal on your bills, or cancelling that unused subscription, it all counts.

"This Talk Money Week I would encourage everyone to take that first step towards feeling more confident and in control of their financial future."

The research also revealed that 44 per cent of adults in the region have hidden or downplayed their spending from a partner or family member, underscoring the importance of open conversations about money.

MaPS is urging people to take small, realistic actions to improve their finances as part of this year’s Talk Money Week theme, "It All Counts."

Suggestions include checking a pension, checking eligibility for financial support, starting a money conversation with someone you trust, or using a budget planner.

Carolyn Pearson, a 61-year-old self-employed woman who turned to MoneyHelper, the free guidance service operated by MaPS, shared her experience.

Ms Pearson said: "In the past I would look at my online banking, set monthly goals, and make a spreadsheet to see how much I needed to pay myself and how much I could afford to save.

"MoneyHelper's budget tracker took things to the next level because it shows all the hidden costs like travel insurance, house insurance, car insurance, car tax, car MOT, and subscriptions.

"It showed me I was in the red each month, and so I needed to pay myself more money."

Ms Pearson used the service to identify savings opportunities and renegotiate her energy tariff.

She said: "I clicked on an energy comparison site and renegotiated my tariff with Eon, slicing off £20 a month on my gas and electric bills.

"In total I'll probably save about £500 pounds a year.

"If I can inspire people to use the budget tracker then that’s great – I think it's a really empowering tool."

MaPS has outlined five small steps people can take to improve their financial wellbeing: shop around for the best deal on bills, check entitlement to financial support, create a budget, talk openly about money, and remember that every action counts.
