ScotRail has drawn up a proposal to bring Sunday into the contracted working week for its drivers and train crew, MSPs have been told.

Managing director Joanne Maguire told Holyrood's Transport Committee in a letter on October 21 that the proposal is going through the necessary approval process and will be followed by negotiation with the trade unions.

The operator needs extra drivers, extra train crew and a deal with unions to change existing contracts before it can run a fuller Sunday timetable.

Sunday is not a rostered day for drivers, so a significant proportion of services depend on drivers choosing to work overtime or a rest day.

That can make it difficult to plan and ScotRail has previously been forced to cancel Sunday services due to a lack of available drivers.

Sunday working was a commitment made when ScotRail entered public ownership in 2022.

Ms Maguire and Network Rail's Liam Sumpter face the committee on Wednesday, as part of its pre-budget scrutiny of road and rail ahead of the Scottish Budget on December 3.

They were previously in front of MSPs on September 16, when Ms Maguire said: "We have developed a proposal, which is going through the necessary approval process.

"We would need to negotiate with staff on their terms and conditions if we were to ask them to work contractually on a Sunday.

"We would also need to increase the number of train crew that we have, to be able to deliver our train services reliably, as we would be stretching our resources across a seven-day commitment rather than a six-day one."

She said ScotRail is committed to it because of the revenue potential, and because it would get more people out of their cars on Sundays.

Ms Maguire said demand was already there. This summer's Edinburgh festivals and the Commonwealth Games in Glasgow produced some of ScotRail's busiest Sundays since public ownership, and journeys are up about 7% year on year.

Her letter says any change must take a whole-system view, because engineering work, train maintenance and cleaning take place overnight or on Sundays, when the railway has traditionally been quieter.

Ms Maguire also told MSPs this year's Boxing Day timetable will be ScotRail's biggest yet, with hourly services added between Edinburgh and Tweedbank and between Edinburgh and North Berwick, and an extra hourly service between Aberdeen and Inverurie.

However, engineering works will block trains from reaching Dundee, so there will be no Edinburgh to Aberdeen services.

The letter also updates MSPs on the troubled ScotRail app. The transport boss there are still problems with Flexipass tickets when a phone's connection is intermittent, and automatic logouts for a small group of iPhone users.

The app is part of a wider contract with SilverRail, which runs for an initial four years to July 29, 2030, with options for five more. ScotRail can terminate from the first anniversary of the contract's start on May 29, 2026, on at least 12 months' notice, which the letter says would have financial consequences.

Ms Maguire said ScotRail is not currently considering using its termination rights.

Responding to the Sunday working proposals, ASLEF's Scottish organiser Kevin Lindsay said: "ASLEF has a long-standing policy that supports bringing Sundays into the working week.

"No formal approach or discussions about Sunday working have taken place with the employer this year.

"However, we are ready and willing to negotiate with ScotRail once they complete their internal processes. If we reach an agreement, we would then also have to go through our own process and consult with our National Executive Committee and of course our membership."
