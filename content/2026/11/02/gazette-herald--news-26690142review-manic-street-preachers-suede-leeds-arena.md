**Review: Manic Street Preachers and Suede, Leeds Arena, October 30**

Live music doesn’t really get much better than what was served up by the Manic Street Preachers and Suede on Friday evening at First Direct Arena in Leeds.

Despite each band being decades deep into their careers they remain more than capable of delivering satisfying, sometimes thrilling, performances and their current co-headlining tour provides opportunities for their friendly rivalry to bring the very best out of each other.

Manic Street Preachers took the stage at 7.40pm with many audience members still streaming in. They kicked off their set with a rip-roaring Motorcycle Emptiness, following up with Ocean Spray and Faster. The latter a track from the band’s classic The Holy Bible album (a live performance of the album recorded in 2014 was released last week).

Before No Surface All Feeling, James Dean Bradfield sang a verse of Total Eclipse Of The Heart, “For Bonnie,” he said in tribute of the late Welsh singer Bonnie Tyler.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21658451.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James Dean Bradfield of the Manic Street Preachers at Leeds Arena. Photo by Dave Lawrence

Bradfield performed a poignant solo performance From Despair To Where before the band rejoined him midway through The Everlasting.

Highlights included You Stole The Sun From My Heart, Everything Must Go, Slash And Burn, Your Love Alone Is Not Enough and, of course, A Design For Life.

Bassist Nicky Wire handled vocals for Hiding In Plain Sight, and referring to the absent Richie Edwards commented that “there are three of us here but really there’ll always be four.”

Closing the set the band delivered powerful performances of You Love Us and If You Tolerate This Your Children Will Be Next.

On the face of it, Suede had a difficult task following the Manics but it was one they rose to. Like the Manics, the band have a back catalogue of great songs to call upon and that succeed in transporting the listener back to the heady days of Britpop with a healthy dose of glam thrown into the mix.

Suede were not content to rest on their earlier work, instead performing several tracks from last year’s superb Antidepressants album, including the two opening numbers of their set, Disintegrate and Dancing With The Europeans as well as the title track.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21658452.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Brett Anderson of Suede

With a shout from singer Brett Anderson of “C’mon Leeds, let’s do it!,” the band crashed into a formidable version of Trash and followed it up with Animal Nitrate with the twin guitars of Richard Oakes and Neil Codling to the fore.

A new song, My City was a treat and could have come from one of the band’s early albums.

Anderson still draws the eye constantly – a prowling, physical presence on the stage. One minute on all fours during Animal Nitrate the next balancing on a stage monitor for We Are The Pigs, then laying prostate on the stage for the first half of The 2 Of Us or dropping into the pit for hugs and handshakes with the audience.

The Manics’ set was more nostalgic, relying extensively on strong, tried and tested arena favourites whereas Suede were arguably the more adventurous with their set.

That said, both performances were hugely enjoyable leaving both sets of fans happy.
