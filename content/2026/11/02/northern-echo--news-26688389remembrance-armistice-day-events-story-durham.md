Thousands of handmade poppies created in memory of fallen Durham Light Infantry soldiers are to form part of a programme of Remembrance events in Durham.

The Story will host commemorative activities between November 7 and 11, culminating in the laying of wreaths and the national Two-Minute Silence on Armistice Day.

The events include A Community Remembers, a project created by Durham Markets to honour Durham Light Infantry soldiers who lost their lives during the First and Second World Wars.

Its original aim was to collect 16,386 knitted and crocheted poppies, representing one for every DLI soldier who died in service.

But the appeal exceeded that target, with individuals and craft groups from across the UK creating more than 30,000 poppies.

A total of 16,386 have been displayed at the entrances to Durham Market Hall since October 30, with the additional poppies being used for installations elsewhere in the region.

![Durham Market Poppy display pictured Arthur Lockyear Chairman of Durham City Remembrance group Picture: SARAH CALDECOTT](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21654781.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Arthur Lockyear, chairman of Durham City Remembrance Group, in front of the poppy display at Durham Market Hall

Some have found a new home at The Story, where its Learning Team has worked with schools from across County Durham to create commemorative wreaths using surplus poppies from the display.

On Wednesday, November 11, artist Denis Harry Fox and pupils from a local school will lay the wreaths beside the Wakenshaw Gun as the national Two-Minute Silence is observed.

The Story will also be illuminated from November 7 to 11 as a symbol of remembrance and reflection.

Meanwhile, work by Denis Harry Fox will go on display at the venue over Remembrance weekend.

A selection of the artist's new work exploring conflict, sacrifice and remembrance through the RAF will be exhibited on Saturday, November 7, and Remembrance Sunday, November 8.

The exhibition will be open from 10am until 4pm on both days and will be free to visit, with no booking required.

Families visiting The Story between November 7 and 11 will also be able to take part in Remembrance-themed activities in its Search and Discover space.

![Tour of The Story, Durham County Council's new cultural venue Picture: SARAH CALDECOTT](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657120.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Story in Durham, which is hosting a programme of activities to mark Remembrance

These will include an origami make-and-take activity as well as colouring sheets.

The programme accompanies A Debt Beyond Words, The Story's wider exhibition exploring the impact of conflict on communities and the experiences of people both in military service and on the Home Front.

The exhibition brings together personal stories and memories alongside art and propaganda, looking at the ways war affected individuals, families and communities.

More information about The Story's learning activities is available on its website.
