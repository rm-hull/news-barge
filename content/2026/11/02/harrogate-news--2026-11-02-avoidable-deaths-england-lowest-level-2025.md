Health inequalities and the gap between deprived and affluent communities remain a significant concern, despite avoidable mortality in England falling to its lowest recorded level.

New figures published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS) show that 110,804 deaths in England during 2025 were considered avoidable among people aged under 75.

The avoidable mortality rate fell by 3.1% to 219.5 deaths per 100,000 people, the lowest since records began in 2001.

However, the figures highlight a substantial divide between communities, raising questions about health inequalities in areas such as Harrogate and the wider North Yorkshire region.

## Deprivation linked to significantly higher death rates

Across England, people living in the most deprived areas had avoidable mortality rates 3.3 times higher than those in the least deprived areas.

Men had higher rates of avoidable mortality than women across all levels of deprivation, with the largest differences recorded in the most deprived communities.

Respiratory diseases showed the strongest association with deprivation. Mortality rates from these conditions were more than eight times higher in the most deprived areas than in the least deprived, for both men and women.

For Harrogate and North Yorkshire, the national findings raise questions about whether the county’s more affluent areas sit alongside communities experiencing significantly poorer health outcomes.

## Cancer remains leading cause of avoidable mortality

Neoplasms, or tumours, remained the leading cause of avoidable mortality in England, accounting for 70.4 deaths per 100,000 people.

Diseases of the circulatory system were the second largest cause. The ONS reported that mortality rates for all the major groups of causes of death fell in England during 2025.

## North East has highest regional rate

Avoidable mortality rates fell in every region of England. The North East continued to record the highest rate, at 270.0 deaths per 100,000 people, while the South East had the lowest, at 190.6.

London recorded the largest regional reduction, with its rate falling by 5%.

Overall, 20.7% of deaths in England during 2025 were considered avoidable.

The ONS defines avoidable deaths as those among people aged under 75 that could have been prevented through effective public health interventions or treated through appropriate healthcare.

The latest figures do not provide a specific breakdown for Harrogate or individual North Yorkshire communities.

Source: Office for National Statistics, *Avoidable mortality in England and Wales: 2025*, published 2 November 2026.
