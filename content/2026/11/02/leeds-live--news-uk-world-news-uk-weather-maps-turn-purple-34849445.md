# UK weather maps turn purple as snow hits 31 counties in -4C freeze - full list

## WXCharts weather maps have turned purple indicating a possibility of snowfall across 31 counties

Britain is preparing for a significant cold snap as the latest weather maps have turned purple, signalling the prospect of snowfall across swathes of the country. Charts produced by WXCharts indicate that no fewer than 31 counties across Scotland and England could experience snowfall as temperatures plunge to as low as -4C at 6am on November 11.

The freezing conditions are forecast to sweep across the nation next week, with most of Scotland bearing the brunt of the snowfall. The wintry weather will subsequently push southwards into England, with areas surrounding Leicester, Surrey, Birmingham and Oxford also in the firing line, according to the maps, reports the Express.

Temperatures look set to nosedive below zero across numerous regions as a fierce Arctic blast moves in, with those closer to Inverness facing the most severe conditions. Weather charts indicate that Inverness, Fort Williams, Portress and Stirling will experience temperatures fluctuating between bitter lows of -3C and -4C, while the Midlands braces for lows of -1C.

The predicted snowfall shown on the latest weather maps follows a succession of yellow warnings for fog issued by the Met Office, blanketing vast swathes of the UK in recent days, with dense fog reducing visibility to below 100 metres anticipated across numerous areas.

The Met Office's long-range forecast covering November 6 to 15 states: "High pressure is likely to dominate conditions across much of the UK initially, with large amounts of cloud at times but any amounts of rain or drizzle tending to be small."

"Quite breezy in the south, but a greater chance of some fog patches in northern areas where winds will be lighter. As the high pressure begins to migrate away to the east, frontal systems may attempt to bring some occasional rain to parts of the southwest and/or northwest at times, though exactly how much progress into the UK this makes is uncertain.

"In any case, rainfall is likely to be below average in many areas for this period as a whole, with temperatures initially a little below average before returning to near or slightly above average."
