Calderdale Council is facing a projected £18.7 million budget gap within three years, according to a new financial plan.

Cabinet papers seen by the Local Democracy Reporting Service show the previous financial plan had projected a shortfall of £6.8million by 2028/29, but that is set to grow to £30.1m in the same period before coming back down to £18.7m by 2029/30.

The local authority has warned of cuts, while blaming mounting pressures in children’s social care, adult social care, and special educational needs provision.

Cabinet papers also show the Council overspend for 2025/26 was £7.9m, but this is projected to come down to £5.3m for 2026/27.

The majority of the projected overspend will come in Adults and Wellbeing at £4.586m, with Children and Young People’s Services accounting for £992,000; Public Services at £186,000; Resources and Transformation at £164,000; Chief Executives at £83,000; and Regeneration and Strategy at £35,000.

Centrally controlled budgets are forecast to be £511,000 under budget.

A key reason for the revised financial plan is lower council tax income, because fewer new homes are being built than previously expected.

The council report states: “Based upon the current estimates there is a net deficit forecast for 2026/27. This is primarily relating to the assumed level of growth in dwellings and due to the assumed reduction in single person discount not materialising.

“There has been some progress to the increase in new homes being completed, a slowing down is now taking effect on the council tax income assumptions, a detailed analysis of potential future housing growth needs to be undertaken.”

The report puts the projected collection fund deficit at £2.9m.

In a move that would reduce council revenues even further, last month Council Leader Dan Sutherland (Reform UK) pledged not to put up council tax by the usual maximum of 4.99 per cent while accusing the previous Labour administration of ‘wasteful’ spending.

Last Friday the former Labour cabinet member for Planning, Housing and Environment said his Reform-led Council will focus on what matters most to local people.

Cllr Sutherland said: “Times are tough and Council finances are stretched. The budgeting process is about prioritising spend and getting the best value out of every pound in the public purse.

“The next budget will see a shift in focus to reflect our pledges in the Corporate Plan – the things people told us are important to them – value for money, the condition of our roads, crime and anti-social behaviour, fly-tipping and cleaner streets, housing, good-quality and safe care, and a vibrant local economy.

“We will use the Medium Term Financial Plan as the basis to prioritise investment. It helps us estimate what resources are available to deliver for Calderdale.”

The papers also show the projections account for ‘budgetary pressures’ of £5m, £7.5m, and £9m over the next three years, with rising care home (664 to 698 people ), mental health (up 31 per cent), and disability placements (up nine per cent) given as key reasons.

The ‘SEND High Needs Deficit’ charge of £11.2million is the reason for a large jump to £30.1m in 2028/29, though ‘it is assumed’ the government will continue to provide a recovery grant to partially fund the deficits.

The report also allows for ‘future inflation’ of £8m in 2029/30.

A Calderdale Labour spokesperson said: “Value for money isn’t simply about cutting costs. It’s about protecting essential services, supporting vulnerable residents and making sensible investments that prevent bigger problems and greater costs in the future.”

The spokesperson said the previous Labour administration had continued to invest in communities and services while facing the same pressures but added that “difficult choices will be necessary”.

“Our priority will be ensuring those choices are fair, transparent, properly evidenced and made in the best interests of Calderdale residents,” they added.

“Reform now has responsibility for setting Calderdale’s budget, and with that comes accountability for the choices it makes.”

The new financial plan will be discussed at the next Cabinet meeting on 9 November, which will inform the new budget to be set at the annual Budget Council meeting on 1 March 2027.
