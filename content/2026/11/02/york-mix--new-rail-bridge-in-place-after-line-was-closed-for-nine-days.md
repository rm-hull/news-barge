Between Saturday 24 October and Sunday 1 November, engineers worked around the clock to install the new Common Lane railway bridge at **Church Fenton** and completed the fitting of new overhead power lines, allowing electric trains on the route in the future.

The improvement work was the **Transpennine Route Upgrade (TRU**).

The direct line from York to Leeds had to close for nine days.

The previous bridge, built in 1901, was reaching the end of its lifespan.

By carrying out the replacement works a year earlier than necessary, TRU has maximised their time accessing the operational railway to modernise infrastructure while also working to prepare the railway for the future installation of overhead line equipment.

This means further disruption to the local community will be minimised, said Network Rail.

The team didn’t let Storm Boelo stand in its way, with activities rescheduled in advance, enabling work to be temporarily paused for safety during the height of the high winds and poor weather.

Teams worked hard to stay on target and the railway re-opened to trains on time.

During the nine-day closure customers were kept on the move through a comprehensive network of replacement bus services.

Following the installation of the new bridge at Church Fenton, work has now started to relocate diverted utility services within the new structure, before the road is restored and re-opened in June 2027.

A major programme of improvements was also carried out on the East Coast Main Line in North Yorkshire over the past two weekends, including renewing track and points, upgrading overhead line equipment and drainage, managing vegetation and improving platform infrastructure.
