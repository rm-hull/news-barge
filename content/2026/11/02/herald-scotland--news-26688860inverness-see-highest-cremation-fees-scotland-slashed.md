Bereaved families in Inverness could see cremation fees slashed by more than £250 as the city currently has the highest prices in Scotland.

A paper due to be discussed by councillors at a meeting of the communities and place committee on Thursday, November 5, proposes the price cut to direct cremation fees after the cost of living has driven a rise in demand for lower-cost funeral options, it explains.

If approved, the price for direct cremation would drop from £820 – the highest price in Scotland – to £565 far closer to the national average price of £522.

The report states: “The proposals are driven by increasing demand for lower-cost funeral options and evidence that financial pressures are affecting many bereaved families.

“Direct cremations now account for around 21 per cent of funerals across the UK, reflecting changing customer expectations and preferences.”

Councillors will also be asked to agree to a new “reflection service” with a price tag of £820, it is described in the report as “a simple and dignified attended service in the small chapel with a 15-minute reflection period, one piece of music and the display of a photograph”.

The proposed new service is offered as a lower cost option to a traditional service held within the crematorium.

If the plans are approved the new prices will be implemented this month.
