BAK Diabira was an incredibly hard-working man, both on and off the field.

The black scrum half knew he was a talented rugby league player and so did Bradford Northern.

The French native had only been at the club for a week after arriving from Constable Street Youth Club in Hull, but on this day 60 years ago, he got the call, as reported in the T&A.

And not to play for Bradford’s ‘A’ team either, but for the senior side against Doncaster that weekend.

The 18-year-old had blown club officials away with his ability and attitude, with his debut going perfectly, as Northern beat their South Yorkshire visitors 22-8 at Odsal.

From there, Diabira began to get regular game time, particularly during his first five years at the club.

He stayed put for nearly a decade, making 125 appearances in total, before spells at Hunslet and Blackpool Borough.

Diabira returned to the district in a rugby league capacity to coach Keighley for a few years in the early 1980s, but he had remained in Bradford since that move as a teenager and was hugely popular in the city.

Away from the game, the scrum half had worked for Bradford-based car parts firm Federal Mogul Aftermarket UK Ltd since 1969.

But he was sacked in May 1998 from his role as warehouse manager, out of the blue.

The Wyke resident showed the same tenacity he had shown on the pitch, taking his case to a tribunal for unlawful discrimination, unfair dismissal, loss of earning and injury to feelings.

And that paid off, as in February 1999, Diabira was awarded nearly £48,000, after it was ruled he had been sacked because he was black.

You can read the full remarkable story of that case and its aftermath here.

Happily, Diabira made a happier appearance in the T&A a few years later.

In 2008, he helped launch of a new children’s rugby league book, Bullyboy and the Magic Album.

He posed with the book at Odsal, with various primary schools attending a game that summer in order to receive a copy.
