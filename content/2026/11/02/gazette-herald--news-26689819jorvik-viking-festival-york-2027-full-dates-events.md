Vikings are set to invade York once again as plans are unveiled for the return of the Jorvik Viking Festival in 2027.

Vikings and Normans will battle for York when the festival runs from February 8 to 14 with a week of combat, living history, family activities and events inspired by the Viking-led rebellion against William the Conqueror in 1066.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21658076.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Vikings vs Normans: Jorvik Viking Festival returns to York for 2027 *(Image: Submitted)*

The festival will bring the historic clash between Normans and Norsemen to life through immersive, city-wide activities.

Mark Jackson, of York Archaeology, which organises the festival, said: "Most people assume York's Viking story ends with the Norman Conquest in 1066. It didn’t.

"Just three years later, a huge Danish fleet of 240 ships joined Northumbrian and Anglo-Saxon rebels to take York from the Normans.

"It is an extraordinary episode, and it gives us the perfect story around which to build the 2027 Festival."

The 2026 festival attracted nearly 65,000 visitors and organisers are preparing for an even larger turnout next year.

Parliament Street will once again be transformed into a bustling Viking encampment, featuring tents, craftspeople, replica ships, food, drink, and the Berserker Bootcamp for children.

New for 2027 is an axe-throwing experience.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21658079.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Vikings in York during JORVIK Viking Festival 2026. *(Image: Submitted)*

Other highlights include the Viking Games and Battle at the Eye of York, re-enacting the 1069 invasion, and Kids’ Barbaric Battles in Museum Gardens.

The ever-popular Poo Week at DIG, inspired by the Lloyds Bank fossilised poo, will also return, alongside the Viking Traders market and regular crafting sessions at Barley Hall.

Mr Jackson said: "This is one of the most ambitious programmes we've ever put together, and as usual, York Archaeology has worked hard to ensure that JORVIK Viking Festival includes a host of free activities for all ages, as well as discounted tickets to our Eye of York events for low-income families.

"We’re delighted for the encampment to be supported by the University of York, with our Bloodaxe Book Challenge and other literary-themed events supported by Osprey Publishing."

Due to York's earlier half-term break, the Viking encampment will open two days ahead of the main festival, coinciding with the York Ice Trail on February 6 and 7.

![- Vikings descend on the city of York for JORVIK Viking Festival 2026 – Picture date Saturday 21 February, 2026 (York, North Yorkshire) Photo copyright, contact for licensing. An ice sculpture will feature in the encampment that weekend.

Mr Jackson said: "We would not have been able to do this without the University of York’s sponsorship, but it should add some additional Norse culture to an already popular weekend activity – and we’ll have an ice sculpture in the encampment, too."

Tickets for most paid events are now available at jorvikvikingfestival.co.uk, with full details of locations and timings listed on the website.

Organisers strongly advise early booking, as nearly all events sold out weeks in advance during the 2026 festival.
