An alpaca farm offering therapeutic experiences could be forced to close due to a planning dispute.

Butterwick Alpaca Retreat, near Malton, has been told it must stop residential use of its site after North Yorkshire Council refused planning permission for owners Anna and Chris Ramsey to live on the farm.

The couple said living on-site is essential for animal welfare and for running their business, which provides sensory experiences to more than 30 organisations, including charities, care homes, and facilities supporting people with special needs.

Anna Ramsey said: "Our animals need 24 hours a day support in case of emergencies, to assist with births and to make sure they are safe.

"Even our vets agree that our constant presence on site is essential.

"We have been living here since we bought the land and simply want to get planning permission for our small dwelling on site."

She said the animals have a "positive impact" on people of all ages, especially those who find it difficult to communicate or express their feelings.

Anna said: "We are now asking local people to help so that we can continue to offer our services to this community into the future."

The retreat has faced planning challenges since 2022.

In July 2023, Ryedale District Council refused a change-of-use application on highways grounds, despite an independent survey commissioned by the Ramseys which found limited impact on local roads.

The couple appealed to the inspectorate in October 2024, and in December 2024 were told their business could continue operating.

However, due to errors in the planning inspectors, the couple returned to the inspectorate in April 2026 after a second application was refused.

North Yorkshire Council rejected their application in July, and the couple have been told they must leave the site within months.

Anna Ramsey said: "The report that North Yorkshire Council received was full of inaccuracies and we want to challenge the result as it is based on the false premise that we are not a sustainable business.

"Over the last five years we have grown our business into a hugely successful enterprise and have exciting plans for the future which have been largely ignored."

The retreat is supported by Ryedale Special Families, whose chief executive Lisa Keenan said: "Ryedale Special Families has used Butterwick Alpaca Retreat for a number of years.

"Our main use has been to give work experience opportunities to disabled young people.

"Work experience opportunities are in short supply locally and Ryedale Special Families would be very disappointed if the farm were to close."

Mike Pagham, executive chairman of St Cecilia’s Care Group, said the loss of the retreat would be a "serious blow" to the community.

He said: "We have used Butterwick Alpaca Retreat many, many times and place enormous value on what it offers our residents, particularly those living with dementia."

In response, the couple have launched a petition and GoFundMe page to raise support and cover legal fees for their next planning application.

North Yorkshire Council’s head of development management, Martin Grainger, said: "The site has been the subject of planning and enforcement action over a number of years.

"In each case, decisions have been taken in accordance with relevant national and local planning policies and procedures.

"The most recent planning application, which sought retrospective planning permission for residential use, was refused planning permission and an appeal against that decision was later dismissed by the Planning Inspectorate.

"There are currently no planning applications being considered for the site, and the enforcement notice relating to the unauthorised residential use remains in place."

Anna said: "We just want to continue to do what we do and have the security of knowing we can continue to live here to make sure our animals are safe and well cared for – it really isn’t asking a lot."
