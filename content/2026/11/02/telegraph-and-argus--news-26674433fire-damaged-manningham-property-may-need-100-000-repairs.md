Construction workers say a historic Manningham property severely damaged by fire could require around £100,000 of repairs, as concerns grow over the future of the building within one of Bradford's designated conservation areas.

Ahsan Manzoo and Sedic Ollah had just finished work at a neighbouring property on Apsley Crescent and gone out for food when they received a call informing them the building next door was on fire.

"We had only just sat down to eat when the landlord called and asked what had happened," Mr Manzoo said.

"We didn't know anything about it. He told us the building next door was on fire and we started panicking."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637430.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Residents fear the blaze could have threatened neighbouring properties had it spread further. *(Image: T&A)*

The pair rushed back to the street fearing the blaze may have affected the property they had been working on.

## Smoke spreads to neighbouring homes

Although the neighbouring property escaped the flames, Mr Manzoo said smoke quickly spread through the house.

"When we came back, there was smoke throughout the property," he said. "You can still smell the smoke inside and it's difficult to breathe. The family had children there and they struggled because of the smoke, especially upstairs."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637431.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Apsley Crescent forms part of one of Manningham's designated conservation areas. *(Image: T&A)*

He said windows and doors were left open to help clear the smell, while occupants temporarily stayed elsewhere as cleaning work was carried out.

According to the workers, smoke damage resulted in carpets and furnishings requiring professional cleaning, with around £1,000 spent on remedial work.

## "Everything's gone"

Attention is now turning to the future of the fire-damaged building itself.

Looking at the extent of the damage, Mr Manzoo believes extensive restoration work would be required before the property could be brought back into use.

"Everything's gone," he said. "The doors and windows need replacing and the bathrooms and kitchens would need doing again as well. It's going to take a couple of months."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637435.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Overgrown vegetation surrounds the entrance to the fire-hit building. *(Image: T&A)*

Asked how much repairs could cost, he estimated the bill could reach around £100,000.

The workers said there is uncertainty over what happens next, with the building likely requiring significant investment before it could once again be occupied.

## Fears for historic conservation-area property

The damaged building sits within the Apsley Crescent Conservation Area, one of five conservation areas in Manningham.

A resident who has lived on the street for around 30 years said the incident could have been far worse had the fire spread through neighbouring properties.

He said: "The matter could have been much worse. The fire could have spread from roof to roof...it was very concerning because the smoke was transferred into the next-door house."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637434.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The fire-damaged property on Apsley Crescent has been vacant for some time. *(Image: T&A)*

He added: "If the fire service hadn't acted as quickly as they did, the matter could have been much worse. I have a huge amount of admiration for the firefighters who stopped it from spreading."

Residents say they are now concerned about the future of the prominent property and what impact its condition could have on the character of the historic crescent.

## Major emergency response

West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service mounted a major response after receiving reports of the fire at 8.18pm on October 21.

Five fire engines and an aerial appliance were sent to the scene, alongside specialist resources including a Hose Layer Unit.

The fire service said the blaze involved a derelict property consisting of three storeys and a basement, with fire affecting both the ground and first floors.

Six breathing apparatus wearers were deployed during the incident and crews used three hose reels and positive pressure ventilation equipment to bring the fire under control.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637433.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Workers estimate repairs to the fire-damaged property could cost around £100,000. *(Image: T&A)*

One firefighter suffered an injury while dealing with the blaze and was taken to hospital as a precaution.

The cause of the fire has not been disclosed.

## Historic street

Apsley Crescent forms part of one of Manningham's designated conservation areas, recognised for its architectural and historic significance.

The terrace is identified in Bradford Council conservation documents as an important part of the area's Victorian character, making the future of the fire-damaged property a concern for some residents living nearby.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21637436.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A faded sign offers a glimpse into the historic character of the Apsley Crescent property. *(Image: T&A)*

Apsley Crescent conservation area developed during the prolonged boom in Bradford’s textile industry during the mid 19th century.

Many of the villas and terraces were built for textile merchants and manufacturers and other tradesmen and professionals. The area developed as a desirable suburb of Bradford and the various houses give a unique insight into the architectural fashions of the time and give the area its strongly consistent character.

Over the years, Bradford Council has noted a number of properties displaying inappropriate alterations and additions, including unauthorised windows on listed buildings.
