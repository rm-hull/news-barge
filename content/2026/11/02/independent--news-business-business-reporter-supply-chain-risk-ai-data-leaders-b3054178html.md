*Blue Yonder is a Business Reporter client*

**The gap between supply chain leaders and laggards is widening – waiting to act is the one strategy guaranteed to leave you behind.**

When we speak with supply chain executives, they often say: “We’re doing everything we can, and we’re still falling behind.”

The research supports this sentiment. Blue Yonder’s annual Supply Chain Compass report surveyed 678 senior supply chain professionals and found **that** 34 per cent of leaders do not believe their supply chains are ready for the future. That figure is up from 27 per cent the year before. This is a rapid decline in the confidence of leaders to handle change and disruptions, seven points in a single year. A major reason behind these numbers is that the frequency of disruptions has increased. Whereas they used to be occasional, isolated events, now they are frequent and pervasive.

The supply chain systems of today were not built to handle this volatility. They were built as isolated systems designed to do one thing well, such as demand planning, transportation management or warehouse management.

The result is that decisions are made slowly and laboriously, with incomplete data and without regard to their effects on other parts of the supply chain. This makes the supply chain slow to react to demand shifts and supply disruptions, and costs rise while service levels plummet. In particular, when planning and execution run on separate systems, disruptions are difficult to anticipate, responses are delayed and costs spiral out of control.

What we need is a comprehensive supply chain platform that integrates planning, execution and collaboration on a common data foundation. This enables changes and information to flow in real time across the supply chain, providing the visibility necessary to anticipate and manage disruptions. Agentic AI and cognitive solutions can then be layered on top of this, enabling them to work across every tier in real time, providing real-time updates, disruption alerts and trade-off analysis. AI agents are able to respond quickly and effectively before problems escalate.

## **The confidence gap is real and measurable**

The research reveals a clear division between two groups of leaders. Close to half – 46 per cent of leaders – say they are highly optimistic about their supply chain’s future. We call them “the optimists”. What separates them from the rest of the pack is how they have built their operations.

![Close to half of leaders say they are highly optimistic about their supply chain’s future](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/22/10/47/Blue-Yonder_Body-Image.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Their less optimistic peers are two and a half times more likely to operate in silos and four times more likely to describe their supply chain as disjointed. This confidence of the optimistic group stems directly from having an integrated supply chain that supports end-to-end visibility.

## **“But it still works” is costing you more than you think**

If your current setup keeps the lights on, that’s a credit to your team more than it is to your technology. The ability to function and get by is not the same as being ready for the future.

This is clear from the Blue Yonder Supply Chain Compass survey results: 82 per cent of respondents agreed that outdated technology will hold back their supply chain performance. And today, siloed systems and scattered data sources will almost certainly undermine the success of any AI initiative. AI systems and decision-making agents need complete, up-to-date data sets that include trading-partner information to provide full context. Without that infrastructure, AI initiatives are likely to fail or deliver subpar results. A new, connected and unified operating model is the only path forward.

## **Why waiting is the riskiest decision**

Delaying only compounds the problem:

* Complexity grows; technical debt continues to climb, pressure builds and disruptions hit when you are least ready to respond
* Competitors pull ahead; 64 per cent of the optimists have committed more than $5 million to technology investment over the next one to five years, compared with 42 per cent of the other respondents
* Customers move on; when resolutions take days or weeks, your customers start to shop elsewhere

While change is challenging, not adapting in the face of rapid change is a recipe for decline, even extinction.

## **What progressive leaders do differently** The leaders making progress are doing three things that most are not.**They build connectivity first.** They build a foundation of end-to-end connectivity enabling visibility. Without a unified data foundation, AI and automation remain fragmented and suboptimal and decision-making remains slow.**They start where friction is highest.** They do not try to overhaul everything overnight. Implementation teams begin by targeting the highest friction process – for example, demand planning or exception management, and deliver measurable results quickly.**They treat it as transformation, not just technology.** They ensure their roadmap is aligned with and reflects their strategic priorities. They acknowledge that transformation and realising full business value require the entire organisation to align and adapt.

## **The clock is ticking**

The supply chains that will lead the next decade will be built on a cognitive platform where planning and execution share a common data foundation. Agentic AI will see across every tier, analyse trade-offs in seconds, and act to avert or mitigate crises.
