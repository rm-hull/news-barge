A feud between two families ended with the death of 23-year-old Daniel Ambler, a court has heard.

Daniel Ambler suffered serious injuries when Ashton Anderson drove a Nissan X-Trail into him on January 15, Teesside Crown Court heard.

The jury in the trial of murder-accused quintet Barry Anderson, his sons Ashton and Logan, Aja Henry and Tyler Leadbetter, was shown CCTV footage which prosecutors say shows the five hunting down and attacking Mr Ambler and his friend Reece Wilkin.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20435374.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Daniel Ambler *(Image: DURHAM POLICE)*

It was the culmination of a feud which began after Barry Anderson was stabbed last August.

Anderson’s partner provided CCTV footage to police, but by the following month, Anderson was being uncooperative with the police investigation, the jury heard.

Laura McLaughlin, an intelligence analyst with Durham Constabulary, saidthat messages and calls between the families were collected during the investigation.

The jury heard that messages and threats continued between members of both families in the months before Mr Ambler’s death.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20439774.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Esso New Seaham Service Station, in Stockton Road, Seaham, the scene of Daniel Ambler's death *(Image: Sarah Caldecott (The Northern Echo))*

On January 5, Logan Anderson received a message saying Daniel Ambler wanted to “sort things out like men”, the court heard.

The Nissan X-Trail had also been bought days before the crash.

The prosecution claims the attack was coordinated and that the four defendants aided or encouraged Ashton Anderson to use unlawful violence against Mr Ambler.

Witness Alexis Prest told jurors that nobody in the Nissan X-Trail encouraged Ashton Anderson to drive on to the forecourt or celebrated after Mr Ambler was struck.

Ajay Henry, of Melrose Crescent, Seaham, and Tyler Leadbetter, of Elgin Avenue, Seaham, were both 17 when charged with murdering Daniel Ambler.

Ashton Anderson has pleaded guilty to violent disorder. All other charges are denied.

The trial is expected to last up to five weeks at Teesside Crown Court.
