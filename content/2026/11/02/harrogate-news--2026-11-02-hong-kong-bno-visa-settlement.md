Hong Kong BN(O) visa settlement rights are to be protected following Government reassurance welcomed by Tom Gordon, MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, after months of uncertainty for members of the local Hong Kong community.

The Liberal Democrat MP said the Home Secretary’s statement on preserving the existing settlement arrangements was long overdue, following more than a year of campaigning to ensure people who relocated to Britain under the British National (Overseas) visa scheme could continue with their original route to permanent residence.

Gordon, who is a member of the Immigration and Asylum Bill Committee, has been pressing the Government to maintain the existing five-year settlement pathway, commonly known as the 5+1 route.

His campaign has included a petition presented to Parliament last year, which attracted more than 300 signatures with the help of Harrogate campaigner Albert Kam.

## Harrogate MP campaigns to protect Hong Kong BN(O) visa settlement rights

The issue has particular significance for Harrogate and Knaresborough, which has an established Hong Kong community made up of people who have relocated to the area to build new lives in the UK.

Many arrived under the BN(O) visa scheme with the understanding that, after five years of continuous residence, they could apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain (ILR), giving them permanent permission to live in Britain.

However, proposed changes to the wider immigration settlement system created uncertainty about whether the arrangements would remain unchanged for people already living in the UK.

Gordon argued that changing the rules after people had made significant decisions based on the original arrangements would be unfair and could undermine the confidence of families who had already left their homes, careers and established lives in Hong Kong.

## What is the Hong Kong BN(O) visa scheme?

The British National (Overseas) visa route was introduced in January 2021 following China’s introduction of a national security law in Hong Kong in 2020.

The legislation prompted widespread international concern about political freedoms and civil liberties in the territory.

The UK Government introduced the visa route to provide eligible British National (Overseas) status holders and certain members of their families with an opportunity to relocate to Britain.

The scheme allows eligible applicants to live, work and study in the UK. Applicants can generally apply for permission to stay for either five years or an initial period of 30 months, with the possibility of extending their permission.

Under the original settlement arrangements, eligible applicants can apply for Indefinite Leave to Remain after completing five years of continuous residence in the UK, provided they meet the relevant immigration requirements.

After holding settled status for a further 12 months, eligible individuals can apply for British citizenship.

This is commonly referred to as the 5+1 settlement route: five years before applying for permanent settlement, followed by a further year before becoming eligible to apply for citizenship.

For families who moved under the scheme, this pathway was an important consideration when deciding to relocate. It provided a degree of certainty about how they could establish permanent lives in Britain.

## Proposed immigration reforms raised concerns

The uncertainty arose from wider Government proposals to reform the UK’s immigration settlement system through an earned settlement model.

The proposals, published in November 2025, included plans to increase the standard qualifying period for Indefinite Leave to Remain from five to ten years for many migrants.

The proposed system would place greater emphasis on factors such as employment, earnings, English language ability and contributions to the UK economy.

Under the proposals, some individuals could potentially qualify for settlement sooner, while others could face longer qualifying periods depending on their circumstances.

For Hong Kong BN(O) visa holders, the concern was whether the new arrangements would affect people who had already relocated to Britain and were working towards the five-year settlement milestone.

Many had made long-term decisions about employment, housing, education and family life based on the original immigration pathway.

The possibility of changing those arrangements created anxiety among families who wanted reassurance that the commitments underpinning their move to Britain would be respected.

The Government had previously indicated that BN(O) visa holders would receive a five-year reduction against the proposed standard settlement period. However, questions remained about the mandatory requirements and how the final arrangements would operate.

In January 2026, ministers confirmed that BN(O) visa holders would continue to be able to settle after five years of residence, subject to meeting mandatory requirements.

The latest reassurance is therefore significant for a community which has been seeking clarity over its long-term position.

## Petition presented to Parliament

Tom Gordon’s campaign has involved working with members of the local Hong Kong community to make representations to the Government.

In November 2025, he presented a petition to Parliament calling for the existing 5+1 settlement pathway to be protected for BN(O) visa holders already living in the UK.

The petition attracted more than 300 signatures, with local campaigner Albert Kam playing a significant role in gathering support.

The campaign highlighted the importance of allowing people who had moved to Britain under the original arrangements to continue planning their futures without the prospect of unexpected changes to their settlement rights.

Gordon has also raised the issue in Parliament, arguing that the UK should honour the commitments made to people who had relocated following the situation in Hong Kong.

## Tom Gordon says Government action was long overdue

Welcoming the Home Secretary’s statement, Gordon said:

> “This is long overdue. I’ve been campaigning to preserve BNO (British National Overseas) rights for our local Hong Kong community for over a year.”

He continued:

> “The government has finally seen sense but only after months of cruel uncertainty for visa holders from Hong Kong and dedicated campaigning from people like Albert Kam in Harrogate.

> Last year, we delivered a petition with over 300 signatures urging the Government to change course and keep the current 5+1 settlement route in place for BNO visa holders already residing in the UK, ensuring that they can continue to settle into their new lives here.

> It shouldn’t take you this long to decide to do the right thing.”

## What does this mean for Hong Kong families in Harrogate?

For people who have relocated from Hong Kong, the ability to obtain permanent settlement is about more than immigration paperwork.

It provides greater certainty when making decisions about buying a home, pursuing a career, establishing a business and planning their children’s education.

Many families have invested considerable time and money in building new lives in Britain. Permanent settlement provides an important step towards long-term security and full participation in British society.

The BN(O) visa scheme has also enabled communities across the country to benefit from the skills, experience and contributions of people arriving from Hong Kong.

In Harrogate and Knaresborough, the local campaign has demonstrated how members of the community have been willing to engage directly with their MP and Parliament to protect the arrangements they relied upon when moving to the UK.

While the Government’s reassurance has been welcomed, applicants must still satisfy the relevant immigration and settlement requirements. The Home Office continues to publish guidance setting out the eligibility and residence conditions for settlement under the BN(O) route.

For Gordon, the announcement represents an important outcome for the local campaign and the wider Hong Kong community, although he believes the Government should have provided certainty much sooner.

Further information about the Hong Kong BN(O) visa route is available through the Home Office guidance.
