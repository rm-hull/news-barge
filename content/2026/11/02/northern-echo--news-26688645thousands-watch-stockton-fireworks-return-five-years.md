Thousands flocked to Stockton’s much-loved riverside fireworks as the skies above the Tees lit up for the first time in five years.

Crowds lined Riverside Road, packed the riverbank viewing areas and filled the amphitheatre at the town’s new Waterfront urban park for the free event on Sunday, November 1.

Launched from the Teesdale side of the River Tees, the 20-minute display was set to an orchestral soundtrack inspired by water and ended a day of town-centre entertainment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657541.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stockton Council's firework display, Urban Waterfront Park on Sunday (November 1) *(Image: Dawn McNamara Photography)*

Families gathered from lunchtime for fairground rides on Stockton High Street, live music in Finkle Square and food and drink stalls before the fireworks began at 6.30pm.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657542.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stockton Council's firework display, Urban Waterfront Park on Sunday (November 1) *(Image: Dawn McNamara Photography)*

The display returned to Stockton’s riverside for the first time since 2021, giving visitors a backdrop of the town’s transformed waterfront.

Councillor Nigel Cooke, Stockton Council’s cabinet member for regeneration and housing, called the return of the fireworks an "unforgettable experience" and praised Waterfront Park as a viewing spot.

As darkness fell, bursts of colour lit the sky above the Tees as thousands watched the display, one of Stockton’s most popular community celebrations.
