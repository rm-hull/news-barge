First-look images have offered a glimpse of the ongoing multi-million-pound transformation at one of Scotland’s best-known hotels, marking a “major new chapter” for the property.

Family-owned Apex Hotels, founded in Scotland and operating nine properties across the UK, acquired the historic Dunblane Hydro in 2024 and rebranded it under the Apex name the following year.

The phased refurbishment covers the 200-bedroom hotel's accommodation, public areas, food and beverage outlets, indoor and outdoor leisure spaces, as well as its conference and events facilities, while allowing it to remain operational throughout.

At the heart of the plans is a revamp of its bedrooms and suites alongside a completely reimagined food and beverage offering.

A brand-new signature restaurant, Circadian, will be part of the next phase of planned works and is set to open in early 2027.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21658865.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 1813 bar, named after the year in which the Dunblane spring was discovered, has now opened to guests and locals *(Image: VIKTOR KERY PHOTOS)*

Averil Wilson, managing director of Apex Hotels, said: “Apex Dunblane Hydro is a very special hotel, with an incredible history and a strong connection to the local community. Being from Stirlingshire myself, with many happy memories of celebrations at the hotel, I’m hugely proud to see Apex Hotels bring this much-loved landmark into an exciting new chapter.

“Our investment is about retaining everything that makes the Scottish Hydro hotel distinctive as a cornerstone of our country’s hospitality offering. We’re continuing to elevate every element of the experience for today’s guest, to create thoughtful spaces which remain true to the character of the building.

“As a family-owned business, we are committed to a long-term investment in Apex Dunblane Hydro and putting this landmark property back at the heart of celebrations in Scotland.

"We want the hotel to be somewhere local people are proud to have on their doorstep as well as somewhere visitors from across Scotland, the UK and beyond actively choose to experience.”
