James Robertson, founder of Isle of Skye Candle Company, highlights his ambitions to keep expanding the business internationally.

Mr Robertson, who was previously a snowboard instructor in Canada, underlines the importance of such growth to creating “great job security for the people who work for us so even if there was a downturn in tourism we could still be a great employer on Skye”.

He flags the difficulties in trading with the European Union created by Brexit and declares: “Getting back into the EU would be massive for us.”

Reflecting on a “few tough moments”, he says: “One time I remember being so skint and was waiting on getting some invoice paid that I had to try and find coins down the side of the sofa to help pay for fuel for my car.”

Mr Robertson adds: “I always want the best for myself, my family and for the Highlands and islands of Scotland.”

And he declares: “I do think if it wasn't for the fame, being First Minister would be interesting.”

**Job title:** Founder**What is your business called?** Isle of Skye Candle Company**Where is it based?** Portree on the Isle of Skye

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21652037.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James Robertson highlighted international growth ambitions. *(Image: Supplied)*

**What does it produce/do?** We manufacture, wholesale and retail candles and diffusers**To whom does it sell?** We sell via our own stores, wholesalers in the UK and via distributors in other countries.**What is its turnover?** Prefer not to say**How many employees?** Over 100**What attracted you to your current role?** I was working for the Isle of Skye Soap Company - which we recently acquired -  and I saw they were buying candles from England. I spotted an opportunity where I could make them locally.**What were you doing before?** I was working as a snowboard instructor in Canada.**What do you least enjoy?** Paperwork.**What do you consider to be the main successes of the business?** Making great premium products and keeping them at affordable prices so people can keep buying them.**What are your ambitions for the firm?** To keep building the brand internationally so that we create great job security for the people who work for us so even if there was a downturn in tourism we could still be a great employer on Skye.**What are the challenges facing the sector and market, and what could be done to overcome or address these?** Competition. Every place you visit seems to have its own candle company whereas we were one of the only ones when we started. We just keep trying to innovate with new fragrances and maintaining the highest quality, as well as investing in packaging.**What single thing would most help?** Getting back into the EU would be massive for us, since Brexit it’s made trading in Europe 10 times harder to get products sold there and for absolutely no benefit. I’m sure most people in the UK would now agree time to get negotiating our way back in.**Read more**

**What is the most valuable lesson you have learned?** The biggest lesson for me is to keep innovating and going with my gut. It's amazing how opportunities seem to present themselves at the right time and how you have to use good judgement to let some go and grab others.**What was your best moment?** The best moment for me was when we finally completed our purchase of what was the Aros centre and now our visitor centre, it just felt unreal that we managed to pull it off with a lot of thanks going to our business bank manager Graeme at Virgin [Money] - now Nationwide - who pulled out all the stops for us.**What has been your most challenging moment in life or business?** Had a few tough moments when we’ve been really close to running out of money and just getting by, by the skin of our teeth! One time I remember being so skint and was waiting on getting some invoice paid that I had to try and find coins down the side of the sofa to help pay for fuel for my car.**How do you relax?** I’m very much an outdoors man. I love being up the mountains and in the sea, on a snowboard or wakeboard. I also still play our local sport here, shinty, although it feels weird now being the oldest in the team!**What phrase or quotation has inspired you the most?** Weirdly the phrase that sticks with me most is “Never Settle” from the phone manufacturer OnePlus. I like to believe I never have, and never will as I always want the best for myself, my family and for the Highlands and islands of Scotland.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21132950.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The company employs more than 100 people. *(Image: Supplied)*

**What is the best book (fiction or non-fiction) you have ever read? Why is it the best?** I can never get past Lord of the Rings. I just love those books so much as it really gets your imagination going into other worlds. I feel sorry for people now though as most likely they will have seen the films before the books so lose some of the magic of the imagination. Although the films are excellent too.**Where do you find yourself most at ease?** Up the Cuillins in Skye or on the beaches in North Uist with my family. I’m so lucky with so many good people around me.**If you weren’t in your current role, what job would you most fancy?** I can’t see myself ever working for anyone else as I love how you only get out of your business what you put in but I do think if it wasn't for the fame, being First Minister would be interesting.**What countries have you most enjoyed travelling to, for business or leisure, and why?** I love France so much and could happily go there for every holiday and business trip. It’s got it all: fantastic food, amazing mountains, the Mediterranean coast, the Atlantic coast, wine, beer and of course sun too but it still can’t quite live up to my beloved Highlands.
