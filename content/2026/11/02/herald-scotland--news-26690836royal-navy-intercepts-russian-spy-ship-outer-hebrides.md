The Royal Navy intercepted a Russian spy ship off the coast of Scotland.

Merlin helicopters were deployed at short notice from Royal Naval Air Station Culdrose in Cornwall to Glasgow Prestwick Airport to conduct Operation Iceblast.

From September 29 until October 8, the Merlins utilised their powerful sensors to keep track of Vishnya-class intelligence-gathering ship Viktor Leonov in the Outer Hebrides.

The Russian ship was closely monitoring NATO warships taking part in Exercise Strike Warrior, a major, Royal Navy-led multinational NATO military exercise which involved more than 2,000 personnel, eight warships, and 19 aircraft from six NATO nations.

Meanwhile, Type 45 destroyer HMS Dauntless and patrol ship HMS Tyne were tasked from October 1 to October 14 to report on the movements of RFN Neustrashimy, as the Russian frigate escorted sanctioned cargo ships through the English Channel.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21659547.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HMS Tyne (bottom) intercepts RFN Aleksandr Shabalin as she escorts a cargo ship and tug *(Image: Royal Navy)*

Captain James Hall, the commanding officer of RNAS Culdrose, said: “I am immensely proud of the efforts of our people, who have once again demonstrated that the UK is prepared to respond to immediate operational requirements.

“This tasking was part of a wider series of operations, known collectively as Operation Iceblast, which was designed to test the ability of Merlin Helicopter Force to deploy away from its home base of RNAS Culdrose in force and at short notice.

“This operation has been a critical means for demonstrating to ourselves, our adversaries and the nation that we are willing and capable of securing the homeland 24/7. The Merlin Helicopter Force stands ready to answer the call at a moment’s notice.”

After handing over responsibility of Neustrashimy to HMS Dauntless, HMS Tyne was tasked to intercept RFN Aleksandr Shabalin between October 11 and October 14, as the Ropucha-class landing ship escorted cargo ship MV Energy-2 accompanied by tugboat Proton.

HMS Tyne was activated from North Shields to watch every move as the Russians sailed through the English Channel enroute to the Baltic Region and was accompanied by a Wildcat helicopter from 815 Naval Air Squadron.

Lieutenant Commander Sam Fields, HMS Tyne’s Commanding Officer, said: “Operating from the North East to the English Channel in quick succession, HMS Tyne’s recent activations demonstrate the flexibility and enduring utility of our patrol ships.

“Maintaining a persistent presence around the UK and beyond, we stand ready to respond at a moment’s notice to protect our nation’s interests. I am proud of my ship’s company for their unwavering professionalism and adaptability.”

HMS Dauntless' Commanding Officer, Commander Neil Skinner, added: "The Royal Navy's ability to monitor and respond to activity around the United Kingdom is fundamental to our role in protecting the nation's interests at sea.

"HMS Dauntless maintained the close and proficient monitoring of the Russian warship Neustrashimy during her operations within the English Channel, providing reassurance to the UK and our allies whilst contributing to a comprehensive understanding of the maritime environment that surrounds us.

"I am immensely proud of the professionalism, dedication and operational readiness displayed by every member of HMS Dauntless throughout this tasking."
