**The Scottish Greens have hit back after the Israeli embassy in London accused one of their MSPs of planning to engage in “intentional provocation for social media clout”.**

Edinburgh and Lothians East MSP Kate Nevens was refused entry to the West Bank last Monday, alongside SNP MP for Arbroath and Broughty Ferry Lara Bird and members of political group Progressive International.

In a statement provided to the Daily Mail, the embassy claimed that the delegation was turned away after questioning “revealed that their initially provided itinerary did not reflect their actual plans”, which was described as “political activities and intentional provocation for social media clout”.

Speaking in support of Ms Nevens, a spokesperson for the Scottish Greens said Israel was an "apartheid state”.

The spokesperson told The Herald: “Israel … has inflicted decades of abuses on Palestinians, and is illegally occupying the West Bank, where Kate and the delegation were headed. Israeli border guards already have a long history of refusing entry to their critics.

“Particularly with settler and state violence escalating, it is totally inappropriate for an occupying army to block human rights observers from being able to see the repression they are responsible for.”

However, the embassy told the Daily Mail that it was "misleading" to represent the incident “as an arbitrary attempt to silence foreign politicians or prevent them from hearing different perspectives”.

“Israel welcomes dialogue, open communication and constructive engagement,” the embassy said.

“Such engagement must be based on honesty and transparency, not misrepresentation. Like any sovereign state, Israel has the right and responsibility to verify the identity, intentions and activities of individuals seeking to enter its territory, and to deny entry when there are reasonable grounds to do so.

“The issue was not their political views, but the lack of transparency surrounding their visit and the discrepancies between the purpose they initially declared and the activities they intended to undertake.”

The attempted visit came amid worsening tensions in the West Bank.

Last month, human rights group Yesh Din reported that 561 incidents of violence took place against Palestinians between September 27 and October 17, in at least 180 Palestinian communities in the illegally occupied territory.

"The theft of olive trees, similar to violence and the prevention of the harvest, are part of Israel's arsenal intended to suppress and expel the Palestinian residents,” said Yonatan Kanoitz, head of Yesh Din’s research department.

“The cooperation between the settlers and the army creates a vicious cycle: settlers with the help of soldiers forcibly remove Palestinians from their private land; later settlers build on it; and then the army issues an order to ban Palestinians from entering the area.”

After being turned away from the border, both Ms Nevens and Ms Bird posted on social media that the group had planned to support the harvesters.

Ms Nevens said: “We had travelled to the West Bank to stand in solidarity with Palestinians facing escalating violence, and to join the olive harvesters.

“We went to bear witness, to listen to Palestinian communities and to help shine a light on the abuses taking place.

“The Israeli forces didn’t want us to see the demolished homes, or the settlers inflicting so much misery or the day to day indignities that are so central to the occupation,” she added.

Ms Bird added: “I came to stand alongside Palestinians being attacked for harvesting olives on their own land. Instead, we were turned away, with no reason given.

“Increased blockades, demolitions and unthinkable violence have become the norm for Palestinians in the West Bank.

“It is more important now than ever that we bear witness and show our solidarity.”

According to the United Nations, 98 Palestinians have been killed in the West Bank in 2026, while 2,603 have been injured.

By comparison, six Israelis, including four soldiers, have been killed in the territory this year, while 32 have been injured.
