Two sites have been identified for further investigation after a survey by a specialist boat during searches for a missing fishing vessel off the Isle of Lewis, police have said.

The survey was carried out by the Northern Lighthouse Board vessel NLV Pharos over the course of the past weekend east of the Butt of Lewis.

Further work will now be carried out to further assess these sites as part of the investigation by the Marine Accident Investigation Branch (MAIB), police said.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21660835.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The MAIB has put in place an exclusion zone excluding anchoring in the vicinity unless in an emergency, fishing by nets, trawls or lines in the vicinity, and any unsanctioned diving operations to allow the two sites to be investigated. *(Image: Police Scotland)*

The MAIB has put in place an exclusion zone excluding anchoring in the vicinity unless in an emergency, fishing by nets, trawls or lines in the vicinity, and any unsanctioned diving operations to allow the two sites to be investigated.

The activity is part of the ongoing search for the Kilda, which was last known to have been operating close to the Port of Ness in the north of Lewis on Tuesday, October 13.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21581373.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The fishing boat went missing off the coast near Port of Ness on the Isle of Lewis (Alamy/PA)

Norman Murray, 40, who is known as Tam, and Murdo Morrison, 23, from the boat’s crew remain unaccounted for and are being treated as missing people.

The body of their crewmate Douglas Craigie was recovered in the area around 2.45am on Wednesday, October 14.

Inspector Neil Taylor said: "Our thoughts remain with everyone affected by this tragic incident.

“It is imperative we get answers for everyone involved and please be assured our efforts are continuing. We are grateful for the specialist work of NLV Pharos over the past weekend to progress the search and investigation.
