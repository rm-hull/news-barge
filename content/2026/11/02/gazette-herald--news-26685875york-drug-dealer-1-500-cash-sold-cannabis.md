A drug dealer caught with more than £1,500 in cash claimed she sold cannabis to pay another person's debts.

Deborah Paxton, 51, denied selling the Class B drug to users until the day of her trial at York Magistrates' Court when she changed her plea.

She had been arrested after police raided her home in Clifton and found mobile phones and £1,521 in cash.

When police inspected the phones they found evidence of her involvement in drug supply, the court heard.

Paxton, of Spalding Avenue, Clifton, admitted being concerned in the supply of cannabis between September 2024 and December 2024 and was given a 12-month prison sentence, the maximum possible at a magistrates’ court, suspended for 12 months on condition she does 12 months’ probation supervision and does not commit offences.

Andrew Craven, for Paxton, said she knew a man who used several drugs and had run up drug debts. Mr Craven said the people owed the money were demanding payment.

So the man suggested to Paxton, that since they both knew cannabis users, they sell cannabis to pay off his debts or sold cannabis on behalf of those who supplied him and pay off the debt that way.

When Paxton had been charged, she had denied the charge because the man was more involved than she was.

“He has not been the gentleman and not made a statement to police as he has promised this lady (he would do) throughout these proceedings,” said Mr Craven.

Deputy district judge Nick Hayles said: “It was a conspiracy between the two of them. She has taken the blame because he is more vulnerable.”

Mr Craven said that the man had mental health problems and had never worked.

Paxton had not worked for 20 years and said through her solicitor she had various medical problems.

The judge noted she did not produce any evidence of her medical issues.
