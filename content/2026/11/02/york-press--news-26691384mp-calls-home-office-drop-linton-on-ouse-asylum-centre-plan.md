## Kevin Hollinrake MP calls on Home Office to scrap plans for asylum centre near York

The Home Office said accommodation at the former military sites is designed to be self-contained to reduce impact on the local community. *(Image: Owen Humphreys/Press Association)*

A former MP for the village near York at the centre of a controversial decision to open an asylum centre in a former RAF base said "this is the wrong plan in the wrong place" and called on border security and asylum minister Anna Turley MP to meet residents "face to face".

Kevin Hollinrake, Conservative MP for Thirsk and Malton, said he visited Linton-on-Ouse on Friday, October 30 in support of residents opposing Home Office plans to accommodate single male asylum seekers in the former base and that he fought proposals in 2022 "tooth and nail".

A spokesperson for the MP said campaigning residents have gathered evidence since then which showed local infrastructure "cannot sustain a facility of this size", and Mr Hollinrake also called on the Home Office to drop the plan.

The Home Office said accommodation at the former military sites, including those earmarked for MOD facilities in Bicester and Barnham, is designed to be self-contained to reduce impact on the local community.

The chairman of the Conservative party was joined in Linton-on-Ouse by the local MP Sir Alec Shelbrooke, shadow home secretary Chris Philp MP and Joy Morrissey MP, shadow minister for women and equalities.

Mr Hollinrake's spokesperson said the MP is asking the Home Office to publish its data for feasibility studies behind the plans.

Kevin Hollinrake said: "“I have seen first-hand what a proposal like this does to a small village.

"People in Linton-on-Ouse are worried sick, and the fear and uncertainty are profound.

"The damage is already being done - homes standing empty, families choosing not to move here and the village school feeling the effects, all before a single decision has been made.

“When plans for an asylum centre at Linton-on-Ouse were first put forward in 2022, I fought them tooth and nail alongside residents, and together we saw them off.

"I will do everything I can to help this community beat them again.

“This is the wrong plan in the wrong place.

“The Prime Minister says these concerns are understandable.

"Then his government should act on them.

"The Home Office must stop hiding behind civil servants and come to Linton-on-Ouse to listen to residents and scrap this plan.”

![Thirsk and Malton MP Kevin Hollinrake](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19503100.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kevin Hollinrake MP said he visited Linton-on-Ouse on Friday, October 30 in support of residents opposing Home Office plans to accommodate single male asylum seekers in the former base *(Image: Pic supplied)*

In an interview with BBC Yorkshire political editor Gemma Dillon, which aired on Sunday, September 27, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he "understood" concerns that the village population could potentially double under the proposal.

Sir Alec Shelbrooke MP said: "Our shadow team have seen first-hand today – and, quite frankly, been shocked – at the proximity that 1,500 single male illegal immigrants will be to the village of Linton-on-Ouse, which has forever been integrated with the original base since it opened in 1937.

"Despite all of my communications with the Home Office being ignored to date, today's visit has proved that Labour ministers can no longer ignore us and must come and see the issues for themselves."

A Home Office spokesperson said: "The Government is closing every asylum hotel, saving taxpayers over £1bn on asylum support since the election.

"We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, moving asylum seekers into alternative accommodation, including former military sites which are designed to be self-contained to reduce impact on the local community.

"As part of the process, we engage with local authorities and police forces to ensure the safety and security of those in the surrounding area."
