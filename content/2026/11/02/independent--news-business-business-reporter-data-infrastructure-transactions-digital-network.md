*Ark Data Centres is a Business Reporter client*

Much of modern life depends on an economy that is underpinned by interconnected advanced technologies, from optical fibre networks to sophisticated digital applications. And data centres are at the centre of this, the platform from which everything that constitutes the digital economy delivers.

Over time and with an increase in demand and scale, the inefficiencies of operating in-house facilities have prompted service providers and data users to turn to specialist providers, giving rise to the modern data centre operator.

Despite their fundamental role in this complex ecosystem, these infrastructures largely operate out of sight. Everyday activities – from online transactions to social media, public services to telecommunications – rely on them, yet their significance is either taken for granted or not fully understood. Yet as digital activity, global communications and technological innovation continue to expand, their importance has never been greater.

Ark Data Centres has been at the forefront of this evolution since its founding in 2005. From its beginnings on a former Ministry of Defence site in Wiltshire, the company is now actively developing seven sites across the south of England and expanding into Europe, with sites in Brussels and Barcelona.

A key driver of Ark’s success is its commitment to energy efficiency, developed in partnership with BladeRoom Group, UK specialists in critical environment air quality management and cooling. Given that power is the primary cost of running digital infrastructure, efficient energy use not only reduces operating costs but also lowers carbon emissions. Ark’s cooling systems further minimise water consumption, distinguishing its approach from more resource-intensive models seen elsewhere.

Ark has grown in line with increasing demand for data and transactional driven requirements, and has demonstrated its capability to support high-performance computing and AI workloads. This has been achieved on behalf of a wide range of customers including enterprise, service provider and hyperscale cloud companies. Through continuous innovation and close alignment with leading technology manufacturers, the company is also well positioned to meet the increasing demands of AI at scale.

As data centres grow in importance, security remains paramount. Data – often described as the “crown jewels” of the modern economy – requires rigorous physical and digital protection, supported by robust access controls and resilient site infrastructure.

The sector’s designation as critical national infrastructure reflects this significance. Key Ark sites have held this status since 2015 through Crown Hosting, Ark’s joint venture with the Cabinet Office, supporting the UK public sector and aligning with national data sovereignty priorities.

Ark is proud of its unique position and its longstanding commitment to a predominantly UK-based supply chain. The company continues to attract significant investment as it expands the capacity needed to support the UK’s digital future – enabling today’s economy while anticipating the demands of tomorrow.
