Residents opposed to proposed housing on green belt land have launched a door-to-door leaflet campaign ahead of a key Local Plan consultation deadline.

Campaigners say hundreds of households across the town are receiving flyers highlighting concerns about plans for at least 256 homes on fields north of Skipton Road in Ilkley, identified as site H1547 in Bradford Council's draft Local Plan.

The move comes as the consultation period enters its final weeks, with responses due by 5pm on Monday, November 30.

According to residents, more than 200 letters have already been drafted through a dedicated campaign website since it went live on October 22.

The leaflet highlights several issues raised in Bradford Council's own assessment of the site, including the potential impact on the historic Hollin Hall farmhouse, flood risk concerns, and proposed new road access points onto Skipton Road.

Andy Willers, a west Ilkley resident involved in the campaign, said: "Hadfield Farm was added to the plan at this stage, so nobody in Ilkley has had an earlier chance to comment on it.

"The council's own documents find unacceptable harm to Hollin Hall farmhouse, flood modelling still to be done, and three new junctions onto Skipton Road, which is Ilkley's only road west.

"We're not asking anyone to take our word for it. We want people to read what the council has published and respond in their own words before 5pm on 30 November."

Mr Willers added: "The site was not in the 2021 draft, so this soundness-only consultation is the first and last chance residents have to comment on it.

"The council's own site entry is frank. It says building near Hollin Hall farmhouse is likely to cause harm 'of an unacceptable level', notes river flood risk on part of the site, and recommends three new access points onto the A65 without saying where.

"The district needs an adopted plan, and Bradford has a large target to meet. But Ilkley's new sites have grown from 280 homes in the 2021 draft to 593, with Hadfield Farm the largest, and the evidence for this one site raises more questions than it answers."

The residents are encouraging people to submit responses directly to Bradford Council before the consultation closes by emailing: [planning.policy@bradford.gov.uk](mailto:planning.policy@bradford.gov.uk)

"Whatever your view, please respond before 5pm on Monday 30 November, and include your name and address or it will not count," Mr Willers said.

Neighbours have also created the website hadfieldfarm.org.uk, which they say brings together council documents and guidance on how to submit a response.
