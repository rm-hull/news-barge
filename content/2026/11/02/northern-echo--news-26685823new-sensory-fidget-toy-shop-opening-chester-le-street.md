A County Durham mum has opened a new sensory toy shop after spending hours travelling around the North East and queuing for the latest fidget toys for her children.

Haley Woodhead, 44, opened Fidget Fusion in Chester-le-Street on Saturday( October 31), after turning her family's struggles into a business designed to make shopping more accessible for children with additional needs.

The shop, at 5a Turn Park, Station Road, will stock a range of popular fidget and sensory products, including the hugely sought-after NeeDoh toys.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653545.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Haley and her son George *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

But Haley says the business will offer more than just toys, with plans for a dedicated sensory room where children can escape busy surroundings if they become overwhelmed.

The idea came after Haley, her 10-year-old son George and 12-year-old niece Evie repeatedly travelled across the North East and queued for hours to get their hands on popular fidget products.

Haley said: "Me and my sister were having to literally drive the whole of the North East and stand in queues that were hours long for things like fidget toys and anything that's a sensory product related."

Her son George is currently undergoing the process of potentially being diagnosed with autism and ADHD, while niece Evie is diagnosed autistic and has ADHD.

She said the experience of struggling with long queues and busy shops, as well as being very familiar with children with more of a need for sensory and regulating toys, prompted her to take matters into her own hands.

Haley said: "I just thought, hold on a minute, why are we waiting for other people to do it? Why can't I just do it myself?"

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653544.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside the new Fidget Fusion in Chester-le-Street *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The shop is set to feature a specially designed sensory room with calming colours, sensory lights and soft furnishings, giving children somewhere to take a break if they become overwhelmed.

Haley is also exploring dedicated shopping evenings where families can book private time slots to browse without the pressure of busy crowds.

She said: "We understand first hand how some children need quieter shops so we want to offer everything we possibly can so that everyone can have the best experience with us."

The business will also aim to offer flexible opening hours to accommodate families who may struggle to visit during traditional shopping times.

Haley has reduced her hours in her existing job to two days a week to concentrate on getting Fidget Fusion off the ground.

On the opening she said: "I am so excited, obviously a bit nervous as well, but more excited. I just want to be able something special to help all of these children, and the parents too as we know first hand how hard it can be."

Fidget Fusion opened for the first time at 10am on Saturday, with a limited supply of NeeDoh products available to buy.

The shop has confirmed it is an official NeeDoh stockist, with purchases restricted to one item per customer and in-store sales only.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21653541.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Haley and her son George *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The day before the official opening, Haley also made a special gesture for a young girl with an incurable cancer diagnosis.

After spotting a comment on Fidget Fusion's TikTok page asking where the shop was based, Haley discovered it had been left by someone running a page called Evie's cancer journey.

Having discovered Evie loved fidget toys, Haley contacted her mum and arranged for her to become the shop's first customer on Friday, ahead of the official opening.

Haley said: "We opened the shop just for her so it's safe as she is unwell and we just did it because we wanted to help a little family in need who are going through something absolutely horrendous."

The shop will initially open from 10am until 5pm on Saturday, with further opening hours to be confirmed.

Haley hopes to build a welcoming space for families across the region, with updates available through Fidget Fusion's social media pages.
