August saw a rise in reports of anti-social behaviour in York, new figures show.

There was a a 27.5% increase in ASB between August 2025 (218 cases) and this year (278 cases) in the city.

Hotspots for trouble included Blake Street, Bridge Street, Exhibition Square and Gillygate.

A number of people took to social media during August to report anti-social behaviour in York.

One resident described groups of young people “stealing handfuls of stuff then leaving” from a branch of Greggs.

Another described how a gang including girls and boys approached her son and his friend and took the friend’s bike. “The bike was brought back but my son’s phone was then taken”.

Two boys aged 12-14 were spotted “dangerously cycling, cutting off cars and verbally harassing/swearing at drivers, and at cyclists in the cycle paths” near Clifton Backies.

The model houses on the Foss Fairy Trail were thrown in the river during August, and “two teenage boys shouting and swearing at children” were reported in Rowntree Park.

## July figures ‘significantly down’

Inspector Tom Watson, the Neighbourhood Policing Inspector for York Inner, said: “Having reviewed the anti-social behaviour data, there was an increase in reported incidents during August 2026 compared to the same period in 2025.

“However, it is important to note that ASB levels during July 2026 were significantly lower than those recorded in the previous year.

“The increase in August is likely to reflect the additional footfall within the city centre and the higher number of young people attending the area during the school summer holidays.

“Anti-social behaviour reporting naturally fluctuates throughout the year, and it is also worth noting that similar increases have been observed in other parts of York and are not exclusive to the city centre.

“We continue to work closely with partner agencies, including City of York Council, York BID, retailers, and local businesses, to address anti-social behaviour through a combination of proactive enforcement and preventative activity.

“This includes targeted patrols in identified hotspot locations, early intervention work with young people, home visits to those involved in repeat incidents, and educational inputs delivered within schools.

“Our collective approach is focused not only on addressing incidents when they occur, but also on tackling the underlying causes of anti-social behaviour and preventing issues from escalating in the future.”
