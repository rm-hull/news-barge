MORE than 80 “remarkable” young people and adults have been celebrated at a North East education trust’s inaugural awards.

Bishop Hogarth Catholic Education Trust staged its first ‘Virtues Awards’ at Ramside Hall Hotel, uniting pupils, families, staff, governors, clergy and community leaders from across its 35 schools.

Almost 500 nominations were received for the awards, recognising the trust’s eight core virtues: compassion, justice, confidence, resilience, honesty, respect, responsibility and self-belief.

The trust’s Chief Executive, Stuart McGhee, paid tribute to the “remarkable” finalists, saying their actions had made a difference to the lives of others.

The ceremony recognised young people who have supported classmates through difficult times, stood up for what is right, overcome personal challenges, and helped others to believe in themselves.

Staff members, volunteers, and community figures were also honoured for making a positive impact within their schools and local communities.

Awards were presented in each virtue category for young people and adults, with shortlisted nominees receiving a commemorative certificate and Virtues Awards pin badge, before the category winners were announced.

One of the most moving moments of the evening came during the Resilience Award presentation, when a special award was presented to James and Imogen Smith, of St Bede’s Catholic Primary School in Darlington, whose story of strength and perseverance following a family bereavement inspired a standing ovation.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21647147.jpg?type=mds-article-620)James and Imogen Smith at the awards ceremony *(Image: Bishop Hogarth Catholic Education Trust)*

Sporting excellence was celebrated with a Special Achievement Award being presented to Sacred Heart Catholic Primary School, in Hartlepool, and St Augustine’s Catholic Primary School, in Darlington.

Both schools reached the National League Trust finals at Wembley Stadium after progressing through a nationwide football competition involving more than 900 schools. Sacred Heart were crowned national champions, with St Augustine’s finishing proud runners-up.

Music is a key strength of the trust, with guests enjoying performances by St Bede’s Catholic Primary School Choir – recently named North East Primary Choir of the Year – and Carmel College Choir, which holds the title of North East Secondary Choir of the Year.

There was also a special performance of *Today*, the trust’s song, celebrating community and shared identity, and performed by a choir comprising pupils from across the network of schools.

Deputy Chief Executive, Donna Hubbard-Young, said: “Our first Virtues Awards evening was a special celebration of the people who make our schools and communities such wonderful places to learn, work, and belong.

“We were overwhelmed by the number and quality of nominations received. Every nomination told a powerful story and highlighted the kindness, courage and dedication that can be found in all corners of our school community.

“The evening gave us the opportunity to recognise those who consistently put others first, overcome challenges with resilience, stand up for what is right, and make a positive difference to the lives of those around them. We are incredibly proud of every nominee and award-winner.”
