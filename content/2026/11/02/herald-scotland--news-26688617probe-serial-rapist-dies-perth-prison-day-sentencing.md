The Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) has lodged a first notice to begin the court process for a fatal accident inquiry (FAI) into the death of a serial rapist in prison.

John Ferrier, a prisoner within HMP Perth, died on November 7, 2023, a day before he was due to be sentenced after being convicted of seven rapes.

The former hotel worker committed the crimes over a six-year period between 2009 and 2015 across locations in Fife.

He was convicted at the High Court in Edinburgh.

Mr Ferrier was in legal custody and in the care of the state at the time of his death.

COPFS is obliged to investigate such deaths, and a FAI is mandatory.

A preliminary hearing will be held on December 10, 2026, at Perth Sheriff Court.

The purpose of a FAI includes determining the cause of death; the circumstances in which the death occurred; and to establish what steps, if any, could have been taken to minimise the risk of future deaths in similar circumstances.

Unlike criminal proceedings, FAIs are inquisitorial in nature, and are used to establish facts rather than to apportion blame.

Procurator Fiscal Andy Shanks for the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service, Scotland’s independent deaths investigation authority, said: “The Lord Advocate considers that the death of John Ferrier occurred whilst he was in legal custody and as such a Fatal Accident Inquiry is mandatory.

“The lodging of the First Notice enables FAI proceedings to commence under the direction of the Sheriff.”
