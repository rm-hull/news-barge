Are we entering the era of TV “dupes”? Because to the untrained eye, or even a reasonably trained one, it’s hard to distinguish TCL’s mini-LED-powered QM7L from a lower-end OLED. So when your brother-in-law plops down in front of your new TV to watch the Lions and says, “Wow, you splurged, eh?” you can just nod and give a wink. Dupe accomplished.

I know this because the QM7L arrived close on the heels of a new budget OLED, the Roku Pro Series, so I was able to compare the two side by side. Results: surprising. Although it has a potentially problematic design element and an AI that’s not exactly Claude-level sophisticated, the QM7L might just be the best midrange TV you can buy right now.

## Simple Setup, But Where Does the Soundbar Go?

Available in sizes ranging from 55 inches (which I tested) to a mammoth 98 inches, the QM7L boasts a virtually borderless screen that stands on a pedestal instead of the usual feet. That’s a win if you own a small piece of furniture that might not accommodate the width of such feet, and I’d argue it’s more aesthetically pleasing too.

However, it does pose a problem: Because the pedestal base sticks out in front by a little over 4 inches, it doesn’t allow a soundbar to sit snug up to the screen. You could balance it on top of the base, but that looks awkward. And because it slopes down, the soundbar doesn’t even sit level.

What’s more, although the base has two height options, the taller one leaves the bottom of the TV just 2.25 inches off your TV stand. That may be enough clearance for ultra-compact soundbars like the Bose TV Speaker, but something like the Sonos Arc, which measures 3 inches high, ends up blocking a sliver of the screen.
