A mother-to-be has been spared a prison sentence for an “evil” and “barbaric” attack on an unconscious man because York’s top judge didn’t want her baby to be born in jail.

Christian Watson, 24, was given four years and eight months in jail for the attack that left the victim with a bleed on the brain, suspected fractured breastbone, broken nose and other injuries.

His accomplice, Fallon Johnston, 20, who York Crown Court heard started the attack during which she and Watson, 24, repeatedly kicked the victim as he lay unconscious on the ground, kept her freedom when she was given a three-year community order with a mental health requirement.

Catherine Duffy, prosecuting, said at the end of the attack, the pair walked away hugging and kissing each other before Watson returned to punch the victim so hard he himself fell over.

Then they walked away again, leaving the victim unconscious on the pavement late at night.

Johnston pleaded guilty 12 months ago to causing grievous bodily harm with intent to cause grievous bodily harm and was eight months pregnant when she attended York Crown Court for sentence.

Watson pleaded guilty to the same offence.

Sentencing Watson, the Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, said the attack could have resulted in murder.

“This was barbaric. It is a miracle that the injuries were not worse,” he said.

He told Johnston, formerly of Bradford Road, Brighouse and now of Blackpool: “What you did was evil. You acted like a barbarian.”

But it was not fair on her baby for him to be born in prison.

“That is what saves you from going to prison, together with (the offence) was out of character, your genuine remorse, guilty plea and you are bitterly ashamed of what you did,” he said.

He said sending a pregnant woman to prison “must be a sentence of utterly last resort.”

Ms Duffy said Johnston and Watson were arguing in Scarborough town centre very early on July 3, 2025, and at one point Fallon appeared to take a ring off her finger and throw it away.

Then they encountered the victim at 1.20am and all three talked for some minutes.

All three had been drinking.

The defendants got up and Johnston kicked the victim in the body or face and continued to kick him as he lay on the ground.

In the attack that followed, Watson kicked the victim eight times, including in the face, made a running “soccer kick” several times, stamped on him and got onto a low wall before jumping onto the victim, said Ms Duffy.

Since leaving hospital, the victim had left the country and the long term consequences of his injuries were unknown.

Sentencing Watson, of Abbeydale Grove, Hawksworth, Leeds, the judge said he had intended to cause worse injuries than he actually caused.

For Watson, Jeremy Barton said he had long-standing mental health problems, which were out of control when he carried out the attack and which had been exacerbated by his use of illegal drugs.

Being in prison had improved his health through medication and drug abstinence.

He had written a letter apologising to the victim and saying he was “utterly sorry” for his actions.

Rachel Webster, for Johnston, said the NHS regarded all prison pregnancies as “high risk.”

Jailing Johnston could lead to a “profound emotional” impact on her.

She had several mental health issues and had suffered abuse as a child.

She was now with a new partner.

The judge told Johnston that Watson was the “real villain” in the case because without him plying her with drink and drugs she would never have committed the offence.
