# UK Met Office snow latest as forecast changed for next week

## The Met Office has confirmed the risk of snow on Sunday is increasing

The likelihood of snowfall this Sunday is on the rise, the Met Office has confirmed – with further wintry conditions potentially heading our way into next week.

Weather forecasters had already indicated that wintry showers and snow could hit parts of the UK as early as today, with Met Office weather maps also pointing towards Sunday snowfall, reports The Express.

Now the Met Office has advised UK households that the snow risk this Sunday (November 15) continues to grow, as part of its latest forecast released on Friday. The national weather service stated that the UK forecast 'remains in a colder phase', with temperatures potentially plummeting to -6C in the coming days alongside the prospect of snow.

Met Office Chief Forecaster Chris Bulmer warned: "The UK forecast has moved to a colder phase with the daytime temperatures for most locations likely to stay in single figures. Overnight temperatures on Friday night will dip again to -4 or even -6C in some locations, meaning another significant ground frost for many locations."

Deputy Chief Forecaster Steven Keates added: "The outlook is changeable with spells of rain, sleet and some hill snow." He continued: "On Sunday, low pressure will bring rain or showers to many areas. Precipitation will be most persistent across northern Scotland, where there is an increasing chance of snow developing later in the day. This will mainly fall over high ground, where accumulations are likely, but some snow could fall to lower levels too. Ice may also become a hazard."

The forecaster adds that next week, unsettled and cold conditions remain with "rain, sleet and (mainly hill) snow key features." Weather specialists explained on Thursday that snowfall occurs when cold air collides with moisture.

They elaborated: "A northerly airflow can bring colder Arctic air towards the UK, while easterly winds can draw cold continental air from Europe. If that cold air encounters moisture, either from a weather front or showers developing over the sea, precipitation has a greater chance of falling as snow."

This follows predictions suggesting that a weakening polar vortex could deliver snow and freezing conditions to the UK later this month. The current spell of cold weather is unconnected to the polar vortex phenomenon — a mass of Arctic air circulating around the North Pole that could potentially be driven towards the UK.

Nevertheless, Met Office forecasters have warned that an early cold snap could arrive before the end of November should stratospheric warming continue to destabilise the vortex, pushing frigid air across the country.
