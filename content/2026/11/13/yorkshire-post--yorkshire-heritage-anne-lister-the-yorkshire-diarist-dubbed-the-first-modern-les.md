# Anne Lister: The Yorkshire diarist dubbed ‘first modern lesbian’ who inspired TV series Gentleman Jack soon to be introduced to Netflix

## Anne Lister was a diarist and landowner who lived at Shibden and known for her candid accounts of her relationships and inspired Sally Wainwright’s TV show Gentleman Jack.

Anne Lister had several lesbian love affairs from her schooldays onwards, often during her travelling periods.

She was known for her muscular and androgynous appearance; she always dressed in black and was later known - generally unkindly - as “Gentleman Jack”.

Anne took part in many activities that were not generally perceived as the norm for women at the time, including opening and owning a colliery.

![Anne Lister](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjFiZGNkZjdmLTI1MWYtNDRhNy1hMTBiLTA5YjYzNmZkZDI4OToxMDc4NjI1ZS1iNzA5LTRlODQtOWExMC1hMzlhYmFjMDAyNjI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Yorkshire TV writer Sally Wainwright created and wrote the 2019 BBC-HBO historical TV drama series Gentleman Jack, starring Suranne Jones as Anne.

It depicts her life as ‘the first modern lesbian’ and the series ran for two seasons. It has been announced that the series will be introduced to Netflix.

## **Early life of Anne Lister**

Anne was born in Halifax on April 3, 1791, to Captain Jeremy Lister who, as a young man, served with the British 10th Regiment of Foot in the Battles of Lexington and Concord in the American War of Independence, and Rebecca Lister nee Battle.

She was the second child and eldest daughter and the family moved in 1793 to an estate named Skelfer House at Market Weighton, which is where Anne spent her earliest childhood.

Jeremy and Rebecca had four sons and three daughters, but it was only Anne and her younger sister Marian who survived beyond the age of 20.

When she was seven years old, Anne was sent to a school in Agnesgate, Ripon, run by a Mrs Hagues and a Mrs Chettle.

She was homeschooled between the years of 1801 and 1804 by the Reverend George Skelding.

![Shibden Hall estate.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjIxMWFiM2QwLWI5NTUtNDU5NS05YTIxLWZhMTkxOTVmMTVjMzpjNmM3MGEzMy0yMGQyLTRlYjEtYmQ2MS1lMDlhOGU3YzkzNGM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Anne regularly visited her aunt Anne and Uncle James at Shibden Hall, where she was taught by Mrs Mellin.

During her schooling at home, she developed an interest in classical literature, which inspired her writing.

In 1805, Anne was sent to the Manor House School in York, where she met her first love, Eliza Raine, who was the illegitimate half-Indian daughter of an East India Company surgeon in Madras, brought to Yorkshire after his death and set to inherit a significant amount of money.

They both shared a bedroom at the boarding school and Eliza later became a patient at Clifton House Asylum in 1814.

She was later transferred to Terrace House in Osbaldwick and died there on December 31, 1860 and is buried in the Osbaldwick churchyard across from the house.

## **Anne Lister’s inheritance of Shibden Hall**

Shibden estate was passed onto her following her uncle’s death in 1826 but she only controlled part of its income until 10 years later when her father and aunt died and their shares were passed to her.

As well as her earnings from the agricultural tenancy, Anne’s financial portfolio included properties in town, shares in the canal and railway industries, mining, and stone quarries.

Her wealth allowed her some freedom to live as she wanted, and she used the income from her varied portfolio to finance her two passions: the renovation of Shibden Hall, and travelling around Europe.

## **Anne Lister’s diaries**

Her writing began in 1806 as scraps of paper, recording in secret code parcels sent to and from Eliza Raine, and later became the 26 quarto volumes, ending at her death in 1840.

While her handwriting was difficult to read in itself, around 16.67 per cent of the diary is encrypted in a simple code that she and Eliza had invented.

![Helena Whitbread - author, historian and the editor/decoder of the diaries of Anne Lister.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBlNjI1MzU2LWIzNDAtNGE3NC1iMDRhLWEyMjhkOGIzMDNlMjozNTdjMjU5Yi1hZDYyLTQ4OGItODE0YS1jMjY5MjcxYTNiZDI=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It merged the Greek alphabet, zodiac, punctuation, and mathematical symbols, and it describes in great detail her lesbian identity and affairs, as well as the methods she used for seduction.

The diaries also include her thoughts on the weather, social events, national events, and her business interests.

Most of her entries deal with her daily life, and not merely her sexuality, and provide detailed information on social, political, and economic events of the time.

The cypher used in her diaries was decoded by the last inhabitant of Shibden Hall, John Lister and a friend of his, Arthur Burrell. John’s father was the descendant of a brother of Anne Lister’s paternal grandfather.

Even though his friend Arthur advised John to burn all the diaries, he did not take this advice and instead continued to hide them behind a panel at Shibden Hall.

In 2011, Anne’s diaries were added to the register of the UNESCO Memory of the World Programme and the citation notes that, while a valuable account of the times, it was the “comprehensive and painfully honest account of lesbian life and reflections on her nature, however, which have made these diaries unique. They have shaped and continue to shape the direction of UK Gender Studies and Women’s History.”

## **Anne’s relationship with Ann Walker**

Anne and Ann both met in the 1820s on various occasions but it wasn’t until a decade later that they embarked on a relationship.

Ann Walker became a wealthy heiress in 1832 and after they took communion together on Easter Sunday (March 30, 1834) in Holy Trinity Church, Goodramgate, York, they considered themselves married, but without legal recognition.

The church has been described as “an icon for what is interpreted as the site of the first lesbian marriage to be held in Britain”, and the building now hosts a commemorative blue plaque.

The couple lived together at Shibden Hall until Anne’s death in 1840.

Anne renovated Shibden Hall extensively to her own design. In 1838, she added a Gothic tower to the main house, to serve as her private library.

She also had a tunnel dug under the building so that the staff could move about without disturbing her.

She had a strong Anglican faith and identified politically as a Tory, “interested in defending the privileges of the land-owning artistocacy.”
