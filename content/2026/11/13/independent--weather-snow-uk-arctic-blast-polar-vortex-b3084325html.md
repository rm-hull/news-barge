Freezing Arctic air is sweeping across the UK bringing widespread low temperatures, harsh frosts and a chance of snow.

Another widespread crisp early frost is expected on Saturday morning as the cold snap brings temperatures down to -4C in parts of the south of England and Wales.

Most of England should be dry, sunny and frosty on Saturday, but conditions are forecast to turn cloudier, milder, and unsettled throughout the weekend.

Rain will become increasingly widespread across northern and central Scotland, with further showers affecting northern England and north Wales.

Sunday morning is expected to see warming weather, with temperatures reaching double figures (11C) by the afternoon.

![Freezing temperatures are forecast across central parts of England and Wales on Saturday morning](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/8/51/Screenshot-2026-11-13-at-08-15-23.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

More cold weather could also hit on Friday night ahead of the weekend as the Met Office has forecast lows of -6C in rural parts of southern England.

The Met Office has warned parts of northern Scotland could see snow on Saturday night into Sunday as rain is forecast to meet colder air.

The forecaster has said some snow could temporarily reach lower levels, but the most significant flurries are expected over higher ground. Around 2 cm is possible above 200 metres, with several centimetres over higher routes and potentially 5-10 cm over the tops of the hills.

On Sunday snow is expected to ease, as rain showers spread into western areas and Northern Ireland. England and Wales should remain largely dry, although increasingly cloudy.

![Snow could fall in some pats of Scotland](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/01/06/14/10/Winter-weather-Jan-6th-2026-mhq2kmmn.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It comes as a yellow cold health alert has been issued by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) for the North West and West Midlands. The alert will be in place from 9am on Thursday until 12pm on Saturday.

Health leaders have encouraged people to check on their loved ones and neighbours, saying low temperatures can lead to a spike in the use of health and care services.

Experts added that colder weather can also result in increased risk for over-65s and people with certain health conditions, such as heart and lung problems.

![A yellow cold health alert has been issued by the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA) for the North West and West Midlands](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/8/14/Screenshot-2026-11-11-at-08-19-45.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Dr Anya Gopfert, consultant in public health at UKHSA, said: “This is the first cold-health alert of the winter, but we can expect more as we move further into the season.

“Cold weather can have a serious impact on the health of some people, including those aged 65 and over and those with pre-existing health conditions, so it is vital to check in on friends, family and neighbours who are most vulnerable.

“We’re also reminding people to take simple steps to stay warm and look after their health as temperatures begin to fall. Keeping homes heated where possible and dressing warmly can help people stay safe and well during colder weather.”
