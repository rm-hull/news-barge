**Friday's top story:Mercedes halts all new investments in German plants**

Mercedes-Benz has frozen all new investments across its German sites and warned of potential factory closures amid high domestic operating costs and deadlocked union negotiations.

In an internal workforce interview released on Thursday, production chief Michael Schiebe confirmed the executive board’s decision, stating the carmaker must now evaluate which domestic plants could face closure without structural cost reductions.

Human resources director Britta Seeger described the closure risk as a concrete option after intensive talks with employee representatives failed to yield a breakthrough.

The standoff centres on a management "productivity offensive" demanding longer weekly working hours without pay increases – a stark clash with the IG Metall union’s push for a five per cent wage rise.

Chief executive Ola Källenius defended the freeze, noting that foreign locations offer lower expenses and longer annual working hours, while urging European policymakers to address rising energy and regulatory burdens.

Facing weak demand in China, tariffs, and intense competition from cheaper Chinese electric vehicles, the Stuttgart-based manufacturer faces mounting financial pressure.

**Germany’s climate standing sinks to lowest level since 2019**

Germany has dropped in global climate protection rankings for the third consecutive year, falling to 25th place in its worst performance since 2019.

Compiled by Germanwatch and the NewClimate Institute, the annual Climate Change Performance Index (CCPI) evaluates 64 countries and the European Union. Germany received a "medium" overall rating, trailing nations such as Brazil, Colombia, and Romania.

Lead author Jan Burck blamed Germany’s poorest-ever score in national climate policy on Economy Minister Katherina Reiche’s focus on expanding natural gas, warning that such measures threaten renewables and electrification.

Across the wider G20, most major economies performed poorly, with only the United Kingdom achieving a "high" rating in seventh place. The United States, Russia, Saudi Arabia, and world-leading emitter China – despite leading in renewable expansion – all received "very low" overall scores.

With the top three index spots left vacant because no country is meeting global climate targets, Denmark led all assessed nations in fourth place.

**Merz and Macron play down push to name Europe's Russia-Ukraine envoy**

French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Thursday appeared to dismiss the urgency to name a representative for Europe in talks between Ukraine and Russia after calls from Washington and Kyiv.

"Above all, what is needed is for Europe to have a clear and united vision of what it wants for Ukraine and for itself. I believe that the content determines the format," Macron said at a meeting of European leaders in Dublin.

Merz said it was first necessary to get Russian President Vladimir Putin to the negotiating table on the Ukraine war before deciding if an envoy was needed.

"It's not about individuals; what matters is that Putin is prepared to hold talks," Merz told reporters.

But the German leader welcomed Russia's "growing willingness... to acknowledge that Europe cannot be talked about without Europe. And that is good news".

Representatives from Britain, France, Germany and Italy were present on the sidelines of talks in Abu Dhabi this week -- but did not meet directly with Russia.

Merz backed continuing for now in that format -- with the addition of Poland.

**Siemens sees no AI slowdown despite Republican rout in US**

The midterm elections rout for US President Donald Trump's Republicans is unlikely to slow the AI boom, German industrial giant Siemens said Thursday as it reported record profits boosted by data centre demand.

Fears that Democrats could clamp down on data centre construction after seizing control of Congress hit stock markets worldwide last week.

Speaking on an earnings call, Siemens CEO Roland Busch said the company was banking on the boom carrying on even if the elections had shown growing opposition to data centres.

"There are discussions on the one side, on the other side there's bipartisan acknowledgment that a big driver of economic growth in the United States is data centres," Busch said.

"There's nothing that worries us, at least not for the next couple of years," he added.

US tech giants such as Microsoft and Meta have together invested hundreds of billions of dollars in AI data centres, boosting the fortunes of companies like Siemens that make much of the electrical and cooling equipment needed to operate the sites.

**Nearly a quarter of Germans favour right-wing authoritarian rule**

Satisfaction with democracy in Germany has fallen to a record low as antisemitism, nationalism, and support for authoritarian rule rise, according to a major study presented in Berlin on Wednesday.

Conducted biennially since 2002 by the University of Leipzig’s Else Frenkel-Brunswik Institute, the representative survey of 2,507 respondents found that approval of democracy as an ideal dropped below 90 percent for the first time.

Only one-third of Germans are satisfied with how democracy functions in everyday life, with discontent especially pronounced in eastern states.

Consequently, 22.3 percent of respondents – rising to 30.9 percent in the former East – support a single strong party embodying the national community. Study author Oliver Decker warned that an "authoritarian yearning" is alarmingly replacing democratic acceptance.

The research also recorded a surge in far-right attitudes and chauvinistic nationalism, closely linked to voter preference for the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD).
