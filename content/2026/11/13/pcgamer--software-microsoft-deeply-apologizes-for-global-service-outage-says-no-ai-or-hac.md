Microsoft has issued an apology for the global services outage that began on November 12, which disrupted access to Microsoft 365 products for governments, hospitals, schools, and businesses around the world. The corporation says that neither AI nor malicious actors were at fault, but that the outage occurred as a result of an underlying infrastructural issue introduced during a network upgrade.

"We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners. We know the trust they place in our services and take that responsibility extremely seriously," Microsoft said. "We are seeing broad recovery and stability across our services."

> We are deeply sorry for the disruption this incident has caused our customers and partners. We know the trust they place in our services and take that responsibility extremely seriously. We are seeing broad recovery and stability across our services. This was a technical issue not related to cyber or AI / agents. The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change. We have addressed the underlying issue and continue to restore service to remaining customers and regions. Given the scale of demand, we restored services gradually, validating stability at each stage. While this took longer, it helped ensure a stable restoration. We will provide a full post-incident report once our review is complete.November 13, 2026

While popular speculation has been eager to attribute the outage to vibe-coding—and I can't blame anyone for speculating, considering Microsoft's push for company-wide AI adoption—the corporation says the outage was "a technical issue not related to cyber or AI/agents." It's unclear whether another word was meant to follow "cyber." I'm just telling you what the company wrote.

"The incident was introduced by an issue during an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced following a recent network change," Microsoft said, casually brutalizing the syntax of causality. "We have addressed the underlying issue and continue to restore service to remaining customers and regions."

Microsoft acknowledged that the restoration of service was slower than many might have hoped, but said that it brought services back online gradually to ensure stability.

Even if AI wasn't to blame for this week's outage, it's tempting to wonder whether any of the 10,000 employees Microsoft laid off in 2023 as part of its ongoing cost-cutting strategy might have known how to avoid the costly hiccup. Or any of the 6,000 employees it laid off in May 2025. Or the 9,000 it laid off that July. Or the 4,800 it laid off a year later, including 1,600 at Xbox with another 1,600 to follow. Or the 60,000 employees it extended voluntary buyout offers to last month.

Ah, well. No way to know. Microsoft said it will provide a more detailed incident report once its internal review of the outage is complete.
