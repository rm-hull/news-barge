President Donald Trump has attempted to distance himself from the Republican Party’s disastrous performance in last week’s midterms elections, insisting his approval rating remains high and throwing the GOP candidates he endorsed under the bus.

The president was interviewed by Stephen A. Smith for his *Straight Shooter with Stephen A* podcast Thursday about his side losing control of the House of Representatives and Senate and suffering crushing defeats in typically Republican states such as Michigan, Kansas, Texas and Mississippi.

Sitting together in the White House’s Roosevelt Room, Smith got straight to the point: “What happened, Mr. President?”

Trump gave a meandering answer, pointing out that he had not been on the ballot himself, complaining about the two Dan Sullivans running in the Alaska Senate race (which has still not been called) and finally spoke on the Iran war.

“Maybe the war was not unpopular as a war but unpopular because of the fact that the gasoline prices were higher, oil prices, and I actually thought that was something that people would be able to handle pretty well,” he said.

![President Donald Trump is interviewed by Stephen A Smith about the Republican Party's crushing defeat at the 2026 midterms, for which he accepted little blame](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/16/32/Trump-Smith.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The president insisted the electorate needed to understand that paying more for gas in the short-term was worth it to ensure Iran never obtained a nuclear weapon, blaming his own candidates for failing to sell the message.

“But that doesn’t seem to be what your own party is saying,” Smith interrupted, reminding Trump of the extent of the damage by reeling off the cold, hard facts – his party lost more than 40 House seats, at least eight Senate seats and five governorships.

“You said going into the midterms, ‘Put you on the ballot,’ so how can it not be that you were on the ballot?” Smith asked.

“Well, they didn’t do it,” Trump replied. “They didn’t put me on the ballot. People didn’t believe in the candidates.”

From there, the president pointed to his electoral successes in 2024 as proof that the GOP’s candidates were at fault, not him, refusing to see the results as a damning verdict on his own record in office.

Smith challenged the president on whether the outcome was really a matter of voters not liking the conservatives up for election or because they were frustrated by the high price of groceries.

“I think it’s both,” Trump said, going on to insist the war was necessary and that he knew he would pay a price for it politically, before lamenting the impact on the stock market, which he said signaled that “people aren’t in love with the Democrats.”

“But your approval rating is lower than theirs,” Smith interjected. “Your approval rating is lower than the Democrats.”

![Trump blamed the GOP he candidates he endorsed for failing to sell his message on the Iran war](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/16/00/Donald-Trump.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=146%2C0%2C58%2C2&auto=webp)

Trump responded by citing an Elon University poll he had seen Harry Enten detail on CNN in August that had given him a 99 percent approval rating within the GOP, putting him ahead of Abraham Lincoln and Ronald Reagan at the equivalent points in their presidential careers.

The president usually disparages the network and ignored the many other polls that put his approval rating closer to 32 percent.

Returning to his earlier point, Smith persisted: “Mr President, you said to put you on the ballot, and now you’re saying they didn’t. That means they didn’t listen to President Donald Trump.”

“Well, I say that they maybe didn’t like the candidates,” the president came back with. “Did you like them?” Smith asked.

“Some of them I liked, some of them I liked less, but I thought every one of them was better than the Democrats in terms of policy,” Trump answered, going on to insist Sen. John Cornyn would have performed just as poorly in Texas as his nominee Ken Paxton, who was trashed by Democrat James Talarico.

![Smith challenged the president about his appeal to voters to 'pretend I'm on the ballot,' saying he must have been at fault for the defeat in light of the message](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/13/16/39/Stephen-A-Smith.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=22%2C4%2C203%2C0&auto=webp)

The president’s answers were not dissimilar to those he gave to Fox News on Election Night when he also blamed anyone but himself.

Trump and Smith also discussed his tariffs strategy’s impact on the economy, the media allegedly underselling his achievements, whether Black Americans had benefited from his policies and his contention that he could beat the “highly overrated” Barack Obama “easily” were they to face each other in an election.

Finally, Trump expressed sympathy for former Nvidia boss Jensen Haung, who he said was “going through hell” after he was forced to resign Thursday after a string of extramarital affairs came to light.

Reflecting on his interview subsequently with Laura Coates on CNN, Smith said Trump was taking “zero” responsibility for the midterms and did not believe he was in any way accountable for his party’s shellacking.
