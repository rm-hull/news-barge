More than £1 million has been allocated to City of York Council and North Yorkshire Council to help end rough sleeping across York and North Yorkshire.

The cabinet of the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority (YNYCA) approved the funding on 6 November.

It is provided under the Government’s Rough Sleeper Programme and aims to offer everyone sleeping rough in the region a route off the streets by Christmas.

In the first year, City of York Council will receive £484,314 and North Yorkshire Council will receive £578,361, a combined total of £1,062,675.

The councils plan to use the money for rapid support and accommodation for people sleeping rough, support for those who have been affected for the longest, and the delivery of longer-term accommodation.

York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith said: “Not everyone has a safe place to call home, so by Christmas we’ll offer everyone a route off the streets.”

Cllr Anna Perrett, Executive Member for Housing at City of York Council, said: “York has a great track record in meeting its commitment to rough sleepers and providing safe routes off the street; no one who wants a place to stay need sleep on our streets.”

She said the funding would enhance existing support and would be allocated in line with a city and regional approach. The council is working with partners in health, probation and the voluntary and community sector.

North Yorkshire Council’s leader, Cllr Carl Les, said the council is investing more than £11 million in temporary accommodation and is using funding from the second homes council tax premium to create a new supported housing scheme for people with a history of rough sleeping.

The YNYCA cabinet also approved a Homelessness and Rough Sleeping Action Plan on 6 November. It will be submitted to the Government in early December and seeks to make homelessness and rough sleeping rare, brief and non-recurring across the region.

The funding is part of a proposed three-year programme worth £4.3 million up to and including 2028/29, subject to confirmation by the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government.

The Government says rough sleeping has risen nationally in recent years. Its figures estimate that 4,793 people were sleeping rough on a single night in autumn 2025.
