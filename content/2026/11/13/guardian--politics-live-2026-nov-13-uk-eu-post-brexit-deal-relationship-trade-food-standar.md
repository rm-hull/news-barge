Piddington will bring legal challenge over asylum base plans, says parish council chairman

The government will face a legal challenge over plans to convert a Ministry of Defence site near the Oxfordshire village of Piddington into accommodation for more than 1,200 detained asylum seekers, the parish council chairman has said.

Communities secretary Angela Rayner has approved full planning permission, a decision welcomed by the Home Office, who want to end the use of asylum hotels.

Ministers have faced angry opposition from villagers over the plans, who even made a symbolic attempt to declare the village independent from the rest of the country. There is now a six-week window when the decision can face a legal challenge.

Parish council chairman Tim McNally said there would be “many actions” by villagers, and that ministers should “absolutely” be braced for a legal challenge. He told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme that the village could also apply the new Hillsborough Law.

Once passed, the law would create a legally enforceable duty of candour which compels public officials and authorities to act transparently when investigations and inquiries take place.

McNally told the programme:

Last night, Andy Burnham was on a podium talking about the violence, sexual violence towards women, and bringing it upon the media industry as their responsibility to manage.

But the reality is, is that this is exactly what the government is doing to us here.

Defence minister Luke Pollard said the government would continue to engage with the community. He told LBC:

It’s pretty obvious that there’s a strong sense of feeling from the community around Bicester, especially in Piddington. The Home Office continues to engage with those.

There’s still a few more stages to go here, so it’s not yet fully locked in and delivered, so that we can understand where the concerns are, so they can be mitigated.

UK-EU deal will lead to 'growing pressure' for parliament to scrutinise legislation, committee warns

Hello and welcome to the UK politics live blog.

There will be a “growing pressure” for parliament to scrutinise EU legislation under new deals on food and emissions to be sealed ahead of the UK’s summit with the bloc next week, a Lords’ committee has warned.

Prime minister Andy Burnham is due to meet EU leaders in Brussels next week and sign agreements that he says could raise GDP by 0.4%. This includes a sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) deal on food and farming, as well as one on emissions trading and another for a youth mobility scheme.

A deal is also thought to include a health security agreement to help address shortages of drugs such as antibiotics and ADHD medication, which have got worse since Brexit, according to a draft text seen by the I newspaper.

It will mark a significant shift in the Britain’s post-Brexit relationship with the EU as they will require dynamic alignment, the process of matching laws to the bloc’s evolving legislation, the House of Lords European Affairs Committee said in a new report.

The committee of peers says this will mean parliament needs to set up a “robust, consistent and adequately resourced cross-departmental system” to manage the UK’s dynamic alignment commitments.

The committee chairman, Jock Stirrup, said:

Although important details remain to be determined of the legal and institutional form that dynamic alignment will take, its introduction will mark a major change to the nature of the UK-EU relationship as it exists under the Trade and Cooperation Agreement.

As the UK aligns with more EU law, parliament will face growing pressure to scrutinise significant volumes of EU legislation.

The food and farming deal alone could generate at least 780 pieces of in-scope EU law every year, the committee heard.

Meanwhile, the I reports that the government is close to securing a deal with Europe to help tackle post-Brexit medicine shortages, including antibiotics, ADHD medication and epilepsy drugs.

Experts on post-Brexit health policy have reportedly described the deal as “significant'” and a “good first step” in addressing shortages that have hit both sides in recent years.

The prime minister was meeting fellow leaders at the European Political Community summit in Dublin, ahead of the summit next week, where he hopes to bring Britain closer to Europe.

Burnham said:

I believe there is a good deal to be done that could be a win-win for Britain and Europe.

The deal is not done, and there’ll be some long nights ahead in the next seven days.
