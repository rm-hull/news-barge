Plans have gone in to transform a former York school building into a new arts hub and café.

As ***YorkMix*** first reported in August, number 37 Dalton Terrace, near Holgate Bridge, is set to become The Moxie Centre for Art.

Now, a formal planning application has been submitted to City of York Council detailing how the historic site will be converted.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/moxie-art-gallery-cafe-pd-3.webp)

The red-brick building dates back to 1880 and was originally built as a Sunday school and parish rooms for St Paul’s Church.

More recently, it formed part of The Mount School estate, housing nursery accommodation and a dance studio before being sold off as surplus to requirements.

Behind the gallery project is York couple Chris and Lucy Haltiner, who are setting up The Moxie as a not-for-profit organisation.

Their goal is to create a welcoming creative community where “seeing art should feel as normal as going for a coffee”.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/37-dalton-terrace-york-1-gsv.webp)

According to planning documents, the conversion will see modern additions to the building stripped back to reveal original timber wall panelling and quarry-tiled floors.

The stained brickwork on the Dalton Terrace frontage will be steam-cleaned, and old windows replaced to improve acoustics and stop the south-facing rooms from overheating in summer.

Inside, the building will be divided into several distinct creative zones:

* **A free public gallery:** Taking over a 1,000 sq ft space on the ground floor, hosting four to eight curated exhibitions a year ranging from visual art and photography to experimental and immersive installations.
* **Artist studios:** Around 15 affordable workspaces across two shared rooms, ranging from a single desk to a 60 sq ft partitioned studio, let on flexible rolling monthly contracts.
* **Photography facilities:** A 1,000 sq ft studio available to hire by the hour, alongside a fully equipped traditional black-and-white darkroom, which the founders believe will be the only public-access darkroom in York.
* **Workshops and events:** A dedicated first-floor meeting room for artist talks, taster sessions, and local club meetings.
* **A community café:** A counter-service café serving coffee, tea, pastries, and snacks, complete with a small shop selling darkroom supplies, postcards, and prints.

Outside, a landscaped courtyard and sculpture garden will be created.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/moxie-art-gallery-cafe-pd-2.webp)
![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/moxie-art-gallery-cafe-pd-4.webp)

A free-standing timber canopy with a black zinc roof will shelter the main entrance and provide covered outdoor seating for the café.

New metal entrance gates featuring The Moxie’s ‘M’ logo will also be installed between numbers 35 and 37, alongside secure cycle racks.

Because there is no visitor parking on site, guests will be encouraged to walk, cycle, or use nearby bus routes on Holgate Road and The Mount, with access also available from The Mount School car park outside of school hours.

The centre is set to open in stages. Building conversion work is pencilled in for 2027, with the first studios opening in the first half of the year before the full gallery programme and café launch in 2028.
