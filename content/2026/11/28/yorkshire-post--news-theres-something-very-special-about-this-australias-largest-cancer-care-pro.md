# ‘There's something very special about this’: Australia’s largest cancer care provider investing £30m in Leeds

## The CEO of Australia’s largest provider of cancer care has described Headingley Stadium as the perfect place to demonstrate the “great relationship between Australia and the UK” – as the company prepares to launch its specialised cancer centre at the site as part of a £30m investment into Leeds.

Plans were announced in March for the Icon Cancer Centre Leeds, to be located at AMT Headingley Stadium as part of a wider health offering at the site.

The centre is expected to employ around 30 people and house what Brisbane-based Icon Group has described as “the most advanced radiotherapy technology available”.

According to the group, the technology will be the first-of-its-kind to be implemented in the North of England and only the second in the UK, joining Icon’s other cancer centre in London.

![Icon Group CEO Mark Middleton. Picture: Lorne Campbell / Guzelian](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjEyMmYxZTBhLTQxOGMtNGI3ZS04ZjU1LWU3Mzk1OTdjZTI2OTo2MWQyYWVlNC0yZDhiLTQ1NWQtOWE0My01MWQ3OTNhYjkwZDA=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Icon Group CEO Mark Middleton said: “Headingley is an iconic location, a sporting ground not only for the people of Leeds, Yorkshire, and the United Kingdom, but also for the people of Australia. The history between us at that location is incredibly compelling

“What better place to portray the great relationship between Australia and the UK than Headingley, the scene of so many rugby league and cricket battles.

“We've won a few, we've lost a few, but that's the nature of sport, and that's the nature of relationships. I think there's something very, very special about this.”

AMT Headingley Stadium already hosts a string of community health, wellbeing and sporting initiatives.

![A patient is treated at an Iconig Group facility in Adelaide.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjQ3NTUyZDE4LTczY2ItNDU3Mi1hNGFhLTMyODhlZjdhNzc3NTplMWI4MjJjYi1jNDY2LTRlZmQtYTNlZC0wZmI2NGRjZjFjN2I=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The West Yorkshire region is also widely regarded as a hub for health technology firms, with companies including Huddersfield-based Paxman Scalp Cooling calling the county home.

As part of its wider £30m investment, Icon also recently acquired Nova Healthcare, a cancer and haematology care provider based within Leeds Cancer Centre at St James’s University Hospital.

One of the main reasons for the acquisition, Mr Middleton said, was the firm’s specialist ‘gamma knife’ technology.

Gamma knife is a specialised radiation therapy machine designed to treat conditions located upwards from the neck, including cranial tumours.

“We're really excited by that,” said Mr Middleton, speaking on the technology. “We have three of those machines in Australia, one in Brisbane, one in Sydney, one in Melbourne. So we're building out one of the largest networks of gamma knifes in the world, and it just so happens Leeds is also part of that global network.

“There's a new machine going in very, very soon. Ourselves and the previous vendors have invested in new technology, and that's obviously going to be a great benefit to the community. “

The acquisition is also part of Icon’s drive to take advantage of the region’s wider technology expertise. What is really clear is that Leeds is a hotbed of innovation, tech, digital capabilities and AI,” said Mr Middleton.

“Those things really resonate with us as well, because we want to be at the cutting edge of what we do, and I think Leeds offers some really unique opportunities for working with some of the tech organisations, and working with the universities, who we've already met with multiple times.

"I think this is a project that's going to bring a lot of people in the community together.”

Icon’s investment in Leeds also forms part of its wider expansion in the UK.

Already active in New Zealand, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and Germany, as well as its home country, Icon expanded into the UK in 2024 with the acquisition of a cancer centre in Wimbledon.

The company is now aiming to expand “rapidly” in the UK, and hopes to be treating patients at the Headingley site by April.

Icon also hopes to bring some of its specialised workforce over to the UK to work in Leeds.

“The £30m we’ve invested doesn't take into account highly specialised workforces, all of the things that we need to do to also bring people to Leeds,” said Mr Middleton.

“Our site manager has arrived in Leeds. He's actually from Australia, so he's relocated with his partner to live in Leeds, and there will be more Australians coming as well.

“There's workforce shortages for everyone, and we want to make sure that we're actually contributing to the workforce in the UK.

"We have the great benefit of over 60 cancer centres around the world. We train a lot of people, and we're actually bringing a lot of people to the UK as well, so I'm hopeful that the community also sees that as a real benefit."

Icon has also appointed Dame Linda Pollard as Strategic Advisor to support its engagement in Leeds and across the UK. Dame Linda previously served as chair of Leeds Hospitals NHS Trust.

Though the company has no direct plans to expand further in Yorkshire at the moment, it does plan to widen the services it offers from Headingley.

“What I'd say is that we're going to continue to invest in people and expand those services in terms of what makes sense,” added Mr Middleton.

“So once we have our cancer centre up and running at Headingley, we're also thinking about other things. What about cancer survivorship programmes for patients? Headingley is a unique location for perhaps rehabilitation services, prehabilitation. How do we have education sessions for patients?

“We're going to expand those services as we continue to grow, and should the opportunity arise in terms of taking our services beyond Leeds, we absolutely will. But we also want to make sure that we've got these hub facilities that can look after as many people as possible. So watch this space.”
