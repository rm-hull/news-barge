Durham County Council expects to spend £111m less than planned on building and improvement work this year.

The council originally expected to spend around £363.5m during 2026/27, but this has now fallen to £252.3m.

This money is used for longer-term work such as building and improvement projects, rather than the day-to-day running of council services.

Figures were revealed in a report to be discussed by the authority's audit committee on Monday (November 30).

The report says the latest figures take into account changes to the council's spending plans agreed by Cabinet earlier this month.

The council has also avoided taking out any new loans since October 2025 and has instead been using money it already holds.

It says this has allowed it to delay borrowing while interest rates remain high.

However, the money will still need to be borrowed and the council expects to take out around £100m in new loans during the second half of the financial year.

At the end of September, Durham County Council had around £461m in external loans.

Deputy council leader Darren Grimes promised a "laser-like focus on reviewing contracts."

Speaking ahead of the audit committee, he said: "The lower capital spend this year largely reflects projects and expenditure being reprofiled into future years, rather than £111 million simply being saved.

"Separately, we are maintaining a laser-like focus on contracts, waste and avoidable costs.

"Where there are genuine opportunities to save taxpayers’ money without harming services residents rely on, we will take them."

When other long-term financial commitments are included, the council's overall debt stood at around £557m, down from £579m at the end of March.

The council said it had followed its agreed rules for managing borrowing and investments during the first half of the year.
