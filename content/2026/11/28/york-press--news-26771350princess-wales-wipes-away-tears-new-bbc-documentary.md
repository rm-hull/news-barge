## Emotional Princess of Wales wipes away tears in new BBC documentary

The Princess of Wales' new documentary airs this weekend *(Image: Kensington Palace/Detail Films/Press Association)*

A documentary has captured the Princess of Wales wiping away tears during her Three Peaks Challenge.

The new 30-minute film titled Heal follows Kate, 44, as she climbs the highest mountains in Scotland, England, and Wales within 24 hours while reflecting on her cancer experience.

In an emotional scene, filmed at Scafell Pike, her brother James Middleton plays voice messages from her and Prince William’s three children.

While listening to the messages, the Princess of Wales brushed away tears.

![](https://www.bournemouthecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775330.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Princess Kate was diagnosed with cancer in 2024 *(Image: Kensington Palace/Detail Films/Press Association)*

In a voice recording, Kate's youngest child, Prince Louis, says: "Hello, Mummy.

"I really love you and I hope you do well going up the mountain.

"Remember, I’m always in your heart with you, and I love you very much.

"And I’ll be following behind your back with you.

"And remember, I love you really lots and lots, and I give you lots and lots of love.

"Bye."

Prince George, her eldest child, tells her: "Hi, Mumma.

"Hope you’re doing well.

"We’re all thinking of you right now and we all love you so much and you’re gonna do a great job."

He added: "I’m tracking you on Flightradar up the mountains and you’re looking… you look really, really strong.

"Keep going and we’re right behind you."

Princess Charlotte’s message says: "Hello, Mummy.

"I love you so much.

"We are so proud of you doing your walk and your challenges.

"We really wish you good luck and I’ll be cheering you on.

"See you soon."

In tears, Kate says: "So sweet."

Mr Middleton explains that Prince William sent him the messages to play once they reached the summit.

The Princess of Wales says: "They gave me the reason to get out of hospital.

"They’re going to give me the reason to finish this challenge."

Elsewhere in the documentary, Kate describes her time in hospital as "really tough" but says she feels "so grateful because it’s really made me appreciate life so much."

![](https://www.bournemouthecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775329.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Princess of Wales listened to voice messages from her three children during the Three Peaks Challenge *(Image: Kensington Palace/Detail Films/Press Association)*

She describes the experience as "stark and alien and stripped back," but also recalls a moment of comfort during a dark period.

Kate says: "The darkness was a really scary, disorientating experience."

She adds: "I think I was sort of in and out of consciousness.

"It was a really dark time.

"But also then, somewhere through that too, I became aware of someone holding my hand.

"They weren’t talking to me.

"They weren’t trying to fix anything.

"They were just there.

"And the presence of someone that stood by me in that time, I’ll forever be grateful for."

Kate was supported on her Three Peaks challenge by Mountain Rescue.

The documentary also shows Kate meeting with patients and survivors from Shine Cancer Support, reflecting on identity and support following a cancer diagnosis.

During a gathering at a pub in Richmond-Upon-Thames last June, she says: "Genuinely, meeting you all here today really means a lot to me because it’s just lovely to be able to share my personal experience, but also hear your stories and be able to connect with others who’ve gone through something similar.

"I haven’t really explored this outside my family walls, really.

"So thank you for allowing me the time to do that and the open space to explore that."

Ceinwen Giles is the co-founder of Shine Cancer Support and a cancer survivor.

Speaking after the screening in Soho, central London, on Tuesday afternoon, Ms Giles said: "Connecting with other people who have had a cancer diagnosis who aren’t your family is incredibly important because you will have a special relationship with your family, but also they care for you, they love you, they don’t want anything to happen to you.

"And so it can be really difficult sometimes to have those conversations where you’re saying, ‘Actually, I’m really scared of dying’."

The 51-year-old, who lives in south-east London but is originally from Canada, explained she was diagnosed with non-Hodgkin lymphoma – a type of blood cancer – when she was aged 34, six weeks after prematurely giving birth to her daughter.

She then spent six months in the hospital getting treatment as an inpatient.

A few years after her diagnosis, Ms Giles – who is now in remission – co-founded Shine Cancer Support with Emma Willis, with the charity now counting 16 support groups across the country.

Speaking of the support meetings, Ms Giles added: “We have a lot of laughter as well, but when you’re in a space where you are surrounded by people who have had that diagnosis, it then gives you permission to explore other things as well.

“That whole idea of being able to explore who you are after something really fundamental has happened to you is really important.”

Before the screening on Tuesday, Kate addressed the audience in an introductory speech, saying she hopes Heal will “encourage compassion, showing and the real value in standing alongside one another in the hardest of times”.

“Every year, hundreds of thousands of people receive the words no one wants to hear,” the princess further said.

She added: “Cancer doesn’t just affect the body.

“It changes how you think and feel and profoundly affects every aspect of life.

“I know this personally and that the journey through and beyond treatment requires more than medicine alone.

“But it’s often the people, the places and experiences that can help people through the darkest times.”

The princess was diagnosed with cancer after undergoing major abdominal surgery in 2024.

She had chemotherapy at the Royal Marsden Hospital in Chelsea, south-west London, and announced she was in remission at the start of 2025.

## How to watch the Princess of Wales' new documentary, Heal

Heal will air on BBC One and iPlayer at 6.40pm on Sunday, November 29.

It will then be shown internationally via NBCUniversal in the US on November 30, Channel 9 in Australia on December 1 and CTV in Canada on December 7.  
  
All funds received through the documentary’s distribution have been directed to The Royal Marsden Cancer Charity to help people living with and beyond cancer.
