Frontline ambulance staff in the North East are to lead projects aimed at improving support for people experiencing mental health crises.

Thomas Hague and Jayne Donaghue MBE will spend a year researching how patients can be connected with help, drawing on their experience of working for North East Ambulance Service (NEAS).

Mr Hague is an ambulance support practitioner based at Blucher station, while Ms Donaghue is a paramedic based at Bishop Auckland station.

Both have been appointed to the trust's Clinical Fellowship Programme, which gives staff dedicated time to research issues they encounter on the frontline and make recommendations to improve care.

Mr Hague will look at how ambulance clinicians can connect patients with community support, including social prescribing services, charities and voluntary organisations.

His research will consider how people can access help with needs that may be contributing to poor health and wellbeing.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21771495.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Thomas Hague is an ambulance support practitioner based at Blucher station *(Image: NEAS)*

Mr Hague said: "I notice day to day that we are attending more and more patients in mental health crisis.

"We often meet people whose wellbeing is affected by factors beyond their immediate clinical needs.

"I want to explore how we can improve awareness of the support available in communities across the North East and help colleagues connect patients with services that may make a real difference to their lives.

"Hopefully, this work will help us better support patients and ensure people can access the right help when they need it."

![A woman wearing an NHS paramedic uniform poses for a portrait.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21771056.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bishop Auckland paramedic Jayne Donaghue will examine support following mental health crisis calls in County Durham. *(Image: NEAS)*

Ms Donaghue's project will focus on what happens after an ambulance attends someone experiencing a mental health crisis in County Durham.

She will work with colleagues in healthcare, mental health services and community organisations to examine barriers to timely support and opportunities for closer partnership working.

Ms Donaghue said: "You want to do the very best for every patient but knowing someone needs support and being able to connect them with it can be very different things.

"Sometimes A&E becomes the only accessible option, even when it's clear a person's needs could be better met elsewhere.

"This fellowship gives me the opportunity to bring frontline experiences together with the views of mental health and community partners so we can identify practical improvements that benefit patients and clinicians alike."

NEAS said crews were responding to growing numbers of patients whose needs went beyond immediate medical treatment, including people facing mental health crises and wider social challenges.

In 2025/26, its health advisers answered more than 1.475 million emergency 999 and NHS 111 calls, while more than 143,800 patients were treated at home or in their communities.

The fellowship programme was introduced by the trust last year to support professional development and help frontline experience shape improvements to services.

Its previous clinical fellow, Russell Barnard, completed a project on cardiac arrest care, with his recommendations now helping to inform improvements across the trust.

Andrew Hodge, director of paramedicine and allied health professionals, said: "Our frontline colleagues have invaluable experience and insight into the needs of patients and communities across the North East.

"The Clinical Fellowship Programme gives them the opportunity to turn that experience into action by researching issues they are passionate about and developing ideas that can improve care.

"Thomas and Jayne are tackling an area of growing importance for healthcare services, and their work has the potential to make a real difference for patients, colleagues and partner organisations across the region."
