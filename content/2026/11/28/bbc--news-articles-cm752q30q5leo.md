**Air strikes by Myanmar's military have killed dozens of people across the South East Asian nation, including at a wedding venue and a school.**

Rescuers said at least 21 were confirmed dead, and at least 35 others injured, at the wedding site in Moe Bye village, in the country's southern Shan state, on Saturday afternoon.

On Thursday at least 20 civilians were killed, including students, young monks and teachers, when an air strike hit a school at In Yon village, in central Myanmar's Magway region where resistance forces are active, according to Myanmar's exiled National Unity Government (NUG).

Myanmar's military-led government has not commented on the strikes.

The military also carried out air strikes in Mrauk-U town, in western Myanmar's Rakhine state, on Saturday evening.

At about 20:00 local time (13:30 GMT), a residential area in the town was bombed, reportedly killing more than 10 people and injuring about 20 others.

The area is controlled by the Arakan Army (AA) - one of the strongest ethnic armies fighting the country's military regime.

Myanmar's military took over the country in a coup in 2021

Military air strikes have been happening almost daily across the country since a coup in 2021 toppled the elected civilian government led by former leader Aung San Suu Kyi, resulting in a lengthy civil war.

Villages have been burned and ground offensives have been carried out by the military on areas it is unable to control, including territories controlled by the NUG and ethnic armed organisations.

The latest strikes come a week after Myanmar was found guilty of committing genocide against its Rohingya minority by the International Court of Justice (ICJ).

A 2017 military crackdown killed thousands and forced more than 700,000 to flee, but Myanmar denied committing genocide - saying its forces had responded to attacks by armed groups.

The verdict marked the first time a state had ever been found to have committed genocide by an international court.
