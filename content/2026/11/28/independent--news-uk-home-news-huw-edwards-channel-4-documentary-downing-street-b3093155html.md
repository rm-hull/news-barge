Channel 4 has defended its decision to air a controversial documentary about disgraced broadcaster Huw Edwards after Downing Street slammed the move.

The former BBC newsreader, 65, resigned in 2024 after allegations of grooming surfaced and he was subsequently convicted of making indecent images of children and received a six-month suspended prison sentence. Edwards was never charged with a criminal offence in relation to grooming.

News that the convicted sex offender was being interviewed and profiled in a new Channel 4 documentary *Huw Edwards – Guilty* was met with criticism this week, with No10 insisting “we have no interest in hearing what he has to say”.

In a new statement issued on Saturday, the Channel 4’s director of news, Louisa Compton, argued Edwards’ account is there “to be tested, not accepted”.

She said they are “acutely conscious” that behind offences involving indecent images are real children and real victims who have been abused. Edwards received more than 40 images over several months, with the worst featuring a child aged between seven and nine.

“Nothing about making this film diminishes the seriousness of that harm, nor does Edwards’ participation entitle him to sympathy, redemption or rehabilitation,” she said. “His account is there to be tested, not accepted.”

![Huw Edwards, 65, is the subject of a new Channel 4 documentary](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/13/44/Huw-Edwards-Guilty-92405a0b.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She argued journalism is not about only putting people on television “whose actions or opinions we agree with” but to “look directly at people who have done terrible things, confront them with difficult questions, test their explanations and allow audiences to judge what they say for themselves”.

“That is what this film does. Huw Edwards is not being allowed to tell his story unchallenged or on his own terms,” she said.

“He is questioned throughout by an experienced director; he was not paid; he had no control over the content of the film; and there were no restrictions on the questions he could be asked.”

The statement continues: “Edwards was one of the most prominent and trusted broadcasters in Britain.

“He pleaded guilty to serious offences involving indecent images of children and has never publicly faced detailed questioning about his criminal behaviour or the complete collapse of his career and reputation.

“There is a clear public interest in confronting him with those questions rather than allowing them to remain unanswered.”

![Former BBC broadcaster Huw Edwards received a suspended sentence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/10/953018e370b4bb01f0c89ff532b15a4dY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1ODYwNDIy-2.77480467.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Shadow culture secretary Rebecca Paul was among those to criticise the decision to air the special, writing on X: “Why on earth is a state-owned broadcaster platforming this depraved sex offender? This man shouldn’t get a minute of airtime.”

Independent MP Rosie Duffield, who sits on the Women and Equalities Select Committee and campaigns on behalf of victims of abuse, told the BBC’s *Newsnight* programme it was a “bizarre decision” by Channel 4 bosses to air the documentary.

Government minister Anna Turley told LBC she was “deeply uncomfortable” with the interviews and that Edwards is one of the “many people in the public eye” given a “free pass”.

However, Ms Compton insisted discomfort is not a reason to look away.

“We should be capable of examining difficult issues rather than looking away because we don’t like what we see,” she added.

“We understand that some people will profoundly disagree with our decision to make this film. But discomfort is not, in itself, a reason for journalism not to ask difficult questions. We believe those questions are in the public interest.”

![Edwards was convicted in 2024](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/13/15/Huw-Edwards-Guilty-3-35746983.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a trailer for the documentary, which follows Edwards at home in rural Wales, he appears to respond angrily when questioned by an off-camera producer.

Sounding frustrated, he responds: “You want me to analyse what I was doing? I mean, seriously?”

Later in the trailer, the interviewer asks: “What do you think you were doing?” Edwards responds: “I’d lost the plot.”

Edwards will also be the subject of an *Observer*podcast entitled* What Happened To Huw Edwards*, which “explores the truth behind the most sensational public downfall in recent memory”.

In a clip shared ahead of the release next week, he can be seen saying: “Silence is toxic, I think. Lots of the advice I’ve had is just be quiet, don’t say anything.”
