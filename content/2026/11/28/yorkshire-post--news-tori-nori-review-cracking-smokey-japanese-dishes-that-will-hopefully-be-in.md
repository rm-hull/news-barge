# Tori Nori review: Cracking smokey Japanese dishes that will hopefully be in Leeds for the long haul

## Tori Nori is an enticing restaurant bringing the many flavours of Japan to the bustling centre of Leeds. Chris Bond has his chopsticks at the ready and is keen to dive straight in. Pictures by Tony Johnson.

It was that wise old bird Heraclitus who reportedly once said: “There is nothing permanent except change.” We’ll never know exactly what inspired this pearl of wisdom, given that he was knocking around 2,500 years ago, but it would make an apt slogan for the food scene in modern-day Leeds.

I’ve been eating out in the city centre for more than 25 years, and I can’t remember seeing more restaurant comings and goings than I have during the past 18 months or so. Tastes change, of course. A decade ago, you could happily wend your way through Leeds on a Spanish tapas crawl, something you would be hard-pressed to do today.

On the other hand, the street-food flavours of Thailand, Vietnam and South Korea are now booming. The influx of overseas students has no doubt shaped the food landscape not just of Leeds, but of many other towns and cities. And that’s a good thing in my book. I like the fact that you could go out every night in Leeds for the best part of a month and find a different country’s food to explore each time.

![Tori Nori on Vicar Lane, Leeds.  Salmon nigiri. Photograph by Tony Johnson.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjhmNTdiZTllLTAzNjItNDZlNS1iYmIxLWUyNWJjYWM0OTI1MDozZDBjOGRiYy02ZjljLTQ1OWYtYWQ4OS05ZTVhNDhmZTQ1OTQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


It certainly feels like Japan is among those enjoying a moment right now. Japanese restaurants are nothing new in the city (Teppanyaki has been wowing customers with its theatrics for more than 30 years), but their proliferation certainly is.

At the vanguard of this is Lattawan Buchanan – better known simply as Chef Fern. She opened Fern Modern Sushi in Leeds’s Central Arcade in the summer of 2024, which has gone on to become a big hit with sushi fans.

Clearly not one to rest on her laurels, Chef Fern expanded her food empire with Tori Nori back in April and earlier this month opened Fern Modern Sushi 2 on Park Row.

And while Fern Modern Sushi is all about sushi, ramen and Japanese street food, Tori Nori is a robatayaki-inspired restaurant where the focus is on the smoky flavours of its charcoal grill.

![Beef Tataki.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjY1ZTQxN2ZmLWY5ZjUtNGE5Yy1iY2Y1LTk1NDdmYmFlZmFhNTo1MzA5NjQ1Yy02NmYyLTRkOWMtYTk4ZS03ZWQ5NjAxODY3ZWQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The glut of swanky high-rise apartments that now pepper the city centre has helped inject a bit more life into Leeds during the middle of the week. Even so, I’m pleasantly surprised that on an inclement autumn evening Tori Nori is buzzing and practically full.

Music fans among you may recognise this as the former home of the Old Red Bus Station on Vicar Lane, a popular bar with a reputation for supporting up-and-coming talent.

It’s understandable why people get misty-eyed about losing cherished venues like this, but I’d rather a local celebrity chef take over the building and breathe new life into it than for it to become part of some ubiquitous corporate chain.

Tori Nori is certainly a welcoming space. The big windows allow you to peer into the open-plan kitchen and watch the brigade of chefs at work before you’ve even stepped through the door. Inside, the L-shaped room has been stylishly decked out with pendant lighting and colourful artworks that adorn the walls.

![Cheesy torched salmon uramaki.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjQxMDk2ZmQwLTg5ZDYtNGU4Zi1iM2U5LWRhMzI2YWQzYjliNTpjMGQ1MjBhNS0zMzQ5LTRkNDQtOTFlNi05ZmIyOTc0MWNjYTM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


There’s a broad menu, with the main challenge being trying to narrow down what you’re going to have. We went for the ‘little of a lot’ approach, starting with some expertly made salmon and eel nigiri.

From the tempting selection of uramaki – an ‘inside-out’ style of sushi roll where seasoned rice forms the outer layer with a sheet of nori (seaweed) on the inside – we had the cheesy torched salmon. This came with king prawn tempura, avocado, truffle mayo, teriyaki and parmesan shavings, balancing the east meets west flavours with aplomb.

The small plates menu sounded particularly appetising, and along with a moreish bowl of lightly salted edamame beans, we plumped for the beef tataki, seared beef with daikon, crispy onion, micro herbs and a truffle and ponzu sauce, and chicken karaage with spicy mayo (posh comfort food at its best), both of which hit their mark.

We were going to share a dessert until I spotted the Kuro wagyu slider with smoked cheese and truffle chips, which sounded too tempting to ignore. I’m glad we didn’t because this was a cracker of a dish and an affordable way of sampling wagyu beef, which can be eye-wateringly expensive. Smoked cheese and truffle chips aren’t exactly ingredients you associate with Japan, but this was a good example of how fusion cooking can work well when in the right hands.

![Tori Nori on Vicar Lane, Leeds.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjlmOWYzYmEzLTIwNmQtNDMyYi1iMDJhLTFlY2EwMTY4YmM2ZDo5ZGUwOTk2Mi0wOTY4LTQ0N2QtYmE2Ni04ZGI3ODk4Y2M2YTQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


As you can probably gather by now, Tori Nori is inspired by, rather than sticking religiously to, Japanese ingredients. It’s a smart move, because there’s enough here to keep the purists happy as well as those with less adventurous palates.

There were just a couple of minor grumbles.

Firstly, we were sitting near the door, and there was an unwelcome draught in our part of the dining room, which could do with being rectified before winter kicks in. The other, and perhaps this was partly on us, is that the food all came out in a matter of minutes. As we had ordered a mixture of hot and cold dishes, it wasn’t a problem, but my advice would be to order gradually. Unless, of course, you’re off to the theatre, or a gig, and want a quick bite. In which case, fill your boots.

The final bill for two, including service and a couple of glasses of Riesling, came in at £121, which is par for the course these days. The staff are young, friendly and welcoming, adding favourably to the overall vibe.

In an ever-changing food scene, it feels like Tori Nori has quickly established itself not only as a serious player but also as one that is hopefully here for the long haul.

To misappropriate a well-worn footballing cliché, if you can pull in punters on a chilly midweek evening in November, then you’re doing something right.
