England is known for its strange place names that are likely to confuse tourists when they visit.

Even people who have lived here their whole life can even get caught out by the pronunciation of towns and villages around the country.

The North East has plenty of place names that non-locals will not know how to correctly say.

If you are travelling through the town of Prudhoe, in Northumberland, you might want to be sure you know how to pronounce its name.

## How to pronounce Prudhoe

For anyone travelling through the area or visiting for a quick trip, you will want to make sure how to say the town’s name to impress the locals.

You might think it is an easy name to pronounce and say it like prud-hoe.

However, this is completely wrong.

Those who are from the area will call it prudda, which will likely catch out anyone who does not live in or around Prudhoe.

Content creator Liam Lav shared a video about the commonly mispronounced place names in the North East, with Prudhoe making the list.

He said: “It’s not prud-hoe, that’s just outrageous.”

### What is Prudhoe like?

Described as a “growing picturesque town” found along the River Tyne.

If you are thinking of moving to Prudhoe, the average house price was £193,138 over the last year, according to Rightmove .

The majority of properties sold were semi-detached houses, costing around £161,558.

The town has a few green spaces to explore, including the Spetchells nature reserve, the Tyne Riverside Country Park for a riverside walk, and Eastwoods Park.

If you are a fan of history, you can explore Prudhoe Castle, which is completely free to enter.

If you visit on the first Sunday of each month, you can step inside the castle and climb the steps to the gatehouse chapel.

Reviewers on Tripadvisor say the castle is a "hidden treasure" surrounded by a "stunning setting".

For food, there are several places to enjoy a meal, such as Aramee, an Indian restaurant offering traditional flavours.

The Adam and Eve pub offers loaded fries, smash burgers, and a few pub classic dishes to enjoy alongside a drink.

For the summer, the pub has a great garden area to soak up some sun in.

You can pop into the Tyne Riverside Café in the country park, which offers hot drinks and snacks.
