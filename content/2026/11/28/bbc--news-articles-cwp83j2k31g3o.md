Over a career spanning more than two decades, Gallagher became a familiar face on television, radio and stage

**Taoiseach (Irish prime minster) Micheál Martin has led tributes to comedian and broadcaster PJ Gallagher, who has died at the age of 51.**

Born in Cork and adopted and raised in Dublin, Gallagher was a familiar presence on television for more than two decades, appearing in numerous programmes, including BBC's The Young Offenders.

He first came to prominence in 2005, co-creating the RTÉ hidden-camera series Naked Camera, which established him as one of Ireland's best-known comedy performers.

As well as his work in stand-up comedy and acting, Gallagher presented television documentaries and was a member of the breakfast show team on Radio Nova.

Posting on social media, Martin described Gallagher as a "gifted comedian, actor and broadcaster" who brought "immense warmth and laughter to so many across Ireland".

Gallagher's agency LR Comedy confirmed his death in a statement on Friday evening, saying his family and friends were "devastated" and asked that they be given privacy and time to come to terms with their loss.

The statement also highlighted how Gallagher spoke with "remarkable candour about his experiences of mental illness", wanting people to "talk about what they were going through without shame".

He published a memoir in 2023 titled Madhouse: The naked truth about my chaotic childhood, losing my mind and finding a place to call home.

Following this he made a documentary called Changing My Mind, which explored mental health issues.

More recently Gallagher headed the RTÉ One series The Random Driving Test, after participating earlier in the year in the second season of Uncharted with Ray Goggins, where he joined actor Liam Cunningham, and Goggins on a motorbike expedition across the Himalayas.

## 'The sweetest soul'

Gallagher's The Young Offenders co-star Alex Murphy said his heart was "broken" after learning of his death.

"I can't believe it. The relentlessly funny PJ Gallagher will be missed beyond belief," he posted on Instagram.

Hilary Rose, who plays Murphy's on-screen mother on the show, said it was difficult to "wrap her head" around Gallagher's death.

"Days were better with PJ around," she added.

Writing on Bluesky, comedian Dara Ó Briain said Gallagher was "the sweetest soul; and a warm, instinctive, funny-bones performer".

Podcaster Blindboy Boatclub echoed Ó Briain's comments, describing Gallagher as an "incredibly sweet and kind person".

Liverpool comedian John Bishop also paid tribute, describing Gallagher as "funny, kind, caring, humble and vulnerable.

"My thoughts go out to his close friends and family and he will be in my mind whenever I get on my bike to clear my head."

In 2007, Gallagher won an Irish Film and Television Academy Award for his RTÉ series Naked Camera

Radio Nova, where Gallagher hosted the Morning Glory breakfast show with Jim McCabe, said he "will be so dearly missed by all who had the privilege to cross his path".

Steve Carson, Irish broadcaster RTÉ's Director of Video, said that Gallagher had "brought his extraordinary wit, warmth, empathy and energy to everything he did".

"We are saddened to hear of the passing of PJ Gallagher," said Chris Sussman, Director of BBC Comedy.

Sussman added he "will be fondly remembered for his role" in the BBC Three series The Young Offenders.

Tánaiste (Irish deputy prime minster) Simon Harris said he was "deeply saddened" by the news, describing Gallagher as a "true treasure of Irish broadcasting and comedy".

Sinn Féin President Mary Lou McDonald said she was heartbroken for Gallagher's family, describing him as "down-to-Earth and warm".

"A true blue-Dub, proud northsider, a passionate supporter of Dublin GAA and Bohemians. He loved this place. PJ had so much to give and he gave it with such spirit, generosity and glint in his eye," she said.

"What do you say when words are not enough? RIP, PJ", said Dublin club Bohemians FC, of which Gallagher was a lifelong fan.
