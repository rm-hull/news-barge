## Black Friday Power Station deals - buying advice

### What power stations should I choose during Black Friday and Cyber Monday?

That comes down to two things: how much battery do you want, and do you intend to move it around?

Batteries, in the amounts needed to run mains-powered equipment, aren’t light, and the realistic maximum size of mobile devices is 4kWh. In fact, unless you have help, much above 3kWh is a challenge. If you want mobility, go with 2 Wh batteries, though some brands let you connect multiple units for longer run time.

If you don’t intend to make the power portable, and instead want a home device, then you can buy heavier solutions as they’re not likely to be moved. With static installations, expansion is an important consideration, since a home can’t run for long on even 5kWh.

A typical UK home uses around 10-15 kWh per day, although most batteries can’t output enough to run multiple heavy-use items (oven, kettle, washing machine, etc.) at the same time.

The other aspect of these devices you need to consider is using them with solar panels, as these require inputs and most batteries that have them limit the wattage and amperage they can handle from DC solar sources. Many Power Station makers offer panels specifically for use with their batteries, and a few even use proprietary connectors.

Before you spend anything, think about how you intend to use this hardware, and if it's up to the job. A smaller 1024 Wh battery might be fine for overnight camping and charge reasonably quickly from portable solar panels, but it won’t power a large TV for more than about four hours.

### What features should I check when selecting the right Power Station deal?

The feature you should think about most, other than battery capacity, is the output limit in watts. As a general rule, larger batteries can output more, which enables them to cope with more demanding devices.

As an example, I use one of mine with the Bambu H2D 3D printer, and it can use plenty when it's first powered up and heating the bed and nozzles. But once it's printing, it uses much less. Therefore, the battery must handle both the peak power demand and the sustained demand. This is especially important if you intend to use the Power Station for some remote DIY, as circular saws and planes have high initial demands that might overload smaller batteries, causing them to cut power to protect the equipment.

Check what the equipment makers say the normal and peak demands are, and compare that to the battery's specs before plugging anything in.

I’ve already mentioned the weight of these things, and if you are older or have a strength issue, don’t buy a battery you cannot move with the help of another person.

If you want to use solar panels, research what watts and amps the solar inputs accept, and get panels that work within those ranges. You can connect panels in series or parallel to charge faster, but this won’t work if the watts or amps are too high, and you could damage your equipment. A smaller battery might only take 400W, limiting it to a single panel of that size, but larger batteries might take much larger single inputs or an array of smaller panels.

The final consideration is what the battery technology is, ideally LiFePO4, and how many recharge cycles the equipment is rated for. Smaller batteries might only be rated for 4000 cycles, whereas larger batteries, like the EcoFlow STREAM 5000, are rated for 10,000 complete recharges before dropping to 70% of their original capacity.

If you use the battery every day, then being able to handle more cycles is clearly more important than one that gets occasional use.
