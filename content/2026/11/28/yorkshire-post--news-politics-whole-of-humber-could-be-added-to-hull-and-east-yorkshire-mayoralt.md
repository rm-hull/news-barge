# Exclusive:Whole of Humber could be added to Hull and East Yorkshire mayoralty

## Hull MPs have long been pushing for the Hull and East Yorkshire Combined Authority to include the south bank of the Humber.

The south bank of the Humber could be added to mayor Luke Campbell’s Hull and East Yorkshire Combined Authority (HEYCA) as part of a Government review that will start next year.

The Yorkshire Post understands this is under consideration by Devolution Minister Jim McMahon, after MPs raised concerns that the current boundaries do not accurately reflect the region’s economic area.

They made clear that this is separate from allegations around Mr Campbell’s conduct, which has prompted a police investigation.

Currently HEYCA, which elected its first mayor last year, is made up of Hull and the East Riding, with the idea that it should only contain Yorkshire councils.

That meant North and North East Lincolnshire, which include Scunthorpe, Immingham and Grimsby, would be part of the Greater Lincolnshire Combined Authority, which stretches all the way down to Stamford near Peterborough.

Much of the Humber’s industry is now split across two mayors - meaning there is no single focal point to push the Energy Estuary on the world stage, such as on business trips to India.

Earlier this month, Mr McMahon confirmed “it is my intention to initiate the preparation of the review in early 2027, with the review itself taking place from May 2027 onwards”.

He explained: “I agreed with the leaders of all the now constituent councils that there would be a review of governance and geography in the third year after the mayors were elected.

“We agreed the review would report on the effectiveness of partnership arrangements in delivering successful economic and public service outcomes which reflect the unique nature of the Humber area, with the exact terms of reference to be confirmed by MHCLG.”

Hull MPs have been pressing Mr McMahon to shift the heavy industry on the south bank - such as at Immingham port - into HEYCA.

Dame Diana Johnson, Emma Hardy and Karl Turner met with the minister earlier this week, after he confirmed the timetable for the review.

Ms Hardy, the MP for Hull West and Haltemprice, said in a statement: “I welcome this review. I never believed the current geography was right and have consistently argued for a devolution deal that includes Hull, East Yorkshire and the south bank, better reflecting our local economic footprint.

“I also believe we could achieve far more than we currently are within the existing boundary.

“Local people deserve the same ambition, investment and results seen elsewhere, and it is frustrating to watch other areas race ahead while we stand still.”

Dame Diana, who represents Hull North, told the BBC: “We want devolution from Whitehall to be a success.

“However, HEYCA as currently constituted has one hand tied behind its back by not representing both sides of the Humber around which economic regeneration should be focussed. This means that is not leading to the progress that it could achieve.

“We want to make it clear that these concerns are entirely separate from the allegations about the conduct of Mayor Luke Campbell, his performance and the working culture at HEYCA.”

Mr Campbell has had a tricky time since taking over as mayor in May 2025.

The Olympic gold medal winner has missed a number of key meetings with Cabinet ministers and the Overview and Scrutiny Committee, and the combined authority is still searching for its first full-time chief executive.

He recently told LBC that allegations he showed naked photographs to a HEYCA staff member are "just political point scoring”.

“I strongly deny the allegations. I can’t wait and I’m excited for the police to come to a conclusion, so then we can move forward and I can get my job done,” he added.
