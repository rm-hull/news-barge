Parts of the UK are set to be battered by torrential rain and gale-force winds at the start of next week, with the Met Office issuing two yellow weather warnings.

More than 100mm of rainfall could fall in the worst-affected areas across Scotland, north-west England and Wales on Monday and Tuesday, raising the risk of flooding, power cuts and severe travel disruption.

The first yellow warning for rain comes into effect at 3am on Monday 30 November and runs until midday on Tuesday 1 December, covering parts of western, central and southern Scotland.

![Met Office yellow rain warnings come into force across western Scotland, Cumbria and Wales on Monday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/16/26/Screenshot-2026-11-28-at-16-27-34.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=2%3A3%2Csmart&trim=0%2C246%2C6%2C230&auto=webp)

Forecasters expect 30mm to 50mm of rain widely across the warning areas, with up to 90mm possible over the western Highlands and Dumfries and Galloway, accompanied by strong to gale-force south-westerly winds.

Rising temperatures are also expected to melt lying snow across the Highlands, adding to river flows and increasing the likelihood of flooding where ground is already saturated and drains are blocked by fallen leaves.

A second yellow rain warning takes effect from midday on Monday until 11.59pm on Tuesday 1 December, covering Cumbria alongside large parts of north and south Wales, including Gwynedd, Conwy, Powys, Carmarthenshire and the South Wales Valleys.

Within that zone, 30mm to 60mm of rainfall is forecast broadly, with more than 100mm possible over higher ground in the wettest locations.

![Heavy downpours are expected to continue across Cumbria and parts of Wales throughout Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/16/22/Screenshot-2026-11-28-at-16-28-26.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=2%3A3%2Csmart&trim=0%2C315%2C5%2C235&auto=webp)

Met Office meteorologist Dan Holley said a “conveyor belt of frontal systems” moving in from Sunday night will push persistent rain onto western hills, shifting southwards through Tuesday.

The Met Office has warned spray and surface water could lead to difficult driving conditions and road closures, alongside delays or cancellations to train and bus services.

There is also a small chance that homes and businesses could be flooded, communities temporarily cut off, and power supplies disrupted, while fast-flowing or deep floodwater could pose a danger to life.

Southern and eastern parts of England are expected to escape the worst of the downpours, enjoying drier and brighter spells at the start of the week despite brisk winds.

The full list of areas covered by the Met Office yellow rain warnings:

**Warning 1: 3am Monday 30 November – 12pm Tuesday 1 December**

* Central, Tayside & Fife: Angus, Clackmannanshire, Falkirk, Perth and Kinross, Stirling
* Grampian: Aberdeenshire, Moray
* Highlands & Eilean Siar: Highland
* SW Scotland, Lothian Borders: Dumfries and Galloway, Scottish Borders
* Strathclyde: Argyll and Bute, East Ayrshire, East Dunbartonshire, East Renfrewshire, Glasgow, Inverclyde, North Ayrshire, North Lanarkshire, Renfrewshire, South Ayrshire, South Lanarkshire, West Dunbartonshire

**Warning 2: 12pm Monday 30 November – 11.59pm Tuesday 1 December**

* North West England: Cumbria
* Wales: Blaenau Gwent, Bridgend, Caerphilly, Carmarthenshire, Conwy, Gwynedd, Merthyr Tydfil, Neath Port Talbot, Powys, Rhondda Cynon Taf, Torfaen

## UK five-day weather forecast

#### Saturday

Rain and mountain snow clearing eastwards, leaving scattered blustery showers in the West, locally heavy with a small chance of thunder near coasts. Gales in the far north-east soon easing.

#### Sunday

Cloud and rain clearing south-east England, followed by sunshine and showers, mostly in the North and West. Some showers may be heavy with hail or thunder. Breezy with average temperatures.

#### Outlook for Monday to Wednesday

Rain quickly moving across the UK on Monday with strong winds, turning heavy in the North and West through to Tuesday. Very windy at times, with coastal gales.
