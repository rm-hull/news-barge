Voting in person or by postal ballot: what you need to know

Bertin Huynh

More people will be voting in this election than ever before and almost half have already voted. But if you haven’t already voted, here’s what you need to know:

Polling places open from 8am to 6pm on Saturday 28 November. If you’re still in line at 6pm you will still be able to vote.

Legislative council – the upper house – voting has changed. Now voters must number at least five party groups above the line, in order of preference, or at least five individual candidates below the line. Watch our explainer below:

Victoria election: the upper-house vote explained – video

For postal votes, your ballot papers must be sent before 6pm on Saturday 28 November. The Victorian Electoral Commission must receive it by Friday 4 December for it count.

Federal politicians largely absent from state election

Just following on from the last post, it is notable that the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, did not do a press conference with the state premier, Ben Carroll, during the campaign, and the federal Liberal leader, Angus Taylor, did just one press conference with his state counterpart, Jess Wilson, during the campaign.

We’ve seen more of the One Nation leader, Pauline Hanson, than the two majors.

That could change soon for the Liberals at least. We have heard that Taylor is out on the hustings in Hastings today, and he posted a video on Facebook simply titled “Jess We Can” as he puts on a blue Liberal shirt to campaign today.

Hastings is currently held by Labor MP Paul Mercurio (of Strictly Ballroom fame) but it is expected to be a contest between the Liberals and One Nation.

The Victorian Liberal senator Jane Hume, meanwhile, is out campaigning with candidate Amelia Hamer in Malvern today.

Hamer attempted to unseat independent MP Monique Ryan at the last federal election.

Bowen says election has been ‘a tough one for Labor’

The federal climate minister, Chris Bowen, was asked at a doorstop in Fairfield, NSW, this morning on what he’s expecting from the Victorian state election.

He said Labor went into the election as the underdog and it was going to be a “very, very tough night for the Labor party in all likelihood”.

He said:

I give a shoutout to all those Labor party workers who are handing out how-to-votes, the candidates who are working their guts out, but they know that it’s going to be very, very tough.

And we know that this election campaign has been dominated by really the sorts of issues that you get when a government’s been in for more than 10 years.

At the state level, it’s very, very uncommon for a state government to be re-elected after more than 10 years. You’ve had issues of crime, issues of debt, issues of the of corruption in the building industry dominate this campaign.

He sent his best wishes to the premier, Ben Carroll, and his team but said it would be a tough night given the issues that dominated the campaign.

‘People are absolutely ready for change’, One Nation Victorian leader says

One Nation’s Victorian leader, Warren Pickering, was visiting voting booths with the federal leader, Pauline Hanson, this morning, and told reporters he was feeling “really good” about the party’s prospects today.

He said:

Surprisingly, I think it’s a consequence of knowing that we’ve put our best foot forward. We’ve left nothing on the table. There’s nothing left in the tank. So, you know, now it’s up to the people of Victoria.

Pickering said One Nation was looking at potentially 30 three-way contested seats across the states but acknowledged preferences “won’t be kind to us”.

We are definitely not taking anything for granted. Things feel good. People are absolutely ready for change. As to whether they want to vote to achieve it by voting differently remains to be seen, but we’re hopeful.

Liberals’ sewage plan ‘incredibly deceptive and dishonest, Greens leader says

Greens leader Ellen Sandell said the Liberals had been “incredibly deceptive and dishonest” in not revealing the plans to lease Melbourne’s sewage plants until two days before the election after nearly half of Victorians had already voted.

She told ABC Weekend Breakfast:

There’s a reason that they hid this plan until nearly half of Victorians had already voted, because they know it’s a terrible plan that people would not support.

And if they were honest with the Victorian people earlier about their plans to sell off all our assets from under our feet, including our sewerage, I do not think that the Victorian community would support it.

And so I think if Jess Wilson does end up having a victory tonight, it won’t be an endorsement of her policies.

It’ll be a rejection of Labor and a rejection of the two party system that just isn’t working for people.

The Victorian Greens leader, Ellen Sandell, told ABC’s Weekend Breakfast the Greens are competitive in seats Labor has held for 99 years, showing the “Greens surge is coming” amid disaffection with Labor.

She said:

What people are telling me is that they are just so dismayed and disappointed at how Labor has moved away from their traditional values.

They have really followed the Liberals and One Nation down a rightwing rabbit-hole, and people are sick of that.

They don’t want politicians that just keep taking donations from big corporations and then giving handouts to the gas industry or giving in to the gambling industry, or rolling out the red carpet for datacentres like Labor has done.

And that’s why they’re telling me that they’re voting Greens because they actually want a progressive voice that’s going to stand up for the values that this community cares about.

Jess Wilson and Ben Carroll ‘confident’ as they cast votes

My colleague Benita Kolovos has informed me that the premier, Ben Carroll, voted at his old primary school St Christophers in Airport West, while the Liberal leader, Jess Wilson, voted at Our Lady of Good Counsel primary school in Deepdene.

Wilson told a reporter at the polling booth she was “confident of the victories statewide today” and downplayed what it would mean for her to become the first elected female premier in Victoria.

She said:

It’s not about me. If I’m given that honour, we’re absolutely ready and I’m ready. But this is about Victorians and fixing this state.

Carroll told reporters he was “fighting every day” for the state. He said:

I am confident because I’m getting that message. I’ve got it all day yesterday. People do feel like they’ve been sold down the river by the Liberal party, after 50% of Victorians voted, to come out with a radical privatisation agenda around kinder, renewables and water. People do feel very angry about that.

Victorian premier Ben Carroll and his family on election day

Going into the election, the Labor government is led by the premier, Ben Carroll. Carroll, who has only led Labor since late July, became leader after a spill against Jacinta Allan. Allan herself only became leader after the resignation of Daniel Andrews, who led Labor to the “Dan-slide” of 2018.

Victorian Labor has never won an election after changing leaders and this time they have done it twice. But Carroll has made an effort to distance himself from his predecessors saying, “I will do things differently”.

The Victorian premier, Ben Carroll, outside the Olivia Newton-John Cancer Research Institute in Heidleberg, in Melbourne, on Tuesday. Photograph: James Ross/AAP

Jess Wilson is the Liberal leader, hoping to become the next premier. She became leader after a period of instability in the Victorian Liberal party that saw three leaders since the last election.

If she wins, she will be the youngest person to lead Victoria and the first woman to be elected premier. (Both Joan Kirner and Jacinta Allan became premier after their leaders resigned).

There’s also the minor parties.

The Victorian Greens are led by Ellen Sandell. In 2014, she became the first Greens MP to be elected to Victoria’s lower house after campaigning against local toll roads.

The Victorian Greens leader, Ellen Sandell, in Melbourne. Photograph: Con Chronis/AAP

Leading the insurgent One Nation is Warren Pickering. He is running in the marginal Labor seat of Pakenham in Melbourne’s outer east. The former Australian army combat engineer was also a unsuccessful One Nation Senate candidate.

At the News24 leaders’ debate, Pickering was outperformed by Sandell but audiences still preferred him over Carroll.

The One Nation Victoria leader, Warren Pickering. Photograph: Joel Carrett/AAP

The Coalition is poised to win 55 of 88 lower-house seats according to a YouGov poll of more than 4300 voters, commissioned by the Herald Sun and News24 and published on election eve.

One Nation would gain 12 seats and the Greens would add five more MPs to their pack of three, according to the analysis conducted between 6 and 23 November.

A Newspoll reported by The Australian late on Thursday had the Coalition extending to a 15-point lead over Labor on a two-party preferred basis.

The Coalition’s lead of 57.5% to Labor’s 42.5% compares with the Coalition’s 55-45 lead in October before the election campaign began.

If the polls are correct the Victorian Greens will have their most successful campaign on record, gaining between 13% and 17% of primary votes.

The party leader, Ellen Sandell, received the second-highest approval rating among undecided voters during the campaign’s only four-way leaders debate on 17 November.
