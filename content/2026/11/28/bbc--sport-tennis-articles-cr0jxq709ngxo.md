Alcaraz sends Spain to the final with dominant win against Fery

**Great Britain were unable to give Leon Smith the perfect send-off as Davis Cup captain as Carlos Alcaraz inspired Spain to a 2-0 victory in the semi-finals.**

Competing in their first Davis Cup semi-final since 2019, Britain fell short as Arthur Fery was unable to match the dazzling brilliance of Alcaraz, with the seven-time major winner surging to an emphatic 6-2 6-1 win.

That was after rising star Rafael Jodar defeated Cameron Norrie 6-2 7-5 in the opening singles match to put Spain in the driving seat in Bologna.

The defeat brings the curtain down on Smith's near 17-year tenure as captain, during which he led Britain to Davis Cup success in 2015.

Spain captain David Ferrer, who is also stepping down after this year's tournament, will have the chance to guide his nation to their first Davis Cup title since 2019 when they face Italy in Sunday's final.

Italy are the three-time defending champions, although they are without talisman and world number two Jannik Sinner because of injury.

Norrie losses to Jodar as Spain take lead

## Alcaraz back to brilliant best against Fery

After Norrie's defeat by Jodar, Fery was faced with the mammoth task of beating world number three Alcaraz - a player he had never faced before.

Alcaraz lifted his first ATP Finals title six days earlier, a triumph that completed his astonishing return to the sport following four months out with a wrist injury.

And his return to top form was on full display as he dismantled Fery, ranked 32nd, in a dominant first set in which he dropped just two points behind his first serve.

Alcaraz's movement and shot-making continued to dazzle the crowd in Italy and Fery could do little to stop the Spaniard's sprint to the finish line.

Alcaraz started the season by becoming the youngest man to complete the career Grand Slam with his Australian Open triumph, and now has the chance to end the year with his first Davis Cup title, one of the few trophies he has yet to win.

For Fery, who enjoyed his career-best season and a fairytale run to the Wimbledon semi-finals, it was another harsh reminder of the gulf in class between himself and the game's top players after he fell in straight sets to world number one Alexander Zverev in Thursday's quarter-final.
