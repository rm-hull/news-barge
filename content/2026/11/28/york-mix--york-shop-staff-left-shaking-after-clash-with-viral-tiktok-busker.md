Staff at an independent York shop say they were left shaking and scared to come to work after a dispute with a viral TikTok pianist triggered a wave of online abuse.

Workers at Fabrication on Stonegate said they were subjected to intimidating behaviour, more than 500 one-star Google reviews, and threatening livestream comments following a clash with James Tofalli, known online as PianoManOfficial.

James, who is cycling a full-sized upright piano from Land’s End to John o’Groats, performed outside the shop and livestreamed the encounter to hundreds of viewers.

His US-based business manager, known as Yorkie, defended his right to busk on the street and accused the shop of subjecting him to “mental warfare” by placing a speaker in an upstairs window and repeatedly turning music on and off to disrupt his performance.

Their full response is below.

However, Fabrication CEO Dawn Clark and duty manager Jo Dale said the backlash left their team fearful, with staff walking to and from work in groups for safety.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-fabrication-piano-man-4.webp)

“Our staff were quite frankly terrified,” Dawn told ***YorkMix***. “It was appalling watching the comments on the livestream of people threatening to come and smash our windows, telling him to wait and stay and meet and confront the people leaving the shop at the end of the day.

“It was awful.”

Dawn said someone found her private phone number and called her repeatedly, forcing her to put her phone on silent.

She added that she cried reading internal staff impact statements where people “were saying my place of work doesn’t feel safe any more.

“And people have been scared to come in this morning to work.”

Jo, who was working in the shop during the incident, said she was booed by people outside when she walked near the doorway. “It was incredibly stressful,” she said. “I was shaking.

“I haven’t eaten all day. I didn’t sleep. Staff are frightened to do their shifts next week because of the impact of one selfish, arrogant person.”

Fabrication sells the crafts of around 60 local artists and makers. Like James himself, many of those involved with the shop are neurodivergent.

Regarding the shop playing music, Dawn said they were playing pieces by a local musician before he arrived – a practice they also used for a window animation last Christmas.

She said that Stonegate is a designated busker-free zone and claimed the crowds gathering around James’s cargo bike blocked their shopfront, costing the business £60,000 in lost trade last Christmas and £10,000 so far this week.

She said Google has now taken down most of the one-star reviews.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/james-tofalli-busking.webp)

In response James and Yorkie strongly disputed the shop’s account, and said they were unhappy with how local businesses and authorities have treated him.

Yorkie told ***YorkMix*** that James follows all City of York Council busking regulations, does not block doorways, and is legally entitled to perform on Stonegate.

She said Fabrication had never played music outside before and accused staff of targeting him.

“They would consistently turn that music off, give him five minutes to where he thinks he’d get to play, and then as soon as he started playing, they’d turn it back on, and then they’d laugh at him,” Yorkie said.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-fabrication-piano-man-3.webp)

“It was like mental warfare, like they were trying to break him.”

She added that James, who is neurodivergent and uses his journey to raise awareness for mental health, had been issued a public order warning in York over causing an obstruction, which they have formally challenged.

“At what point is somebody going to believe the busker?” Yorkie said. “Why is it always that the business is believed?”

James said he stayed livestreaming in the cold for six-and-a-half hours to document the shop’s behaviour, insisting he actively discouraged viewers from leaving negative reviews or entering the store.

He also questioned why the shop did not work with him to benefit from his large social media following.

“There’s ways that you can capitalise on people like me, and there’s ways that people like me can mess up your business,” James said.

“I don’t want to do it. I hate doing it. It’s the only protection I’ve got against being pushed on and bullied by businesses that have absolutely no right to do it.”

He said he would welcome a meeting with the staff at Fabrication and council officials.
