A North Yorkshire councillor has quit the Conservative group and resigned his party membership following a dispute over home-to-school transport policy.

Cllr Clive Pearson, who represents the Esk Valley and Coast division on North Yorkshire Council (NYC), said he had decided to become an independent councillor following a row with the Conservative group over a recent motion relating to school transport arrangements.

He recently voted in favour of a motion by the Liberal Democrats to restore free school transport eligibility based on school catchment areas rather than the new policy of providing transport to a child’s nearest eligible school.

However, at a full meeting of the Conservative-led council last week, the proposal was narrowly rejected by three votes.

Speaking to the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS), Cllr Pearson described how the policy was affecting families in his division.

He said: “There’s a family with three kids and two have been going to Whitby and the other has been told they have to go to Guisborough.

“Not only is the school losing funding, but you’ve got another bus having to pick that schoolchild up and take them to school.”

Mr Pearson said he believed local representatives should be free to vote in line with the wishes of their residents.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/north-yorkshire-council-leader-cllr-carl-les.jpg)

He said: “I don’t think any councillor should be put in that position where they are being whipped to vote.

“It should be, right, OK, it’s your area, you vote to your heart and vote what people want.”

Asked whether he felt voting differently would have let down constituents, he said: “Oh God, would I? Absolutely. They would have been fuming.”

Cllr Pearson said he had already been considering standing as an independent at next May’s local elections because of boundary changes affecting his area.

He said he hoped to contest the Danby and Mulgrave division following the retirement of current Conservative councillor David Chance.

“The only way I could do it was at the next election, to go independent,” he said.

However, he said the disagreement over school transport had accelerated his departure from the Conservative group.

The councillor, who has served on the authority for several years, said he was disappointed to be leaving.

“I’ve had a lot of years with the group and worked well with them,” he said.

“But on this one issue, I’ve got to stick up for what I think is right.”

Cllr Pearson also confirmed he would be resigning his Conservative Party membership.

However, Conservative group leader Carl Les said the disagreement was rooted in Cllr Pearson’s intention to stand against a Conservative candidate at next year’s election rather than solely the school transport vote.

Cllr Les, who is leader of North Yorkshire Council, said Cllr Pearson was “being selective in his account”.

He added: “I was told that Cllr Pearson indicated that he would stand at the next election as an independent as he did not like the seat he had been selected for, even though he took part in the selection process.

“He asked me if he could remain a member of the Conservative group until the election.

“I told him that the group might accept that if he continued to support group policies. When asked if he would support issues at the last council meeting, he said he would not because ‘it would damage his election prospects’.

“I said that as he was saying he would be standing against a Conservative candidate, the group would be unhappy with him remaining. No action has been taken about him to date.”

Speaking to the LDRS, Cllr Les added: “We have a monthly group meeting next Wednesday where this was to be discussed, but if you are correct and he has resigned from the Conservative Party then he cannot hold positions that are allocated to Conservative members.

“Standing against a nominated and selected candidate is against the rules of membership of the party.”

Mr Pearson said he intended to stand as an independent candidate at the next election and believed he would receive strong local support.

He said: “Within the Conservative group [there are] mixed feelings, but on the whole very supportive. The other groups have been very supportive, but more so the public.”
