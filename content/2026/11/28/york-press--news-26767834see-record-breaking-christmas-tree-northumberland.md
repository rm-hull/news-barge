A National Trust site with “festive fun waiting around every corner” and a record-breaking Christmas tree could be the ideal activity to keep the family entertained over the festive period.

If you are looking for a family-friendly and Christmassy spot in the North East to visit, you might want to try Cragside.

The National Trust site in Northumberland has a range of activities available, from walks to Christmassy decorations to see to get you in a festive mood.

## What is Christmas at Cragside?

There are lots of events happening at Cragside over the festive period, including A Journey Through Fairyland.

This activity is ideal for children and will take you around the different rooms in Cragside that have been decorated for Christmas.

After exploring the house, it is worth visiting the record-breaking Christmas tree.

Cragside is home to the world’s tallest bedded Christmas tree that has been covered in more than 1,300 lights.

The Giant Redwood tree is 44.7 metres tall, making it more than twice the height of the Angel of the North.

The area is also great for a long winter walk with the festive five-miler that will take you through the grounds.

You will discover “rugged trails, sparkling lakes, and towering trees” on the walk that takes around three hours to complete.

To keep children entertained, you might want to do the Escape from Fairyland trail, where kids will need to help the stranded William Armstrong.

Children will need to search the Formal Garden for supplies to help William get back for Christmas.

After exploring the grounds, you can head to the on-site tea room for a hot chocolate to warm you up alongside a slice of cinnamon-scented cake.

If you need something a bit more substantial, there is also a festive turkey sandwich with all of the trimmings.

To get some Christmas shopping done during your visit, there is a shop at the visitor centre packed with homeware, jewellery, books, and toys.

Christmas at Cragside will be running from Saturday, November 28, to Sunday, January 3, with the site being closed on Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and Boxing Day.

National Trust members can visit for free, with tickets for non-members costing £18 for adults and £9 for children aged five to 17.
