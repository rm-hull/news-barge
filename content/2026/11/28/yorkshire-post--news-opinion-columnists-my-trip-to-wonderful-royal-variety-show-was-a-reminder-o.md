# My trip to wonderful Royal Variety Show was a reminder of wise advice to seize the day: Christa Ackroyd

## In the words of The Greatest Showman it was indeed everything you ever need. I don’t really have a bucket list. I prefer to just accept that every day will bring opportunities which, if you grasp things you never could have imagined doing, places you never even dreamed of seeing or even people who didn’t know you had yet to meet, are often the most unexpectedly joyous addition to our lives.

I feel to write them down on a list of all you want to do in life literally before you kick the bucket takes away the fun and joy of the spontaneous. And spontaneous is how I plan to roll from here on in. Sometimes life’s biggest surprises come from the unexpected and those times we have looked forward to, planned for and anticipated with great excitement are often not quite as we imagined them to be. Dare I say it, a bit of a let down. And those we were dreading turn out to be the best of times.

How many times have I thought I wish I didn’t have to drag myself out, slap on my make up and a smile only to find that the smile becomes laughter and the memory bank is replenished. Last weekend was one of those times. Not that I didn’t want to travel to London with a friend but it was always going to be a rush I could do without. Or so it seemed.

Over the past few weeks I have had the sad task of clearing out the last items from my closest friend’s home, a house where we shared so many happy happy times. Memories came flooding back of cold collations – as she called a buffet – kitchen table suppers as her partner the late great Richard Whiteley called something rustled up for friends and family without fuss or formality.

For almost three decades we had the best of times in that house and just in case the new owners happen to be reading this I wish them all the happiness that I know is still embedded in the walls. But it was sad to shut the door and walk away.

Sad to see each room stripped bare of the things she loved. And boy, like me, did she love things. Some I had bought her, the little poem about friendship which I took with me. Some were momentos of times we had shared, such as the engraved shot glass from my daughter’s wedding and the best photo we ever had taken of us screaming on the roller coaster at Tivoli Gardens in Copenhagen, her with fear (she didn’t do heights though it was the tamest of rides) and me with laughter. And others well I just had to say goodbye to. The bedroom where I slept. The terrace where we celebrated birthdays and good times and the gardens filled with fruit trees that provided myself and half the village with apples for pies and plums for jam.

But you know what they say, you can’t take it with you, and really all we leave behind are memories. So, in her honour I accepted a trip the following day to an event I am pretty sure she had been to (Kathy and Richard had invitations to all the best happenings in town), the Royal Variety Show in London.

I did so because she would have wanted me to, especially as it was a celebration of musical theatre. She and Richard would have loved it. Not only did they travel the world watching musicals, but Kathy and I made our annual trip to the Christmas production at the Crucible in Sheffield. She would have loved too that the invite came from a friend she never got to meet, one whom I only bumped into three years ago and a positive joyful person whom Kathy was insistent had been sent to fill my life with fun and laughter, as she had done, after she was gone. She was right about that too.

What an evening it was. From the reception team who applauded our choice of outfits (we had made an effort), to the doorman at our hotel who hailed us a cab complimenting us on our attire, to the couple stood there in their glad rags who insisted they pay for it. The maitre’d in the restaurant at the Royal Albert Hall who saved us a table for the interval and even though we hadn’t planned to eat produced a plate of cheese and biscuits ‘for the nice ladies who smiled a lot’.

It was like it used to be. Everyone in their finest, the men in their evening suits and the women in long dresses sparkly and bright. The show was more than I ever could have dreamed of. Seated slap bang centre stage it began with the loudest of cheers for their Majesties the King and Queen and Alfie Boe singing the national anthem which raised not only the roof but the hairs on the back of your neck right through to Andrea Bocelli reminding it was Time to Say Goodbye. And everything in between.

The preview of the new Greatest Showman musical complete with fireworks around the auditorium, the cast of the new Billy Elliot singing Electricity, the wondrous Idina Menzel the original Elphaba singing from Wicked accompanied by the man who composed it, the cast from Operation Mincemeat with the Royal Marines buglers and drummers and Sam Ryder with Jesus Christ Superstar to name but a few. There was not a dry eye in the house when the winners of Britain’s Got Talent took to the stage, the magnificent Hawkestone Farmers Choir performing This is Home, a reminder of the generations they represent who toil the land and tend the countryside for all of us.

It was quite simply the best evening I have had in ages. And capped off when we saw what was a queue for the taxis was in fact hundreds of people standing in the freezing cold outside waiting to cheer their Majesties on their way. Who says the best of our traditions have died ?

And to think I almost said I was too busy to go. A quick dash up North to host a charity event for Happy Days in Halifax who tend the homeless and the vulnerable, £95,000 raised by the most generous of guests, and don’t forget the Royal Variety is also a charitable event to support those who served us in the theatre and on our screens and I am left with a sense that whatever it is we think we have lost, whatever we think is the downturn in generosity of spirit and showing the very best of British it is very much alive and kicking.

So forget the idiots such as those who thought it fun to play sex noises during the Blackpool edition of Strictly, despise those who would do us harm by releasing whatever substances forced concert goers in Manchester to leave their seats coughing and spluttering and concentrate instead on the best of people. And the best of times.

Watch the documentary that the Princess of Wales took part in on her Three Peaks climb and listen to the words of gratitude she has that she is a cancer survivor. Thank Wayne Rooney and Tyson Fury for opening up about male depression and mental health issues and ignore those who insist this country is going to the dogs. Take pleasure in the simple things and grasp every opportunity going for you and yes put on your gladrags and celebrate. We know times are tough for so many so dig deeper where you can and as my lovely friend would say Carpe Diem, seize the day. Say yes instead of no and above all be thankful for all we have, including friends, family and opportunities simple or extravagant. And remember as the song which rang out at the Royal Albert Hall this week says, that really is everything you ever need.
