The campaign groups attempting to keep Britain out of the EU are splintering into political factions after a leading Labour MP confirmed that a separate “progressive” pro-Brexit campaign is set to be launched - rejecting an alliance with Tories.

Veteran leftwing Labour MP Jon Trickett told *The Independent*that he was asked to get involved with a cross party campaign group involving the Tories but “took an hour” to decide he would not get involved.* The Independent* understands that No2EU is to be launched involving senior Conservatives and Blue Labour, founded by peer Maurice Glasman - allied to home secretary Shabana Mahmood, from the right of the party.

But instead Mr Trickett, an MP for 31 years and former ally of Jeremy Corbyn from the Tony Benn tradition of opposing EU membership, said that he would launch a version of Labour Leave which campaigned in the 2016 referendum.

![Labour's Jon Trickett will launch a Labour pro-Brexit campaign](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2018/07/30/14/gettyimages-681884806.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It means that there are set to be at least four rival groups campaigning for the Brexit status quo with a Tory-focused Stay Out launched by Lord Michael Gove and Keep Britain Out launched by Nigel Farage and his long term ally, businessman Arron Banks already campaigning.

Mr Trickett told *The Independent*: “We will be launching a proper progressive anti-EU group which will involve the trade unions.

“We don’t have a name for it yet but at the moment we are working around Labour Safeguards, the name from the campaign group in the 1973 referendum.”

He said the group will “be in the tradition of Labour giants like Barbara Castle, Peter Shore, Michael Foot, Tony Benn and Aneurin Bevan.”

And he questioned why prime minister Andy Burnham is so keen to join the EU.

![Andy Burnham has strongly hinted he wants to rejoin the EU](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/12/2306065823..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“How can the prime minister say he's going to destroy neoliberalism and then he wants to join a neoliberal institution like that? It’s completely ridiculous.”

Mr Trickett believes that the protectionism of the EU is a core progressive reason to keep the UK out of it.

“The European Union is tens of millions of white Christians exploiting Africa and the rest of the world. In no way does it represent progressive values.

“So at the moment we're speaking to trade unionists, labour activists, labour MPs, and others, and we will launch a campaign.”

The launch of the two groups is a serious challenge to Mr Burnham’s authority early into his premiership after taking over from Keir Starmer in July when the former prime minister was ousted.

MPs on the left of the party were particularly influential in bringing Mr Burnham in but have been kept out of the government since he arrived.

The vast majority of Labour MPs though support taking the UK back into either the EU as a whole or the customs union or single market.

Mr Burnham has described Brexit as “divisive and responsible for low growth”.

![Farage and Arron Banks launched a Reform focussed pro-Brexit campaign](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/12/01M89XQGH3X921664DJBSAE6DQ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He is set to launch a national conversation with his 10-year plan to look at the four options of the status quo, joining the customs union or single market or the EU altogether.

The prime minister has already rejected the advice of five of his predecessors including Lord David Cameron, Baroness Theresa May, Boris Johnson, Liz Truss and Rishi Sunak not to reopen the debate.

And already the Get In campaign has been launched as a united umbrella group for a rejoin movement involving the European Movement UK and Best for Britain.

On Friday, Scottish secretary Douglas Alexander, who has to deal with a pro-EU SNP government in Holyrood, became the first cabinet minister to make a speech demanding an end to the status quo on Brexit pointing to one of the three other options.

But Mr Trickett warned that reopening the Brexit debate is “a very bad mistake”.

He said: “It's misjudged. It was divided. It'll be it'll be very bitter. It'll become poisonous again, like it was last time, and it will not resolve the country's economic problems.”

Mr Trickett argued that it did not matter that there are different campaign groups and suggested there could be low level cooperation.

“We can work out schedules, make sure that we're not making big statements on the same day. There's an argument for that.”

But he noted that Labour’s reputation was tarnished in Scotland when the party got together with the Tories and Lib Dems to fight against Scottish independence.

He said: “I was getting the phone call and text last night from quite senior Tories, and I thought about it for an hour, and I thought actually, 31 years in here, and thought about my reputation.”
