**Harrogate and Knaresborough’s Liberal Democrat MP calls for focused health measures following concerns over widening health inequalities between northern and southern England.**

Tom Gordon, the Liberal Democrat Member of Parliament for Harrogate and Knaresborough, has called for the introduction of regional health targets aimed at addressing the widening health disparities between northern and southern England. Speaking during a session with Layla Moran MP, Chair of the Health Select Committee, Gordon highlighted the persistent and growing gap in health outcomes, including life expectancy, faced by regions such as Yorkshire and Humber compared to the South East.

The concerns were raised in the context of a recent Health and Social Care Select Committee report on obesity, which drew on evidence from Diabetes UK and Yorkshire Cancer Research. The committee presented its findings in Parliament this week, spotlighting the urgent need to tackle the patchwork of health services across the country.

Layla Moran agreed with Mr Gordon’s observations, emphasising the necessity of making the reduction of health inequalities a central objective of government health policy. She described this as a “core mission” to ensure uniform progress across all regions.

> **Tom Gordon:**  
> North-South health inequalities are widening and we won’t stand for it. We have to make sure progress is equal across our regions.  
> The work of regional charities like Yorkshire Cancer Research is vital in making sure we have equal progress across the country. I’m proud to have worked with them and see their work making a big impact.  
> Focused regional sub-targets are a vital part of that puzzle, making sure the numbers aren’t obscured by a bias towards the South East.

The introduction of such regional sub-targets seeks to provide a clearer picture of health outcomes by area, rather than relying solely on national averages that may mask local disparities. This approach aims to better inform policy decisions and resource allocation to benefit underserved communities in the North.
