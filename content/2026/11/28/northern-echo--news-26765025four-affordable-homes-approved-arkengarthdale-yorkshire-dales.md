Plans to build four affordable homes in Arkengarthdale have been unanimously approved by Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority, despite objections from the parish council and concerns raised by a neighbouring resident.

Members backed the long-running scheme at Langthwaite, which one councillor described as a "chance of a lifetime" for the dale.

The development, proposed by the Upper Dales Community Land Trust, will see four homes built on agricultural land between the former Methodist Chapel and the village war memorial.

The two two-bedroom and two three-bedroom houses will be available for social rent and managed by North Star Housing Association.

The authority agreed to approve the scheme subject to a legal agreement in 2018.

However, the application remained undetermined while the land purchase was completed and revised designs were prepared.

Members again backed the proposals in July this year, but the decision was not formally issued before a complaint was received from a neighbouring resident.

The resident raised concerns about the impact of the development on natural light, privacy, noise, wildlife and the setting of the former Methodist Chapel and nearby war memorial.

Planning officers accepted that the resident's earlier comments should have been included in the previous committee report.

However, after reassessing the concerns, they maintained their recommendation for approval, with additional conditions covering noise from air source heat pumps, landscaping and construction work.

Arkengarthdale Parish Council also objected to the scheme, questioning whether there was sufficient demand for affordable housing in the dale and highlighting its limited public transport, services and employment opportunities.

The parish council argued that the housing needs survey covered a much wider area, including Swaledale, and did not demonstrate a specific need for four homes in Arkengarthdale.

However, North Yorkshire Council's housing team supported the development, pointing to a survey identifying a need for 17 affordable homes across Arkengarthdale and neighbouring parishes.

Planning officers said opportunities to deliver affordable housing in the northern dales were limited and that the benefits of the development outweighed the minor harm to the landscape.

Speaking at this week's meeting, committee member Allen Kirkbride said: "This is a chance of a decade or of a lifetime for Arkengarthdale for this sort of development.

"It is going to be rented development for local people, and therefore I think we should give it our full support."

Member Richard Foster added: "The reality is that this is giving four families the opportunity to live and/or work in that area, which is surely a good thing.
