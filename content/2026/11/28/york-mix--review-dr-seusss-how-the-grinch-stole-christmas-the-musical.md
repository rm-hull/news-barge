Leeds paints the town green this Christmas as Dr Seuss’s festive classic, *How The Grinch Stole Christmas! The Musical*, brings storybook magic to the stage.

And the magic begins before you even take to your seat. As you enter the beautiful Leeds Playhouse, illustrations and thoughtful design choices immediately transport you into the world of Whoville, setting the scene for the festive adventure ahead and adding to the already welcoming atmosphere of this much-loved venue.

Timothy Mason and Mel Marvin’s adaptation of this classic is a sure-hit with audiences of all ages, especially children. From the very first burst of mischief, to the last flake of snowfall, the music and lyrics capture the whimsical wonder of the original tale while bringing plenty of fun, energy and theatrical flair to this stage adaptation. It has all the charm of the original story, with just enough extra sparkle to make it a festive treat for the whole family.

This is thanks in no small part to the hard work of set and costume designer Simon Higlett. Eccentric props, clever blink-and-you’ll-miss-it moments, larger-than-life, bright and pointy-nosed Whos; every detail adds another layer to the magic. There’s a real sense of imagination at play, with plenty to catch the eye.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/The-company-of-Dr-Seusss-How-the-Grinch-Stole-Christmas-The-Musical.-Photo-by-Johan-Persson-14.webp)

*Dr Seuss’s How the Grinch Stole Christmas! The Musical*

Alongside Jai Morjaria’s bold lighting design, we’re transported into a world that feels familiar yet still manages to impress; a revolving playground of wonder for director Tom Wright to work his magic.

And work his magic he certainly does, making sure every inch of the set is put to good use through his thoughtful direction. From great snowy heights to the much-loved audience interactions, there’s always something happening to keep those little eyes firmly fixed on the stage.

A cast bursting with energy ensures the Christmas spirit is felt throughout, even with the grumpy Grinch in attendance and the late November chill in the air. It’s a production that never really sits still, and quite rightly so.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Joel-Montague-The-Grinch-in-Dr-Seusss-How-the-Grinch-Stole-Christmas-The-Musical.-Photo-by-Johan-Persson.webp)

You’re a mean one, Mr Montague! This seasoned West End professional makes an eerily charming Grinch, strutting around the stage with enough character to rival Jim Carrey himself. He’s playful and silly, delivering all the mischief and misanthropy you’d expect from our green-fingered Christmas antagonist. Yet somehow, he still manages to receive a warm reception, even on his first grand entrance – a Christmas wish only the best pantomime villain could hope for!

I particularly enjoyed his relationship with Max, the lovable dog portrayed by Wakefield’s own Jack Arnold, bringing a delightful good cop, bad cop energy to their scenes; their interactions are a real highlight.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Jack-Arnold-Young-Max-Joel-Montague-The-Grinch-Matt-Sutton-Old-Max-in-Dr-Seusss-How-the-Grinch-Stole-Christmas-The-Musical.-Photo-by-Johan-Persson.webp)

For those dog lovers out there, while we may not have had a four-legged furry friend present, we did have two Max’s! Matt Sutton portrays Older Max, our reflective narrator, bringing Seuss’s distinctive storytelling and rhymes to the forefront of the tale.

It’s a lovely way of keeping the original narrative alive, whilst also giving us another character to invest in along the way. The duet between old and young Max in ‘This Time of Year’ is another particular highlight, giving us the opportunity to appreciate their beautiful vocals.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Minnie-Day-Cindy-Lou-in-Dr-Seusss-How-the-Grinch-Stole-Christmas-The-Musical.-Photo-by-Johan-Persson.webp)

Of course, we must take a moment to highlight our junior ensemble (27 November cast). With our leading lady, Cindy Lou Who, played wonderfully by Minnie Day. Cute as a button, but with a beautifully mature quality to her portrayal, she commands the stage with confidence. For such a little lady, she certainly holds her own amongst the cast, bringing just the right amount of innocence and charm. Her solo, ‘Santa for the Day’, provides a lovely moment of stillness in an otherwise animated offering.

But with Tinovimbanashe Sibanda’s jazzy choreography and an expert band led by Livi van Warmelo, this production rarely halts and is undoubtedly a full team effort. The choreography brings an added burst of energy to the production, whilst the musicians provide the perfect accompaniment to a score that is every bit as playful and entertaining as the story itself: it’s a combination that keeps momentum.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/The-company-of-Dr-Seusss-How-the-Grinch-Stole-Christmas-The-Musical.-Photo-by-Johan-Persson.webp)

This is a production that certainly lives up to the high expectations of the Christmas classic, delivering a festive spectacle packed with colour, character and plenty of heart. And for those young children stepping into the theatre for the first time, there really is no better story to help their love for theatre grow. After all, if a grumpy old Grinch can discover the true meaning of Christmas and find his heart growing three sizes, who knows what a little theatrical magic might inspire?

Grab your ticket to the meanest, greenest, but possibly supreme-ist, show on offer this winter and step inside a tantalising tale that will certainly help restore your faith in Who-manity this season.
