## Amazon workers across the world joined global protests from Black Friday through to Monday to denounce working conditions, demand decent pay and collective agreements.

Workers from countries around the world, including France, Poland, the UK, the US, Japan, Brazil and South Africa, are staging a global strike in their Amazon workplaces demanding better wages and labour rights.

For the seventh consecutive Black Friday, Amazon warehouses, delivery stations and offices are set to run at a slow rate as 50 organisations — trade unions, environmental movements and tax justice campaigners — launch Make Amazon Pay strikes.

Strikes are staged from Black Friday to Cyber Monday in some countries, the busiest shopping period of the year for the retail giant.

In Germany, United Services Union (ver.di) called for strikes in 11 Amazon workplaces on Friday, while unions have managed to rally in at least 23 locations across India.

"Despite massive intimidation, Amazon employees are striking for recognition of the collective agreements for the retail sector and a collective agreement on good and healthy work," said the United Services Trade Union, reported by Sky News.

## Higher pay, collective agreements

Some demands have been refused for many years, like in Bangladesh, where workers have been demanding since the 2013 Rana Plaza disaster that killed more than a thousand workers that Amazon sign binding safety agreements.

Bangladeshi workers have also been protesting the inadequacy of the pay rate — €128 a month against the €220 their unions are demanding, according to the left-wing political group Progressive International organising the protest movement.

"Amazon is building a future in which fewer workers produce ever greater profits under ever tighter surveillance," Varsha Gandikota-Nellutla, coordinator of Progressive International, said in a statement.

"The fact is at Amazon we provide great pay, great benefits and great opportunities — all from day one," an Amazon spokesperson told Sky News.

"We directly employ more than 1.5 million people around the world, and provide a modern, safe and engaging workplace whether you work in an office or at one of our operations buildings," he said.

In Germany, 150 employees stopped working for 24 hours at their Amazon facility in Frankenthal to demand less performance pressure and join a collective agreement, EUobserver reports.

Despite these strikes being legal in most countries, some workers feel that such demonstrations will have consequences if they choose to join the strikes.

"We have had other demands for a million years, but they will hardly be listened to if we cannot strike," Magda Malinowska, from the Polish labour union Inicjatywa Pracownicza, told EUobserver.

"We want to make Amazon start paying for the costs linked with its revenues," she told EUobserver.

Amazon employs more than 230,000 people in Europe, mostly working on packing and delivering orders. In 2026, the firm already earned €678 billion, making it the world's second most important company after JPMorgan Chase, in terms of sales, profits and market value.
