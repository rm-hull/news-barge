**Plans to expand specialist places at Applefields School and Joseph Rowntree School aim to better support children and young people with Special Educational Needs and Disabilities in York.**

York City Council is set to consider proposals next month to invest over £4.5 million in expanding specialist education provision for children and young people with Special Educational Needs and/or Disabilities (SEND). The investment, if approved at the council’s Executive meeting on 1 December, would fund enhancements at two schools: Applefields School and Joseph Rowntree School.

Applefields School, which specialises in supporting young people with a wide range of learning needs, could see the addition of five new classrooms. This expansion is expected to increase its capacity for secondary school-age pupils by up to 50 places. Meanwhile, Joseph Rowntree School’s specialist autism base is proposed to be enlarged to offer 20 additional places to pupils with autism.

> **Cllr Bob Webb:**  
> This investment is rooted in our vision to support children, young people with SEND and their families in the city.  
> We need to make sure all our young people and their families feel they can access the right support at the right time, that they belong in their school or setting, and are able to shape the support available to them locally. Proposals to increase specialist support within the city are a key element of our Reform Plan.

The proposed development aims to enable children to remain within their local communities, which would reduce travel time and associated costs. Furthermore, it intends to ensure high-quality SEND provision is available within York. The enhancements should also improve educational outcomes by providing access to specialised environments, facilities, and therapeutic support tailored to individual needs. Greater inclusion and family engagement are expected to empower parents and carers to play a more active role in their child’s education and support planning.

If the investment is approved, funding will come from specialist SEND capital grants provided by the Department for Education along with dedicated national schools’ capital funding. The council has published additional information and illustrations related to the plans, which can be viewed on its official newsroom website.

This development is part of York City Council’s ongoing efforts to better support children and young people with SEND as part of their local Reform Plan.
