Councils and organisations across the North East are stepping up the fight against domestic abuse and gender-based violence.

Events, campaigns and community projects took place across the region to mark White Ribbon Day on Wednesday (November 25).

The date also marked the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women and the start of the 16 Days of Activism Against Gender-Based Violence.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775826.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Middlesbrough's White Ribbon event on Wednesday (November 25) *(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

In Middlesbrough, a White Ribbon event at the Town Hall brought together council representatives, specialist services, victims and survivors.

As part of the event, artists Claire Harrison and Beth Johnson invited attendees to contribute to a collaborative mosaic centred on healing, solidarity and collaboration.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775829.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke attends White Ribbon event on Wednesday (November 25) *(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke said: "As a council we are committed to doing everything within our power to stop violence against women and girls by changing harmful male cultures, challenging misogyny and promoting gender equality.

"We are working across all departments and with partner organisations to change culture and raise awareness."

Specialist local organisations My Sister's Place and the Halo Project also attended, along with Cleveland Police and Crime Commissioner Matt Storey.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775827.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Middlesbrough's White Ribbon event on Wednesday (November 25) *(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

Alongside the event, White Ribbon and Middlesbrough Council have devised a three-year action plan involving campaigns, training and events to create cultural change across council services and local communities.

![Cleveland PCC Matt Storey with White Ribbon UK](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775825.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cleveland PCC Matt Storey with White Ribbon UK *(Image: PCC Matt Storey)*

Landmarks across the North East were lit orange to mark the start of the 16 Days of Activism, the UN campaign’s colour of hope for a future without violence.

Newport Bridge and Stockton Riverside were among the landmarks lit up, while Stockton Council promoted its Domestic Abuse Strategy and “No Wrong Door” approach to support people affected by domestic abuse.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775823.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Stockton turns orange to mark the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women. *(Image: Stockton Borough Council)*

Redcar’s Beacon and Darlington’s council buildings were lit orange, while Darlington Soroptimists shared campaign information, planted orange tulip bulbs and distributed orange-ribboned flowers.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775824.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darlington Clock Tower illuminated orange to mark the International Day for the Elimination of Violence Against Women. *(Image: Darlington Borough Council)*

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service is also supporting White Ribbon by urging men to challenge inappropriate behaviour.

Health and safety officer Steven Linfoot said: "I believe it’s important for men to express their support of all women and girls in our workplaces, families and communities.

"It must be ensured that they always feel as respected, valued and safe as everyone should and men play a vital role in promoting this by displaying our support and calling out any inappropriate behaviour."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775822.jpg?type=mds-article-620)County Durham & Darlington Fire and Rescue Service Health and Safety Officer, Steven Linfoot, discusses White Ribbon day. *(Image: County Durham & Darlington Fire Service)*

Durham County Council is using the campaign to promote respect, equality and healthy relationships and direct people experiencing domestic abuse to support.

Further north, North Tyneside schools are using campaign resources to discuss healthy relationships, harmful stereotypes and everyday sexism.

Northumberland Domestic Abuse Services also held a free session on recognising domestic abuse and helping people who ask for support.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775833.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Redcar Beacon illuminated orange on Wednesday *(Image: Redcar and Cleveland Council)*

This year’s campaign coincides with new protections for domestic abuse victims across England and Wales.

Domestic Abuse Protection Notices and Orders, known as DAPNs and DAPOs, were rolled out nationally on Wednesday (November 25) after trials in selected areas, including Cleveland.

More than 3,000 victims were protected by DAPOs during the nationwide pilot.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21775828.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Women's Street Watch Middlesbrough join the region's White Ribbon event on Wednesday (November 25) *(Image: Middlesbrough Council)*

Introduced under the Domestic Abuse Act 2021, the measures give police and courts greater powers to protect people from coercive and controlling behaviour, stalking and economic abuse.

DAPOs can stop alleged abusers contacting victims or going near their homes, and decide who can stay in or return to a shared property.

They can also require electronic tagging or attendance at programmes tackling abusive behaviour, substance misuse or mental health problems.

Breaching the order would be a criminal offence.

The international campaign runs until Human Rights Day on Thursday, December 10, and calls for action to end violence against women and girls.

White Ribbon UK works with individuals, workplaces and communities across England and Wales to challenge attitudes and behaviour that can lead to harassment, abuse and violence.

The message from councils, services and charities across the North East was clear: "Together, we can take a stand against violence towards women and girls."
