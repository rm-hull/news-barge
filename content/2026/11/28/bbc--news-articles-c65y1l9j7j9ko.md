Charlie visits Poundland almost daily and buys everything from toiletries to sweets

**For Charlie Franklyn, a trip to Poundland is almost as routine as going to work.**

The care worker from Teesside pops into her local branch nearly every day – it's where she buys cleaning products, beauty bargains and the budget-friendly finds she loves sharing online.

Charlie has been creating social media content for five years, and Poundland is one of her regular filming spots.

Her videos showcasing the store and its wares have proved popular, and although she's never been paid by the retailer, she's been gifted products over the years and built relationships with staff who invite her in to film.

"For me, Poundland is more than just a shopping stop. I buy so many things from there that are much cheaper than in other shops."

## 'We don't need empty shops on the high street'

The budget retailer has gone into administration, putting 11,000 jobs at risk in the run-up to Christmas. For now, its 600 stores continue to trade as usual but they will eventually close if a buyer can't be found to rescue some or all of the business.

Charlie says she was "surprised by the news" and is worried about what it could mean for shoppers like her.

"With the cost-of-living crisis going on, places like Poundland are so important and we really don't need empty shops on the high street," she tells the BBC.

"I don't know what I'll do without it."

![Woman with pink and blue hair leaning against a shop selling Christmas decorations ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/010e/live/f7998b90-ea7e-11f1-ad70-ab41382bb405.jpg)

Kimi recently lost her job in retail and says she knows what a difficult time this must be for staff

Other shoppers the BBC spoke to are equally concerned. Kimi Browne, 26, from London, doesn't often shop at Poundland but relies on it for seasonal bargains, including Halloween decorations and accessories for her clothes.

She says the retailer is "an important part of the community as it offers affordable products for families".

Rea Catedrilla has a friend who works at the Poundland store in Notting Hill and feels "sad and sorry" for those who could lose their jobs.

She visits Poundland around four times per week because "it's less expensive than other shops and it's very convenient".

![Woman wearing a black coat and grey baseball cap stands on the side of a road with two cars behind her](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f8ba/live/12bebb50-ea7c-11f1-9358-491ec7031642.jpg)

Rea is worried for her friend who works at Poundland in Notting Hill

Poundland opened its first store in Burton-upon-Trent in 1990 and grew into one of the UK's best-known discount retailers, with 900 shops across the country at its peak.

But rising costs and competition from supermarkets such as Aldi and Lidl put pressure on the business and, in June 2025, it was sold to US investment firm Gordon Brothers for a nominal £1.

There had been signs of a recovery – Poundland made a profit of £18.1m in the year to September, compared with a £68m loss the previous year. But Gordon Brothers insists the business has missed its budget forecasts and would continue to struggle even if only its worst-performing stores were closed.

![Man standing in Poundland wearing a grey and blue fleece ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e840/live/4ffd43f0-e9b0-11f1-859a-37130cd3a1d7.jpg)

Aboud says locals and tourists are continuing to visit his store in Camden despite the news

News of the administration has left many Poundland staff on edge. Aboud, 39, who manages the Camden branch in north London, says it feels like a "slap in the face", as he thought the chain had started to recover over the last 18 months.

"Everything we've worked hard for was for nothing. Someone has come in and dismantled all that effort."

Aboud says his colleagues at the store have been emotional and that it's "very worrying to have a job at risk given the current climate".

But he adds that his team "are still hopeful" that a buyer could rescue the chain and that they continue to give "100% regardless of the circumstances".

## 'Yesterday's business'

![View of the exterior of stores in Union Street, Aberdeen. ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2e67/live/0a0d4c50-ea46-11f1-9fc4-ef897a334b75.jpg)

Poundland opened in 1990 and had around 900 stores in the UK at its peak in the late 2000s

Retail expert Mary Portas said that when Poundland first opened, it was "everything under one roof that you didn't even know you wanted or needed" but it failed to keep pace as the market evolved.

While inflation has made it harder to sustain the £1 pricing model, other discount retailers have also made value shopping a more appealing experience, she says.

"Value brands, instead of looking shabby, which quite frankly Poundland often did, have taken themselves into a much better place," she says.

"On so many levels, Poundland just felt like yesterday's business."

The company has now appointed consultancy AlixPartners to oversee a sale of part or all of the discount retailer. Administrators will decide whether to sell the business as a whole or break it up, with the latter considered the more likely outcome.

![Woman standing in a Poundland aisle wearing red glasses and Poundland uniform ](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2a1d/live/b0d4e8b0-e9bd-11f1-b0dc-c126790ce036.jpg)

Helen is hopeful that someone will buy Poundland and the stores will be saved

Helen Howes, Poundland's store manager in Camborne, Cornwall, says she wishes staff "could have been told more at times".

She adds that losing Poundland would be "devastating" for the town as it's one of its larger shops and an important draw for shoppers.

Jilly, who shops at the branch in Ealing, says the store has been a "godsend" and that it would be a blow to the local community if it closed.

Her shopping trolley is filled with everything from cleaning products and stationery to "upmarket things that I will gift for Christmas".

"It's tragic, everyone losing their jobs just before Christmas," she adds.
