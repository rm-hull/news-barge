## The latest deaths highlight the dangers of an increasingly busy migration route from Libya to southern Greece, as rising arrivals put mounting pressure on the country’s coastguard and migrant reception facilities.

Two migrants died and hundreds of others were rescued in a series of operations off the Greek islands of Crete and Gavdos, authorities said on Saturday, as Mediterranean crossings from North Africa continued to increase.

More than 1,100 migrants reached the two islands between Friday morning and Saturday afternoon, according to Greek coastguard figures reported by the Associated Press.

In one operation on Saturday, rescuers found two people unconscious and brought 41 others to safety in a rocky coastal area of Gavdos, a small island south of Crete.

The operation involved the European Union’s border agency Frontex and a private vessel. Four survivors were transferred to Crete for medical treatment, Greek newspaper Kathimerini reported.

The cause of the two deaths was not immediately clear.

In a separate operation, 102 migrants, including 16 women and five minors, were taken to the port of Heraklion, Crete’s largest city.

## **Increasingly dangerous migration route**

Crete and Gavdos have become increasingly prominent entry points into the EU for migrants travelling from North Africa, with many boats departing from eastern Libya.

More than 23,000 migrants had reached Crete and nearby islands since the beginning of the year as of early November, putting considerable pressure on local reception facilities.

The crossings are particularly dangerous during the winter months, when strong winds and rough seas can leave overcrowded boats in distress.

Earlier this month, at least 22 migrants died while attempting to reach southern Greece from Libya.

The International Organization for Migration (IOM) has warned that the growing number of crossings along the route has exposed shortcomings in search and rescue capabilities.

The latest arrivals come as European governments seek to tighten migration policies. Greece is among five European countries backing a proposal to establish a so-called return hub in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers, a plan criticised by human rights organisations.
