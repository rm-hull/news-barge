The girl was recognised for her bravery during the July 2024 Southport attack

**A girl who shielded her younger sister during the Southport attack has received a national bravery award.**

The girl, who cannot be named for legal reasons, suffered life-threatening injuries after being stabbed during the attack at a Taylor Swift-themed dance workshop on 29 July 2024.

Merseyside Police said the girl had suffered serious injuries but "then instinctively placed herself between the attacker and her sister".

She was one of four people from Merseyside to receive gold awards at the National Police Chiefs' Council's Police Public Bravery Awards.

Alice da Silva Aguiar, nine, Elsie Dot Stancombe, seven, and six-year-old Bebe King were murdered in the attack, while eight other children and two adults were seriously injured.

The force said the girl's "selfless act almost certainly saved her sister's life".

Both sisters survived and, despite the severity of her injuries, the older girl remained calm, asking whether her sister and other children were safe, police said.

![A man wearing a blue suit and red tie holds a framed certificate and medal alongside two senior police officers in ceremonial uniforms.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/5574/live/db48f9f0-eb2b-11f1-bb10-912617c39af7.jpg)

Daniel Barr received two awards for his bravery during the Liverpool victory parade attack

Daniel Barr, 41, from Liverpool, received a gold award for helping to stop Paul Doyle's car during the attack at Liverpool FC's Premier League victory parade on 26 May 2025.

Barr was also presented with the Binney Award, which recognises particularly exceptional acts of bravery.

Damon Emina and student Jake McDonald received gold awards for intervening in an incident in Liverpool in January 2025 in which a shopkeeper was racially abused and Emina was seriously injured.

Merseyside Police Chief Constable Rob Carden said all four had shown "unbelievable courage".

"It wasn't their job, it wasn't expected of them, they chose to do the right thing and gave very little regard for their own safety to help other people," he said.

"Hero is perhaps an overused word, but every one is thoroughly deserving of that title."
