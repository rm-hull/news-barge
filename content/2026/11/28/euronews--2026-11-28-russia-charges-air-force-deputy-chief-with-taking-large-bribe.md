## Yuri Grekhov, 64, who oversaw procurement of aircraft, missiles and air defence systems and has been under EU sanctions since 2022, is a serving officer, unlike most of the more than a dozen senior defence figures detained since Andrei Belousov replaced Sergei Shoigu as defence minister in 2024.

Russia has arrested Aerospace Forces (VKS) Deputy Commander in Chief Yuri Grekhov on charges of accepting a particularly large bribe, the latest in a series of corruption cases against senior military figures since the country changed its defence minister in 2024.

Grekhov, 64, oversaw procurement of aircraft, munitions, missiles and air defence systems. The charge carries a prison sentence of up to 15 years under Russian law.

Grekhov has been on the EU sanctions list since 2022, when Brussels deemed him responsible for Russian strikes on military and civilian sites after Moscow launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

A career military officer since 1979, Grekhov became deputy commander in chief under Alexander Chaiko in 2021, after commanding the air and missile defence forces from 2018 to 2021.

Unlike most of the other officials detained in recent cases, Grekhov is a serving officer, making him one of the most senior figures to come under investigation while still in post.

The VKS controls Russia's military aviation, space operations and strategic missile defence, and has played a central role in strikes on Ukrainian cities and infrastructure throughout the war.

## String of arrests since Shoigu's removal

At least a dozen senior ministry employees and generals have been detained or become criminal suspects since Andrei Belousov replaced the dismissed Sergei Shoigu as defence minister in 2024.

Belousov, an economist with no military background, was appointed with an explicit mandate to impose financial discipline on the defence ministry in what Moscow said was part of a crackdown on corruption within military ranks.

The most prominent case was the arrest of former Deputy Defence Minister Timur Ivanov in April 2024.

A close associate of Shoigu for more than a decade, Ivanov was convicted of taking a particularly large bribe in Defence Ministry contracting work, embezzlement and money laundering. He was sentenced to 13 years in a penal colony and fined 100 million roubles (€1 million).

Former first deputy defence minister Ruslan Tsalikov was detained in March 2026 on charges of creating a criminal organisation, embezzlement and laundering budget funds. In November he received 10 years in a high-security penal colony and a fine of 500 million roubles (€5 million).

Others detained for bribery and embezzlement include former Deputy Chief of the General Staff Vadim Shamarin, sentenced to seven years, former head of the Defence Ministry's Main Personnel Directorate Yuri Kuznetsov, sentenced to 12 years, and former Deputy Defence Ministers Dmitry Bulgakov and Pavel Popov.

Several Western governments have assessed that the prosecutions also serve to selectively remove officials who have fallen out of favour with the Kremlin.

The pattern of cases allows Moscow to present itself as enforcing accountability while using criminal charges as a tool of internal political control.
