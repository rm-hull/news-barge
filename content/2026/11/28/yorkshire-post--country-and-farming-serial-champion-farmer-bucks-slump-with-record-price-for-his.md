# Serial champion farmer bucks slump with record price for his pigs

## A serial champion pig farmer has bucked the slump in prices enveloping the sector by recording the highest price ever paid for pigs at the English Winter Fair.

As pig producers are losing up to £50 per animal, Mark Horsley, who runs a 370-sow breeder finishing unit at Vicarage Fields Farm, Skirpenbeck, near York, received £1,200 for each of his supreme champion pigs.

According to the National Pig Association, producers are losing between £20 and £50 per pig, while the Standard Pig Price has fallen from 208p/kg in August 2025 to around 178p/kg.

The national pig market has been declining for a year due to oversupply, lower demand for pork and bacon, and increased production costs. This has been compounded by higher feed costs, and in particular soya price rises.

![Mark Horsley's supreme champion pair of Pietrain cross pigs, which achieved £1,200 each at the English Winter Fair.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjMxY2UxYzRlLTY2MWQtNGFlMi1hOTFiLWFmMzc1MzhiYzkxNDowODhjNjExYy00NzM4LTQwMDItYWRhOC1jOTVmNTU3MjUzYmY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Although the victory at the national event at the Staffordshire County Showground was his 11th in 15 years, Mr Horsley said achieving such a strong trade with his Pietrain cross pair had represented “a great success” and had given him a huge boost. He said: "These winter fairs are something that lifts your spirits for Christmas.”

Mr Horsley was speaking as it was announced Britain's meat industry had secured a trade breakthrough in Asia, unlocking £70 million in potential exports to Vietnam and safeguarding £172 million in pork sales to the Philippines over five years.

Nevertheless, numerous Yorkshire pig producers are continuing to report pigs backing up due to workforce shortages at processing plants limiting slaughter capacity alongside contract cancellations. Mr Horsley said he had managed to keep all his customers, but did not foresee any uptake in pig prices until spring or even next summer.

The third generation farmer said: “I think pig producers are going to struggle until then. There’s a lot of people going out of the industry. Hopefully the quality is shining through and butchers are paying a bit extra to support us.”

Earlier this week, the Environment, Food and Rural Affairs Committee has warned Defra pig production - Yorkshire’s most important agricultutral sector economically - is approaching crisis point, with financial pressures threatening entire supply chains and the wider rural economy

Committee chair Alistair Carmichael MP said the industry's survival should not be taken for granted, particularly as smaller independent producers face mounting financial difficulties.

He said: "Time is running out to keep the pig sector on its feet. Multiple challenges have made 2026 a tumultuous year, and the industry is now approaching a crisis point."

Nevertheless, Mr Horsley said he was determined to maintain the region’s rich pig producing heritage. He said: “Pricing is very tough at the moment to make ends meet. We’ve increased feed costs and rising fuel costs. Unfortunately it’s the independent side of the sector that is getting decimated and the big integrators are taking over. The smaller independent farm is suffering, but we will endeavour to keep going whatever we have to do.

"I still get that buzz when I see a pen full of good quality good shape pigs and whilst I still have that I’ll keep going.”
