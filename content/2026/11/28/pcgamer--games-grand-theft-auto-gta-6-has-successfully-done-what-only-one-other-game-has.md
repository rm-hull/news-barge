![Jason and Lucia entering The Vault nightclub.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/uH4HVCG6DkzwRjendWpp2D-1920-80.jpg)

When Hitman 3 first launched in 2021, it was the first time since Absolution that I'd felt a strong urge to step into the shoes of Agent 47. The reason: its Berlin level. Set in the grimy, sweaty Club Hölle, it was the first time I'd ever seen a videogame get a nightclub right. The density of people on the dancefloor, the slight griminess that coated the whole place. The lights, the music. I didn't think another game would out-do it anytime soon.

And then Grand Theft Auto 6 happened.

![Nightclubs in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/PMGY4hsc5FAtPJnKwAhM2E-1200-80.png)

There's an awful lot to be impressed with in Rockstar's newest blockbuster—and I should hope so, considering how much money and time went into making the damn thing—but nothing has stood out to me more than Leonida's nightlife. Hitman 3 did it excellently in isolation, but GTA 6 has managed to incorporate an immersive, lively club scene right into the open world.

And after spending far too much time in Vice City's Big Three—that's NINE1NINE, Jack of Hearts, and The Vault—I'm floored not only by how good a job Rockstar did of making them feel like a club you would end up inside in real life, but how distinct they all are from one another.

![Nightclubs in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/TPo9jQMfitifNkNfFLzTxD-1200-80.png)

You've got your overpriced luxury half-strip-club, half-regular-club with NINE1NINE (inspired by Miami's own E11EVEN), where a beer costs 15 goddamn bucks even after you already fork out the $150 cover charge to get in. There's the sticky, dollar bill-laden floors of Jack of Hearts where MCs belt out over the mic and the bass practically splits the speakers open. And then you've got the cool, underground rave vibes of The Vault with multiple floors offering different music styles.

Even before heading inside each one, its patrons spill out of the doors and onto the sidewalks. Some are queueing up waiting to get in, while others huddle around back entrances having a smoke and talking about all the diabolical topics you get into after a few drinks. They aren't just isolated interiors, capturing the parts of the night that happen outside of the club itself.

## Evacuate the dancefloor

![The Vault in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/S6YKxoGmcL5XEYnMvYcQfh-1080-80.jpg)

That's an issue videogame nightclubs so often have: its people. A smattering of NPCs here and there, standing idly around in a venue that's a little too well-lit and making me feel like I just rocked up at 2 pm on a Monday afternoon.

Yet everywhere you look in GTA 6's nightclubs, you'll find swarms of bodies. The Vault is by far my favourite nightclub of the three—perhaps because it's the closest to what I've experienced in the real world—and as I walked around it shocked me just how carefully placed every single person felt.

![The Vault in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ctVqZiKCr9hkhnwFP3WZph-600-80.jpg)

The dancefloor is densely packed and I have to weave through sweaty bodies to make my way to the front. That's already impressive enough in itself. But it's also the punters who clutter the stairways; some sat on a step alone as they scroll through their phone, others gathered in clusters in an attempt to converse away from the noise and throng of drunken ravers. I've seen those people countless times in my own real-life nighttime excursions. Hell, I've *been* those people.

Sure, these people are ultimately set pieces, rarely (if ever) moving from their carefully curated posts. Dancers might wander around and bartenders will walk up and down serving drinks, leaving the rest of its people surprisingly static. But in the grand scheme of things, it doesn't matter. How often are you paying attention to what everyone else is up to in a nightclub, anyway?

Even the ways these things are decorated, man. Seeing half-drunk beer bottles perched atop ATM machines and drink cups cluttering up the bar makes me feel like people have actually been here and lived their life. There are so many small details that have gone into making each place feel like somewhere you've been in the real world, and that's even without me mentioning all the extra little ways Sony's DualSense controller amps up the experience.

I'm not sure how long it'll be until we get nightclubs that feel this damn good again. They feel like venues put together by people who've stayed out dancing until the lights come on at 3 am, only filtered out onto the streets by harsh fluorescents and the sudden absence of music. From the music choices, to the strobed lights and fog machines, to those utterly wild conversations you'd only be privy to in the smoking area, Rockstar has managed to curate the best nightlife going.
