At least two hospitals in England have reintroduced mask-wearing in a bid to stem the spread of influenza, as nationwide admissions soared by 80 per cent in just a week.

The Royal Preston Hospital and Chorley and South Ribble Hospital in Lancashire announced that coverings would be required in their inpatient areas in order to protect staff and patients.

Steve Canty, chief medical officer of the Lancashire Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust, said: “Increased rates of respiratory illness place additional pressure on our services and the availability of hospital beds, which in turn places more pressure on our urgent and emergency care services.”

He said the trust would continue to monitor the situation and thanked staff and visitors for their support for the safety measures, which came into effect on Wednesday.

Flu admissions in England reached nine people per 100,000 last week, up from five per 100,000 during the seven day period before, according to the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA).

![Pharmacist Atulkumar Patel gives a flu jab to public health minister Dame Diana Johnson during her visit to Lincoln Pharmacy in Bow, east London, on Friday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/10/01M8GK3KRFTWPNE4RYTKV5YSYV.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The agency has urged the most vulnerable people to book vaccinations as soon as possible in a move they say “could save your life”.

Only around a third of the 9.5 million people in clinical risk groups had received the jab as of 22 November, leaving almost seven million unprotected, they warned.

“Flu can be very serious for people of all ages living with certain long-term health conditions, especially chronic liver disease, chronic neurological disease and those with a weakened immune system or ‘immunosuppression’,” said Dr Suzanna McDonald, UKHSA national lead for the Flu Programme.

“Every winter we see many thousands having to be hospitalised and tragically too many unnecessary deaths. A flu vaccine will help protect you and could save your life.”

According to UKHSA’s figures, just 34.9 per cent of pregnant women had been vaccinated against the flu, and 39.2 per cent of children aged two or three-years-old.

Dr McDonald advised people to check with their GP if they have a long-term condition but are unsure whether they are eligible for the jab.

![The Royal Preston Hospital said that face masks will be required in its inpatient areas (file)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/10/1361896433..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Health experts say people in clinical risk groups may be at much greater risk of death if they are unvaccinated against the flu, compared with the general population.

Fiona Loud, policy director at Kidney Care UK, said that people with stage 3-5 chronic kidney disease are 19 times more likely to die from flu than someone without the condition.

“Many people are not aware of this and booking an appointment can easily get lost in the long to do list we all have at this time of year,” she said.

“But it is so important, which is why we urge those who are eligible to book their free flu vaccine as soon as possible, to help themselves - as well as those around them - to be protected this winter.”

The NHS website says flu symptoms can come on very quickly and include: a sudden high temperature; an aching body; feeling tired or exhausted; a dry cough; a sore throat; a headache; difficulty sleeping; and loss of appetite.

Those with the virus are encouraged to rest and sleep; to take paracetamol and ibuprofen to lower their temperature and treat aches and pains; and to drink plenty of water to avoid dehydration.
