Violent clashes broke out during a student-led protest in Albania on Saturday as weeks of anti-government demonstrations boiled over.

Demonstrators gathered in the capital Tirana on Independence Day and hurled petrol bombs and stones at riot police who responded with tear gas, water cannon and rubber bullets.

The rally, which attracted thousands, began peacefully before small groups of masked protesters broke away and began smashing the windows of ministry buildings along the march route.

Some peppered a government building with stones and attempted to reach the office of prime minister Edi Rama, as the crowds renewed calls for his resignation.

Footage from the capital showed petrol bombs exploding near a phalanx of police wielding riot shields. Fires also burned near a riot police truck as officers faced off against demonstrators.

Clouds of tear gas thrown by police billowed through the area as protesters fanned out over several streets.

Albanians had travelled to the capital from across the country to mark the date usually celebrated as a public holiday, according to the Associated Press.

Police deployed along the main roads in and out of the capital were carrying out vehicle checks.

The protests against perceived government corruption, a lack of jobs and poor education standards have rocked Tirana since mid-October and since spread to other cities, often turning violent.

“Enough is enough; the corruption, the poverty, the immigration,” said Eralda Baze, 45, an engineer who joined Saturday's rally. Others demanded a better life and healthcare.

Arian Lleshi, a 33-year-old who traveled to Tirana from the United Kingdom, told AP: “I came here specifically for the protest. Why? Because we have been suffering for far too long.

“We have spread ourselves across Europe and around the world, and it is almost as if we are citizens without a state. We are like stateless citizens.”

Another banner at the march read “Albania is not for sale”. The country was moved over the summer to rally against a planned luxury development backed by US President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner.

Those protests became known as the Flamingo movement and followed by the student demonstrations from September, with students calling for better education and living standards.

The demonstrators' main demand has since broadened to include calls for Rama's resignation.

These latest protests, which have seen wider and more intense clashes, pose the biggest challenge yet to the prime minister, who has governed since 2013 and won a fourth term in office only last year.

Protesters chanted “Resignation!” as they gathered in Tirana’s main Skanderbeg Square.

“We have come out to demand our rights. We have had enough. Life has become very difficult. We are calling for a change to the entire system,” student Juna Vasili said.
