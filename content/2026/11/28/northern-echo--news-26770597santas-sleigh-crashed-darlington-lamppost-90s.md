Darlington's annual charity sleigh ride has delivered festive cheer to generations of families.

But, one memorable night saw Santa's journey come to a sudden and painful end when his sleigh broke free and crashed into a lamppost.

Over more than half a century of fundraising, Darlington Round Table's famous sleigh ride has seen its fair share of challenges, but few compare to the night Santa ended up in hospital.

The dramatic incident happened during a freezing evening in the '90s as the sleigh travelled downhill through Darlington. At some point, it became detached from the vehicle towing it and began rolling away uncontrolled.

The stunned driver looked out of the van window to discover he was being overtaken by Santa himself.

The runaway sleigh eventually came to an abrupt halt when it smashed into a lamppost.

Santa on that particular night was local dentist Andrew Wilson, who was thrown forward by the impact and struck his head. Although his padded Santa suit softened the blow, the accident was serious enough to require an ambulance.

Recalling the incident years later, Andrew laughed: "I often think how someone had to ring 999 and say, 'Help! Santa's had a crash and fallen off his sleigh'. They must have thought we were taking the mickey."

While the sleigh's journey came to an end, Andrew's evening continued in the Accident and Emergency department at Darlington Memorial Hospital.

Thankfully, the festive mishap did not derail the charity tradition, which has become a cornerstone of Christmas in Darlington.

The story dates back to 1969 when the Bondgate 999 Round Table was formed.

Early fundraising efforts saw members spread festive cheer by singing carols around the town while collecting money for local good causes.

As the years passed, members began discussing ways to create a bigger spectacle. The idea of building a proper sleigh for Santa soon gained momentum, although not everyone was immediately convinced.

Some members preferred to continue the traditional carol-singing approach, but debate quickly ended when one volunteer announced he had found what he described as a "perfectly sound" old caravan for just £50.

Using the caravan's chassis as a base, members created the first official Darlington sleigh, which began touring neighbourhoods 50 years ago.

Since then, the sleigh has become a festive fixture, travelling through communities on cold December evenings, raising thousands of pounds for local charities and bringing Christmas magic to generations of families.
