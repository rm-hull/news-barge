Thousands of children who missed their school flu vaccine can now receive a jab at a local pharmacy for the first time.

NHS England is urging parents to ensure their children are protected as cases rise nationwide.

More than 6,000 high street pharmacies across England are offering appointments, with bookings opening Monday via the NHS app or NHS Find a Pharmacy webpage, and appointments available from Tuesday.

Pharmacies are also offering nasal spray flu vaccines for children aged two and three.

Children can fall severely ill with flu and easily spread it to more vulnerable groups, including the elderly.

The rollout comes as hospital admissions for flu run at their highest level for this time of year since before the pandemic.

Flu admission rates in England reached 9.0 per 100,000 last week, up from 5.0 per 100,000 seven days earlier, according to the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA).

![More than 3.5 million children from Reception to Year 11 have already had their flu vaccine](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/27/15/18/iStock-1470803848.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Admissions stood at 4.9 per 100,000 in the equivalent week in 2025.

NHS national medical director Professor Frankie Swords said: “The run-up to Christmas can be such a busy time for parents, so it’s great news that for the first time, it’s now really easy to book your child’s flu vaccine at your local high street pharmacy if they missed it in school.

“Winter viruses like flu are on the rise again, which puts real pressure on the NHS and could also ruin your family’s plans for Christmas.

“This offer is another way to protect children, families and communities, so if you need an appointment for your child, please book now.”

More than 3.5 million children from Reception to Year 11 have already had their flu vaccine.

Dr Suzanna McDonald, flu vaccination programme lead at the UK Health Security Agency (UKHSA), said: “Vaccinating school children against flu not only helps protect them, but crucially helps protect younger siblings and other more vulnerable family members at greater risk, like grandparents.

“Ensuring all children from aged two receive a flu vaccine every winter is so important to help prevent the thousands of unnecessary hospitalisations we see among unvaccinated children.

“Please don’t dismiss the importance of a flu vaccine for your children. It will help keep them out of hospital and even save lives.

“Don’t put your child needlessly at risk, book that appointment with your GP surgery or at some community pharmacies offering the vaccine.”

![Flu admission rates in England reached 9.0 per 100,000 last week, up from 5.0 per 100,000 seven days earlier, according to the UK Health Security Agency](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/04/16/01M3V12F7ZMZZ0FZBY3MD7HYTF.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Outgoing health chief Sir Jim Mackey warned the NHS should prepare for a “horrible spike in flu”, as he said some hospitals have introduced mask wearing to stem the spread of respiratory illnesses.

Dr McDonald, said experts expect the flu jab to work well against the strain of virus currently in circulation even though it has mutated slightly.

Dr McDonald told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme on Friday: “Hospital admission rates have increased 80% in the latest data that we’ve published this week compared to last week, and that is a very sharp rise.

“What we’re seeing is that sharp rise is coming in a few weeks earlier than we saw this time last season, but last season was the earliest season that we’d seen for over 20 years.

“This is very early to be seeing such high demand in hospitals.”

Dr Matt Swallow, clinical lead at Asthma + Lung UK, said: “Flu can be particularly dangerous for young children, especially for the 1.2 million children living with asthma…

“It’s great news that pharmacies are offering another opportunity to ensure more children are protected.”

Henry Gregg, chief executive of the National Pharmacy Association, said: “Pharmacies have been working hard to vaccinate millions of patients in recent weeks but it is clear more can be done by the Government and other agencies to promote this service to those who are eligible.

“If you are a parent who has not yet got your child vaccinated, please come forward for vaccination at your local pharmacy, based conveniently on your doorstep.”
