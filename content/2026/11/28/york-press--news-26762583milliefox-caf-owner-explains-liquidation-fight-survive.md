## Café owner explains liquidation fight that saved every staff job and business

Jo Millner of Milliefox *(Image: Pic supplied)*

*Here Jo Millner of Millie Fox talks about the liquidation of Millie Fox Kitchen and how she fought to save the business and staff jobs.*

When I received an email from the local press asking for a comment on the liquidation of Milliefox Kitchen Ltd, my stomach sank.

I genuinely felt sick.

After months of working incredibly hard to rebuild our business, protect jobs and create something positive for the future, it was heartbreaking to think that this might be the story people would read about Milliefox.

I'd love to be talking to the press about our afternoon teas, the amazing cakes we create, our newly refurbished events space, the friendships formed in our café, how we look after our local community or the laughs our wonderful team share with our customers every day.




![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763916.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Some of the Milliefox delights *(Image: Pic supplied)*

I'd love to be celebrating the fact that, despite everything, we've managed to retain every member of our team and create an additional job.

But sadly, it's the liquidation of our former café company that has attracted attention.

I want to tell my own story, including the highs, the lows, the mistakes, the sacrifices and the determination it takes to keep a small independent business alive in today's economic climate.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763918.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The cafe interior *(Image: Pic supplied)*

**Milliefox is still open and this is the story of how hard we've fought to stay here.**

When I opened Milliefox Cakes in October 2019, I had no idea what the next seven years would bring.

I started as a sole trader in a small industrial unit, specialising in bespoke celebration cakes, wedding cakes and cupcake bouquets.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763943.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Joanne Milner and some of her team *(Image: Pic supplied)*

My order book was full and I was excited about the future.

Then Covid hit.

Almost overnight, I had to refund deposits, cancel bookings and find a way to pay the rent on premises I could no longer use as intended.

Like many independent business owners, I adapted.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763922.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The cafe interior *(Image: Pic supplied)*

I homeschooled my son from my industrial unit whilst making afternoon teas, brownies and cupcakes, delivering them across York.

We managed to survive the pandemic and even make a small profit.

As restrictions lifted, we outgrew our premises.

Customers were asking for somewhere they could come and enjoy our afternoon teas, and in October 2021, Milliefox opened its doors in Huntington, with a community café at the front and my established cake business at the back.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763929.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The cafe interior *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Following professional advice, the two businesses were later structured separately, with Milliefox Cakes remaining a sole trader and the café operating as a limited company, Milliefox Kitchen Ltd.

In October 2022, I lost my mum.

I was devastated, and I'll openly admit that I took my eye off the ball for a while.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764085.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Staff outside the cafe *(Image: Pic supplied)*Running a business doesn't give you much opportunity to stop and grieve.**The reality of running a small hospitality business**

Both businesses were doing well, but the financial landscape for hospitality was changing dramatically.

Rising ingredient prices, energy costs, business rates, wages and changes to employer National Insurance meant that I calculated we needed to generate an additional £2,500 in sales every month simply to stand still.

That's £30,000 a year in additional turnover without being any better off.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21763947.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The cafe interior *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Meanwhile, our customers were experiencing their own cost-of-living crisis.

How much more can you realistically charge someone for a cup of coffee, a sandwich or a slice of cake?

I genuinely believe the government needs to take a long, hard look at how its policies are affecting independent hospitality businesses.

We are being asked to absorb increasing employment costs whilst trying to keep our prices affordable for the communities we serve.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764047.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new events space. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

The additional burden of VAT makes it increasingly difficult to operate sustainably on already incredibly tight margins.

As the café company fell behind with VAT and PAYE, I approached HMRC for help.

The repayment arrangements available to us would have left us struggling to meet our ongoing liabilities whilst clearing the arrears.

I felt trapped in a cycle that was becoming increasingly difficult to escape.

**A sale that never happened**

In January 2026, I made the difficult decision to put the café up for sale.

A buyer was found relatively quickly, and I genuinely believed I had secured a future for both the café and its team.

After almost six months of negotiations, just days before we were due to complete, the sale fell through following a disagreement over the transfer of employees.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764048.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new events space. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Following legal advice, I had to withdraw from the sale.

I was left with an £8,000 legal bill, declining café sales following months of uncertainty and a commercial lease in my personal name that still had around 18 months remaining.

Perhaps the hardest part was sitting down with my team and explaining what had happened.

They had spent months wondering what their future would look like.

I sought further professional advice and ultimately made the incredibly difficult decision to liquidate Milliefox Kitchen Ltd.

**Rebuilding Milliefox**

My original cake business, Milliefox Cakes, continued trading throughout.

With professional guidance, I restructured the café and events side of the business, which now operates under Naughty & Nice Events Ltd, continuing to trade as Milliefox.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764153.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An afternoon tea *(Image: Pic supplied)*

With the support of my husband, we personally invested a further £5,000 into refurbishing the premises and creating a more versatile space for parties, private events, business meetings and afternoon teas.

We introduced a more affordable menu, extended our opening hours and moved to seven-day trading.

Most importantly, every member of our team has been retained, and we have created an additional job.

I'm currently working upwards of 80 hours a week to give this business the best possible chance of succeeding.

The stress and anxiety of the past year have taken an enormous toll on my health and family life.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764157.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazing baking to enjoy *(Image: Pic supplied)*

Yet every morning, I put a smile on my face, open the doors and welcome our customers.

Because despite everything, I still love what I do.

I love seeing someone's reaction when they collect their celebration cake.

I love watching friends catch up over an afternoon tea.

I love hearing laughter coming from the café.

I just wish that doing something you love didn't come with the constant fear that a few quiet days could bring your business to its knees.

**Independent businesses need their communities**

My story is sadly one that many hospitality business owners will recognise.

There are independent businesses across the country facing similar pressures.

Behind their doors are people who have invested their savings, sacrificed family time and worked countless unpaid hours to keep their businesses alive and their teams employed.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21764167.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More amazing cooking *(Image: ic supplied)*

I would urge the government to listen to those of us working on the ground and understand the cumulative impact of its decisions.

For Milliefox, my focus now is on building a sustainable future.

**We are open, our team is here, and we have so much more to offer.**

Whether you're popping in for coffee, ordering a birthday cake, buying Christmas treats, booking a party or simply recommending us to a friend, your support makes a genuine difference.

I know how difficult things are financially for everyone right now.

But when you can, please consider supporting your local independent businesses.

We want to be here for many years to come, and with the support of our community, I sincerely hope we will be.

Jo Millner

Milliefox, Huntington, York
