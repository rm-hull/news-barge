A MAN who dedicated himself to his home village has been honoured by friends and neighbours ahead of his 90th birthday.

More than 130 villagers attended the event at Hurworth Grange Community Centre to honour Peter Allan, who served as a parish councillor and parish clerk for 15 years.

Now he has made history after being dubbed the first Freeman of the Parish of Hurworth-on-Tees since records began more than 600 years ago.

Peter said: “I love this community, I really do. It's a beautiful village. I've been singled out, but everyone here tonight has given a huge amount.”

Born in Tynemouth and raised in Whitley Bay, Peter first came to Hurworth in 1948, when he attended Hurworth House School as a boarding student.

![Peter Allan Freeman of the parish of Hurworth November 2026](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21762288.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Peter, supported by his wife Val, was overcome as villagers thanked him for years of hard work *(Image: Chris Barron)*

After graduating, Peter specialised in selling computer systems, a career which took him and his wife, Val, all over the world, before they eventually settled back in Hurworth in 1988.

Peter and Val became involved in supporting the village's community centre, and he became a parish councillor in 2008, but stepped down three years later to take on the role of parish clerk.

“I agreed to take the role on a temporary basis for 12 months,” he said. “After 15 years, I decided it was time to retire. But I enjoyed every minute of it.”

During his time with the parish council, Peter threw himself into improving village life through community projects and events, earning him the title of Citizen of the Year at the 2018 Best of Darlington Awards.

He is credited as the driving force behind popular local events like the village’s first duck race, the annual Hurworth Country Fair, and Music for All events, and helping to set up the Hurworth Classic Car Show.

His efforts to secure grants and other funding also helped bring more than £200,000 into the village, supporting projects including the local children's playground and renovations to the village hall.

The birthday party, which took place shortly before Peter turned 90, was organised by locals Ann and Tony Gent, and featured a performance by singer-songwriter Hayley McKay, who grew up in the village.

In an emotional speech, Peter’s longtime friend Ken Pattison paid tribute to him, saying: “The parish council now has some power to its elbow, and in my opinion, that is entirely due to Peter. They have the money to help projects and organisations, and to my mind, that’s what a parish council should be all about.

“Peter is, beyond any shadow of a doubt, the best clerk we’ve ever had.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21762291.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 130 people turned out to celebrate Peter's 90th birthday *(Image: Chris Barron)*

Ward councillors Lorraine Tostevin and Paul Waters also shared their gratitude for Peter’s work, and read a letter of congratulations from the Mayor of Darlington, Councillor Helen Crumbie.

She wrote: “The difference you’ve made cannot be measured simply in meetings attended or funds raised. It can be seen in the thriving community you’ve helped build.”

Peter was also presented with a bench, inscribed with a bronze plaque, which will be placed in the village in his honour.

But in what came as a complete surprise, he was also named an Honorary Freeman of the Parish – a ceremonial title historically awarded to people who have 'rendered eminent service to the community'.

Parish councillor Graham Wylie formally conferred the honour, which is believed never to have been bestowed before in Hurworth.

As a Freeman of the Parish, Peter is entitled to graze his livestock on the village green, be escorted home by a police officer if found drunk in the street and – should capital punishment ever be reinstated – be hanged with a silk rope rather than one made of common hemp.

“I felt a bit embarrassed,” Peter confessed. “I was singled out for this fantastic recognition, but that room was full of people who have put in so much time and effort for years on end.

“I was totally surprised, but I am delighted to have received this honour.”
