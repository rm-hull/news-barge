John Swinney insisted the NHS was fundamentally safe while at an anti-racism rally for St Andrew's Day

**The first minister has insisted that Scotland's NHS is "fundamentally safe" despite concerns raised about care at NHS Grampian.**

The health board has continued to face scrutiny following the publication of a leaked report that revealed 19 avoidable deaths among 45 patients in liver and pancreatic surgery at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary (ARI).

Last week, the Scottish government announced an independent review into concerns around hepatobiliary (HPB) surgery at the health board.

John Swinney acknowledged that mistakes can occur but insisted patients should still attend appointments, procedures and surgeries.

Speaking at an anti-racism rally for St Andrew's Day in Glasgow, Swinney said: "We have a fundamentally safe National Health Service in Scotland that operates to the highest level of quality.

"There are issues that go wrong, I accept there are some issues that go wrong.

"But fundamentally we have a safe, strong and effective National Health Service, and I would encourage the public to participate in that service."

Swinney insists NHS in Scotland is safe

When asked about claims the Scottish government was aware of the decision not to publish the 2022 report, Swinney said the upcoming review would answer any questions.

He said: "I want to make sure there is total transparency about all of these issues, that we understand what went on, what was the sequence of events, what judgments were made, and that there can be lessons learnt from all of that."

The first minister said the government had to "satisfy members of the public with our answers".

"But this is in one isolated area of surgery, in NHS Grampian," he added.

"Every day, there are thousands and thousands and thousands and thousands of procedures that are carried out in the National Health Service perfectly safely, perfectly effectively by high-quality professionals, clinicians and members of staff and that should give members the public confidence."

## 'Major deficiencies'

NHS Grampian has been at the centre of controversy since a report from the health watchdog published two weeks ago highlighted "major deficiencies" in its leadership and culture.

Healthcare Improvement Scotland's medical director Dr Simon Watson described the report as "the most serious review" it had ever written, calling the findings "unprecedented".

Dr Mohammed Habib, a consultant surgeon at Aberdeen Royal Infirmary (ARI), leaked the 2022 report the following week.

He called for NHS Grampian to face a corporate homicide probe.

An oversight team was appointed for NHS Grampian, and Health Secretary Angela Constance announced an independent review of the health board last week.

She said it would be carried out by experts from outside Scotland and be "100% transparent".

A number of patients have come forward to raise concerns about their treatment.

One woman said she lay awake on an operating table during knee replacement surgery listening to surgeons argue as they struggled to control her bleeding.

While another patient said he felt mistakes were made after he went into cardiac arrest during a liver operation.

On Thursday, Scotland's chief medical officer Sir Gregor Smith, who had faced questions over his role, resigned on mental health grounds.

The chairwoman of the BMA in Scotland said trust had been damaged across the NHS by the revelations and this should be a watershed moment for healthcare in Scotland.

Separately, the health board apologised on Friday after confidential papers containing sensitive medical and personal details of 22 patients were found discarded on a street near the ARI.

A passer-by found the documents near a bin on Raeden Park Road, near Foresterhill Health Campus, the day before.
