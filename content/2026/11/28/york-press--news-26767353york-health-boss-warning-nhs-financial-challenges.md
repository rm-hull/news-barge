“Significant financial challenges” are facing “our entire health and care system” heading into winter, the boss of Healthwatch York has said.

Siân Balsom said the heightened demand on resources brought by the colder weather will come as many organisations grapple “with greater demand and less resource to meet it”.

It follows Rachael Maskell MP warning that the cash-strapped NHS trust that runs York hospital has ruled out cutting the opening hours of urgent treatment centres to provide health services as the underfunded organisation prepares for winter.

The York Central MP said the extra pressure is being placed on the trust’s staff who "have given their all in providing the very best care possible under the circumstances".

Trust bosses are looking at ways to save money but earlier this week ruled out cutting the opening hours of urgent treatment centres, which could have saved £200,000.

The York and Scarborough Teaching Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust is seeking an £8 million support package from NHS England as it battles a growing deficit and attempts to deliver tens of millions of pounds of planned savings.

Ms Balsom told The Press: "We have stopped talking about austerity, but the reality is that our entire health and care system is experiencing significant financial challenges.

"Many organisations are grappling with greater demand and less resource to meet it.

“This is clearly a concerning story and people reading this may be worried about what this means for them if they need healthcare over the winter months.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19696118.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Siân Balsom, manager of Healthwatch York *(Image: Supplied)*

Ms Balsom admitted it is “easy to feel helpless in the face of these realities” but said: “We can all play a role in helping our NHS services, particularly our hospitals, get through winter.

“First, if you are eligible for flu and Covid jabs, please take them up if you can. We are expecting a bad flu year so this could make a huge difference to overall sickness levels across our communities.

“Second, have a well-stocked first aid box and make use of other health services including your pharmacy, GP and NHS 111 wherever possible. This can prevent us becoming more unwell, give us faster access to appropriate health advice and support, and this all helps reduce demand on our hospitals.

“Finally, we hear from people every day about good experiences of care.

"Our hospital remains full of dedicated staff who help people to stay well or recover from ill health.

"So please do not let this stop you from accessing hospital care if you need it.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19715684.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rachael Maskell, MP for York Central *(Image: UK Parliament/Press Association)*

Ms Maskell said the pressures facing staff at the trust are "not helped by the condition of [York] hospital, the paucity of funding to primary care to prevent admissions and a 35 per cent shortfall in public health funding since 2015".

"We need a serious dialogue with the ICB [integrated care board] and government as to why funding is not meeting need, so that people in our city can have the healthcare they need in a timely way," the Labour MP said.
