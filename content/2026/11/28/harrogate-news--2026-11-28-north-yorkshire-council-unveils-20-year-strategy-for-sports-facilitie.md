**New playing pitch and built sports facilities strategies aim to modernise and expand sports infrastructure, addressing growing demand and ageing centres across North Yorkshire.**

North Yorkshire Council has published its first-ever comprehensive playing pitch and built sports facilities strategies, setting out plans to upgrade and expand sports venues across the county over the next two decades.

Developed in collaboration with Sport England, national sports governing bodies, local clubs, schools, and community stakeholders, these strategies provide a unified framework to guide investment in playing pitches, leisure centres, sports halls, swimming pools, and other community sports facilities.

As the county continues to grow, the strategies aim to address challenges including ageing leisure centres, rising demand for pitches, and the increasing need to support residents aged over 65 in maintaining an active lifestyle. They identify priorities such as modernising older facilities, improving energy efficiency, and expanding capacity in some of North Yorkshire’s fastest-growing areas.

The playing pitch strategy forecasts current and future demands for youth football pitches, artificial grass pitches, cricket grounds, and rugby fields. It highlights opportunities to improve pitch quality, invest in changing facilities, develop additional 3G pitches, and better utilise existing sites. Meanwhile, the built sports facilities strategy responds to growing activities like gymnastics, swimming, padel, and athletics.

Together, the strategies also explore opportunities for greater use of schools, community halls, and existing sports venues to broaden access to sport and physical activity within local communities.

> **Cllr Simon Myers:**  
> Sport and physical activity play a vital role in helping people to stay healthy and active.

We want residents across North Yorkshire to have access to good-quality sports facilities and the opportunity to take part in sport and physical activity close to where they live.

These strategies give us the clearest picture we have ever had of sports facilities across the county. They show where facilities are meeting local needs, where there are gaps in provision, and where improvements may be needed in the future.

They also highlight some significant challenges, including ageing leisure centres and sports facilities, growing demand for pitches, and the need to support an increased number of residents aged over 65 to stay active and independent.

Having a strong evidence base will put us in a much better position to plan for the future, and attract external funding so facilities can continue to meet the needs of residents as our communities grow. I look forward to discussing the strategies with councillors when we meet next week.

The strategies will be presented to councillors on 1 December and include action plans that will help direct future investment and support discussions on developer contributions linked to new housing developments.

If adopted, the strategies will support stronger funding bids and ensure that investment is effectively targeted to benefit residents, encouraging greater participation in physical activity and fostering healthier communities across North Yorkshire.

The full report to the council executive is available to view or download here: [Playing Pitch and Built Sports Facilities Strategy](https://edemocracy.northyorks.gov.uk/documents/s98870/Report%20-%20North%20Yorkshire%20Council%20Playing%20Pitch%20Strategy%20and%20Built%20Sports%20Facilities%20Strategy%20261201.pdf).
