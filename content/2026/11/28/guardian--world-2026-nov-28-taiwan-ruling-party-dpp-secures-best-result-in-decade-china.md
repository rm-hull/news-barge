Taiwan’s ruling party secured its best result in a decade in Saturday’s local elections, doubling the number of constituencies it controls, in an outcome expected to anger Beijing.

The Democratic Progressive party (DPP) won 10 of Taiwan’s 22 mayoral and county magistrate races, up from five in its disastrous showing in the last local elections four years ago.

The DPP is led by Lai Ching-te, Taiwan’s president, who is detested by Beijing. The Chinese Communist party (CCP) regards Lai as a “separatist” who opposes its plans to unify China and Taiwan. China regards Taiwan, a self-governing island, as part of its territory, a stance that the majority of Taiwanese people reject.

Taiwan’s main opposition party, the Kuomintang (KMT), won 10 races, down from 14 in the last vote. But it retained control of Taiwan’s capital, Taipei, and the neighbouring New Taipei.

The local elections were fought mainly on bread-and-butter issues such as housing costs and wealth distribution rather than cross-Strait relations. Nevertheless, “China will be breathing a sigh of relief” at the KMT’s success in Taipei and New Taipei, said William Yang, a senior analyst at the International Crisis Group.

The KMT promotes closer ties between Taiwan and China and the annual Taipei-Shanghai forum is one of the only major remaining exchanges between China and Taiwan.

The race for Taipei mayor had been a close contest between the KMT’s incumbent, Chiang Wan-an, and Puma Shen, a DPP legislator who has faced sanctions from China. On Saturday afternoon, thousands of supporters gathered outside Shen’s campaign office to watch the results come in.

![Puma Shen scratches the back of his head while on stage](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/f2a6aac31ef7b209445c38fbaf4ae1409d11fc47/440_0_2917_2335/master/2917.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

The mood started optimistic, with chants of “Puma, keep going!” and “Turn it around!”, but as Chiang extended his lead, a sombre mood descended. Shortly before 8pm, Shen took to the stage to concede defeat, greeted by a prolonged standing ovation.

“The results are in and I’ve lost,” Shen said. Visibly emotional, Shen emphasised that he would continue to work hard.

“Tonight, the people of Taipei made the best decision. As I made clear in previous interviews with foreign media, no matter who wins today, it is a victory for Taipei and a victory for democracy,” he said.

Shen, a criminologist turned politician, is also the co-founder of Kuma Academy, which runs civil defence courses for Taiwanese people to prepare for a potential Chinese invasion.

Yan Jing-na, 50, a Shen supporter, cried after he conceded defeat. “I don’t care which party he belongs to. As long as he puts Taiwan’s interests first, I’ll support him,” she said, adding that she thought it was “cool” that Shen was labelled a “Taiwan independence separatist” by Beijing.

“He has long defended Taiwan’s rights,” she said, “and that’s why I have to support him, because the people of Taiwan have had it tough.”

Yan said Shen had helped raise public awareness of China’s campaign against Taiwan.“So we’re not worried. I think many people are now more aware of China’s influence on Taiwan because of him,” she said.

Taiwanese officials raised the alarm over thousands of incidents of suspected misinformation or election interference before Saturday’s vote. There have also been reports of hundreds of China-linked AI-generated videos spreading disinformation about Taiwanese politicians disliked by Beijing.

China has also maintained physical pressure around the island. Taiwan’s defence ministry said that 33 Chinese warplanes and eight warships had encircled Taiwan on Friday. China’s Taiwan Affairs Office has not yet commented on Saturday’s results.

Lai, Taiwan’s president, said in a Facebook post that he thanked “the people of Taiwan for their support and trust in the DPP’s nominated candidates”. Lai did not mention China in his statement.

Zhan Ying-yi, 51, another Shen supporter, said that he was “disappointed” in the result, “but that’s how democracy works”. “We can only respect the outcome. After all, we still have the freedom to vote and that is something we should cherish,” he said, adding that the election result had heightened his concerns about the threat posed by China.

“There’s only one thing I fear: that one day we might be ruled by the People’s Republic of China. Then we would lose everything.”
