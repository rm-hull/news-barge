The final weekend of Australia’s wettest spring on record for an El Niño year is delivering a deluge of storms and heavy rain.

Severe weather and flood watch warnings are in place across the eastern states as a weather band moves from central Queensland through New South Wales to Victoria and Tasmania.

The Bureau of Meteorology’s Helen Reid said El Niño attracted great interest but was only one factor to consider when looking at a season and the continent in its entirety.

“El Niño is part of it, and we have had quite a lot of rainfall in the south-east but that’s … also a balance of what we didn’t have earlier in the winter season,” she said.

Tropical humidity has drifted across the continent and is being swept into the eastern states.

“We are looking at a very active system that’s going to be affecting southern Queensland, inland NSW, Victoria and also into Tasmania,” Reid said.

Damaging winds were forecast for the NSW alpine region and Victoria’s central and eastern districts, which were also expecting heavy rainfall.

The heaviest overnight falls in Victoria were 85.4mm at Amphitheatre Reservoir in the northern Pyrenees region and 72.8mm at Bet Bet Creek in the state’s west.

Victorian State Emergency Service volunteers reported a busy 24 hours, with more than 340 calls for help to 6am on Saturday.

Most calls related to fallen trees (105), building damage (96) and flooding impacts (97).

The state’s north-east and eastern ranges can expect six-hourly rainfall totals from 55mm to 70mm on Saturday morning.

A moderate flood warning is in place for Warrill Creek in Queensland, with initial flood warnings in Tasmania for the Meander and Mersey rivers and flood watches for most of the catchments in western, central and north-east Victoria.

Meanwhile, a heatwave in the Northern Territory is easing but intensifying from severe to extreme in Western Australia’s Kimberley and Pilbara regions.

“It’s only going to be a shortlived heatwave but it is going to be a couple of hot days through there,” Reid said.

“Picking out Kununurra as an example, they’re looking at 42 or 43C for the next few days during the daytime but only dropping down to about 27C overnight.”

An offshore trough off WA’s coast would create a low-intensity heatwave stretching to just north of Perth.
