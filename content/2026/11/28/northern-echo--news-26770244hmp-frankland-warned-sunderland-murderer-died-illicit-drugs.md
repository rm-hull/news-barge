A murderer's death at a County Durham jail having taken a cocktail of drugs has prompted a warning from a coroner over drugs being smuggled inside.

Wayne Miller attacked his girlfriend's uncle twice in one night in Sunderland then took a "trophy picture" of his victim. The 36-year-old was found dead in his HMP Frankland cell in December 2024.

An inquest heard Miller had taken a cocktail of metonitazene and spice.

Nine days before his death CCTV spotted him swallowing a suspected illict package handed over during a prison visit.

A coroner has now warned he fears other inmates could die due to the prison's "unclear, confusing and unhelpful" policy on how it investigates "secreted items".

Assistant Coroner for County Durham and Darlinton Simon Connolly says it "constitutes an important aspect of the prison’s strategy to disrupt and prevent the ingress of illicit items and particularly illicit substances into HMP Frankland" as he issued a Prevention of Future Deaths report.

The prison service said it will "carefully consider the coroner’s findings and respond in due course".

An inquest Miller he was last seen alive at 8.37pm on December 13, 2024 and seeemd his 'usual self'.

He was found lying face down in his cell at 9.15am the next day during a routine roll call on the J-Wing, with yellow powder on his chest. Resuscitation was not attempted.

A jury concluded his death was drug-related and caused by the combined effects of the two drugs.

The inquest heard Miller had a history of illicit substance misuse while in prison and had received support from the prison staff.

But it was the circumstances of a suspected incident nine days before his death which prompted the coroner's warning.

## Suspected handover

On December 5, 2024, Miller was said to have been passed an illict item he swallowed during a social visit, with CCTV capturing the suspected handover.

The Coroner said he had concerns about how prison staff responded to the incident and whether the correct procedures were followed.

Despite a body scan taking place, Mr Connolly was concerned Miller was not immediately referred to healthcare staff despite the prison's policy describing the swallowing items as "extremely dangerous".

Mr Connolly described the policy as "unclear, confusing and unhelpful" and noted that the same wording remained more than a year and a half after the incident. He said senior members of prison staff had given differing interpretations of when the policy should be triggered, with some suggesting it required evidence that an item had been swallowed rather than simply a suspicion.

A man was arrested in May 2025 on suspicion of conveying a prohibited article into a prison but, despite a thorough investigation, no charges were brought.

The Coroner wrote: "In my opinion unless action is taken to address the above concerns then there is a significant risk of future deaths."

The report was sent to HMP Frankland on September 15, 2026, with the prison required to respond

A Prison Service spokesperson said: "We will carefully consider the coroner’s findings and respond to the report in due course."

## Brutal murder

Miller, who was born in Sunderland, was serving a life sentence for murdering his girlfriend's uncle, Andrew Mather, in 2020.

The then 32-year-old brutally attacked 48-year-old Mr Mather twice in one night at his Sunderland home before leaving him for dead.

He was jailed in December 2020 and ordered to serve a minimum of 21 years behind bars.

During a week-long trial in December 2020 Newcastle Crown Court heard there had been ill-feeling between the families of Miller’s girlfriend and Andrew which came to a head on the evening of June 2.

Neighbours on Aintree Road heard screaming and arguing at Andrew’s home about 7.30pm, with CCTV footage then following Miller’s movements thereafter.

At about 9pm, Miller and his girlfriend were seen walking away from the address – with the defendant wearing shorts and tan boots.
