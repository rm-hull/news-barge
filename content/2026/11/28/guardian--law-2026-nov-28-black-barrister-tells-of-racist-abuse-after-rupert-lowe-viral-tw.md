A newly qualified black barrister who took part in a group photo that went viral after Rupert Lowe cited the image as evidence of discrimination against white Britons has said he has never before experienced such racism in the UK.

Joseph Ashong, who was in a cohort attending their “call to the bar” ceremony in London, said he was amazed and upset by the reaction to the picture as it was viewed by millions on the afternoon of the ceremony.

Lowe, the leader of Restore Britain, the hard-right rival to Nigel Farage’s Reform UK party, reposted the formal photograph of 80 newly called barristers that was shared by the Middle Temple inn of court on Wednesday.

The Great Yarmouth MP wrote on X: “To those who still say White British men and women face no discrimination in our country, show them this image.”

Lowe’s post has been viewed 2.3m times. It was amplified by the far-right agitator known as Tommy Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, and it triggered a wave of racist abuse online. Millions more have since viewed the image and other group photographs shared by Middle Temple this week.

Isabel Oakeshott, the journalist and partner of Richard Tice, the deputy leader of Reform, also posted the photograph. “These are the judges of tomorrow,” she wrote. “One particular demographic appears to be missing. There is something very wrong with our country if we can’t say that there is something not quite right about this.”

According to a survey published in March by the Bar Standards Board, 82.3% of barristers in England and Wales are white. Official government statistics suggest that the proportion of judges from minority ethnic groups is 13% – up from 6% a decade ago. The photograph was just the latest of a series of call to the bar ceremonies held this year at Middle Temple, one of the four institutions entitled to call members to the English Bar as barristers.

Many international students come to the UK because English common law forms the foundation of legal systems in over a third of the world.

Ashong, who is from Ghana but earned a scholarship to pursue a career at the bar in the UK, where he arrived in 2023, was at the front of the image shared by Lowe, which was taken shortly before the ceremony. “If only I had been given a pass for being black,” he said of the tough journey to become a barrister.

Ashong, 36, said it amazed him that an MP had triggered the racism. “There may have been unconscious bias in the past but I have not experienced racism to this extent before,” he said.

Ashong said he had chosen to study at City St George’s, University of London, due to the shared English common law heritage between England and Wales and the Commonwealth countries. The Middle Temple has a strong tradition of taking on members from the Commonwealth.

“A friend reached out to me to say there’s a picture on Twitter which is making the rounds and they’ve seen my face in there,” Ashong said. “So I go to Twitter and I find that the MP is making a statement about discrimination. We know what is being insinuated: people who are supposed to look British, so to speak, now don’t and the bar is a mixed bag of people from all over the place, mostly black and brown people. It is ignorant.”

He added: “In my case, I was awarded the Harmsworth scholarship from Middle Temple. That means going through an intense application, going through interviews before you are finally merited the award.”

The Harmsworth scholarship was established by the great-grandfather of the current proprietor of the Daily Mail.

Ashong said he would apply for a pupillage – the final part of training to become a barrister – in the UK but that he was considering returning to Ghana to build a practice. “It’s a bit sickening because what’s going on out there is pure ignorance,” he said. “An MP of all people should know that there’s no design for immigrants to take over the bar.”

Yi Vonne, 23, who had been studying law at Cardiff University since 2024, was in a later group photograph that was also highlighted on social media as apparently showing bias against white people.

![Yi Vonne.](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/1bfe22b602a67ef2076d73542a23c8980d869f46/161_0_2500_2001/master/2500.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

She said she would return to Malaysia in December to start her pupillage but had opted for that day’s call to the bar, which was relatively late in the year, to allow for her mother to organise time off from work and join her on a “special day”. She said: “I’ve never been treated like this before here.”

Vonne said Middle Temple had offered for other groups to opt out of the photograph “because of all this racial abuse that’s going on”. “But of course, who wants to do that?” she said.

On Thursday, the Liberal Democrat MP Al Pinkerton raised the row in the Commons, saying that a “wonderful celebratory photograph of young barristers called to the bar at Middle Temple has become the subject of the most appalling racialised abuse, including, I am very sorry to say, by certain members of this house”.

Alan Campbell, the leader of the House of Commons, responded that it was “beyond inappropriate and entirely unacceptable for anyone to follow up online and make the sorts of comments that he talks about”.

Middle Temple has informed its members that the posts were “considered carefully at the most senior level” but that the “conclusion was that there is nothing to be gained from adding to the comments by Al Pinkerton MP in parliament”.

A Restore Britain spokesperson said: “Rupert’s post speaks for itself on the point he was making.”
