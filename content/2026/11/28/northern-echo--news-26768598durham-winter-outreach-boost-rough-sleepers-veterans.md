Durham County Council has reiterated its commitment to ending rough sleeping after the Prime Minister called for specific support for veterans.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has asked councils in England to draw up plans to offer accommodation to veterans sleeping rough before Christmas, according to the Prime Minister's Office.

Durham County Council says it is using some of its £643,000 allocation from the Government's Rough Sleeper Programme to increase the number of workers on duty.

The authority says the extra staff will help it identify and engage with people sleeping rough, including those who have served in the armed forces.

The council says it is looking at whether it can enhance its support further following the request.

The council's homelessness team provides support to people who are homeless or at risk of losing their homes, including prevention work and temporary or crisis accommodation where needed.

When someone sleeping rough is a veteran, the council says it works with the Armed Forces Outreach Service to provide enhanced support.

That can include working with armed forces charities such as SSAFA and specialist services including VALOUR Recognised Centres, where appropriate.

The council says it is also developing new processes to provide a route off the streets, which veterans will be eligible to access.

As previously reported in The Northern Echo, former soldier Blair Murray spent 15 weeks sleeping in woodland near Crook while waiting for social housing.

In August, he was in temporary hotel accommodation arranged by the council while a longer-term solution was sought.

Cllr Robert Potts, the council's armed forces champion, said: “Our support programme aims to get all rough sleepers off the streets, and our teams work hard to provide support all year round.”

Cllr Potts added: “However, we also take our duties under the Armed Forces Covenant seriously, so have measures in place to specifically support those who are homeless or sleeping rough and are also former members of the armed forces.”

He said: “These are people who have served our country.”

“Now it is our turn to support them.”

The Prime Minister's Office says veterans sleeping rough, or people supporting them, can call the UK-wide helpline on 0800 9520774 for housing and other specialist support.
