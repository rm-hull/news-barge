More than £2.5m is coming to North East communities to tackle stalking and child-to-parent violence and help prevent further victims of abuse.

Cleveland Police and Crime Commissioner Matt Storey will receive £2.59m from two Home Office funds for programmes that will run until 2029.

One will focus on stalking, while the other will address violence by children towards parents, with both bringing together several agencies.

The Cleveland Stalking Intervention Programme has been awarded £1,594,639.05.

Its multi-agency approach will focus on high-risk and complex cases, providing specialist help to manage perpetrators, coordinated safeguarding and victim advocacy.

It will complement existing responses and be used where risk, complexity or escalation means agencies working together can help protect victims and tackle harmful behaviour.

The Child to Parent Violence Programme has been awarded £997,394.95, with the aim of reducing harm and the severity of incidents, increasing feelings of safety and strengthening families.

It will provide a specialist Child and Adolescent Parent Violence and Abuse service, clinical support for children and tailored support for families from voluntary, community and social enterprise organisations.

Staff will also receive additional training.

The Home Office money was awarded after a competitive process, with the two bids recognised for their quality and partnership working.

Mr Storey said: "This additional funding from the Home Office will help us strengthen our work with perpetrators and those, who harm."

"It will allow us to address the root causes of behaviours and intervene earlier to prevent further harm."

"This investment will support our wider commitment to preventing violence, safeguarding vulnerable people and breaking the cycle of abuse across Cleveland."

"Ultimately tackling perpetrator behaviour is about preventing future victims and creating safer communities."

The programmes will involve Cleveland Police, North East Probation Service, a mental health trust, children's social care, Youth Justice Services and specialist voluntary and community organisations.

Earlier this year, more than 100 children's practitioners in the North East received training to identify stalking behaviour in young people, including at two events held in Cleveland.
