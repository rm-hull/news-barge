# Exclusive:Linton-on-Ouse asylum camp decision to be made in 2027 over feasibility assessment delays

## The Government has pledged to close asylum hotels and wants to move migrants into a former RAF base in North Yorkshire.

The decision on whether to turn former RAF Linton-on-Ouse into an asylum camp will be made in the first part of 2027 at the earliest after the feasibility assessment has been delayed, The Yorkshire Post can reveal.

The Home Office has identified the Ministry of Defence site in the North Yorkshire village as one of three - as well as Piddington in Oxfordshire and Barnham in Suffolk - it wants to use to house migrants to help shut all asylum hotels.

This would see the prospect of the population of the small village of 650 people being tripled with the arrival of around 1,300 men.

The Home Office has been carrying out feasibility work on the site, which failed a technical assessment when a Conservative government tried to turn it into an asylum camp in 2022.

![RAF Linton-On-Ouse in North Yorkshire. PIC: Owen Humphreys/PA Wire](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjZhYmI1MjYyLWVhNGMtNDk2OC05YzU2LWIxY2QzNTExMzNhNjphYjJiNDZlMi1hZDBkLTRhNjctYjZmOC1hNGQyYTRhYmQwOGY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Campaigners have maintained that issues with utilities, such as electricity and water, mean that the base - first opened in 1937 to carry out propaganda drops and bombing raids during the Second World War - cannot be used to house migrants.

Earlier this month, planning permission was granted to turn Piddington into an asylum camp, despite the villagers voting in a symbolic referendum to leave the UK.

This paper can reveal that a decision on Linton-on-Ouse will not be made until the first part of 2027 due to extended feasibility work.

In a letter to York and North Yorkshire Deputy Mayor for Policing Jo Coles, the Home Office’s asylum accommodation director Louisa Le Roux explained that the assessment had gone beyond the expected autumn deadline.

She said: “We are engaging with policing, health, local authorities and other statutory partners to understand existing capacity, potential impacts and any measures that would need to be put in place if the proposal were to proceed.

“The feasibility work had previously been expected to conclude in late autumn. That work remains ongoing and we have since advised key stakeholders that a decision is not expected before early 2027.”

![Campaigners met with Shadow Ministers at the Linton on Ouse site on Friday to discuss the plans](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmFjYTFjNGU0LWNiNTEtNDVjMS04MGM5LWZjNzE4NzIwODE4MzoxODIzYjlmOS1kYTJmLTQ0YjUtYTIzNC1hOWE1ZjAyM2IxNTg=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


Ms Le Roux also confirmed that the MoD had only leased the site for three years, so the prospective camp could be wound up after that.

She added: “As discussed at our meeting, ongoing surveys, feasibility activity and routine site-management arrangements should not be interpreted as evidence that a decision has been taken.

“These activities are necessary to assess the site’s suitability and inform future decisions, but they do not predetermine the outcome.”

Both Ms Coles and York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith are contesting against the Home Office’s decision over concerns around policing and housing capacity.

The Labour mayor has raised concerns with Prime Minister Andy Burnham that it will block valuable brownfield land that could be used to build thousands of houses.

Ms Coles has written to the Home Office previously on behalf of North Yorkshire Police with concerns over policing capacity.

Ms Le Roux said that her colleagues “would be happy to engage directly with you and North Yorkshire Police about the arrangements under which eligible additional costs may be considered”.

This includes the prospect of protests from both right-wing and left-wing groups.

A Home Office spokesperson said: “We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, engaging with local authorities to reduce the impact on communities.

"This includes closing every asylum hotel and moving to alternative accommodation, including former military sites, saving taxpayers over £1bn on asylum support since the election.”
