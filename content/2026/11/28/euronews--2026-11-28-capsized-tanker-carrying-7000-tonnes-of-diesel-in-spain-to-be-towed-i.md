## The ship, carrying about 7,000 tonnes of diesel, will be towed on its side despite objections from regional officials and could reach the Spanish port of Cartagena as early as Monday.

The chemical tanker that sank on Wednesday with 7,000 tonnes of diesel aboard will be towed “immediately” to the Spanish port of Cartagena despite environmental concerns, Spain’s transport ministry said on Friday.

The Maltese-flagged Med Emre, which capsized between Valencia and the holiday island of Ibiza, is expected to reach the Spanish port as early as Monday 30 November.

“After checking the structural integrity and seaworthiness of the ship, the towing operation to a safe port will begin immediately, taking advantage of the favourable weather conditions,” the ministry said in a statement.

The port, in an industrial area of the southeastern region of Murcia, met the depth, size and safety requirements for unloading the fuel, the ministry said. With conditions at sea liable to worsen, Cartagena “offers a calm environment” close to pollution response, logistics and petrochemical facilities.

Murcia’s regional president, Fernando López Miras, however, has objected to the move and demanded that the central government find another location, citing “a potential risk to our coastline and our marine ecosystems”.

López Miras said ten protected areas in Cartagena could be affected. He also warned of the risk to the Valle de Escombreras, which he said accounts for “approximately 40% of the fuel supply in our country”.

Environmental organisations have also argued that the area’s flora and fauna, including endangered species, and warned of risks to fishing and tourism.

The tanker has been stabilised in open water and the fuel has so far been contained, though fears of a major spill remain.

## Tanker’s owner pledges to minimise environmental impact

The vessel’s owner, the Turkish company YMN Tankers, said it was carrying out a salvage plan to minimise the environmental impact.

In a statement on LinkedIn, the company expressed its “deep concern about the incident” and pledged to “take all necessary measures to minimise the possible impact on the marine environment”.

“We are carrying out the rescue plan for the vessel in coordination with the Spanish authorities, maritime rescue experts and other relevant groups,” the company said.

The ship’s chief engineer died in the early hours of Thursday as a result of the accident, according to the owner.

Valencia’s harbourmaster, Elena Delgado, said waves exceeded four metres during the storm. One possibility, she said, was that inspection hatches on one side of the vessel had been left open, allowing water into the ballast tanks and causing the ship to list and eventually capsize.

The tanker will remain on its side throughout the tow to the port, as it has been since it sank on Wednesday.

Unloading the fuel and bringing the ship upright will take “several weeks”, the ministry said.
