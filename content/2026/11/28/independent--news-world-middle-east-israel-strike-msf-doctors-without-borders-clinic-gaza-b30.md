An Israeli strike on a Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) clinic in Gaza killed two staff members, the medical charity has said.

The two men were watchmen at the clinic and they were killed in a direct strike on Friday night, MSF said.

“Our premises were clearly marked with MSF logos and their location had been communicated to and was known by the Israeli military,” said the charity, which described itself as “devastated and outraged” by the attack.

MSF, also known as Doctors Without Borders, said the latest killings brought to 17 the number of its colleagues killed in the besieged Palestinian territory.

The Israeli military described the two men as Hamas operatives who were "acting as security guards at a clinic operated by MSF", without giving any evidence. The Israeli military added it was another example of Hamas "embedding itself within international organizations".

It named them as Ahmed Sufyan Muhammad Ali Abu Hasira and Moaz Yousef Alitsbach. It alleged they had "advanced plans to carry out attacks against Israeli civilians and [Israeli] troops".

Dr Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, the head of WHO, described attacks on health workers and facilities in Gaza as "outrageous and unacceptable".

"Those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked," he said in a post on X.

![A Palestinian staff member at Doctors Without Borders visits the site in the clinic grounds where two colleagues were killed in an Israeli strike in Gaza](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/08/2306679039..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The strike on the MSF clinic came despite a ceasefire between Israel and Hamas, brokered by the US in October last year. Israeli strikes take place almost daily in areas under the control of Hamas, which are home to more than million people, most of them displaced by the Israeli war on Hamas inside the territory.

Israeli forces have killed at least 1,705 people since the ceasefire began, according to Gaza’s health ministry.

The UN and its partners have called on Israel, which controls Gaza's crossings, to let in more supplies to help Palestinians prepare for the impending winter, including tents, heaters and water pumps, as well as vaccines and medicines to combat infectious diseases.

The Gaza war was triggered by the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, when about 1,200 people were killed and 251 others were taken hostage.

Since then, more than 74,770 people have been killed in Israeli attacks in Gaza, the health ministry says.
