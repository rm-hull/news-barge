Counter-terrorism police are monitoring a far-right group whose members pose as security guards and which has threatened to disrupt a Muslim event, the Guardian can reveal.

Sources in counter-terrorism policing confirmed to the Guardian that members of British Security UK were being monitored, with two on bail for public order offences.

Twelve people were arrested in July after police were made aware of a “potential serious threat” towards a large Muslim event in Suffolk called an ijtima, which means a “large gathering” in Arabic.

At the event, people attended talks, watched the World Cup in a local pub, and shared food. The event, of 15,000 people who gathered at Shrubland Hall in Barham, Suffolk, was cut short on police advice.

Counter-terrorism police have confirmed that those arrested were members of a group known as British Security UK (BSUK).

The organisation is made up of uniformed figures wearing all black and body armour, and they have been seen in the entourages of far-right figures including the convicted criminal Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, who goes by the name Tommy Robinson, and Paul Golding, the leader of the far-right political party Britain First.

The registered director of BSUK, Anthony Roberts, has volunteered as a security guard for Yaxley-Lennon and Golding.

Police added that the investigation involved searching homes and vehicles, and more than 35 digital devices were seized. Most of those arrested – including Roberts – were released without charge, but a man and a woman remained on bail, they added.

This group of “security volunteers” has also been seen at far-right protests, most recently last week in London and in Oxford, where the controversial “race philosopher” Nathan Cofnas was due to speak. Some were seen scuffling with anti-racism protesters. They have also provided security for the rightwing provocateur Katie Hopkins.

One of their number was convicted this year for weapons offences after being arrested in connection with the planned disruption of the Suffolk event.

Darren Brown, 42, of Basildon, was driving with a stun gun and a baton when he was arrested. He was sentenced to six months in prison, suspended for 12 months, for the weapons offences. They were not related to the alleged threat towards the ijtima event, according to court documents. BSUK has confirmed he was one of its members.

Roberts, 53, appears to be a supporter of the hard-right political party Restore Britain, and has posed for photographs with its leader, Rupert Lowe. He has confirmed he was one of those arrested in July as part of the counter-terrorism investigation.

Writing on social media, Roberts complained he was “labelled a terrorist with no apology”, and added that “British security project and its team [were] wrongly accused of terrorism”. He has asked for an apology from counter-terrorism police for calling him and his group “extreme rightwing terrorists” and confirmed he is “on a terrorist watch list”. Roberts also confirmed he was “held in custody” at the time.

Prior to the arrests, the group filmed the Muslim event using a drone, and posted the footage on social media.

The video shows a masked man dressed all in black launching a drone, while a similarly disguised companion waves a St George flag. Others in the group are seen creeping on to the estate through bushes.

The video was posted with the caption: “We won’t stop til they do” and was soundtracked with the song Seven Nation Army by the White Stripes.

Those who attended the ijtima have spoken of their distress at being evacuated because of a suspected terrorism threat.

A local councillor, Ruman Muhith, said the disruption was “surreal”, and added that the gathering had been a “wonderful event” attended by people from all over the world. On one of the evenings, he said, attenders had “put their England tops on” and gone to the local pub to watch the match against Norway.

He added of the event: “Having it cut short because of a threat was deeply upsetting. Everyone there had the right to gather peacefully and return home safely. Nobody should have to weigh up their personal safety before attending a religious event.

“What stayed with me was the dignity of the response. People listened, followed the advice and left peacefully. I thanked the police for their intervention then, and I stand by that. The officers and organisers put people’s safety first.”

Sohayb Peerbhai, an imam at Craven Arms Islamic Centre, was among those evacuated. Writing in the Independent, he said: “The planned duaa (supplication) and prayers on Sunday morning came to a sudden halt, the closing ceremony was cancelled and thousands of guests were evacuated en masse within two hours, packing bags as quickly as possible and preparing for long journeys home.

“As WhatsApp groups exploded with rumour, many wondered if we would meet danger on our way out. The armed police presence – rifles at the ready – at nearby roads was reassuring, but also upsetting. At the bottom of our hearts, we knew it shouldn’t be like this.”

BSUK says its members volunteer as security guards for political figures and at rightwing protests, but they are not accredited.

Usually security guards are asked to have a Security Industry Authority qualification, meaning they know how to de-escalate situations, only use proportionate force and are trained in keeping members of the public safe.

Roberts has been contacted for comment.
