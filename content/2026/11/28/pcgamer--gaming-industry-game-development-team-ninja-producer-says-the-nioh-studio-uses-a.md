Team Ninja producer Kohei Shibata has revealed that the studio behind Nioh and Wo Long: Fallen Dynasty uses AI in its development processes. But he stressed that games should still ultimately be crafted by humans.

Speaking to INVEN Global at South Korea's CGON 2026 (via Eurogamer), Shibata said that the studio uses "AI in QA and various verification tasks to streamline certain workflows."

Shibata argued that if AI can "shorten task times" then developers can focus their efforts on "elevating the game's overall quality," although he caveated that "since it's still a new technology, we are continuously evaluating the most appropriate ways to implement it."

One thing Shibata emphasises, however, is that the quality of a game still rests upon human-centred design. "What matters fundamentally is that a game becomes fun when crafted by human hands. Repetitive, simple tasks that anyone must perform can be streamlined by technology. But ultimately, deciding how to craft the game and what makes it enjoyable must be done by humans."

He also pointed out that "using AI itself should never become the goal. I believe the principle that humans must think for themselves and finish the game will remain unchanged in the future."

![Wo Long: Fallen Dynasty - Launch Trailer - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/pi7vQOpfquo/maxresdefault.jpg)

It's frustrating that Shibata doesn't go into detail about what he means by "AI" here. The term is now applied to an extremely broad range of technologies alongside large-scale LLMs, and this reveal doesn't necessarily mean Team Ninja is getting ChatGPT to do its QA for it. Back in October, Capcom announced that it planned to turn the RE Engine into an "AI generation game engine" but what it meant was it was implementing machine-learning tech into its engine to improve its performance and data handling, and not that it was pivoting to AI-generating Leon Kennedy or whatever.

Presumably, Team Ninja's application of AI runs along similar lines, but it's hard to say without knowing specifics. Either way, this is not to dismiss people's concerns about AI's growing presence in game development. A recent survey of game developers reported that, while 40% of developers have now used AI at some point, more than half believe the tech is having a negative impact on the games industry. Many of these negative impacts are easy to spot, too, from Steam being inundated with AI slop, to PC hardware becoming increasingly unaffordable.
