![Homer Simpson screams, running through vehicular carnage as part of the box art for The Simpsons: Hit &amp; Run.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/jiqfF2vgbYwtQCLgsWpq4S-1920-80.png)

Earlier this year, rumours began to circulate that a remake of The Simpsons: Hit & Run was in the works, rumours which were lent further credence after Simpsons creator Matt Groening implied that a return of the PS2-era GTA clone was on the cards. Now though, it appears that this ephemeral project may have been scrapped as part of a larger swath of cancellations.

On LinkedIn (via Eurogamer) game designer Darren Evenson announced that he had been let go from New Radical Games, a studio formed from the ashes of Radical Entertainment, which developed The Simpsons: Hit & Run as well as the open-world superhero game Prototype. New Radical was rumoured to be working on a redux of Hit & Run, but it seems that, if that was indeed the case, it no longer is.

"It seems that not even a fantastic project with an incredible team is immune from the current state of the industry right now, as what we were working on at New Radical Games (although mind blowingly awesome) was part of a whole gaggle of cancellations. A gaggle I say!" Evenson wrote. He then added: "Oh you silly suits with spreadsheets… will you EVER learn? If you don't make the game, you can't make the money…and if you don't make the money then you don't get the power…and… well, there's more to that quote, but I digress."

As for why this post may refer to The Simpsons: Hit & Run, alongside New Radical's historical connection to the tie-in, Evenson's post contains several phrases that echo lines spoken in the TV Show. For example, in the episode Marge be Not Proud Troy McClure says "Oh, Shakazaramesh, will you ever learn?", while in Lisa's Rival, Homer protects a pile of sugar, saying "In America, first you get the sugar, then you get the power, then you get the women." This latter line is itself a reference to the movie Scarface, however, and Radical Entertainment also worked on a Scarface game, so it still isn't wholly clear what project Radical was working on.

It also isn't clear why the project was cancelled, or which publisher cancelled it. But Evenson says that he's now looking for work as a result of the project's premature end. "Please reach out, shout out, or perform some interpretive dance if you know of an opportunity that a hip cat like me could groove to."
