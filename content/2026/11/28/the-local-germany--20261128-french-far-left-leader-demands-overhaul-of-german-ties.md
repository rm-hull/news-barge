The France Unbowed (LFI) party leader, who is polling in second place for April's election, told French broadcaster LCI during a Berlin visit that he sees a "need for a thorough overhaul of the French-German relationship".

"I am hostile to the liberal (economic) policy of German governments that dominates the European Union," the 75-year-old said ahead of the launch of the German edition of his latest book at Berlin's Babylon cinema.

Mélenchon's economic platform, which includes a proposal to cancel French debt held by the European Central Bank, is dramatically opposed to traditional German doctrines of tight budgets and low borrowing.

Mélenchon -- currently the favourite to reach a run-off against far-right veteran Marine Le Pen -- has also railed against Chancellor Friedrich Merz's commitment to transform the German military into the "strongest conventional army in Europe".

"We have the duty to ask: to make war against whom?" he said Friday, warning that Paris could be drawn into unwanted conflict as Germany's growing military strength makes it a "target" for other countries.

Mélenchon's rivals have accused him of cosying up to President Vladimir Putin's Russia, with LFI's pledge to leave NATO a particular concern for many of France's allies amid the ongoing war in Ukraine.

The far-left party leader has been highly critical of Germany's position in the conflict, accusing Merz's government of sabre-rattling.

"They need to tone it down, because all this one-upmanship leads nowhere," he said.

At the book launch, the conflict between Russia and Ukraine was not mentioned directly, but Mélenchon said he would "immediately" begin discussions with China in the event of his victory in the election.

"We do not want war with China and we will not help the Americans to make war against the Chinese," he said.

Earlier, Mélenchon flatly denied charges he is "Germanophobic", calling himself a "sort of disciple of Karl Marx" and a lover of Beethoven.

He also expressed pride at the German translation of his latest book, published in English under the title: "Now, the People! Revolution in the 21st Century."

The politician further touted his close ties with Germany's far-left Die Linke party, which finished first in Berlin's state election in September.

Mélenchon met the party's national leader Ines Schwerdtner on Friday, saying the two were "united in mobilising against an economy of rearmament and war".
