For many people across the North East, a trip to Wynyard Hall's annual Christmas Fayre has become as much a festive tradition as decorating the tree or watching Christmas films.

The popular event attracts thousands of visitors each year, transforming the historic estate into a winter shopping destination packed with independent traders, festive food, gifts and seasonal entertainment.

Its popularity has also spread across social media, where TikTok users have been sharing their experiences of the market.

One creator, Mica Jasmine Newcomb, described the event as the moment Christmas officially begins, telling followers: "It's officially Christmas once we have done Wynyard Christmas Markets."

In her video, she highlighted the vast number of stalls inside and outside the hall, artisan gifts, festive decorations and food vendors, describing it as the "ultimate girls' day" and a yearly tradition for her family.

Its sheer scale helps make it one of the region’s standout Christmas markets.

Visitors can browse dozens of independent businesses selling everything from handmade gifts and homeware to candles, fashion accessories and festive decorations.

The markets extend beyond the estate grounds and into multiple rooms inside Wynyard Hall, giving shoppers plenty to explore throughout the day.

For those looking to get ahead with Christmas shopping, the fayre offers the chance to support local and regional businesses while finding gifts that are not typically available on the high street.

Food and drink are another major draw, with seasonal treats, sweet and savoury snacks, hot drinks and festive refreshments available across the site.

Combined with the backdrop of the historic hall and its Christmas decorations, it creates an atmosphere that many visitors return for year after year.

Unlike some festive attractions, tickets remain relatively affordable for the whole family.

Adult admission costs £8, while concessions and children aged four to 13 cost £7. Children under four can attend free of charge.

Running over three days, the event features morning and afternoon entry slots, with organisers advising visitors to book ahead due to high demand.
