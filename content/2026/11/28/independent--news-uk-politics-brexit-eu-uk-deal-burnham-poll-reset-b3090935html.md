The public back Andy Burnham’s landmark deal with the European Union and are willing to risk reopening the Brexit debate to secure a closer relationship with the bloc, a major new poll reveals.

More than half of voters believe it is worth risking a fresh political row, while just a third disagree, the survey shared exclusively with *The Independent* found.

The findings appear to vindicate Mr Burnham’s agreement as well as his push to explore options to get closer to the bloc – including potentially rejoining – despite pressure for him to drop the plan.

On Monday, the prime minister hit back at critics and accused his predecessors of being “stuck in the past”, after five former prime ministers – Boris Johnson, Liz Truss, Lord David Cameron, Baroness Theresa May and Sir Tony Blair – questioned whether the Brexit debate should be reopened.

![Andy Burnham has reopened the debate around Brexit (Hannah McKay/PA)](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/23/17/09226eb1ede48e4072ec55b120a164adY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1NTQwMjk3-2.86549762.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Tory leader Kemi Badenoch also accused Mr Burnham of “looking backwards”, adding: “Reopening the Brexit debate is the last thing our country needs right now.”

But the new poll, by More in Common, which was carried out after the deal was agreed, shows the British public supports both the agreement and the PM’s decision to reopen the conversation around Brexit.

It found 55 per cent think the deal benefits the UK – or the UK and the EU equally – while just 31 per cent think the opposite. Fourteen per cent don’t know.

More than half, 52 per cent, are also willing to risk reopening the Brexit debate if it means a closer relationship with the bloc, compared to 33 per cent who think it is not worth reigniting divisions.

![The new EU-UK deal will bring in a long-awaited youth mobility scheme and end food and drink checks at the border](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/19/01M7CH79KZGQKX4YNH4DGFTRHZ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Significantly for Mr Burnham, 80 per cent of Labour voters think the risk is worth it.

*The Independent*has been at the forefront of the debate with its* Europe: The Way Back* campaign, which is calling for the UK to rebuild its relationship with Europe.

The poll also found two-thirds believe rejoining the EU would require a referendum – ruling out the idea it could be simply included in a general election manifesto.

The PM conceded as much on Monday, saying it would be “unthinkable” to rejoin the EU without another In/Out referendum.

Earlier this month, Mr Burnham secured an agreement which will bring about a long-awaited youth mobility scheme and end food and drink checks at the border, in what the government says would be a £17bn boost to the British economy.

The public is split – 44 to 38 per cent – on whether the youth mobility scheme will create or take jobs. Most young people are optimistic about its effects, with 53 per cent of under-30s believing it will create employment and opportunities, compared to just 24 per cent who think the opposite.

![Former prime minister Boris Johnson accused the PM of attempting to reopen ‘civil war’ around Brexit](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/25/10/01M87FMZW5EQEKMW60SNF44CWZ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage has said that, if elected, his party would ditch the entire agreement, while Tory leader Ms Badenoch has accused the PM of a sell-out.

The poll also reveals what relationship with the EU the public thinks Mr Burnham wants.

The biggest group, 39 per cent, think the PM is trying to rejoin the EU, while 34 per cent think he wants to get closer to the bloc but stop short of actual membership, while 13 per cent believe he wants to keep things as they are.

Luke Tryl, the executive director of More in Common, said: “As a whole, Burnham’s new deal has landed well with the public.

“Britons tend to think it benefits the UK and EU equally, and are open to closer ties with Europe. And while few want to return to the endless Brexit stalemates of the 2016-2019 era, more than half of Britons think it’s worth reopening the debate if it brings us a closer relationship to the EU.”

He said the EU “remains a political danger zone” for the PM, however, adding: “Telling a clear story about UK-EU relations will be increasingly important.”

Stella Creasy, Labour MP and chair of the Labour Movement, said: “The people who campaigned for this Brexit want you to believe nobody wants to talk about fixing their mess. The truth is that most of the British public are not stuck in 2016. They can see how the world is changed and they want this government to do something about it.

“The next step is to talk to our European partners about the options on the table, so that whatever comes next is tried and tested, and not a repeat of chaos caused by the Brexiteers taking us out of the EU without a plan.”

Al Pinkerton, the Lib Dem Europe spokesperson, called on Mr Burnham to “seize this momentum”.

He added: “These results confirm what we’ve said for years: the Conservatives and Nigel Farage’s botched Brexit experiment has failed. The country is ready for a much closer, constructive relationship with our European neighbours, to cut costs for businesses, get growth into our economy, and tackle the cost-of-living crisis.”

Tom Brufatto, policy and research director at Best for Britain, said: “While the polling shows the public believes the summit deals were the right thing to do, the real takeaway is for Labour MPs who can see precisely where 80 per cent of their voting bloc want to go – and that’s closer to Europe.”

And Emma Knaggs, deputy chief executive of European Movement UK, said: “The key to the success of this deal is that this is reciprocal.

“It is good for both the UK and the EU. We are happy that this polling shows that people can see past the old divisive arguments of the last decade and focus on making our economy stronger and people’s lives better.”

A government spokesperson said: “The status quo helped drive up food prices, saddling British businesses with costly paperwork and the threat of new carbon border taxes on approximately £6.5bn worth of British industrial exports.

“The deal we have secured will slash red tape on business and put downward pressure on food prices. It offers opportunities to young people that were lost in the Brexit settlement and will deliver up to £17bn a year in growth by 2040.”

More in Common polled 2,533 adults between 20 and 23 November.
