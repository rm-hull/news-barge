Ukraine is planning to completely destroy the main bridge connecting Russia with Crimea, a report claims – just days after fresh strikes paralysed Vladimir Putin’s showcase crossing.

Strikes last week partially destroyed the prized link to the occupied peninsula and bolstered a months-long campaign to sever Moscow’s supply lines to the battlefields of southern and eastern Ukraine.

An emboldened president Volodymyr Zelensky has now told his commanders he wants to see the rest of the Kerch Bridge demolished, military sources told *The Telegraph*.

“The president wants it demolished completely,” one insider told the newspaper, adding that further strikes were being prepared.

![A collapsed section of the Kerch bridge following Ukrainian strikes](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/8/33/kerch.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine dealt a series of blows to the bridge last week, destroying the central road span and damaging railway tracks in a symbolic show of confidence that left Putin visibly rattled.

While the Russian leader denied the bridge was being used for military purposes, the Neptune missile strikes show Ukraine’s growing ability to pressure Moscow with homegrown technology.

They also marked the first time the bridge had sustained such persistent damage from Ukrainian strikes, according to Emily Ferris of the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI) think tank.

John Helin, a visiting fellow at the Atlantic Council, said the strikes demonstrated Kyiv's capacity to strike Russian logistics across the Azov Sea and its occupied southeast as Moscow’s advances slow.

“The more important story here is not necessarily the destruction of the Crimean Bridge itself, but the growing pressure Ukraine is putting on Russia's logistics system as a whole,” he told Reuters.

![A drone view of what appears to be smoke rising and explosions at the Crimea bridge on November 17](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/22/04/2026-11-20T074005Z_1453040575_RC2QPOA4W8KU_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-RUSSIA-LOGISTICS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Over the summer, Ukraine to isolate Crimea with attacks on massive tankers ferrying fuel to the peninsula, sparking widespread shortages.

Months earlier, Ukrainian forces targeted Russian supply lines on the M-14 highway through the country’s occupied south - a major artery feeding Moscow’s war effort on the front.

With the Crimean Bridge partly disabled, Russia now has few alternatives but to force more traffic through that heavily targeted roadway, said Franz-Stefan Gady, a Vienna-based expert with the Center for a New American Security.

“More traffic means more targets,” he said, adding that a “sudden logistical breakdown” would still be unlikely, because Russia has developed evasive tactics.

Still, the developments underscore how Ukraine has shifted its footing to squeeze the invading force and make the continuation of the war less palatable to the Kremlin.

![The aftermath of a Russian drone attack in Chernihiv region, in a photo released on November 28](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/09/2026-11-28T083418Z_450490706_RC2WWOAZSPVN_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ATTACK-CHERNIHIV-REGION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine only last weekend sank a Russian naval corvette and a cargo ship in Libya, a day after a drone strike on a Russian military-linked cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland.

For Russia, now losing access to the Crimean Bridge would ultimately affect military and civilian logistics in Crimea more than Moscow's battlefield operations across southern Ukraine, said Gady.

“The real question is whether Ukraine can keep the bridge out while also sustaining pressure on the corridor,” he said. “Doing both at once is what would actually squeeze Russia.”

Russia’s defence ministry said meanwhile that air defences had shot down 1,246 Ukrainian drones overnight, as Kyiv steps up its waves of aerial attacks.

More than 400 drones were launched towards Russian capital Moscow overnight, of which 123 were downed in close proximity to the city, mayor Sergei Sobyanin said on Telegram.

![A woman holds a child in her arms near damaged buildings following Russian drone attacks in Odesa on November 18](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/16/2305172944..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukraine’s foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said on Friday that Russia had asked for a ceasefire on the Russian oil sector as part of a Black Sea ceasefire to enable food exports.

He said that Kyiv would only consider such a truce as part of a wider energy ceasefire deal.

Ukraine still faces an uphill battle in securing viable peace terms, with US president Donald Trump seen to be aligning closer with Russia’s Putin despite Washington’s mediating role in talks.

Zelensky told the BBC this week that it was “strange” that Trump had called Putin his “friend” at a recent summit in China.

“He's an aggressor ... and a killer. He can't be a friend,” Zelensky said of Putin, speaking in English.

![Russia's President Vladimir Putin and US President Donald Trump shake hands on the sidelines of the Apec Leaders' Summit in China](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/26/17/2026-11-26T162433Z_1015617438_RC2TQOANQQ5V_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-TRUMP.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“It's the same that somebody say about Hitler during the Second World War that he's a good friend. No, he's not a friend.”

Trump's relationship with Zelensky has long been uneasy, most memorably breaking down publicly in February 2025 when the US president rebuked him in the Oval Office.

More recently, the pair have clashed as Trump blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries for high energy costs and threatened to cut off intelligence support, while Zelensky has said Trump's position is encouraging Russia to continue attacks on Ukraine.
