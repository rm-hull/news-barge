A giant Scalextric and a disco are taking visitors back through Christmases past at a North East light trail.

Northern Lights Newcastle opened at Leazes Park on November 20 and will run until December 31, with installations inspired by toys and traditions from previous generations.

Called A Christmas Time Machine, the after-dark walking trail combines light, music and interactive displays and is being staged by events company From The Fields.

It is the fourth year Northern Lights has been held at the park, with this year's route taking in everything from Rubik's Cubes and a Slinky to a Christmas catalogue.

![An adult and child walk beneath an illuminated arch on a wooded path.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21770892.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An illuminated arch at the launch of Northern Lights Newcastle in 2026. *(Image: Kevin Gibson)*

Visitors enter through a tunnel of light before reaching a 1960s-inspired scene, where lava-lamp colours spread across the park's bandstand and along the path.

At Nutcracker Salute, a group of giant nutcrackers stands alongside commands projected underfoot, inviting families to hop, twirl, skip and jump their way along the route.

Further installations include mirrored kaleidoscope arches, 65 UV-lit lights inspired by Rubik's Cubes in the trees and a giant rainbow Slinky overhead.

A large illuminated storybook tells The Night Before Christmas, while elves at the North Pole Parcel Post can be seen crafting, wrapping and arguing over missing sticky tape.

![Visitors gather beside an illuminated storybook showing Santa in a woodland setting.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21770893.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An illuminated storybook at the 2026 launch of Northern Lights Newcastle. *(Image: Kevin Gibson)*

Midway around the trail, Disco Wonderland turns the route into a dance floor, while visitors can also race on the giant glowing Scalextric.

Young patients who have had extended stays at the Great North Children's Hospital have contributed drawings of classic toys, which are projected on the trail as a reimagined Christmas catalogue.

The organisers also say the event includes a collaboration with the Alan Shearer Foundation's Move a Mile campaign.

Emma St.Croix, senior marketing manager at From The Fields, said: “Since January, we’ve been reimagining every part of the experience.

“A Christmas Time Machine takes visitors on a journey through the festive moments that stay with us long after the decorations come down.”

![Visitors gather near food stalls and an illuminated Ferris wheel at night.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21770894.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Visitors enjoy the Christmas Village at the 2026 launch of Northern Lights Newcastle. *(Image: Kevin Gibson)*

The trail has a Christmas Village with food, drinks and fire pits, as well as vintage fairground rides and places to roast marshmallows.

Among the food traders, Davey's serves smash burgers and BagHead offers festive spice bags.

Two inclusive evenings are planned for December 2 and December 9, with visitor numbers reduced by 75 per cent and audio levels lowered throughout the route.

Organisers say the sessions are intended to provide a calmer environment for neurodivergent visitors and others who prefer a more relaxed experience.

![A visitor walks along a path through brightly lit trees and illuminated festive figures.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21770895.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A visitor walks through the Northern Lights Newcastle trail at its 2026 launch. *(Image: Kevin Gibson)*

The route has wheelchair-friendly paths, ramp access points and seating throughout, while access time slots and queue-jump wristbands are available for visitors with additional needs.

Tickets start at £19.50 for adults and £13.50 for children, and a £1.75 transaction fee applies to each order.

Children under three and essential companions can attend free of charge.
