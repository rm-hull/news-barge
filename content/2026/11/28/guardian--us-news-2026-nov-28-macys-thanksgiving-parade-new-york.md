The rescheduled Macy’s Thanksgiving Day parade kicked off on Saturday in New York City after severe storms brought heavy rain, winds and flooding that disrupted travel across the country.

Bands scheduled to perform in New York rallied to adjust their schedules, hotel stays and travel after the parade was postponed for the first time in its 100-year history.

NBC quoted Macy’s as saying: “The safety of our participants and spectators is our priority.”

On Saturday morning, more than 186,000 Americans were still without power, according to Poweroutage.us. The National Weather Service said that while conditions were expected to improve across the eastern US, “this huge system will take some time to weaken”, according to the Associated Press.

Bands originally scheduled to perform on Thursday had to scramble after the postponement. As one of the 10 marching bands scheduled to perform, the Nā Koa Aliʻi Hawaiʻi All-State’s Marching Band created a donation page to stay in New York. On Friday night, they announced that they raised more than $800,000 to cover the added costs of extending their stay.

Alaska Airlines and Hawaiian Airlines added a Saturday-evening flight from New York to Seattle to help the group’s members and their supporters get home after the rescheduled parade, waiving change and baggage fees, the airline told CNN. The travelers will connect in Seattle to a flight to Hawaii, landing on Sunday.

Similarly, Tennessee’s Dobyns-Bennett High School Band was left with a costly bill that they were not prepared for after the news of the parade’s postponement.

The band’s performance was two years in the making after spending 18 months fundraising, including “lots of selling of fruit and various products”, the band’s director, Lafe Cook, told CNN.

After seeking support on social media, the band successfully raised enough money to stay in New York for their performance.

Even though some bands, such as the University of Alabama’s Million Dollar Band, had to miss the parade due to scheduling conflicts, the parade’s executive producer, Will Coss, called the community support proof of “the power of the parade”.
