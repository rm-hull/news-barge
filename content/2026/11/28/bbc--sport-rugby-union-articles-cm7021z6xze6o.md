Wales beat Japan to finish autumn fixtures

**Nations ChampionshipWales (26) 40Tries:** Adams 2, Murray, Morgan-Williams, Morgan 2**Cons:** Costelow 5**Japan (0) 10Tries:** Komura, Vailea**Wales finished their Nations Championship campaign with another victory against Japan in a six-try display at the Allianz Stadium in Twickenham.**

Wing Josh Adams and replacement flanker Jac Morgan claimed two tries apiece, with full-back Blair Murray and scrum-half Reuben Morgan-Williams also dotting down, while player of the match Sam Costelow kicked 10 points.

This set up the most comprehensive margin of victory of the Steve Tandy coaching era in a match that was affected by the miserable conditions.

Despite the scoreline, Wales again spent large periods of the game defending against a weakened Japan side who managed two late tries through replacements Shinya Komura and Halatoa Vailea.

Returning flanker James Botham made 38 tackles as he equalled Dafydd Jenkins' world record effort in the 35-28 win against New Zealand, a mark they now both share with Thierry Dusautoir of France.

It was a curious contest, low on quality, played in front of a half-empty Twickenham as part of a double-header before England faced New Zealand.

The victory means Wales secured ninth in the inaugural Nations Championship and finished the year ninth in World Rugby's rankings.

It was a second win in the space of three weeks against Japan, and a third victory in a November campaign that will forever be remembered for Wales beating New Zealand for the first time in 73 years.

## Wales struggle to create early energy

Wales had unconvincingly defeated Japan 28-21 in the opening game of the November series, following up that with the famous win against the All Blacks before a narrow loss to Australia.

Both Wales and Japan had finished fifth in their respective hemisphere sections to set up a fifth contest between Wales and Japan inside 17 months.

Eddie Jones' side had suffered heavy 60-point defeats to England and Scotland and were down to the bare bones with flanker Tiennan Costley named on the wing.

Wales initially made five changes with Morgan-Williams making his first Test start and prop Rhys Carre, hooker Ryan Elias, lock Adam Beard and Botham also included.

With hooker Dewi Lake and Morgan dropping to the bench, lock Dafydd Jenkins captained the side for the first time in more than two years.

There was another late alteration when wing Louis Rees-Zammit was ruled out because of illness, with Scarlets back Ellis Mee promoted from the bench for his first action of this November campaign.

With tickets sold for both games there was a sparse crowd when the two sides came out and there was the unusual sound of being able to hear the players on the field when the match started.

After the Principality Stadium cauldrons of the past couple of weeks, Tandy told his side they had to create their own atmosphere and energy.

Number eight Aaron Wainwright followed that advice by producing a charging run from the kick-off, but Morgan-Williams kicked the ball straight into touch before another poor effort in a nervy start for the Ospreys man.

Japan were sharper in the early exchanges with co-captains Naoto Saito and Warner Dearns breaking the Wales defence and wing Koga Nezuka almost wriggling through.

Fly-half Riku Kitahara missed an attacking kick to touch and the momentum changed.

## Wales attacking kicks lead to four tries

Wales' attacking kicking game proved fruitful in the first half and helped create the opening four tries.

Mee produced a towering high-ball take before fellow wing Adams dotted down for a try after collecting a Sam Costelow cross-kick.

Wales caught Japan on the counter-attack with a kick from centre James expertly picked up and finished by Murray.

James provided another unlikely kick assist with Adams this time the beneficiary for his second score.

Wales' defence continued to be tested and it required a thundering double tackle from Jenkins and James to deny Japan, with Botham then turning over the ball.

Botham has had an eventful couple of weeks. After being named player of the match against New Zealand, he was left out against Australia, a decision that his father took to social media to criticise.

Botham was present at the birth of his child earlier this week before coming back in for the Japan game.

His Cardiff back-row colleague Alex Mann gave away a silly offside penalty but Japan were again denied.

In tricky conditions, a period of distinct mediocrity followed from both sides before Morgan-Williams started and finished the move that led to Wales' fourth try and his first international score.

That looked like becoming his second soon after as Morgan-Williams cantered over following good work from Mann, but the move was called back for a knock-on from prop Archie Griffin.

Wales' defensive defiance continued when Mee's foot somehow denied Japan's Charlie Lawrence from grounding the ball.

Beard was shown a yellow card for Wales' persistent offending in their own 22 and flanker Ben Gunter appeared to have broken the Welsh resistance, but his score was ruled out by the television match official (TMO).

## Super-sub Morgan makes immediate impact

Wales led 26-0 at half-time but the statistics heavily favoured Japan.

Tandy's side only had 32% possession and 25% territory and made 160 tackles to 45 compared to Japan, who made 121 carries compared to Wales' 55.

The lack of quality and Wales' defending again dominated the early second-half exchanges.

Wales made four changes, including the introduction of Lake and Morgan, who scored within a few seconds of coming on from a driving maul.

Morgan doubled that when he powered over from a tap-penalty move.

Tom Rogers' wait for his first Test chance since scoring a hat-trick against New Zealand 12 months ago ended when he came on for Murray.

One of the loudest cheers of the day came when Japan replacement Komura dived over as Japan finished the game the stronger.

They gained further reward when Vailea burrowed over for the final score before the match was, somehow fittingly, ended with a skewed kick going out on the full.
