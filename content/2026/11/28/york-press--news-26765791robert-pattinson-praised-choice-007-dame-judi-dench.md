A York-born actress who performed opposite two incarnations of the character James Bond said she thinks the new 007 is a great choice for the role.

Dame Judi Dench praised Robert Pattinson as a choice for the iconic secret agent but said she will struggle to watch the film due to her worsening eyesight, according to reports in The Independent.

The newspaper reports Dame Judi speaking on ITV’s Lorraine, and saying: "I can’t wait to see him in action.

"I can’t see now properly – I can vaguely see – but he looks terrific to me."

Dame Judi revealed in 2012 that she had been diagnosed with age-related macular degeneration (AMD), an eye condition that has made everyday activities increasingly challenging and has ultimately forced her to step back from acting.

During a recent appearance on BBC Radio 4’s Today programme, Dame Judi said: "I can’t be on stage. I can’t do a play anymore. I can’t do a film anymore. I can’t read anymore."

Despite the obstacles, she emphasised the importance of adapting to her condition, according to the Independent.

She said: "You’ve got to adjust to all that, and make allowances for it, and just get on and don’t think about it too much."

Dame Judi also shared her views on growing older, saying: "There’s nothing to say for it at all.

"[I] don’t think about it too much."

Her comments come as Robert Pattinson was confirmed last week as the next actor to take on the iconic role of James Bond.

Dame Judi played MI6 chief M in the Bond films from 1995 to 2012, appearing across both the Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig eras.

Her career on stage and screen has spanned seven decades, including roles in Iris, Mrs Brown, Notes on a Scandal, and Shakespeare in Love, for which she won an Academy Award in 1999.

Dame Judi has also earned a Tony award, seven Olivier awards, as well as multiple BAFTAs and Golden Globes.

She recently released a new memoir, offering further reflections on her life and career.
