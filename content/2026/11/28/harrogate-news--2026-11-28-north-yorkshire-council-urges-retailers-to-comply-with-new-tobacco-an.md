**New legislation restricting sales of tobacco and vape products to younger people in North Yorkshire has come into effect, with enforcement action taken against a Harrogate retailer for illegal sales.**

North Yorkshire Council is reminding retailers across the county to adhere to recently introduced tougher laws on the sale of tobacco and vaping products aimed at protecting children and helping to create a smoke-free generation.

Since 29 October 2026, it has been illegal for retailers to sell any vaping product—including zero-nicotine vapes—or nicotine products such as nicotine pouches to anyone under the age of 18. From 1 January 2027, the law will prohibit selling tobacco products, herbal smoking products, and cigarette papers to individuals born on or after 1 January 2009.

It is also an offence for adults to purchase these products on behalf of underage individuals.

The council’s Trading Standards officers recently conducted an enforcement operation at Sweet Puffs on Kings Road, Harrogate. On 25 November, the shop’s proprietor, Omer Mohamedamin Bapir, appeared at York Magistrates’ Court after pleading guilty to supplying a vape to someone under 18 in March 2026.

Bapir was sentenced to a 12-month community order including 120 hours of unpaid work, along with a £114 victim surcharge and £1,000 in prosecution costs. He additionally admitted supplying counterfeit cigarettes and possessing for supply multiple packs of counterfeit and incorrectly labelled tobacco products.

> **North Yorkshire Council’s Executive Member for Managing Our Environment, Cllr Richard Foster, whose remit includes Trading Standards, emphasised the importance of compliance:**
>
> Laws restricting the sale of products such as tobacco, vapes and alcohol exist to protect children and young people from harm.  
> The introduction of tougher tobacco and vaping laws marks an important step towards creating a smoke-free generation, but those laws are only effective if retailers comply with them.  
> Recent enforcement action shows that we will continue to take robust action against businesses that fail to follow the law, whether under the new legislation or existing age-restriction requirements.  
> Retailers have a responsibility to know and follow the law, and where they fail to do so, we will not hesitate to take enforcement action.

Residents who suspect retailers of selling age-restricted products to underage customers are encouraged to report concerns either online via Citizens Advice or by calling the Citizens Advice Consumer Service on 0808 223 1133.

Retailers seeking guidance on age verification and compliance with the new laws can contact North Yorkshire Council on 0300 131 2131 or visit www.northyorks.gov.uk/tradingstandards.
