Snooker legend Dennis Taylor has described York’s traffic as a “nightmare”.

The commentator made the comments ahead of the UK Championship at York Barbican, which runs from November 28 to December 6, when speaking to The Press.

Mr Taylor said: "I am always so pleased to get the car into the hotel parking space because getting around the one-way system in York is not the easiest thing in the world and it can be a bit of a nightmare.

"We tend to walk to most places which is easy to do in York and there is a good choice of options.

"Everyone enjoys coming to York for the snooker."

A full, exclusive interview with Dennis Taylor ahead of the UK Championship at the Barbican in York, which starts today, can be found here.

His comments come as York prepares for a weekend of travel disruption, with 200 First Bus drivers set to strike on Saturday and Sunday in a dispute over pay.

Unite the Union has warned disruption could intensify during the Christmas period unless a deal is reached.

A representative for York Barbican said: "We are aware of the First Bus driver strike taking place on Saturday 28 and Sunday, November 29.

"We are sending communications to our attendees advising them to plan their journey in advance and allow plenty of time to get to the venue and through security checks."

First Bus has also warned of significant disruption.

Megan Hope-Rushforth, service delivery director for First Bus, said: "Industrial action will be damaging to everyone and cause severe disruption to the daily lives of our customers who rely on the bus to get to work, education and for appointments as well as the thousands of people that want to enjoy the city in the lead up to Christmas."

The strike coincides with one of York’s busiest weekends of the year, with the snooker championship, Black Friday shopping, and the Christmas market all drawing large crowds to the city.

Unite said vital services, including Park and Ride routes, will be “severely impacted” by the action.
