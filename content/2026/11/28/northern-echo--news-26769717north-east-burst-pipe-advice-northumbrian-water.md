North East residents are being urged to protect their homes from costly burst pipes this winter.

Northumbrian Water says changing temperatures can put pressure on pipes inside homes and across its network.

Water can freeze and expand inside pipes, while a subsequent thaw can cause pipes and the ground around them to move, increasing the risk of cracks, leaks and bursts.

The company advises households to find their stop tap and check that it turns easily, so they can shut off their water supply quickly in an emergency.

It says the valve is usually under the kitchen sink, under the stairs or in a utility room.

Residents are also advised to fix dripping taps, because even a small amount of water can freeze and block a pipe.

Exposed pipes should be wrapped in foam tubing or pipe lagging, particularly in colder places such as lofts and garages, as well as outdoor taps.

The company also recommends keeping homes heated at a low level during very cold spells.

Carly Gray, Northumbrian Water's head of water networks, said: “Cold weather can be tough on pipes, particularly when temperatures start moving up and down, and a burst at home is the last thing anyone wants.”

“Taking a few simple steps now to wrap exposed pipes and check your stop tap works can help save a lot of stress and costly damage later.”

The company said its teams had repaired nearly 10,000 leaks across the region so far this year.

It is asking people to report puddles or running water where it should not be.

Customers can report minor leaks online at www.nwl.co.uk/leaks or call 0800 393 084 to report bigger bursts.

Northumbrian Water says repairs are prioritised according to their impact on customers, traffic management and the amount of water being lost.

Urgent bursts affecting supplies or safety come first, while smaller leaks may take longer, particularly if roadworks are needed.

More winter advice is available at www.nwl.co.uk/winter.
