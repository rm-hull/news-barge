## French prefect Hugues Moutouh has already lost his position as part of this affair that now reaches Ambroise de Rancourt, coordinator of Marine Le Pen's presidential programme.

The case is growing as the days go by, and investigators are trying to determine whether senior officials took advantage of their positions to inform the far-right party about legal matters that could concern it.

Police are investigating facts that could notably amount to a "breach of the secrecy of the investigation."

Ambroise de Rancourt, Marine Le Pen's former right-hand man, has been caught up in the investigation into leaks from the Interior Ministry to the benefit of National Rally (RN).

39-year-old and a graduate of ENA, the elite school specialising in training senior civil servants, Ambroise de Rancourt is the coordinator of the presidential programme of the far-right party's candidate and was previously her chief of staff.

Police searched his home and his office at RN headquarters.

The search took place on 9 November. The surprise that day was that police found prosecutor Christian de Rocquigny on RN premises. Le Parisien (source in French) describes him as a 53-year-old civil servant, "unknown to the general public" and "holding an important position in the judiciary system."

The senior judge later admitted that, in July, he had passed on the report on the leaks from which the RN would allegedly benefit. The justice minister has opened an investigation into this aspect of the case.

The 9 November search was not aimed at catching Christian de Rocquigny by surprise, but at searching Ambroise de Rancourt's office.

According to information from the evening daily Le Monde *(source in French)*, investigators suspect Ambroise de Rancourt of being the main recipient of the leaks.

Le Monde notes that Ambroise de Rancourt "went from activism alongside Jean-Luc Mélenchon" (the far-left party leader) before joining the ranks of the RN.

In Marine Le Pen's view, "the search that took place a few days ago does not call National Rally into question at all", Marine Le Pen said on Friday. The RN president was speaking at a press conference carried by RTL (source in French).

"If it called National Rally into question, I suppose Jordan Bardella's office would have been searched, which is not the case. It does not call the candidate into question either, because otherwise I suppose the candidate's office would have been searched. That has not been the case", she said.

The case began with a discovery during a search in Italy, carried out as part of suspicions that the RN had diverted European Parliament funds, at the home of Frédéric Chatillon, another close associate of Marine Le Pen, who once headed the small far-right fringe group GUD.

On that day, police found a copy of a document from the French Interior Ministry concerning suspicions weighing on the RN over the illegal funding of election campaigns.

Investigators suspect the prefect of Centre-Val de Loire, Hugues Moutouh, of being behind the leaks, and the senior official was formally removed from his post on Wednesday 25 November in the Council of Ministers.
