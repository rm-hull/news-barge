## York Normandy veteran, 101, opens museum exhibition with 'personal connection'

York Normandy Veteran Ken Cooke with Chair of York Army Museum Trustees and Collections Managers *(Image: Gareth Buddo, Furmoto Photography)*

Stories of courage, service and sacrifice have come together in a new exhibition in York.

York Army Museum in Tower Street has opened Medal Stories, exploring the history and significance of military medals, featuring Victoria Crosses and gallantry medals alongside campaign, service and commemorative medals.

Ken Cooke, a veteran of the D-Day Normandy landings in the Second World War, was the guest of honour at the opening on Tuesday, November 24.




Ken said: "It's so important to remember just what these medals stand for - service and sacrifice, and we should never forget."

The 101-year old from York was a young private with the Green Howards when he landed at Gold Beach in Normandy on June 6, 1944.

Medal Stories features three notable loans: a George Cross from the National Army Museum, a Cumberland Medallion from the Clan MacPherson Museum, and a Dickin Medal from the Royal Pigeon Racing Association.

The Dickin Medal is particularly intriguing as it acknowledges acts of bravery by animals.

An interactive display, developed in collaboration with a local developer, highlights the stories of regiment members who received the Victoria Cross, offering visitors a glimpse into the lives behind these acts of bravery.

A collaborative artwork of remembrance was also part of the exhibition opening, with visitors contributing to the piece.

The exhibition was curated by the museum's collections team and volunteers, with local businesses handling the design and printing of the exhibition panels.

Organisers said Ken Cooke's presence at the opening of Medal Stories brought a personal connection to the exhibition, which delves into how military service and acts of exceptional bravery have been recognised through medals over time.

The exhibition is open to visitors at York Army Museum, 3 Tower Street, York, until the end of June 2027.
