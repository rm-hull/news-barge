As the clock ticks toward final settlement for a sex-trafficking-related lawsuit against Jeffrey Epstein’s accountant and attorney, some experts are expressing concern that the agreement might stymie survivors’ search for justice in other litigation involving child sexual abuse images.

The suit involves Darren Indyke and Richard D Kahn, who for decades worked as Epstein’s personal lawyer and accountant respectively. Both men also serve as executors of Epstein’s estate. Indyke and Kahn have insisted they knew nothing about Epstein’s crimes and have rejected allegations of wrongdoing.

In the soon-to-be-settled 2024 Manhattan federal court civil complaint against them, survivors’ attorneys allege that Indyke and Kahn “were personally essential to the Epstein enterprise’s success – among other things, they helped structure Epstein’s bank accounts and cash withdrawals to give Epstein and his associates access to large amounts of cash in furtherance of sex trafficking”.

A final settlement hearing in this trafficking-related case is scheduled for 2 December, during which a judge might sign off on the agreement brokered between survivors’ attorneys at Boies Schiller & Flexner LLP, and Indyke and Kahn. This agreement would establish a $35m settlement pool for survivors who qualify for compensation with these claims, according to court papers.

Epstein, a high-powered financier who counted numerous wealthy and prominent men as his associates, was arrested in July 2019 and charged with sex-trafficking minors following years of allegations against him. The Manhattan US attorney’s office said that his victims were as young as age 14 when he abused them. He killed himself in jail while awaiting trial.

Jennifer Freeman of Marsh Law, who helms other litigation against Epstein, has voiced concerns about the proposed settlement, namely that this deal might set up roadblocks to legal claims against his estate that involve child sex abuse material, or CSAM.

Survivors and law enforcement have said that Epstein possessed, transported, produced and distributed CSAM over decades. Freeman said she fears that Epstein’s estate could try arguing that the proposed Kahn/Indyke settlement protects his estate from any possible litigation brought by CSAM survivors.

“We objected to the settlement on the grounds that CSAM has its own separate claim with its own statutory remedies and this was not, in our view, considered in putting together this settlement,” Freeman told the Guardian.

Freeman represents two women in a 15 September lawsuit involving CSAM claims, as well as several class members of the trafficking-related litigation.

“We fully support the existence of a settlement for survivors, and we want that to go forward, and we very much hope it does,” Freeman said. “However, we believe that the release of [Epstein’s] estate doesn’t make sense here, since the case was not against the estate – it was against two individuals, the lawyer and the accountant.”

Freeman also said she had concerns that survivors might not be aware of the proposed settlement and what she believes might be potential implications on CSAM claims against Epstein’s estate. Freeman wants any Epstein survivor who endured CSAM abuse – including those who participate in the trafficking settlement – to have an opportunity to pursue claims related directly to CSAM.

While the judge on 16 September extended the deadline for survivors to submit or opt out of trafficking-related claims, Freeman said that “we’ve heard from many, particularly those who are outside the United States, [who] had no idea anything was going on”.

“They don’t have class actions typically in their countries, so this would not be a familiar process for them to look out for or understand … that it might apply to them.”

CSAM claims “should not be included in the settlement at all. That should be explicit,” Freeman said of her hopes for the final agreement. “The release of the estate should be at least limited and, in my view, eliminated.”

Asked for comment, attorneys for Indyke and Kahn deferred to prior statements on allegations against them.

In a recent lawsuit against them brought by New Mexico’s attorney general, Raúl Torrez, one of their lawyers said they “emphatically rejected” claims “they were complicit in or in any way knowingly facilitated Mr Epstein’s sexual abuse or trafficking – conduct that by all accounts occurred behind closed doors, in secluded locations, and at times when neither Mr Indyke nor Mr Kahn was present.

“Not a single woman has ever accused Mr Indyke or Mr Kahn of committing sexual abuse or witnessing sexual abuse, nor claimed at any time that she or anyone else reported to them any allegation of Mr Epstein’s abuse,” the statement also said.

Attorneys for Indyke and Kahn do not agree with concerns that the trafficking-related settlement is over-broad. Daniel Ruzumna, who represents Indyke, said: “As the parties pointed out at the hearing and the court appeared to recognize, the putative class only includes women who were sexually abused and/or trafficked by Epstein.”

“That would include any woman who also may also have a CSAM-related claim, in which case that woman may be entitled to a larger recovery from the settlement fund,” Ruzumna said. “But if a woman only had CSAM-related claims, she would not be part of the class … at the September 16 2026 hearing, the parties made clear that women with CSAM-only claims (ie who were not abused or trafficked themselves) are not part of the class and therefore would not release claims.”

Epstein’s apparent possession of CSAM has been detailed by law enforcement officials, as well as materials released under the Epstein Files Transparency Act. An FBI memo, for example, said files included “a large volume of images of Epstein, images and videos of victims who are either minors or appear to be minors, and over ten thousand downloaded videos and images of illegal child sex abuse material and other pornography.”

Maria Farmer, whose sister Annie was abused by Epstein and his accomplice Ghislaine Maxwell at age 16, told authorities in 1996 that he possessed nude images of her siblings. “Complainant [Maria Farmer] stated that she is a professional artist and took pictures of her sisters 12 and 16 yrs for her own [personal] Art Work,” the law enforcement report stated.

“Epstein Stole the photos and Negatives and is believed to have sold the pictures to potential buyers,” the authorities’ report on Maria’s allegations also said.

Maria also reported “pictures of young girls at swimming pools” and recounted that Epstein said “if she tells anyone about the photos he will burn her house down”.
