## An international maritime tribunal has rejected Argentina’s request to suspend a major offshore oil project, reigniting a decades-long dispute with the United Kingdom over the Falkland Islands.

The International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) on Saturday rejected Argentina’s bid to temporarily halt oil drilling off the British-administered Falkland Islands, dealing a setback to Buenos Aires in its longstanding sovereignty dispute with London.

The Hamburg-based tribunal voted 18-3 against Argentina’s request to suspend operations at the $2.1 billion (€1.8 billion) Sea Lion offshore oil project.

Judges found that Argentina’s claims could not be addressed without determining sovereignty over the South Atlantic archipelago, a matter that falls outside the scope of the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea.

The ruling does not settle the wider dispute between the two countries, which fought a 74-day war over the islands in 1982. The Falklands, known in Argentina as the Malvinas, have been under British administration since 1833 and are claimed by Buenos Aires.

## **UK welcomes ruling as Argentina vows to fight on**

The British government welcomed the decision, reaffirming its sovereignty over the islands and the surrounding waters, as well as the islanders’ right to self-determination.

In a 2013 referendum, more than 99% of those who voted backed retaining the islands’ status as a British Overseas Territory. Argentina rejects the result.

The office of Argentine President Javier Milei said it respected the tribunal’s decision but disagreed with its conclusions, insisting that the legal battle was far from over.

“This decision is not the last word,” Milei’s office said.

Buenos Aires intends to pursue its case before a separate arbitration panel. Argentina argues that extracting oil from the disputed waters would cause irreparable harm to its claims over the region’s natural resources.

The UK maintains that the dispute is fundamentally a question of territorial sovereignty and therefore falls outside the tribunal’s jurisdiction.

## **Oil project set to go ahead despite Argentine pressure**

British company Rockhopper Exploration and Israel’s Navitas Petroleum are expected to press ahead with the Sea Lion development, located around 220 kilometres north of the islands.

Drilling is not expected to begin until March 2027, with oil production due to start the following year. The project could transform the islands’ economy, which relies heavily on fishing.

Meanwhile, Milei has pledged to maintain economic pressure on companies involved in the development. His government announced fresh sanctions proceedings on Friday against five individuals or companies linked to the project.

The dispute has strained relations between London and Buenos Aires, despite Milei’s initial efforts to forge closer commercial ties with the UK after taking office in 2023.

Saturday’s ruling removes an immediate legal obstacle to the oil project, but the broader dispute remains unresolved and could lead to years of arbitration.
