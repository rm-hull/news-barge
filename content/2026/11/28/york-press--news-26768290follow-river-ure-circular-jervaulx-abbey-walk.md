## 'Evil waters' and a legendary kelpie on this Yorkshire abbey walk

Jervaulx Abbey Thornton Stewart. Images: Jonathan Smith

*Follow the River Ure in circular Jervaulx Abbey walk says JONATHAN SMITH*

START at Jervaulx Abbey, visiting the abbey ruins before embarking through the grounds which spread to both side of the river. A delightful walk with history and geography combining impressively.

Park at the car park at Jervaulx Abbey. From the car park cross the road (A6108) and enter the abbey grounds. A voluntary donation is asked for to help with the upkeep of the abbey.

After exploring the ruins return to the donation box and take a path heading east/left for one mile across the well-kept estate. The walking is straightforward, sheep plentiful and the deer elusive. After passing the gate house on the edge of the estate turn left at the road and follow it down to Kilgram Bridge.

Although it was the monks who originally built this fine bridge, if you lean over the side and look carefully in to the running water you will be able to see the foundations of an old Roman road, part of a ford for their legions on their way north.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21765125.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jervaulx Abbey Thornton Stewart. Images: Jonathan Smith

An ancient legend tells a different story; the devil promised to donate a bridge to the people on the condition that the first living thing to walk over the ‘evil waters’ was given to him as a sacrifice.

An enterprising shepherd swam the river and whistled for his dog to follow over the new bridge; the dog did and became the devil’s sacrifice. The dog was called Grim, so Kill Grim became Kilgram Bridge over time!

No such risk these days but once across the bridge leave the road at a signpost to your left and head diagonally uphill over the fields towards the lovely village of Thornton Steward.

Walk through this immaculately maintained village before joining a track at its western end and towards the fascinating St Oswald’s Church. It is the oldest church in the area, some parts dating back to over 1.000 years.

From here head west through open fields towards (and below) another grand building, Danby Hall. You will be unable to gain entrance to the hall (it is privately owned) but the building is impressive and the grounds, which sweep down to the river, very impressive. A Georgian masterpiece.

After one-and-a-half miles the estate grounds meet the estate road and some lovely renovated estate buildings. Turn left at the main road and cross two road bridges, the second being Cover Bridge, another fine pack horse bridge with a pub.

Cross Cover Bridge and immediately turn left on to the riverside path. The final one-and-a-half miles of this interesting walk is a real pleasure. The path sticks close to the River (Cover soon becoming the Ure) as it passes serenely through the landscape of the area.

As well as walkers it is popular with fishermen...do watch out for the legendary ‘kelpie’ fish which leaps out to devour its victims.

The path follows a mix of woodland and open walking before all too soon there is a sharp right turn and a short lane leading back to the car at Jervaulx.

\* Fact Box:

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21765126.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jervaulx Abbey map. Jonathan Smith

Distance: Roughly seven miles

Height to Climb: 45m (150 feet)

Start: SE 169857. There is a free car parking at Jervaulx Abbey

Difficulty: A low level walk which can be muddy in places during the winter months.

Refreshments: The inn at Cover Bridge is full of character and it is a short walk back from there. Alternatively, Masham or Leyburn are only a few miles further with cafes and pubs.

Be Prepared: The route description and sketch map only provide a guide to the walk. You must take out and be able to read a map and in cloudy/misty conditions a compass. You must also wear the correct clothing and footwear for the outdoors. Whilst every effort is made to provide accurate information, walkers head out at their own risk.

Please observe the Countryside Code and park sensibly.
