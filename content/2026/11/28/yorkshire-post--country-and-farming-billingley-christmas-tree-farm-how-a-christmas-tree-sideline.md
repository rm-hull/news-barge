# Billingley Christmas Tree Farm: How a Christmas tree sideline turned into a growth industry

## A South Yorkshire farming family which launched a Christmas tree venture in response to the 2001 foot-and-mouth outbreak says diversification has given them “opportunities we never imagined” as it enters its 25th year of growing the festive plants.

Andrew and Sally Stenton, who run Billingley Christmas Tree Farm near Barnsley with their three children, said they grow around 100,000 Christmas trees alongside wheat, pumpkins and sunflowers.

What started with the sale of a few hundred Christmas trees from the corner of a barn has now welcomes thousands of visitors each year to pick their own as well as supplying stately homes, hotels, hospitals, councils, garden centres, farm shops and independent retailers.

The diversification began after the foot-and-mouth crisis severely affected the third generation family's pedigree cattle business, forcing them to explore alternative income streams. The farm sees about 15,000 trees planted every year with each fir taking between six and eight years to reach maturity.

Alongside supplying commercial customers including Chatsworth House and distributing young trees to growers across the UK, the farm welcomes families each festive season to choose and cut their own Christmas trees. The business has also developed an international reputation within the Christmas tree industry and employs around 17 staff during its busiest period.

NFU member Andrew Stenton said: "Sometimes we sit back and think, I can't believe what we've built. It all started with a few hundred Christmas trees in the corner of a barn and look where we are now. It's a different way of farming for us, but it's an enjoyable one. Diversification has given us opportunities we never imagined and helped secure the future of the farm for the next generation as well as bringing joy to people as part of festive celebrations."

As well as growing Christmas trees, the farm has expanded into pumpkins, sunflowers and visitor attractions, helping bring the public closer to farming while also creating additional income streams for the family business.

The Stentons also support Bluebell Wood Children's Hospice through its annual Christmas tree collection scheme. Each January, used Christmas trees are collected from households across the region, raising vital funds for the hospice. The trees are then recycled and used as forage ensuring they continue to provide value long after the festive season while helping to reduce waste.

The family's influence extends beyond their own farm. During the Covid-19 pandemic, they created Christmas Tree Growers UK, a networking community that has grown into an international group of around 650 members across Europe and the United States.

NFU County Adviser Jennifer Costello said: “The Stentons have built an impressive business over the past 25 years, and their success is a real credit to the hard work and vision of the family. What began as a small seasonal venture has grown into a nationally recognised business supplying customers across the UK and creating valuable opportunities in the rural economy. By opening up the farm and welcoming families to pick their own Christmas trees, they've also helped strengthen people's connection with farming and provided a memorable experience for thousands of visitors over the years.

"Their story demonstrates the innovation and resilience that exists throughout British farming and shows how farm businesses continue to evolve to meet new opportunities and challenges."
