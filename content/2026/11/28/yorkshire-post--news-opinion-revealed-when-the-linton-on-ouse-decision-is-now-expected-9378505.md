# Revealed: When the Linton-on-Ouse decision is now expected

## The revelation published exclusively by this newspaper today, that the decision on whether or not to squeeze 1,300 illegal immigrant men into a tiny North Yorkshire village community, we understand, will cause significant concern amongst the 650 people who currently live in Linton-on-Ouse.

![Campaigners outside Linton-on-Ouse Village Hall, North Yorkshire (photo, PA)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmI4OGQ1ODcxLWVlZTEtNDc1OS04YTUwLTAyNGJlZTY4YTRmZjphOTEwYjAzMC1iMDcxLTQ1ODMtYWQ3NC04MGVmZDUzZmUwMWQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


At a time when families should be slowing down in order to spend quality time with one another, as schools break up and day jobs slow down in the lead up to Christmas, people ought to be looking forward to reconnecting with friends and relatives. Instead, those living alongside the former RAF base that Prime Minister Andy Burnham wants to use to pack in hundreds of likely mostly young men, will be able to think of little else but the impending decision that we now know will be taken early next year.

Should the Home Office proceed with its deeply flawed plan to turn the currently defunct military site, which is nestled right next to family homes, a playground and a primary school, into a male migrant super-camp, the population of the village would be tripled.

The Yorkshire Post now understands that, according to the Home Office’s asylum accommodation director Louisa Le Roux, that the feasibility study that is assessing whether or not this is a sensible thing to do has run over time. Little wonder, because nothing will add up to the answer, ‘yes.’

The lack of amenities, the insecure nature of the site; few economic prospects, a skeleton public transport provision.

To be clear, this newspaper supports the premise of closing down migrant hotels, and the determination to stop the dangerous boat crossings. What cannot be supported, though, is the abandonment of common sense, due care and responsibility on the part of a Government whose first thought should be for the people who can legitimately call this home.
