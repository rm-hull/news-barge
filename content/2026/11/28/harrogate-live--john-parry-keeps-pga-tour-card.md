Harrogate-born golfer John Parry finished 100th in the FedExCup Fall standings, the last place that keeps full PGA Tour status for 2027, despite missing the RSM cut.

## Overview

Harrogate-born golfer John Parry will keep full PGA Tour status for 2027. He finished exactly 100th in the FedExCup Fall standings after the season’s last event, the RSM Classic, which ended on Sunday 22 November. The top 100 keep their cards. He missed the cut at the RSM, so he had to wait for the rest of the field to finish.

## How close it was

The PGA Tour set out the stakes before the autumn events began. Players inside the top 100 of the FedExCup standings after the RSM Classic have full status in 2027. Those outside drop to partial status or face demotion.

Parry went into the final week at Sea Island, Georgia, in 96th place. He shot 70 and 72 to finish level par after two rounds and missed the cut, according to his PGA Tour results page. Other players’ weekends could still have pushed him out. When the event finished he had dropped four places, to 100th, and was still inside the line.

His autumn, event by event, from the same page:

| World Wide Technology Championship (8 Nov) | T25 | 93 |
| Austin Championship (15 Nov) | 64th | 96 |
| The RSM Classic (22 Nov) | Missed cut | 100 |

He turned 40 on 17 November, between the Austin event and Sea Island.

## His first PGA Tour season

Parry was born in Harrogate and turned professional in 2008, according to his PGA Tour profile. He won three times on the Challenge Tour in 2024, which promoted him to the DP World Tour. He then earned his PGA Tour card for 2026, the PGA Championship’s player page records.

His best results this season came early:

* tied 8th at the Puerto Rico Open in March, his one top-10 finish
* tied 11th at the US Open in June, worth $405,862
* tied 28th at The Open in July
* tied 70th at the PGA Championship in May

## What it means for golf fans in Harrogate

A Harrogate-born player will have full status on the PGA Tour again in 2027. Finishing 101st or lower would have meant partial status or possible demotion, under the rules the Tour published in September. Instead he starts a second season with the same standing as his first.
