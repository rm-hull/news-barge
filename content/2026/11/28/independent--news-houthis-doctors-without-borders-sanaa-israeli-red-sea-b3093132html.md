An airstrike in the Yemeni capital Sanaa early Saturday wounded at least 15 people, according to the Iran-backed Houthis, and another strike in a Red Sea port city ignited a fire at a power plant.

Medical humanitarian organization Doctors Without Borders on Saturday expressed outrage at an Israeli strike near one of its clinics in Gaza the day before that killed two of its watchmen. The Israeli military said it killed two Hamas militants working as security guards at the clinic.

Here is the latest news from the Middle East on Saturday. Read our full coverage here.

Houthis report airstrikes in Yemeni capital and Red Sea port city

Houthi-run Health Ministry spokesman Anees al-Asbahi said two women were among at least 15 people wounded in a strike early Saturday in the Abu Shamla neighborhood in the capital Sanaa.

Al-Massirah satellite television reported damage to at least eight houses and three shops, while a gas station was also hit.

Al-Massirah also reported that strikes ignited a fire at a power plant in Mokha.

The Houthis blamed Saudi Arabia for the strikes.

Meanwhile, Saudi military spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki said air defense systems early Saturday intercepted and destroyed a Houthi cruise missile targeting the Saudi Arabian capital, Riyadh.

This came after the Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said it launched an air operation Friday against the rebels in Sanaa and the provinces of Hodeida on the Red Sea and Saada on the border with Saudi Arabia.

Doctors Without Borders decries Israeli strike that killed 2 security staff

Doctors Without Borders, known by its French acronym MSF, said Saturday it was “devastated and outraged” by the killing of two of its colleagues in an Israeli strike near one of its clinics in Gaza City late Friday.

MSF said the two men worked as watchmen at the clinic, adding that it was clearly marked with its logos and that its coordinates had been shared with the Israeli military.

The deaths bring the number of MSF colleagues killed in Gaza to 17, the organization said.

The Israeli military on Saturday said, without offering evidence, it killed two Hamas militants in Gaza City who worked as security guards at an MSF-operated clinic.
