## Protesters and police have clashed on Saturday in the Albanian capital as thousands of people in anti-government protests called for Prime Minister Edi Rama to resign.

Albanians from all across the country travelled to the capital, Tirana, on Saturday, for a rally where thousands of people called for Prime Minister Edi Rama to resign.

Clashes broke out between protesters and police, who used tear gas and water cannons to push back protesters throwing rocks and using sticks to try and smash street cameras and government building windows. Police forces were deployed along main roads to conduct vehicle searches.

“We have come out to demand our rights. We have had enough. Life has become very difficult. We are calling for a change to the entire system,” said student Juna Vasili. Protesters chanted "Resignation!" while gathering in Tirana's main Skanderbeg Square.

The health ministry said eight people were injured, including a police officer and two journalists.

This protest coincides with the 114th anniversary of the country's independence, a public holiday usually dedicated to national celebrations.

This protest was part of a series of anti-government demonstrations that began in late May, initially sparked by the opposition to a major luxury tourism project on Albania's southern coast.

A project linked to an investment firm founded by Jared Kushner, US President Donald Trump’s son-in-law.

## 'We are like stateless citizens'

Albanians also travelled from abroad to protest in Tirana: "I came here specifically for the protest," says Arian Lleshi, a 33-year-old living in the UK.

"Why? Because we have been suffering for far too long. We have spread ourselves across Europe and around the world, and it is almost as if we are citizens without a state. We are like stateless citizens."

For many like Lleshi, political parties that have emerged since the fall of communism in the 1990s "no longer represent anyone."

"They need to make way for a new generation of intellectuals and people who have a greater stake in the country’s future."

Suzana Gjeta, a retiree, was also out on the streets to support young people and those like herself "who are living on just 120 euros a month. We hope this system will go, that Edi Rama will go, and that this corruption will end."

The rallies took the name of the Flamingo movement and were soon joined by student demonstrations in September. In late October, the government set up fines of up to 500,000 leks (€5573) for violence, looting, or concealing one's identity during protests.

While many of these protests have turned violent, most of the demonstrators called for better education and living standards, and lately for Rama's resignation.
