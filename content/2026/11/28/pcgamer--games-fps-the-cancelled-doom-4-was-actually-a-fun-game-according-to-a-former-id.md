Before id Software created its excellent 2016 reboot of Doom, the studio was working on a more direct follow-up to its earlier demon-blasters. Doom 4 was in production between 2008 and 2011, and much like Doom 3, followed popular trends of first-person shooters at the time. But after multiple false-starts, id Software went back to the drawing board, resulting in the glory-killing FPS that has defined the series' direction ever since.

But according to one former id Software developer, Doom 4 was by no means a terrible game, and possibly should have even been released in some form. It just had one big problem—it wasn't Doom.

Speaking to FRVR, former id Software principal programmer Dustin Land said that he thought Doom 4 showed a lot of promise. "I never thought there was anything wrong with that game", he explained. Unlike the 2016 reboot, Doom 4 aimed to be more of a narrative and spectacle-driven experience, channeling the approach of concurrent shooters like Call of Duty. "Playing Call of Duty but you're shooting demons is actually a fun game," Land continued. "The problem is always that it's not Doom. And so the question is always 'What's Doom'?"

![Unreleased DOOM 4 [cancelled] & DOOM 2016 Development Gameplay - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/Uvb04gU_UmU/maxresdefault.jpg)

Yet while Doom 4 may not have been a fit for the series, Land believes that Doom 4 could have stood up on its own merits. "I thought it could've just been a new IP. It could've been sold under some other brand."

Obviously, this didn't happen. After three years of trying, Doom 4 was canned, and afterward id Software spent a good while figuring out what the next Doom should look like. "It was a laborious process," Land says. "It was a couple years trying to find what exactly it should be. And that was the era of cover-based shooters, long-distance poking, health recovery, stuff like that. And so the push-forward combat really subverted people's expectation of what a first-person [shooter] should be, and it's had a large impact on how shooters are designed these days."

This is certainly true for id's own lineage, with every subsequent Doom game since 2016 looking to reinvent how the Doom Slayer fights. What lies in store for id Software next remains unclear, however. While the studio is purportedly working on a new project, in the summer, Xbox laid off a reported 136 developers from id Software. Earlier this month, Land announced the formation of Hidden Horses, a new, democratically governed studio composed of former id Software developers, which is working on a new shooter based on The Running Man and 1990's Smash TV.
