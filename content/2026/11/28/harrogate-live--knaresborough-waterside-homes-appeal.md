An appeal has been lodged over three homes refused on the riverside meadow by Waterside car park, Knaresborough. Flood risk and the conservation area sank it.

## Overview

An appeal has been lodged against North Yorkshire Council’s refusal of three homes on open grassland beside the council car park on Waterside, Knaresborough. The council turned the outline plan down on 22 October 2026 on flood risk and harm to the Knaresborough Conservation Area. Of 40 public comments, 38 objected and none supported it.

## What happened

North Yorkshire Council’s planning register now lists the application’s appeal status as “Appeal lodged”. The application, reference 26/02909/OUT, sought outline permission for three houses with all matters reserved. That means only the principle of building there was being decided, not the design.

The plot sits at the riverside end of Waterside. The officer’s report describes it as an open parcel of grassland with mature planting around its edges and a stone wall along two sides. A public car park lies to the north and the River Nidd to the west.

The entrance falls within Knaresborough’s development limits. The rest of the site is classed as open countryside.

## Why the council said no

The decision notice gives two reasons:

* **Flood risk.** The site is in Flood Zones 2 and 3, with the homes themselves in Zone 2. The council said the flood risk assessment and sequential test did not show the homes would be safe for their lifetime or would not increase flooding elsewhere.
* **The conservation area.** The council said the houses would be “visually intrusive and incongruous” on an important parcel of land. Removing part of the stone wall along the frontage would also harm the setting of Waterside.

The report notes that the Knaresborough Conservation Area appraisal calls Waterside one of the most picturesque and visited parts of the town. The appraisal also names the stone wall as an important feature of this stretch.

On flooding, the case officer found the sequential test had looked only at land inside Knaresborough’s development limits. It found one other site for sale on Rightmove, on Thistle Hill, and set it aside because it was sold subject to contract. The officer said villages around the town, such as Goldsborough, Scotton and Scriven, should also have been searched.

## What counted in its favour

North Yorkshire cannot show a five-year supply of land for housing. The report puts the figure at 2.84 years, and says this weighed in favour of the scheme.

Most technical consultees did not object once extra information had been supplied:

| Consultee | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Highways | No objection, with a construction plan condition |
| Yorkshire Water | No objection, with drainage conditions |
| Council ecologist | Concerns resolved by further information |
| Tree officer | No objection, with conditions |
| Environment Agency | No formal comment |

## Who objected

Knaresborough Town Council objected. It cited harm to the landscape, wildlife, flood resilience and drainage. Knaresborough Civic Society raised concerns about losing the open meadow on the riverside.

The report counts 40 public comments: 38 objections, two neutral and none in support. Objectors said the meadow soaks up surface water from nearby homes. They also raised the loss of trees, the dark corridor bats use along the river, and traffic on a road busy with people on foot.

## What it means for you

The case now goes to the Planning Inspectorate, which will decide it independently of the council. No appeal reference or procedure had been published when we checked on 28 November.

When an appeal formally starts, the council normally writes to everyone who commented on the original application. They then get a deadline to send further comments to the inspector. If you objected or supported, watch for that letter.

If the inspector allows the appeal, the applicant would still need a further reserved matters application for the design, layout and landscaping before building.

We will add the appeal reference and the comment deadline once they appear.
