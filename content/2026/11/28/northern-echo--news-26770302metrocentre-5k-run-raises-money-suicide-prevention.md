The Metrocentre was transformed into a unique 5km running route recently as more than 200 people took part in an early morning charity event supporting suicide prevention.

Taking over the shopping centre before its doors opened on Saturday (November 21), runners came together to raise money for North East charity If U Care Share, while honouring the memory of Ben Goodman, who died by suicide aged 24.

Organised by Everlast Gym personal trainers Alex Graham, 24, and Chaya Blackett, 30, the event proved to be about much more than running, with Chaya describing it as an opportunity to build connections, bring people together and create happy memories.

Reflecting on the event, she said it was "just the start" of something bigger, with hopes of continuing to build a community around running and wellbeing.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21696906.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Co-organiser Chaya Blackett said: “Most importantly, it’s bringing people together – runner, spectator, or volunteer." *(Image: Supplied)*

Spaces for the run were fully booked within just hours of registration opening, organisers said, with more than 200 participants gathering at the start line on Saturday ahead of the 6.45am kick-off.

The 5km route took runners through the length and breadth of the Metrocentre, and followed a gym fundraiser from Alex and Chaya in September, in which staff and members covered 1,450km, the length of the UK, in just two days on treadmills.

Alex said: “With our first fundraiser, the aim was to do something in Ben’s honour and support his family, but we were completely taken aback by the response.

"The more people opened up and shared their own personal stories, the more we realised the scale of the problem."

He added: "It made us ask what more we could do to help raise awareness and encourage anyone who might be struggling to talk to someone about how they are feeling.”

After the run, Chaya took to social media to thank the more than 200 runners and 20 volunteers who travelled to Gateshead to make the event happen, describing it as an opportunity to bring people together, build connections and create happy memories.

She also praised fellow organiser Alex Graham for his dedication to the event in memory of his best friend, adding that she hoped this would be the beginning of something bigger.

Participants were split into four groups based on their estimated finishing times, with each group setting off from Red Mall in five-minute intervals.

The route passed through the centre’s malls and levels, as well as its multi-storey car park, before finishing outside Sports Direct at around 8am.

A raffle included two tickets to see Sam Fender at St James’ Park, donated by a friend in Ben Goodman’s honour.

Fitness groups SpeedX, from Newcastle, and Sunderland-based Believe to Run also supported the event.

Funds raised through donations and the event will go to If U Care Share and its suicide prevention and support services.
