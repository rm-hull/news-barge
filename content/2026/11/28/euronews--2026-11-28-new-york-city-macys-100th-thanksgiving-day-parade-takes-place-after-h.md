## Heavy rain and strong winds forced organisers to postpone the iconic New York parade for the first time in its history, disrupting preparations for its landmark 100th edition.

New York City is celebrating the 100th edition of Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade on Saturday, two days later than planned after a powerful storm forced organisers to postpone one of the United States’ best-known holiday traditions.

Originally scheduled for Thanksgiving Day on Thursday, 26 November, the event was rescheduled following consultations between Macy’s, city officials and the New York Police Department (NYPD) over safety concerns.

"The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority," Macy’s said in a statement on Wednesday.

Despite the disruption, Saturday’s programme is expected to feature 100 balloons, elaborate floats, marching bands and around 1,000 clowns.

## Performers scramble to adapt to last-minute changes

The postponement presented a major logistical challenge for organisers, who had spent more than two years preparing for the milestone event.

Crews had to deflate the giant balloons on Wednesday, transport them back to a facility in New Jersey and prepare them again for Saturday's procession.

Marching bands and performers from across the country also faced unexpected expenses, with some launching fundraising campaigns to cover additional accommodation and travel costs.

A marching band from Hawaii raised more than $700,000 (€605,000) to extend its stay in New York, according to the Associated Press. The Nā Koa Aliʻi Hawaiʻi All-State Marching Band comprises around 250 musicians and 100 traditional Hawaiian hula dancers, all secondary school students, many of whom had never travelled outside Hawaii before.

Donations from individuals, banks and other institutions helped cover the unexpected costs of accommodation, meals and transport, allowing the young performers to represent their home state at the landmark parade.

Others, including the University of Alabama’s marching band, were forced to withdraw because of previous commitments. Nevertheless, organisers said the vast majority of performers had managed to rearrange their plans.

## **Familiar faces and new attractions**

The parade follows a four-kilometre route through Manhattan, starting on the Upper West Side and ending outside Macy’s flagship department store in Herald Square.

Familiar characters such as Snoopy, Spider-Man and Buzz Lightyear feature alongside new inflatable attractions, including Bluey and Hello Kitty.

The musical line-up includes Queen Latifah, Teddy Swims and Megan Moroney, with rapper LL Cool J and singer Gloria Gaynor joining the programme following the postponement.

As is customary, Santa Claus rounds off the procession, marking the traditional start of the Christmas shopping season in the US.

## **A tradition dating back more than a century**

First held in 1924, Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade has become a fixture of American popular culture and one of the country’s most recognisable annual celebrations.

Although the event has faced adverse weather conditions in the past, it had never previously been postponed.

The parade was cancelled between 1942 and 1944 during the Second World War because rubber and helium were needed for the war effort.

Strong winds forced organisers to ground the giant balloons in 1971, while adverse weather conditions in 2006 and 2019 meant they had to fly at lower heights.

Weather conditions for Saturday’s celebrations were, by comparison, considerably more favourable, with forecast wind gusts of around 40 kilometres per hour remaining below the threshold at which the giant balloons would have to be grounded.

## **From department store promotion to American tradition**

Macy’s is one of America’s best-known department store chains, founded in 1858 by Rowland H. Macy as a small shop in New York City. Its flagship store in Manhattan’s Herald Square opened in 1902 and became the world’s largest department store in 1924.

That same year, Macy’s employees organised the first Thanksgiving parade to promote the store and herald the start of the Christmas shopping season. Originally known as Macy’s Christmas Parade, the procession featured marching bands, floats and live animals from Central Park Zoo.

Giant character balloons were introduced in 1927, eventually becoming the event’s defining feature.

What began as a commercial initiative has evolved into a national holiday tradition and a fixture of American popular culture.
