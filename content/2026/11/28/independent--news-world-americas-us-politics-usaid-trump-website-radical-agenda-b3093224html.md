USAID’s webpage is back online for the first time in years - but now is filled with information about how the agency was “weaponsized” to push a “radical agenda.”

President Donald Trump and his staff quickly targeted USAID when they returned to the White House as an example of government overspending. Led by Elon Musk and the Department of Government Efficiency, the Trump team quickly ended most of USAID funding and shuttered the agency.

That left its webpage offline for years. But now it’s back with a simple message for readers.

“Under previous administrations, USAID was weaponized to advance a radical agenda fundamentally at odds with American values. In the interest of transparency and accountability, we are publishing files, memos, and internal communications detailing how the agency operated,” the site reads.

![A new USAID webpage features files and documents that the Trump administration claims shows how it pushed a ‘radical agenda.’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/14/42/GettyImages-2214127329.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The webpage currently features various documents and files the administration says backs up their claims. But they are offered without context or explanation.

*The Independent* has reached out to the State Department about the changes.

Conservative commentator Mike Benz appears to be helping in the effort on the “USAID files” and made note of their release online. “In the coming weeks, you'll get an inside look at USAID's "Platform Accountability" programs. USAID isn't a regulator, let alone a speech regulator. But it set itself up as a legal & regulatory body to force US platforms to censor by getting other nations to pass censorship laws,” he wrote on social media.

The advocacy group, Aid on the Hill, which was formed by staffers who lost jobs amid the DOGE job cuts, posted a statement blasting the change in the webpage.

“After 18 months offline, the site returned with documents and internal communications chosen to score political points. Many carry the unredacted names of American and local staff, American tech companies and local organizations that worked with the United States in conflict zones and fragile states,” it read. “Publishing those names, particularly out of context, puts people and their families at risk.

“The material also omits where the work originated: USAID's programs to counter foreign disinformation grew out of a bipartisan mandate Congress wrote into law in 2017.”

![USAID cuts, along with other international groups reducing funding, could lead to 23 million additional deaths by 2030, one report found](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/28/15/46/GettyImages-2226535630.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Early in Trump’s second term, the administration worked to cut USAID funding, ending $60 million in humanitarian aid for millions of people around the world.

A recent study by health journal *The Lancet* found that cuts to aid programs such as USAID and others in the UK will lead to 23 million additional deaths by the year 2030.

That total includes 5.4 million children under the age of 5.

“These findings give a voice to millions of vulnerable people and show the profound moral cost of the zero-sum approach many political leaders are taking,” said Dr Rajiv J Shah, president of The Rockefeller Foundation, which helped to fund the report.

“Though it will take years to adequately assess the full toll of aid cuts, this early projection is an urgent call to action,” added Dr Shah, who is also a former administrator of USAID.
