!["This is for the kiddies: Princess reveals how family inspired her in fight against cruel disease," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/bf1e/live/dfb782a0-eab2-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

The Princess of Wales features on many of the front pages as a new documentary teases further details around her cancer diagnosis. "This is for the kiddies" headlines the Daily Mirror next to a photo of Catherine, who "reveals how family inspired her in fight against cruel disease".

!["Children helped me fight cancer, says Kate," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/521c/live/d8678fe0-eab2-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

The Daily Telegraph says "the Princess of Wales has spoken for the first time about the 'darkness' she experienced during cancer treatment, crediting her children with giving her a reason to recover".

!["Kate reveals 'darkest of times' of cancer fight," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/64f0/live/2d1a7430-eab3-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"Astonishingly candid testimony," reads the Daily Mail's kicker, hovering above the headline: "Kate reveals 'darkest of times' of cancer fight". A sombre-looking Princess Catherine is shown as the story continues that she "recalls how she drifted in and out of consciousness in hospital following major abdominal surgery in 2024".

!["My children gave me the reason to get out of hospital," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f4ad/live/db4b9d00-eab2-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"My children gave me the reason to get out of hospital" is the Daily Express's summary of the princess's testimony.

!["Benefits and pensions in chaos after 'cyber incident'," reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/726a/live/47c8e5f0-eab3-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Meanwhile, the Guardian leads with a "cyber incident" causing "chaos" for benefits and pensions. It explains "the government took its portal down late on Thursday after detecting 'unusual activity', meaning anyone trying to check their state pension, make an application for disability benefits or claim universal credit was not able to do so". The web shutdown "came days after the government issued an alert warning of an increased risk of Russian sabotage attacks in the UK".

!["DWP exposed as cyber failures rise," reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/e155/live/11a5d2a0-eabb-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

The Financial Times headlines with "DWP [Department for Work and Pensions] exposed as cyber failures rise". It reports this follows cyber incidents impacting the US Treasury, the CIA and Fifa. Network outages and cyber attacks have stoked fears about the vulnerability of digital infrastructure "to hacking and cyber-terrorism in the age of AI", the paper writes.

!["All schools must book time to read for pleasure," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/0747/live/65967200-eab3-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

The Times says "all schools must book time to read for pleasure", writing that Education Secretary Lucy Powell will announce new guidance in Parliament on Monday. "All primary schools must create time every day for children to read," the secretary is expected to say, while secondary students will "focus on entire books, poems and other literature".

!["Burnham surges to big poll lead over Tories - as support for rejoin-EU drops," reads the headline on the front page of the i Weekend.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/2719/live/4bebdbb0-eab3-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

The i Weekend leads with polling it commissioned suggesting that Prime Minister Andy Burnham "surges to big poll lead over Tories" while "support for rejoin-EU drops". Its poll suggests the Labour Party enjoys a 10-point lead over the Conservatives, which the paper says is "the biggest lead since [the] general election" in 2024. It writes that the cabinet remains split over whether to re-open the EU debate, with the poll suggesting that support for going back into the European bloc sits at 43% while staying out is 37%, with a significant 20% "don't know" on the matter.

!["Son's appeal over 'cruelty of mother's birthday in Iran jail," reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/0815/live/544464d0-eab3-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

"Son's appeal over 'cruelty' of mother's birthday in Iran jail" says the Independent, reporting the plea from Craig and Lindsay Foreman's family as the British couple "approach two years languishing in a Tehran prison".

!["No 10: Shut it, Huw," reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/26f3/live/60243ff0-eab3-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

The Sun reports that "Downing Street has blasted Huw Edwards' shameless attempt to rehabilitate his image," following revelations the ex-BBC newsreader – "convicted in 2024 of making indecent images of children" – will feature in a Channel 4 documentary. It quotes the prime minister's spokeswoman saying the TV network should "act responsibly" and have appropriate regard for audiences, adding: "We have no interest in hearing what he has to say".

!["Tyson's brawl clear: The battle of Britain is on," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/7e7e/live/5c0a9900-eab3-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Finally, the Daily Star declares "the battle of Britain is on" with the announcement that boxer Tyson Fury will fight Anthony Joshua after the bout was put in jeopardy by claims of a schizophrenia diagnosis. Fury spoke of his mental health struggles in a Netflix documentary with his wife, Paris, saying he had been diagnosed with schizophrenia. But Paris has since retracted that statement and Netflix have edited the remarks out of their film.
