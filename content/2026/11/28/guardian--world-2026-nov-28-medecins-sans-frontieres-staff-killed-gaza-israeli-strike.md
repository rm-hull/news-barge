Israeli forces have bombed a clinic run by Médecins Sans Frontières in Gaza City, killing two of its staff members, the organisation has said.

MSF said Israel had directly struck the clinic, despite the fact that its location was known to the Israeli military and that it was well marked with MSF logos. The organisation denounced the attack and said that with the deaths of the two security guards on Saturday, Israel had killed 17 of its staff members during the war in Gaza.

The head of the World Health Organization, Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus, condemned the bombing, saying: “Those risking their lives to save others must be protected, not attacked.”

In a post on X, Ghebreyesus also said: “Attacks on health facilities, health workers and humanitarian personnel in Gaza are outrageous and unacceptable … These attacks MUST STOP.”

The Israeli military claimed that the two men whom they killed, Ahmad Abou Hassira and Moaz Alitsbach, were members of Hamas’s military wing. It also added that the strike was “not directed at a hospital” and had caused no damage to the facility.

The Israeli military has often accused Hamas of embedding itself within civilian infrastructure, including hospitals. However, in 2024 the head of the international criminal court’s Palestine investigation said claims about the presence of Hamas fighters in Gaza’s hospitals had been “grossly exaggerated”.

Israel has killed Palestinian medical workers en masse since the start of the war in Gaza. According to the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics, at least 1,700 have been killed by Israeli attacks. Rights groups say the Israeli military has engaged in systematic destruction of civilian infrastructure, including medical infrastructure.

The UN has repeatedly called on Israel to stop its restrictions on the entry of supplies to allow Palestinians to prepare for the winter and begin to rebuild the destroyed strip.

The result is a healthcare system “experiencing systemic collapse, severely undermining its capacity to meet the basic health needs of the civilian population”, according to a UN report in August.

The humanitarian situation is also deteriorating, as heavy rains exacerbate crowded and unsanitary living conditions, leading to the spread of infectious diseases.

Israel has continued to bomb Gaza on a near-daily basis despite a ceasefire signed in October 2025, killing at least 1,705 people since then. Israel has repeatedly accused Hamas of violating the understandings of the ceasefire.

The war began after a Hamas-led attack on Israel on 7 October 2023 that killed about 1,200 people.

More than 74,000 people have been killed and more than 175,000 injured in Israel’s war in Gaza. A UN independent international commission of inquiry found that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza and that Israeli officials, including the prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, incited these acts. Netanyahu is separately wanted by the international criminal court for war crimes. Israel denies these claims.
