Torrential rain over inundated East Coast communities started easing Friday, with electricity gradually returning to hundreds of thousands of customers while several locations remained on guard against high-tide flooding following a barrage of holiday storms.

The severe weather disrupted travel and downed trees and power lines, prompting water rescues from New York down to Virginia.

In New York City, rain and high winds forced an unprecedented postponement of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday.

Elsewhere, wet snow fell as far south as the Tennessee Valley, while freezing rain moved across northern New England.

The weather front is expected to exit the region Saturday morning, bringing drier weather into Sunday. Cold rain was recorded across parts of the Midwest, including the Ohio Valley and lower Great Lakes.

Another weather system in the Northwest brought rain along coastal Oregon. Additional snow was forecast across the Cascade Range and within mountain passes in Montana and Wyoming.

## Storm means post-holiday cleanup for many

Conditions were expected to improve across the eastern United States, "but this huge system will take some time to weaken," the National Weather Service said.

Coastal flood warnings remained posted from New Jersey to Virginia.

The number of power outages, which peaked Thursday at more than 900,000, fell to 230,000 by late Friday evening as utility crews worked to restore electricity, according to poweroutage.us.

Pennsylvania had the highest count, followed by Missouri, where heavy snow created major problems.

In Hampton, Virginia, the storm felled a beloved landmark at Grandview Beach: a weathered, leafless tree known as the "Grieving Tree" or the "Shell Tree." Visitors regularly adorned its branches with seashells, making it a popular subject for photographers.

After surviving many storms, the tree "may have finally met its match," local meteorologist Ricky Matthews posted on Facebook, sharing a photograph of the scene.

Cynthia Battles, 70, said the storm ripped a two-story deck off her waterfront cottage in Oak Orchard, Delaware, and sent waves crashing through a sliding glass door. Glass was scattered everywhere, her pontoon boat was in pieces and her partner’s boat was left completely submerged.

Battles was drying out photographs and important documents. Some of her treasured books were ruined.

"I know it’s stuff — you can’t take it with you — but it’s the stuff that I need," said Battles, unsure if the cottage could be saved.

A portion of a fishing pier collapsed in Virginia Beach, Virginia, closing a 3-mile (4.8-kilometer) section of the beach, including the boardwalk, the city said.

Downtown merchants in Avalon, New Jersey, rushed to pump out saltwater after some of the most significant tides in the seaside community’s history. Santa Claus was scheduled to arrive on a fire truck by evening to light the local Christmas tree.

Conditions were a "million times better than they were 24 hours ago," borough business administrator Scott Wahl said.

Water flooded the garage and surrounded the property of Cheryl McKenna in Ocean City, New Jersey, keeping her family inside for six hours on the holiday.

"Our family’s been here for 36 years," McKenna said. "But honestly, we’re thinking, is this the right place for us to be? We’re now retired, and we want to stay down here, but how often can we continue to do this?"

## Parts of the South and Midwest see snow while California faces floods

Parts of the South and Midwest saw snow while California faced flooding.

A portion of Tennessee was under a winter storm warning for up to an inch of snow Friday, after rain turned into light snow on Thanksgiving.

Conditions were calmer in the Midwest than the previous day, when as much as 15 inches (38 centimeters) of snow fell in eastern Missouri.

An ice arena in Wentzville, Missouri, was empty when its roof caved in.

High-tide waves caused flooding along the Pacific coast and continued impacting rail service. Buses were deployed after Amtrak’s Pacific Surfliner canceled or shortened over a dozen trains between San Diego and Los Angeles.

Record tides along the California coast prompted evacuations from Humboldt County in the far north to Ventura County near Los Angeles.

Heavy snow was expected in parts of Montana and Wyoming. A renewed surge of moisture associated with an atmospheric river across the Pacific Northwest moved toward the northern Rockies, the weather service said.
