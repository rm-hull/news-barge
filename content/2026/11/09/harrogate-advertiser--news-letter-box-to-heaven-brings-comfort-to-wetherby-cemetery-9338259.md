A Letterbox to Heaven has been installed at Wetherby Cemetery to help grieving people express their thoughts for a lost loved one.

The installation on the Hallfield Lane site was the idea of local resident Sarah Shourie, who approached the Town Council following the passing of her husband.

Town council Facilities Officer Laura Tune said: "The proposal was considered by the Council’s Property Committee, where members unanimously supported the idea, recognising it as a wonderful initiative that could bring comfort to many people experiencing grief and loss.

"The project was made possible thanks to generous donations from Sarah herself and Councillor Dawn Carr, who felt passionately about the idea and wanted to help bring it to life."

The letter box has now been installed in the cemetery's Memorial Garden, where visitors are welcome to leave letters, messages or personal thoughts for loved ones.

"Whether it is a special memory, words left unsaid, a message for a birthday or simply a few words to say 'I miss you', the letter box offers a private and personal way to remember those who are no longer with us," added Laura.

"Every letter placed in the box will be treated with the utmost care, respect and confidentiality. Letters will be removed regularly and will remain unopened.

"Once collected, they will either be composted and returned to the earth or carefully burnt, allowing the embers to rise towards heaven. No letters will be read or shared."

Laura explained that the installation of the letter box is the start of plans to enhance the Memorial Garden.

Further improvements are planned for the New Year, with the hope of introducing a memorial structure and additional benches to create a beautifully peaceful space where visitors can sit, reflect and remember their loved ones.

"Wetherby Town Council would like to extend its heartfelt thanks to Sarah Shourie and Councillor Dawn Carr, not only for their generous donations but for helping to create something that will leave a lasting legacy for the community," said Laura.

"Their kindness and thoughtfulness have provided a facility that we hope will bring comfort to many people for years to come.

"The Council hopes that the Letter Box to Heaven will become a special place for those grieving the loss of a loved one, offering a little comfort, a moment of reflection and somewhere to send those words that will always remain in our hearts."

The Council is also keen to hear from anyone interested in buying a memorial bench or tree within the cemetery.
