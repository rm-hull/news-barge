A community campaign has been launched amid concerns that plans for new housing could threaten one of the village's most cherished landscapes.

Residents have raised objections to proposals in Bradford Council's Draft Local Plan which would see a minimum of 100 homes built on Green Belt land at Great Pasture Lane in Burley-in-Wharfedale.

The 18.26-hectare site, identified as H119 in the draft plan published in October 2026, lies close to the village's famous Stepping Stones and surrounding riverside countryside, an area widely used by walkers and nature lovers.

Campaigners say the proposed development could have a lasting impact on the landscape and environment around the River Wharfe.

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21689750.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The 18.26-hectare site in Burley-in-Wharfedale, identified as H119 in the draft plan for the Bradford district, published in October 2026 *(Image: submitted)*

One resident said: "We're not against appropriate housing, but there are serious questions about why this particular piece of Green Belt countryside has been selected when Burley already has almost 500 homes either built or under development either side of the proposed allocation.

"There are also significant environmental and infrastructure concerns.

"These include the prospect of another access onto the A65 and the pressure on local education provision, with the secondary school already at maximum capacity and expected to accommodate the additional demand from around 800 new homes proposed across Ilkley, Addingham and Burley.

"We have to ask how much more development this area can reasonably absorb. Once this landscape is lost, it is lost for good."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21689720.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Green Belt land at Great Pasture Lane in Burley-in-Wharfedale *(Image: Burley residents)*

Residents argue that development of the site could affect not only the Green Belt but also local wildlife, the character of the landscape and the wider environment around one of Burley's most recognisable beauty spots.

The campaign comes as Bradford Council consults on its draft Local Plan, which sets out how and where future development across the district could take place between 2025 and 2043.

Campaigners are urging residents to review the proposals and submit representations before the consultation closes on November 30. Responses can be sent by email to [planning.policy@bradford.gov.uk.](mailto:planning.policy@bradford.gov.uk.)

Bradford Council is also holding online information sessions on November 12 and November 18, from 5pm to 6pm, to explain the consultation process and how residents can have their say. Places can be booked by emailing [planning.policy@bradford.gov.uk.](mailto:planning.policy@bradford.gov.uk.)

Residents have also been distributing flyers to locals informing them of the campaign and have launched the website, www.greatpasture.org.uk, which provides links to council documents and guidance on submitting responses to the consultation.
