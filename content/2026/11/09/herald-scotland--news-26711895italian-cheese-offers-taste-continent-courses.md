*From delicate starters and comforting pasta dishes to inventive desserts, quality Italian cheeses offer Scottish families and chefs an extraordinary palette of flavours, textures and possibilities*

For professional kitchens looking to bring something distinctive to the menu, the country’s rich dairy heritage provides inspiration well beyond the traditional cheeseboard.

Whether fresh, creamy, mature or full of character, Italian cheeses each have their own qualities, making them remarkably versatile ingredients. The challenge is simply discovering the combinations that allow them to shine.

The Real Italian Cheese website, an initiative from the Italian Trade Agency in collaboration with Assolatte, the Italian Dairy and Milk Association, showcases this diversity through an extensive selection of cheeses, recipes and culinary inspiration.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689797.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nettle risotto with Asiago PDO *(Image: Supplied)*

For lighter dishes and starters, fresh specialities offer plenty of scope. Creamy Burrata provides a delicious filling for handmade ravioli, while Mozzarella di Bufala Campana PDO and Mozzarella Fior di Latte bring freshness and delicate milky flavours to combinations featuring seasonal ingredients.

Ricotta, meanwhile, lends its soft, delicate texture to elegant creations such as asparagus soup with ricotta quenelles and Piedmontese hazelnuts.

For more substantial dishes, Italian cheeses can add depth and complexity. Asiago PDO brings character to creamy risottos, while Pecorino Toscano PDO and Pecorino Romano PDO offer distinctive savoury notes. Grana Padano PDO and Parmigiano Reggiano PDO are equally at home enhancing pasta dishes and baked creations.

The website www.realitaliancheese.co.uk features recipes ranging from spaghetti with broccoli, tomatoes and Grana Padano to a savoury tart with caramelised red onions, thyme and Parmigiano Reggiano.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689798.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Roman-style gnocchi with Gorgonzola *(Image: Supplied)*

There is also plenty of room for experimentation. Gorgonzola PDO brings richness to golden, oven-baked Roman-style gnocchi, while Provolone is paired with earthy mushrooms in a fresh salad.

Provolone Valpadana PDO provides a more unexpected savoury accent to roasted octopus served with chickpea cream and rosemary.

Nor should Italian cheese be confined to savoury courses.

Mascarpone’s wonderfully smooth, creamy texture makes it an ideal ingredient for a millefeuille layered with crisp pastry, while Taleggio PDO offers an intriguing contrast in a dessert combining pears and hazelnuts.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689799.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Millefeuille with Mascarpone *(Image: Supplied)*

Behind the selection is a tradition of craftsmanship and regional identity. The website highlights cheeses protected by European quality designations, including Protected Designation of Origin (PDO), helping visitors discover their individual characteristics and heritage.

For chefs seeking fresh ideas, new flavour combinations or inspiration to transform familiar ingredients, Italian cheese offers possibilities throughout the menu.
