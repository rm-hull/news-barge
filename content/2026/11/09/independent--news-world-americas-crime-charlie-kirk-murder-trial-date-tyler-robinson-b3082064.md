A June 2028 trial date for Tyler Robinson, the man accused of murdering Charlie Kirk last year, has been set by a Utah judge.

Robinson, 23, has pleaded not guilty to seven counts, including aggravated murder, in the shooting of right-wing activist Charlie Kirk during a debate at Utah Valley University.

Utah District Court Judge Tony Graf set jury selection for the trial for June 21, 2028, after Robinson's attorneys requested a late 2029 date to give them time to challenge Utah's request for the death penalty, gather additional evidence and seek a change of venue.

Robinson's lawyers ​expect a six-to-seven-month trial.

Prosecutors had sought a date of January ​31, 2028 to start what they expect to be a 17-week trial for the shooting death of Kirk, an ally of President Donald Trump.

In ‌a separate filing, Erika Kirk, Kirk's widow requested the trial to start in 2027 to speed what she described as a painful legal process.

Trump last month slammed Graf's handling of the case, ​saying the trial should start within a month and the judge was asking "questions that people already know the answer to."

Kirk was a polarizing figure in ​US politics. He was admired by conservatives for mobilizing young voters ​around his ⁠Christian and right-wing views. Civil rights advocates criticized ​Kirk for rhetoric they called racist, anti-immigrant, ​transphobic, ⁠and misogynist, citing his public remarks denigrating Black Americans, LGBTQ communities, Muslims and immigrants.

Prosecutors allege Robinson targeted Kirk for his opposition to gay marriage and transgender rights. Defense attorney ⁠Richard ​Novak has said in court that Kirk spoke "hatefully" about people who did not ​have traditional gender identities or were not heterosexual, but Robinson's defense say there was no evidence Robinson opposed those views.
