Miners from County Durham who witnessed the Battle of Orgreave during the 1984 miners’ strike are being urged to come forward and tell their stories as part of a national inquiry.

The Orgreave Inquiry is holding a listening event at Redhills Durham Miners Hall on Thursday, November 12, as it investigates the events of June 18, 1984.

Around 5,000 miners and 6,000 police officers were at the Orgreave coking plant in South Yorkshire that day, with clashes leaving many injured.

A total of 95 miners were arrested, but proceedings against them later collapsed after the prosecution offered no further evidence.

Baroness Mary Bousted, a member of the inquiry panel, said significant numbers of County Durham miners travelled to Orgreave and urged them to share their experiences.

![Redhills will host the listening event.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21491168.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Redhills will host the listening event. *(Image: Sally Ann Norman)*

She said: “We know that considerable numbers of Durham miners were going to picket around the country, we know that considerable numbers of them were there at Orgreave and we want to hear their stories.

“We do hope that it will provide an opportunity for people who maybe haven't had such an opportunity before to tell their story.

“Lots of people there haven't told their story to their families really. They couldn't talk about it even to their families.”

Blackhalls and Hesleden councillor Rob Crute, who was on strike at Easington Colliery at the time, said: “I have asked governments of all colours to open an inquiry into what happened at Orgreave.

“There were 95 people arrested and the trauma they went through was immense.

“What actually happened on that day in Orgreave needs to be uncovered and justice needs to be served.

“The inquiry is a first step and people need to be able to trust the process. I am hoping there will be a good turnout.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681256.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Rob Crute was picketing at Easington Colliery during the Miners' Strike of 1984. *(Image: ROB CRUTE)*

The inquiry stressed that nobody attending would be required to speak and people could share their experiences in a group or privately on a one-to-one basis.

Baroness Bousted added: “We understand that engaging with the inquiry can feel daunting, particularly when memories are painful or deeply personal.”

Durham County Council deputy leader Darren Grimes said: “Orgreave wasn't just a fight in a field in South Yorkshire.

"It was the day the state decided the men who powered this country were the enemy, and then tried to fit them up for it.
