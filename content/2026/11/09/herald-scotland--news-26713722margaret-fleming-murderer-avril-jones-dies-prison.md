The woman who murdered vulnerable teenager Margaret Fleming and concealed her death for almost two decades while claiming £182,000 in benefits has died in prison.

Avril Jones, 66, died at HMP Stirling on Sunday, the Scottish Prison Service has confirmed.

Jones and her partner Edward Cairney were convicted in 2019 of murdering 19-year-old Margaret, who vanished from their home in Inverkip, Inverclyde, in December 1999.

Her remains have never been found, despite repeated appeals from police and her family for the killers to reveal where they had hidden her body.

Cairney died in prison aged 82 in 2023, meaning both killers have now taken the secret of Margaret's final resting place to their graves.

Margaret had gone to live with the couple following the death of her father in 1995. Cairney and Jones had been friends of her father and were entrusted with looking after the vulnerable teenager.

She was last confirmed to have been seen alive on December 17, 1999.

However, her disappearance was not formally reported to police until 2016, more than 16 years later.

During that time, Jones continued to claim benefits in Margaret's name, receiving a total of £182,000.

The couple spun a series of elaborate stories to explain Margaret's absence, claiming at various points that she had run away with a traveller, become a gangmaster travelling around Europe or was involved in drug dealing under several different identities.

Their accounts were so extraordinary that detectives investigating the disappearance even questioned whether Margaret had existed.

In 2019, following a lengthy investigation, a jury found Jones and Cairney guilty of murdering Margaret within three weeks of her final confirmed sighting.

The case became one of Scotland's most notorious murders, with prosecutors securing convictions despite the absence of a body.

Neither Jones nor Cairney disclosed what had happened to Margaret's remains.

Scottish Conservative leader Russell Findlay, who interviewed the couple during his former career as a journalist, condemned the killers following confirmation of Jones's death.

He said: "Margaret Fleming was a vulnerable and innocent young woman who fell into the hands of two monsters.

"With both of her killers now dead, the only tears that should be shed today are in Margaret's memory.

"The refusal of Cairney and Jones to ever reveal the location of her remains confirms that they didn't possess an ounce of decency or compassion."

Jones's death is expected to be the subject of a fatal accident inquiry, in line with the usual procedures following a death in custody.

Her death closes another chapter in the long-running murder case, but leaves the central mystery unresolved.

More than 26 years after Margaret was last seen alive, her body has still not been recovered and her family remains without answers about where she was laid to rest.
