As weather warnings for fog come into place across parts of England, drivers risk a £50 fine for not observing a simple Highway Code rule.

The Met Office has issued a yellow fog warning across the Midlands, London, Wales, Yorkshire, the north west, east, south east and south west of England. The warning began at 6pm and will remain in place until 10am on Tuesday, meaning commuters will have to battle through the mist on their journey to work.

The forecaster warned that public transport and flights may be affected. Motorists also face the challenge of restricted visibility, but with the help of fog lights they should be able to make the journey into the office without too much difficulty.

![A fog warning is in place until 10am Tuesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/22/14/Screenshot-2026-11-09-at-22-00-43.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But when visibility clears, motorists should remember Rule 236 of the Highway Code: “You MUST NOT use front or rear fog lights unless visibility is seriously reduced as they dazzle other road users and can obscure your brake lights. You MUST switch them off when visibility improves.”

Those who don’t switch off their fog lights when the fog clears risk a £50 fixed penalty notice, according to the AA.

The Met Office has warned drivers to check road conditions before driving and leave extra journey time. Drivers should be confident in switching on fog lights and check they are working prior to their journeys.

![By Friday midnight, parts of the UK will see 0C temperatures](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/22/42/Screenshot-2026-11-09-at-22-02-02.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

It won’t be the first time Britons have woken up to fog this month with the Met Office issuing yellow warnings multiple times last week in Northern Ireland, London, and the south-east of England.

The fog is expected to give way to colder, frostier weather conditions in the south of England later this week. Meanwhile, parts of Scotland can expect wintry showers.

Met Office Chief Meteorologist Jason Kelly, said: "Snow showers could briefly fall on lower ground in northern Scotland and the Northern Isles, mainly Shetland, overnight on Wednesday and into Thursday. However, snow may only settle on higher ground and this is relatively normal for the time of year."

Temperatures are expected to fall below average widely by Thursday and Friday, as frost becomes more widespread. The far north east will also feel particularly cold in stronger winds, the forecaster said. By midnight on Friday, Manchester is expected to see freezing temperatures while parts of Scotland will see 1C.
