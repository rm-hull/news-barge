A Scottish energy support ship operator has unveiled a rise in turnover to £153 million.

The 139-year-old Aberdeen-based company also earned revenue outside the UK for the first time, with £7.7m coming from the rest of Europe.

North Star said turnover was up 11% in 2025, set against £138m the year before, while gross profit rose 19% in a year of on-schedule vessel deliveries and a landmark charter deal with German energy giant RWE.

The integrated offshore infrastructure services company grew gross profit by 19% to £49.4m in 2025, as three new service operation vessels (SOVs) entered service on schedule and the company signed the largest chartering deal in the global SOV sector.

Adjusted EBITDA (Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) rose 19% to £34.4m, compared with £29m. The firm's balance sheet also strengthened, with net assets up 18% to £191.3m and cash rising from £5.7m to £24.4m.

During the year to December 31, 2025, the Grampian Tweed began a 10-year contract at Dogger Bank, completing North Star’s four-vessel fleet at the UK’s largest offshore wind farm, with the Grampian Kestrel and Grampian Eagle following in June and July.

In August, North Star agreed four long-term charters with RWE totalling 44 years of commitment, including two newbuild vessels due in 2028 and 2029 which are expected to support up to 200 roles, including up to 100 new jobs.

RWE hailed at the time a “long-term partnership with North Star, the UK’s largest shipowner-operator, backed by global private markets firm Partners Group, acting on behalf of its clients”.

RWE said: “The companies have agreed that RWE will charter two next-generation service operation vessels (SOVs), the Grampian Eagle and the Grampian Kestrel, from North Star - both capable of using alternative low-emission fuels.

“Full marine services will be delivered by North Star as part of the charters. RWE and North Star have also signed reservation agreements for two newbuild vessels delivered by the Norwegian Vard Group.

“This partnership will pave the way for the efficient and low-emission operation of RWE’s existing and further growing offshore wind portfolio in Europe.”

The company’s emergency response and rescue vessel (ERRV) business ended the year with £275m of firm backlog, maintaining steady demand despite the pressures facing UK oil and gas.

North Star’s pre-tax loss of £19.3m is a result of interest costs which rose to £25.6m, set against £15.6m the year before, because of the company’s continued investment in new vessel assets as it builds out its SOV fleet for the future, it said.

A spokesperson for the company said that “these vessels take up to three years to build, so financing costs come ahead of earnings, however North Star will earn attractive returns on its investment in years to come, with each ship providing around 40 years of future earnings capability”.

Growth has continued into 2026 through the acquisition of four SOVs from Edda Wind, funded through new debt and further equity from Partners Group as part of £228m of debt and equity raised since the year end, and a £275m refinancing that brought three new banks into the lending group and covers existing shipbuilding commitments in full.

![exec](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21690472.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Gitte Gard Talmo, North Star chief executive officer, and some of the fleet *(Image: North Star)*

Gitte Gard Talmo, North Star chief executive officer, said: “Our strategy is to invest for long-term growth, backed by long-term customer commitments, and 2025 shows that investment beginning to translate into performance. We grew turnover by 11% and adjusted EBITDA by 19%, while expanding our fleet, securing a landmark agreement with RWE and establishing our first revenues outside the UK.

“We have continued that progress in 2026, including the acquisition of four SOVs from Edda Wind. With 14 SOVs, including four under construction, and our recent £275m refinancing fully covering our existing shipbuilding commitments, North Star is financially well positioned for the next phase of our growth.”

The company said that since entering offshore wind in 2021, North Star has committed more than £750m in SOVs and secured significant contracts including wins with Dogger Bank, RWE, EnBW and Siemens Gamesa Renewable Energy. The four-ship acquisition completed in April 2026 takes its offshore wind fleet total to 14, a “step closer to achieving its ambition of 40+ SOVs by 2040”.

North Star is described as “the world’s leading owner and operator of SOVs and ERRVs, delivering mission-critical offshore infrastructure support services across the offshore wind and oil and gas sectors”.

Headquartered in Aberdeen with operational bases in Lowestoft, Newcastle and Hamburg, the company employs over 1,700 offshore and onshore personnel and is backed by Partners Group, one of the largest firms in the global private markets industry.

Originally established to support the fisheries industry, North Star moved into oil and gas in the 1970s and later offshore wind and operates a fleet of 50 vessels.

Its 36 ERRVs make it "the UK’s largest ERRV operator, providing support to approximately 50 North Sea installations around the clock, 365 days a year".
