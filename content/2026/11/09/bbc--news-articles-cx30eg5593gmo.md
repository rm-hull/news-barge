!["There are more like Brand," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/6c7d/live/d9f4d880-dbbd-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

Warnings about other possible abuse in the media industry from Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy fill many of the front pages in the wake of comedian Russell Brand's conviction on rape and sexual assault charges. "There are more like Brand" reads the Daily Mirror's headline, which goes on to explain that Nandy "fears showbiz could be shielding more predators like Russell Brand".

!["More 'Russell Brands' in telly," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a891/live/dd596040-dbbd-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

The Daily Star continues by quoting Nandy saying the media industry "hasn't learned" from "the Russell Brand rape scandal".

!["Brand's £5m house switch," reads the headline on the front page of the Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/df4d/live/e46ff380-dbbd-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The Metro leads with "Brand's £5m house switch", reporting that he "is transferring his £5million mansion to his wife", citing documents that apply to the "luxury six-bed thatched cottage". In a separate story, Catherine, the Princess of Wales, is seen wearing an all-black assemblage, wide-rimmed hat and three-poppy pin at a Remembrance Sunday ceremony, depicted under the headline "Kate pays tribute to the fallen". It also notes that she "wore a drop pearl earring belonging to the late Princess Diana".

!["As beast is jailed... Brand hides £9m stash," reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/c737/live/dc92b0b0-dbc4-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

"Brand hides £9m stash" says the Sun, with the paper suggesting he "moved nearly £9million from one of his firms – which may stop money going to his victims".

!["Ministers set to agree steep cuts to electric vehicle targets," reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9e72/live/d3e7c1d0-dbc4-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

"Ministers set to agree steep cuts to electric vehicle targets," the Guardian reports. Government sources say the plan could involve agreeing to "cut a key target for electric vehicles sales by about a quarter", a reduction that "will alarm Labour MPs already upset about changes to green agenda", according to the paper. Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Conservative Party leader Kemi Badenoch are also shown laying large poppy wreaths at the Remembrance Day ceremony at the Cenotaph War Memorial in London.

!["'Climate crisis is here' – UK makes case for COP," reads the headline on the front page of the Independent.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/a669/live/d90432c0-dbc4-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

Ahead of the COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, the Independent writes "UK makes case for COP", citing comments made by Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh attending the UN summit. "After a summit of record-breaking heatwaves, wildfires and school closures, Britain is now facing worsening droughts," the paper says, with the headline declaring: "Climate crisis is here". The paper shows a "record nine former PMs" lining up at the Cenotaph as King Charles "leads nation in remembrance".

!["Cameron: End NHS dementia drug block," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/4ac1/live/cf888660-dbc4-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

Lord David Cameron's call to "end NHS dementia drug block" leads the Daily Telegraph. The former prime minister "has joined 48 leading dementia specialists and former health ministers in demanding action to end the block on providing the treatments through the health service", the paper reports, adding that he is "also calling for a maximum 18-week wait for a dementia diagnosis so that sufferers can access treatments as soon as possible". Elsewhere, the King's is shown saluting at the Cenotaph – under the headline "Salute to fallen heroes".

!["Dementia carers' postcode lottery," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f9c6/live/c9a3bc60-dbc4-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

"Dementia carers' postcode lottery" is the Daily Mail's headline, "with those in some parts of the country struggling far more than others", referencing findings from the government's survey of adult carers in England. "Looking after a loved one with the disease is robbing people of their friends, hobbies and career," the paper writes. In a separate headline, the paper reports: "Boss of tech giant Nvidia admits he cheated on wife with 'Chinese spy'."

!["UK rail network at risk from grey war cyber attack," reads the headline on the front page of the i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/1e13/live/d278a5a0-dbbd-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

The i Paper carries the warning "UK rail network at risk from grey war cyber attack", quoting Department for Transport documents expressing concerns about possible vulnerabilities to threats across the network. According to the paper, ministers have been warned that "major hack from hostile state could disrupt train services for a week and cost economy £1.8bn", with experts telling the paper "a hack on Britain's railway communications would paralyse some lines immediately".

!["'Hunt forces' target cyber plots against UK military," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/bec8/live/e0896650-dbc4-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The Times reveals "hunt forces" are being deployed "overseas to disrupt enemy hackers who are preparing to cripple Britain's military system". Such a mission requires sign-off by the defence secretary, the paper adds, with experts dispatched "to stop cyber-operations in their infancy before they escalate into a large-scale attack".

!["'Ghost bosses' behind vape shop blight," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/91d5/live/e0fffa10-dbbd-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The Daily Express leads with its own investigation revealing that a "crime fixer who allegedly helps asylum seekers work illegally is linked to a network of suspect vape shops". According to the paper, a number of "ghost bosses" allegedly "disguise" the real owners of a cluster of vape shops in the North West of England.

!["Democrats pledge to impeach Trump aides as infighting grips Republicans," reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/58ac/live/cbcae650-dbbd-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

The Financial Times looks at the fallout from the rout of the Republican Party at the US midterm elections. "Democrats pledge to impeach Trump aides", writes the paper. It quotes Robert Garcia – the top Democrat on the House oversight committee - saying "there was 'real investigative work' to be done into Elon Musk's purge of federal employees". He adds: "There are so many allegations... whether it's the pardons that have gone out to billionaires and donors, whether it's been companies that have been giving to the ballroom – in exchange for what? We need to find out."

According to the Sun, external, Russell Brand has moved almost £9m out of one of his companies, which could stop money going to his victims. The Metro, external says Brand, who will be sentenced for rape and sexual assault on Monday, has transferred ownership of a £5m mansion to his wife. The Daily Mirror, external focuses on comments from Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy who warned the entertainment industry was not "doing enough" to protect women. "There are more like Brand" reads its headline.

The Times, external leads on its interview with a senior military commander, who says "hunt forces" are being sent overseas to disrupt hackers preparing to attack Britain's military systems. Major General Mike Fayers, the head of the Defence, Cyber and Electromagnetic Force, confirms to the paper that missions are already under way.

The i Paper, external front page says ministers have been warned that the UK's rail network is at risk from a major cyber attack, that could cost the economy almost £2bn. It says Department for Transport documents have set out plans to bolster resilience against such threats.

The Guardian, external leads on government plans to reduce electric vehicle targets. The paper notes the move will alarm Labour MPs already worried about ministers' commitment to the green agenda. Downing Street tells the paper that the government is united in its commitment to the zero emission vehicle mandate and no decisions have been made.

After taking control of Congress in the US midterm elections, the Democrats have reiterated their intention to impeach close aides of President Donald Trump, according to the Financial Times, external. The report says the defence secretary, Pete Hegseth, Elon Musk's purge of federal employees and donations made by businesses to Trump's ballroom could all be investigated.

The Daily Telegraph, external focuses on calls from the former prime minister Lord Cameron for drugs proven to slow Alzheimer's to be made available on the NHS. The paper says he has joined a number of leading specialists in demanding an end to the block on the treatments through the health service. A government spokesman says two drugs are currently being evaluated by The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence.
