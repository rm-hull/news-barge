Stage and screen star Brian Cox has revealed plans to return to the stage in Scotland – performing on stage with Alan Cumming at Pitlochry Festival Theatre.

The pair will be starring in a revival of The Dresser, Ronald Harwood's hit 1980s West End and Broadway play on the struggles of an ageing and forgetful actor about to take his final bow with the help of his personal assistant.

The Dundee-born actor worked with Cumming on the recent feature film Glenrothan, the Succession star’s directorial film debut, which he also appeared in.

Cox, who worked as a stage manager at Dundee Rep when he left school, was appearing during a fundraising event at the Royal Lyceum Theatre Edinburgh.

He praised the "amazing" job Cumming has been doing since being appointed artistic director at Pitlochry Festival Theatre two years ago.

Cox started his professional acting career at the Lyceum after graduating from the London Academy of Music and Dramatic Art.

Cox, who was a founding member of the Lyceum’s in-house company in 1965, told the audience that we preferred working in theatre to film television.

However, he also revealed that he wants to reunite with writer Neil Forsyth, creator of the dramas Guilt and The Gold, for a revival of his BBC Scotland comedy Bob Servant.

Cox starred as the fictional cheeseburger van owner in the both the radio and TV adaptations of Forsyth best-selling books.

Cox, who was interviewed by Lyceum artistic director James Brining, suggested Scottish theatres were now outperforming equivalent venues in England.

He said: “What I love about Scotland is that it has kept its head over theatre.

“Whether it is here, at Dundee Rep or the Citizens (in Glasgow), the standard has always been maintained and there are really gifted people working here.

“I think we are doing much better here than down south. We have a sense of continuity and have a sense of who we are.

“The acting talent, the directing talent and the writing talent are just second to none.

“The Lyceum is one of the most beautiful theatres I’ve ever been in. When you've got that grounding, the world's your oyster.”

Cox and Cumming appeared on screen together earlier this year in the Highlands-set comedy-drama Glenrothan, playing estranged brothers reunited in the Scottish Highlands after 35 years as they try to save their family’s historic whisky distillery.

Glenrothan also starred Shirley Henderson, who performed with Cumming at Pitlochry Festival Theatre earlier this year.

It was announced last week that the musical they starred in, A History of Paper, will be transferring to New York next year.

Cox worked with its director, Andrew Panton, Dundee Rep’s artistic director, on his last Scottish stage play Make It Happen, which saw him play the ghost of economist Adam Smith returning to Edinburgh to haunt Royal Bank of Scotland chief executive Fred “The Shred” Goodwin.

Cox returned to the Lyceum in 2015 to star with Bill Paterson in the theatre’s 50th anniversary production of Waiting for Godot.

He said: “For a country of this size, Scotland produces an enormous amount of incredible work. It makes me very proud to be part of it.

“Coming back to Scotland is so important to me because it reminds me where my theatre roots here.

“What Andrew has been doing in Dundee and what Alan is now doing in Pitlochry is wonderful. In fact, we’re going to do The Dresser in Pitlochry. Will be playing the dresser and I will be playing ‘Sir’. It couldn’t be more perfect.

“Alan is extraordinary. He does everything. He never stops. There is a rumour that there are actually two Alan Cummings. I can believe it. What he is doing in Pitlochry is amazing. He is a very cunning beast.”

Cox appeared at the Lyceum after finishing a run of shows in Washington playing the German composer Johann Sebastian Bach in the play The Score.

He said: “Theatre to me is the real business. At the end of the day, you can talk about movies and all the other stuff that you do but, ultimately, theatre is the business. That’s why I come back to it. You can't stay away from it.

“It is probably the way an actor gets his or her fullest expression. There is something about that relationship between an audience in the dark and you up here, and that magic thing that you create. It's an extraordinary thing.”

Cox played Bob Servant for the first time in 2010 when Forsyth secured a commission for a Radio Scotland show. The Dundonian writer’s subsequent TV adaptation also starred Jonathan Watson, Rufus Jones, Greg McHugh, Pollyanna McIntosh, Daniela Nardini and Alex Norton.

Speaking at the Lyceum event, Cox said: “I’m a proud east-coaster. I’d always wanted to do something that was Dundonian. Bob Servant is Dundonian, but lives in The Ferry (Broughty Ferry).

“I have said that we should do a Bob Servant special. It has become quite cult. People love it and it is really good.

“Neil is an incredible writer. I know he's got he's become sort of serious writer, but his comic writing is absolutely brilliant.”

Discuss the demands of making Glenrothan, Cox joked: “The preparation was demanding, especially because I was also acting in it. But I was very good in it. The director really knew how to handle me.

“It is very gratifying when you are working in a community. If you don’t create the community then you don’t have the work because it’s the community that creates the work. The audience gets a sense of that.

“I knew my shortcomings on Glenrothan. I knew that I could do scenes, but I knew that I'm not particularly visual. I had a very good director of photography. I gave him carte blanche. He did an amazing job."
