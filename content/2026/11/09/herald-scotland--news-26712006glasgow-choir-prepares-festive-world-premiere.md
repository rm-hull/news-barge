The Glasgow School of Art Choir will give the world premiere of a specially commissioned composition at its 2026 Christmas concert.

The choir will perform Stars, written by composer Kim Porter and set to a poem by Marjorie Pickthall, at the New Auditorium in Glasgow Royal Concert Hall on December 5.

Described as a celebration of the winter night sky, the piece will be performed by the choir’s 150 members.

Porter has written music for television and performed as an alto with the BBC Singers, The Gabrieli Consort and The Sixteen. She is known for her choral compositions.

The concert programme will also feature Jay Capperauld’s The Winter’s Brightening, Benjamin Britten’s A Ceremony of Carols and a selection of seasonal favourites.

The GSA Choir is one of Scotland’s largest non-auditioned choirs and regularly commissions new work, having previously collaborated with composers including Cecilia McDowall, Ailie Robertson and Sir James MacMillan.

Founded in 2012, the choir will mark its 15th anniversary in 2027 with a concert at Glasgow’s City Halls on May 29. The event will feature the premiere of a 30-minute composition by Jay Capperauld, with poetry by Niall Campbell.

Jamie Sansbury, musical director of the GSA Choir, said: "This is an extraordinary new composition, very fitting for this time of year, and the choir is enormously excited to be given the opportunity to perform its world premiere to set the tone for Christmas.

"Through her atmospheric choral writing, Kim has brought musical life to Marjorie’s poem, perfectly evoking the slender moon, the brightness of Orion, and the vastness of the night sky.

"Our rehearsals of the piece have been quite spine-tingling.

"The GSA Choir has a commitment to supporting composers to create exciting new choral music.

"In the last fourteen years we have commissioned over 20 new works and it is a particular privilege for us to then share these with our audiences."
