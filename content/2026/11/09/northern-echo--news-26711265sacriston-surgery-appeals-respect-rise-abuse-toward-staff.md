A County Durham GP surgery has appealed for greater respect towards its staff amid a rise in abusive behaviour from patients.

Staff at Sacriston Surgery, on Front Street, have reported facing a rising number of incidents involving rude, abusive or disrespectful behaviour, with the surgery appealing for more "courtesy, patience and respect" from patients.

In a Facebook post, Sacriston Surgery urged patients to be more understanding with reception staff, adding that they were the first point of contact for patients and worked hard to help, support and assist people contacting the practice.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21161820.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sacriston Surgery reminded patients that its staff are "here to help," adding: "A little kindness and understanding can make a huge difference." *(Image: PA MEDIA)*

Sacriston Surgery wrote: "We understand that people can become frustrated, anxious or upset. However, please remember that some things are simply beyond our control."

The appeal follows wider concerns about violence and aggression towards NHS workers, with recent figures showing abuse of healthcare staff remains a significant issue across the health service.

Reports of abusive behaviour towards healthcare staff have increased since the Covid-19 pandemic, with staff at County Durham and Darlington NHS Trust reporting in October that they had been "spat at" and threatened with violence while at work.

Sacriston Surgery wrote: "There is never an excuse for personal abuse, aggression or disrespect."

The practice also reminded patients that reception workers "are people too," who deserved courtesy, patience and respect amid the rising pressures across the healthcare industry.

Residents were quick to defend the surgery, describing the rise in aggressive incidents as "appalling" and "shocking" in the comments.

One Sacriston local wrote: "Never in the world. Totally unacceptable. The reception staff pass on the position of the practice at time and if you have a concern contact the manager and he or she should deal with it.

"If you target reception you are taking the easy, but wrong, route."

The pattern of frontline NHS staff taking the brunt of patient frustrations extends nationally, with NHS England data published in March showing a three-year high in physical attacks on staff.

Sacriston Surgery reminded patients that its staff are "here to help," adding: "A little kindness and understanding can make a huge difference."

The surgery also reminded patients that those who wish to make a formal complaint should contact the practice manager.
