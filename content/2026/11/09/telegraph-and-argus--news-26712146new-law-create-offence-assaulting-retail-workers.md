*This week's MP column is written by Bradford South MP Judith Cummins*

No one should go to work on a morning expecting to be verbally or physically abused.

We all expect to go to work, do our job and come home to friends and family.

It’s an extension of the old line: a fair day’s pay for a fair day’s work — and a fair day’s treatment.

Yet, more front-line workers in retail and hospitality than ever are having to deal with higher levels of verbal and physical abuse in the workplace.

This is especially the case for those working in the retail industry.

This week is Respect for Workers Week, an annual campaign by workers' union USDAW that highlights the unacceptable rise of abuse to shop workers.

And the research from USDAW is shocking: Abuse against retail staff has more than doubled in the last four years.

Damning figures show that nearly 80% of shopworkers have been verbally abused in the past year, while over half say they have been threatened with violence while on shift.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21690313.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Keep Your Cool *(Image: LucasJayBowman)*

Ahead of Respect for Workers Week, I spent time with front-line staff to hear directly about their experiences.

I heard examples of workers receiving horrific sexist and racist abuse when doing their best to deal with matters outside of their control: dealing with retail crimes like shoplifting, enforcing laws on the sale of age-restricted goods like cigarettes and alcohol, or dealing with customer frustrations when staffing is short.

These experiences are harrowing.

Workers told me how, in recent years, the growth of abuse has led to a growing acceptance that being on the receiving end of people’s frustration is now “part of the job” of working in retail.

Yet we wouldn’t expect that working in an office or in a trade.

![Bradford South MP Judith Cummins](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20450018.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bradford South MP Judith Cummins *(Image: Newsquest)*

Retail and hospitality is no different: Absolutely no one, in any role, should have to go to work expecting to be threatened with violence at work: Abuse is never “just part of the job”.

Let’s not forget that it was retail and shop workers who put their own health at risk to keep us fed during the pandemic.

This makes it even more worrying that, since then, violence against shop workers has only increased.

That is why I have long supported USDAW’s Freedom From Fear campaign that raises awareness, and empowers workers to speak up and out about abuse at work.

And things are changing.

It is reassuring that definitive action is now being taken to increase protection for front-line workers:

In 2024, the Labour Government announced that it would be bringing legislation to better protect retail workers.

And, earlier this year, the Crime and Policing Act passed into law meaning that there will soon be a new standalone criminal offence of assaulting a retail worker, giving front-line staff the recognition and protection at work that they deserve.

What’s more, the Act will remove the £200 threshold for police to deal with shoplifting as a summary offence, finally taking the crime seriously.

Shoplifting is not a victimless crime, the repercussions of repeated offences cause harm beyond the theft itself, causing fear, worry and physical harm to retail staff.

Meanwhile, workers’ reforms that came into force just last week as part of the Employment Rights Act are promoting more positive change in the workplace, requiring employers to take proactive steps to prevent and deal with sexual harassment in the workplace, including from customers and clients.

The new protections now becoming law are an important step forward, but legislation is only part of the answer.

All of us - employers, police, customers and communities all have a responsibility to stand with front-line workers and make clear that abuse, threats and violence will never be tolerated.

Retail staff keep our communities running and deserve to be safe at work.

Respect for Workers Week is a timely reminder that we must all play our part in creating workplaces where people are valued, protected and treated with respect.

That is a goal worth working towards, and one I will continue to support both in Parliament and in Bradford.
