Hazel Powell is the co-founder of Baern, a café and bakery at Bowhouse in Fife’s East Neuk, which she opened with Giacomo Pesce in 2022.

Built around a love of cooking, eating and foraging, Baern champions local, seasonal produce and takes a low-waste approach to its food.

Powell trained at Ballymaloe Cookery School in Ireland and went on to work at Ballintaggart in Highland Perthshire and Fhior Garden Cafe in Edinburgh before taking the leap and opening Baern.

This week, she takes on our chef Q&A:

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689770.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hazel Powell and Giacomo Pesce at Baern in the East Neuk of Fife *(Image: Mike Sim)*

**When did you realise you wanted to be a chef?** Being a chef didn’t even cross my mind until I was well into my twenties and well into what I thought was my dream job after art school. My family suffered a huge amount of loss at that time, and the only thing that really helped was cooking and eating together at the kitchen table. I thought three months at Ballymaloe Cookery School would be a nice break and haven’t looked back since.**What was your first kitchen job like?** My very first kitchen job was as a KP when I was 13. I remember wondering how I was meant to wash pots that felt bigger than me. My first proper chef job was wonderful. I worked with two brilliant female chefs at Ballintaggart; there was so much laughter, brilliant food and no ego.**What does "good food" mean to you?** Good food is real food, properly seasonal food, grown or reared by people who care about the soil and the planet. Local to wherever you are. It’s the opposite of anything wrapped in plastic or dunked in chlorine.**Can you share a memory of your worst kitchen disaster?** Hard to choose. But probably dropping a huge tray of lamb fat from the oven at 1 am after an incredibly long day. The fat sets like wax, and the entire kitchen was coated in a thick, glossy layer. I sat with my burnt feet in a bucket while poor Giacomo cleaned it up.**Where is your favourite place to eat?** Gloriosa. Hands Down. Rosie Healey cooks everything I want to eat, the drinks are exactly what I want to drink, and the service is just right. I always leave joyful.**What's the best thing you've cooked recently?** I’ve been poaching a lot of chickens at home lately. So simple, but sets the world, and the week, right.**What do you think is the biggest misconception people have about working in a professional kitchen?** Things that seem the most simple in a restaurant have probably taken the most time and care.**Read more:If you weren't a chef, what do you think you'd be doing instead?** Oh, a million things. Food seems to be the right creative outlet for now, but there are so many other things I think about. On the other hand, if I could work out how to make money from just pottering around the house, I’d be doing that.**Is there one ingredient or kitchen tool you think everyone should have at home?** A wide cast iron pan with a lid. My sister gave me one last Christmas, and I use it six nights out of seven.**What's been the standout highlight of your career so far?**

Opening Baern with Giacomo. We did it on a mad post covid whim when life felt like it needed to change. It’s the hardest and best thing I’ve ever done.
