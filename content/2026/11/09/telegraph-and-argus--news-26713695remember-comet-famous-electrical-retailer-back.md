High street giant Comet is making a comeback.

The electrical retailer, which collapsed in 2012, is being relaunched as an online marketplace offering more than one million products from 2,500 sellers.

Cas Paton, the entrepreneur behind the revival, said: "I remember being a very young boy and going into Comet and just being so excited by the potential of all this new technology.

"Whether it was the TVs or the electronics, these were things you aspired to own."

Comet was founded in Hull in 1933 and grew to become a household name across the UK before its collapse, which resulted in the loss of nearly 7,000 jobs.

The company’s downfall was one of the most high-profile retail failures since Woolworths closed its doors in 2008.

Mr Paton, 42, who has purchased the rights to the Comet name, is relaunching the brand with its original black and orange branding in an effort to tap into public nostalgia.

He said: "It was non-negotiable that we went live with the brand everyone actually remembered.

"We wanted to try and bring that nostalgia of the brand and the history and the way it makes people feel into an online marketplace."

The new Comet will operate as a marketplace rather than a direct retailer, meaning it will not hold its own stock.

Instead, the platform will connect customers with a wide range of products from third-party retailers, including TVs, washing machines, mobile phones, computers, and other electronics.

While the business will begin as an online-only operation, Mr Paton is already considering ways to bring the brand back to the high street.

He said: "I want Comet to be able to connect to stores again.

"We’re exploring a few options that might put Comet’s name back on a few buildings."

Its website will also sell products ranging from TVs and washing machines to mobile phones, computers and the latest gadgets.

Although the new business will initially operate online, Paton is exploring click-and-collect partnerships which could eventually see the Comet name return to high street buildings.
