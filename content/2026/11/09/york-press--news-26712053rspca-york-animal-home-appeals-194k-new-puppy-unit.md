A cardboard box containing four weak and cold puppies has highlighted the urgent need for new facilities at an animal rescue centre.

The two-day-old litter was surrendered to RSPCA York Animal Home after their mother died during birth and they had been left without the warmth and care they desperately needed.

Without a dedicated facility for newborns, staff cared for the puppies in their own homes, feeding them every two hours throughout the day and night.

The unit will offer a safe, purpose-built environment for puppies and their mothers, ensuring they receive specialist care from the moment they arrive.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21213354.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An arial image of the new-look RSPCA York Animal Home. *(Image: Made with Google AI)*

Construction is already underway, with the unit expected to open by January 2027.

The puppy unit will cost about £300,000 to build. Pets Foundation has contributed £100,000 while supporters have raised nearly £6,000 through virtual brick sales.

After these contributions, about £194,000 is still needed to fund the unit.

![Work on the new puppy unit at RSPCA York Animal Home is in progress.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21690184.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Work on the new puppy unit at RSPCA York Animal Home is in progress. *(Image: Made with Google AI)*

Lucy Lowthian, capital campaign lead at RSPCA York Animal Home, said: "Stories like these four tiny puppies remind us that this is about so much more than bricks and mortar.

"These are incredibly vulnerable little lives, and the care they need from the moment they arrive can make all the difference.

"Our team will always do everything possible to give every animal the best chance, but having the right environment and facilities is absolutely vital.

"The animals who come through our doors deserve the very best, and we need our community to help us give them that future."

The puppies, named James, Olivia, Luke, and Jane, were diagnosed with a bacterial infection, and Jane required emergency veterinary care.

Thanks to the efforts of staff and Green Vets, all four are receiving the treatment they need, said a centre spokesperson.

A spokesperson said: "Every donation, virtual brick purchase, and fundraising effort will help bring this specialist facility to completion and ensure the home can provide vulnerable puppies and their mothers with the care and environment they need."

The charity is encouraging support from individuals, businesses, community groups, and schools.

Options include corporate sponsorship, employee fundraising, organising community events, or making a personal donation.

Supporters can also buy a £50 virtual brick, which will be displayed on the new puppy unit wall.

![Work on the new puppy unit at RSPCA York Animal Home is in progress.](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21690183.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Supporters are being invited to pay £50 for a 'virtual brick' to help towards the costs of the new puppy unit at RSPCA York Animal Home. *(Image: Made with Google AI)*

Ms Lowthian said: "We are so pleased to see the puppy unit taking shape, but there is still work to do to reach our fundraising target for this building.

"Every contribution will help us create a warm, safe, and specialist environment for puppies and their mothers."  
  
The first phase of York Animal Home's wider £5 million redevelopment also includes a modern, purpose-built cattery and a new community and training hub, with phase one targeted for completion by June 2027.

To support the appeal, purchase a virtual brick, or discuss a corporate partnership, visit www.yorkanimalhome.com.
