One of the UK's largest independent dealers of new and used heavy equipment has collapsed into administration.

The business employed 345 people in the UK when it collapsed, administrators said, with 203 immediately made redundant.

Molson Group, headquartered in Bristol, called in administrators on Friday, November 6, after facing "challenging trading conditions" and "weaker demand" in the UK construction market.

A statement on PwC's website says Tom Crookham, Mark (Toby) Banfield and Ross Connock of PwC have been appointed joint administrators of Molson Group Limited (MGL).

Crookham and Connock are joined by Vesela Romagnoli as joint administrators of Molson Equipment Services Limited (MES), Capital Equipment Finance Limited (CEF), Aggregate Processing Solutions Limited (APS) and Molson Scotland Limited (MSL).

Connock, joint administrator and director at PWC, said: “It is regrettable that Molson Group’s UK businesses have entered administration and that this has resulted in a significant number of redundancies.

"We recognise how difficult this will be for those affected and their families.

“Our immediate priority is to support affected employees through the redundancy process, while working with other stakeholders to undertake an orderly wind-down of the UK businesses and maximise value from the companies’ assets."

Administrators said they explored several options, including selling the UK operations and assets, but no buyer completed a deal in time, and so the firm will now be wound down.

The group has experienced significant financial difficulties in recent months and faced near-term funding needs driven by "challenging macroeconomic factors including weakened UK demand and uncertain trading conditions".

Immediately after the appointment of joint administrators of Molson Group Limited, the group sold the shares it held in its US businesses to a newly incorporated company.

Molson Group's shares in its American operations were transferred to Fieldsec Hastings Limited along with a small number of UK staff and all US employees.

Mr Connock added: "The sale of the US businesses means those operations can continue outside the UK Group, preserving the business and employment there."

The group's website carries a statement from the administrators.
