A Glasgow biotech company has teamed up with Premier League club Nottingham Forest to investigate whether blood tests could help identify injury risks in professional footballers before they happen.

Entourage, based at Kelvin Campus, is conducting a 12-week study involving the club’s 25-player men’s B team, with support from Scottish Enterprise.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689044.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Entourage Team. *(Image: Martin Shields)*

The research will use plasma proteomics, the analysis of proteins in blood, to monitor players’ biological responses to training, matches and recovery.

Players will provide blood samples twice a week, allowing researchers to analyse thousands of proteins and track individual responses to physical demands, injuries and recovery.

By combining the results with existing training, workload and clinical information, the team hopes to identify protein patterns that could indicate a greater risk of non-contact soft tissue injuries.

The trial is described as the first high-frequency plasma proteomic study involving a professional sports team.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689054.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A blood sample being collected. *(Image: Supplied)*

The research comes amid concerns about the impact of hamstring injuries in elite football. UEFA figures for the 2021/22 season found they accounted for one in five days of injury absence across elite European football, double the rate recorded two decades earlier.

Entourage’s technology focuses on using protein analysis to better understand inflammation, immune function and recovery. The company is also building a reference dataset for elite athletes, supported by its research centre at Kelvin Campus.

The findings could help clubs develop more personalised training and recovery plans based on players’ individual biological responses.

The company also sees potential applications for its technology beyond sport, including the early detection of conditions such as cardiovascular disease, diabetes and Alzheimer’s.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689043.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Entourage lab technician Matthew Mitchell prepares a blood sample for testing in their lab. *(Image: Elaine Livingstone)*

Entourage was co-founded by Professor Patrick Gunning, Dr Taleb Sedghi and Dr Mark Lindsay and launched with a $5 million pre-seed funding round.

Professor Gunning previously co-founded Dunad Therapeutics and helped secure a $1.3 billion partnership with Novartis.

The company plans to report its findings to Nottingham Forest at the end of the 12-week study.

Professor Patrick Gunning, chief executive and co-founder of Entourage, said:"One of the questions we are trying to answer is whether there are signals in the blood that could indicate an increased risk of a non-contact injury.

"That is something we need to investigate through the study."

Dimitrios Kalogiannidis, head of medical at Nottingham Forest, said: "We are deeply committed to evidence-led player care.

"We believe in the importance of building a more robust biological profile of each player, so we’re pleased to be taking part in this research study that could benefit Nottingham Forest and the wider game for years to come."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21689046.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Entourage’s chief executive and co-founder, Professor Patrick Gunning pictured with lab scientist Daniel Poloske. *(Image: Elaine Livingstone)*

Dr Mark Lindsay, co-founder of Entourage, said: "We're moving soft-tissue care from estimation to measurement.

"We read the biology of the injury, precision directives guide every stage of treatment, and return to play is decided by the data.

"You can't manage what you can't measure."
