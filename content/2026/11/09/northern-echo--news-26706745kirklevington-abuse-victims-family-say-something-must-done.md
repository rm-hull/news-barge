The family of a "torture camp" victim say "something must be done" after the Prime Minister branded the abuse “deeply disturbing”.

Paul Overton was one of hundreds of young boys subjected to terrifying abuse from prison officers at the Kirklevington Detention Centre between the 1960s and 1990s.

The 65-year-old kept the attacks a secret for decades, turning to alcohol to mask his trauma - but sadly died in September while still waiting to see his abusers held to account.

This is a fear of many of the other “forgotten” Kirklevington victims - and a stark reminder of how Cleveland Police’s 12-year Operation Magnolia is yet to see a single former officer charged.

The grandad-of-three family have now vowed to fight for closure and justice in their father’s name so that he can finally rest in peace.

This week, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Paul’s story was a “powerful reminder” of the impact of “deeply disturbing” abuse, saying victims “deserve to have their voices heard”.

Now, Paul’s daughters Kirsti and Natalie Overton have said it was “amazing” to hear that the PM had read his story but urged that “something to be done”.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21622309.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Natalie and Kirsti Overton from Newcastle with photo of their dad Paul *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Saleswoman Kirsti, 38, said she now wants justice for her dad “more than ever” - and that his story being taken to the corridors of Westminster is the “best place it could have gone”.

“I think it is amazing [that Andy Burnham is aware],” she said. “It is really positive.

“I hope that makes them do something about it.

“There are people still waiting for justice who need answers and hopefully that gets the ball rolling. But then what is going to happen?”

She argued that victims and their families now remain “in limbo”.

"Our brother said something the other day - it feels like they are putting it off and waiting for the victims to pass away.

“No investigation should take this long with nobody in jail.”

Cleveland Police say victims remain at the heart of their investigation and detectives remain in communication with the CPS.

## 'But what now?'

Painter and decorator Paul, who was sent to “torture camp” Kirklevington in 1975 and 1976, kept the abuse he suffered behind bars a secret from his family for decades.

It was only around ten years ago that he finally opened up and they finally understood why the grandad-of-three had relied on alcohol throughout his life.

Paul, who lived in Longbenton, Newcastle, died on September 18 of liver failure.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21622310.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Natalie and Kirsti Overton from Newcastle with photo of their dad Paul *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Heartbreaking notes written by Paul before his death detailed some of the horrific abuse he was subjected to, including punches, kicks, and whacks with broom shanks and porridge stirrers.

Paul said he could not go to the toilet “on an officer's whim” - and for the rest of his life was left feeling anxious and always needed to be near a toilet.

His other daughter Natalie, a 40-year-old counsellor, said it was great Mr Burnham had read their story, but said this must turn into action.

She said: “He is agreeing with it, which is great, but then what?

“The officers [who abused boys] that are still alive need to face some sort of justice and get it put to bed, for everybody who is still waiting in limbo.

“For everybody who has never had answers.

“Maybe when they get justice, it will validate victims so they can move on. Our dad didn’t get to move on, he just carried it for his whole life.”

Mr Burnham’s comments on the scandal came after the Echo included compensation for victims in its six point manifesto for priorities we want to see delivered in the region.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21681037.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Prime Minister Andy Burnham *(Image: Alastair Grant / PA)*

The PM said: “The accounts of abuse at Kirklevington are deeply disturbing, and I know how long victims and their families have been waiting for answers.

“Paul Overton’s story is a powerful reminder of the lasting impact this has had on people’s lives. His family, and every person affected, deserve to have their voices heard.

“The Northern Echo has rightly kept a spotlight on issues that matter deeply to people across the North East, and I am committed to ensuring the concerns raised in its manifesto are taken seriously.”

## Police probe

Cleveland Police has been investigating abuse at Kirklevington since 2014 - but despite 21 suspects being interviewed, not one abuser has been convicted.

Seven former prison officers under investigation by police are now dead - with police now awaiting decisions on whether it can charge seven officers.

One of the dead officers was exposed by the Echo in May as the “barbaric” Douglas Harrison, who was due to be charged in 2019 but died days before he could be.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20906498.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HMP Kirklevington *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

Earlier this year, the Echo called for police to prioritise the probe as victims continue to fear they will never see an officer dragged before the courts to answer for their actions.

We also called for a settlement scheme, where victims can claim compensation and receive a formal apology, while avoiding complex legal proceedings.

Victims of the scandal say they continue to feel forgotten over the length of the probe, saying they feel that justice is slipping away as more and more officers die.

Detective Chief Inspector Chatterton, said: “I wholeheartedly understand the concerns, frustrations and questions around the length of time this investigation has taken.
