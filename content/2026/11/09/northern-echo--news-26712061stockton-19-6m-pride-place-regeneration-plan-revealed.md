A proposed small grants programme will launch in the coming months to start spending £19.6m of government money.

Stockton Council’s cabinet is to be told of a 10-year plan for the Pride in Place (PiP) programme for central Stockton and Portrack.

The area is receiving £19.6m over the next decade from the government under a national regeneration drive.

A council report says: “The plan will set out a number of next steps, including ongoing resident engagement, community workshops based around the key themes to better understand the way the PiP programme can have the biggest impact, and a proposal to launch a small grants programme late 2026/early 2027.”

Leading members of the Labour-run council are being asked to endorse a vision for the “hyper-local, community-led” programme and approve the submission of the 10-year plan to the Ministry of Housing, Commnunities and Local Government.

The council says it has made “significant progress” in the last seven months, setting up a neighbourhood board with an independent chair.

The boundaries of the neighbourhood have been changed to include the Tilery estate and North Shore Academy.

Drop-in sessions, community events and sessions, group discussions and a survey led to almost 1,000 interactions along with drawings, diagrams, photos and a mural, says the council’s report.

This found 69 per cent of respondents pinpointed fear of crime as affecting them going out or taking part in activities.

The vision for the programme aims to “create a thriving, safe and connected Stockton where young people can fulfil their potential, communities feel proud and secure, businesses and neighbourhoods prosper, and everyone can enjoy the cultural, recreational and green spaces that support a high quality of life”.

## What Stockton residents want to see change

People put forward suggestions including youth clubs, community hubs, mentoring, apprenticeships, training, job opportunities, protecting young people from exploitation and substance-related harm, visible policing, CCTV, better lighting, enforcement with practical support and rehabilitation for people affected by addiction and homelessness, supporting local businesses and start-ups, an “enhanced evening economy”, bringing empty properties back into use, events, sport, wellbeing activities, community learning, cleaner neighbourhoods and more use of parks and public spaces.

The report says the board is expected to sign off its plan and vision later this month, adding: “The vision and priorities are the key focus for the 10-year plan. The documents will reflect not only what our communities aim to tackle but also the way that this work will progress.

“It sets out how the programme will work with communities to develop projects and ideas and turn these into deliverable, sustainable projects with long term impacts, rather than specifying individual projects.”

It says the launch of a small grants programme in 2026-7 will focus on key areas and deliver some of the ideas put forward by residents, and early projects may shape longer-term major funding decisions.

The council proposes regeneration director Tracey Carter and deputy leader Councillor Paul Rowling to agree and submit the plan following its approval.

The report goes on: “Following submission of the plan the board will publicise its contents and ask the community to be part of delivering it.

Workshops based around each of the themes will take place to further understand the context of the topic, where PiP funding and influence can add the most value and to start to shape projects to be delivered.”

The Pride in Place programme will be discussed at Stockton Council’s cabinet meeting at 4.30pm on Monday, November 16 in the council chamber at Dunedin House, Thornaby.
