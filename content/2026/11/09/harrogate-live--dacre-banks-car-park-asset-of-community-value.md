The free Oak Lane car park behind Dacre Banks village hall has been listed as an asset of community value, giving villagers a chance to bid if it is ever sold.

The free car park behind Dacre Banks village hall has been listed as an **asset of
community value**. That gives the village a chance to bid for it if it is ever
put up for sale.

North Yorkshire Council’s Head of Localities approved the listing on Wednesday
4 November
(decision record).
Dacre Parish Council nominated the **Oak Lane car park** in September.

## Why the council agreed

The car park covers about **0.15 acres** in the centre of the village, close to
the Royal Oak. It is free and unmonitored, and steps lead from it to a side
entrance of the village hall. The council owns it.

The officer’s report says the car park does not host community activities itself. Its value is that people can reach the ones that happen around it (assessment report).

The nomination was backed by:

* the village hall management committee
* Holy Trinity Church
* the medical practice
* local businesses
* the ward councillor

The evidence sent to the council says the hall next door hosts luncheon clubs, exercise classes, meetings, cultural events and fundraisers. It says the parking matters most to elderly and less mobile people.

The church said the car park supports children’s activities, theatre productions
and a **fortnightly lunch club for older residents**. It said losing the car park
would significantly affect many of those activities.

The council’s own localities team called it “the only open access car park in the village”.

## Is the car park under threat?

Not according to the council. Its property team told the assessment that it has no plans for another use of the land and the site is not scheduled for disposal. The car park earns the council no income.

The report calls the decision a “re-designation”. The land charges search found an earlier listing of the same site as an asset of community value.

The planning service traced the car park to a permission for a surface car park, reference HGT76/13442/FUL. An earlier application to build housing on the land, HGT75/11746/FUL, was refused. The council holds no further detail on either.

## What it means for you

* **Nothing changes day to day.** The car park stays free and open.
* **If the council ever decides to sell it,** the listing triggers a protected period in which a community group can raise money and bid. The report notes the owner does not have to accept that bid, and can sell to anyone once the period ends.
* **The rules are changing.** The report says the English Devolution and Community Empowerment Act 2026 will alter how these listings work, once the Government publishes regulations.
