# DWP verdict on future of PIP as a non-means tested cash benefit

## The Department for Work and Pensions has said it is 'committed' to keeping Personal Independence Payment as a non-means tested cash benefit

The Department for Work and Pensions has confirmed it remains "committed" to maintaining Personal Independence Payment as a non-means tested cash benefit. The announcement comes as anticipation builds ahead of the final Timms Review report.

The Timms Review was established to examine potential reforms to PIP, aiming to make the disability and incapacity benefit - currently claimed by around 4 million people - more sustainable for both recipients and taxpayers, reports Birmingham Live.

Neil Duncan-Jordan has challenged the Labour cabinet in the Commons, questioning "whether cash will remain the default way in which PIP is provided to all claimants following the Timms Review, and that any non-cash-support will be accessed voluntarily."

"The Timms Review steering group is considering its recommendations ahead of the final report's publication in autumn," Sir Stephen Timms responded

The East Ham MP added: "The Secretary of State for Work and Pensions will consider the findings before deciding any next steps." Meanwhile, Conservative MP Joy Morrissey questioned the DWP minister: "If he will make an assessment of the potential merits of means testing PIP to ensure targeted support?".

Sir Stephen responded: "The purpose of the Timms Review is to ensure PIP is fair and fit for the future. As set out in the Terms of Reference for the Review, this government is committed to ensuring that PIP is a non-means tested cash benefit which is there for people in and out of work, now and into the future, because anyone can be impacted by a long-term condition or disability."

To ensure that those with first-hand experience remain central to the process, the DWP has pledged that the Timms Review "will be co-produced with disabled people, the organisations that represent them, carers, clinicians, experts, Members of Parliament and other stakeholders."

The review is being co-chaired by Timms, alongside Minister for Social Security and Disability Sharon Brennan and Dr Clenton Farquharson CBE. The co-chairs will oversee a steering group tasked with jointly leading the Review.
