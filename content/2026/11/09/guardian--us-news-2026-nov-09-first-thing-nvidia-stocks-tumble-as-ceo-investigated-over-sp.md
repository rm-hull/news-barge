Good morning. Nvidia’s chief executive, Jensen Huang, has been placed under “independent investigation” by the tech company after reports emerged he had been “the target of a foreign intelligence effort”, namely a close relationship ‌with a Chinese businesswoman who is accused of passing information to ‌Beijing. Nvidia stock is down 5% in pre-market trading as a result.

The company’s board said in a statement that while it had no reason to believe that either US national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, its inquiry would examine all available information. Republican lawmakers have expressed alarm, asking Huang to give the House foreign affairs and intelligence committees a closed-door briefing next week on Nvidia’s “security protocols against foreign adversary espionage”.

## **Cop31 climate talks open in Turkey in need of ‘tangible outcome’**

![A security officer stands watch as Murat Kurum speaks on screens during the Cop31 opening ceremony](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/5ea38d16f37dfc504dfab5069c81ac78e717fb44/207_0_4583_3667/master/4583.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

More than 90 heads of state and government, along with officials from close to 190 countries, are expected in Antalya this week for the latest round of UN climate talks. Against a backdrop of rapidly rising temperatures and extreme weather, with fractious geopolitics made worse by soaring inflation driven by the US-Israel war on Iran, the fortnight-long meeting will focus on “implementation” of the world’s targets to cut greenhouse gas emissions and help those stricken by the climate crisis.

Chris Bowen, Australia’s climate and energy minister and president of negotiations for Cop31, which his country is co-hosting with Turkey, told the Guardian: “We need a tangible outcome … that people can see is real. We need progress, it needs to be a step forward on previous Cops.”

## **Climate check: ‘A new age of climate extremes’ – Earth accumulating heat at accelerating rate**

![Earth photographed from the Orion spacecraft showing swirling clouds and oceans](https://i.guim.co.uk/img/media/18e858a8c436d0472b93759fc45a7277541e06b5/701_37_3924_3139/master/3924.jpg?width=445&dpr=1&s=none&crop=none)

Heat is accumulating in Earth’s systems at an accelerating rate, the UN’s weather agency has reported, with 2026 on track to be one of the four warmest years on record. The imbalance between incoming energy from the sun and energy leaving Earth reached its highest level since monitoring began, which will supercharge extreme weather events, such as heatwaves, droughts and tropical cyclones.
