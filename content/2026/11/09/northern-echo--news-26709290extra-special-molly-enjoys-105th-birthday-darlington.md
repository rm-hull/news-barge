A pioneering great-great grandma celebrated her 105th birthday at the weekend with family members praising her positivity and “bouncebackability”.

Molly Ingham is reputed to have been the first woman to learn to drive a car in Darlington, leading to her becoming a wartime driver for British officers, including the legendary General Montgomery.

She celebrated her 105th birthday at the town’s Elderwood care home on Saturday – just a few weeks after overcoming a Covid infection.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21684332.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molly's joy at seeing her cake, made by Emma Pearson, of Hurworth *(Image: Chris Barron)*

“I feel fine – you just have to get one with it, don’t you?” said Molly. “It doesn’t really feel any different to any other birthday. Mind you, I don’t know what I’m going to do with all these cards!”

Molly was surrounded by generations of family members, piles of gifts, and almost 100 cards as she celebrated the big day with a cake and glass of bubbly.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21684327.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molly with members of her family at her 105th birthday party *(Image: Chris Barron)*

“It’s having such a lovely family that keeps me going – that and clean living!” added Molly, who once turned down a young Tom Jones when he asked her to dance in Darlington’s La Bamba nightclub.

Molly also had to face the sadness this year of losing her middle daughter, Heather, who passed away in July.

Her presents included a framed photograph of Heather, and another of her baby great-great grandson, Archie, who was flown 11,500 miles from his home in New Zealand to meet her in September.

High up on Molly’s birthday present list were also her favourite bubble bath and red wine.

Despite reaching 105, she still enjoys doing daily exercises and games of bingo with the other residents at the care home.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21684326.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molly with great-granchildren Sireena, six, and Ismena, four *(Image: Chris Barron)*

Her granddaughter Aylsa Nugent - Heather's daughter - said: “To reach this age is beyond most people’s dreams and what makes it extra-special is what my Nan’s had to overcome this year, with Covid recently and losing my Mum.

“She just always bounces back with a positive attitude and a big smile. She’s extra-special.”

Molly, who has six grandchildren, nine great-grandchildren and one great-great grandchild, started life as Molly Marr.

She originally lived in Haughton-le-Skerne, on the outskirts of Darlington, before moving to the Drinkfield area of the town, where her family kept a smallholding.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21684339.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molly at 15 *(Image: Family handout)*![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/13183359.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Molly as a young woman*(Image: Family handout)*

She married Ralph Ingham and they lived in Hurworth Place, and then Croft-on-Tees, until Ralph passed away in 1985.

Molly’s daughter Barbara Auld said: “She’s amazing – she’s got wonderful bouncebackability.

“She makes friends so easily, but she also speaks her mind and stands up for herself. She’s had an incredible life and it’s so lovely for us all to celebrate her 105th birthday.”

Her other daughter, Diana Chapman, added: “She’s set the bar extremely high for us all and I think it’s having such a positive outlook that keeps her going. Whatever happens, she just keeps smiling.”

However, Molly did have one complaint about her birthday party: “You’d think Tom Jones might have made the effort to come, wouldn’t you?” she giggled.
