A major airport used by thousands of passengers in York and North Yorkshire is preparing to double its security screening capacity.

Leeds Bradford Airport plans to introduce two additional extra-long security lanes by the end of the year, building on the recent launch of its new Central Search Zone.

Three new lanes are already in operation as part of an ongoing redevelopment programme.

The upgrade means passengers will soon spend less time queuing and more time enjoying departures.

Vincent Hodder, chief executive officer at Leeds Bradford Airport, said: "The opening of our new Central Search Zone is a landmark moment for Leeds Bradford Airport and a major milestone in the delivery of our LBA:REGEN programme.

"This is a truly transformational investment that will significantly enhance the passenger experience.

"Not only are we increasing capacity, but we're introducing some of the most advanced security processing technology available at any UK regional airport.

"The new facility is outstanding in its field.

"By doubling our current security capacity, introducing dynamic queue management and increasing throughput from around 350 passengers per hour to up to 600 passengers per hour on a single lane, we're creating a more efficient, more comfortable journey for our customers.

"Most importantly, passengers will spend less time queuing and more time enjoying our terminal facilities before departure.

"The new environment is lighter, brighter, more intuitive and designed around the needs of today's travellers."

The full upgrade will ultimately double the airport’s security screening capacity and forms part of the LBA:REGEN redevelopment programme.

Mr Hodder said the improvements will enhance comfort and reduce wait times for travellers.

Each of the new lanes can process up to 600 passengers per hour, compared to the previous rate of about 350.

The Central Search Zone features dynamic virtual queue management, six eGates for boarding card scanning, and dedicated routes for Fast Track and Passengers Requiring Assistance (PRM).

It also houses Leidos next-generation screening equipment and Rohde & Schwarz body scanners.

Passengers will be able to leave electronics and liquids under 100ml inside their bags, making the security process quicker and more convenient.

This significant increase in throughput capability will help reduce queues, improve operational resilience and provide a more comfortable experience for travellers.

The changes are part of a broader redevelopment of Leeds Bradford Airport.

Passengers will also find new escalators leading directly to an expanded World Duty Free store, which now covers more than 1,000 square metres and includes new brands such as Charlotte Tilbury.

The airport said the investment supports its long-term goals to modernise infrastructure, boost operational efficiency, and deliver a passenger experience in line with regional growth.

Millions of passengers are expected to benefit from the improvements each year.
