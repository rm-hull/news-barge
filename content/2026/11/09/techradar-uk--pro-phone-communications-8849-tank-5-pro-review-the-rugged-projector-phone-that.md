## 8849 TANK 5 Pro: 30-second review

The Tank 5 Pro has but one purpose - it adds thermal imaging to the Tank 5 and changes almost nothing else from the prior design. 8849 admits as much on its own website - the only difference between the two phones, it states, is the thermal camera.

This design was already the most complete rugged projector phone on the market. It had a flagship chip, a gorgeous screen and a battery that recharges in about 90 minutes. The Pro keeps all those strengths and adds one more: thermal imaging.

You get a FLIR Lepton 3.1R sensor with a 95-degree field of view. 8849 claims that is a world first for a phone. Most thermal phones manage 50 or 57 degrees. A wider view means you see more of a room, a wall or a fuse board without having to move the device around.

The price of that view is resolution. The sensor delivers 160 x 120 native pixels. Software upscaling takes it to 480 x 360, and visible light edges are overlaid to help you read the image. It is useful, but it will not rival a dedicated high-end imager.

So who is it for? Electricians, builders, surveyors, solar installers and rescue teams spring to mind. Anyone who has ever wondered where the draught is coming from might also apply. If you will never point it at a hot pipe, save your money.

Would I consider this one of the best rugged smartphones? That depends on how much functionality you want in a single handset, but it deserves some recognition for its unique feature set.

## 8849 TANK 5 Pro: Design

* **Thick and heavy**
* **Vents for the projector on both sides**
* **Now with a thermal camera**

![8849 TANK 5 Pro](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/geMrhqX5mqpNewjyiDecX8-1200-80.jpg)

Hold the Tank 5 Pro, and it feels exactly like the Tank 5, which is good if you want something that feels built to double as a sledgehammer. The chassis blends brushed metal plates, rubberised corner armour and polycarbonate panels. It’s built for abuse, and if you drop it on your foot, you'll be the one giving, not the Tank 5 Pro.

The dimensions are identical to the Tank 5 at 177.1 x 87 x 33.8mm. So is the weight at 715g. That is surprising. A thermal module with its own shutter and optics has to go somewhere, and it must weigh something. Well, when I weighed it, the scales said 733g, so the thermal electronics don’t snub the laws of physics.

The thermal lens sits top right in the centrally positioned camera cluster, with the IR emitter below it. The visible light sensors are on the left. What’s great about this arrangement is that the primary sensor is physically close to the thermal camera, so when the two images are overlaid, there is almost no difference in the viewing angle between them, producing a good alignment when the data is merged.

![8849 TANK 5 Pro](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/Q899PLfxQQyubmdabuyGX8-1200-80.jpg)

The camping light bar, night vision lens, and four infrared LEDs remain in a V-shape in the middle of the back, which prevents this phone from offering wireless charging. Given the battery size, that wasn’t a practical option, so it's not a real loss.

The projector vents still split the left frame in two, and the right frame still has its second grille and fingerprint power key.

My verdict about this phone design remains unchanged, because the form factor is almost identical, and it's slightly heavier.

To be precise, this isn't a pocket phone, but one better suited to a belt holster or vehicle mount. Those travelling on foot wouldn’t take long to conclude this wasn’t the best phone to carry any distance.

One new note on the Tank 5 I made related to the thermal camera. 8849 recommends an operating range of 0°C to 35°C for the phone. That is fine for a summer survey, but not being able to handle below-zero temperatures might be an issue. It is less helpful for a winter loft inspection, which is exactly when thermal imaging earns its keep. However, if you want to warm up the Tank 5 Pro, running the projector for a couple of minutes will noticeably raise the device's temperature. And the thermal sensor has a range of -10°C to 400°C.

## 8849 TANK 5 Pro: Hardware

* **MediaTek Dimensity 9400e**
* **AMOLED display**
* **17600 mAh fast charge battery**

The Dimensity 9400e remains the star of this show, as it was on the Tank 5. It is a 4nm flagship chip, and it sits far above the mid-range silicon found in most rugged phones. The Immortalis G720 MC12 GPU keeps games and sustained workloads moving.

As I’ve already mentioned, this phone comes in three flavours, matching 16GB or 18GB of RAM with 512GB or 1TB of storage. My review hardware has 18GB of RAM and 512GB of storage. And I noticed that the only Tank 5 SKU now comes with this much memory.

The 6.73-inch AMOLED display is also unchanged. It runs at 3200 x 1440 with a 120Hz refresh rate and 3000 nits peak. My only complaint about this screen is that it needs a reflection-reducing coating or screen protector, but it can still be seen outdoors, thankfully.

Thermal work often means bright roofs and sunlit walls, and this screen copes most of the time. Protection is still Panda Glass rather than Gorilla Glass Victus, which remains a disappointment, if you like the latter.

The battery is 17,600mAh, built as two 8800mAh cells. That is how 120W charging works. A full charge takes about 90 minutes, and reverse charging at 25W turns the Pro into a field power bank.

There is a small caveat, and I only noticed it with this phone, not its predecessor. After charging at 120W for 90 minutes, the charger gets hot to the touch. I suspect this also happened with the Tank 5, but for whatever reason I didn’t notice.

In my experience, electronics that get hot eventually fail, so keep your receipt.

## 8849 TANK 5 Pro: Cameras

* **50MP main + 50MP telephoto + 50MP night vision + Thermal camera**
* **32MP on the front**
* **Five cameras in total**

![8849 TANK 5 Pro](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/aHT97D46E9poSoHEqxfiW8-1200-80.jpg)

The 8849 Tank 5 has four cameras:

**Rear camera:** 50MP Samsung ISOCELL S5KGN1SP, 50MP OmniVision OV50C40, 50MP OmniVision OV50D40, FLIR Lepton 3.1R Thermal camera**Front camera:** 32MP OV32B40

Here I’m going to focus exclusively on the Thermal sensor, because you can read my opinion of the other sensors in my Tank 5 review.

The Pro uses a FLIR Lepton 3.1R, which captures 160 x 120 native pixels, 19,200 calibrated temperature points. VividIR fuses frames in real time and upscales the image to 480 x 360. MSX overlays edges from the visible camera, making pipes, wires, and structures easier to read.

Those numbers sound modest, because they are. This is a compact sensor, not a professional handheld imager. Treat it as a very capable tool for finding problems, not for documenting them to survey grade.

Where it diverges from many phones that include a thermal sensor is the field of view, as 95 degrees is exceptionally wide. Typical thermal phones offer a 50 to 57-degree view, according to 8849, and that wider field is super-useful when you are working in a confined space. In a small room or a loft, that could be a big gain. You see more per frame, so you can scan faster and miss less, hopefully.

There is a trade-off, and it is simple maths. Spread 160 pixels across 95 degrees, and each pixel covers about 0.6 degrees. At 10 metres, one pixel spans roughly 10cm. That is my calculation from the stated field of view, not an 8849 figure. A small target at distance will be a handful of pixels, so that’s less of an issue.

I estimated that the nearest that this sensor will focus is about 15cm, which probably isn’t near enough to easily track problematic components on a circuit board unless they’re big.

Thermal sensitivity is under 50mK, or 0.05°C. That lets the Tank 5 Pro pick out subtle differences, such as damp patches or a warm pipe behind plaster.

There are two gain modes. High gain covers minus 10°C to 140°C, with finer detail for building work. Low gain stretches to 400°C for engines, machinery and flames.

Despite the resolution limitations of this sensor, what makes it exceptionally easy to use is the MyFLIR Pro app, which comes pre-loaded onto the phone.

As you might expect, it offers spot and area temperature readings, alarms, nine colour palettes, photo, video and time-lapse capture and picture-in-picture. Images are compatible with FLIR devices and FLIR Thermal Studio Suite for desktop analysis. That is a proper workflow, and it sets the Pro apart from cheaper thermal add-ons.

For those who need thermal accuracy, it’s a mixed bag. In high-gain mode, accuracy is ±5 °C or 5%, whereas in low-gain it's double that. To put that in simpler terms, low-gain isn’t suitable for soufflé baking if your oven isn’t reliable.

In terms of Emissivity, there are four choices: matte (recommended, semi-matte, semi-gloss and glossy (not recommended. These settings allow you to match the texture of the object you are scanning, avoiding wildly inaccurate readings.

In video capture, the rate is only 7.9 fps, but with these devices, less than 9 fps is normal.

The team behind MyFLIR has focused on making it easy to use, and it certainly ticks those boxes. It still won’t compete with a dedicated camera for flexibility and accuracy, but for jobs like plumbing and working on engines, it's more than adequate.

## 8849 Tank 5: Final verdict

The Tank 5 Pro is the most capable rugged projector phone yet made, and it puts the cherry on that cake by being able to see heat.

Without question, the thermal camera is the reason to buy, as it makes the Tank 5 Pro the complete package. It isn’t the highest-resolution thermal solution around, but the 95-degree view makes it quick to use, and the FLIR software makes it credible.

Nothing else has changed, including the drawbacks. The phone is very heavy. The projector still needs darkness. And an update policy that 8849 now says covers five years needs to be proven. But given how competitive the rugged phone market has become, launching phones on a two-year life cycle probably isn’t enough for more savvy purchasers.

The choice is straightforward. Need thermal imaging? Buy the Pro. Do not need it? Buy the Tank 5 and pocket the extra cash. Well, not pocket it, since the phone definitely won't fit then, if it does now.
