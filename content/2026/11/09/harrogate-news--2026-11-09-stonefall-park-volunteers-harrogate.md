**Volunteers who help look after two popular Harrogate green spaces are appealing for more local people to get involved after Stonefall Park received an Outstanding rating in the RHS It’s Your Neighbourhood awards for the second consecutive year.**

The Hookstone and Stonefall Action Group (HASAG) works to maintain and improve Stonefall Park and Hookstone Woods, bringing together local residents who want to protect the area’s green spaces for the wider community.

Although the group has achieved recognition for its work, it says more volunteers are needed to help maintain the sites and support future improvements.

## A community group established in 2015

HASAG was formally established in 2015 by local residents and two ward councillors. Its aim is to work alongside the local authority to enhance Stonefall Park and Hookstone Woods, both of which are regularly used by families, dog walkers and other members of the community.

The group works closely with North Yorkshire Council’s Parks and Countryside Services team, with Open Country also providing support on specific projects.

Regular volunteer sessions take place during the spring and summer, involving practical work such as pruning, cutting back tree branches and litter picking.

The group’s second consecutive Outstanding rating from the Royal Horticultural Society recognises the standard of care being maintained at Stonefall Park.

Fiona Quinn, speaking on behalf of HASAG, said the recognition meant a great deal to the volunteer-led group.

> **Hookstone and Stonefall Action Group**
>
> HASAG is volunteer led, so it means a great deal to achieve an Outstanding rating as it is testament to the hard work of our group that this special and much loved local green space is recognised by RHS as being maintained to a high standard!

## Improving the park and woodland

Like many public green spaces, Stonefall Park and Hookstone Woods face ongoing challenges, including littering, wear to paths and the need to manage vegetation.

HASAG also wants to protect wildlife habitats, encourage biodiversity and ensure the areas remain safe, accessible and enjoyable for visitors.

Over the past two years, the group has helped deliver a number of improvements.

* Repairs to the bridge in Hookstone Woods, where the sides had been crumbling and the willow had failed to thrive. Funding was secured through a local councillor’s locality budget, with ivy and grass planted to help stabilise the sides.
* New benches installed in both Hookstone Woods and Stonefall Park.
* Support from Open Country to clear ditches in Hookstone Woods and improve drainage.
* A new bug hotel installed in Stonefall Park to attract a variety of insect species.
* Fencing repaired and replaced in Stonefall Park.

The group also has plans to install an owl box and an additional notice board in Stonefall Park, alongside continuing its regular maintenance work.

## Volunteering does not need to be a big commitment

HASAG is keen to emphasise that people do not need gardening experience or specialist knowledge to become involved.

The group says that giving around an hour a month is enough to make a useful contribution, and welcomes people of different ages and abilities.

Children can also take part in activities such as litter picking. One committee member regularly brings her seven-year-old and five-year-old daughters along to help.

The group also participates in the annual RSPB Big Garden Birdwatch in January, which it encourages families and children to join.

People with knowledge of plants, birds or other wildlife are welcome, but this is not a requirement. There are also opportunities for volunteers who would prefer to help with administrative tasks such as preparing agendas and minutes.

> **Hookstone and Stonefall Action Group**
>
> We very much welcome everyone! A willingness to lend a hand for an hour or so once a month is really all that is needed.

## AGM offers opportunity to find out more

Anyone interested in finding out more about the group is invited to attend its annual general meeting on Thursday 26 November at 7pm at Woodlands Methodist Church Hall.

The meeting is open to everyone and will include a short talk by George Eglese entitled *Taking the Cure Again: Harrogate’s Spa Renaissance*.

The illustrated talk will explore Harrogate’s spa heritage and the emerging movement to reconnect its waters, landscapes, health, culture and public life. It will also introduce the newly established Harrogate Spa Society.

People interested in joining HASAG’s mailing list can contact the group at [HASAGgroup@outlook.com](mailto:HASAGgroup@outlook.com) or visit its website.

The group hopes to increase its membership and build a larger team of volunteers to help care for Stonefall Park and Hookstone Woods for future generations.
