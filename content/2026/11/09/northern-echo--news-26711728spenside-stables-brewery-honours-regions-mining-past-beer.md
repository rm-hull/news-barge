A brewery set up to keep a village's brewing tradition alive is growing while celebrating its mining past.

High Spen residents Mark Lynn and Mark Walker founded Spenside Stables Brewery in 2023 in two renovated pit-pony stables.

The buildings, in Rowland's Gill, Tyne and Wear, are believed to be the only original pit pony stables still standing in North East England, with the stables at Beamish Museum modelled after them.

Lynn and Walker wanted to keep brewing alive in the village after the owner of former local brewery The Old Potting Shed retired following the Covid pandemic.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689791.jpg?type=mds-article-620)High Spen residents Mark Lynn and Mark Walker founded Spenside Stables Brewery in 2023 in two renovated pit-pony stables. *(Image: Spenside Stables Brewery)*

Lynn, a keen brewer, took over the business and joined forces with micropub owner Walker, who brought sales and industry contacts.

Together, they renovated the stables and created Spenside Stables Brewery to supply cask ale across High Spen and the North East, while developing new flavours from established recipes.

The brewery produces IPAs, dark ales, blonde beers and porters, giving customers across the region a choice of heritage-inspired brews.

Its range includes Pit Pony, More Dogs Than Men, Victoria Seam, Top Marks, and fan favourite Pit Yakka, all named after elements of North East history.

Pit Pony is a pale ale named after the brewery's stables, while Victoria Seam is a dark ale named after a nearby coal seam and brewed with Challenger hops.

Pit Yakka takes its name from a term for residents of North East mining villages and is a chocolate-malt porter.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689594.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Co-founder Mark Lynn recently attended Lanchester Food Week's food festival, praising the event for introducing the brewery to more customers in County Durham. *(Image: Northern Echo)*

Spenside Stables' beers are sold at venues across the region, including Wig's Place in High Spen, Tyne Bar and Mean Eyed Cat in Newcastle, Station East in Gateshead and Tyne Amateur Rowing Club in Newburn.

Other outlets include The Lanehead in Ryton, The Office in Morpeth, The Pilot in Berwick and two pubs in Leicester.

Spenside Stables has proved popular since it began brewing in 2023, appearing at food and drink festivals across the North East.

Its beers have even reached international audiences, with customers from the USA importing cans of Pit Yakka and Pit Pony to enjoy.

Co-founder Mark Lynn recently attended Lanchester Food Week's food festival, praising the event for introducing the brewery to more customers in County Durham.

Mr Lynn added: "It's really great getting feedback off the locals, especially when they like my beer!"

From its historic base, the brewery is keeping the village's brewing tradition alive while building links with customers, pubs, restaurants and community groups across the region.
