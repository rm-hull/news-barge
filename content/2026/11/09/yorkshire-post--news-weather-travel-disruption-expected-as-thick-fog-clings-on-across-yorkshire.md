# Travel disruption expected as thick fog clings on across Yorkshire

## Thick fog has descended on large swathes of the UK and is set to cling on well into the morning, with commuters warned to prepare for delays.

A yellow weather warning for fog, which stretches from York to Bath and covers most of the South East and eastern England, is in place until 11am, and the Met Office has warned of difficult travel conditions on Monday morning.

Visibility could be down to 50 metres or less in some places, with delays to bus and train services possible and a chance of flights cancellations.

Niamh Murray, Met Office operational meteorologist, said current weather conditions are the “perfect recipe” for fog.

“With autumn and winter and getting fog during this time of year, we get much longer nights, and that allows temperatures to fall away,” she told the Press Association.

“We get a longer period of cooling and also the temperatures are much lower, so they’re much closer to the temperature that we need for the air to become saturated and therefore form fog.

“Another thing that we need to form fog is quite light winds, not absolutely no winds.

“We just have the perfect recipe at the moment: light winds, clear skies, long nights, to allow that fog to develop. And particularly if we see the negative numbers (temperature in degrees Celsius), it can quite stubborn to clear the next day.”

Motorists have been urged to take extra care in the low-lying cloud.

AA president Edmund King previously said: “Fog can transform a familiar road in a matter of minutes, so drivers need to be prepared to adapt their speed and leave more room between themselves and the vehicle ahead.

“One of the biggest mistakes is simply following the rear lights of the car in front. It can give you a false sense of security and means you may be travelling too close to react safely if that vehicle suddenly stops.

“Ultimately, there’s no prize for arriving a few minutes early. Give yourself extra time, slow down and drive to what you can actually see ahead.”
