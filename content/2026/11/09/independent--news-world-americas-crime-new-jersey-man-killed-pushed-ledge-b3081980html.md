A 61-year-old New Jersey woman is facing manslaughter charges after allegedly pushing a man off a porch ledge during a dispute over an alcoholic beverage, leading to his death weeks later, court records show.

Lisa D. Jones, of Wildwood, was charged on Thursday following the October 31 death of 70-year-old Edward F. Evans. Jones was initially arrested on October 7 and charged with aggravated assault and endangering an injured victim, according to Cape May County jail records.

According to a criminal complaint seen by *NJ.com*, the incident occurred on October 6 outside Jones’ residence on East Pine Avenue in Wildwood. Witnesses told investigators that Evans was sitting on the front porch ledge when he was pushed, falling six feet onto a concrete sidewalk.

Following the incident, Jones allegedly shouted, “You didn’t see anything, you didn’t see anything,” before running back into the house, as reported by *NJ.com*.

Evans was airlifted to AtlantiCare Regional Medical Center, City Campus, where medical staff determined he suffered a fractured skull and a brain bleed. He died from his injuries on October 31.

![Edward F. Evans, a 70-year-old commercial fisherman and local handyman in Cape May County, succumbed to a fractured skull and brain bleed weeks after the fall](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/09/20/27/NJ-man.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Court papers state that Jones initially told officers she was inside cooking when Evans fell. However, during a follow-up interview with investigators the following day, Jones allegedly admitted to pushing him off the ledge after he declined to get her a drink.

The nature of the relationship between Jones and Evans was not detailed in court filings, and no defense attorney was listed for Jones in public court records.

Evans, a Philadelphia native born to the late William F. Sr. and Birdie Smith Evans, worked as a commercial fisherman throughout Cape May County.

“Ed was an easygoing, fun-loving individual who was always available to help a friend or neighbor,” his obituary stated. “He was known throughout his neighborhood to be the local handyman.”

Evans was preceded in death by his parents and a brother, William F. Evans Jr. He is survived by his brother, Thomas F. Evans; his caretaker, Vallynne Couillard; and a community of fellow fishermen.

A Mass of Christian Burial is scheduled for November 19 at the Parish of Notre Dame de la Mer, Church of St. Anne in Wildwood.
