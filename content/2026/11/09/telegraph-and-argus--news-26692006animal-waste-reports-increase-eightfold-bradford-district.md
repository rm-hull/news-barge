Complaints about animal waste spillages across the Bradford district have risen sharply, leading to calls for tighter regulation of vehicles transporting animal by-products.

After receiving several complaints about offal and animal-waste spills, the Telegraph & Argus asked Bradford Council for the figures under the Freedom of Information Act,

The council’s response revealed 28 reports from September 2023 to September 2026. In that time, reports have gone up from two per year to 16 per year.

The true number could be even higher, but these are the incidents reported to and recorded by the council.

## What did the figures show?

* Sep 2023 - Sep 2024: **2 reports**
* Sep 2024 - Sep 2025: **10 reports**
* Sep 2025 - Sep 2026: **16 reports**

That is an eightfold increase in three years.

## Locations with the most reports

Keelham in Denholme emerged as a repeated hotspot in the figures.

Keelham, Denholme — six reports

* 26 Jun 2024
* 23 Jun 2025
* 4 Aug 2026
* 4 Aug 2026
* 5 Aug 2026
* 3 Sep 2026

'Denholme & Brighouse Road' also came up on 27 Dec 2025 so that's a total of seven incidents in the Denholme area.

Other locations also came up multiple times over the three-year period.

Buck Street, Bradford — three reports

* 22 Apr 2025
* 14 Jul 2025
* 16 Oct 2025

Cullingworth and the Thornton area each saw three reported incidents.

## This summer has seen a flurry of reports

Interestingly, summer 2026 saw a cluster of gruesome complaints.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21194423.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An animal carcass on a public road in the Hammerton Street area of Bradford *(Image: Rahan Khan)*

There were at least 10 reports involving carcasses, innards, animal waste and offal between July 27 and September 3, 2026.

## Companies were identified in minority of cases

Council officers investigated all but one of the reports, but most cases, the council could not identify the responsible vehicle or company.

Records often noted “no vehicle information” or said officers found “no spill/evidence seen”.

When companies were identified, causes included faulty vehicle fittings, damaged storage containers and incorrectly loaded vehicles.

A company admitted responsibility for a spill on Thornton Road, Thornton, in August 2025 and received a warning letter.

Another warning followed an offal spill on Denholme and Brighouse Road in October 2025.

Several other incidents resulted in repairs made to vehicles or advice given, but the council took no further action in 18 cases.

No one was prosecuted and no fixed penalty notices were issued over the incidents.

A July 2026 report of an offal spill on Haworth Road, Keighley, was still under investigation when the council responded.

A separate investigation into a September 3 spill on Haycliffe Lane was also ongoing but records noted that the company responsible had been identified and had cleaned up the waste.

In response to the increase in reports of offal spillages, Cllr Rebecca Poulsen, Worth Valley Ward Councillor, said: "As one of the ward councillors for Denholme, I received a high number of complaints about horrendous smells in the village which peaked in the hot summer weather.

"It was so bad that residents couldn’t open windows or sit outside and at times it could make you physically sick.

"I have been raising this with Environmental Health and was very annoyed that they did not respond to the complaints I raised and have taken four months to finally meet with myself and my ward colleague Cllr Goulding.

"We have met with them today and we have raised the issues of rendering factories as well as the wagons and the dreadful smells and spillages that occur.

![Conservative Councillor Rebecca Poulsen](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/17577451.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Conservative Councillor Rebecca Poulsen *(Image: T&A)*

"Often these wagons don’t have company names, and it is difficult to know where they have come from and are going to.

"We have discussed lobbying for more regulation over transporting offal including in sealed refrigerated vehicles to minimise spillages and smells.

"In the meantime, they will be providing us with details of how residents should report spillages so that evidence can be obtained and enforcement action taken. At present many residents may report incidents on social media so it is likely there will be a lot more incidents that have gone unrecorded.

"It is not fair to residents to have to deal with these appalling smells because wagons may be overfilled, damaged or be driven badly and action needs to be taken.”
