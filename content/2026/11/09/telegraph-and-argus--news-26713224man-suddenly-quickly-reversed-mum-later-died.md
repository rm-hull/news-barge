A 40-year-old man has gone on trial accused of causing the death of a Bradford mum by dangerous driving.

Donna Smith, 48, died following the incident outside her home in Queensbury last year.

A jury at Bradford Crown Court heard on Monday that Kiel Sanderson had already admitted a separate allegation of perverting the course of justice relating to him setting fire to his Seat Ibiza car shortly after the collision took place on Hillcrest Drive on August 29, 2025.

## Man 'suddenly and quickly' reversed the car

Prosecutor Paul Mitchell described how there had been a heated and violent argument that afternoon involving a number of people after Sanderson arrived in the Seat and parked it outside his girlfriend’s home on Hillcrest Drive.

It was alleged that during the disturbance a baseball bat was used to damage the windscreen and rear window of Sanderson’s vehicle after he drove the car a short distance up the road and stopped.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/19918280.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A number of floral tributes have been left on Hillcrest Drive, Queensbury, following the death of Donna Smith *(Image: Newsquest)*

Mr Mitchell alleged that Ms Smith had been standing behind the Seat when Sanderson “suddenly and quickly” reversed the car causing her extensive crush injuries before hitting a telegraph pole.

## Woman died in hospital days later

The court heard that Ms Smith went into cardiac arrest and although she was revived by ambulance staff she died in hospital a few days later.

Sanderson, who has denied the causing death by dangerous driving charge, drove his damaged vehicle away after the collision and later set fire to the Seat on a country lane before handing himself in to police.

## Jury told to focus on whether driving was dangerous

Mr Mitchell said during two police interviews Sanderson, of Calderstone Avenue, Low Moor, answered “no comment” to the questions.

He alleged that at the time of the collision Sanderson knew there were people all around his car and choosing to reverse at speed had been highly dangerous.

Mr Mitchell told the jury that before they could convict the defendant they would have to be sure that his driving was dangerous and that it caused the death of Donna Smith.

“There is unlikely to be much dispute about the second part.

"Your focus is whether or not the defendant’s driving was dangerous,” he said.

The trial continues and is expected to last about three days.

## Tributes paid to mum

After the incident, floral tributes were left on Hillcrest Drive.

One message said: “Rest in peace Donna.

“You will forever be loved and missed.

“Although I only knew you 11 months, you brought so much light and happiness into my life and helped me through the bad.”

Another tribute said: “You will always and forever be loved and missed.

“We all love you loads.

“You weren’t just my friend’s mum but also my friend.

“We will always love you my friend."

Another said: “Rest in peace. You will be missed.”
