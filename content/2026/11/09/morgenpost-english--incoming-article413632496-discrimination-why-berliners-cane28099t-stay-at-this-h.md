Booking a hotel room in your own city, whether for a short break from everyday life or to avoid a long journey home after an event, should not be a problem. With around 700 hotels offering more than 145,000 beds in the capital, everyone should be able to find something suitable. But the devil is often in the details.

That is also true of the Mercure Hotel Berlin Zentrum in Schöneberg. The three-star establishment does not accept guests with a residential address in Berlin. The hotel has apparently been enforcing this policy for several years.

According to RBB24, the restriction stems from a series of incidents involving certain groups of guests, including noise disturbances and property damage. The decision not to accommodate guests from Berlin is intended to protect staff, other guests and local residents. Fire safety is also a concern.

### The Mercure Hotel Makes Exceptions – But Only in Special Cases

The hotel is exercising its right to refuse guests. According to Germany’s Federal Anti-Discrimination Agency, this is entirely legal. Several hotels apply what is known as a place-of-residence clause. The main purpose is reportedly to prevent stag and hen parties.

From a legal perspective, the blanket exclusion of potential guests with a residential address in Berlin therefore does not constitute discrimination. The situation would be different if people were turned away because they came from a particular district or had a particular ethnic background.

However, the hotel apparently makes exceptions. Berlin residents can book a room if they can demonstrate a need for accommodation, for example, if repairs are being carried out in their home. It is also possible to book a room using a company address or on behalf of an acquaintance who does not live in Berlin.
