Households receiving certain benefits could be in line for extra cash as temperatures plunge, with Cold Weather Payments providing £25 for each qualifying spell of freezing weather.

The one-off payments are designed to help people on low incomes with heating costs when temperatures drop sharply.

But not everyone receiving benefits will qualify. The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) has specific eligibility rules, with six benefits potentially opening the door to the support.

Cold Weather Payments are made when the average temperature in an eligible area is recorded as, or forecast to be, zero degrees Celsius or below for seven consecutive days.

If the conditions are met, eligible households receive £25 automatically for each qualifying seven-day period between November 1 and March 31. That's more than 20 weeks, meaning some postcodes could get up to £500 this winter.

Here are the benefits that could qualify you for the payment.

## 1. Pension Credit

People receiving Pension Credit will usually qualify for Cold Weather Payments.

Pension Credit is a means-tested benefit for people who have reached State Pension age and have a low income.

It can also unlock other support, including help with housing costs and certain other benefits.

## 2. Income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA)

People receiving income-related Employment and Support Allowance (ESA) will usually qualify if they are in either the work-related activity group or the support group.

If you are not in either group, you may still be eligible if you receive one of the following:

* A severe or enhanced disability premium.
* A pensioner premium.
* A disabled child premium.
* Support because you have a child under five living with you.

## 3. Universal Credit

Some people claiming Universal Credit can also receive Cold Weather Payments, but there are extra conditions.

You or your partner must not be employed or gainfully self-employed.

You must also meet at least one of these conditions:

* You or your partner have a health condition or disability and have limited capability for work, with or without work-related activity.
* You have a child under five living with you.

However, there is an important exception for households with a disabled child.

If your Universal Credit claim includes a disabled child amount, you can qualify regardless of whether you or your partner are employed, self-employed or not working.

## 4. Support for Mortgage Interest (SMI)

People receiving Support for Mortgage Interest (SMI) may also qualify for Cold Weather Payments.

This is help towards mortgage interest payments for eligible homeowners receiving certain benefits.

To qualify for Cold Weather Payments through SMI, you must be treated as receiving a qualifying benefit and meet at least one of the following conditions:

* You receive a severe or enhanced disability premium.
* You receive a pensioner premium.
* You have a disabled child.
* You have a child under five living with you.

You may still be treated as receiving a qualifying benefit if you have applied for it but do not receive it because your income is too high.

## What about people in Scotland?

Cold Weather Payments are not available to people living in Scotland.

Instead, eligible households may receive an annual Winter Heating Payment, which provides support towards heating costs regardless of how cold the weather gets.

## How do you get a Cold Weather Payment?

You do not usually need to apply.

If you qualify and your area experiences a qualifying spell of freezing weather, the £25 payment should be made automatically.

The payment is made for each qualifying seven-day period between November 1 and March 31.

You can check whether your area has triggered a payment through the Government's Cold Weather Payment service.
