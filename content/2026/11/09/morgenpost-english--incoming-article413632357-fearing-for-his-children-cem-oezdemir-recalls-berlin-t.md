Followed, shouted at and harassed in a taxi with his own children: Baden-Württemberg’s Minister-President Cem Özdemir says he has experienced several frightening moments during his political career.

He has found himself in extremely unpleasant situations on trains and at railway stations, the Green politician said in Stuttgart. “The worst part isn’t worrying about yourself. The worst part is worrying about your children, your partner and also your neighbours.”

Özdemir described a taxi journey through Berlin with his children. When the driver, who was of Turkish origin, recognised him, he suddenly accelerated sharply before slamming on the brakes – repeatedly. Özdemir said the driver was trying to force him and his children out of the taxi through his aggressive driving. His daughter comforted his frightened son. “That is simply unbearable,” Özdemir said.

### Özdemir: “I Thought the Police Would Arrive Any Moment”

Özdemir also described a threatening encounter at Leipzig Central Station. He was travelling with a female intern when supporters of the Legida movement followed them after a demonstration. Legida was a far-right, Islamophobic and xenophobic offshoot of the Pegida movement. “I thought the police would arrive any moment. But nobody came.”

The pursuers eventually caught up with him, insulted him, shouted in his ear and held a mobile phone right in his face. He told the intern not to worry and to remain calm. “I had no idea what I would actually do if he punched me in the face.”

Two police officers eventually escorted Özdemir and his companion to the platform.

### No Longer Able to Shop Alone

A turning point for Özdemir was the Bundestag’s Armenia resolution in 2016. In it, the German parliament recognised the massacres and deportations of Armenians and other Christian minorities in the Ottoman Empire in 1915–16 as genocide.

As the Green Party’s leader at the time, Özdemir was one of the main initiators of the resolution. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan personally attacked Özdemir, who is of Turkish descent. He also received death threats and was placed under police protection.

Özdemir said he had been aware that the resolution would drastically change his everyday life. He could no longer simply go shopping or go out as he pleased. He made sure not to leave the house alone, for example taking a neighbour with him when he went shopping.

### Threats Reach the Kindergarten

According to Özdemir, the threats also affected his children’s everyday lives. Other parents at the kindergarten asked whether their children were safe if “the Özdemir children” were also being looked after there.

“You feel guilty when other parents go there to pick up their children,” the Minister-President said. “These are questions you wouldn’t wish on anyone.”

Özdemir recounted these experiences against the backdrop of the debate surrounding Berlin Left Party politician Elif Eralp‘s fears for her family‘s safety. Eralp hopes to become her party’s first Governing Mayor of Berlin.

A report by *Der Spiegel* caused controversy after reporting that Eralp feared for her family’s safety if she spoke explicitly about a well-known pro-Palestinian activist.
