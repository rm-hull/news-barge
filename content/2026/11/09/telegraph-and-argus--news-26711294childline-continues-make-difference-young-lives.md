OCTOBER 30 marked 40 years since Childline opened its telephone lines and received 50,000 attempted calls from young people seeking help and support in just one night.

Since the launch of Childline, the service has delivered over six million counselling sessions to children across the United Kingdom, on a range of topics, including abuse, online harms and bullying.

In a new survey of teenagers in England commissioned by the NSPCC, shows that one in three (34per cent) said that what the future would bring was a top concern for them.

The survey of 1,492 teenagers in England, conducted by Savanta, also found that more than one in five young people (22per cent) are most troubled by the state of the world and one in four (26per cent) said mental health was a key concern. Only 14per cent cited social media and being online as a major worry.

In a parallel poll, 1,504 adults in England - who were teenagers when Childline launched in 1986 - said social media and being online (65per cent), body image and appearance (48per cent) and mental health issues (46per cent) were the main reasons why it was harder being a teenager now than when they were growing up.

One 15-year-old girl told Childline counsellors: “I spend a lot of time worrying about what’s going to happen in the future. Sometimes it feels like everything in the world is changing so quickly and I don’t know where I fit into it all.”

And one 16-year-old boy said: “Everyone tells us these are supposed to be the best years of our lives, but I feel under pressure all the time.

"I’m worried about exams, money and what comes next after school.”

During Childline’s early years, physical and sexual abuse were the most common reasons young people contacted the service for help. Today, and for the past decade, mental health has consistently been the number one concern.

It’s not just the worries that children have which have changed over the decades.

The way children access support has also changed significantly over the last 40 years, from phone calls and letters to online support. In Childline’s first decade, more than half of contacts came via telephone boxes, with the remainder made through landline phones.

Today, almost three quarters of Childline contacts take place online, with trained counsellors providing support through one-to-one chats alongside the service’s telephone helpline.

Young people are growing up in a world that often feels fast paced and constantly changing.

The survey findings reveal how many are grappling with concerns about the future, the world around them and their mental health, which reinforces why Childline remains as important as ever.

Childline has evolved alongside young people, expanding from a telephone-only service to providing online support and one-to-one counselling chats.

However, one thing has remained constant over the last 40 years: children still need someone who will listen to them, without judgement, and help them find a way forward.

A 14-year-old boy said: “ Talking to Childline helped me realise I didn’t have to deal with everything on my own. Just having someone listen made a huge difference.”

Forty years after Childline was founded by the remarkable Dame Esther Rantzen, our counsellors around the United Kingdom continue to provide a safe, confidential space where children and young people can talk openly about anything that is troubling them.

Whatever their concern, Childline will be there to listen and help them find a way forward.

Every generation of children faces challenges, but those challenges can look very different over time.

Childline was founded to ensure children always had someone to turn to when they felt frightened, worried or alone. Forty years later, that need remains just as important.

\* Children with any worries or concerns can speak to a trained volunteer counsellor by calling 0800 1111, 24 hours a day, 365 days a year.

Or they can chat online through the 1-2-1 chat service on the Childline website - www.childline.org.uk
