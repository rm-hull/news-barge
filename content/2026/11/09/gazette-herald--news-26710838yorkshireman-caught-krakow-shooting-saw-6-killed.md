A Yorkshireman has said he hid in a gift shop after a gunman opened fire in central Krakow killing six people.

Neil Thaxton, of Knottingley, said he and his wife were on holiday in the Polish city when the attack happened on Saturday afternoon (November 7).

Polish police on Monday said the gunman, who is also suspected of killing three members of his own family, died in hospital after the alleged terror attack in Krakow’s Main Square.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21687892.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Police officers stand near the market square in Krakow, Poland, Sunday (November 8) where several people were shot the day before *(Image: AP Photo/Beata Zawrzel)*

A British man was among six people shot dead and five foreign nationals from the UK, Portugal, Russia, Germany and Austria were among the 16 wounded.

Mr Thaxton said he and his wife had “just walked through the main square and heard the gunshots”.

“We thought it was fireworks then people started running,” he wrote on social media.

“We went into a gift shop and the staff took us in the back of the shop and locked the doors and turned the lights off.

“It was quite scary for 5–10 minutes, fortunately we’re alright, our thoughts are with the ones who’ve lost their lives and their loved ones.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21687928.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A woman lights a candle near the main square in Krakow, Poland, on Sunday (November 8), where several people were shot the day before *(Image: AP Photo/Beata Zawrzel)*

Malopolska Policja on Monday said the “perpetrator of the tragic events died in hospital early this morning”.

Authorities found three dead bodies at the 24-year-old suspected gunman’s family home in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, a city about 93 miles north-east of Krakow.

Police on Sunday said they were the bodies of his sister, mother and father, and they are believed to have been shot.

Footage on social media shows an armed police officer approaching the gunman, who appears to be wearing tactical gear and lying on the ground with a rifle beside him.

Sirens can be heard as the officer appears to fire six shots towards him before dropping to his knees.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21687956.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A woman lights a candle to commemorate the victims two days after a shooting in Krakow, Poland, on Monday (November 9) *(Image: AP Photo/Beata Zawrzel)*

The suspected gunman, a Polish national, was believed to have been inspired by “far-right ideologies” and was admitted to hospital after being shot by police.

Local police have launched a blood appeal for officer Jakub Kulik, who is fighting for his life in hospital.

The force said the senior constable had confronted the armed attacker and suffered serious injuries.

Polish interior minister Marcin Kierwinski said the attacker held “very radical views” and “was fascinated by far-right ideologies”.

Polish defence minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz called Saturday’s fatal shooting an “act of terror” on the people of Krakow.

The Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office is advising British nationals in Krakow to “follow the advice of local authorities and monitor local media”.

Foreign secretary Ed Miliband said: “The UK government deeply mourns the British national and other victims who have died after the appalling attack in Krakow.

“All of my deepest condolences go to the families of the victims.

“The UK stands resolutely in solidarity with the people of Poland.”

Krakow mayor Lukasz Gibala said the city would observe a period of mourning until Wednesday.
