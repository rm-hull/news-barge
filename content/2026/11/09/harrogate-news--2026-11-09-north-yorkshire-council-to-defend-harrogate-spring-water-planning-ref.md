North Yorkshire Council has confirmed it will defend its decision to refuse Harrogate Spring Water’s proposed factory expansion at Harlow Moor Road, setting up a contested planning appeal over the future of the site adjoining Rotary Wood and Harrogate’s Pinewoods.

The confirmation is a significant development in the long-running planning dispute. In April 2026, councillors rejected the company’s detailed proposals despite council planning officers having recommended that the application should be approved.

Harrogate Spring Water, which is part of the Danone group, has appealed against the refusal. The company maintains that its proposals have been developed carefully over a number of years, taking account of community feedback and environmental considerations.

Asked by the Harrogate Informer whether the council would defend the planning refusal, a spokesperson for North Yorkshire Council confirmed by email on 9 November that it would do so.

The council’s decision means it intends to put forward its case in support of the planning committee’s refusal rather than allowing the company’s appeal to go uncontested.

## Why councillors rejected the expansion plans

The application was considered by North Yorkshire Council’s Harrogate and Knaresborough Area Planning Committee in April 2026.

Planning officers had recommended approval, but councillors took a different view and voted unanimously to refuse the application.

The committee identified concerns about the proposed landscaping and the appearance of the proposed extension.

On landscaping, councillors considered that the proposals would not adequately compensate for the loss of existing planting and habitats. Their concerns included the amount, location and characteristics of the replacement landscaping and whether it would sufficiently mitigate the impact of the development.

The committee considered the proposals to be contrary to a number of North Yorkshire Local Plan policies relating to biodiversity, trees, woodland and the natural environment.

The second reason for refusal concerned the design and appearance of the proposed building. Councillors considered that it lacked local distinctiveness and would be unacceptable in its setting, which includes a Special Landscape Area and Local Green Space.

The committee concluded that the proposal would not adequately respect the character and appearance of its surroundings.

The decision was notable because it went against the recommendation of the council’s planning officers. The elected members were not persuaded that the proposed design and landscaping addressed the concerns sufficiently to justify approval.

## The distinction between the 2017 permission and the latest application

The planning history is important in understanding the current dispute.

Outline planning permission for the development was granted in 2017. The application considered by councillors in April 2026 was a reserved matters application, dealing with the detailed elements of the scheme.

These matters included the appearance, landscaping, layout and scale of the proposed development.

This means the latest decision was not a completely fresh consideration of whether development could take place at the site. The principle of development had already been established through the earlier outline permission.

The dispute in the current appeal centres on whether the detailed proposals were acceptable, particularly in relation to the building’s appearance and the proposed landscaping.

That distinction will be important when the appeal is considered. The council will need to defend the specific reasons given by the planning committee for refusing the reserved matters application.

## Harrogate Spring Water defends its environmental proposals

Responding to the council’s decision to defend the refusal, Harrogate Spring Water has reiterated its position that the expansion proposals have been developed carefully, with consideration given to the local community and the environment.

Richard Hall, managing director of Harrogate Spring Water, provided the following statement to the Harrogate Informer:

> **Richard Hall, managing director of Harrogate Spring Water, said:**
>
> We understand the importance of Rotary Wood to the local community. So we have developed our plans very carefully over a number of years, taking into account community feedback and environmental considerations, including a new two-acre publicly accessible woodland, six trees planted for every one lost and a 10% net gain in biodiversity.
>
> As a proud Harrogate business, we want to grow sustainably, supporting local jobs and the wider economy, while continuing to listen to the community. We believe our appeal has a strong basis and look forward to the opportunity to have our plans properly considered.

![Richard Hall MD of Harrogate Spring Water](https://harrogate-news.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Richard-Hall-HSWL.jpg)

The company says its proposals include a new two-acre publicly accessible woodland, alongside a tree planting programme intended to provide six replacement trees for every tree lost through the development.

It also points to a proposed 10% net gain in biodiversity as part of its environmental case.

These measures form an important part of the company’s argument that the environmental impact of the development has been addressed through mitigation and additional planting.

However, the planning committee’s concerns were specifically about whether the proposed landscaping was sufficient in its amount, location and characteristics to compensate for the loss of existing planting and habitats.

The appeal will therefore bring those competing assessments into focus: the company’s position that its environmental measures provide appropriate mitigation, and the committee’s conclusion that the detailed proposals did not adequately address the impact on the site and its surroundings.

## The company’s case for expansion

Harrogate Spring Water has also argued that the expansion is important to the future of its business and would bring economic benefits to Harrogate.

The company has presented the development as an opportunity to increase its operational capacity, support the long-term future of its Harrogate facility and create additional employment.

Its managing director has emphasised the company’s intention to grow sustainably while continuing to support local jobs and the wider economy.

The economic case is one of the considerations surrounding the application, alongside the environmental and design issues that led councillors to refuse the detailed proposals.

The planning appeal will require the arguments to be considered against the relevant planning policies and the reasons for the original refusal.

## Company asks for a six-day public inquiry

Harrogate Spring Water has appealed against the council’s refusal and has requested that the appeal be dealt with through a public inquiry lasting six days.

A public inquiry is a more formal appeal process than written representations. It can involve the presentation of detailed planning evidence, expert witnesses and the examination of the arguments put forward by the parties.

The company’s request does not in itself guarantee that a six-day inquiry will take place. The Planning Inspectorate will determine the appropriate procedure for considering the appeal and set out the next steps.

North Yorkshire Council’s confirmation that it will defend the refusal means the authority will put forward its case in support of the committee’s decision.

The company will have the opportunity to challenge the council’s reasons for refusal and argue that its proposals should be allowed to proceed.

## What the council’s decision means for the appeal

The council’s confirmation is significant because there had been uncertainty over whether it would contest the appeal, particularly given that its own planning officers had recommended approval of the detailed application.

By deciding to defend the refusal, the authority is committing to supporting the decision taken by its elected planning committee.

This does not mean the council is guaranteed to win the appeal. The final decision will be made by the appointed planning inspector after considering the evidence and the arguments from the company and the council.

It does, however, mean that the reasons given by councillors for rejecting the scheme will be formally defended by the local planning authority.

For those concerned about the impact of the development on Rotary Wood and the surrounding Pinewoods, the council’s decision removes the possibility that the refusal would be left effectively uncontested by the planning authority.

## What happens next?

The next stage is for the Planning Inspectorate to progress the appeal and determine how it will be examined.

The company’s request for a public inquiry will need to be considered as part of that process. The timetable and arrangements for representations and evidence will depend on the procedure selected.

Harrogate Spring Water will seek to demonstrate that its detailed proposals are acceptable and that the council’s reasons for refusal do not justify rejecting the application.

North Yorkshire Council will need to defend the committee’s conclusions on landscaping and design, explaining why it considers the refusal to be justified in planning terms.

The outcome will determine whether the detailed expansion proposals can proceed in their current form or whether the council’s refusal will stand.
