Two Bradford men have been jailed for more than 50 years combined for a brutal shooting that left a man injured in a street attack.

Nathaniel Collins and Mohammed Nageen, both 20 and from Bradford, were sentenced at Leeds Crown Court on Monday after being found guilty of attempted murder following the shooting of a 32-year-old man on Smiths Avenue in the Marsh area of Huddersfield.

Collins, of St Paul’s Road, Manningham, was handed a 27-year sentence, while Nageen, of Ringwood Road, Canterbury, Little Horton, received 25 years behind bars.

The shooting took place shortly before 8pm on December 1, 2025, when the victim was approached by two men who opened fire before fleeing the scene in a car.

He sustained gunshot wounds to his arm and leg and was taken to hospital for treatment.

Detective Sergeant Ade Belle, of West Yorkshire Police’s Homicide and Major Enquiry Team (HMET), said: "Nathaniel Collins and Mohammed Nageen clearly used a very significant level of violence on that night on December 1 and we firmly believe they fully intended to kill their victim.

"It is extremely fortunate he did not suffer more severe injuries and this incident did not prove to be a murder.

"Both men now face substantial prison sentences for their actions and our streets are safer with them behind bars."

A police investigation led officers to identify Collins and Nageen as the suspects.

Collins was arrested on December 12 and later charged, while Nageen was arrested and charged in January.

Although the firearms used in the attack were not recovered, investigators confirmed that the victim’s injuries were consistent with bullets fired from handguns.

DS Belle said: "Firearms offending is treated incredible seriously by West Yorkshire Police, and we can assure residents that all necessary specialist resources are used to identify and arrest those responsible.

"Specialist detectives also continue to investigate organised criminal activity involving firearms across West Yorkshire.

"Anyone who has information about organised crime or persons possessing these weapons in their communities is urged to contact West Yorkshire Police or the independent Crimestoppers charity.

"Information can be reported to Crimestoppers anonymously via 0800 555 111."
