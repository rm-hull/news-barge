Rep. Thomas Massie has moved to force a vote on the impeachment of Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, setting the stage for a high-stakes congressional showdown over a top figure in the Trump administration.

Massie, a Kentucky Republican, introduced articles of impeachment on Monday, setting off a two-day clock for an expected House vote on charges that include waging war against Iran without congressional approval and other abuses of power.

According to Massie, the secretary's conduct constitutes "high crimes and misdemeanors" and his record leading the Pentagon "thus warrants impeachment."

A floor vote in the House could take place by mid-week, placing significant strain on Republicans — many of whom attributed their election losses directly to the conflict in Iran — though the final trajectory of the measure remains uncertain.

![Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth is bearing the brunt of anger after the Iran war and its economic impact contributed to severe Republican losses in the midterm elections](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/19/47/GettyImages-2300179710.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

House Speaker Mike Johnson sought to block the measure, cautioning lawmakers against attempting to impeach a Defense secretary while American forces are actively deployed in military operations across the Middle East.

"Impeaching a Cabinet secretary right now, especially the Department of War in the middle of hostilities abroad would be a terrible, terrible look, a terrible thing for our allies and our enemies to see that," Johnson, a Republican, told lawmakers as the session commenced.

"We don’t impeach Cabinet secretaries for political gain or for cheap tricks, and I think that’s what this is and I think everybody knows it."

A majority of House Democrats are expected to support the effort to impeach Hegseth, following statements from Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries over the weekend declaring that the Defense secretary should step down.

Should the House pass the impeachment resolution, the matter would proceed to the Senate for a trial. Republicans currently hold majorities in both chambers until the incoming Congress convenes in January under Democratic control.

## Hegseth ‘not worried about the noise’

Earlier Monday, Hegseth said he is eager to publicly defend the foreign military campaigns conducted under President Donald Trump against Iran, Venezuelan leadership, and Iranian-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen.

"If you want to take issue with what we did in Epic Fury or Rough Rider or nabbing Nicolás Maduro, you’re taking issue with the troops and what they accomplished, which is historic," Hegseth told reporters Monday, referencing the operational code names for the Iran conflict and maritime strikes targeting Houthis.

The Pentagon chief also revealed plans to hold a press briefing on Tuesday regarding the impeachment resolution. The rare appearance will mark his first news conference in more than 180 days, having held no formal media briefings on U.S. military strikes or the Iran war since May.

![Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY) holds a press conference about his resolution to impeach Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/20/2026-11-09T203325Z_1742625666_RC2KKOA2EGXS_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-HEGSETH-IMPEACHMENT.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Calls for Hegseth's removal have intensified as he faces primary criticism over the Iran conflict and its subsequent economic fallout, factors widely cited in the midterm losses that surrendered Republican control of Congress.

Speaking after an address at the Naval War College in Rhode Island, Hegseth dismissed the legislative push for his removal.

"There’s a lot of politically motivated people who may have substantial policy disagreements, but their disagreements are with success," he stated, adding that "if we want to litigate" the administration's military decisions, "I’m happy to do it all day long."

"My team, we’re not worried about the noise — it’s what I told the troops, too — ‘don’t worry about the noise,’" Hegseth said.

When pressed on comments from Vice President JD Vance pointing to the Iran conflict as a key factor in the GOP's electoral defeat, Hegseth noted that Trump expressed a willingness to repeat the action, adding, "I agree."

He aligned his stance with remarks made a day prior by Secretary of State Marco Rubio: "It doesn’t have to be necessarily popular, but it can be necessary."

Hegseth argued the action was imperative to address Iranian nuclear ambitions, stating "someone had to confront the Iranian regime’s pursuit of a nuclear weapon" and that "the choice was don’t act and wish it away or take a bold move."

Tehran has consistently asserted its nuclear development is strictly for peaceful purposes. However, data from the International Atomic Energy Agency shows Iran holds 440.9 kilograms (972 pounds) of uranium enriched to 60% purity — approaching the 90% threshold required for weapons capability.

## Trump defends Hegseth

Trump voiced support for Hegseth during comments to reporters late Sunday night.

"He’s guilty of two things, basically winning two wars," Trump said. "I believe we saved the Middle East and Israel combined. And it’s awfully hard to say let’s impeach somebody who did that."

![Trump defended Hegseth in remarks to reporters on Sunday night](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/05/16/53/GettyImages-2301357323.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Hegseth insisted that "Iran is devastated," despite continued Iranian strikes on U.S.-utilized bases in the region, which have caused billions in damages, and ongoing disruptions to commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz that have driven up energy costs for consumers.

He indicated that further decisions regarding the conflict remain with Trump, suggesting a message to Tehran: "Hey, we’re done, and we’re going to watch your nuclear material. You better not move it."

The Defense secretary's defense comes as findings from The AP Voter Poll, which surveyed over 100,000 voters, revealed that roughly 6 in 10 respondents felt the U.S. made the "wrong decision" by launching military strikes against Iran.

Amid mounting congressional pressure, Hegseth launched a social media campaign, sharing a video last week acknowledging the impeachment effort as "all about the politics of personal destruction" while promising it "won’t work with me. You can rest assured of that."

Following his post, numerous Pentagon political appointees published images of Hegseth carrying the slogan, "Stand. Fight. Defend the Republic." The White House subsequently shared a video featuring promotional comments from Trump.
