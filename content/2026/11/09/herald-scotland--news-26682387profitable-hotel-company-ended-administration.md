A major hotel company was in profit when it was placed into administration.

The family-run Scottish company operating eight hotels was placed into administration following an HMRC tax bill dispute, I revealed in an exclusive Herald story last month.

The business is operating as normal as the South Lanarkshire-based hotel group’s portfolio is marketed for sale.

This comes amid a “substantial volume” of inquiries, and tax debt is expected to be covered, as events leading to the administration were revealed for the first time.

I wrote that Strathmore Hotels Limited, headquartered in East Kilbride, has sites including the Ben Wyvis Hotel in Strathpeffer, Royal Hotel, Oban, and the Salutation Hotel in Perth, said to be one Scotland's oldest hotels, and is a large-scale operation with over 800 rooms.

Administrators outlined events leading up to the move last month, while a spokesperson for His Majesty’s Revenue and Customs said it only takes court action when all options have been exhausted.

It was also revealed by the administrators Interpath, appointed in August, stated: “The company is a family-run business, which was incorporated in 1990.

“It operates a portfolio of eight three-star hotels, six of which are owned on a freehold basis, one is occupied on a long lease, and one is occupied on a virtual freehold basis (a very long lease).”

Around 400 staff working in the hotels have been kept on with three of the seven head office made redundant. Another creditor, Handelsbanken, which was owed £11.1 million, moved towards administration.

![paper](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21685543.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Newsquest)*

The administrators continued: “The company had been involved in a long running dispute with HMRC, who had claimed that there were significant VAT payments overdue, and, to a lesser extent, other unpaid tax liabilities.

“The claim includes a combination of VAT and other tax liability assessments made, late payment charges and interest on overdue sums.

“On July 22, HMRC presented a petition for the company's winding-up in relation to outstanding balances totalling £2.8m."

Interpath added: "Upon learning of presentation of the petition, which sought appointment of an interim liquidator, Handelsbanken appointed Alistair McAlinden and James Dewar as administrators, under the terms of its floating charge.

“This course of action was motivated by a desire to have a controlled insolvency process, protect asset value and to enable the insolvent estate to benefit from the moratorium protection and comparative flexibility offered by an administration appointment in comparison to a liquidation process.”

Draft accounts for 2024 report turnover of £20m and a profit after tax of £800,000. Management accounts for 2025 indicate the company achieved an operating profit of £5.6m from a total turnover of £22m.

It can only be hoped the hotels can continue to serve guests around the UK.

New accounts for the year ended December 31, 2025 showed pre-tax profit was £8.7m, compared with a £5.7m loss in 2024, while the bottom line moved from a £4.6m loss to a £5.9m profit.

The directors said in their annual report: “The UK economic environment provided general challenges in the year with low demand for pallets seen across our primary operating sectors.

“Despite this reduced turnover compared to the prior year, key initiatives around improved pricing and direct cost reductions helped deliver a notably improved gross profit margin."

The directors said: "The main factors influencing the economic conditions is the inflationary and cost of living pressures that continued into 2025.

“Costs have remained high due to global political uncertainty. This has had an impact on commodity costs throughout Europe. The sustained cost of living pressures could impact the domestic demand for timber products.”
