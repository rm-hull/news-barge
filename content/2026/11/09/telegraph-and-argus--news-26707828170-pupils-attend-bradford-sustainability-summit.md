MORE than 170 pupils have been inspired to consider careers in the green sector.

They attended the first-ever Bradford Pupil Voice Sustainability Summit, held at Bradford City Football Club and hosted by Bradford Council in partnership with Bradford Diocesan Academies Trust (BDAT) and Bradford College.

The event connected students with green employers and organisations, including Yorkshire Water, the NHS, and the Yorkshire Wildlife Trust.

Matthew Hill, deputy chief financial officer and sustainability lead at BDAT, said: "It was great to see pupils, employers and educators coming together to talk about sustainability and the careers it can lead to.

"We are incredibly proud to see our pupils developing an interest and passion for these topics.

"The Sustainability Summit gave them the chance to learn more about the sustainability sector and how this interest could one day become a career."

Pupils heard from schools that have started sustainability projects, such as installing solar panels and forming eco committees.

They also learned about Bradford City FC’s Greener Bantams initiative, which focuses on reducing waste and lowering the club’s carbon footprint.

Workshops on green careers and climate action were held, and students contributed to a 'Pledge Tree' by writing their sustainability promises on leaves.
