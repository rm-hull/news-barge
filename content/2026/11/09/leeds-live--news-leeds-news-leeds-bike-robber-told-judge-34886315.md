# Leeds bike robber told judge 'alright mate' - then got six-and-a-half years

## A man considered jumping into a river to escape Alfie Smith

A Leeds robber who lay in wait for cyclists has been jailed - after calling a judge "mate" in court.

Alfie Smith, 20, was told off by Recorder Simon Jackson KC for his lack of courtesy when he appeared at Leeds Crown Court on Monday, November 9 to be sentenced for two burglaries, two robberies and an attempted robbery.

The court clerk asked him: "Are you Alfie Smith?" To which he replied: "Yeah mate." The judge told him: "She’s not your mate, speak to her with some courtesy please."

He then replied: "Alright mate."

Prosecutor Syed Khukon told the court the burglaries took place on February 3 and March 17 this year. It was said Smith, of no fixed abode, was joined in the basement of a building in the city centre by another male and he kicked through a door in a storage area.

He did that again at another room, but was unable to enter. Nothing was stolen but damage caused cost around £5,000.

Smith’s offending then escalated to robbery. One victim told police he considered jumping into the river with his bike to avoid being hurt, before handing it over.

Mr Khukon said: "On the tenth of August 2026 at around 11.30am, the victim was riding an electric bike from Castleford to Leeds and saw three men on e-bikes. The defendant was on one of them and was heard shouting. He [the victim] looked and saw them chasing after him.

"He tried to lose them and heard one of them shouting that it was a dead end and to drop his bike. He heard the defendant say, ‘Give us the f\*\*\*\*\*\* bike or I will f\*\*\*\*\*\* hurt you.’

"He considered jumping into the river with his bike to avoid being hurt but handed it over to the defendant. He had paid £3,400 for the bike. He identified the defendant at an ID procedure."

The court heard Smith also carried out a robbery and attempted robbery on Thwaite Lane, near Hunslet.

Two men were part of a larger group, riding slowly and slightly behind. Mr Khukon said: "The defendant and another male appeared on the pathway and moved towards one of the men and rode around him. The other male pulled a balaclava over his face and stood in his way.

"The man tried to get around him and was struck to the chest and fell off his bike. The other man stopped, getting off his bike and helped him. The defendant shouted at the other male, ‘Leave that f\*\*\*\*\*\* bike, get that.’ The defendant tried to punch the man and they took the bike and went off in the direction of Leeds city centre."

One of the men used the bike that was left to chase after the men, to no avail.

The value of the bike that was taken during the robbery was around £2,000. On the bike was also a bag with tools valued at £200 inside.

Smith was eventually arrested and answered "no comment" to all questions asked in his interview. The court heard he had previous convictions for offences of dishonesty, handling stolen goods, theft from a person and aggravated vehicle taking.

Mitigating, Dan Lee said: "There is no basis of plea and he doesn't stand by his comments in the pre-sentence report. He accepts he will be sentenced as to how the Crown have opened the case. A psychological report has been obtained. There is a pre-sentence report that covers both offences.

"He just turned 20 when he started committing these offences. You have heard that in relation to four out of five offences he pleaded guilty at the first available opportunity. In his pre-sentence report, he was open about the reasons for the offending and was driven by immediate financial gain. He was leading an unstable and chaotic lifestyle.

"He is someone easily influenced by his peers."

The court heard Smith experienced a "very disrupted childhood" and entered the criminal justice system at 16. Mr Lee said: "It is unsurprising he found school difficult and received multiple exclusions so had a limited amount of education and lack of support and he had to look after himself and survive.

"He has done an English course in prison and that goes towards improving his chances when he is released to get some legitimate income. He is someone who doesn't want to return to prison.

"Certainly, those small steps in custody does show a willingness to progress. He has a young daughter."

Recorder Jackson told Smith as he jailed him for six-and-a-half years: "These two robberies were planned. You knew where to lie in wait as a group and had disguises ready to deploy and used threats of violence."
