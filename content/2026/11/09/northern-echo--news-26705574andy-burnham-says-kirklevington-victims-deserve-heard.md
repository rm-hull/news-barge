The Prime Minister has said victims of “deeply disturbing” abuse at Kirklevington “deserve to have their voices heard”.

Andy Burnham today (November 9) committed to ensuring concerns raised over the Kirklevington Detention Centre abuse scandal by The Northern Echo are “taken seriously”.

The Labour leader said he knows how long hundreds of victims - who were abused as young boys by “barbaric” officers between the 1960s and 1990s - have been waiting for answers.

He said the story of victim Paul Overton, a “sensitive” grandad who died in September waiting for justice, was a “powerful reminder” of the impact of the horrific daily attacks.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21622310.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Natalie and Kirsti Overton from Newcastle with photo of their dad Paul *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

His death is a fear of many of the other “forgotten” survivors - and a stark reminder of how Cleveland Police’s 12-year Operation Magnolia is yet to see a single former officer charged.

Paul’s daughter Kirsti, who is now fighting for her dad’s abusers to be held to account, said it was “amazing” that the PM had read his story but urged for “something to be done”.

Speaking exclusively to the Echo, the Prime Minister said: “The accounts of abuse at Kirklevington are deeply disturbing, and I know how long victims and their families have been waiting for answers.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21679169.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Prime Minister Andy Burnham at 10 Downing Street *(Image: Jonathan Brady / PA)*

“Paul Overton’s story is a powerful reminder of the lasting impact this has had on people’s lives. His family, and every person affected, deserve to have their voices heard.

“The Northern Echo has rightly kept a spotlight on issues that matter deeply to people across the North East, and I am committed to ensuring the concerns raised in its manifesto are taken seriously.”

Kirsti said she wants justice for her father, who was sent to “torture camp” Kirklevington in 1975 and 1976, now "more than ever".

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21622386.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Paul Overton with his family *(Image: FAMILY PIC)*

She said his story being taken to the corridors of Westminster is the “best place it could have gone”: "I think it is amazing [that Andy Burnham is aware].

“I hope that makes them do something about it.

“There are people still waiting for justice who need answers and hopefully that gets the ball rolling. But then what is going to happen?”

## Police ‘continuing to place victims at heart of probe’

Cleveland Police has been investigating abuse at Kirklevington since 2014 - but despite 21 suspects being interviewed, not one abuser has been convicted.

Seven former prison officers under investigation by police are now dead - with police now awaiting decisions on whether it can charge seven officers.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21345533.jpg?type=mds-article-620)HMP Kirklevington *(Image: THE NORTHERN ECHO)*

One of the dead officers was exposed by the Echo in May as the “barbaric” Douglas Harrison, who would have been charged but died in 2019 days before he could be.

Earlier this year, the Echo called for police to prioritise the probe as victims continue to fear they will never see an officer dragged before the courts to answer for their actions.

We also called for a settlement scheme, where victims can claim compensation and receive a formal apology, while avoiding complex legal proceedings.

Victims say they continue to feel forgotten over the length of the probe and feel that justice is slipping away as more and more officers die.

Detective Chief Inspector Chatterton, said: “I wholeheartedly understand the concerns, frustrations and questions around the length of time this investigation has taken.

“I also acknowledge the significant impact this investigation had had on victims, and in many cases their families, too.

"We continue to work closely with the Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), continuing to place the victims at the very heart of this investigation."

Kirklevington Detention Centre was operated by the Home Office - now the Ministry of Justice - and housed boys aged 14-17 sentenced to between three weeks and four months' detention.

It changed to an adult male resettlement prison, as it is known today, in 1992.
