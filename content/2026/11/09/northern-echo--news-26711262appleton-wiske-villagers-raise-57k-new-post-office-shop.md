Residents from several North Yorkshire villages have raised £57,000 to save their much-loved shop and Post Office.

People in Appleton Wiske, near Northallerton, spent four years raising money for a permanent home for the village shop and Post Office.

Residents held raffles, dances and even pig races, while grants helped them reach their £57,000 target.

![Rev Simon Goulding, Appleton Wiske Parish Council Ken Blackwood and Appleton Wiske Postmistress Jackie McReddie](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689622.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rev Simon Goulding, Appleton Wiske Parish Council Ken Blackwood and Appleton Wiske Postmistress Jackie McReddie *(Image: POST OFFICE)*

When moving day came, about 60 volunteers of all ages shifted the shop’s entire contents to its new home in less than two hours.

The community effort was celebrated last Friday (November 6), when 93-year-old June Gill, the village’s oldest resident, cut the ribbon at the new premises.

The Post Office and shop once operated from part of a resident’s home, but fundraising paid for a new prefabricated building in the grounds of Appleton Wiske Village Hall on Front Street.

Postmistress Jackie McReddie said the project had brought Appleton Wiske and neighbouring villages even closer together.

She said: "This is a really proud day for us. We were already a close-knit community, but it brought people from Appleton Wiske and surrounding areas even closer together.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689623.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The ribbon cutting photo  shows June Gill cutting the ribbon with her son, Nev Gill, and Jonny Walker, Post Office Retail Change Lead *(Image: POST OFFICE)*

"We had a common goal as we all wanted to keep a Post Office and Village Shop. We had lots of fundraising events which helped us to reach our target, and we had fun on the way."

The community spirit was clear on October 31, when dozens of residents moved everything from the old premises into the new building.

A farmer used a forklift truck to move heavy refrigeration units, while volunteers pushed boxes and stock on sack barrows and wheelbarrows.

Children, pensioners and everyone in between joined in, completing the move in just one hour and 45 minutes.

Angela Carlin and other volunteers kept everyone going with tea, coffee, scones and cakes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689624.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Official opening of Appleton Wiske Post Office *(Image: POST OFFICE)*

Jackie said: "For transferring everything from one location to another, we had a really good turnout.

"Many pairs of hands meant that it took an hour and three-quarters to move everything.

"Luckily it was dry, and there was a fun atmosphere getting the job done. It was great seeing all the different ages and people from different villages wanting to get involved."

Jackie and her team prepared the new premises after the move, with the shop reopening on Monday, November 2, and Post Office services resuming the following day.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21689625.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Postmistress Jackie McReddie is pictured behind the counter *(Image: POST OFFICE)*

Celebrations continued last Friday as residents from Appleton Wiske and nearby villages gathered for the official opening.

June Gill, the village’s oldest resident, cut the ribbon, while the Yorkshire County Women’s Association provided refreshments.

The new green building has a ramp and wide entrance for easier access, and customers can use the village hall car park instead of parking on the road.

The shop and Post Office will serve Appleton Wiske and nearby communities with banking, postal services, bill payments and top-ups.

Jackie has also taken on two new staff to meet local demand.

The new Appleton Wiske Post Office and Village Shop is in the village hall grounds on Front Street, DL6 2AB and is open from 6.30am to 1pm and 2pm to 5.30pm on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays, and from 6.30am to 1pm on Tuesdays and Saturdays.
