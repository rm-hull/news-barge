A group of Darlington knitters have transformed Haughton Green with hundreds of handmade poppies and figures paying tribute to the often-overlooked role of women during wartime.

The Haughton knitting group has spent months creating its latest Remembrance display, with this year’s theme focusing on Women in the War.

Knitted figures around the green represent figures including Florence Nightingale, Mary Seacole, Flora Sandes, Noor Khan and Violette Szabo, alongside representations of other wartime roles.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21688661.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Haughton le Skerne Remembrance display *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The photographs show detailed knitted figures decorating bollards around the green, while a striking wave of red poppies stretches across the grass.

At Haughton Methodist Church, a cascade of knitted poppies has also been installed alongside a life-sized Wren, complete with a handmade uniform.

Group member Karen Duncan said the small group began thinking about this year's creation in early summer.

![Haughton le Skerne Remembrance display](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21688674.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Haughton le Skerne Remembrance display *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

She said: “This year we decided that we'd concentrate on women at war because they’re overlooked so often.

“We started thinking about it in early summer and we've been working on it since then, getting together and making sure that we knew what everybody was doing and that we had enough displays to fill the bollards on the green.

![Haughton le Skerne Remembrance display](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21688632.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Haughton le Skerne Remembrance display *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

“The big cascade of poppies on the green itself took a long time to make.”

For Karen and the other knitters, the display is about much more than their handiwork.

She said: “We all have links with the past, either through family members or friends. It’s something that we should remember.

“The children need to remember and know what our older relatives went through. Then you have relatives who never came back. It is something that means a lot to all of us.”

Karen, who has been part of the group for around five years, hopes people will stop and take a closer look.

She added: “We hope they enjoy it and get pleasure from it, and that it maybe stops them and makes them think about the sacrifices people have made for them.

“If people walk through the village, it would be nice for them to stop and have a look at some of the specific women who have been featured.”
