As more homeowners try to go green, their contractors may tell them they are required to upgrade their electrical panel — but some experts now say it’s an unnecessary expense.

Electric dryers, induction ranges and central air conditioners: The more appliances people add to their homes, the more power they will need from their electrical panel, also called a breaker box.

It has long been the industry standard to determine whether an electrical panel can safely power all the appliances in a home by adding up the maximum amount of power each appliance label says it uses, a new article by *The Washington Post* explains.

But there’s also a less common method, in which a building inspector can find the hour of the year that the house used the most power and then make sure the panel can handle 125 percent of that amount.

![As more homeowners try to go green, their contractors may tell them they are required to upgrade their electrical panel — but some experts now say it’s an unnecessary expense](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/17/22/GettyImages-2270290632.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In most cases, “even at peak demand, we see there’s not much cause for concern,” Jenny Low, from a group called POWER that seeks to make home electrification easier, told the *Post*.

“If you’re trying to add these appliances to your home, the money should be invested in the appliances. You don’t want to put it toward something that’s not needed.”

Ninety-eight percent of homes with 100-amp electric panels, typically the smallest size, have room to power more appliances, according to a report published in *ScienceDirect* in May.

![Ninety-eight percent of homes with 100-amp electric panels, typically the smallest size, have room to power more appliances, according to a report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/17/43/iStock-184303809.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

If homeowners do choose to upgrade their electrical panels, it will cost a pretty penny.

Maryland electrician Bill Hyman, who is skeptical about the power load that an older panel can handle, said his company upgrades at least one or two breaker boxes a week for about $6,000 to $13,000 per house.

“If they tell me they have central AC and an electric dryer, I know it’s overloaded. If you’ve been doing this as long as I’ve been doing this, we know,” Hyman told the *Post*.

“If we followed the code on everything we did, we would have problems. It’s the minimum standard, and minimums are not necessarily the right thing to do.”
