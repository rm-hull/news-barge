Certain types of pornography, which have been accused of normalising violence against women and girls, have been banned in England and Wales.

It was announced in October that particular content would be prohibited from Friday, 6 November, after cross-party pressure about the negative impact on women and girls led ministers to announce the new laws earlier this year.

So what is now banned – and what will the punishments be for breaking the law?

## The types of pornography that are banned

Pornography depicting strangulation or suffocation, sex between people who are related or pretending to be related, or when somebody is pretending to be a child under 16, is now illegal.

Justice Minister Alex Davies-Jones previously said: “This government has a commitment to halve violence against women and girls in a decade. For many people, it is still easy to access harmful pornography online.

“From November 6, it will be illegal to possess or publish pornography showing strangulation or suffocation, sexual activity between certain relatives, or adults roleplaying as children under 16.

“This isn’t about criminalising harmless sexual preferences. But porn can shape what people think a healthy relationship looks like, especially young people.

“Content that normalises, or even glorifies, violence against women is dangerous and can blur the line with what is acceptable in real life.”

## What will the consequences be of ignoring the ban?

Disobeying the ban is a criminal offence, with consequences ranging depending on the type of violation.

Possessing or publishing pornography that depicts strangulation or suffocation, or penetrative sex between relatives, carries a penalty of up to two years’ imprisonment.

![The ban came into effect on 6 November](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/02/15/22/iStock-1133497857-b84070df.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

The maximum sentence for someone possessing or publishing pornography depicting sexual activity involving a person who is, or is pretending to be, under 16 is three years’ imprisonment.

The Ministry of Justice clarified that the ban would only apply to individuals a “reasonable person” would believe appear to be real people.

## Why these types of pornography specifically?

Conservative peer Baroness Gabby Bertin made the recommendations in her review into pornography regulation, which was reported in 2025.

She said the news came “not a moment too soon”, after previously saying strangulation in porn had become “totally normalised”.

Lady Bertin’s report found pornography which shows women being choked can have “devastating” real-world consequences.

It highlighted “disturbing” role-play, including incest and adults portraying children.

A study by the Institute for Addressing Strangulation in 2024 found more than a third of 16- to 34-year-old adults had reported being strangled or choked at least once during consensual sex.

A survey by the same group in 2025 found 43 per cent of 16 to 17-year-olds, who are sexually active, said they had been subjected to it.

Non-fatal strangulation and suffocation were outlawed in 2022 under domestic abuse legislation.

In her report, published in February 2025, the Conservative peer said there had been a “total absence of government scrutiny” of the pornography industry.

![Lady Bertin’s report found pornography showing women being choked could have ‘devastating’ real-world consequences](https://static.independent.co.uk/2025/06/28/18/Official_portrait_of_Baroness_Bertin_crop_2%2C_2025-copy.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Lady Bertin previously said that strangulation had “gone into the bloodstream” of society and it should be “wiped out of the culture”.

”It is very good news these offences will come into force, and not a moment too soon,” she said.

“This change in law alongside better enforcement and stronger regulation will send the unequivocal signal not only to the companies that host and facilitate porn but also to wider society that extreme and violent content that encourages real-life harm is not acceptable.”

Ms Davies-Jones said: “Young people especially deserve better examples of what healthy relationships look like. Real intimacy is built on respect and trust, not abuse.

“If you’ve got this material, delete it. If you’re sharing it, stop.

“It’s not as harmless as you may think, and after 6 November it is criminal.”
