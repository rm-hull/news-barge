An out-of-control gambling addict who stole more than £140,000 from his employers over a five-year period may have to pay back some of the money.

Between March 2017 and late 2022 Gerrard Hartley took a total of £146,972.25 from the family-run Keighley scrap business of which he was an administrator.

Bradford Crown Court heard that Hartley spent most of the money on online gambling and at other gambling venues.

In March this year Hartley, 53, of Warley Drive, between Bradford Moor and Thornbury, Bradford, was handed a two-year prison sentence suspended for two years.

Today (November 6) he returned to court for a brief Proceeds of Crime hearing to determine whether he had the means to re-pay the money that he stole.

Hartley’s barrister John Bottomley said the amount available was £74,000, which represented £30,000 in cash and the remainder in what he described as “payment gifts” to an ex-partner.

He told the court that cash had been used to gamble, and that Hartley had a gambling disorder with cash having been taken from an ATM that was near to a bookmaker.

His Honour Judge Thomas KC adjourned the matter until November 30.

At the earlier hearing in March the court heard that Hartley had been trusted completely in his role as “paymaster” at the scrap firm, but he had secretly diverted money to fund his out-of-control gambling habit.

Prosecutor Elizabeth Noble told the court that when Hartley’s fraud came to light back in 2022, he was dismissed and in an interview with police the following year he admitted the offence and said his gambling addiction had got worse over the last five or six years.

Hartley admitted the fraud by abuse of position charge when he first appeared before magistrates in January this year and Mr Bottomley said it was accepted that the offending was serious.

But he submitted that Hartley was suffering from a gambling disorder at the time, and that it was a recognised mental illness.

“His conduct was driven by compulsion, not by greed or malice,” said Mr Bottomley.

The court heard Hartley, who had no previous convictions, had been assessed by an expert in gambling disorders who concluded that the offending would not have taken place but for the disorder.

Mr Bottomley said Hartley’s last bet had been placed in December 2022, and he had since engaged fully with his treatment and recovery.

Mr Bottomley conceded that some items had been bought from stores such as Harrods and Louis Vuitton, but he said the defendant had been driving a 2009 Ford Focus and most of his shopping was done at supermarkets.

He said it was not a case involving a luxury vehicle, a yacht, or first-class travel.

In a victim personal statement, the owner of the business referred to seeing a photograph of Hartley on holiday in Mexico drinking champagne and smoking a cigar.

“Knowing that our hard-earned money funded this lifestyle has caused me additional distress, anger and a deep sense of injustice,” he said.

“Gerrard was someone we trusted as family. To learn that for years he had been secretly stealing from us while sitting at our table, sharing celebrations, has caused deep emotional distress that I still struggle with every day.”

In sentencing Hartley back in March, Mr Recorder Bryan Cox KC said it had been a serious fraud involving a substantial breach of trust.

He told him: “The bottom line is that you stole a substantial amount of money over a prolonged period of time.”

The judge accepted that Hartley’s serious gambling addiction had caused him to behave as he did.

He said he was satisfied that Hartley’s remorse was genuine and he had used the time since his offending to take steps to rehabilitate himself.

As well as imposing the suspended sentence order Recorder Cox ordered Hartley to do the maximum 300 hours of unpaid work for the community, to comply with a Mental Health Treatment requirement, and attend 15 rehabilitation activity requirement days.

“You must appreciate you have come very close today to going to prison,” he added.
