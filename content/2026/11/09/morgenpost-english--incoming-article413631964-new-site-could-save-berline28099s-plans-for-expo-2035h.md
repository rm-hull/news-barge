Experts familiar with the project see a new potential site in Berlin as a game-changer in the debate over the 2035 World Expo. This could provide an opportunity to host the Expo within the city itself if Brandenburg withdraws from the joint Expo project near BER Airport, as some insiders privately expect.

The new front-runner is Buchholz-Nord, a business park already planned for the northern outskirts of the city in the district of Pankow. According to Berlin’s Urban Development Plan for Industry and Commerce, the site offers 173 hectares of land, more than the Expo requires for its main exhibition grounds. The Paris-based Bureau International des Expositions (BIE), which has 184 member states and selects the host, places great importance on having a single location where all participating countries can build their pavilions.

This requirement made the original decentralised concept proposed by Berlin’s Expo supporters, led by former Chamber of Commerce and Industry (IHK) president Daniel-Jan Girl, unworkable. Since then, they have been searching for a suitable site. The former Tegel Airport has already been earmarked for other uses, while the CleanTech Business Park in Marzahn is simply too small.

### Berlin Expects Brandenburg to Withdraw from the Joint Expo Project

Attention initially turned to Brandenburg, partly because Governing Mayor Kai Wegner (CDU) opposed the Expo initiative, fearing it would complicate Berlin’s Olympic bid. The site in Schönefeld was thoroughly assessed. However, according to reports, the state government in Potsdam has objections. Above all, the financial risk appears too high for Berlin’s neighbouring state. This is partly because large portions of the land are privately owned, and the owners could demand substantial sums.

Minister-President Dietmar Woidke (SPD) was never enthusiastic about Berlin pushing the project onto him. Berlin now expects Brandenburg to announce its withdrawal from the joint Expo project soon, even though business representatives in Brandenburg strongly support it.

Faced with this situation, Berlin’s Expo supporters took another close look at the city map and found a possible solution in the far north-east.

“If Brandenburg does not want to participate, we have to examine how we could do this on our own in Berlin and what options are available,” said Senator for Economic Affairs Franziska Giffey (SPD) when asked by the Morgenpost about the new site.

“We are taking a close look at Buchholz-Nord, as we are already working on plans for the business park. So far, it looks promising.”

### Buchholz-Nord Was Already Intended to Be Developed as a Business Park

The city already plans to develop the land in Buchholz-Nord for commercial use, although not until the late 2030s. The framework plan, which the Senator for Economic Affairs wants to submit to the Senate before the end of this year, could now be updated with new data and the possibility of using the site for the Expo in the meantime.

Plans for the necessary transport connections, including new roads, S-Bahn services and railway lines, could be brought forward for the Expo. The site between the A114 motorway, Schönerlinder Straße and the Berlin Ring motorway is currently used as farmland, meaning that relatively few objections from nature conservation and species protection groups are expected. There are virtually no neighbours who could hold up the project.

The Expo might even be able to operate independently of external energy supplies thanks to nearby wind turbines or solar farms. Businesses could later move into some of the pavilions.

However, even with a suitable site, hosting an Expo would not be straightforward. Public authorities would have to provide substantial upfront funding so that the exhibition could cover its costs with enough visitors, or even generate a surplus, as the Expo organisers have calculated.

Brandenburg remains sceptical. Whether Berlin would commit to the project under a possible new Left-Greens-SPD coalition remains an open question.

### The Left Is Not Opposed to an Expo but Has Questions About Financing

The exploratory agreement states that the parties intend to continue pursuing “a bid for a financially viable, sustainable World Expo”.

“We have nothing against an Expo, but we still have questions, particularly about the financing,” said the Left Party’s economic policy expert Damiano Valgolio, who is also involved in the coalition negotiations.

A prerequisite is financial participation by the federal government. Chancellor Friedrich Merz (CDU) said at the Chamber of Commerce and Industry this week that he would support the Expo if the concept was right.

At the same time, there are concerns within the CDU/CSU about providing substantial funding for a project that might then be overseen by a Berlin Senate led by the Left Party.

Moreover, Merz’s bid to host the Expo in Germany is likely to face strong competition. US President Donald Trump is expected to announce an American bid to host the Expo in Miami on the sidelines of the G20 summit in Florida in mid-December.
