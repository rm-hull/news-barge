MPs investigating allegations of abuse linked to former Harrods owner Mohamed Al Fayed are seeking a meeting with Police Scotland following fresh claims that his brother trafficked and sexually assaulted a woman at a Highland estate.

The Herald understands the All-Party Parliamentary Group (APPG) for Survivors of Fayed and Harrods is keen to meet the force following reports that Salah Fayed drugged, sexually assaulted and held a young woman prisoner at the family's Balnagown Estate in Easter Ross.

If that goes meeting goes ahead, the group's co-chair, Liberal Democrat MP Wendy Chamberlain, confirmed its findings would be included in a report being prepared for MPs on the wider allegations of abuse and the role of those suspected of enabling it.

The development comes after a woman, identified only as Jill, told BBC Scotland she was flown to the Highland estate on the Fayed family's private jet in the 1990s while working for Harrods.

She alleged Salah Fayed forced her to take drugs, repeatedly sexually assaulted her and controlled her movements during visits to the property.

Police Scotland has confirmed three people have been identified in connection with offending against a number of victims at Balnagown Estate, with inquiries ongoing.

The parliamentary group, established in October 2025, is examining allegations of sexual abuse, harm and trafficking for sexual exploitation linked to Mohamed Al Fayed and his wider network over several decades.

Its work extends beyond Harrods to other businesses and properties associated with the former owner, including Fulham Football Club, Park Lane Apartments and the Ritz Paris.

The group, which has 40 parliamentary members and is in contact with around 100 survivors, is gathering evidence to help Parliament understand the scale of the alleged abuse and the response of institutions.

It is also investigating the role of people suspected of enabling the abuse.

Ms Chamberlain said: "Fayed and his brother did not act alone. They were part of a wider network of enablers who brought women to them to be abused.

"And it's not just about Harrod's either. There are other locations. We've had testimony in relation to Fulham Football Club, where abuse was perpetrated."

She confirmed the APPG was preparing a report for MPs and would meet the Metropolitan Police again before Christmas.

The group previously held an evidence session with the Met in July over Operation Cornpoppy, its investigation into allegations involving Mohamed Al Fayed and suspected facilitators, and its review of how previous complaints were handled.

A further session is scheduled for November.

The APPG also met the Independent Office for Police Conduct in September to discuss its investigation, Operation Fordington.

Ms Chamberlain said the group also intended to examine the involvement of medical professionals in the alleged abuse.

She added: "Beyond that we want to look at other aspects. We know, for example, that there were doctors involved in some medical examinations of victims of trafficking and so we want to look very closely at that."

The group has previously facilitated meetings between survivors and senior politicians, including safeguarding minister Jess Phillips and former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer.

Further meetings are planned with the London Victims' Commissioner, the Victims' Commissioner for England and Wales and the Independent Anti-Slavery Commissioner.

The Metropolitan Police has said seven suspects have been interviewed under caution in connection with its investigation, including over suspected offences involving sexual assault and human trafficking.

It said 168 victims had come forward, while around 70 people had been referred to the Home Office as potential trafficking victims, according to a confidential note of a meeting with the APPG reported by the BBC.

Salah Fayed died in 2010, while his brother Mohamed died in 2023.

Police Scotland said its investigation remained ongoing and it would be inappropriate to comment further.
