It has been six and a half weeks since Berliners went to the polls. It will probably take twice as long again before a new government is in place. Elif Eralp of the Left Party, who is likely to become Berlin’s next governing mayor, could therefore move into the Red City Hall no earlier than the end of January 2027, nearly three months from now. The chief negotiators from the Left Party, Greens and SPD agreed on this timetable on Thursday. One thing is now certain: Kai Wegner (CDU), the current Governing Mayor, will still deliver this year’s New Year’s address.

When elections are held in September, as they were this year, Berlin usually has a new government shortly before Christmas. The reason for the delay in forming a red-green-red coalition is that all three potential coalition partners want their members to vote on the final coalition agreement. This has never happened in Berlin before – and it takes time.

### Red-Green-Red in Berlin: Membership Votes Cause Delays

The three parties aim to conclude their negotiations by the turn of the year. Sources close to the negotiations said on Monday that membership votes could not be held over the Christmas and New Year holiday period.

The necessary discussion forums ahead of the votes cannot be organised until the new year either. All three parties want to complete their membership votes in the first half of January. The Greens will then have to organise a party conference to give final approval to the coalition agreement and the proposed Senate team. If everything goes according to plan, the new government could be elected at the end of January.

Until then, the CDU-SPD Senate will remain in office. The Greens said on Thursday that they had met with Stefan Evers, the current Finance Senator and CDU parliamentary group leader. During the meeting, they told him they expected the caretaker government to refrain from making decisions with long-term consequences before handing over power. This includes decisions on personnel appointments, tree felling and the removal of bicycle streets. Evers reportedly gave a verbal assurance that he would comply.

### Berlin: Business Climate Index Rises, but Businesses Warn of Risks

Meanwhile, business representatives are putting pressure on the negotiators.

“Uncertainty about the prospective coalition’s economic policy direction is weighing heavily on business expectations in the skilled trades,” said Jürgen Wittke, chief executive of the Berlin Chamber of Skilled Crafts, on Thursday.

Despite a rise in the business climate index, Manja Schreiner, chief executive of the Berlin Chamber of Commerce and Industry (IHK), also issued a warning. An economic upturn would look different, she said. “Particularly worrying signs are the cautious employment and investment plans.”

Following the start of coalition negotiations this week, the 14 specialist groups are now getting to work. The chief negotiators are not scheduled to meet again for another two weeks, when the first results from the working groups are expected.
