Yorkshire Water faced questions from residents and councillors last night (Monday 9 November) at a meeting of Ripon City Council

There is public anger over polluted rivers, and one resident, Brian McHugh, told *YorkMix Radio* he was rather disappointed by the response of Tim Myatt, Head of Corporate Affairs, when he asked when the company could expect to reach a level where there were zero discharges.

His question was put to Mr Myatt by the Mayor of Ripon, Cllr Andrew Williams, who chaired the meeting. Mr Myatt replied that their target isn’t zero, and they don’t have a timeline to get to that.

They are aiming for fewer than 10.

Mr McHugh said: “It’s quite surprising because they had put out this information that they’ve managed to reduce sewage discharges from 75 to 43 over the space of four years, and that’s clearly something they’re proud of.

“But when they were then asked when that 43 is going to hit zero they they couldn’t really answer that, and really it looked as if they had no plan to to achieve that. And that’s really the takeaway that I had that there was no roadmap to reduce sewage discharges for the people of Ripon.”

UK water companies are legally permitted to release untreated or partially treated sewage into rivers and coastal waters only in exceptional circumstances—specifically during periods of heavy rainfall or extreme weather**.**

Brian McHugh added: “In fairness to Yorkshire Water, it is good that they turned up and it was also good that councillors and and the Mayor really pushed for them to to appear and to be held accountable because Yorkshire Water should be accountable to its customers and should be accountable to the people with whom it serves.”

Around 25 people turned up to watch the meeting.

There were questions about bonus payments to the outgoing Chief Executive and the meeting was told that was a matter for the parent company, Kelda Holdings Limited**,** which is controlled by a consortium of global investment and infrastructure funds.

Another question was on the subject of Mink in the river and when they could be removed, but that is not something that falls within Yorkshire Water’s remit.
