A canny business venture has seen a Scotland-based food start-up racing to keep up with demand for its tinned products after relaunching this year.

Inspired by the vast and colourful array of premium tinned fish found elsewhere in Europe, the idea for Canny Mushrooms first came to founder Joanna Davies around six years ago.

“I used to eat quite a lot of tinned sardines with different lemon or chilli and things added,” she explains.

"I had a sort of epiphany, realising I could create something as convenient and flavourful as tinned fish but using mushrooms.”

Then based in Boston and working as a project manager in the technology industry, Davies was far removed from the world of food and drink and the Scottish markets where she would eventually become a regular.

It was only after leaving her job to embark on a two-year cycling adventure that she began to consider what her future might look like if she pursued a different career path.

“I've always wanted to start my own business with a strong sustainability angle,” she says.

“I had a lot of time to think about what I was going to do next and decided I was going to go for it.

“I moved to Edinburgh when I came back to the UK, and that's where it all started.”

Davies signed up for a food and drink entrepreneurship course at Queen Margaret University and worked with its Scottish Centre for Food Development and Innovation to test the feasibility of preserving mushrooms in tins.

“No one has really canned mushrooms in the way I wanted to can them, in an oil and not kind of boiled into rubber,” she says.  
“There are other tinned mushrooms that are in brine, but that wasn't what I was looking to do. I had a very specific idea about the result I wanted.”

After developing the concept and testing different recipes and processes, the budding entrepreneur travelled south of the border to produce her first small batches at the Food Incubator in Leicester, where she could access specialist canning equipment.

Davies launched Canny Mushrooms during the summer of 2025, introducing her tins to markets in Stockbridge and Leith and securing a number of independent Edinburgh stockists by approaching local shops directly.

Time spent meeting customers face to face at markets was crucial to this development phase and remains a core part of the business today.

“I spent most of last year learning about what customers wanted,” she says.

“Even now, when I get return customers, I make a big point of asking them how they've eaten the mushrooms.

“Just learning about what they've done with the product, that kind of feedback has been really, really valuable.”

Away from the markets, however, manufacturing the tins efficiently and cost-effectively was proving challenging. And, when the Leicester site closed, Davies had to seek out a new base with the specialist equipment needed for production.

She subsequently secured temporary access to a space at Kirsty's Allergen Free Ready Meals factory in Yorkshire.

Then, after around six months of trading, Davies decided to pause sales and focus on refining the range while streamlining the production process.

It was a conversation with Pip Murray, founder of nut butter brand Pip & Nut, that would ultimately give her the confidence to take what could have seemed a backwards step.

Meeting at a women-in-business event, Murray told Davies how she too had initially sold her products at markets before stopping to concentrate on getting the business ready to scale.

“I was really nervous about stopping selling, because I thought that everyone would forget about me instantly,” she says.

“But Pip said that was a really important step for her because it enabled her to fully focus on setting up ready to scale, and that gave me confidence to do the same thing.”

Following the advice, Davies ‘shut up shop’ at Christmas 2025 and turned her attention to preparing Canny Mushrooms for growth, later shifting production to Aberdeen.

There, she began working with ONE SeedPod, a £27 million food and drink innovation hub at Craibstone, which includes food-safe manufacturing kitchens and new-product-development facilities.

Working with the team, she embarked on a project to redevelop her recipes and production process.

“I basically moved up to Aberdeen for this facility and the support that's available here,” Davies says.

“There's nothing else like this in and around Edinburgh at the moment. I don't think anywhere else in Scotland, really. That's been great for me.”

While preparing for the relaunch, Davies also secured a place on Fortnum & Mason's shelves after successfully pitching Canny Mushrooms through the London retailer's Open Call programme, in an experience she describes as “quite Dragons' Den-style”.

“At that point, I hadn't set up production here in Aberdeen yet, so I didn't actually have any of my new product to be able to provide them to taste.

“It still felt a bit like, ‘Oh no, they're going to say I'm too early stage.’ But they didn't. They went for it.”

With a new production home in Aberdeen and the backing of one of Britain's best-known retailers, Canny Mushrooms returned to the market in May 2026.

“Since then, things have taken off in quite a big way,” Davies says.

The Fortnum & Mason listing has been a catalyst for securing other stockists as Davies focuses on building Canny Mushrooms' presence in delis and farm shops around the UK, with an online shop also planned further down the line.

“At the moment I'm struggling to keep up with demand as it is,” she says. “I don't really have enough stock to be able to do online orders as well, but it is in the plan.”

With flavours including shiitake mushrooms with chilli and soy sauce and oyster mushrooms with Aleppo pepper and lemon already available, Davies also hopes to eventually begin canning other produce, although she is keeping the details of what might come next under wraps.

For now, you'll continue to find her at markets across Scotland as she attempts to change perceptions of tinned food from “back-up things in the back of your cupboard” to “a hero in your meal”, all the while staying true to her vision for a business that is “useful, good for people and the planet, and fun”.
