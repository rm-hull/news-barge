Health Secretary Angela Constance has said she is keeping an "open mind" about whether Glasgow's Queen Elizabeth University Hospital should be placed in special measures following The Herald's revelations that senior doctors believe patient harm has become a "daily reality" in A&E.

Ms Constance described the warnings from the hospital's entire emergency consultant body as painting a "very distressing picture" and pledged to raise the concerns with NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde (NHSGGC) and Scotland's health watchdog.

It comes after The Herald revealed that consultants at Scotland's flagship super-hospital had issued a desperate plea for help over "indefensible" conditions in its emergency department, warning that chronic overcrowding and delays of more than 24 hours were putting patients at risk.

In a damning letter to Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS), seen by The Herald, the senior medics warned that staff were routinely being forced to move patients out of resuscitation areas earlier than would normally be considered appropriate and identify patients for corridor care on wards.

They said: "Patient harm associated with prolonged delays, overcrowding and exit block is not only a theoretical risk but a daily reality for staff."

Asked whether she found the consultants' account unacceptable by LBC following The Herald's reporting, Ms Constance said: "It paints a very distressing picture that is certainly not in the interests of patients but it must make their working conditions exceptionally difficult.

"Now I know that healthcare improvement Scotland are scrupulously independent, they have a very important role giving assurance but also scrutinising where local systems can improve and where they need to demonstrate more grip of a situation so I will certainly respond to the situation that’s been outlined this morning.

"I’ll certainly engage with both Greater Glasgow and Clyde which I do regularly, I can assure you but also with Healthcare Improvement Scotland not withstanding that they are independent."

Pressed on whether the QEUH should be placed in special measures, the Health Secretary did not rule out further intervention.

She said: "I always keep a very open mind about what is required. I have regular engagement with Greater Glasgow and Clyde, as you would expect.

"What I do know is that there are increased pressures right across the system and that means that we have to employ not just one solution but all the solutions particularly as we enter winter."

Her comments mark a shift in tone from the Scottish Government's initial response to The Herald's investigation.

Despite HIS confirming it had raised the consultants' concerns with government officials, a Scottish Government spokesperson previously declined to comment on the substance of the warnings.

The spokesperson said: "This would be for NHS GGC to respond to as it is an operational matter."

The consultants first raised concerns with HIS in 2023, warning that patient safety was being seriously compromised.

After complaints about the watchdog's initial handling of their concerns, HIS carried out a review of emergency departments at the QEUH, Glasgow Royal Infirmary and the Royal Alexandra Hospital.

Its report, published in March 2025, made 41 recommendations, including 30 directed at NHSGGC, and identified overcrowding, poor patient flow and a lack of compassionate, respectful and positive leadership.

However, in a further letter dated July 10 this year, the QEUH consultants warned that the situation had continued to deteriorate despite the recommendations.

They wrote: "More than a year has now passed since publication of the original HIS report and its recommendations, yet our day-to-day experience suggests that conditions have continued to deteriorate."

The scale of the problem is underlined by the latest Public Health Scotland figures.

In the first ten months of this year, 5,961 patients spent more than 12 hours in the QEUH emergency department, a 33% increase on the total recorded across the whole of 2025.

In the week ending October 25, 501 patients waited more than eight hours to be seen, admitted or discharged, while 223 spent more than 12 hours in the department.

Research by the Royal College of Emergency Medicine estimates that prolonged A&E delays contribute to around 800 excess deaths annually in Scotland, equivalent to one avoidable death for every 72 patients waiting more than eight hours for admission.

The consultants have also described a collapse in confidence in the hospital's leadership.

A survey of senior doctors in the department gave their overall experience of working there a score of just 3.5 out of 10, while confidence and trust in the health board's leadership was rated at 32 out of 100.

In its response to the consultants, HIS acknowledged the seriousness of their concerns but stressed that responsibility for implementing improvements rested with NHSGGC.

The watchdog said its role was "not to design, direct or implement service changes".

Scottish Labour's health spokesperson Dame Jackie Baillie has called for ministerial intervention, warning that Ms Constance "cannot look away from the crisis".

She told The Herald: "This is a horrifying warning about the state of our emergency departments from those who know best — the clinicians on the frontline.

"This is not the first time that chaotic and often incompetent management at the QEUH has compromised patient safety.

"The Health Secretary cannot look away from the crisis unfolding in A&E departments in Glasgow and across Scotland.

"It's time to listen to the clinicians and act to prevent unnecessary loss of life."

Scottish Liberal Democrat health spokesperson Adam Harley described the consultants' letter as "chilling" and also called on the Scottish Government to intervene.

NHSGGC has said patient safety remains its highest priority and acknowledged that further support is required at the QEUH.

A spokesperson said: "We recognise that some patients continue to wait too long within the Emergency Department, particularly when there are pressures on hospital capacity and delays in discharging patients who are clinically ready to leave, and improving this situation for both our patients and colleagues is really important to us."

The board said improvement work was continuing to address patient flow, strengthen escalation arrangements and reduce hospital occupancy.

Ms Constance's latest remarks leave open the possibility of further action as the hospital approaches the winter period, with the Health Secretary now promising to engage directly with both the health board and the independent watchdog over the consultants' warnings.
