At least 26 people have been killed and dozens injured after Russian forces unleashed a barrage of heavy aerial bombs on an aid distribution centre in southern Ukraine, in a strike president Volodymyr Zelensky has condemned as “utterly horrific.”

At least nine 3,000kg guided bombs struck the village of Komyshuvakha on Monday, targeting a local school used for humanitarian operations before a second bomb hit a shelter where survivors were taking cover, regional head Mykhailo Semikin said.

The massacre casts a dark shadow over fragile diplomatic momentum ahead of proposed trilateral peace talks involving Ukraine, Russia, and the United States in Abu Dhabi later this week.

Zelensky has warned the strike proves Moscow “does not want any de-escalation,” coming just days after a rare humanitarian ceasefire had facilitated civilian evacuations from Russian-occupied Oleshky.

The deadly bombardment in the south follows a huge technological blow for the Kremlin at home, after Ukrainian military intelligence reported on Monday that a $150m Su-57 stealth fighter “exploded” during engine startup at the Akhtubinsk military airbase.

## At least 20 missing after Russia’s strike on Zaporizhzhia

About 20 people are considered missing and the death toll could rise, officials involved in the search and rescue operation in Zaporizhzhia said.

As night fell in Komyshuvakha, emergency responders used flashlights to search through the rubble of collapsed buildings, according to photos and video posted online by local officials.

Efforts to rescue people on Monday evening were hindered as Russian drones launched additional attacks in the area that had been bombed, according to Mykhailo Semikin, a military official in Zaporizhzhia.

## Russia does not want de-escalation, says Zelensky after strikes kill 26

Russia bombed an aid distribution centre and neighbouring shelter in southern Ukraine on Monday, killing 26 people, Ukrainian officials said.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said in a post on X that strikes first targeted a school building used to distribute humanitarian aid and then took aim at a nearby shelter.

“This is further proof that Russia wants no de-escalation,” Zelensky said.

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/01/12/2302864925..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The attacks in the village of Komyshuvakha, in the southern Zaporizhzhia region, occurred as a limited ceasefire expired Monday in Kherson, a different region in the country's south. The limited ceasefire agreement had allowed humanitarian aid and evacuation missions into front-line areas in Russian-occupied territory.

“There was a moment when the next steps could have shown that progress toward peace was possible,” Zelensky said.

“With these killings, Russia is sending an entirely opposite signal: Russia is counting on more killings.”

Zelensky later said in an address to the nation that Ukraine was expecting Russia to escalate its attacks at a time when the country is facing a shortage of missiles needed to defend itself.

## ICYMI: Hacked alert apps blast fake nuclear and chemical warnings across Ukraine and Russia

Residents in both Ukraine and Russia have received fake emergency warnings after government-run mobile alert applications in both countries were breached by hackers.

In Ukraine, the targeted mobile app pushed rogue notifications in Ukrainian, Russian, and broken English falsely warning of imminent radiation spikes, chemical threats, and street disturbances in Kyiv.

One Russian-language message warned: "Hi! There will be a nuclear threat across Ukraine. Do not worry, the Oreshnik is incoming."

Ukrainian Air Force Command spokesman Yurii Ihnat said the cyberattack had no impact on defence operations, and authorities restored the app after a temporary suspension.

Hours later, at approximately 2:45 a.m. Moscow time, hackers breached Russia’s Emergency Situations Ministry app, triggering false alerts across 33 regions.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov has since called on all state bodies to bolster their cybersecurity.

## Death toll rises to 26 in aid centre strike as emergency teams uncover more bodies

The death toll from the Russian aerial bombardment on an aid distribution point in Komyshuvakha has risen to 26, Ukrainian emergency officials confirmed as search teams continue to clear heavy debris.

Rescuers recovered two additional bodies from the wreckage of the shelter and school site in the Zaporizhzhia region, following an attack carried out with heavy guided aerial bombs.

Emergency crews, police, and military personnel are still searching the site for remaining missing residents, with operations repeatedly disrupted by incoming Russian reconnaissance and strike drone threats.

## Exclusive: Russia ramps up disinformation war on UK with propaganda claiming Britain is ‘ungovernable and defenceless’

Russia’s disinformation war on the UK has reached new highs as Kremlin-backed media seek to sow chaos amongst Britons and ramp up attacks on their “main adversary”, experts warn.

In the past six months, state-affiliated media have pushed hundreds of thousands of stories on social media portraying Britain as ungovernable, defenceless, and a legitimate Russian target, *The Independent* can reveal.

Analysis by risk intelligence firm Osavul, which tracks global hybrid threats and supplies technology to Nato, shows such media output rose by around 15 per cent in September, when it produced around 40,365 stories about the UK, while similar levels of disinformation pushed in October.

## Report: Moscow’s most advanced stealth jet blows up miles from combat

Russia suffered another battlefield blow as a $150mn advanced stealth jet “exploded” before takeoff, Ukrainian military intelligence reported on Monday.

The fifth-generation Su-57 multipurpose fighter blew up during engine startup at Akhtubinsk military airbase on October 29, the Defense Intelligence Directorate (HUR) reported.

It was unclear what caused the incident, but HUR said the engines can undergo “catastrophic destruction” when filled with poor quality fuel.

The costly jet has been used sparingly in Ukraine, launching long-range stand-off missiles from Russian-controlled airspace. Analysts estimate Russia currently uses fewer than 30 Su-57s, having received only around 20 between 2020 and 2025, according to RUSI.

Russia has suffered a string of losses in recent weeks, with Ukraine last month claiming to have damaged six Su-34M medium bombers in strikes on the Borisoglebsk airfield in Voronezh region.

Delegations from Russia, Ukraine and the United States are expected to sit down for talks aimed at ending the nearly five year war later this week, although specifics are yet to be confirmed.
