A Bradford artist has issued an appeal after three of her original artworks, including a print featuring the city's iconic boar symbol, were stolen from a public exhibition.

Cathryn Iliffe discovered the pieces had gone missing from a heritage display at Bradford Arts Centre, where they had been exhibited as part of a project celebrating some of Bradford's most historic buildings.

The missing works include a lino print of the Bradford Boar, as well as prints depicting an owl and a sheep inspired by engravings found on decorative glass within Bradford City Hall.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21663742.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The missing Bradford Boar lino print. *(Image: Cathryn Iliffe)*

Ms Iliffe said: "It's upsetting to have my work taken when it was made and donated for a public display to celebrate Bradford's heritage."

## Inspired by Bradford City Hall

The exhibition was created through a heritage project involving four groups, each focusing on a different historic Bradford building.

Ms Iliffe's group was assigned Bradford City Hall and participants were given a tour of the landmark building before creating artwork inspired by what they saw.

Among the features that caught her attention were intricate engravings on glass doors and windows.

"We all took photographs and I thought I was going to make some lino prints based on them," she said. "I hadn't done lino printing since school. I'm still doing it now and I'm continuing with it."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21663744.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The missing owl lino print inspired by Bradford City Hall. *(Image: Cathryn Iliffe)*

## 'It's a bit of a shock'

Ms Iliffe only learned the artwork had disappeared after another person involved in the exhibition mentioned pieces had gone missing.

After visiting the display, she realised three of her prints had been taken.

She said: "It's a bit of a shock really because it's a public exhibition and it's in a public building...maybe the person responsible will have some conscience and return them."

While she cannot explain why somebody would remove the pieces, she hopes publicity may help recover them.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21663743.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The missing sheep lino print inspired by City Hall engravings. *(Image: Cathryn Iliffe)*

## Appeal for information

The artist is now hoping members of the public who recognise the distinctive prints, particularly the Bradford Boar artwork, may be able to help trace them.

She added: "If nothing else, at least if they are seen in the Telegraph & Argus it gives people a chance to see them."
