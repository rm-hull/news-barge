North East households are being invited to show off their Christmas displays this month to help with their energy bills.

It comes as a festive competition will give out thousands of pounds to those who go all out with their festive decorations.

The contest, launched by Compare the Market, will judge Christmas displays across the UK for their visual design and creativity, festive spirit, originality and whether they contribute to their local communities.

The winning household will win £3,000 to cover energy costs, with up to 50 neighbours each receiving £300 for their December bills.

Entries are now open and will run until 11:59pm on November 15.

Martine McCutcheon, star of Love Actually, will chair the judging panel alongside 2025 winner David Carter and energy expert Emma Spencer, before visiting the winner’s home to help switch on their Christmas lights.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21660797.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The winning household will win £3,000 to cover energy costs, with up to 50 neighbours each receiving £300 for their December bills. *(Image: Ryan O’Donoghue/PinPep)*

McCutcheon said: "I absolutely love Christmas, and I’m a big believer that when it comes to decorations, more is more," adding that streets full of festive lighting were "magical" to see.

The actress praised the effort families across the country regularly put in to decorating their homes ahead of Christmas, and shared her thoughts on what made a good Christmas display.

She said: "The best displays aren’t just about how many lights you can squeeze into your front garden, though, they’re the ones that bring people together, make a difference to the local community and put a smile on everyone’s face."

McCutcheon said the judging panel were looking for "homes that go all out, spread a little festive magic and bring their communities together."

The competition celebrates the power of Christmas lights to lift spirits as rising energy costs force some households to scale back.

Research from Compare the Market found that 81 per cent of people are concerned about their winter energy bills, while 64 per cent have worried specifically about the cost of Christmas decorations.

The prize is key for households in Yorkshire and the North East, where energy debt has hit record highs.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21660767.jpg?type=mds-article-620)McCutcheon said the judging panel were looking for "homes that go all out, spread a little festive magic and bring their communities together." *(Image: Ryan O’Donoghue/PinPep)*

Research from the University of York, based on the government’s Family Resources Survey, identified the North East as one of the regions most affected by mounting energy debt.

Despite widespread affordability concerns, 79 per cent of Compare the Market respondents said Christmas lights make them feel happier during the winter months, and 55 per cent feel more connected to their community when festive displays are lit up.

People who plan on entering the competition are invited to submit photos of their outdoor Christmas light displays along with a written entry explaining why they deserve to win, highlighting how they meet the judges' criteria.

The competition opens today (November 3) and closes on Sunday, November 15, at 11.59pm.

Entries must include the entrant’s full name, email address, contact number, and city or region, and be emailed to [christmas@comparethemarket.com.](mailto:christmas@comparethemarket.com.)

The competition aims to reward households that do more than put on a dazzling display, with judges looking for entries that bring neighbours together, support local causes and spread festive cheer across their communities.

Full details of the competition and judging criteria are available through Compare the Market.
