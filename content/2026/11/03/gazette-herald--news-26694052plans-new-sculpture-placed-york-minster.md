PLANS to install a new sculpture at York Minster by famous artist have been submitted to City of York Council.

The Chapter of York has applied to install Meet, a new work by Sir Antony Gormley, the artist behind the Angel of the North, on the cathedral's west façade.

The sculpture would remain in place for three years from spring 2028, and is the first of two structures planned for the Minster by the artist.




Planning documents said: "As one of Britain's most celebrated contemporary sculptors, Sir Antony Gormley's involvement presents a significant opportunity to attract new and returning visitors to York Minster and the city more broadly.

"The installation will encourage engagement not only with the artwork itself but also with the Minster's worshipping life, heritage, educational programmes, exhibitions and cultural activities."

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21664149.jpg?type=mds-article-620)"This work will be made of two stacks of mutually supporting cast-iron blocks evoking two slightly over-life-size bodies that come together to form a single structure" - Sir Antony *(Image: Supplied)*

Meet will be made from cast-iron blocks and will depict two people embracing.

The news comes four years after a full-length stone statute of Queen Elizabeth II was unveiled by the King at the cathedral’s west front in 2022.

Ahead of Meet's installation, repairs will need to carried out to the Minster's stonework.

Sir Antony said: "I would like to make a work called Meet for the empty double niche on the west façade.

"This work will be made of two stacks of mutually supporting cast-iron blocks evoking two slightly over-life-size bodies that come together to form a single structure."

![Sir Antony Gormley, the artist behind the Angel of the North, is hoping to unveil two new sculptures at York Minster](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21649968.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sir Antony Gormley, the artist behind the Angel of the North, is hoping to unveil two new sculptures at York Minster *(Image: PA Media)*

The renowned artist first gained recognition for his work in contemporary art when he won the prestigious Turner Prize in 1994, is best known for creating the Angel of the North – a 65ft steel sculpture overlooking the A1 near Gateshead.

After Meet is installed, Antony plans on introducing a second work of art to the Minster.

Named Close, this is proposed for display inside the cathedral and is subject to a separate approval process.

The work would be created at York Minster's Centre of Excellence for Heritage Craft Skills and Estate Management.

![The Meet sculpture would remain in place for three years from spring 2028, planning documents have said](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21664197.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Meet sculpture would remain in place for three years from spring 2028, planning documents have said *(Image: Supplied)*

Minster officials say the work has been developed through discussions between Sir Antony and the cathedral team as part of the Minster’s 1,400th anniversary celebrations.

Speaking about the sculptures, The Very Reverend Dominic Barrington, Dean of York, said of the proposed sculptures, said: “We are immensely excited by the opportunity to work with Antony Gormley, celebrating the centuries of art that come together to form this extraordinary place.

“The proposed works would offer a contemporary examination of the body, which features prominently throughout York Minster, most famously in the pre-Reformation Kings’ Screen, but will also offer a fresh lens for us to examine the fundamentals of what make us human.”
