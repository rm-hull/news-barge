A curry house says it has increased menu prices by around 15 per cent in recent years, but has refused to cut portion sizes despite mounting pressures on the hospitality sector.

International, on Morley Street, is one of 10 finalists competing to be named Bradford's Best Curry House 2026 in the Telegraph & Argus competition.

The restaurant, which took the title in 2024 and celebrated its 50th anniversary this year, is hoping to reclaim the crown.

Owner Safeer Khan said: "It's always a great recognition for us to be in the top 10 and it shows that even after 50 years we are still being recognised."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21659040.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The restaurant is among the 10 finalists competing to be crowned Bradford's Best Curry House 2026. *(Image: The International Restauarant)*

## 'No compromises' on portion sizes

Mr Khan said rising costs across the industry had forced the restaurant to increase menu prices by around 15 per cent over the past four to five years.

However, he said there had been no reduction in portion sizes.

Mr Khan said: "If you look at the rising costs since Covid and the after-effects, they have probably been a lot higher than 15 per cent.

"But we have kept customers and their rising costs in mind, therefore increasing prices slightly but not so much that it becomes unaffordable.

"At the same time our portion sizes have always been big and there has been no compromises."

He added: "We felt that increasing prices slightly is something customers can understand, as long as quality and quantity are not diminished."

![A curry served at The International Restaurant, a finalist in the Best Curry House 2026 competition.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21659036.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A curry served at The International Restaurant, a finalist in the Best Curry House 2026 competition. *(Image: The International Restauarant)*

## Looking for savings behind the scenes

International said it has looked for ways to reduce costs without compromising standards.

Mr Khan said: "We have had to look at many other suppliers far and beyond and even bulk-buy certain items to keep costs down so we can survive and maintain the standards we have always tried to set."

## Diners feeling the pinch

Mr Khan said the restaurant has noticed customers eating out less frequently than they once did.

"Customers have also been hit hard and we have seen a difference where they are not able to eat out as often as they used to and would like to," he said.

The restaurant said this has increased the importance of attracting new customers through advertising, social media and maintaining its reputation.

![CEO Danny Rayman in the restaurant](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21659039.jpg?type=mds-article-620)CEO Danny Rayman in the restaurant *(Image: The International Restauarant)*

## Giving back to customers

As part of its 50th anniversary celebrations, International offered 50 per cent off meals every Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday throughout June and July.

It has also introduced a series of weekday offers throughout November.

Mr Khan said: "This is something we are always doing to say thank you and also help support customers dealing with rising costs."

He added: "We want to say thank you to all our customers who have voted for us so far to make the top 10 and hope they can continue to vote for us to win the competition."
