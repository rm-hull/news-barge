A Bradford shop is under a closure order following an operation where more than 30 tonnes of fireworks were seized across Bradford and Halifax.

Officers uncovered the haul at sites in the two areas while investigating suspected illegal storage.

They removed fireworks containing more than 5.5 tonnes of explosive material from several locations.

## Big Shotter closure order

The operation began after inspections at two Darley Street premises in Bradford in October.

Fireworks were seized, and the investigation widened to sites across Bradford and Halifax, where officers found more stockpiles.

Bradford Magistrates' Court granted a closure order covering premises on Darley Street used or operated by Big Shotter Fireworks Ltd.

The order has been extended until November 12 and remains in force pending further court proceedings.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21664023.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A photo of a fire engine. *(Image: WYFRS)*

## 'Unprecedented' amount of fireworks

West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service, the Health and Safety Executive and West Yorkshire Police are investigating how the fireworks were stored and whether laws were broken.

Christopher Kemp, senior fire protection manager, said the quantities recovered were "unprecedented" and warned that strict storage laws protect businesses, workers and the public.

The investigation continues, and no outcome or enforcement action has been announced.

## Raids seized goods worth more than £100,000

In the past two weeks, WYFRS, West Yorkshire Police and West Yorkshire Trading Standards have also carried out joint raids across all five West Yorkshire districts, targeting businesses suspected of selling illegal goods or fireworks unlawfully.

The raids seized illegal goods including cigarettes, vapes and unlicensed children's toys worth more than £100,000.

Sareth Humpage, chief superintendent and gold commander for West Yorkshire Police's Bonfire Night operation, said: “The recent days of action demonstrate the value of agencies working together to protect our communities and disrupt the sale of illegal goods.

"I would like to thank colleagues from West Yorkshire Fire and Rescue Service and Trading Standards for their continued support and the work undertaken across all five districts.”
