A County Durham teenage girl who was hit by a train tragically took her own life, a coroner has ruled.

Newton Aycliffe's Ruby Nancy Jacqui Bill, 14, died at Newcastle's Great North Children's Hospital on May 2, ten days after the incident in April.

An inquest at Crook Coroners Court heard how Ruby had experienced difficult circumstances throughout her young life, including the death of her mother in May 2018.

The inquest heard Ruby, who studied at Greenfield Academy, had been referred to Child and Adolescent Mental Health Services (CAMHS) and had been under their care for a period of time.

However, she continued to struggle with her mental health.

At the inquest, her grandmother described Ruby as having a "strong, creative side" and said she was "brilliant at art, something she used as a way to process her thoughts".

She said Ruby had "hopes for the future, despite everything she had gone through".

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20819218.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Greenfield Academy, where Ruby went to school *(Image: GOOGLE)*

Tragically Ruby was involved in an incident on April 22 which saw paramedics, police and the air ambulance called.

Ruby was airlifted to hospital and taken to Newcastle's Great North Children's Hospital, where doctors established she had suffered a "catastrophic brain injury".

She passed away ten days later.

The inquest heard British Transport Police have made a recommendation to Network Rail regarding access to the trackside.

The coroner is issuing a Prevention of Future Deaths report in relation to the matter.

Concluding the inquest, *assistant coroner Dr Leslie Hamilton* found Ruby "deliberately" intended to take her own life.

Her death was formally recorded as suicide.
