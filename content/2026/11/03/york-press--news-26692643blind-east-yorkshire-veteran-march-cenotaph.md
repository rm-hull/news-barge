A BLIND veteran from East Yorkshire will be among those marching at the Cenotaph this Remembrance Sunday.

Peter Graves, 71, from Bridlington, will join more than 40 other people supported by Blind Veterans UK - a national charity for vision-impaired ex-service personnel - at Cenotaph next week.

He began his Army career in 1972 aged just 17, joining the Royal Electrical and Mechanical Engineers (REME) where he served for more than 43 years.

His service took him to Germany, Belize, Northern Ireland during The Troubles, the Falklands War, Rwanda during the genocide, and Kosovo.

Peter said: "I was a young lad looking for adventure. I'd gone down to the selection centre and seen films about places like Aden, Bahrain, Singapore and Hong Kong and thought it all looked brilliant.

"My first choice wasn’t REME at all, but one of the corporals looked at my aptitude test results and told me I’d be wasting my talents. He told me I should join REME and luckily he got me on the right path."

He qualified as a Commando and Paratrooper, became an artificer engineer, and eventually rose to the rank of Major, before being medically discharged from the Army in 2016.

His sight loss began after trauma to his right eye, followed by a diagnosis of retinitis pigmentosa (a degenerative genetic eye condition).

Peter said: "My sight loss happened gradually over a number of years, but I knew it was getting worse. Slowly your confidence and self-esteem start to ebb away because you can't do the things you once took for granted.

"For someone who’d spent a lifetime being independent, that was probably the hardest thing."

He began receiving support from Blind Veterans UK in 2015.

He added: "When I first joined, I imagined Blind Veterans UK would offer tea and sympathy and not much else. I couldn't have been more wrong. The charity taught me how to carry on doing the things I enjoy."

Peter will march with his fellow blind veterans at the Cenotaph on Remembrance Sunday.

He said: "Remembrance is about the people I knew during my career who didn’t make it home. It’s also about those who came home but paid a heavy price through injury or trauma."

Blind Veterans UK chief executive Adrian Bell said: "Every November, we honour those who served our country, remembering their sense of duty, sacrifice and service.

"This Remembrance Sunday, I am proud to march alongside our blind veterans as we remember those who gave their lives in service to our country and reflect on the contribution of all who have served."

Blind Veterans UK supports thousands of blind veterans like Mr Graves and encourages others who may need support to reach out.

Veterans or their families seeking help can call 0800 389 7979 or visit blindveterans.org.uk/apply.
