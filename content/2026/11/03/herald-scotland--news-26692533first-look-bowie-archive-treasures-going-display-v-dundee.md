*(Image: Jane Barlow/Press Association)*

From handwritten song lyrics and designs for album covers to flamboyant stage costumes, they chart the career of one of the most influential musicians of all time.

Now they have been brought together under one roof in Scotland for a major exhibition devoted to the life and work of David Bowie.

Rarely-seen photographs, precious instruments, personal possessions, notes plotting out recording sessions and tours will be on display at V&A Dundee when it opens on Wednesday.

Drawn mainly from the vast Bowie archive held at the V&A in London, the new exhibition offers insights into the origins of classic songs and videos, including Let’s Dance, Modern Love and Ashes to Ashes, and features designs for stage sets and outfits, and recalls the defining eras of Bowie’s career.

The exhibition explores Bowie’s collaborations with the likes of actress Tilda Swinton, designers Freddie Burretti and Kansai Yamamoto, producers Nile Rodgers, Brian Eno and Tony Visconti, and photographers Masayoshi Sukita, Terry O’Neill and Brian Ward.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662039.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Curator Harriet Reed takes a closer look at a costume designed by Natasha Korniloff for David Bowie's Ashes to Ashes video in the new V&A Dundee exhibition devoted to the singer. *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA)*

Harriet Reed, contemporary performance curator at the V&A in London, said the exhibition would offer “a rare chance to step inside Bowie’s imagination and experience the curiosity and daring that defined his life’s work”.

She added: “From fan art and his first instrument to unrealised projects, handwritten notes and unseen sketches, over 100 highlights bring visitors up close to the ideas behind his work, including many objects that have never been displayed before, offering new insight into how his creativity took shape.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662043.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Singer-songwriter Callum Beattie attended a preview of V&A Dundee's new exhibition David Bowie: On Tour. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

Highlights of the exhibition include a focus on Bowie’s sold-out 1973 concert at the Caird Hall in Dundee during his Ziggy Stardust era, which features heavily in the exhibition, which includes the first public display of an acoustic guitar he played at the time.

The show features costumes from the Ziggy Stardust live shows and the Ashes to Ashes video, includes a host of memorabilia linked to Bowie’s foray into films, and recalls the impact of his time two year spell living and recording in Berlin in the late 1970s.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662062.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Former Dundee United player John Reilly was among the first David Bowie fans to get a preview of V&A Dundee's new exhibition devoted to the singer. *(Image: Jane Barlow)*

Objects going on public display for the first time are lyrics and performances notes from Bowie’s final albums The Next Day and Blackstar. Among the oldest objects in the exhibition is Bowie’s first musical instrument, an alto saxophone, which was a gift from his father in 1961.

V&A Dundee curator Svetlana Panova said: “We all know David Bowie from his music videos, his concerts and his stage presence.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662070.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An acoustic guitar played by David Bowie: during his Ziggy Stardust era is among the star attractions in V&A Dundee's new exhibition devoted to the singer. *(Image: Jane Barlow)*

“But this exhibition is such a unique lifting of the curtain on his creative process.

“We can see how he cut-and-pasted his lyrics together, and what a brilliant visual artist he was.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662071.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new David Bowie: On Tour exhibition at V&A Dundee recalls the singer's famous 1973 concert at the city's Caird Hall during his Ziggy Stardust tour. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

“We really trace the relationship between his music, album covers and videos, and see how he thought about all those things together.”

V&A Dundee director Leonie Bell said: “There has been a lot of work between ourselves and our colleagues at the V&A to bring the exhibition to Dundee. There is so much really precious archival material on show, some of which is quite old, such as handwritten and early artworks for album covers.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662100.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 100 objects charting David Bowie's entire career have been brought together for the new exhibition at V&A Dundee. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

“But it is part of the preciousness of the show that it gives you access to stuff that David Bowie held, created and worked on. It is very intimate and moving to look at what is here and acknowledge that he pre-determined our right to have access to all this.

“I’m really blown away by the handwritten lyrics that are in the exhibition. They are often on graph paper, and his handwriting is quite small and is really neat. You sometimes look at them and wonder if it is the first or second draft. There is a real precision to his creativity.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662080.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The new David Bowie: On Tour exhibition at V&A Dundee recalls the singer's famous 1973 concert at the city's Caird Hall during his Ziggy Stardust tour. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

“He had such a compelling command of visual expression, as well as his musical and lyrical ability. Some of the artworks in the exhibition, from the late 1970s and early 1980s, which he has written and drawn on paper, I just find mind-blowing.”

Scottish singer-songwriter Callum Beattie visited the waterfront museum for a preview of the exhibition, which will be running for more than three months in the city before going on tour around England in 2027 and 2028.

He told The Herald: “I’m a massive fan, which my dad made sure of by giving me my middle name after David Bowie.

“He has been an enormous influence on me. There is no better education than immersing yourself in his music – lyrically, melodically, the art of getting away with so many lyrics that are quite out there but still connecting with people.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662102.jpg?type=mds-article-620)More than 100 objects charting David Bowie's entire career have been brought together for the new exhibition at V&A Dundee. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

“I think the exhibition will appeal to anyone who appreciates art and people who push the boundaries of art. You don’t have to be a musician.”

Ms Panova said: “For Bowie’s fans, the exhibition is such an incredible opportunity to see these personal objects of his, but also for people who are really interested in creativity, the creative process and design.

“As Scotland’s design museum, it is really important for us to tell stories like this one. It is not just about one aspect of his career. It looks at all aspects of his creativity, and also shines a lot on the designers, producers and collaborators that made all his ideas possible."

The exhibition’s run in Dundee has inspired the creation of a “Bowietown” campaign to encourage music venues, arts organisations, shops and cafes to stage their own celebrations of the singer.

Highlights include an in-conversation event with guitarist and long-time Bowie collaborator Earl Slick at the Caird Hall, screenings of the Bowie films Labyrinth, The Man Who Fell to Earth and Merry Christmas, Mr Lawrence at the Dundee Contemporary Arts centre, a Bowie-themed bike party around the city and a week of events at city centre venue Canvas.

V&A Dundee has already warned Bowie fans to book in advance for the exhibition, with some sessions already sold out before the show opens.

Ms Bell added: “I’ve been here six years now. Every time we launch and open an exhibition it feels like it generates an amazing atmosphere, but this one feels particularly special. It feels like Dundee has been really wanting, waiting on us to do, this exhibition, since we opened.”
