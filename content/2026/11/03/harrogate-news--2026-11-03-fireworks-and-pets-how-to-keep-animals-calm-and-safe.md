Fireworks displays are a familiar part of autumn and winter, but the sudden bangs and flashes can be frightening for pets. Dogs may bark, hide or try to escape, while cats and smaller animals can also become distressed.

Planning ahead can help reduce anxiety and keep animals safe during the fireworks season.

## Preparing pets for fireworks

There are several practical steps owners can take to make the fireworks period less stressful for their pets:

* Keep pets indoors during fireworks and make sure doors, windows and cat flaps are secure.
* Create a comfortable, safe space where your pet can retreat, with familiar bedding and toys.
* Close curtains to reduce the impact of flashes and sudden bursts of light.
* Use background noise, such as the television or music, to help mask the sound of fireworks.
* Plan dog walks during daylight hours and keep dogs on a lead, as frightened animals may bolt.
* Check that your pet’s microchip details and contact information are up to date.

## Recognising signs of distress

Dogs may show signs of anxiety by panting, pacing, trembling, barking, hiding or seeking reassurance from their owners.

Cats may hide, withdraw or attempt to get outside. Small animals, including rabbits and guinea pigs, can also be affected by loud noises.

Where possible, outdoor hutches and enclosures should be moved to a sheltered area, while ensuring animals have adequate ventilation and remain in familiar surroundings.

Owners should avoid forcing frightened animals out of hiding. Allowing them to remain somewhere they feel safe can help them cope.

## Veterinary advice on helping pets cope with fireworks

> **Marrianne Griffiths, Partner and Co-owner of Medivet Harrogate, said:**
>
> Fireworks as wonderful as they are for us can be a real source of stress and anxiety for our pets. To help reduce their stress levels you can create a warm, safe environment for your pet/s by closing doors, windows, curtains, offering them their favourite blankets, toys, treats, putting the TV on or playing a premade pet friendly sounds playlist. Make sure that you give them plenty of reassurance, staying nice and calm.
>
> You can also use calming aids such as pheromone diffusers or anxiety wraps.
>
> For those that are particularly sensitive to firework noises we can use desensitising techniques where we use firework sounds at an almost imperceptible level monitoring over 30 seconds for any signs of distress or changes to behaviour. If there aren’t any, we give them their favourite treat and slowly increase the volume in increments, rewarding at each stage. Creating a positive experience and association to the noise.
>
> If you know that your pet finds the firework season very stressful then please don’t hesitate to contact your local vets to seek further help and guidance.

For pets experiencing severe or recurring anxiety, owners should speak to their veterinary practice ahead of the fireworks season. Vets can discuss behaviour support and whether any additional treatment may be appropriate.
