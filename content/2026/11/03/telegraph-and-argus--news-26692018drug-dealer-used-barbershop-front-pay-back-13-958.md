A drug dealer who used his house and a barbershop business as a front for selling cannabis, crack cocaine, and heroin will pay back just under £14,000.

In May, Danyaal Hussain, now 28 and formerly of Amberley Street, Bradford, was sentenced to five-and-a-half years in prison.

During a brief hearing at Bradford Crown Court on Monday Mr Recorder David Kelly heard that a Proceeds of Crime order had already been made and that the available figure was £13,958.68.

That is the amount Hussain will pay back via a confiscation order.

Recorder Kelly ordered the forfeiture and destruction of the drugs and mobile phones seized.

Hussain was not in court for the hearing.

## What police found upon arrest

The court previously heard that when police arrested Hussain at his home in Bradford on the morning of December 9 last year the room he was in smelled strongly of cannabis.

Officers found more than two kilos of the drug, valued at more than £16,000, in the house as well as a coded safe inside a wardrobe.

Inside was a pink wallet containing £3,400 in cash.

More cannabis was found in tubs inside a coat on a banister, and in a car.

Bradford Crown Court heard that the barbershop run by Hussain was used as a front for criminal activity.

When the shop was searched on the same day, storage compartments and a black rucksack were discovered revealing 33 wraps of crack cocaine and heroin plus a further half kilo of cannabis valued at £4,000.

The total cash seized from both premises was just over £17,500.

Analysis of phones seized revealed bulk adverts for cannabis as well as messages consistent with Hussain selling controlled drugs and using the business for that.

## Drug dealer jailed

Prosecutor Nathan Davis said that was “indicative of people being instructed to attend the shop in order for them to be provided with drugs.”

The court heard that Hussain had two previous convictions for similar offending for which he had served prison sentences.

Hussain pleaded guilty to three counts of possession of Class A drugs with intent to supply, and possession of criminal property, namely cash.

## 'Income from barbershop was not enough'

Mitigating, Soheil Khan said Hussain was back using cannabis after being released from prison and that it “spiralled from there”.

He added: “Income from the barbershop was not enough for it.”

He described Hussain being “deeply remorseful” and that he was a man “who could be decent”.

He stressed that the preponderance of drug-dealing related to cannabis, and not to Class A drugs.

## 'No stranger to drug-dealing'

Sentencing Hussain, Her Honour Judge Sophie McKone said he was “no stranger to drug-dealing” and that previous prison sentences had not prevented him from peddling drugs.

She said she was satisfied that he used his barbershop “as a cover” for dealing drugs, and that he occupied an operational role.

She added: “Drug dealers like you drain society of much-needed money that should be going elsewhere but is being spent on dealing with people like you and the people that buy drugs from you.”
