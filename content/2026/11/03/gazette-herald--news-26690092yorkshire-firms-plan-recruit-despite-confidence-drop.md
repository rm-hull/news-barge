Business confidence is down in Yorkshire, but many firms still plan to hire and expand.

The latest Business Barometer from Lloyds reported a 10-point drop in confidence for October, bringing the region’s headline figure to 35 per cent, below its 12-month average of 44 per cent.

The dip was largely driven by a 19-point fall in firms’ trading outlook, down to 32 per cent.

Martyn Kendrick, Regional Director for Yorkshire and the Humber at Lloyds, said: "While confidence has fallen this month, Yorkshire businesses continue to see opportunities for growth, supported by strong customer demand and investment in capacity.

"These plans demonstrate the ambition and resilience of businesses across the region, and we’ll continue to support them as they invest, adapt and grow."

Despite this, economic optimism in the region edged up by one point to 39 per cent.

A net balance of 32 per cent of Yorkshire businesses said they expect to increase staff levels in the coming year, though this figure is down 14 points from the previous month.

Growth priorities identified by companies include entering new markets (48 per cent), investing in teams through training (45 per cent), and introducing new products or services (38 per cent).

Nationally, UK business confidence rose six points to 47 per cent in October, above the long-term average of 30 per cent.

Fiamma Morton, Managing Director, SME Banking at Lloyds, said: "There are still challenges, but increasingly the conversation is about what comes next: where new customers will come from, how technology can make the business better, and where investing today might create an opportunity tomorrow.

"There are careful choices to make.

"Cost pressures haven’t gone away, so investment needs to work hard.

"But the ambition is absolutely there.

"Businesses are continuing to find ways to grow, adapt and make their next move."
