A plan to transform a North Yorkshire power station site with a major data centre, delivering investment that could exceed £20 billion, could help secure jobs and boost the digital economy.

The proposal, announced by Drax, Brookfield, and AI infrastructure firm Radiant, aims to develop gigawatt-scale data centre capacity at Drax Power Station in North Yorkshire.

The first phase of the project, backed by £2.5 billion in investment, will deliver up to 100MW of capacity from Q1 2028, with the potential to expand to 500MW by 2031.

Will Gardiner, chief executive of Drax Group, said: "We are proud to be partnering with leading AI infrastructure providers on this project which can help secure the long-term future of the site and protect hundreds of jobs, maintain a critical source of this country’s energy security, support northern growth and enable us to continue delivering for our local communities.

"This country has a unique opportunity to bring together AI leadership, energy infrastructure and industrial renewal to drive national growth."

The partners say the development aligns with the Government’s ambitions for AI sovereignty and reindustrialisation.

If fully realised, the project could become one of the largest data centre sites in the UK, delivering a critical piece of national infrastructure while supporting the transition to an AI-driven economy.

Sikander Rashid, head of AI infrastructure at Brookfield, said: "Teaming up with Drax and Qai for this project represents a major opportunity for our AI infrastructure strategy.

"Our capital and deep operating capabilities will help accelerate the construction of a critical piece of sovereign AI, delivered by Radiant, our UK-headquartered compute infrastructure platform."

Radiant CEO Mahdi Yahya said: "We are proud to expand our UK AI compute footprint with Drax.

"This is a unique opportunity for the UK’s AI ecosystem to build at scale.

"By designing, building and operating from the UK, it will give British researchers, startups and enterprises access to frontier compute at home.

"Drax has powered Britain for more than fifty years.

"Together we can power its AI economy for the next fifty."

The development hinges on planning, regulatory, and network approvals, as well as securing a suitable tenant.

Drax Power Station’s 1,000-acre site offers 4GW of grid access, with 2.6GW of active, dispatchable generation.

A community consultation will begin shortly, and the project partners have committed to a voluntary community benefits fund, separate from planning obligations and Drax’s existing initiatives.

Selby and Kippax MP Keir Mather welcomed the announcement.

Mr Mather said: "As Selby’s MP, I’m supportive of measures to invest in our local economy and drive economic growth across Yorkshire.

"I look forward to working with the Labour Government and with Drax to make sure that these plans will help protect jobs at the power station, deliver a community benefit for local people and help realise our area's full potential."
