VITAL drainage work aimed at making the East Coast Main Line more resilient during wet weather has been completed near York.

Network Rail engineers renewed 300 metres of drainage at Chapman's Bridge near Shipton over two weekends, helping water drain away from the railway more effectively and reducing the risk of flooding and weather-related disruption.

The work was carried out on October 24-25 and October 31-November 1 as part of a wider programme of improvements across North Yorkshire.




Poor drainage can contribute to flooding, damage to track layouts and problems with earthworks, making it an important part of preparing the railway for autumn and winter conditions.

The project forms part of Network Rail's wider investment in drainage across the East Coast route, which includes more than 35,000 drainage assets. The organisation has allocated £19.3 million for drainage renewals and maintenance over the five years to 2029.

Alongside the work at Chapman's Bridge, engineers carried out a series of upgrades between Colton Junction, south of York, and Skelton Junction to the north of the city.

These included installing new track, upgrading several sets of points, which allow trains to change lines, and improvements to overhead power lines.

Vegetation management was also undertaken between Tollerton and Thirsk to improve safety and performance on the route.

At the same time, the Transpennine Route Upgrade programme installed a new road bridge over the railway at Church Fenton and fitted new overhead line equipment designed to support faster electric trains in future.

John Grainger, joint head of performance for Network Rail East Coast and LNER, thanked passengers for their patience while the work was carried out.

He said: "The work at Chapman's Bridge is a very good example of some of the less visible work we do to keep the railway running safely and reliably.

"Effective drainage is really important, especially as we head through autumn and into winter, in helping to keep water away from the railway and reducing the risk of flooding, damage and delays.

"Alongside our other work to prepare for severe weather, improvements like these will help make the railway more resilient and keep passengers moving."
