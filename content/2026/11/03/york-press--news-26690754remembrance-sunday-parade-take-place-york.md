The city is preparing to gather in tribute to its fallen heroes.

York will mark Remembrance Sunday with a parade, service and a series of events to honour those who served and sacrificed in military conflicts.

The parade will begin on Sunday, November 8, departing from Clifford’s Tower at 10.20am and making its way to the City War Memorial in Leeman Road via the Mansion House.

The Rt Hon Lord Mayor of York, Councillor Margaret Wells, said: "Remembrance Sunday is a very special day.

"It brings the city together to pay our respects for those who have fallen, and show our gratitude to those who serve, including reservists and veterans.

"It is an honour to be able to mark this day alongside members of the Armed Forces, residents and visitors, and share our hopes for a peaceful future."

The Lord Mayor and the Civic Party will lead the procession, joined by serving military personnel from regiments across York, invited dignitaries, and representatives from local organisations.

The service will begin at 10.58am with the ‘Ode to the Fallen’ and ‘The Last Post,’ followed by prayers.

Wreaths will be laid by various organisations, led by the Lord Lieutenant, Mrs Johanna Ropner, and the Lord Mayor.

Music will be provided by the Band of the Royal Yorkshire Regiment and the York Railway Institute Band.

Following the service, the parade will return to St Helen’s Square, where the Mansion House flag will be lowered to half-mast.

The Lord Mayor, Civic Party, and guests will salute the parade as it continues along Coney Street.

Councillor Claire Douglas, leader of City of York Council and Armed Forces Champion, said: "This annual service of remembrance carries particular significance in York, a city with a 2,000-year-old military history.

"On Remembrance Sunday and later in the week, on Remembrance Day itself, we will reflect on conflicts past and present, and honour those who have served and fallen, and those who continue to serve.

"We do this as we proudly support our Armed Forces community by working with partners to ensure that those who serve, or have served, are treated fairly and with respect."

A number of road closures will be in place to support the parade.

From 10.10am to 12.30pm, Eye of York, The Castle, Tower Street, Clifford Street, Nessgate, Spurriergate, Coney Street, St Helen’s Square, Lendal, Museum Street, Lendal Bridge and Leeman Road will be closed.

Parking restrictions will apply to Coney Street, Leeman Road, Lendal, Spurriergate and St Helen’s Square from 7am to 1pm.

Castle Car Park will be closed from 7am to 2pm.

A lane closure will also be in effect in Station Avenue from 10am to 12pm.
