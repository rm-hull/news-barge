A controlling ex-police officer sent a video of himself performing a sex act in a police station toilet while on duty.

Ex-PC Joshua Davison was jailed for 16 months in January for controlling behaviour, but details of his behaviour including the lewd clip emerged at a misconduct hearing last month.

Davison, who served with Northumbria Police, was said to be having a "sexualised conversation" while on duty on August 22, 2023 just before sending the video of him performing a sex act on himself.

The misconduct hearing was told the background in the video matched that of police staff toilets.

The footage emerged as detectives investigating his controlling behaviour examined the victim's phone for evidence.

A string of messages including discriminatory language was also found.

The misconduct hearing was told he subjected her to around 18 months of coercive and controlling behaviour during their relationship.

Davison, who worked at Whickham police station in Gateshead, also accessed confidential police records to check on what his former partner, who also worked for the force was doing.

His former partner, referred to as Person A, said he would demand constant updates about her whereabouts and expected her to send videos proving she was alone.

She said he would become angry if she did not respond to his thousands messages, questioned her if she was delayed on her journey home from work and told her to fasten the buttons on her blouse higher.

He would grab her by the arms and shake her and once turned up on a girls' weekend.

During a police interview, Davison accepted that his behaviour towards Person A had not been acceptable.

He said: "I do accept that my role in a lot of the messaging was not great."

He also admitted: "I appreciate that you know there was times where I was quite forceful via text".

The report said he accepted the level of contact he expected from Person A was "ridiculous".

Davison joined Northumbria Police on June 3, 2019, but resigned on October 17, 2025 before conviction.

The misconduct hearing on October 8, found all eight allegations against Davison proved and ruled his behaviour amounted to gross misconduct..

Chief Constable Vanessa Jardine ruled that, had Davison still been a serving officer, the only appropriate outcome would have been dismissal without notice.

The report concluded: "The former officer’s conduct was wholly inconsistent with the standards of behaviour which members of the public expect police officers to observe and maintain."
