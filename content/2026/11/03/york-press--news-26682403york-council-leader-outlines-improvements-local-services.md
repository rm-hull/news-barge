## 'Getting the basics right': How York Council says it is improving services

Claire Douglas and Bob Webb at the Glen Gardens play area

For this month’s column I’m focussing on our work to get the basics right.

There’s been a lot of work behind the scenes to change how we operate as a council. We’re responding to what residents want to see in the areas where they live including making improvements to our roads and pavements, weed clearance, improving travel routes and bus services, and investing in our parks.

We know the council doesn’t always get things right, but we constantly strive to improve how we work and fixing the basics in our communities is a big part of that.

Many council services are performing well and others we’re working to improve. We’ve made a real impact on roads standards, as we’re confident residents will see as they travel around the city. A year after coming into power a third of roads in our communities needed repairs and in just two years, we’ve managed to get this down to one in five. For the city’s key connecting roads, we’ve got this down to under nine per cent for the first time in almost a decade.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21647958.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Claire Douglas at Heworth Green

We know more funding is essential if we’re to tackle all the repairs needed. We’ve successfully argued for more funding meaning this year we’ve agreed the biggest road repairs budget ever, helping to address what we know is always a high resident priority. With more time, we’ll do even more.

Similarly, this council administration’s focus on prioritising our parks and green spaces is seeing long overdue investment where it’s needed. Recent overhauls include Crombie Avenue and Glen Gardens play areas in Clifton and Heworth, with more to come in the next six months.

Having outdoor play is so important to children and young people’s development so it’s only right they have good play facilities near where they live.

The importance of parks, including major parks like Hull Road Park, has been a priority for our Parks Investment Fund.

A mix of updated play equipment and new pathways and fencing has seen over £118,000 spent on the park to date, contributing to its status as York’s newest Green Flag park - awarded only to those parks with the highest standards.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21647957.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Claire Douglas and Bob Webb at the Glen Gardens play area

Residents are likely to have seen the impact of our Neighbourhood Caretaker team, wherever they live in York. The team is making a big difference in our communities whether it be clearing overgrown pathways, cleaning up fly tipped waste, removing weeds or simply cleaning our streets. Residents should contact their ward councillors if they have a job that needs doing.

Our Children’s Services are recognised as ‘outstanding’ following a national Ofsted judgment last year, and we’ve now been given funding to help other councils reach the same high standards.

And more recently, York’s made great improvements in getting children toilet trained, ready for starting primary school. We’ve almost halved the number of children not toilet trained within the space of 12 months through a citywide trial. It didn’t cost huge sums of money and has led to great outcomes for schools and for children, and their readiness to learn.

Also preparing children for the school day and their ability to learn has been the expansion of free school breakfasts across the city, through the York Hungry Minds (YHM) appeal. Generous donations from local businesses, such as Persimmon, means that starting out with just one primary school in 2024, YHM is now feeding children across 13 primary schools. Crucially, this is helping families with their household budgets, improving children’s concentration and leading to better attainment.

The council is the landlord for around 7,500 homes in the city, which is a significant responsibility and one we take seriously for our tenants. It’s really important tenants get timely repairs and we’re working hard to make improvements in this area.

Alongside our existing homes, we’re developing significant plans to build more affordable homes. These plans took a big step forward this month with the appointment of a housing delivery partner, meaning we’ll be able to move at a faster pace to deliver these much needed affordable homes.

The council’s financial position continues to be a major challenge for us all, but we will lead the city through it and take the difficult decisions required to balance the budget. We’ll continue to listen to residents and their priorities and implement those priorities wherever we can.

Our job is never fully done, but we’re committed to getting the basics right, improving services where needed and continuing to ensure our decisions reflect the pride we all have in the incredible city we’re lucky enough to live in.
