A new Lidl supermarket and a 50-bedroom care home could be built under proposals that would see a rugby club relocate to a new site.

The discount retailer has launched a public consultation on plans to redevelop the existing Otley RUFC grounds at Cross Green, creating a new foodstore, care home and associated parking.

Lidl says the scheme would allow the rugby club to move to a new site east of the town, helping to secure its long-term future while maintaining a multi-sport offer for the local community.

The proposals include a new bespoke Lidl store built to the supermarket's latest specification, featuring an in-store bakery, a range of fresh produce, solar panels and electric vehicle charging points.

Alongside the supermarket, a care home of around 50 rooms would be constructed, including facilities such as a bistro and rooftop terrace.

![](https://www.ilkleygazette.co.uk/resources/images/21664303.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A plan of the proposed new Lidl supermarket and care home in Cross Green, Otley *(Image: Lidl)*

According to consultation documents, the development would provide a total of 120 parking spaces for Lidl customers and 27 spaces serving the care home. A further 30 parking spaces would also be made available for the neighbouring Otley Cricket and Squash Club.

Lidl says the plans would include significant tree retention and new landscaping, with planting and green space incorporated throughout the site.

The retailer argues that the redevelopment is tied to the future of Otley RUFC, one of the country's oldest rugby clubs.

Consultation documents state that the club's existing facilities at Cross Green are "out of date, non-compliant, and offer no long-term revenue raising opportunities."

The documents add: "To secure the club's future for the over 550 members including seniors, juniors, and players, it must relocate to provide modern facilities to inspire new and existing players."

Lidl says the sale of the Cross Green site would unlock investment in new rugby facilities, including pitches, a clubhouse, changing rooms, a bar and spectator facilities.

The company claims the scheme would provide a number of benefits, including helping to secure the future of the rugby club, delivering new shopping facilities, creating jobs and providing additional care accommodation for older residents.

A public drop-in consultation event will take place at Otley Rugby Club on Monday, November 16, between 4pm and 7pm, where residents can view the proposals and provide feedback.

People can also comment on the plans online through Lidl's consultation website before a formal planning application is submitted.
