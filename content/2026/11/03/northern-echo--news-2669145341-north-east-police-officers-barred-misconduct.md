More than 40 police officers from four of our forces have been barred from ever serving again in a year.

A total of 41 officers from Durham, Cleveland, Northumbria and North Yorkshire were added to the police barred list from April 2025 to March 2026, according to figures from the College of Policing.

More than half had resigned before they were dismissed, with 23 officers added to the list after leaving policing.

A further 16 were dismissed while still serving and two after retiring.

The figures come as 757 regular officers across England and Wales were added to the barred list in the same year, alongside 25 special constables and 348 police staff.

The number of regular officers barred nationally was more than twice the average for the previous four years, with the College of Policing saying it represented 0.5 per cent of all serving officers.

Northumbria Police recorded the highest number of barred officers among the four North East and North Yorkshire forces, with 22 added during the year.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21282401.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham Police HQ, Ayckley Heads, Durham. *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

This included seven dismissed while serving, 13 after resigning and two after retiring.

Northumbria Police said it had around double the number of officers of its neighbouring forces.

Durham Police had eight officers added to the list, including two dismissed while serving and six after resigning.

Cleveland Police had six officers added, while North Yorkshire Police had five.

A further 18 police staff from the four forces were also added to the barred list.

The figures come amid a series of misconduct cases involving officers from the four forces.

A jury found Lamb, 47, had grabbed his partner tightly by the arms and shoved her back into her home when she tried to get him to leave following a night out. The incident, which took place in September 2023, left the woman with bruises.

He was handed a 12-month community order and a five-year restraining order. He was cleared of a charge of intentional strangulation.

At an accelerated misconduct hearing on November 7, Chief Constable Victoria Fuller ruled his behaviour amounted to gross misconduct, saying domestic violence by a serving officer “significantly harms public trust and confidence”.

The hearing was told she had tried to kiss the officer, pulled his head towards hers and touched his upper thigh or groin area while repeatedly telling him “you know you want to”.

Canning had resigned after an investigation was launched, but the panel concluded she would have been dismissed had she still been serving.

Following the release of the College of Policing data, a Durham Police spokesman said: “Last year, Durham Constabulary announced a reorganisation of our Professional Standards Department to address long-term issues of under investment.

“Those measures include an additional 12 officers and staff, a new dedicated conduct investigation team and more than £200,000 investment into new IT systems to tighten up monitoring.

“Those measures assist us to identify and deal with those officers and staff who fall below the standards of integrity that we and the public rightly expect”.

Northumbria Police referred The Northern Echo to comments from its head of Professional Standards Department, DS Donna Rose, who said the force was determined to remove those who failed to meet expected standards.

She said: “As a force we are determined to be outstanding in the service we deliver – and that starts with all officers, staff and volunteers upholding the high standards rightly expected of us.

“Sadly, as we have seen both nationally and locally, there are times when individuals fail to meet these qualities. In the more serious cases, this can lead to criminal action being taken against those same people who made a commitment to uphold the law.”

DS Rose said the force recognised the impact such incidents could have on communities and added: “We are determined to ensure their efforts are not overshadowed by the actions of those who do not share these same values.”
