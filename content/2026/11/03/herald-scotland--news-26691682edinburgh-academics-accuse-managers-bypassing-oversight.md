**Senior managers at Edinburgh University bypassed statutory requirements to “push through cuts without proper oversight or regulation”, a dossier compiled by 25 leading academics has claimed.**

A 24-page report accuses university leadership of preventing the institution’s academic Senate from “exercising its statutory powers of scrutiny, oversight, and regulation” in deciding to close more than 90 programmes and make £140m in budget cuts.

The document, which has been widely circulated among staff at the university, claims that Senate has been frozen out of academic decision-making processes in what could amount to a breach of “the laws that govern Scottish universities”.

However, a university spokesperson told The Herald that the report contains "a number of inaccuracies".

They did not specify which parts of the dossier are inaccurate.

“This report is about the abuse of governance by the Senior Leadership Team of the University of Edinburgh,” the paper states. “In it, we attempt to document this crisis of governance, to explain how it has unfolded, and to call for its immediate resolution.”

The document goes on to claim that a new “Statement of Primary Responsibilities” drawn up by the university’s governing Court “severely limits Senate’s use of its powers and seems to overturn powers given to Senate by statute”.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21529900.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A five-week strike by the UCU trade union ended on Friday. *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

It argues: “Academic Senate has sought to oversee and regulate substantial changes to the teaching and research taking place at the University of Edinburgh. This is not an aberration but is Senate's fundamental role set out in statute.

“At each point that it has attempted to exercise its powers; the response has been to undermine its regulation and deny its oversight. Indeed, the responses to Senate by the university executive and university Court play no other role than to stop Senate exercising these powers in various ways.

“The laws of Scotland give Senate its powers because academic judgment often requires collective wisdom,” the academics contend. “That collective wisdom comes not only from the leaders of our institutions, but from the whole of the academic staff and student community that compose the university who undertake academic work within it.

“Running a university without this wisdom puts the entire institution at risk.”

The report comes on the heels of five weeks of strike action taken by the University and College Union (UCU) in response to the closure of 90 programmes in the College of Arts, Humanities and Social Sciences.

In mid-September, The Herald reported that the programmes would not enrol new students from 2027, in a decision which managers described as an academic ‘refresh’.

Last week, principal and vice-chancellor Sir Peter Mathieson warned that work to make the university ‘financially resilient’ would continue.

“Urgent, difficult but strategically important decisions need to be taken now,” he wrote in a staff-wide email.

“That is the role of responsible leaders: aiming to safeguard the future as well as taking care of the present.

The current crisis has only contributed to longstanding discord between Senate and the university executive.

The row came to a head last year, leading to the staff-student body passing a motion of no-confidence in Sir Peter’s leadership.

A year later, in May 2026, The Herald revealed how senators attempting to scrutinise restructuring plans were dismissed by university managers, who contended that the proposals were solely administrative and financial in nature, and as such, Senate had “no responsibility or remit to approve the work”.

As the report explains: “The university executive has claimed that the *Academic Size and Shape* project sits outside of Senate’s remit and operates through the principal and Court, despite neither having statutory powers to initiate academic changes such as the merger of departments or the unilateral regulation of degrees.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21540957.jpg?type=mds-article-620)University principal Sir Peter Mathieson. *(Image: Paul Dodds)*

According to the Higher Education Governance (Scotland) Act 2016, from which Senate derives its authority, the academic body is “responsible for the overall planning, co-ordination, development and supervision of the academic work of the institution”.

However, “Edinburgh’s university court has recently limited academic Senate to an extremely narrow role concerning only how students are taught and research undertaken by students,” the academics write.

“It has revoked Senate’s right to make policy on research, limited Senate’s powers of scrutiny to ‘noting’ matters produced by executive committees, and curbed Senate’s powers to make any broader academic policy."

A spokesperson for the University of Edinburgh said: “The University is aware of a document recently produced by some members of University Senate.

"However, the content includes a number of inaccuracies regarding the University’s governance processes.

"The University is committed to adhering fully to its governance frameworks and processes and rejects any suggestion to the contrary.”
