UK Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has defended nurse Sandie Peggie following her employment tribunal defeat against the Royal College of Nursing (RCN), describing the ruling as "disappointing".

Ms Badenoch, who met Ms Peggie during a visit to Edinburgh in January, accused critics of attempting to undermine the NHS Fife nurse and praised her courage in standing up for women's rights.

Her comments follow Monday's judgment dismissing all three of Ms Peggie's claims that the RCN discriminated against her because of her beliefs on sex and gender.

Responding to the ruling, the UK Tory leader said: "Nurses like Sandie should be allowed to do their job caring for patients, not subjected to witch hunts for defending their basic rights to privacy and dignity.

![Kemi Badenoch in Edinburgh](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21587476.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kemi Badenoch in Edinburgh *(Image: Jeff J Mitchell/Getty)*

She added: "This ruling on her union's failures to support her (not about single-sex spaces where she has already won) is disappointing but takes nothing away from Sandie's courage in standing up not just for herself but for women's rights.

"The usual suspects will twist the outcome to attack her after what she's been through but she is braver and better than them.

"Either way, the next Conservative government will put a stop to this nonsense. The NHS should support its nurses, and regulators should focus on what actually matters: professional standards and patient safety not silly fads."

Ms Badenoch previously met the Fife nurse in Edinburgh on 15 January 2026, describing her as an "admirable person who has been through hell".

Following that meeting, the Conservative leader accused authorities of subjecting Ms Peggie to "bureaucratic oppression" and called for updated government guidance on single-sex spaces.

Sandie Peggie's legal battle began after she objected to sharing a female changing room with transgender doctor Dr Beth Upton at Victoria Hospital in Kirkcaldy in December 2023.

She subsequently brought proceedings against NHS Fife and Dr Upton, securing a partial victory in December 2025 when a tribunal upheld harassment complaints against the health board. Other discrimination claims were dismissed, along with all claims against Dr Upton.

Her separate case against the RCN concerned allegations that the union failed to advise her properly, refused legal assistance and did not seek sufficient assurances about single-sex workplace facilities.

Employment Judge Melanie Sangster dismissed all three complaints, finding that the union's actions were not influenced by Ms Peggie's protected beliefs.

The RCN welcomed the decision, saying its staff had followed agreed procedures and acted fairly and impartially.

A spokesperson for Ms Peggie said the ruling raised "serious questions about the value of trade union membership".

Her legal team is considering whether further legal action is possible.
