The fascinating history of Amanda Owen's remote Yorkshire Dales farmhouse is set to be explored further after Channel 4 confirmed two new series of Our Farm Next Door.

The Yorkshire Shepherdess and her family have spent years restoring Anty John's, a historic farmhouse in Swaledale, North Yorkshire, which had fallen into disrepair.

Their efforts have been documented in More4's Our Farm Next Door: Amanda, Clive and Kids, which follows the family as they bring the property back to life while continuing their farming activities at Ravenseat.

The new episodes will explore the life of Anthony Clarkson, a 19th-century Swaledale mapmaker whose diaries provide an insight into the lives of people who occupied the area almost 200 years ago.

According to the broadcaster, revelations from Clarkson's diaries will continue to reveal parallels between his experiences and those of the Owen family today.

## Where is Anty John's farmhouse?

Anty John's is situated in Swaledale, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, close to the Owen family's famous Ravenseat Farm.

The property occupies a remote rural setting surrounded by the moorland and hills that have become familiar to viewers of Our Yorkshire Farm.

Its location has provided both opportunities and challenges for the restoration, with the family undertaking major building work while dealing with the demands of farming and the changing seasons.

## How old is Anty John's?

Anty John's is a historic farmhouse with a past stretching back centuries.

The building had fallen into disrepair before Amanda, former husband Clive Owen and their nine children began their ambitious renovation project.

The restoration became the focus of Our Farm Next Door when the programme launched in 2024.

The family have worked alongside local builders and tradespeople to repair the property while preserving its historic character.

Although the programme has explored the building's past, an exact construction date has not been established in the latest Channel 4 announcement.

## Who was Anthony Clarkson?

Anthony Clarkson was a 19th-century Swaledale mapmaker who previously owned Anty John's.

His connection to the property has become an important part of Amanda's efforts to understand the farmhouse's history.

Channel 4 has confirmed that the fifth and sixth series of Our Farm Next Door will continue exploring Clarkson's life through his diaries.

The broadcaster says revelations from the documents will draw parallels with the Owen family's experiences almost two centuries later.

The historical research adds another dimension to the programme, which combines the restoration of an old building with an exploration of the people who lived and worked in Swaledale in earlier generations.

## How has Amanda Owen restored Anty John's?

The restoration has taken place over several years, with Amanda and Clive working alongside their nine children and local tradespeople.

The project has involved extensive repairs and improvements to make the neglected building suitable for family life.

Previous episodes have documented work on the exterior and interior, including painting, flooring and the creation of individual rooms.

The fourth-series finale, scheduled for November 2, marks an important milestone as Amanda and her daughters prepare for their first overnight stay at Anty John's.

According to the programme synopsis, it will be the first time anyone has slept at the farmhouse in many decades.

The family also moves furniture collected by Amanda over the years into the building as the interior begins to take shape.

## What will happen to Anty John's next?

Channel 4 has confirmed that the farmhouse restoration will continue in two further series of Our Farm Next Door.

Leeds-based Wise Owl Films has been commissioned to produce another ten hour-long episodes, split into series five and six.

The fifth series will pick up immediately after the family's first sleepover, following their lives from early summer 2026 through to spring 2027.

Alongside the historical discoveries, viewers will see the Owen children taking on more responsibilities at Ravenseat.

Edith, Violet and their sisters will reopen the cream tea van, while Sidney and Miles become increasingly involved in farming activities.

James Knight, creative director at Wise Owl Films, said the history of Anty John's was one of the elements that made the programme distinctive.
