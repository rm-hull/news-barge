Plans to reconnect Harrogate with its historic identity as a spa town will be discussed at a joint meeting of the Hookstone and Stonefall Action Group (HASAG) and Woodlands Community Garden group later this month.

The meeting will take place on Thursday 26 November at 7pm at Woodlands Methodist Church Hall on Wetherby Road, Harrogate.

Guest speaker George Eglese will give an illustrated talk entitled **Taking the Cure Again: Harrogate’s Spa Renaissance**, exploring how the town’s historic mineral springs, landscapes, buildings and cultural traditions could play a renewed role in its future.

## George Eglese’s vision for Harrogate as a living spa town

Eglese is the founder of the newly established Harrogate Spa Society, a Community Interest Company created to explore how Harrogate can build on its historic reputation as one of Europe’s notable spa destinations.

A Harrogate-based designer and creative director, Eglese has spent several years researching the town’s spa heritage and developing creative projects and collaborations with local organisations and individuals. His work has also involved visiting historic spa towns across Britain and Europe to explore how their traditions, landscapes and distinctive cultures are being renewed for contemporary life.

The Society’s approach is based on the idea that Harrogate’s spa heritage was never simply about its mineral waters. It also involved gardens, woodlands, architecture, health, entertainment, culture and the wider landscape surrounding the town.

Its ambition is to bring these elements back into conversation and explore how they could contribute to Harrogate’s identity and public life today.

## Three initiatives at the heart of the Spa Society

The Society has outlined three principal initiatives: Harrogate Cure, Spa Commons and Harrogate Wildbelt.

**Harrogate Cure** is its public-facing cultural programme. A new season of activities is being developed for 2027, bringing together art, music, nature, movement and discovery. The intention is to revive the spirit of experimentation and cultural exchange associated with Harrogate’s historic spa tradition, interpreting the idea of the cure for contemporary life.**Spa Commons** will explore the restoration and renewed use of Harrogate’s historic spa buildings, mineral springs, gardens and public spaces. Among the Society’s early priorities is exploring a new public role for Crescent Gardens Pavilion, alongside researching and mapping the town’s mineral springs and developing an archive of its spa history and culture.**Harrogate Wildbelt** is a wider landscape vision intended to connect the town’s rivers, valleys, woodlands and green corridors. It aims to consider Harrogate’s natural environment as a connected network, supporting nature recovery, public access and long-term community stewardship.

## Building on earlier work across Harrogate

The Spa Society brings together several strands of work Eglese has been developing around Harrogate.

One example is **Pinewoods Revealed**, a project led by his Harrogate design practice, Fontis, which explored the historic relationship between Pinewoods and the town’s spa heritage.

The research highlighted the idea of the *Kurwald* – a German term for a woodland associated with a spa town, where walking, fresh air and time in nature formed part of the wider experience of health and recovery.

The project traced the history of Pinewoods, including the planting of the King’s Plantation in 1796 and the subsequent development of the woodland as part of Harrogate’s wider spa landscape. It also worked with Pinewoods Conservation Group to create a new identity and website for the woodland, bringing together its history, conservation work and natural environment.

Eglese has also been involved in developing the Harrogate Wildbelt vision, including work around Crimple Commons and the possibility of a connected greenway through the Crimple Valley. That initiative seeks to bring parts of the landscape into longer-term community stewardship while improving ecological connections and public access.

These projects reflect a common theme: looking beyond individual buildings or green spaces and considering how Harrogate’s heritage, natural environment and public spaces fit together.

## 400 years of Harrogate’s spa history

The launch of the Harrogate Spa Society also comes 400 years after the publication of Edmund Deane’s *Spadacrene Anglica*, or the English Spa Fountain, in 1626. The book documented Harrogate’s mineral waters and helped establish the town’s reputation as a spa destination.

The Society sees this history as an opportunity to look forward as well as preserve the past, exploring how the traditions that helped shape Harrogate could have renewed relevance for residents and visitors.

Its early priorities include building an archive, researching the mineral springs, developing the 2027 cultural programme and establishing relationships with cultural organisations, environmental groups and other spa towns in Britain and Europe.

These ambitions are at an early stage, and the Society has not yet set out a detailed delivery timetable for its wider proposals.

## Community groups invited to attend

The presentation will introduce the Society’s ambitions and explore how its work could connect with local community groups, particularly those involved in gardens, woodlands and the town’s green spaces.

The joint meeting will be followed by separate AGMs for HASAG and Woodlands Community Garden group.

Organisers are welcoming new ideas for the woods and park and invite anyone interested in the future of the area to attend.
