THERE have been some highs on the field for the Bantams over the last two decades.

Reaching the League Cup final in 2013, seeing off Chelsea and Sunderland on way to the FA Cup quarter-finals in 2015, Sarcevic 90+6 against Fleetwood etc.

But those good times on the field have largely been in short supply, which is not an accusation that could be levelled at the Geoffrey Richmond era.

The former Bantams chairman, who died a few days ago at the age of 85, oversaw City’s rise from third-tier also-rans to the Premier League’s promised land.

And in memory of such a colourful character, we thought we’d look back at some of the best games from that era.

**BLACKPOOL 0 CITY 3 (May 1996)**

It would be easy to just focus on the Division Two play-off final itself, but then that was rather routine, City cruising to a 2-0 win over Notts County thanks to goals from Des Hamilton and Mark Stallard.

But it was a minor miracle that Chris Kamara’s side had even reached Wembley in the first place, after a disastrous 2-0 semi-final first leg defeat at home to Blackpool.

Given little chance heading over the Pennines for the return fixture, the Bantams defied the odds with an amazing performance.

After Carl Shutt halved the aggregate deficit, second-half strikes by Hamilton and Stallard sealed the deal at a stunned Bloomfield Road.

![Geoffrey Richmond.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/11499133.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Geoffrey Richmond enjoys Division Two play-off glory with Chris Kamara in 1996, with the final against Notts County a walk in the park compared to the two-legged semi with Blackpool. *(Image: NQ Staff)*

**CITY 3 QPR 0 (May 1997)**

The Bantams enjoyed an eye-opening FA Cup win at Everton at the start of this calendar year, a game which saw Mark Schwarzer really make his name in England and Chris Waddle score an absolutely ridiculous goal from miles out.

But in the nitty-gritty of the league, the upward trajectory Richmond and Kamara had put City on was already in danger.

They went into the final game of the 1996-97 Division One season needing to match Grimsby’s result to survive.

Who knows what would have happened to Richmond’s grand project had City gone down at the first attempt?

Thankfully, that did not come to pass, as the outstanding Nigel Pepper scored twice, including a sensational free-kick, to see City to a 3-0 home win and survival.

**WOLVES 2 CITY 3 (May 1999)**

Richmond and Kamara’s relationship ultimately turned sour and the latter departed in January 1998.

But the chairman pulled off a masterstroke by appointing the club’s former star striker Paul Jewell, hitherto untested as a manager.

Jewell had been on Kamara’s coaching staff though, so he knew what the club and team were all about.

In Jewell’s first full season in charge at City, Ipswich were looking set to claim the second automatic promotion spot behind runaway champions Sunderland.

But in late April, City won away to QPR while the Tractor Boys crashed to a shock defeat against Crewe at Portman Road.

Suddenly, City were in the box seat, but they still had to go and win at a good Wolves side on the last day.

In a brilliant game, the hosts went ahead through Havard Flo, before Peter Beagrie, Lee Mills and Robbie Blake looked to have sealed the deal.

Yet Beagrie missed a penalty and Paul Simpson pulled a goal back to leave nerves jangling.

Simpson hit the post late on when a few inches the other side would have dumped City into the play-offs.

But the woodwork saved the day, Richmond achieving what he had set out to do in 1994, get the Bantams into the Premier League within five years.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21661639.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Geoffrey Richmond signs autographs for young fans in Centenary Square after City's win over Wolves sealed promotion. *(Image: NQ Staff)*

**MIDDLESBROUGH 0 CITY 1 (August 1999)**

An ageing Bantams side were not rated by many fans or pundits once they were up in the Premier League, not least Rodney Marsh.

But they showed they could be competitive instantly, with an excellent opening day win at Middlesbrough.

Andy O’Brien marked the mercurial Paul Gascoigne out of the game, while fellow defenders John Dreyer and David Wetherall also excelled.

And with favourites Middlesbrough getting frustrated they were further stunned at the death, as Mills put debutant Dean Saunders through to score a last-gasp winner.

He celebrated by pretending to carry a walking stick, as ‘Dad’s Army’ showed experience still mattered.

**CITY 1 LIVERPOOL 0 (May 2000)**

While the Bantams proved plenty of people wrong, they still looked likely to go down heading into the final day.

Visitors Liverpool were chasing a Champions League place and started with Steven Gerrard, Michael Owen and Emile Heskey, all of whom would score in England’s famous 5-1 win in Germany less than 18 months later.

City had to better Wimbledon’s result to stay up and Wetherall’s thumping header early on allowed that dream to become reality.

Owen was miraculously kept out by both Matt Clarke and Gunnar Halle and while City had chances to score a second, they had to defend for their lives to keep Liverpool out.

But a bouncing Valley Parade helped them over the line, and with Wimbledon losing to Southampton, the Bantams had survived against the odds.
