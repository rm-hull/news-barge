One of York’s most distinctive medieval timber-framed buildings could soon reopen as a shop with an upstairs bar and a hidden courtyard beer garden.

Plans have gone in to transform Grade II-listed 5 Colliergate, which has stood empty since long-running electrical shop Blackwell & Denton vacated the premises in 2022.

The building, dating back to the late 16th century, sits opposite Barnitts and backing onto Shambles.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/5-colliergate-york-wk.webp)

Last November, the property was briefly brought back to life recently as a film set for the festive comedy movie *Merry Christmas Aubrey Flint*, complete with a false store front dubbed “The Miniature World of Wonders”.* Celebrity Traitors* star Richard E Grant was spotted filming inside.

More generally, however, the property has spent the best part of four years waiting for a permanent tenant.

According to planning documents, a “well-known local retailer” now wants to bring the property back into active use.

As a result, landlord York Conservation Trust has submitted a full planning application to City of York Council to convert the premises into a retail store on the ground floor, paired with a first-floor bar with an outdoor beer garden.

“This calls for the retention of the ground floor as a retailing space, whilst converting the first-floor area and rear yard into a bar with outdoor seating.

“Storage space for beverages and retail produce are required, and there is a desire to ensure inclusive access throughout the property,” planning documents state.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/5-colliergate-york-pd-2.webp)

Under the scheme, the ground floor would feature a shop accessed through the historic nail-studded arched doorway.

A new corridor arrangement would lead shoppers and visitors towards the rear.

Upstairs, the building’s impressive 16th-century timber-framed hall would become a dedicated bar seating area, revealing ancient roof trusses, rafters, and exposed wooden framing that have been concealed or hemmed in by 20th-century partitions.

The adjoining 18th-century brick rear range, once used as a cattle shed when livestock was driven behind Shambles, would house the main bar counter and extra seating.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/5-colliergate-york-pd-6.webp)

Outside, the neglected enclosed rear yard, which directly borders onto The York Ghost Merchants on Shambles, is set for a major transformation.

A modern plastic and felt roof covering would be pulled down to create an open-air beer garden fitted with raised perimeter bench seating.

To make it accessible to all, the applicants plan to install a passenger lift inside a contemporary link building that connects the front and rear blocks. It would feature a zinc roof and cladding.

There would also be level ramps and lowered flooring.

The aim is to make it the only non-museum medieval building in York city centre with step-free wheelchair access to its upper floor.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/5-colliergate-york-pd-5.webp)
![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/5-colliergate-york-pd-4.webp)

The site has enjoyed a colourful past. Beyond its butcher and cattle yard origins, it was occupied by blacksmith James Dixon in the 1840s, a hairdresser at the turn of the century, and florists Mollie Coates in the 1950s and 60s.

Blackwell & Denton, founded in 1973 by Bill Blackwell and Mike Denton, began trading from the address in 1981, becoming a household name in York for vacuum cleaner sales and domestic repairs before leaving four decades later.

The applicants argue the minor alterations to later 20th-century partition work are far outweighed by the public benefit of securing the future of an empty landmark and opening its ancient timber hall to locals and visitors alike.
