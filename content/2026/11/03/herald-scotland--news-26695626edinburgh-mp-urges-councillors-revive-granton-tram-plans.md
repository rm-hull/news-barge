**An Edinburgh MP has urged councillors to revive plans to extend the capital’s tram line to Granton via the Roseburn Path.**

In September, members of the Transport and Environment Committee voted to mothball the proposals, instead choosing to prioritise efforts to expand the tramline south towards the Edinburgh BioQuarter.

Now, Edinburgh North and Leith MP Tracy Gilbert has joined forces with campaign group Looking For Growth to call for a rethink.

The group's Edinburgh branch is asking councillors to propose a motion declaring the northern tramline as an ‘urgent matter’ ahead of a full council meeting on December 10.

They claim that this would allow the issue to be discussed at the meeting, despite councillors voting to pause the plans in September.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21666214.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Members of Looking For Growth's Edinburgh chapter. *(Image: Supplied)*

“The tram to the north is the key to unlocking growth for communities who need it most,” said Ms Gilbert. “Politicians should make decisions that benefit all our citizens, particularly the most left behind.”

Two months ago, SNP, Labour and Green councillors voted to back the city’s planned southern tram extension to the Royal Infirmary over the northern route.

While committee convener Stephen Jenkinson told colleagues that he supported a northern tram extension, he acknowledged that proposals to build the route along the Roseburn Path or Dean Bridge had not mustered enough support from councillors.

“My vision is for the full north-south route, from north to south via the Roseburn Path, including where possible the South Sub rail line,” he said at the time. “We need to be bold and have a vision.

“One of the difficult decisions to take is when you can’t get your own way and you need to compromise. You have to be mindful of the political makeup of this council and what the art of the possible is.”

Two months later, Looking For Growth hopes councillors will change their minds.

"It is embarrassing that Scotland’s capital city allows its long-planned mass transit network to be consistently delayed,” said Calum McCutcheon, the captain of Looking For Growth’s Edinburgh chapter.

“The wealth to build this tram is there, we just need the political will to deliver it.”

Mr McCutcheon added: “In fact, it is a shame that this argument has been framed as cycle path vs tram, because the cycle path would remain and it is the very same inertia and deference to any opposition that has delayed both the tram to Granton and the completion of cycling infrastructure in Edinburgh, like on George Street.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21666215.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Calum McCutcheon of Looking For Growth. *(Image: Supplied)*

A form letter which Edinburgh residents have been urged to send to councillors contends that a “small, organised group of residents are trying to block the trams”, an apparent reference to Save the Roseburn Path, which has campaigned to retain the former rail line as a primary walking and cycling route.

The letter goes on to state: “You have the power to submit a motion asking that the tram be declared an urgent matter at the next full council meeting.

“Will you publicly commit to submitting the motion and pushing the council to approve the entire Edinburgh tram route?”

The Herald understands that Looking for Growth will argue that the Transport and Environment Committee considered the business case for the tram extension with erroneous information relating to popularity and deliverability.

According to section 29 of City of Edinburgh Council’s standing orders, decisions can only be changed within six months if “the Lord Provost or convener rules there has been a material change of circumstances; or the council or committee agrees the decision was based on erroneous, incorrect or incomplete information.”

It is expected that any attempt to reverse the decision would be met with staunch opposition from the Liberal Democrats, who have previously led efforts to explicitly rule out the inclusion of the Roseburn Path in any future route.

“This was one of the biggest consultations in the history of City of Edinburgh Council. What did people tell us?” group leader Kevin Lang asked during the meeting in September.

“68% of people in the consultation told us they opposed using the Roseburn Path. We are supposed to be a local authority which cherishes green spaces and safe spaces for walking, wheeling, and cycling.”

Meanwhile, councillor Neil Cuthbert said the “unfunded, unsupported, and objected to” extension was “not going anywhere".

“At some point this committee must be honest with residents about what is deliverable and what is not,” he added.
