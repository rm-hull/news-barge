A YORK academic whose driving caused a woman in an oncoming car to suffer 'grave' injuries has been sentenced.

Dimitar Kazakov was driving a Kia Sportage when it swerved across the A19 south of York into the path of a Fiat 500 at 5am on April 14, 2026.

The 59-year-old, of Milton Street, off Lawrence Street, York, was sentenced at York Magistrates' Court on Monday, November 2, where he was ordered to undertake 200 hours of community work.

He was also disqualified from driving for 12 months.

The long-serving University of York don had pleaded guilty to causing serious injury by careless / inconsiderate driving at an earlier hearing which was adjourned for pre-sentence reports to be prepared.

Ryan Thornton, prosecuting, told the court the police had been called to reports of a crash by the White Swan Hotel on the A19 at Deighton.

The Fiat driver suffered serious injuries after Kazakov drove a white Kia Sportage "on the wrong side of the carriageway" while travelling northbound on the A19, causing the Fiat to spin and end up in a bush, said Mr Thornton.

Kazakov said in a statement that he remembered 'glancing at the speedometer' before the crash, added Mr Thornton.

He said the defendant's 'unsafe manoeuvre or positioning' had caused the victim to suffer 'grave' and life-changing injuries, including a fractured spine and five fractured ribs.

Mr Thornton said the spinal fracture had healed 'satisfactorily' but she still struggled with some movements while psychological support and CBT had helped her to overcome her anxiety about driving.

In mitigation, defence solicitor Colin Byrne said Kazakov had been driving in the early hours after flying from Bulgaria to an airport in the south of England.

He had taken rest breaks and swapped drivers on the journey but "his concentration was on making sure he was not speeding, causing him to be unaware he was swerving across the road".

He said Kazakov had remained at the scene and assisted, and expressed genuine remorse to the probation service.

Mr Byrne said his client was previously of positive good character, with six character references provided to the court along with documents about his professional and personal circumstances.

He was a 'respected and trusted' colleague at the University of York where he is an associate professor and AI specialist.

Mr Byrne said he had taught thousands of students; mentored more than 100 and taken a number of students through their PhDs.

He had also published numerous research articles, and volunteered in schools.

As a husband and father, his family depended on him for emotional and financial support and he also personally paid for the care of his elderly mother, he added.

Linda Goddard, presiding magistrate, told Kazakov that the community order – reduced from a possible 300 hours – had taken into account his early guilty plea, remorse, previous good character, and the personal references.

He was also ordered to pay a surcharge of £154 and costs of £85.

She said there would be no order for compensation as this would be dealt with as a civil matter through Kazakov's insurance company.
