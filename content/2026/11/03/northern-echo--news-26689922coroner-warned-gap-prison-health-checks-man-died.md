A coroner has warned that a gap in prison health checks could put lives at risk after a 23-year-old man died days after leaving custody.

Francis Conlin was found dead in Middlesbrough on October 7, 2024, seven days after leaving HMP Doncaster.

An inquest into his death concluded he died by a method of suicide "at a time when he had consumed” substances and so his intention could not be established.

Teesside Area Coroner Paul Appleton said a national NHS template used in pre-release checks has no specific questions about prisoners’ mental health and that nurses were expected to assess mental health, but warned missing prompts could leave a dangerous gap.

He said: “I am concerned about the absence of [these] prompt/questions.”

In his report, dated August 17, Mr Appleton warned of a “significant risk of future deaths” unless the gap was addressed.

But NHS England and HMP Doncaster healthcare provider Practice Plus Group have now responded to the coroner's concerns.

NHS England said another national document already contains mental health questions.

It said: "This is the template that should have been used in the release planning process for Francis Conlin."

Practice Plus Group said nurses had been instructed to ask additional questions about a prisoner's current mental state, prescribed mental health medication and whether they knew how to access a GP and obtain medication after release.

The healthcare provider said: "The templates will be audited monthly to ensure compliance with the above steps, or until such a time that the template is updated by NHS England."

It is also updating its local operating procedure and preparing a summary sheet of questions for nurses.

Practice Plus Group said the concerns raised by the coroner had been escalated to NHS England's national team, but it had received no further update on changes to the national template.

NHS England added that all Prevention of Future Deaths reports on health and justice were shared with commissioning teams through its learning-from-deaths work.

It said: “This ensures that key learnings and insights around events, such as the sad death of Francis, are shared across the NHS at both a national and regional level.”

The inquest heard that Conlin had mental and behavioural disorders linked to illicit substance misuse.
