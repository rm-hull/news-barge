A NEW health hub is bringing vital services directly to families where they live and learn.

The School Health Hub, launched by Bradford-based Priestley Academy Trust in partnership with the Education Alliance for Life Chances (EALC), is now operating across six primary schools in the Manningham area.

It aims to improve access to care and reduce health inequalities in the community.

Mathew Atkinson, CEO of The Priestley Academy Trust, said: "With school attendance, delays in SEND referrals, and youth mental health dominating national headlines, standard referral pathways are leaving too many families behind.

"In our school communities, families already face a high level of health inequality so through our partnership with EALC, we have launched a Health Hub which provides targeted, evidence-based interventions that can make a clear difference right on their doorstep.

"For our families, rather than struggling to navigate complex external systems, care now comes to them in a trusted, familiar setting at their local school – and for us as education providers, healthcare issues can be tackled before they escalate into attendance crises.

"The overarching goal of the initiative is to connect health, education, and social care in a way that breaks down systemic silos – a mission deeply tied to the core values of The Priestley Academy Trust."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21101259.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Priestley Connects event in June 2026 *(Image: Supplied)*

Backed by joint funding from the NHS and social care, the hub delivers healthcare, family support, and community services in a single, accessible location.

It seeks to remove barriers such as transport, language, and mistrust of clinical environments.

The hub is funded for one year through the Bradford Children & Families Trust’s Families First Prevention Grant, with hopes of demonstrating its value nationally to secure longer-term support.

This scheme follows two successful pilot sessions under the trust’s Priestley Connects programme, which provided free, holistic support to families and influenced the development of the health hub model.

The EALC has published a guide, Starting a School Health Hub, to help other trusts and education leaders replicate the approach.

The guide offers practical advice on forming multi-agency teams and setting up data-sharing agreements.

Kathryn Loftus, director of EALC, said: "Our work to date in Bradford has shown the value of providing health services to families in a safe space.

"If we can bring health, early help, SEND support and community services into schools, rather than families having to find their own way to them, help arrives earlier, more easily, and in ways that feel safe rather than stigmatising."
