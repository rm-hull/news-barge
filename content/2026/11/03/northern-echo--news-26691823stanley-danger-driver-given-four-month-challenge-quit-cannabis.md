A repeat dangerous driver has four months to quit drugs or face his first prison sentence.

Kaine Kenning appeared at Durham Crown Court for sentence after admitting dangerous driving, driving while disqualified and uninsured, refusing to provide a blood sample and fraudulently using a registration plate.

He was spotted driving a Ford Transit van with a false number plate on Maiden Law Bank, Lanchester, shortly after 12.40pm on Thursday, June 4.

The court heard the van had been reported stolen in the Northumbria Police area weeks earlier.

Kenning refused to stop and sped away to evade a police “box” stop, colliding with two police vehicles in the process.

Prosecutor Jennifer Coxon said Kenning reached 90mph on roads with 30-limits, crossed the centre line and forced oncoming drivers to swerve before taking a roundabout the wrong way.

The 5.5-mile pursuit ended when Kenning and a female passenger abandoned the van and went into a house in Broom Hill, Stanley, where he was arrested.

He failed a roadside drug wipe and refused to provide a blood sample at the police station, saying he disliked needles and adding: “What’s the point?” before stating he had smoked cannabis the previous night.

The court heard that weeks after the June 4 chase, 23-year-old Kenning, of Broom Hill, was banned from driving for a year and given a suspended prison sentence for dangerous driving in a later incident.

Kenning’s barrister, Calum McNicholas, said the defendant “clearly has an addiction issue” and was trying to resolve it.

But Mr McNicholas said the defendant had otherwise responded well to the community part of his suspended sentence, attending every appointment and maintaining contact with the Probation Service.

Mr McNicholas said Kenning had also found work as a labourer and has stable accommodation with his mother.

He urged Judge Nathan Adams not to jail Kenning to enable him to continue making progress under Probation Service supervision.

Judge Adams told Mr McNicholas: “If I’m to consider not sending him to prison his drug use must stop."

He said Kenning would have received an immediate prison sentence if the two June dangerous driving cases had been dealt with together.

He ordered Kenning to return for sentence on March 8.
