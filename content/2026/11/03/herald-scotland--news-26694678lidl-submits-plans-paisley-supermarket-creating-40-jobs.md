A supermarket could be built on a plot of land in Paisley with a deep history behind it – if plans are given the green light by Renfrewshire Council.

Lidl has submitted a request for planning permission as it hopes to open a store at the site of the demolished St James Business Centre in Linwood Road.

The building – once an administration block for the plant where the Hillman Imp was manufactured in the 1960s and 1970s – was knocked down last year.

The location could be repurposed in a move that it is claimed would create 40 new jobs, should the application be approved by the local authority.

A planning statement, prepared by agent Rapleys LLP, said: “The proposal, located on Linwood Road, Paisley, will provide a new and modern discount food store for the residents in the locality.

“The proposal will improve customer choice and enhance the shopping experience for shoppers in Linwood and the neighbouring areas, and will also promote sustainable travel patterns.

“The proposals have demonstrated through a sequential site assessment that there are no alternative sites available to accommodate the broad type of development proposed.

“In addition, through a retail impact assessment, it has also been demonstrated that the proposal will not adversely impact any defined retail centres.

“The proposed building will provide a contemporary design that will complement the visual character of the surrounding area.

“There will be a positive economic impact as a result of new employment and increased earnings in the local area.”

If given the go ahead, it will pave the way for the construction of a single-storey food store, customer car park with 116 spaces and “high-quality scheme” of landscaping.

Touching on the potential economic benefits, the statement added: “It should be acknowledged that a typical Lidl food store will provide around 40 new job positions, together with a multi-million-pound investment into the area, delivering a significant boost to the local economy.”

The local authority has notified neighbours of the site about the plans via letter and invited them to comment.

A decision on the application, which is expected to be made by the planning and climate change policy board, will be confirmed at a later date.
