ASDA has named David Potts, the former chief executive of Morrisons, as its new CEO.

The Leeds-based supermarket chain announced the appointment on November 3, with Mr Potts taking up the role immediately.

Allan Leighton, who has been leading the business, will become non-executive chairman.

Mr Leighton said: "Over the last two years, we have stabilised the business and returned it to some growth and begun to establish Asda as 'more than a supermarket' through our Formula for Growth strategy.

"David has an outstanding record in our industry, his experience is exactly what we now require for the next stage of our transformation and beyond and it will be great to work with him."

Mr Potts led Morrisons for eight years until 2023, guiding the retailer through a turnaround and a subsequent takeover by US private equity firm Clayton, Dubilier & Rice.

He previously held senior roles at Tesco, including chief executive of the company’s Irish, UK, and Asian operations.

Asda said Mr Leighton’s move was planned, following his return in 2024 to lead the business more than 20 years after his previous tenure as chief executive.

He rejoined as the retailer, owned by TDR Capital and the Issa brothers since 2020, struggled with falling sales and declining market share.

Mr Potts said: "By making improvements to the shopping trip every week, which customers can experience and feel in their pockets, we will revitalise the business and bring benefits for all our stakeholders, including our 116,296 very hard-working colleagues.

"Christmas is upon us and the business has a clear focus on keeping Asda prices locked low at this important time for our customers."

Asda has been cutting prices to regain market share but suffered losses last year following an IT upgrade that disrupted product availability and sales.

The retailer reported a pre-tax loss of £989 million for 2025, compared to £599 million the year before.

Adjusted earnings dropped 33 per cent to £761 million, partly due to investments in its Asda Price and Rollback schemes.

However, in August the company reported a return to sales growth for the first time in two years, with like-for-like sales up 0.2 per cent in the first seven weeks of the third quarter, excluding fuel.

Revenues for the second quarter reached £5.1 billion, despite a 2.3 per cent drop in like-for-like sales.
