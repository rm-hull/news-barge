Shoppers queued for up to three hours to get their hands on the hugely popular NeeDoh toys at a brand new County Durham shop.

Fidget Fusion, in Chester-le-Street, enjoyed a mega first days as it opened its doors for the first time on Saturday, October 31.

Huge crowds turned out, with customers queuing around the block to buy the sought-after fidget toys.

Owner Haley Woodhead, 44, said the opening was a "huge success" after customers waited hours to get inside.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21661188.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Hayley and George *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

The shop's opening has also attracted significant attention online, with a video posted on TikTok receiving 195,000 views.

Haley said: "Open day was a huge success people queued for 3 hours to get in to buy Needoh."

The new business, at 5a Turn Park, Station Road, was set up after Haley and her family repeatedly travelled around the North East and queued for hours to find popular sensory products for her son George, 10, and niece Evie, 12.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21661195.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Inside Fidget Fusion *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Haley previously told The Northern Echo how the experience inspired her to open a shop that would make sensory toys more accessible to families.

Her son George is currently undergoing the process of potentially being diagnosed with autism and ADHD, while her niece Evie is diagnosed as autistic and has ADHD.

The shop stocks a range of fidget and sensory products, including NeeDoh toys, and has confirmed it is an official stockist.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21661189.jpg?type=mds-article-620)George, who inspired the shop *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Customers were restricted to buying one NeeDoh item each, with sales limited to in-store purchases.

But Haley's plans go beyond selling toys, with a dedicated sensory room planned to give children somewhere quiet to escape if they become overwhelmed.

The room is set to feature calming colours, sensory lights and soft furnishings.

Haley is also exploring private shopping sessions for families who need a quieter environment, alongside flexible opening hours.

She previously said: "We understand first hand how some children need quieter shops so we want to offer everything we possibly can so that everyone can have the best experience with us."

The opening came after Haley also arranged a private visit for 12-year-old Evie from Washington, who has an incurable cancer diagnosis, making her the shop's first customer on Friday, ahead of its official launch.
