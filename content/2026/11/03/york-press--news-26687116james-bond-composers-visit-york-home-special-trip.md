## James Bond composer's visit to former York home - and room where he played piano

John Barry, flanked by Margaret and John Richardson, in the garden of the York home the composer lived in until he was 14 *(Image: Contributed)*

Many passersby on a busy main road in York may not have noticed a special plaque outside a house dedicated to the work of a film composer born in the city whose work has been loved and recognised by millions across the world.

Multi-Oscar and Grammy award-winning film composer John Barry, born John Barry Prendergast in York on November 3, 1933, would have been celebrating his 93rd birthday today.

He composed the score for classic films such as Out of Africa, The Ipcress File, Midnight Cowboy, Dances with Wolves and is perhaps most memorably known for his association with the James Bond franchise.

He found fame in the city as part of The John Barry Seven, after playing in a York jazz band, The Modernaires, from 1951.

His father Jack Prendergast owned venues in York and the district including the former Rialto cinema in Fishergate.

The home he lived in until the age of 14, before moving to Fulford, has been occupied since 1978 by John Richardson who raised his family there and was born only a few days apart from John Barry.

To commemorate the centenary of film in 1996, the British Film Institute erected 126 plaques across Britain and in recognition of Mr Barry’s body of work, contacted Mr Richardson to ask if he would agree to one being put up at his home.

John, a retired journalist and former news editor of The Yorkshire Evening Press, invited The Press in and shared insights of a 'homecoming' meeting when John Barry received the Honorary Freedom of York, as well as a special story from the past which may have helped the composer get his big break.

![John Richardson said the York-born composer was thrilled to see so many of the features of his childhood house unchanged](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21654713.jpg?type=mds-article-620)John Richardson said the York-born composer was thrilled to see so many of the features of his childhood house unchanged *(Image: Contributed)*

Mr Richardson said: “When John Barry visited York to receive the city title, he visited his former family home with his wife Laurie – the Prendergast family were the first occupants of the 1930s build – and was charmed by the childhood memories as he went around the house, which was still very much as he remembered from when he was a child.

“The dining room where he played the piano, the wooden doors and doorknobs, the bedroom he shared with his brother, and the garden in which he and his siblings climbed trees.”

The Richardson home is full of creativity, including paintings, pictures, sculptures, many from family members and friends.

John said his late wife Margaret was first made aware of the connection to the composer when a neighbour drew her attention to it over the garden fence.

![A plaque to John Barry OBE outside his childhood home in York](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21656988.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The British Film Institute 1996 plaque commemorating the part John Barry played as part of the 'centenary of cinema' at the family home in York *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

She then started out collecting newspaper clippings of anything to do with the composer, as well as building up a healthy pile of CDs and recordings.

John said: “We were a bit excited really, when it became common knowledge among friends that John Barry lived here.

“We used to put a CD of his on for evening parties, but as soon as you got half a dozen people in, you couldn’t hear it.

“My daughter Sarah queued up for a signed CD from him when he was in London.

“Whenever there was a mention of him, Margaret was always interested.

“It wasn’t until the other day that I looked and realised how much there is.”

### 'And so when he conducted, he was always so languid'

Amongst the cuttings are some personal images of the visit.

As well as the memories John Barry had of the place he grew up, John Richardson pointed to the preparation for a sculpture his wife had made in one of the photos.

He said: “Margaret was deputy college bursar at the university and when she retired, she took up a sculpture course and some of her works are here.

“But this one in the picture was done as a John Barry commemoration, as it were.

“We didn’t know in advance that he was coming but we actually had been down to the Royal Albert Hall to see a commemoration concert when he was playing.

“And he was a very laconic, loose man – he was a disco man really, in a way.

“Even though he’d won his Oscars, I’m sure really deep down in his heart he was in the disco.

“And so when he conducted, he was always so languid."

John said: "That maquette was the study for a bronze, which is now in the back garden, in front of a yew tree.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21654706.jpg?type=mds-article-620)John Barry in front of a maquette from which the late Margaret Richardson created a bronze sculpture which sits in the garden of her family home *(Image: Contributed)*

As well as the family connection to the home, John Richardson, in his early years as a journalist in York, would encounter the name John Barry Prendergast professionally on the music scene in the city.

He said he remembered a key moment in the newsroom at The Yorkshire Evening Press, a simple editing decision which may have shaped the composer’s future.

John said: “Your older readers will remember Stacey Brewer, he was the music man, he’d interviewed Dean Martin, and all the top stars.

“He was just helping John Barry Prendergast with his publicity and there was this photograph.

“It was a black and white photograph, with a handwritten caption underneath, and it was saying that this was 'the John Barry Prendergast group which is making its debut at The Rialto' and was going to go in the press as a preview.

“And Stacey had said to John Barry ‘Look, that name’s too long, something has got to go out of that name.’

‘Which name do you want to drop?’

“So he said, ‘Well, Prendergast.’

“And so that caption was scratched out, so he became, with almost a click of a finger, John Barry.

“He may have been thinking of that anyway.”

![John Richardson today, at home in his garden next to one of his late wife Margaret's sculptures](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21654711.jpg?type=mds-article-620)John Richardson today, at home in his garden next to his late wife Margaret's sculpture *(Image: Kevin Glenton)*

One of the key aspects of John Barry’s visit was that the 1930s house has so many of the original fixtures and fittings, which Mr Richardson said were immediately recognisable and spoke of the dining room where he played the piano, and its wooden doors and doorknobs and the original kitchen.

John Richardson said the family home and its connections to his wife Margaret are in many ways steeped in their connections to the composer and he’s fought off any sort of idea of moving.

At her funeral service, the music from a scene in the film Dances with Wolves was played.

He said: “I feel like a custodian of the whole house, because I absolutely love it.

“You can get in the garden in the summer and you can just imagine that you’re really, well, it’s like in being in heaven.

“It’s a family home still, I think, with memories.

“I can still, you know, sit on that chair and I do think, John Barry sat here.

“It’s a silly thing to say really, you know when I’m sitting here reading my book, and think, that’s where he had his piano.”
