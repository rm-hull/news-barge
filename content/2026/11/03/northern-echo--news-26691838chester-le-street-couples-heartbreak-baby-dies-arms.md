A County Durham couple have spoken of their "unimaginable" heartbreak after their precious baby girl died in their arms at just 15 weeks old.

Helen Clarke, 33, and husband Mathew Clarke, 36, of Chester-le-Street, tragically lost their darling daughter Elodie Alice Clarke on October 16 after she was diagnosed with a rare condition.

Little Elodie spent a third of her short life in hospital suffering with mitochondrial disease - a genetic condition which impacts how mitochondria in cells produce energy and can be fatal.

The family say doctors first became concerned about Elodie during pregnancy as she was smaller than expected.

St Cuthbert's Hospice fundraiser Helen had fortnightly growth scans and later doctors told her there was a 10 per cent chance of a chromosomal abnormality.

However, Helen and Mathew remained positive and baby Elodie was born on July 2. The family fell in love with their new addition, but just weeks later her life was cruelly cut short by the illness.

An overnight blood test found high lactate levels, a sign that something could be wrong.

Despite this, parents said doctors reassured them that their daughter was not displaying signs of being unwell and were told tests ruled out mitochondrial disease.

However, the couple's world turned upside down shortly afterwards when Elodie was diagnosed with hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, a heart condition linked to the disorder.

"At the time because we didn't think it was mitochondrial, we thought it was a separate genetic thing that she had," Helen said.

But Elodie was admitted to hospital on September 10 after she began feeding less and losing weight.

Her mum said: "We were at hospital five and a half weeks. When we first got there they gave her a feeding tube, which we were upset about, but we knew it was for the best.

"At this point they just wanted to get her fed and sorted and then we could go home."

Tragically, Elodie was never able to return home and as the family waited for genetic test results - which can take up to six months - Elodie's condition worsened.

Teacher Mathew said: "We knew something was seriously wrong because they would do blood tests and her potassium was too high, her sodium was low and there were quite a few awful days in hospital with different things going on with her blood and emergency treatment."

As the youngster's symptoms worsened, Helen began to question whether mitochondrial disease had really been ruled out.

"I was googling all her symptoms and everything kept coming back as mitochondrial but for so many weeks I dismissed it because we'd been told it wasn't that," she said.

"We were given the test which sees if the mother's DNA can cause mitochondrial disorder and mine had come back clear. But the other way you can have it is if me and Matt both passed on a gene to Elodie and that test hadn't been done."

Helen raised her concerns with doctors, and the family soon met a mitochondrial specialist who believed the baby was suffering with the disease.

Elodie's health worsened and she was moved to intensive care after struggling to breathe.

"It happened so quickly. It was just horrendous", Helen said.

Elodie was put on a ventilator but doctors warned the family she might not breathe on her own if the tube was removed.

On October 13, genetic tests confirmed the youngster had inherited mitochondrial disease after both parents unknowingly passed on the rare gene.

Helen said: "We were told on the Tuesday and by the Friday she'd passed away.

"Unless you live through what our little daughter had to go through on a daily basis, you just don't know. It is no life for a little baby.

Mathew added: "The Great North Children's Hospital did everything they could for her but it just wasn't enough.

"We got four hours of cuddles and then she passed away in our arms. Holding your baby for four hours as they die is unimaginable."

The couple praised the medical teams who cared for Elodie and are now raising money in her name.

They have raised more than £15,000 for the The Lily Foundation, which supports families affected by mitochondrial disease and funds research into treatments and cures.

Helen said: "It's an amazing charity that not only supports families but also pays for research into cures and treatments.

"We want to keep her name out there and keep making an impact for her because she didn't get the life she deserved and we want to make sure this doesn't happen to other families."

Mathew's 13-year-old daughter Lilah, who Helen said shared a "best friend" bond with Elodie, is crocheting and selling scrunchies to raise money for the charity.

"They were literally best friends and she was so over the moon to have a sister," Helen said.
