Some polls are open for Tuesday’s crucial midterm elections, which will decide majority control of Congress for the final two years of Donald Trump’s presidency.

Democrats are looking to take control of Congress to stall Trump’s agenda. They will need to flip four Senate seats and three House seats to gain a majority. Republicans currently have a majority in both chambers.

Trump’s poor approval rating and Americans’ concerns over the high cost of living and war in Iran have been key campaign issues for Democrats.

Many states that Trump previously won are seeing tight races for their Senate seat including Alaska, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, Texas, Kansas and Iowa. In the House, Republicans are on the defense, looking to hold on to battleground districts won by Trump in 2024.

The midterms are seen as a referendum on Trump’s second term. The president made his last-ditch effort to appeal to voters Monday, with a speech at the Reflecting Pool in Washington, D.C., that focused more on his renovation projects and less on Election Day.

## Midterm election betting hits record $1.4B as prediction platforms go mainstream

People across the United States are betting on this year's midterm races at record levels for US election betting.

More than $1.4 billion has been wagered on the midterms, according to the platforms and industry research. Prediction market platforms allow traders to buy and sell "yes" or "no" contracts on a range of race outcomes and variables. The markets rose to prominence in 2024 when they bucked traditional opinion polls in signaling that Donald Trump would win the presidency.

Among those watching the results roll in on Tuesday evening will be Noah Poser, who will be glued to his phone tracking dozens of large wagers he placed on prediction markets when polls close in the US midterm elections on Tuesday night. Poser, 28, works in financial technology and has written about betting strategies in such markets.

## Half of voters want 'substantial change' to how U.S. is run, poll says

About half of voters want “substantial change” to how the country is run, according to a recent AP-NORC poll.

Additionally, about three in 10 want “complete and total upheaval.”

Only about two in 10 said they want “small change” or “no change.”

Democrats and independents polled were more likely than Republicans to indicate they wanted a full overhaul, but even Republican voters indicated they were hoping for a change.

About 45 percent said they want substantial change, while 13 percent want upheaval.

## Republican U.S. Senate nominee Mike Rogers praises Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens for endorsement

Republican U.S. Senate nominee Mike Rogers praised Democratic Rep. Haley Stevens after she endorsed him in Michigan’s Senate race over the Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed.

“I’ve never seen such viciousness online,” Rogers said of the online backlash Stevens has received as a result of her endorsement.

“It took a lot of courage for her to do that, to stand up for America, and listen, I’m proud of her for doing what she believed was the right thing to do,” Rogers said Tuesday after casting his ballot in Michigan.

## Voting in one Florida county delayed due to barcode issue

In addition to severe flooding causing some polling locations to move, voters in Broward County, Florida, were also hit with barcode issues Tuesday morning.

Voting was delayed about 20 minutes at several voting sites in the county due to the malfunction.

Supervisor of Elections Joe Scott said some voters left before they were able to cast the ballot, and encouraged them to come back.

The issue caused some people to receive an error message that they were voting on the wrong ballot, when they were not, Scott said.

“Nobody voted on the wrong ballot,” Scott said. “None of the counts were impacted.”

The problem was ultimately solved by 7:20 a.m., officials said.

## Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer 'nauseously optimistic'

Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer said Tuesday that he expects Democrats to win control of the Senate, making him the next Senate majority leader.

After voting in Brooklyn, New York, he told reporters he felt “nauseously optimistic” about Democrats’ odds.

“We don’t want to count our chickens before they hatch. So I’d characterize our mood as nauseously optimistic,” he said.

He also noted that it would be up to President Donald Trump whether he wants to work with Democrats, should they gain a majority.

Schumer was previously majority leader between 2021 and 2025.

## Bruce Springsteen reminds election officials to do their jobs

Bruce Springsteen issued a reminder Tuesday to Election officials and government leaders to do their jobs.

“I’m speaking to Election officials and representatives of our government on Election Day,” Springsteen said in a video shared on social media Tuesday.

“You have sworn an oath to preserve, protect, and defend our Constitution. That means you have a solemn duty to ensure our votes are securely counted.”

He continued: “ Thomas Jefferson said, ‘Government requires the consent of the governed.’ You who represent us must never consent to any directives that undermine our Constitutional right to a free and fair election. Our vote is our voice, and it’s up to you ensure our voice is heard. I wish you all a good Election Day, and thank you for your service.”

## Florida moves more than 20 polling places due to flooding

Officials in Florida were forced to move more than 20 polling locations that had been impacted by severe flooding.

Portions of Florida’s Space Coast were inundated by up to 28 inches of rain from Sunday to Monday, causing serious damage and prompting Gov. Ron DeSantis to declare a state of emergency in seven counties.

The impacted polling locations are spread out across Brevard County, Broward County, Highlands County, Indian River County, Martin County, Okeechobee County, Palm Beach County and St. Lucie County.
