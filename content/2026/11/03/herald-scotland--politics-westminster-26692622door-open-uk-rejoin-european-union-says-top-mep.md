The "door is open" to the UK rejoining the EU, according to a senior MEP who revealed Europe does "not blame" Scotland for Brexit.

David McAllister, who is chair of the European Parliament's foreign affairs committee, made his intervention amid growing calls for the UK to rejoin the bloc.

He was speaking alongside the Scottish Government's Europe minister Stephen Gethins at an event in Edinburgh on "Europe’s Security Challenges and the Role of the UK" organised by the Scottish Council on Global Relations and the German political organisation, Konrad Adenauer Stiftung.

Prime Minister Andy Burham is to set out the government's options on future EU/UK relations at a summit in Brussels on November 20.

In recent weeks he has raised the prospect that the UK may rejoin the bloc in future or seek re-establishing freedom of movement with the EU.

Mr McAllister, a German MEP, who was born to a Scottish father and German mother in Berlin, was asked about how MEPs regards the UK.

He told the audience that of the European Union institutions including the European Commission and the European Council, "the European Parliament is probably the most UK minded".

![David McAllister MEP, chair of the European Parliament's foreign affairs committee, speaking at an event in Edinburgh organised by the Scottish Council on Global Affairs and the Konrad Adenauer Stiftung.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662115.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David McAllister MEP, chair of the European Parliament's foreign affairs committee, speaking at an event in Edinburgh organised by the Scottish Council on Global Affairs and the Konrad Adenauer Stiftung. *(Image: Kathleen Nutt)*

"We miss you every day - [that's] all political parties, except one," he said understood to be a reference to Nigel Farage's Reform UK.

"We miss you and definitely our door is so wide open. It is up to you to walk through this door," he said.

He added: "The debate has been opened by the Prime Minister, for us, it is too early to assess what will happen. It is up to the British side to define which way they want to go.

"Rest assured that among all the reasonable forces in the European Parliament, there would be a lot of goodwill and I am sure there would be lots of people who would be as flexible as possible to try to follow as many British proposals as possible.

"But it is up to you to decide. The debate has started. Everyone in Brussels knows the atmosphere in Scotland is different from other parts.

"We know exactly how you voted in 2016 - you are not to blame."

He was later pressed about how the EU regarded the prospect of the UK seeking to join the EU.

"Apart from some of the far right and far left, my impression is is that Conservatives, Christian Democrats, Socialists, Liberals, Greens, they are all very very UK minded and still deeply regret that this country, for the time-being has left our family of nations," he told guests, who included European and US diplomats, civil servants and academics, at the event on Monday.

"When it comes to the future. If I understand the Prime Minister correctly, for this parliamentary term he has ruled out going into the single market or the customs union or restoring the freedom of movement.

"If I understand correctly he said in the interviews he would seek a mandate for closer ties at the next general election. So the ball is definitely in the British court to decide which way you want to go....As I've said it is too early to assess what this will mean for the British longer term position, but our door remains open and it is wide, wide open."

He added: "Once we get serious we would like to have a detailed conversation with our British partners if you want to join the club. It comes with many rights, a couple of obligations, it also needs finance."

The Prime Minister raised the prospect that the UK may rejoin the bloc in a speech to the Labour party conference in September saying: “Brexit has done more harm than good".

He said Britain could not have a “clear path into the rest of the century” until it decided on its long-term relationship with the EU.

Last month he as opened the door to Britain readopting the European Union’s free movement rules, arguing that an “honest discussion” is needed.

The Prime Minister has declined to rule out putting a pledge to rejoin the EU in Labour’s next general election manifesto.

Last weekend former Chancellor Rachel Reeves argued that the UK should rejoin the EU.
