The Scottish Greens have hit back at calls to reconsider the election law which allowed Q Manivannan to become an MSP, warning that reversing the legislation would be a "regrettable backwards step".

A Holyrood committee has urged the Scottish Government to rethink rules allowing foreign nationals with limited leave to remain in the UK to stand for election, claiming the policy has "run ahead of what the public want".

This could have implications for the future eligibility of Scottish Green MSP Q Manivannan, who was elected to Holyrood in May while on a student visa.

The Edinburgh and Lothians East MSP, who is originally from India, has since secured a three-year graduate visa allowing them to remain in the UK until 2029, two years before the end of the current parliamentary term.

The Scottish Parliament's Standards, Procedures and Public Appointments Committee has now questioned the principle behind the legislation which enabled people in Manivannan's position to stand for election.

In a report published on Tuesday, MSPs called on ministers to reconsider the policy rather than press ahead with a Bill designed to correct an error in the original legislation.

However, the Greens pointed out that the changes had been backed unanimously by Parliament, including the committee's convener and deputy convener.

A party spokesperson said: "There are thousands of people with limited leave to remain who live and work in Scotland and have made a home here, including Commonwealth citizens. They should be able to stand for our parliament and councils.

"MSPs backed this change unanimously at the time, including the convenor and deputy convenor of the committee. It would be a regrettable backwards step for representation and democracy if Scotland was to roll-back on this commitment."

The row centres on the Scottish Elections (Representation and Reform) Act 2025, which extended candidacy rights at Scottish Parliament and local government elections to foreign nationals with limited leave to remain.

MSPs vote to ban dual mandates after Stephen Flynn row reported on the passage of the legislation, which also included the candidacy changes.

However, a drafting error meant Commonwealth citizens with limited leave to remain were not given the same rights to stand in local council elections.

The Scottish Government subsequently introduced the Scottish Local Government Elections (Candidacy Rights of Commonwealth Citizens) Bill to correct the mistake.

But the committee has now recommended against supporting the proposed change, arguing that the wider policy needs to be revisited.

In its report, the committee said it did not accept the premise that non-UK citizens with limited leave to remain should have a general right to become councillors or MSPs.

Committee convener Jenni Minto said: "It is vital that Scotland has elected representatives who look like the communities they represent. This diversity helps build trust in our elected institutions.

"But we know that this trust is fragile, and so careful consideration needs to be given to all electoral reform that come before us.

"The changes that allowed people with limited leave to remain to stand for election should be reconsidered.

"They appear to have run ahead of what the public want and the rationale for making the change is simply not clear.

"The changes proposed by this Bill risk entrenching a policy that instead needs looked at again."

The committee heard evidence that Scotland was a relative outlier in allowing people with limited leave to remain to stand for election.

It also raised concerns about growing geopolitical tensions and how these could affect an electoral system in which checks on candidates' eligibility are largely self-policing.

MSPs called for a "practical, pragmatic and hard-headed" approach to future electoral reform.

The report also urged the Scottish Government to collect more information about candidates, including their nationality and visa status, to better understand how closely elected representatives reflect the communities they serve.

The recommendations have particular significance for Manivannan, whose election to Holyrood was made possible by the 2025 changes.

The Green MSP, who has lived in Scotland since 2021, confirmed last month that they had secured a graduate visa after previously holding a student visa.

Manivannan has also indicated plans to apply for a Global Talent visa, which could allow them to remain in the UK beyond the expiry of their current permission.

The Green MSP may be unable to serve full term due to visa examined questions around how long Manivannan could remain eligible to serve.

At the time, they insisted: "This is my home and I'm not going anywhere."

Any reversal of the 2025 legislation could affect Manivannan's eligibility to stand in future elections, depending on the terms of any replacement law and their immigration status at the time.

However, the committee's recommendations do not change the law or threaten the MSP's existing seat.

Manivannan is also a Commonwealth citizen, meaning their eligibility under any future rules would depend on how ministers chose to distinguish between different categories of foreign nationals.
