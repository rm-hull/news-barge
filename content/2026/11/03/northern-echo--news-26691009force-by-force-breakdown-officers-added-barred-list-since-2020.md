A record number of police officers have been sacked and banned from ever serving again, with figures revealing how the four North East forces compare.

A total of 757 officers across England and Wales were added to the police barred list in the year to March 2026, the highest annual figure since the list was introduced in December 2017.

The latest figures show Northumbria Police, who have around double the officers of the other forces across the region, recorded 22 officers added to the list, up from 15 the previous year.

Durham Police also saw an increase, with eight officers barred in 2025/26 compared with two in each of the previous two years.

Cleveland Police recorded six officers added to the list, down from 10 in 2024/25, while North Yorkshire Police saw five, compared with eight the year before.

Across the four forces, 41 officers were added to the barred list in the latest year.

The figures mean Northumbria recorded the highest number of barred officers in the North East, followed by Durham, Cleveland and North Yorkshire.

Nationally, the number of officers barred has risen steadily, from 299 in 2021/22 to 394 in 2022/23, 594 in 2023/24 and 735 in 2024/25.

The latest total of 757 marks a further increase and is more than double the 299 recorded four years earlier.

Officers, special constables and police staff dismissed for breaching professional standards are added to the list, preventing them from returning to policing.

The reasons for dismissal included dishonesty in 142 cases, discriminatory behaviour in 110 and unlawful access or disclosure of information in 101.

There were also 87 cases involving sexual offences or misconduct, including six instances of child sexual abuse, five of rape and 46 involving abuse of position for a sexual purpose.

Assistant Chief Constable Arman Mathieson, director of operational standards at the College of Policing, said: “Policing is built on trust and these figures show we will not hesitate to act when that trust is broken.

“757 officers have been removed and barred from ever serving again, including those who retired or resigned while under investigation.

“This represents 0.5% of all serving officers, with the vast majority of officers serving with courage, professionalism and integrity.

“We all deserve a service that roots out those who fall short. Where standards are breached, we will find out and we will act.”

Of the 757 officers added to the list nationally, 334 were still serving when they were dismissed, while 397 had resigned and 26 had retired.

Police constables accounted for the vast majority, with 641 added to the list, alongside 84 sergeants, 18 inspectors, five chief inspectors, five superintendents and four chief superintendents.

The Metropolitan Police recorded the highest number nationally, with 195 officers added, followed by Greater Manchester Police with 46 and West Midlands Police with 38.

Since the barred list was introduced on December 15, 2017, a total of 3,591 regular officers have been added.

The College of Policing said the pace of dismissals had accelerated markedly, with last year's figure more than twice the annual average of the preceding four years.
