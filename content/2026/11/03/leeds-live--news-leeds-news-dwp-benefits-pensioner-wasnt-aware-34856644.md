# DWP benefits pensioner 'wasn’t aware' he had to declare £42k in savings

## He has now been sentenced

A Leeds pensioner who failed to declare he had £42,000 in savings to the DWP has avoided prison.

Kiamarz B Birgani, 69, was overpaid £30,821 in employment and support allowance and £11,087 in housing benefit before he was caught out. Birgani claimed he did not realise he had to declare any savings he had to the Department of Work and Pensions.

Prosecutor Vincent Blake-Barnard told Leeds Crown Court on Tuesday, November 3: "Regarding the employment support allowance and housing benefits, these are benefits people are able to claim if they don't have savings.

"Savings have impact on a claim - anything over £6,000 but then that tapers to zero [no support] after £12,000. The defendant's initial claim for employment and support allowance was on the ninth of June 2016 for himself and his wife.

"He did not declare any savings and from that claim he did not report any savings or changes in circumstances. In September 2003, he claimed housing benefit and that went straight to his landlord.

"The range of the amount exceeded the £12,000 for the period of the claim, at one point peaking at £42,000, with his failure to make those declarations generating an overpayment."

The court heard that during his interview, Birgani, of Raynel Drive, Leeds, claimed he did not know the rules and "wasn't aware he had to disclose the information."

Birgani has already started paying back some of the overpayment on a monthly basis.

Her Honour Judge Rayfield asked his barrister Andrea Parnham: "Is the thrust of your mitigation that he was so unwell at the time he didn't give proper thought to his savings and there was an element of dishonesty in that?"

Ms Parnham said: "Your Honour has my submissions."

The judge told the court Birgani was a working man until his physical injuries stopped him from continuing working.

She said: "In the circumstances I have read about the fact he has paid the lion's share back and continues to pay that back I am willing to sentence him in accordance with the recommendation."

Birgani was made the subject of a 12-month community order.
