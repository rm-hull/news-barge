V&A Dundee has lifted the lid on its eagerly-awaited exhibition celebrating the life and legacy of David Bowie.

More than 100 objects from the singer's personal collection have been brought together for the show, which will be running in the city for more than three months before heading out on tour around England.

Costumes, musical instruments, rare photographs, sketches for music videos and live shows, handwritten song lyrics and personal possessions kept by Bowie for decades all feature in the exhibition.

The show, which explores his interests in music, film, theatre dance, film, photography and art, features many objects going on public display for the first time.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21663393.jpg?type=mds-article-620)V&A Dundee curator Svetland Panova with two of the Ziggy Stardust-era costumes which are part of its David Bowie exhibition. *(Image: Grant Anderson)*

Here are some of the key highlights from the exhibition, David Bowie: On Tour, which are certain to fascinate fans of the trailblazing performer.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21122627.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Bowie's first musical instrument, a Grafton Alto saxophone, is part of the new exhibition at V&A Dundee. *(Image: David Parry/PA Media Assignments)*

**A MUSICAL MEMENTO**

The exhibition features a host of Bowie’s personal possessions and keepsakes, including including a passport, a stopwatch he used to help him write songs and an advance copy of the Velvet Underground song I’m Waiting for the Man. Perhaps the most moving exhibition on display is a Grafton Alton saxophone, Bowie’s first musical instrument, which was a gift from his father, Haywood Stenton 'John' Jones, in 1961.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21122670.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Bowie's Ziggy Stardust-era acoustic guitar is on display at V&A Dundee as part of its exhibition devoted to the singer. *(Image: David Parry/PA Media Assignments)*

**ZIGGY STARDUST**

Bowie’s early 1970s alien rock star alter-ego Ziggy Stardust takes centre stage in the exhibition, which features his handwritten notes plotting out the era-defining concept album and tour. Nestled between two Ziggy Stardust costumes, created by designers Freddie Burretti and Kansai Yamamoto, is the acoustic guitar Bowie played at the time, which is on public display for the first time as part of the exhibition.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21662062.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Bowie's sell-out show at the Caird Hall in Dundee is recalled in the new exhibition at the city's V&A museum. *(Image: Jane Barlow)*

**DUNDEE MAYHEM**

Bowie was at the height of his 1970s fame when the Ziggy Stardust tour arrived at Dundee’s Caird Hall. Tantalising glimpses of the show are on offer thanks to photographs captures on the night, including a selection of colour polaroids. The exhibition recalls how Bowie, who famously climbed onto a speaker stack wearing a red jumpsuit during the show, had whipped the crowd into such a frenzy that he had been rescued from his car by security guard Stuart “Stuey” George.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21663442.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scottish singer-songwriter Callum Beattie poses with Masayoshi Sukita's images of David Bowie, which were shot for cover of his Heroes album, at a preview of V&A Dundee's new exhibition devoted to the singer. *(Image: Jane Barlow/Press Association)*

**THE NOISY NEIGHBOUR**

Bowie and his then wife Angie moved into Oakley Street in the Chelsea area of London in October 1973 after being forced to move from a Maida Vale flat due to noise complaint. But they soon faced more problems with their new neighbours, highlighted in a letter, wrongly addressed to Mr Bowy, which said: “I wonder if you quite realise the thinness of the walls of these old houses…we would be most grateful if you would be kind and considerate enough to have the music played much more softly and stop it at a reasonable hour.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21122687.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Bowie's storyboard sketch for the video for his 1980 single Ashes to Ashes is part of the new exhibition at V&A Dundee. *(Image: David Parry/PA Media Assignments)*

**ASHES TO ASHES**

Partly inspired by the stage show Bowie appeared in with mime artist Lindsay Kemp, the clown costume created by Russian designer Natasha Korniloff for the Ashes to Ashes video, is one of the most striking objects in the exhibition. Not to be missed are his handwritten lyrics for the 1980 song and his storyboard sketches for what was the most expensive music promo yet at the time.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21486603.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tilda Swinton starred opposite David Bowie in the video for his song The Stars (Are Out Tonight). *(Image: PA)*

**THE STARS (ARE OUT TONIGHT)**

Tilda Swinton starred opposite Bowie in the video for The Stars (Are Out Tonight), a Grammy-nominated single from The Next Day, the singer’s first new album for a decade. Bowie’s vision for the video, directed by Italian-Canadian director Floria Sigismondi, was set out in his typed-out script, treatments and props in the exhibition.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21120919.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A polaroid photograph taken during David Bowie's costume fittings for the character of Jareth in the 1986 film Labyrinth is part V&A Dundee's new exhibition. *(Image: David Parry/PA Media Assignments)*

**LABYRINTH**

A section of the exhibition devoted to Bowie’s screen encounters and performances recalls his roles as the narrator of The Snowman, as Lord Royal Highness in the SpongeBob SquarePants Movie and in the Second World War drama Merry Christmas, Mr Lawrence, as well as a rejected role in The Simpsons. However, fans of the Jim Henson-directed fantasy film Labyrinth can see Bowie’s personal copy of the script, as well as a polaroid picture taken at the costume fitting for his character, goblin king Jareth.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21663493.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A set of keys to David Bowie's flat in Berlin are on display as part of a new exhibition at V&A Dundee devoted to the singer. *(Image: Olivia Singleton)*

**THE BERLIN YEARS**

Bowie’s highly-productive two-year spell living in Berlin in the 1970s is celebrated with sketches and concept art for the cover of his album Low, handwritten lyrics for the song Look Back in Anger from the album Lodger and a Japanese musical instrument he played on Moss Garden from the album Low. The Berlin display also features a set of keys for Bowie’s flat in the city.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21394669.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Bowie's collaboration with musician and producer Nile Rodgers on the Let's Dance album is recalled in the new V&A Dundee exhibition. *(Image: Tom Langford)*

**LET’S DANCE**

The exhibition recalls the impact of Bowie’s decision to bring in Nile Rodgers, the co-founder of the American disco outfit Chic, to produce a new album, Let’s Dance. The exhibition features photography from the recording sessions for the 1983 album, handwritten lyrics for the songs Let’s Dance and Modern Love, and insights into Bowie’s plans for the accompanying Serious Moonlight tour, his biggest to date, which saw him staged 96 shows in 15 countries and reach an audience in excess of 2.6 million.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21122707.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Bowie's handwritten lyrics for Blackstar, the title track of his final album, are part of V&A Dundee's new exhibition. *(Image: Olivia Singleton)*

**BLACKSTAR**

The exhibition offers “never-before-seen” insights into Blackstar, Bowie’s final album, which was released just two days before the singer passed away in 2016. Objects on display include handwritten lyrics for the song Blackstar, a hand-painted jumper worn by Bowie in the Lazarus video and a prop used in the Blackstar video. The posthumous Grammy award for the latter song is also part of the exhibition.
