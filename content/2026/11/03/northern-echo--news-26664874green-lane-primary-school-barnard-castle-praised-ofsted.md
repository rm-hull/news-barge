A County Durham primary school has received a glowing Ofsted report just weeks before its headteacher retires after more than eight years at the helm.

Green Lane Primary School in Barnard Castle was inspected in September, with Ofsted praising its inclusive ethos, support for pupils with additional needs and strong early years provision.

For headteacher Rob Goffee, retiring at Christmas after 25 years in education, the report was a fitting end to his time at the school.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21657839.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Robert Goffee. *(Image: GREEN LANE PRIMARY SCHOOL)*

Mr Goffee said he approached the inspection anxiously but called the process positive and said inspectors took time to understand the school’s values and approach.

He said: "The lead inspector was very fair. He came with the attitude of wanting to see what the school was like and really wanted me to show him the evidence that we had in school.

"Over the two days I felt he really got to grips with the school and understood what we were and what we were about."

Inspectors called the school’s early years provision the “jewel in the school’s crown”.

Mr Goffee said: "That was fantastic to hear because we put a lot of work into early years. The children thrive there and that's really important to us."

He added that Ofsted’s recognition gave staff confidence their work was making a real difference.

"You think you're doing the right thing, but when somebody independent comes in and evaluates the school as rigorously as Ofsted does, it gives you that confidence that we are doing the right thing here."

The headteacher was particularly pleased that inspectors recognised the school’s commitment to supporting children with additional needs.

He said: "We have a lot of pupils with special educational needs and Ofsted recognised that we go above and beyond to support them."

"As a church school we want to be a school that welcomes anybody in. We're not trying to turn people away because they've got additional needs. We feel they're the very children we want to help and support."

The words “inclusive”, “compassionate” and “caring” stood out to him.

Mr Goffee said: "Those are words you don't really expect to read in an Ofsted report. I was blown away."

"It's not just about academic success. We are really passionate about helping children develop respect and care for one another."

Inspectors also praised the school’s support for staff wellbeing, confident pupils and rich curriculum.

Mr Goffee was especially proud that pupils felt able to express themselves and celebrate success in different ways.

He pointed to the school’s recent production of The Lion King, featuring children who had overcome significant challenges earlier in life.

"To see them confidently up on stage was really special," he said.

Mr Goffee said teaching was not his first career.

He moved from Hampshire to the North East to study at the University of Sunderland in the 1990s, initially planning to return south.

Instead, he fell in love with the region and stayed.

He worked in the charity sector before becoming a teacher because he wanted to make a difference through education.

"It is a privilege to work with young people," he said.

"Every day is different. It's stressful and there is pressure, but you do feel like you're making a difference to children's lives."

He admits he once would have preferred to retire without an Ofsted inspection looming, but now sees the timing as perfect.

"If you'd asked me before, I'd probably have said I'd rather retire without one," he said.
