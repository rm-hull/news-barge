A four-year convoy plan for one of Scotland’s most notorious roads could cost the local economy £500million, campaigners have warned.

Politicians and business leaders from across Argyll and Bute have warned that reducing the key arterial route to a single carriageway on diversion route controlled by convoys and traffic lights while a permanent scheme is built to deal with the landslip-prone A83 at the Rest and be Thankful would be “economically catastrophic”.

The Rest and Be Thankful Campaign Group, which represents around 1,500 businesses in Argyll and Bute, highlighted there is an opportunity to build a new two-way route through the forestry track as the permanent solution — arguing that “one road could be built not two roads”.

Business leaders and politicians have held crunch talks with Transport Secretary Stephen Flynn over the proposed restrictions, which the campaign group says could hit trade, deliveries and investment across the region.

“We are not asking for the permanent solution to be delayed. We are asking for it to be delivered in a way that protects Argyll’s communities and economy while it is being built,” campaign chairman John Gurr said.

The meeting, held at the Scottish Parliament on October 29 and organised by Jackie Baillie MSP, brought together representatives of Argyll’s business, haulage, construction and farming communities, alongside Brendan O’Hara MP, Jenni Minto MSP and Councillor John Armour, representing Argyll and Bute Council.

The delegation told Mr Flynn that the proposal to rely on convoy-controlled traffic using the Old Military Road for up to four years while the permanent scheme is built was unacceptable, and that reliable two-way access to Argyll must be maintained. The group said its position was supported by elected representatives across political parties and all levels of government.

Mr Gurr said: “We cannot now accept a construction plan that could impose a further four years of restrictions on the communities and businesses that depend on this vital route.”

It is understood Mr Flynn confirmed that he wanted to hear the campaign group’s views before reaching his final decision. He is said to have emphasised his responsibility for road-user safety and his commitment to delivering a solution as quickly as possible, and undertook to discuss the concerns raised at the meeting with Transport Scotland officials before deciding how to proceed.

![The transport secretary Stephen Flynn has intervened as businesses revolt over plans for the notorious Rest and be Thankful road.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21488471.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The transport secretary Stephen Flynn has intervened as businesses revolt over plans for the notorious Rest and be Thankful road. *(Image: NQ)*

However, the group said the transport secretary made no commitment to changing the proposed arrangements and gave no date for a decision. The campaign is now calling on him to reconsider the convoy plans, give proper scrutiny to alternative two-way routes and establish a clear, funded timetable for delivering a reliable road.

The £500million estimate represents the potential economic impact of the proposed restrictions over the construction period. Mr Gurr said it was based on assessments supplied by individual businesses and sectors during round-table discussions, rather than a formal economic impact study.

He said: “The economic consequences are potentially enormous. The uncertainty of journey times, particularly for hauliers, farmers, island communities and businesses dependent on reliable deliveries, is every bit as damaging as the delays themselves.”

Delivery of a solution to end the crisis over the key Scottish tourist route is still in limbo — almost 14 years after the Scottish Government launched a taskforce to find a fix.

Four years after ministers selected a preferred route for a £500m permanent solution, involving building a mile-long debris shelter to protect vehicles from landslips on the A83 as part of a long-term permanent solution, construction has still not begun and Transport Scotland officials have warned a public local inquiry may yet be needed — risking further delays.

The campaign group says businesses in Inveraray have reported a 20% reduction in trade when the Old Military Road is in use, with tourism and hospitality particularly exposed because of their reliance on day-trippers. Mr Gurr said the effects would extend beyond the immediate area, through increased costs, difficulties accessing markets and services and the risk of businesses losing trade.

The group also warned that delays could have serious consequences for fishing and shellfish businesses. Mr Gurr has previously told The Herald that if a lorry carrying shellfish misses its onward connection to Europe, a load worth tens of thousands of pounds could have to be thrown away.

The delegation challenged Transport Scotland’s assessment of alternative routes which could keep traffic moving in both directions, including a possible route using the forestry track on the opposite side of Glen Croe.

The campaign group questioned the estimated cost of up to £255million for that option, arguing that the figures should be independently scrutinised and compared with the costs of other major Scottish road projects.

Brendan O’Hara also highlighted the strength of public opposition, pointing to nearly 9,500 people who have supported his petition calling for the convoy proposals to be reconsidered. Councillor Armour publicly confirmed the support of elected members for the campaign group’s position, describing the case presented by businesses and community representatives as strong and robust.

Ms Baillie said the Government’s plans must protect Argyll and Bute’s industries, businesses and communities. The campaign group said the meeting had demonstrated the strength of concern across the region, but insisted that further discussion must now lead to a decision.

The dispute centres on the arrangements for keeping the A83 open while the Scottish Government builds its long-term protection scheme at the Rest and Be Thankful. The road is a vital link between Argyll and the central belt, but the section through Glen Croe has suffered repeated closures because of landslides and debris falling from the steep hillside above it.

![A computer-generated image of the proposed new £470m shelter over the A83 east of the Rest and Be Thankful Image: Transport Scotland](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/17407386.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A computer-generated image of the proposed new £470m shelter over the A83 east of the Rest and Be Thankful *(Image: Transport Scotland)*

Transport Scotland’s preferred permanent solution is a debris-flow shelter along the existing A83. The structure is designed to protect traffic from material coming down the hillside, with a protective wall and a catch pit intended to collect falling debris and rock before it reaches the road. The preferred design was announced in 2023, and draft orders and environmental documents were published in December 2024.

Because the permanent scheme would be built largely alongside the existing road, Transport Scotland’s plans envisage extensive use of the Old Military Road as a diversion during construction. The published programme estimates that building the permanent scheme could take three to four years, with traffic management potentially involving traffic lights and periods of full closure of the A83.

The Old Military Road is already used when landslides or other incidents force the main road to close. The Government’s separate medium-term plan is intended to improve the route and make it safer and more reliable as a diversion, including extending the length over which two-way traffic can operate.

However, Transport Scotland’s public engagement material says the northern end will remain single lane because of the steep terrain and tight bends, meaning traffic controls will still be required.

The campaign group argues that improving the diversion does not address its central demand for a resilient two-way road that remains open during periods of heavy rainfall. It wants the forestry-track route examined more seriously as an alternative, rather than relying on the Old Military Road for an extended period while the permanent scheme is built.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21661924.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The campaign group who met the transport secretary *(Image: John Gurr)*

A Transport Scotland spokesman said: “The Scottish Government remains committed to delivering a permanent and resilient infrastructure solution to address the landslip risks at the A83 Rest and Be Thankful as a priority.

“The Cabinet Secretary for Economy, Tourism and Transport Stephen Flynn last week met with members of the A83 Campaign Group and local politicians and heard their views on the additional design and appraisal work that has been undertaken.

“The Cabinet Secretary will now consider their views along with feedback from the A83 Taskforce, roundtable meetings and other stakeholders and his visit to the Rest and Be Thankful, to see for himself the challenges of the route, all of which will help inform a final decision on the way ahead for the medium-term solution.”
