The inquest into the death of a 14-year-old girl struck by a train in County Durham is due to resume today.

Ruby Nancy Jacqui Bill died at Newcastle’s Great North Children’s Hospital on May 2, ten days after the incident at Shildon.

Ruby, a Greenfield Academy pupil from Newton Aycliffe, was airlifted to hospital after the train struck her shortly after 8am on April 22.

An inquest was opened at Crook Coroners’ Court on May 27 and heard that Ruby died from a severe traumatic brain injury.

![A Great North Air Ambulance (GNAAS) at the scene](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20869460.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A Great North Air Ambulance (GNAAS) at the scene *(Image: GNAAS)*

Senior Coroner Jeremy Chipperfield adjourned proceedings to allow further investigations to take place, saying: “I know there are a number of strands to this investigation so I am going to adjourn this inquest to a mention hearing in eight weeks time.”

The full inquest is listed to take place at Crook Civic Centre today, Tuesday, November 3, before coroner J R Leslie Hamilton.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20819218.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Greenfield Academy, where Ruby went to school *(Image: GOOGLE)*

As reported, emergency services, including British Transport Police, the North East Ambulance Service (NEAS) and the Great North Air Ambulance Service (GNAAS), were called to the scene.

A spokesperson for the GNAAS said: "Our critical care team was activated at 8.06am to an incident.

"We had two doctors and a paramedic on board our aircraft, and they arrived on scene at 8.15am.

"Our team worked alongside the North East Ambulance Service to assess and treat a patient and airlifted them to hospital."

At the time, James Keating, headteacher at Greenfield Academy, said: “We can confirm that earlier this morning, off-site, an incident occurred concerning a pupil from Greenfield Academy.

“We are liaising closely with the emergency services while providing support for our children and the wider school community.”

British Transport Police said the incident was not suspicious.
