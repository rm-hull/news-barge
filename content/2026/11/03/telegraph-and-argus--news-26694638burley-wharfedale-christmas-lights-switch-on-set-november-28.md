A village is gearing up for the return of its annual Christmas lights switch-on, with residents invited to enjoy an afternoon of festive entertainment, markets and family activities later this month.

The event will take place on Saturday, November 28, with performances from local choirs, primary schools, musicians and dancers beginning at 2.45pm in front of The Grange.

The Burley-in-Wharfedale Christmas lights switch-on ceremony is scheduled for 4.30pm.

Visitors will also be able to browse a craft market and stalls run by local voluntary groups in Queens Hall from 1.30pm, while a street market along Main Street will open from 2pm.

Local businesses will be serving food and drink throughout the afternoon, and children's rides and a visit from Father Christmas are also planned.

Burley-in-Wharfedale Community Trust has asked residents to be aware of road closures during the event. Main Street will be closed between Station Road and Long Meadows from noon until 6.30pm, with all vehicles, including buses, diverted via Grange Road and Back Lane.

The Christmas lights have been a tradition in Burley-in-Wharfedale for more than 40 years, but this year's display will be installed differently following safety, electrical and insurance concerns.

In a statement, Burley-in-Wharfedale Community Trust said: "Following significant safety concerns about our assembly methods and the risk of accidents to volunteers and the public, as well as electrical problems with some of the lights and insurance issues, the Community Trust, with support from the Parish Council, will employ a specialist company to install most of the lights."

They added that changing health and safety and insurance requirements, alongside the growth of the village, had made the move to professional installation necessary.

The trust also paid tribute to the volunteers who have supported the display over the years.

They said: "We want to say a heartfelt thank you to all the volunteers who have brought Christmas light into our lives over so many years. Your time, care and hard work have helped make this tradition what it is."

This year, the lights will be concentrated in the centre of the village and around Grange Park, with organisers hoping to expand the display in future years.

They added: "We are sure the event will still be a wonderful opportunity for the village to celebrate."
