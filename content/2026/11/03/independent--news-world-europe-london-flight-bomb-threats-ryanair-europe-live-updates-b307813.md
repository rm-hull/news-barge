A wave of bomb scares across Europe prompted searches of at least seven passenger aircraft on Tuesday, grounding flights and causing unplanned diversions.

Two Italy-bound Ryanair flights from Lisbon and Dublin were forced to make emergency landings over anonymous tips regarding potential bomb threats, sources at Rome Fiumicino airport told the BBC.

A third Ryanair flight from Manchester to Murcia in Spain also received a threat, but turned out to be a hoax, local media said.

A fourth flight from London Heathrow to Poland also had to divert to another Warsaw airport due to a threat, while a fifth plane from Copenhagen received a scare but landed as planned in the Polish capital.

Bulgaria’s transport ministry separately reported that threats were made against a sixth flight travelling from Italy’s Bergamo to Sofia and a seventh from Dublin to the Bulgarian capital. The Bergamo flight was earlier identified by Bulgarian media as being from Israel.

## In focus: The threats against Wizz Air flights in recent days

A Wizz Air flight from Milan/Bergamo to Sofia was the target of a bomb threat on November 3, Bulgaria’s transport ministry said in a statement to Reuters.

The plane landed safely with 176 passengers on board, and no explosive devices were found during an inspection.

Another Wizz Air flight from London to Sofia diverted to Budapest on November 2 after a bomb threat, Hungary’s defence minister said. A Hungarian Gripen fighter was scrambled to escort the aircraft.

## In focus: The threats against Ryanair flights in recent days

Two Ryanair flights were searched at Rome’s Fiumicino airport following potential bomb threats on Tuesday, airport operator ADR said. A flight from Lisbon landed at 0940 CET and a flight from Dublin landed at 1030 CET, the latter escorted by the Italian Air Force until landing.

Both aircraft went through security protocols including searches and bomb sweeps of the plane, luggage, passengers and crew. ADR said the alert was cleared, there was no bomb threat to the airport itself, and other flights were not affected.

## Passengers in Murcia unaware of the threat until after the plane landed

Passengers on board the flight from Manchester to Murcia have said they were unaware of the threat until after the plane landed.

One passenger, called Sarah, told BBC 5 Live: “We weren’t told anything during the flight. The plane stopped quite suddenly and then we were kept on the runway for around 30 minutes.

“Then the fire engines turned up, the bomb squad turned up and we were asked to evacuate ... We were only later told that it was an actual threat, a bomb threat to the plane itself.”

## Manchester airport source 'not aware of any substance' to threat

A source at Manchester Airport told the Telegraph that they were “not aware of any substance” to the suspected bomb threat.

“These things do happen periodically,” the unnamed source told the newspaper.

“Hopefully it’s people being mischievous and seeing what problems they can cause,” they said.

## Updated: Flight to Sofia from Bergamo, not Israel

Bulgarian media reported earlier on a supposed bomb threat against a flight from Israel that landed in Sofia.

A spokesperson for the Bulgarian interior ministry has since clarified to the AFP news agency that the flight was a Wizz Air flight from Bergamo in Italy, not Israel.

## In focus: Murcia bomb scare only a hoax

Parts of an airport in Murcia, in southeastern Spain, were cordoned off on Tuesday after a bomb threat was reported on a Ryanair flight from Manchester.

But officials said the report proved to be a hoax, and the airport resumed normal operations after authorities found no explosives on board.

## In pictures: An SAS aircraft flying from Copenhagen lands, after receiving a bomb threat, at Warsaw Chopin Airport

An SAS flight from Copenhagen landed at Chopin as scheduled on Tuesday despite a bomb scare.

## The latest: At least seven flights grounded due to bomb threats across Europe

At least seven flights across Europe were grounded on Tuesday following bomb scares.

1) A flight from Lisbon to Rome was searched following an anonymous tip about a potential bomb threat, sources at Rome Fiumicino airport told the BBC.

2) The sources said another flight from Dublin to Rome was also searched.

3) A Ryanair flight from Manchester to Murcia in Spain also received a threat, but it turned out to be a hoax, local media said.

4) A flight from London Heathrow to Poland also had to divert to another Warsaw airport due to a threat.

5) And a fifth plane from Copenhagen received a scare but landed as planned in the Polish capital.

6) Bulgaria’s transport ministry separately reported that a threat was made against a flight travelling from Italy’s Bergamo to Sofia. The Bergamo flight was earlier identified by Bulgarian media as being from Israel.

7) And a seventh from Dublin to the Bulgarian capital was also affected.

## UPDATE: Bulgaria reports bomb threats on two planes landed at Sofia airport

Bomb threats were made against two planes traveling from Bergamo and Dublin to Bulgaria's capital Sofia, Bulgaria’s transport ministry said in a statement to Reuters on Tuesday.

Both landed safely and no explosive devices were found on inspection.

The Bergamo flight was operated by Wizz Air and was carrying 176 passengers, the statement said. The Dublin flight, operated by Ryanair, had 185 passengers on board.
