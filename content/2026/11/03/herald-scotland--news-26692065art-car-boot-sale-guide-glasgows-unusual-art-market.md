*(Image: James Murphy)*Whether you are looking to buy your first piece of artwork, grow your existing collection or find a unique Christmas gift, more than 100 contemporary artists from Scotland and beyond are coming to Glasgow this weekend for the Art Car Boot Sale.**What do you need to know?**

This year's pre-Christmas art sale will take place on Saturday, November 7 and Sunday, November 8 at The Briggait, a historic former fish market beside the River Clyde, which now serves as an events and performance venue and is home to Wasps Studios.

The event will bring together emerging and established artists, as well as artist-led organisations and galleries from across Scotland and beyond, offering a wide range of original artworks and limited-edition pieces.

Visitors will have the opportunity to meet artists, learn about their ideas, techniques and artistic practices, and discover the stories behind their work. Rather than browsing a conventional gallery space, they will be able to buy artwork directly from artists' cars, bikes and even skateboards.

From paintings and prints to sculpture, photography and ceramics, art lovers can discover unexpected finds, from preparatory sketches and exhibition maquettes to other works that might not usually be available to buy.

With prices ranging from £5 to £5,000, there are options for both first-time buyers and more established collectors.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21661781.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Claire Barclay untitled 2025 screen print *(Image: Claire Barclay)*

**Artists**

The line-up features artists working across a range of disciplines, including established names associated with major art prizes and Turner Prize nominees.

Among those exhibiting is Glasgow-based Rachel Duckhouse, whose drawing practice explores structures, patterns and flows within landscapes, as well as the dynamic relationships between different elements.

Glasgow-based, award-winning artist Toby Paterson will also be taking part. Inspired by architectural forms and ideas, his work includes wall drawings, paintings and sculptural installations.

Glasgow-based Lorna Macintyre creates photographic and sculptural installations, exploring the potential of materials within her practice and often pushing them in unexpected directions.

Jessica Wolfson specialises in charcoal drawings and oil paintings, while Claire Barclay, recognised for her large-scale installations, works mainly in sculpture and printmaking. Her work is informed by the history, mood and physicality of the spaces it inhabits.

Glasgow Print Studio will also present a new print by Andrew Cranston. A painter and storyteller, Cranston is known for his large-scale canvases, as well as paintings on the linen-bound covers of old books. His works often depict dreamlike worlds centred around domestic interiors.

Alongside established artists, this year's line-up also includes emerging names such as Nigerian-British multidisciplinary artist Grace Browne and Chicago-born, Glasgow-based printmaker and visual artist Richard Ehlert Domenico, whose work spans drawing, printmaking and photography.

Interdisciplinary artist Noru Innes works across sculpture, photography, text, collage and printmaking, while Frank Waterton creates objects from organic and natural materials, exploring themes of entropy and decay.

**Read moreGalleries**

A selection of galleries, studios and artist-led organisations from across Scotland will present work by a range of artists.

Among them is Edinburgh-based Garvald, which supports artists with additional support needs to create and exhibit their work, and Glasgow's Project Ability, an inclusive arts organisation providing opportunities for people with learning disabilities and mental ill-health.

Glasgow Print Studio, known for its fine-art printmaking, will be joined by Mars Print Studio, a screen-printing studio based at The Glue Factory, specialising in limited-edition fine-art prints.

Other participants include Horsepower, an independent artist-run gallery; Patricia Fleming Gallery, which represents emerging, mid-career and established contemporary artists; SaltSpace, a non-profit artist cooperative supporting emerging artists through exhibitions, studios and community programmes; and Rosie's Disobedient Press, an artist-led multidisciplinary small press.

Also exhibiting will be Sculpture House, an artist-led organisation from Paisley, and Shapeero Murray, an artist-run initiative.

**Beyond art**

Attendees will also be able to enjoy a selection of food and drink from Scottish vendors, including Soul Food Sisters, Smoked Tamago, The Crumbleologist and Sonny's Homemade Pasta.

Really Awesome Coffee will be serving hot drinks, while In The Round by YinYaang will offer prosecco, spritzes and cocktails. DJs will provide music throughout the weekend.

Tickets are available online or on the door, with a booking fee applying to online purchases. Standard adult tickets cost £10, with concession tickets priced at £5. The event is also child- and dog-friendly, with under-16s admitted free and baby-changing facilities available.
