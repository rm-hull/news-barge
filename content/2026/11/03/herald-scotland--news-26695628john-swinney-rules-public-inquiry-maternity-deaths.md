John Swinney has ruled out a public inquiry into maternity and neonatal deaths in Scotland despite demands from bereaved families for independent scrutiny of failures in NHS care.

The First Minister insisted an existing review led by Professor Christine McCourt would provide the answers needed to improve maternity services, following a demonstration attended by more than 100 parents in Glasgow at the weekend.

His refusal came after Scottish Labour's Jackie Baillie raised figures uncovered by The Herald showing NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde recorded 736 serious adverse events in maternity and neonatal services between 2019 and 2025, including 406 classified as involving a death.

The figures, obtained through freedom of information legislation, have prompted growing concerns about the handling of serious incidents and the effectiveness of internal NHS investigations.

At First Minister's Questions on Tuesday, the Scottish Labour deputy leader highlighted the case of Julie Keegan and Angus McLean, whose son Mason Scott McLean died aged just three days at the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital in January 2023.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21603838.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Julie Keegan and Angus McLean, whose baby son Mason died aged just three days in 2023, are calling for a public inquiry into maternity and neonatal deaths in Scotland. *(Image: Supplied)*

The couple led a protest outside Buchanan Galleries on Sunday, calling for a public inquiry into maternity and neonatal deaths and serious adverse events.

Ms Baillie told MSPs: "There were 736 serious adverse events between 2019 and 2025 in maternity and neonatal services, 406 of which involved a death. That is in Greater Glasgow and Clyde alone.

"The Cabinet Secretary for Health has met Julie Keegan and Angus McLean, whose baby son Mason died at the Queen Elizabeth University Hospital in 2023.

"Julie and Angus were protesting outside Buchanan Galleries on Sunday, joined by more than 100 parents.

"They don't believe that the serious adverse event reviews are working. They don't believe that the maternity review led by Professor McCourt goes far enough.

"They want a public inquiry. Does the First Minister agree?"

Mr Swinney expressed his condolences to the family but rejected the demand.

He said: "I want to express my sympathies to Julie Keegan and Angus McLean on the loss of their baby Mason, and to all the other families who joined them on the demonstration at the weekend."

He added: "My view is that the approach that is proposed to be taken by Professor McCourt will provide us with the basis for investigating the issues that have to be addressed within maternity services, and for making sure the Government is furnished with recommendations upon which we can act.

"I don't support a public inquiry."

The First Minister said work undertaken by Healthcare Improvement Scotland to scrutinise maternity units, alongside Professor McCourt's wider review, would provide an opportunity to examine concerns and identify improvements.

He added: "I believe that what we have established with Professor McCourt will provide the opportunity to examine these important issues, just as the Healthcare Improvement Scotland reports have done, to provide an open and transparent assessment of very important issues where there must be an improvement in performance."

The rejection comes just two days after bereaved families gathered in Glasgow demanding answers, accountability and greater transparency over the deaths of babies and mothers in maternity care.

Campaigners lined the steps outside Buchanan Galleries with children's shoes representing babies who had died, while parents held placards bearing photographs and names of their children.

Ms Keegan, an NHS intensive care nurse, has criticised the scope of the Scottish Government's independent maternity review, arguing that its focus on future improvements will not provide answers about historical failures.

Speaking at Sunday's demonstration, she said: "I want to know why my child was allowed to die from failure after failure after failure."

She added: "The review is only forward looking, it is not going to look at the past and does not dig deep on the hundreds of babies who have lost their lives."

Mr McLean said the family wanted an independent public inquiry to establish how many deaths could have been prevented.

"We want accountability and an independent public inquiry to find out how many of these babies could have been saved," he said.

"This is the only thing we can do now for Mason to honour him because he should still be here and we want to find out why he is not."

Mason died after developing sepsis, with failures in his care including missed tests that could have identified a life-threatening infection.

An internal NHS Greater Glasgow and Clyde review identified several systemic issues that may have contributed to his death.

His parents have repeatedly challenged the accuracy of the significant adverse event review into his care and have spent years seeking answers about what happened.

The official had strongly disputed the figure of 406 incidents involving a death, despite it coming directly from the board's own freedom of information response.

NHSGGC later apologised for the confusion and distress caused.

The board's figures recorded 736 Category 1 significant adverse events across maternity and neonatal services over seven years, with 406 classified under the outcome "Death".

The data does not establish how many deaths involved babies, nor does it mean that every death was caused by an adverse event.

However, campaigners argue the scale of the recorded incidents underlines the need for independent examination of how deaths and serious failures have been investigated.

The Scottish Government's nine-month independent review of maternity services, chaired by Professor McCourt, began last month and is expected to report its findings and recommendations in July 2027.

It follows concerns about maternity safety across Scotland, including staffing shortages, delays in care and the way health boards investigate serious incidents.

Healthcare Improvement Scotland has also been tasked with carrying out unannounced inspections of all 18 maternity units in Scotland.

Health Secretary Angela Constance has previously said families' experiences must be central to the review and that its focus on future improvements would identify the systemic changes needed to strengthen maternity services.

But bereaved parents have continued to argue that a forward-looking review cannot replace a public inquiry examining past failures and whether deaths could have been prevented.
