Freemasons are marking a month of remembrance and service.

Durham Freemasons are supporting the Royal British Legion Poppy Appeal and joining commemorative events across the North East to honour those who have served in the armed forces.

Andrew Foster, deputy head of Durham Freemasons, said: "Remembrance is an important time for all of us.

"It was a privilege to lay a wreath on behalf of Durham Freemasons at the national service, but remembrance is also about what we do in our own communities.

"The response to the Poppy Appeal has been tremendous.

"Darlington raised £95,000 last year and more than £4,000 was raised at Asda in just the first four days this year.

"I am proud to see so many Durham Freemasons giving their time to support the Royal British Legion across the region."

Mr Foster laid a wreath at the National Freemasons Service of Remembrance in Staffordshire, on behalf of Freemasons across Durham, and joined Freemasons from across England and Wales.

The national service was led by John Thompson, former head of Durham Freemasons and now assistant grand master of the United Grand Lodge of England.

He is responsible for membership and the Armed Forces Covenant at a national level.

Freemasons have also been volunteering for the Poppy Appeal in towns including Billingham, Hartlepool, Darlington, Sunderland, Bishop Auckland, Gateshead Metrocentre, and Chester le Street.

Durham Freemasons are also being encouraged to wear a special Remembrance tie at their meetings throughout the month.

Sales of the ties to members raised £5,000 for veterans' charities last year.

Similar sales this year are expected to bring the total raised over two years to around £10,000.

Representatives will take part in Remembrance Sunday events on November 8, including in Durham City where John Watts, head of Durham Freemasons, will lay a wreath.
