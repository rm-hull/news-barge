## York exhibition reveals new insight into Guy Fawkes and Gunpowder Plot

York’s Bar Convent has revealed new research around a previously unexplored personal connection relating to the Gunpowder Plot *(Image: Simon Dewhurst)*

New research has revealed a previously unexplored personal connection behind the motives of the Gunpowder Plot conspirators.

Guy Fawkes and fellow York plotters, including the Wright brothers, are now believed to have known Margaret Clitherow, executed in 1586 accused of helping Catholic priests.

The findings have emerged from a new exhibition at York’s Bar Convent, which explores the context, conspiracy and consequences of the 1605 plot to blow up the Houses of Parliament.

Dr Hannah Thomas said: "Guy Fawkes, John and Christopher Wright were pupils at St Peter’s School in York.

"At the time of Margaret Clitherow’s death, Fawkes and Christopher Wright were 16, while John Wright was 18."

Dr Thomas said the execution would have had a "profound effect" on the young men, and evidence now suggests they may have known her personally.

A priest’s vestment known as the Middleton Vestment is believed to have belonged to Robert Middleton, Margaret Clitherow’s nephew and a contemporary of Guy Fawkes.

He was executed in 1601 for being a priest, an event that may have further influenced Fawkes’ actions.

The exhibition, The Gunpowder Plot: York’s Untold Story, is open at the Bar Convent until December 5, from 10am to 5pm, Monday to Saturday.
