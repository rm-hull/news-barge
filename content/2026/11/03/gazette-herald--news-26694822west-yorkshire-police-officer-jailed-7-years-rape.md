A POLICE officer has been jailed for seven years for rape after being convicted by a York jury.

Joe Lavender showed no reaction as Judge Simon Hickey handed down the sentence at York Crown Court on Tuesday (November 3).

Lavender, 45, served as a West Yorkshire Police officer for 22 years before he was suspended from duty when he was charged with rape.

West Yorkshire Police said Lavender will face misconduct proceedings but was barred from working in any role in policing in the future following his conviction last month.

Judge Hickey said Lavender "pinned" the victim down before raping her.

"She asked to stop and you didn’t," the judge told Lavender, adding: "You carried on despite her saying stop ... You ignored her wishes until you were satisfied."

As well as jailing Lavender, Judge Hickey passed a 15-year-old order prohibiting Lavender from contacting the woman.

He also said Lavender, of Castleford, must tell the authorities about any new relationship he forms within seven days of doing so for the rest of his life.

The victim twice gave evidence against Lavender after a jury in the first trial had been unable to reach a verdict.

In a statement read in court, the victim said she struggled to trust people and form relationships after she was raped.

She also said she felt guilt and shame and does not trust the police due to Lavender formerly being a serving officer.

"If I had not been approached by the police, I don’t feel I would have come forward and reported this crime because I didn’t think I would have been believed," she said.

Simon Myerson KC, defending, said Lavender apologised to the victim the day after the rape and had no previous convictions.

"This was an astonishingly poor decision," Mr Myerson said of the rape by Lavender.

## Police praise victim for coming forward

After Lavender was convicted of rape, Detective Chief Superintendent Tanya Wilkins, head of West Yorkshire Police’s Professional Standards Directorate, praised the victim for coming forward.

"The victim has lived with the trauma of what PC Lavender did to her for two decades before coming forward to report this offence in 2024," DCS Wilkins said.

"I would like to express my admiration for the bravery she has shown. I do not underestimate the courage it takes to report something like this, and even more so knowing that by that time the man responsible was a police officer.

"If anyone has been a victim of rape or any other sexual offence, I would urge them to please report it. Please know that we will listen and we will act, it doesn’t matter who the perpetrator is or where they may work.

"Following this court case, we will now continue with misconduct proceedings to ensure PC Lavender is barred from working in any role in policing in the future."
