**The Scottish Government has again been urged to clarify its plans to bring a £2 bus fare cap to Edinburgh.**

The scheme has already been rolled out in the Highlands and Islands and in the west of Scotland, as well as in Aberdeen and Aberdeenshire from April 2027.

However, the government has yet to announce when the cap will be expanded to the Scottish capital.

“The UK Government has confirmed a £2 bus fare cap in England outside London throughout 2027. Scotland’s scheme is expanding, but Edinburgh still has no confirmed inclusion date,” City of Edinburgh Council’s Transport and Environment Committee convener Stephen Jenkinson wrote in a Facebook post.

“I have already written to the Scottish Government seeking Edinburgh’s inclusion,” he said on Monday.

“Our passengers deserve affordable fares too, with proper funding to protect services and accommodate additional demand.”

![SNP Westminster leader Stephen Flynn](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18795607.jpg?type=mds-article-620)SNP MSP Stephen Flynn. *(Image: PA)*

In a letter to Cabinet Secretary for Transport Stephen Flynn in August, Councillor Jenkinson wrote that it would be “unacceptable” if bus users in the capital were “treated as secondary to passengers elsewhere”.

“It is essential that residents and visitors in Edinburgh are afforded the same benefits as those living in other parts of the country,” he added.

“In light of these concerns, I would be grateful if the Scottish Government could give urgent consideration to including Edinburgh within the £2 bus fare cap scheme at the same time as other named areas.”

Councillor Jenkinson also asked for assurances about the impact of a cap on the municipally-owned Lothian Buses, which currently sets fares at £2.40 for a single ticket or £4.80 for a return if purchased via contactless, and whether Edinburgh Trams would be included in the price cap scheme.

He wrote: “The introduction of a national fare policy recognises and protects the particular circumstances of Edinburgh’s municipal transport network.

“Any funding or reimbursement arrangements associated with the £2 fare cap must ensure that they do not inadvertently weaken the financial position of Lothian Buses, distort the relationship between bus and tram services, or undermine the ability of the City of Edinburgh

Council and its arms-length transport companies to continue investing in and improving public transport.”

“A price cap limited to bus services could have unintended consequences for tram patronage and revenue, particularly if passengers are encouraged to switch from tram to bus purely on the basis of price.”

In response, Mr Flynn wrote in early September: “By the end of the parliament, we will take forward legislation to put in place a £2 bus fare across Scotland. We will announce timescales for further roll-out to parliament in due course.

“At our meeting on 19 August, I confirmed my intention to work with the City of Edinburgh Council to ensure a smooth implementation of the scheme and provide opportunity to feed into the future process.

“This will also involve stakeholder engagement on wider considerations of the impact of the bus fare cap on other modes of transport, including Edinburgh Trams.”

![Has Edinburgh City Council made the wrong decision on the city's trams?](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21454738.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Will the £2 fare cap include trams? *(Image: Gordon Terris)*

Speaking to The Herald earlier this year, Lothian Group chief executive Sarah Boyd said the organisation was ready to help the government expand the cap to Edinburgh.

“In any of the discussions I've had about the two pound fare cap, I have been clear that we [need to] model this properly,” she said. “So that's everything from how the scheme is going to operate, to what the reimbursement model looks like.

“There's some really good, rich data already there that we are more than happy to share and to use in terms of the discussions with the Scottish Government.

“If we want world-class public transport in Edinburgh, in Scotland, then of course we've still got work to do in Edinburgh to make it even better.”

However, some organisations have expressed scepticism about the scheme.

In September, a campaign group representing Edinburgh's bus users said it is “highly sceptical" of bus fare caps.

Edinburgh Bus Users Group (EBUG) questioned the scheme’s efficacy, telling The Herald: “If buses remain stuck in heavy urban congestion, a lower fare does nothing to fix poor journey times, unreliability, or infrequent scheduling.

“Regular commuters who rely on weekly or monthly bundles may find their customised savings wiped out or disrupted if ticketing structures are forced to adapt to a blunt single-fare cap.”
