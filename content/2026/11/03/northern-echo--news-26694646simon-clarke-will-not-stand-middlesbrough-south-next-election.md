A former Tory MP ousted in 2024 has confirmed he will not stand again at the next general election.

Ex-Conservative MP Simon Clarke said he will not seek re-election in Middlesbrough South and East Cleveland, citing family commitments and his job in London.

Mr Clarke represented the seat from 2017 to 2024, and served as Chief Secretary to the Treasury.

The former Cabinet minister said he still hopes to return to Parliament, but will seek a seat where he and his family can live and work.

Announcing his decision, he said: "Some messages are rather hard to write, and this one certainly is.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/17694907.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Simon Clarke *(Image: SUBMIT)*

"I am writing to let you all know that I will not be putting my name forward to be the Conservative candidate for Middlesbrough South and East Cleveland for the next General Election."

He called representing the area where he was born and raised a "huge honour and a total pleasure", and thanked voters who backed him in the 2017, 2019 and 2024 elections.

Mr Clarke said: "This is not a decision I have made lightly. Representing the constituency where I was born and grew up was a huge honour and a total pleasure from the first day to the very last, and I am so grateful to all of you who put your trust in me in 2017 and 2019, and when I fell very narrowly short of an against-the-odds victory in 2024.

"I can honestly say I did everything in my ability to reward that faith, and I think we achieved some brilliant things for the area, both small and large."

Mr Clarke held several senior government posts, including Secretary of State for Levelling Up, Housing and Communities, before taking over at centre-right think tank Onward.

He said he was stepping aside because a long election campaign would make it harder to spend time with his family.

"Both my wife and I work in London, and I have two sons who need their Dad (and vice versa)," he said.

"It is therefore the case that if I am to try to return to the House of Commons, it must be in a constituency where we can live, become fully part of the community and carry on our jobs and our lives during what could be a multi-year campaign before an election that may not come until May 2029."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21664741.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Simon Clarke, former MP *(Image: SUBMIT)*

The former MP said he had told the South Tees Conservative Association.

He said: "The Conservative campaign to win Middlesbrough South and East Cleveland will require a candidate who can give absolutely everything to it - and my personal circumstances mean that cannot be me."

Mr Clarke said he would return to Teesside to campaign for his Conservative successor and support the party’s candidates in the May 2027 local elections.

He said: "What we were trying to do for the area continues to matter, and is much bigger than me."

But Mr Clarke made clear that he still wants to return to Parliament.

He added: "I love public service, which is why I took up the role of leading the think tank Onward after the last election, helping to rebuild the centre-right policy offer for the future.

"And I hope to return to Parliament, and a job that I believe really matters."
