# DWP £10 Christmas Bonus 2026 with 24 benefits that qualify from December 1

## The Department for Work and Pensions has confirmed the £10 Christmas Bonus will be paid in December 2026

The Department for Work and Pensions has announced the Christmas Bonus will make its return under Andy Burnham in December 2026. The DWP will reinstate the additional £10 payment for benefit recipients during the final month of the year.

The bonus is distributed at a flat rate of £10 to those receiving any of 24 qualifying benefits. According to the DWP, eligibility requires you to be present or 'ordinarily resident' in the UK, Channel Islands, Isle of Man or Gibraltar throughout the qualifying week.

Recipients must also be claiming at least one of the 24 specified benefits during the 'qualifying week'. The DWP has confirmed payments will commence from December 1, explaining: "The Christmas Bonus is a one-off tax-free £10 payment made before Christmas, paid to people who get certain benefits in the qualifying week. This is normally the first full week of December. You do not need to claim - you should get paid automatically."

There's no need to submit a claim for the Christmas Bonus - it should arrive automatically. Should you believe you're entitled to the payment but haven't received it by 1 January, get in touch with either the Jobcentre Plus office handling your payments or the Pension Service, reports Birmingham Live.

All benefits, pensions and allowances are typically transferred into an account, such as your bank account. The payment may appear as 'DWP XB' on your bank statement.

* Adult Disability Payment.
* Armed Forces Independence Payment.
* Attendance Allowance.
* Carer's Allowance.
* Carer Support Payment.
* Child Disability Payment.
* Constant Attendance Allowance (paid under Industrial Injuries or War Pensions schemes).
* Contribution-based Employment and Support Allowance (once the main phase of the benefit is entered after the first 13 weeks of claim).
* Disability Living Allowance.
* Incapacity Benefit at the long-term rate.
* Industrial Death Benefit (for widows or widowers).
* Mobility Supplement.
* Pension Age Disability Payment.
* Pension Credit - the guarantee element.
* Personal Independence Payment (PIP).
* Scottish Adult Disability Living Allowance (SADLA).
* Severe Disablement Allowance (transitionally protected).
* State Pension (including Graduated Retirement Benefit).
* Unemployability Supplement or Allowance (paid under Industrial Injuries or War Pensions schemes).
* War Disablement Pension at State Pension age.
* War Widow's Pension.
* Widowed Mother's Allowance.
* Widowed Parent's Allowance.
* Widow's Pension.
