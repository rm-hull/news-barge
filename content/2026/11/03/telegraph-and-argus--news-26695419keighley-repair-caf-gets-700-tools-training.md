A scheme has secured a significant financial boost to expand its services and support volunteers.

Based at the Woodville Activity Centre in Keighley, the Repair Café, which is organised by Yorgreen CIC and supported by Woodville Community, has received £700.

## Money to be spent on specialist tools and training

The funding comes from the Craven Trust and will be used to buy specialist tools and provide training for volunteers, including portable appliance testing (PAT) training.

Shaun O’Hare, director of Yorgreen CIC, said: "We are very grateful to the Craven Trust for supporting the Repair Café.

"The grant has enabled us to purchase specialist tools along with a PAT tester and training for 10 fixers as PAT testers, including tenants, volunteers, and young people on placement at Woodville."

## What does the repair café do?

The repair café offers free help to fix household items, electricals, computers, garden tools, and bicycles.

It aims to save money, cut waste, and prevent usable goods from going to landfill.

The first event launched in August and, according to organisers, attracted strong community support and highlighted the demand for local repair services.

## When is the next event planned?

The next café is planned for Saturday, November 14.

It will run from 2pm to 4pm and will include clothing alterations and textile repairs, alongside electrical, mechanical, and digital items.

The volunteer team includes residents, Keighley Lions Club members, and representatives from Yorgreen CIC, Code For Good CIC, and Woodville Metalworks.

Organisers hope the continued success of the café will strengthen Keighley’s reputation as a centre for community-led sustainability.
