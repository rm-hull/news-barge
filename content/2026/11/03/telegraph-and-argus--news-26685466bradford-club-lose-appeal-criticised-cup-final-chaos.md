THE Bradford Mutual Sunday School League have drawn a line in the sand over one of the saddest days in their 130-year history.

The Sir James Roberts Cup final - one of the two major finals in the league alongside the Rizues T20 Cup - between Omars and Bradford Phoenix was abandoned in August at Bankfoot CC after Phoenix walked off and refused to continue.

Omars accepted their punishment from the league's disciplinary panel of three players being given a Level One offence, two being banned for two weeks - plus a suspended one-week ban until the end of the 2027 season - while their captain received a two-week suspended ban, also to the end of the 2027 season.

But Bradford Phoenix appealed against the verdict on them, feeling that it was too harsh.

An independent disciplinary panel meeting was therefore held at Sandy Lane Community Centre and the original verdict of the league was in general upheld, although the independent panel felt that the sanction was too lenient and that all 11 Bradford Phoenix players could have been banned rather than just three.

Mutual Sunday School League chairman Dr Rizwan Hameed said at the league’s rules revision meeting at Al-Hira Islamic Mosque: “What happened at the Sir James Roberts Cup final was a sad day for the league, and all because of a few individuals’ actions.

“Such unsportsmanlike acts demotivate people who are trying to build the Bradford Mutual Sunday School League brand.

“On behalf of the league’s executive committee, I request that clubs learn a lesson from this outcome to avoid any such repeat and request all member clubs to stand united against such actions for the league’s sake.”

Dr Hameed added: “Both the league and the league’s executive committee deserved better than this and I haven’t yet found out what impact this will have on sponsors Nafees.

“The Bradford Mutual Sunday School League is your family and why would a club do this?”

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21652381.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Sir James Roberts Cup was first played way back in 1909 and has a rich history, with losing finalists Denholme Clough pictured here in 1986. *(Image: T&A)*

He continued: “The independent panel felt that our sentences were too lenient and that all 11 Bradford Phoenix players should have been banned instead of just three.

“They also said that we should have worked more closely to get a resolution and that our league rules were clunky, and that is why we are looking at all of our league rules and regulations and setting up a rules sub-committee to make them better.”

Bradford Phoenix walked off after a dispute involving an on-strike batter returning the ball to a fielder and refused to continue despite numerous requests, and they didn’t attend the presentation ceremony either, although it has now been conceded that their players should receive their runners-up medals.

Dr Hameed revealed: “Bradford Phoenix have been banned from all cups within the league for 2027 and 2028 and they have been told to pay costs of £1,186.

“This incident, as it was in front of league officials, sponsors and spectators, plus Bradford Phoenix boycotting the presentation ceremony, caused serious damage to the league’s reputation.”

The initial hearing was held on September 11 and the independent disciplinary hearing on October 18.

The purpose of the second hearing was to review the grounds of appeal submitted by Bradford Phoenix CC, consider all representations made by both parties, assess whether the original decision was reached fairly and reasonably, and determine whether the disciplinary decision should be upheld, amended or overturned.

Clarification was sought from Bradford Phoenix in relation to the player bans imposed on their team members, and the club stated that the bans commenced on September 14.

Phoenix confirmed that they did not dispute that they had walked off during the final and boycotted the subsequent presentation ceremony.

The independent disciplinary panel considered documentation submitted by Bradford Phoenix, the original disciplinary decision and associated reasoning, the representations made by the league, oral submissions presented during the appeal hearing and the responses provided by all representatives during questioning.

The panel further considered whether the original disciplinary process had been fair, whether the findings of the original hearing were supported by the available evidence, if any new evidence or information materially affected the original decision and that the sanction imposed was reasonable and proportionate.

The panel found that both parties had been afforded a fair opportunity to present their cases to the independent panel, relevant evidence had been considered by the original decision makers, the appeal hearing did not identify any significant procedural irregularity that would render the original process unfair and that the grounds advanced by Bradford Phoenix CC had been fully considered and tested during the hearing.

The independent panel was satisfied that the original disciplinary body had acted within its powers and had reached its decision after considering the evidence before it.

It felt that the player charges and subsequent bans were lenient but accepted the decision made at the original disciplinary hearing.

No further consideration was given to increasing this sanction, but the panel felt that the league had the opportunity to charge all of the Bradford Phoenix players.

The panel also found that Bradford Phoenix players’ actions had caused serious reputational damage to the league and contravened the Spirit of Cricket, and they noted that team failed to complete the final despite being given numerous opportunities to do so.

This was compounded by their boycotting of the presentation.

The panel acknowledged that Bradford Phoenix CC commented that had their senior officials been present at the final, the walk off would not have occurred.

The panel felt that this was difficult to put to the test given that it is speculation and that Phoenix’s captain on the day should have had the authority and control to deal with his players’ conduct and behaviour.

The runners-up medals will be made available to Bradford Phoenix’s players.

Having considered all written and oral submissions, the appeal panel unanimously determined that there were insufficient grounds to overturn the decision made by the league but did agree that the costs involved with the hearing be reduced from £1,361 to £1,186.

The appeal submitted by Bradford Phoenix CC was therefore dismissed and the original decision of the Bradford Mutual Sunday School League was partially upheld.

The panel also ruled that the original bans on Bradford Phoenix and its players remain in force, and that the runners-up trophies be collected by Bradford Phoenix from the league by mutual arrangement.

The panel also made the following observations:

• There is no mechanism in the Bradford Mutual Sunday School League’s constitution to ask for any costs pertaining to this appeal; and that the league’s constitution is very “clunky” and “cumbersome”.

• The panel would recommend that these are reviewed to reflect current practice and procedures, which may involve working with their respective Cricket Development Officer and the league’s executive committee.

• The panel also said that all parties, upon reflection, should have worked more closely together to enable a swift resolution, but noted that the league relies on the goodwill and volunteering of officials to maintain its standing and operation.

It also noted that Bradford Phoenix’s email of September 15 said that they felt that the players’ bans were too harsh and that their appeal also focused on procedural irregularities, which the panel felt were ambiguous.

Dr Hameed noting at the recent league meeting that these procedural irregularities were not followed up by Bradford Phoenix.
