The well-known Ripon Farm Services has reported a 21 per cent drop in turnover.

The agricultural machinery giant, which employs around 350 people across the region, recorded the fall to £136 million in its latest annual report.

This decline was attributed to reduced demand for new agricultural products and lower used equipment sales following a stock reduction programme.

Despite the downturn, the company saw positive results from its strategy to cut more than £5 million in stock, which reduced interest costs and helped lower overall debt by £4.6 million in the year to January 31 2026.

A Strategic Report in the annual accounts said: "The year to 31 January 2026 has been conducted against a backdrop of continued challenging market conditions, with the agricultural machinery market contracting further.

"Industry-wide demand remained relatively subdued, with UK tractor sales reaching historically low levels.

"Despite this, the Group has responded decisively and continued to execute its strategy of strengthening commercial and operational performance, supported by a clear financial focus on cost control. cash generation and market share growth."

The group reported an improved gross margin of 11.9 per cent, up 1.8 per cent from the previous year, driven by pricing discipline and better performance in used equipment sales.

Operating profit before exceptional costs, interest, depreciation, and amortisation (EBITDA) rose by 16.6 per cent to £1.6 million, up from £1.38 million the previous year.

The pre-tax loss also narrowed to £3.3 million from £4.7 million.

The report continued: "While overall market volumes declined during the year, the Group has successfully increased market share despite the contraction in demand, placing it in a strong position for future growth.

"In the continued downturn significant cost saving measures were implemented during the year, which will generate ongoing annualised savings, in addition to the cost reduction measures implemented in the prior year."

Stock levels fell from £39.3 million to £34.4 million, mainly due to reduced used equipment inventory.

Net debt at year-end stood at £25.5 million, down from £30.1 million.

The company said it remains focused on evolving operational and commercial efficiency, with improved market sentiment and a stronger order book expected to support future revenue and profit growth.

Ripon Farm Services has a long history dating back nearly a century to the 1930s when Glovers of Ripon began selling cars.

Chairman Geoff Brown began his career with Glovers in 1961 and later co-founded Ripon Farm Services in 1982 alongside Maurice Hymas, Bill Houseman, and two other directors.

The company now operates branches across Yorkshire and the North East, stretching as far north as Stockton-on-Tees and as far south as Lincoln.
