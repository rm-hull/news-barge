CLOSURES of the A59 are due to take place in November as the Kex Gill realignment project nears completion.

In an update on the Kex Gill realignment, North Yorkshire Council says with much of the new stretch of road now open, the scheme is 'moving towards an important milestone'.

This month's final resurfacing and road-marking works at the 'tie-ins' are 'key steps towards removing the remaining restrictions', says the council.

Church Hill, at Blubberhouses, is due to reopen on Friday (November 6), slightly later than originally planned.

This, says the council, will allow the remaining work to be finished for further temporary traffic lights at the A59 junction, helping to avoid delays and reduce safety risks.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21664109.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Kex Gill realignment project is nearing completion *(Image: NYC)*

To complete the tie-ins between the new road and the existing A59, overnight closures will take place between Bolton Abbey roundabout and the crossroads at Menwith Hill, near Harrogate.

From Monday, November 9 to Friday, November 13, the A59 will be closed each night from 7pm to 6am between Bolton Abbey roundabout and Menwith Hill crossroads.

Motorists will be diverted via Ilkley and Otley and with local access maintained 'where possible'.

From Monday, November 16 to November 19, Church Hill and Hall Lane junctions with the A59 will be closed during the day for final resurfacing.

The A59 will remain open during this time.

From November 20 to November 27, the A59 will be closed again each night from 7pm to 6am, between Bolton Abbey roundabout and the crossroads at Menwith Hill, with the diversion once again via Ilkley and Otley.

North Yorkshire Council says the timetable of the work is dependent on the weather and may change at short notice if conditions are unsuitable.

Motorists are also advised to allow extra time for journeys and to follow the signed diversion routes.

A spokesperson for North Yorkshire Council said: "The focus is now on completing the final connections and surfacing so that the remaining restrictions can be lifted.

"These are the last steps towards opening the road network fully, although landscaping, work to the old carriageway and aftercare will continue beyond that milestone.

"Thank you to local communities, businesses and road users for your continued patience and cooperation.

"We appreciate that the November closures will affect journeys, but this essential finishing work brings us closer to a fully connected network with the remaining traffic restrictions removed."
