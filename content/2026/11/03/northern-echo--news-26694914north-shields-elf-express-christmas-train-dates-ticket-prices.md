A much-loved festive train ride is returning to the North East this Christmas.

The Elf Express will return for its sixth year at Stephenson Steam Railway in North Shields, with journeys running on December 5, 6, 12, 13, 19, 20, 22, and 23.

Families will embark on a 40-minute heritage train ride filled with Christmas songs, entertainment, and photo opportunities with Santa and his elves.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21665169.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1727 TWAM Elf Express at Stephensons Works *(Image: North East Museums/Colin Davison)*

The experience begins with a live, interactive performance from Santa and his elves before passengers board the festively decorated train.

Every child aged 0–12 will receive a special gift from Santa, and all guests will be treated to a hot or cold drink and a seasonal sweet treat.

The full experience lasts 90 minutes and includes time to explore the museum.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21665172.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1785 NEM Stephenson Railway Mu. Elf Express *(Image: North East Museums/Colin Davison)*

Families can choose between open-plan carriages or private compartments.

Tickets start from £20.50 per person.

Train times are 9.45am, 11.15am, 12.45pm, 2.15pm, and 3.45pm.

The Elf Express is part of a wider programme of Christmas events from North East Museums.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21665170.jpg?type=mds-article-620)1727 TWAM Elf Express at Stephensons Works *(Image: North East Museums/Colin Davison)*

A spokesperson for the event said: "Children can meet the Chief Elves and the main man himself, as they join passengers for a train journey packed with Christmas tunes, entertainment and plenty of opportunities to capture memories with festive photographs."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21665164.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: North East Museums/Colin Davison)*

Stephenson Steam Railway offers a journey back in time to the days when coal powered the region.

Throughout the year, visitors can ride a two-mile industrial railway in a 1950s British Railway carriage, passing factories and businesses once served by the line.

The locomotives climb steep gradients from the Tyne towards the historic coalfields of Northumberland.
