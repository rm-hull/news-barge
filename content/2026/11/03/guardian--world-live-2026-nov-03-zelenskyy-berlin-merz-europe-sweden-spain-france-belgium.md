The UK government confirmed that two of the planes affected had departed from British airports.

A spokesperson said:

The UK has one of the most robust aviation security systems in the world, with well-tested processes for dealing with any potential threats, as well as malicious hoax threats, like those we’ve seen today.

Our world-leading police and intelligence agencies work closely every day to keep us safe, using the tools they have to assess and respond to potential threats.

Bulgaria confirms bomb threats against flights from Bergamo, Dublin, not Israel

There is also an update from Bulgaria, with the country’s transport ministry telling Reuters the bomb threats were actually made against two planes from Bergamo in Italy and Dublin in Ireland, and not from Israel as previously reported.

That brings the total to seven planes (that we know of).

Back to Berlin, Ukraine’s Zelenskyy also rejects Russia’s suggestion of a meeting in Moscow as “counterproductive.” He says what’s needed is serious talks, and not a gimmick.

He also insists that Russia has no advantage on the frontline at the moment.

Merz then adds that the 19 November meeting will decide further support for Ukraine in 2027, and that concludes the press.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy speaks during a press conference with the German chancellor in Berlin. Photograph: Filip Singer/EPA

Two false bomb threats reported on Italy-bound Ryanair flights

Meanwhile, two more false bomb threats were reported in Italy involving Ryanair flights from Lisbon and Dublin to Rome’s Fiumicino Airport, according to the Italian newspapers La Repubblica and Corriere della Sera.

The plane from Dublin was escorted by two Italian air force fighter jets, newspaper reported.

In both cases, routine checks were confirmed as negative, meaning no threat was identified, SkyTG24 said.

That brings the total of flights affected by false alarms today to at least six, up from four reported earlier (that we know of).

Security guarantees, Ukraine reconstructions, energy and Black Sea truce to be discussed in Abu Dhabi, Zelenskyy says

Asked about his expectations for the Abu Dhabi talks, Zelenskyy says he hopes to see future security guarantees for Ukraine to be covered, alongside a “prosperity package” that would help with the reconstruction of Ukraine.

He also says he hopes to discuss a proposal on energy, Black Sea truce, which is “in the interest of not only Ukraine, but also Russia and various nations in Africa.”

He reiterates that “there can be no stable peace unless Europe is at the negotiating table.”

Merz for his part says that “it is crucial for Russia to finally engage in serious negotiations.”

Europe remains 'crucial' for ending the war in Ukraine, Zelenskyy says, as he confirms Abu Dhabi talks

Responding, Zelenskyy thanks Germany for its continued help, including on air defence, and says that while many people will be following the events in the US today’s midterm elections, “the role of Europe – and specifically Germany – remains crucial.”

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelenskyy attends a press conference with German chancellor Friedrich Merz in Berlin. Photograph: Lisi Niesner/Reuters

He says he briefed Merz on the latest attacks on Ukraine.

He also confirms the upcoming Abu Dhabi talks on 10-11 November (11:21), which he says hopefully “will pave the way for a trilateral meeting at the level of heads of states and government.”

He also confirms further talks on Ukraine will be held in Brussels on Wednesday (12:41), as per the European Commission’s announcement earlier, and that the coalition of the willing will meet in Berlin on 19 November.

Merz declares Germany will keep supporting Ukraine as he warns about Russia's attacks, impact of Black Sea strikes on food security

Germany’s Merz begins by saying Zelenskyy’s visit comes “at a critical moment” as Russian attacks on Ukraine continued with “undiminished – indeed, increasing – severity and ruthlessness.”

German chancellor Friedrich Merz attends a joint press conference with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy, in Berlin, Germany. Photograph: Fabrizio Bensch/Reuters

He says Ukraine “can continue to rely on Germany,” with more support from Berlin in the coming months, including on air defence, energy, and broader winter-related aid.

Merz also warns that the outcome of the war in Ukraine “will determine European security for a long time to come.”

He says Germany will be coordinating with France and the UK, and will host a meeting of the Coalition of the Willing in Berlin later this month.

The German chancellor also talks of growing concerns about the broader effects of the continuing war, including a potential “global food crisis” triggered by Russia’s continued attacks on trading routes in the Black Sea.

He says the upcoming Abu Dhabi talks are “the right step forward,” and Europe hopes to play its part.

In the meantime, Berlin and partners will “increase the pressure on Moscow and continue to support Ukraine,” he says.
