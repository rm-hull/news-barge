A SCHEME planned for York which could help the city face global uncertainty has taken a step forward.

City of York Council has awarded a contract worth £200,000 to consultants Arcadis to draw up a full business case for the Harewood Whin Green Energy Park, in Rufforth.

Council officials said the work would provide a full understanding of the project and prepare it for delivery.

They added it came after the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority provided grant funding towards the business case which the council accepted in May.

It follows a decision in September last year to proceed with the project which is set to see a solar field generating up to 16.5MW of power created at the former landfill site.

Council Labour environment executive member Cllr Jenny Kent said at the time it would help make York less reliant on energy coming from the Middle East and the US.

Cllr Kent said: “Now more than ever we’ve got uncertainty around the world and our previous sources of energy are not reliable.

“They’re polluting and killing our planet, the recent fires on the North Yorkshire Moors are a reminder that we need to act now before climate change gets worse, this is a great step on York’s journey.”

Then chief strategy officer Claire Foale said the project could initially save the council up to £195,000 a year but there were challenges including with plans to build it in phases.

September's decision followed the approval of a planning application in April to build the solar array at the Tinker Lane site.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19922488.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Harewood Whin *(Image: Google)*

Plans for a site generating up to 16.5MW were scaled back from the 28MW initially planned due to safety concerns over panels glinting at aircraft using the nearby Rufforth East Airfield.

Power generated by up to 16.5MW would be enough to power around 11,000 homes.

Council estimates put potential earnings at between £7 million and £17 million over 30 years.

The business case is set be done in the next nine months.

It is set to look at the project's strategic direction, financial planning, technical designs, procurement, ground investigations, grid connections and other issues.
