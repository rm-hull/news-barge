A £150 million government cash boost could help convert a former airbase near York into accommodation for 1,200 asylum seekers.

Treasury documents reveal the government is planning to spend £150 million next year on funding "additional large sites to accommodate asylum seekers and facilitate the government’s commitment to end the use of asylum hotels".

Part of the investment could be made at the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse site where controversial plans have been lodged to house up to 1,200 single adult male asylum seekers.

The government has faced criticism from across the political divide over the proposals, which follow similar plans in 2022 that were abandoned following local opposition and a legal challenge from the then Hambleton District Council.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21659491.jpg?type=mds-article-620)From above: RAF Linton-On-Ouse *(Image: Owen Humphreys/Press Association)*

Sir Keir Starmer’s government announced in June that plans to house asylum seekers at Linton-on-Ouse were back on the table, and the proposals have not been ruled out by Andy Burnham since he became prime minister.

The Home Office has said it is working with local authorities on the plans to rehome asylum seekers after it shuts asylum hotels.

North Yorkshire Council leader Cllr Carl Les, who said he was not told about the proposals for RAF Linton-on-Ouse before they appeared in the media, has asked to meet with the prime minister to discuss the plans.

Mr Burnham is yet to respond publicly to Cllr Les’s invitation but previously said he will ensure the Home Office is "fully accountable to people in North Yorkshire on this particular issue and the precise area concerned".

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/16430661.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Supplied)*

When asked about the plans for Linton-on-Ouse in September, Mr Burnham told the BBC: "That relates to a previous government and we don't always get to see why that decision was taken."

Mr Burnham also told the broadcaster he was prepared to look at the concerns of Linton-on-Ouse residents and "what could be done to mitigate those concerns".

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21612288.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Prime minister Andy Burnham *(Image: Stefan Rousseau)*

Linton-on-Ouse residents have objected to the asylum centre plans, arguing the small village would be overwhelmed by its population almost doubling should the proposals go ahead.

"York is around ten miles away and Easingwold around nine. This is not dispersal across a large urban area with extensive infrastructure. The immediate impact will inevitably be concentrated upon one small rural community," said a spokesperson for the Linton Action Group, which is campaigning against the proposals.

A Home Office spokesperson said: "The government is closing every asylum hotel, saving taxpayers over £1 billion on asylum support since the election.

"We are working to fairly house asylum seekers across the country, moving asylum seekers into alternative accommodation, including former military sites which are designed to be self-contained to reduce impact on the local community.

"As part of the process, we engage with local authorities and police forces to ensure the safety and security of those in the surrounding area."
