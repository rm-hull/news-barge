A new multi-million-pound facility designed to help keep the River Tees operating safely has officially opened.

PD Ports has launched the purpose-built marine base at Cargo Fleet Wharf in Middlesbrough, strengthening its long-term commitment to the River Tees and the surrounding economy.

The facility will support the safe operation of both the River Tees and the Port of Hartlepool under PD Ports’ role as Statutory Harbour Authority.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651829.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The three-storey facility brings PD Ports’ marine operations and engineering teams together in a modern base at the heart of the river they serve *(Image: Michael Sreenan)*

Paul Foreman, chief executive officer at PD Ports, said: "The River Tees is a major economic asset for our region and our responsibility to it extends far beyond our own operations at Teesport.

"As Statutory Harbour Authority, we have an important role to play in keeping the river safe, navigable and ready to support the businesses that depend upon it.

"That means continuing to invest in the people, infrastructure and expertise needed to fulfil that responsibility for the long term.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651827.jpg?type=mds-article-620)PD Ports chief executive Paul Foreman joined guests from across the Tees river community to officially open the purpose-built facility *(Image: Michael Sreenan)*

"This multi-million-pound investment is a very visible example of that commitment.

"It is an investment in our Marine team, but importantly it is also an investment in the river and the wider economy it serves.

"We are proud of the role we play on the Tees and equally proud to work alongside the many businesses, organisations and communities that collectively make this such an important working river."

The three-storey facility brings together marine operations and marine engineering in a single, modern location, replacing older buildings on Vulcan Street that dated back to the 1960s.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21651830.jpg?type=mds-article-620)PD Ports has opened a new multi-million-pound marine base at Cargo Fleet Wharf in Middlesbrough to help keep the River Tees safe and navigable *(Image: Michael Sreenan)*

It includes a specialist vessel maintenance workshop that allows smaller marine vessels to be lifted from the river onto the quay and moved indoors for maintenance and repair.

Chris Stocks, marine director at PD Ports, said: "Our job is ultimately about making sure the river works for everyone who relies on it.

"Every day our teams are surveying the river, maintaining navigational aids, dredging and maintaining our vessels, alongside providing the capability needed to respond to incidents.

"Much of that work naturally happens behind the scenes, but it is fundamental to keeping the Tees safe, open and operating efficiently.

"This facility puts us right at the heart of the river we serve and gives our people the environment and equipment they need to do that job effectively.

"It also gives us somewhere we can bring river users, customers and partners together and continue building on the strong relationships that make the Tees work.

"For me, it is visible evidence of ongoing investment in the river, in the people who keep it running and in the economy it supports."

Attendees at the launch included members of the Tees river community, Andy McDonald, MP for Middlesbrough and Thornaby East, Middlesbrough South and East Cleveland MP Luke Myer, Middlesbrough Mayor Chris Cooke, and Redcar and Cleveland Mayor Councillor Ceri Cawley.

The event also included a performance by the Men of Staithes choir, which counts Tees Bay Pilot Lee Jackson among its members.

The Marine team at PD Ports manages essential activities such as dredging, hydrographic surveying, maintaining navigational aids, and providing oil spill response services to keep the river channels open and safe.

The new base marks another step in PD Ports’ ongoing investment in its marine capability.

The company has also invested significantly in its fleet, including £23 million in the Emerald Duchess.

Designed and delivered primarily in-house, the facility was developed by PD Ports’ civil and facilities engineering teams, with support from local suppliers.

The project reflects the company’s focus on long-term, sustainable investment in both people and infrastructure to ensure the continued success of the River Tees as a commercial waterway.
