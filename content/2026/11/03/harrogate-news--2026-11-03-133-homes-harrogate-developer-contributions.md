A development of 133 homes in Harrogate is set to deliver more than £1.64 million in financial contributions towards local infrastructure and community facilities.

The funding package relates to the residential development at Cow Dyke Farm, off Skipton Road, where developer contributions have been agreed through the planning process.

The largest share of the funding, approximately £1.13 million, is allocated towards education, with further contributions covering open space, healthcare and planning-related costs.

## £1.64m funding package for 133 homes in Harrogate

The contributions are set out in connection with planning application 25/01728/REMMAJ, which relates to the 133-home development.

The reported breakdown of the contributions is:

* **Education:** £1,126,936
* **Off-site open space:** £184,663
* **On-site open space:** £170,324
* **Healthcare:** £154,836
* **Biodiversity monitoring:** £5,165
* **Section 106 monitoring:** £590

The figures listed amount to £1,642,514. The overall package is therefore approximately £1.64 million.

## Education receives the largest contribution

Education accounts for the largest element of the package, at more than £1.12 million. This represents the majority of the contributions associated with the development.

Further funding is allocated to open space, including both on-site provision and improvements away from the development. A separate contribution is also identified for healthcare.

The package also includes smaller sums for biodiversity monitoring and the monitoring of the planning obligations.

## Section 106 contributions and new housing

Developer contributions of this type are commonly secured through Section 106 agreements. These are legal agreements associated with planning permissions and can require developers to contribute towards infrastructure and services needed to support new development.

Depending on the circumstances of a development, contributions can support areas such as education, healthcare, transport, affordable housing and open space.

The Cow Dyke Farm development is one of the schemes adding to Harrogate’s housing supply, with the associated contributions intended to help address some of the infrastructure requirements arising from new homes.

The council’s planning records provide the formal basis for the application and associated obligations.
