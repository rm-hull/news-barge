A MAN who defrauded vulnerable people out of nearly £400,000 has been jailed for more than four years.

James Chambers, 48, of St Johns View, Boston Spa, appeared at Hull Crown Court last Monday (October 26) where he was sentenced to four years and six months for seven counts of fraud.

He had originally been charged with 10 offences but admitted to seven during police interviews and at a court appearance on Friday, May 22.

In total, he took £396,920 from many victims - all whilst being bankrupt himself - with the fraud coming to light after he was reported through the online platform Report Fraud.

Detective Constable Joanne Richardson, who led the investigation, said: "Chambers displayed consistent pattern of predatory and deplorable behaviour.

"During his offending he sought to befriend people, including the vulnerable, before making persistent requests for money, all whilst promising repayments would be imminent."

The investigation said that Chambers told victims he needed money for a 'trust fund' and promised to repay them, but the fund did not exist.

His also breached his bankruptcy order as he failed to declare the funds at the time.

DC Richardson said Chambers impersonated professionals to cover his tracks.

She said: "After asking for money and receiving payment, Chambers would impersonate professionals including lawyers, accountants, government officials as well as personal friends, all in an attempt to offer explanations, all of which turned out to be fiction, for his lack of repayment.

"His offending has caused not only financial damage to his victims but also reputational damage to those he has impersonated.

"Chamber’s offending was not accidental; he knew the damage he was doing and continued with no regard for his victims.

"His offending is unusual as he asked for money as himself, and only impersonated individuals later down the line as a means to cover his tracks."

DC Richardson also used the case to raise awareness of befriending fraud.

She said: "I would like to take this opportunity to raise awareness of fraud, many people are unaware of befriending fraud, which shares many similarities with romance fraud.

"Fraudsters seek people primarily online who they believe they can take advantage of, they message online forming a ‘personal relationship’ which ultimately leads to them asking for money in a variety of ways, this is often for a variety of reasons but there is usually an urgency to their requests."
