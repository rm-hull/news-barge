# 'Ring and bring' Leeds drugs empire comes crashing down - 3 behind bars

## Three members of the organised crime gang have been locked up

Three men have been jailed after a drugs empire came crashing down during a high-stakes police operation.

The investigation, named Operation Angelford, was launched by West Yorkshire Police in May 2023 after a search of Christopher Laking’s home on Ramshead Drive, Seacroft, uncovered more than £14,000 in cash.

Laking and two other men were arrested and his phone was seized. Enquiries into the phone and his banking material showed he had been operating a drugs line.

In December 2025, officers executed further warrants at Laking’s home and at addresses on Inglewood Drive and Skelwith Walk linked to Jordan Parker and Chris Wilson.

Laking, Parker and Luke Cockerill were arrested at Laking’s address, where officers seized expensive items, including jewellery, suspected to have been bought with the proceeds of crime.

The Inglewood Drive address was found to be uninhabited and was being used as a drugs stash. Inside, officers found more than £13,000 worth of cocaine and more than £17,000 worth of cannabis. A further £2,850 worth of cocaine was found at the Skelwith Walk address.

Further phone enquiries uncovered evidence of a ring and bring drugs service, with Laking appearing to be in charge and Wilson and Cockerill assisting.

At Leeds Crown Court, Laking, 35, of Ramshead Drive, was jailed for 14 years for offences including being concerned in the supply of class A and class B drugs. Wilson, 35, of Horton Grange Road in Bradford, was jailed for six years and three months for being concerned in the supply of cocaine and possession of criminal property.

Cockerill, 36, of no fixed abode, was jailed for four years and six months for supply offences involving cocaine and heroin.

Parker, 33, of Inglewood Drive in Leeds, was given a two-year community order for being concerned in the supply of cannabis.

Following the sentencing, Sergeant Kody Bradley, of Leeds East Neighbourhood Policing Team, said: "This has been a long-standing investigation by the neighbourhood policing team to link all four of these individuals to this drug dealing operation. Drug supply causes significant harm in our communities, often exploiting already vulnerable people.

"Some of this group claimed to be unemployed, but it was clear they had a healthy income, just not by legitimate means. During the course of this investigation we have seized a significant quantity of drugs, ensuring they were taken out of the supply chain, as well as seizing cash and other assets from the group.

“We are determined to show that crime such as this does not pay. In addition to the sentences handed down today we will also be pursuing the assets they have gained through this criminality as part of a Proceeds of Crime Act hearing at a later date.”
