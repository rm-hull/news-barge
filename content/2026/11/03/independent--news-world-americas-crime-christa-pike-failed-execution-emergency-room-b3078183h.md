Christa Pike was taken to the emergency room and says she has been forced to clean a “roach-infested shower” since her botched execution, according to a report.

Pike had previously been discharged and returned to prison after surviving two lethal injections of the drug pentobarbital in Nashville on September 30. The now-50-year-old had received the death penalty after being convicted in 1996 of a murder in Tennessee she carried out a year earlier with her then-boyfriend.

​Pike has since been “returned to the emergency room once” after being discharged, according to a personal declaration filed by her legal team in a bid to be reintegrated with part of the prison population. The details were revealed in an emergency motion and obtained by *PEOPLE*. She was not readmitted to ER for inpatient care.

​The convicted murderer added that she has been made to carry out tasks such as cleaning a “roach-infested shower,” arguing that the tasks put her at a “high risk of infection and further injury.”

​In her October 30 statement, Pike detailed her condition following the September 30 execution attempt.

![Convicted murderer Christa Pike has returned to the ER once following her return to prison following a botched execution](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/11/03/10/2026-10-30T195445Z_876146806_RC2PTNAKY9AI_RTRMADP_3_TENNESSEE-EXECUTION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​“Several days later I woke up alone in a hospital confused and in pain,” she said. “There hasn't been a moment since that night when I haven't been in pain.”

​Pike added, “My body hurts. My arms hurt. My hands hurt. My throat hurts. I still struggle to speak.”

​The 50-year-old says in the personal declaration reviewed by PEOPLE that she wishes to return to the prison, but cannot remain in the pod she previously occupied.

​“This pod houses inmates with significant histories of assaults and disciplinary problems,” she alleged. “In my two years living in this pod after entering into the settlement, I witnessed multiple assaults by close custody inmates of other inmates and guards, multiple individuals who were undergoing acute psychological breakdowns, and significant danger.”

![Pike said on October 30 that there ‘hasn’t been a moment since’ the night of her execution when she hasn’t been in pain](https://static.the-independent.com/2026/10/01/15/57/Christa-Pike.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

​Pike is seeking to be able to join “group meals, group classes, lobby time, group religious services, work assignments, or shared recreation.”

​A deadline for defendants to respond to the filing was set for November 4 by a judge, with Pike’s team then having until November 6 to respond to defendants.

Pike was convicted of first-degree murder and conspiracy to commit murder back in 1996, after she and her then-boyfriend tortured and killed their Job Corps classmate Colleen Slemmer a year earlier.

​Tadaryl Shipp, Pike’s former boyfriend, was convicted of first-degree murder and conspiracy to commit murder. He was 17 years old at the time of the murder, making him ineligible for the death penalty.

​Pike had been 18 at the time of the murder.
