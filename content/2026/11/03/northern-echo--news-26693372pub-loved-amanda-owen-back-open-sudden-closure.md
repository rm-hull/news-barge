One of North Yorkshire's most famous pubs has reopened its doors following an unexpected shutdown.

The Tan Hill Inn, recognised as Britain's highest pub, is back open to punters and tourists after an unexpected maintenance issue left its owners with no choice but to temporarily close.

In a message on social media, the remote inn said it was once again welcoming customers, with "the fires roaring" and staff ready to greet visitors.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21663321.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Tan Hill Inn, Britain's highest pub, has reopened and is appealing for visitors' support following an unexpected closure. *(Image: Supplied)*

The appeal comes as the historic pub highlighted the unique challenges of operating in one of the country's most isolated locations.

Situated 1,732 feet above sea level in the Yorkshire Dales near the County Durham border, the Tan Hill Inn has become a well-known landmark, attracting walkers, tourists and visitors from across the UK.

The venue was recently featured in Channel 4's Our Farm Next Door: Amanda, Clive and Kids, where Yorkshire Shepherdess Amanda Owen travelled to the inn on horseback to meet former husband Clive Owen and sons Miles and Sidney following a demanding lambing season.

Now, the pub's owners have revealed the realities of keeping the business running in such a remote setting.

In a statement, they said: "Being Britain's highest inn is something we're incredibly proud of, but running a pub completely off-grid, 1,732 feet above sea level, comes with its challenges.

"Unlike your average pub, the costs involved in simply opening our doors each day are enormous.

"Keeping the lights on, the fires burning, the kitchen running and this historic old inn welcoming visitors takes a huge amount of work and expense."

The inn explained that, unlike many pubs, it does not benefit from a nearby village population or passing trade.

Instead, it relies heavily on visitors making a special journey to the hilltop venue.

The statement continued: "Whether you're a local who regularly makes the journey up, a walker passing through, or someone who's had Tan Hill on their bucket list for years, your support is what keeps this special little place going.

"We don't have a village on our doorstep or a steady stream of passing trade. We rely on people making the journey to see us, and we're so grateful to everyone who does."

The owners have now encouraged people who have been considering a visit to make the trip and support the business.

The Tan Hill Inn has a long history serving travellers crossing the remote Pennine landscape and remains one of the Yorkshire Dales' best-known destinations.

Its exposed location has often made headlines during severe winter weather, with heavy snowfall occasionally cutting off access to the pub and leaving guests stranded.

The inn added: "The doors are open, the fire's on, and we'd love to see you."
