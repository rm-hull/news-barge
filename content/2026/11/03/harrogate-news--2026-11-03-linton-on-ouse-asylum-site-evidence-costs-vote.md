Government MPs have voted down cross-party calls for the Home Office to publish evidence that the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse site is suitable for asylum accommodation, alongside a full breakdown of the costs involved.

Tom Gordon, Liberal Democrat MP for Harrogate and Knaresborough, backed two amendments to the Immigration and Asylum Bill during a Public Bill Committee meeting.

The amendments, new clauses 30 and 31, were tabled by Calum Miller MP for Bicester and Woodstock. They would require the Government to publish a full suitability assessment and a detailed breakdown of costs before any large asylum accommodation site is used.

Despite receiving cross-party support, including from Conservative members of the Committee, the amendments were defeated by Government MPs by eight votes to six.

The proposal for Linton-on-Ouse would see around 1,250 single adult men accommodated beside a village of approximately 600 people.

The rural site has limited public transport, flood-prone roads and no mains gas. Questions have also been raised about the capacity of water, sewerage, drainage and electricity infrastructure. Much of the site’s infrastructure has been removed since the Home Office’s previous attempt to use it, which was abandoned in 2022.

Tom Gordon said his concerns related to the suitability of the site, rather than the people who would be accommodated there. He argued that an isolated location with limited access to services would create difficulties for both residents and those placed at the site.

> **Tom Gordon MP:** Nobody is questioning the Government’s duty to house people seeking asylum. What we are questioning is whether Linton-on-Ouse is the right place to do it – and on every measure, it isn’t.
>
> An isolated site with failing infrastructure, little transport and limited access to healthcare doesn’t work for local residents, and it doesn’t work for the people who would be sent there. It doesn’t work for anyone.
>
> hese amendments ask two simple things. If a site is suitable, publish the evidence. If it’s value for money, publish the costs. Public money has already been spent on this site once, in 2022, before the plan was abandoned. Taxpayers deserve to know what was spent, what more it would cost, and how it compares with the alternatives.

## Local opposition to Linton-on-Ouse proposal

There has been opposition to the proposal from across the political spectrum in North Yorkshire.

In July, North Yorkshire Council passed a unanimous cross-party resolution against the proposal. Tom Gordon also joined MPs from across North Yorkshire and York in backing a letter from the Council Leader to the Minister.

York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith has also said that the site is the wrong place for the plan.

During the committee debate, Gordon challenged the Minister on why the Government believed the Conservative-led council and Labour mayor were wrong.

> **Tom Gordon MP:** The Government talks endlessly about devolution and putting power in local hands. Yet here we have a Conservative-led council voting against it with cross-party support, a Labour mayor opposed and MPs across party lines all saying no. I asked the Minister why she thinks they have all got it wrong. And still the decision is being made in Whitehall, with no prior engagement with the council at all.
>
> This is about responsible decision-making and proper scrutiny of public money. Local communities shouldn’t be left dealing with the consequences of decisions they’ve had no say in. Today, Government MPs voted down a simple, cross-party request to publish the evidence and the costs. If this site genuinely stands up to scrutiny, there should be nothing to hide. I will keep pushing for this transparency as the Bill progresses.

## Action group criticises Government decision

The Linton-on-Ouse Action Group also criticised the Government’s decision to reject the amendments, arguing that the public should be able to scrutinise the evidence behind the proposed use of the site.

> **Linton-on-Ouse Action Group:** The Government has voted against publishing evidence to show the site is suitable or cost effective. These amendments had cross-party support.
>
> They continue to ride roughshod over public finances and small communities without publishing evidence for scrutiny. Who are the Government accountable to if not opposition parties and, more importantly, the British voters?
>
> What do they not want us to know? That the evidence proves that this is the wrong plan in the wrong place perhaps?

## What the amendments would have required

New clause 30 would have required the Government to publish an assessment of every large asylum accommodation site before it is used. This would cover transport, healthcare and policing capacity, safe pedestrian access, safeguarding, infrastructure, alternatives considered and the impact on the local community. It would also require consultation with local authorities.

New clause 31 would have required publication of the full costs, including acquisition, capital expenditure, running costs and the cost per person. It would also require comparisons with alternative options and an updated assessment if estimates changed materially before a site opened.

The amendments were brought forward in response to the use of the urgent Crown development route, which allows the Home Office to bypass much of the usual planning scrutiny.

The debate also raised questions about the potential costs of other proposed accommodation sites. At MOD Bicester, where up to 1,256 people could be housed for ten years, analysis by Calum Miller MP suggests set-up costs could exceed £250 million, with costs of more than £1 billion over ten years. Local people were given ten days to make representations.

At Linton-on-Ouse, the primary school is around 600 metres from the 680-acre site. North Yorkshire Council passed its unanimous cross-party resolution opposing the proposal on 15 July 2026.
