Welcome to the PC Gamer Top 100—our annual list of the best games you can play right now. Every year, we look at the hundreds of thousands of PC games that are available to buy, and think, "yes, we can whittle this down into a numbered list". Hubris? Yes. A months-long crucible of heated arguments and impassioned pleas? Also yes.

More than anything, the Top 100 is designed to capture a moment in time. It's a place where the 'canon' classics of the past rub shoulders against the fresh new hotness. Where being a game that a significant portion of the team *currently* loves is as important as being a game that is recognised worldwide for its contributions to the medium.

It is a deeply subjective process—a way for PC Gamer's editorial team to advocate for the games they're passionate about. One member of our team has spent *years* attempting to get Overwatch back on the list, taking every opportunity to persuade their fellow editors to give it a chance. Not every entry required that level of dedication to earn its spot, but every pick is a game that at least a handful of our staff cares deeply about.

## How we make the list

Ahead of the vote, the PC Gamer team is free to nominate any PC game for voting, giving us a longlist of over 300 games. Each member of the team is then asked to rate those games subjectively across three weighted categories:

* **Quality:** *Is it good?*A rating of the game's calibre. (60% of the Top 100 Score)* **Importance:** *Is it noteworthy?*A rating of the game's influence. (20% of the Top 100 Score)* **Freshness:** *Is it unique?*A rating of the game's originality. (20% of the Top 100 Score)

The weightings are designed around the Top 100's function as an annual, ever-changing list. Too much weight to Importance, and the list stagnates. Too much to Freshness, and there's no consistency year-to-year. Quality is the most important category because, ultimately, if we don't think a game is actually very good, it doesn't make the list.

The sum of each game's ratings is divided by its number of voters, and then run through a few special formulae—including a penalty for games that receive too few votes—to produce the final Top 100 Score for each game.

We then make some manual adjustments. In the interest of variety we *mostly* restrict the list to one game per series—only including multiple if we think the games are different enough from each other to be worth mentioning, as with Baldur's Gate 2 and Baldur's Gate 3. Otherwise, the lower scoring game in a series is removed. We also let editors pitch for a change to the list—each of which is voted on by the wider team, and only ratified if it gets majority approval.

Keep up to date with the most important stories and the best deals, as picked by the PC Gamer team.

What's left is the 100 games you find below. Some might claim it folly—attempting to wrestle the varied tastes of our editorial team into a list of games we wholeheartedly recommend. Nevertheless, we're sure that within these entries, you'll find something new to love.

## 95. Dwarf Fortress

**Released:** August 8, 2006 (Alpha) |**Top 100 Score:** 219.30

![95. Dwarf Fortress](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/HitfzBTjyiKTrGKbsXytYS-1920-80.png)

**Lincoln Carpenter:** Dwarf Fortress is one of the most distilled forms of PC gaming. Acclimating to its demands and expectations is almost a lifestyle in itself, but you're rewarded for the effort with a sprawling fantasy management sim and a deeply simulated roguelike, with systems for modeling nerve damage and geographic water drainage and civilizational ruin. It's a synthesis of conscious craft and procedural generation, an engine for a long tradition of oral storytelling, and the product of the years-long and ongoing effort of obsessive software wizards. It's an absurd achievement of fantasy simulation, and its creators only intend on doubling down.

Oh, and it regularly produces patch notes like "Made the corpses of small animals that die out of water stop teleporting"—the surest sign of quality one could ask for.

**Joshua Wolens:** I'm genuinely convinced Dwarf Fortress might be the best game ever made. At the very least, if I were given some sort of ambassadorial role where I had to explain the power of videogames to an alien race, DF would be the first thing I showed them.

## 94. Inside

**Released:** July 7, 2016 |**Top 100 Score:** 220.67

![95. Dwarf Fortress](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/8H6PqGQJCmVCAfiBiqD4pM-1920-80.png)

**Lauren Morton:** Inside is one of those bucket list puzzle games you play once and remember fondly forever. I wish I could bottle those "Oh do they want me to… Oh wow, of course," moments that you can only ever experience once in a game and only one game in a decade truly gives you. This is that game, a surreal and creepy descent into sci-fi horror that so many sidescroll puzzlers with children in imminent danger have tried to capture since. It's nigh on perfect level design: beauty and blood pressure-spiking combined.**Andy Chalk:** Inside is so good. I had no idea what was going on at any given moment or what the hell it was all about when it was over, but the vibe was pure and immaculate: Horrifying yet starkly beautiful, showing just enough of its world to satisfy without surrendering the mystery, and having the balls to end inconclusively—and, frankly, incomprehensibly. Importantly, too, Inside doesn't overstay its welcome. It's a short, sharp, surreal shock that'll leave you too wondering what the hell it was all about when it's all over.**Sean Martin**: If we're talking space occupied in my brain relative to hour count, Inside is one of the most memorable, atmospheric, and smart side-scrollers I've ever played.

## 93. BattleTech

**Released:** April 24, 2018 |**Top 100 Score:** 221.14

![93. BattleTech](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/LGe9Ta3LW6CBxyZeoxmsYm-1920-80.png)

**Evan Lahti:** Mechs are metal piñatas. Their modular durability make them the most fun-to-shoot targets in video games, more than Nazi zombies, helicopters, or terrorists. Mechs die gradually: first layers of armor, then internal structure (a mech's bones, basically), then a weapon or other component. Worst-case, an ammo compartment explodes, annihilating the equipment around it. That combat pacing is a perfect template for a video game, and it works even better in the context of an XCOM-style campaign where you're a mercenary company that's scavenging parts over time and making risk-reward judgments on which contracts to accept.**Lincoln Carpenter:** The real magic trick of BattleTech is Harebrained Schemes' ability to make speed and motion so crucial in a turn-based tactics game about massive, hulking war machines. A slow mech is just an easy target.**Fraser Brown:** One of the worst things Paradox ever did was not let Harebrained Schemes work on another BattleTech game. The good news is that this one has so many great mods that you can make your own sequel.**Jake Tucker:** One of the best turn-based strategy games ever made, the fact this didn't spawn a multi-part series or even get the level of support of several of Paradox's other titles makes me sad. There's something about the fantasy of running an intergalactic band of mechanised mercenaries that nothing else quite hits and has got me installing this game again on my second screen while writing this entry.

## 92. Hollow Knight: Silksong

**Released:** September 4, 2025 |**Top 100 Score:** 221.14

![92. Hollow Knight: Silksong](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/kyzhWUwocJfrKBq5ggUSXC-1920-80.png)

**Sean Martin:** Year after year, I've advocated for Hollow Knight's position on this list, but I'm more than happy to see Silksong succeed. Silksong is a jerk to you. Hidden benches, traps, unreasonable runbacks, poison maggot swamps and infuriating side-quests. But that acts as a counterbalance to its beauty, and gives it enough personality that I just kept coming back to it.

## 90. Final Fantasy Tactics: The Ivalice Chronicles

**Released:** September 30, 2025 |**Top 100 Score:** 221.40

![90. Final Fantasy Tactics: The Ivalice Chronicles](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/r5oHgdwVfGAXmvkL3rzPKT-1920-80.png)

**Evan Lahti:** "If by our deaths a single drop of noble blood should water the earth, they shall not be in vain." Compared to the 1998 original on PlayStation, Tactics' contemporary English translation is extraordinarily Shakespearean. This is Japanese, turn-based King Lear. Soft colors and art direction blend cuteness with a backstabbing medieval tale.

There are charming, dated aspects to this chesslike RPG, like the need to grind random combats to level up critical abilities like Auto-Potion, but the depth of the jobs system and a light sandbox campaign structure makes Tactics enduring. You've also gotta love the outright strangeness of some of the job classes: the Orator (there's an actual "Lecture an enemy to sleep" ability), the Calculator (target spells based on prime numbers), stealing weapons or armor outright off of enemies as a Thief—all of which resist modern notions of a damage-maxing meta in favor of playing Tactics with a touch of silliness.

## 88. Alien: Isolation

## 87. Terraria

## 86. Overwatch

**Released:** August 10, 2023 |**Top 100 Score:** 222.00

### Personal Picks

Surprise! There are more than 100 good videogames. Below you'll find a selection of individual recommendations—each a game that a member of the PC Gamer team thinks you should play.

![Rocket League Season 2](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ySfXgE9tGxYotNvhTopj9U-2560-80.jpg)

### Rocket League

**Tyler Wilde, US Editor-in-Chief:** Rocket League hasn't evolved much since 2015, but that's because it was already a perfectly-designed competitive team game which no one has yet improved upon. Playing soccer with rocket-powered cars has a high skill ceiling, but great teamwork can trump individual skill, which has led to some of the most exciting underdog wins of my gaming career. I'll be playing when I'm 80 if they don't turn the servers off before then.

![Thank Goodness You&#039;re Here review](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/2sxKgQ8rP5GYuiB7bEwwZK-2560-80.jpg)

### Thank Goodness You’re Here!

**John Strike, Senior Art Editor:** Big pies. Flat beer. Crazy northeners. Drunk cows. Thank Goodness You’re Here! has it all and then some. This beautiful cartoon-style slapstick romp around the fictional and truly bizarre Yorkshire town of Barnsworth is a must-play for anyone with a soft spot for northern ways. Put your trousers on, grab your net and go and catch those spirit level bubbles!

![A Japanese schoolgirl, partially obscured by red flowers](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/26ufMeHTfuivcoySFoD8un-2560-80.jpg)

### Silent Hill f

**Elie Gould, News Writer:** Oh my god, I forgot to put Silent Hill f on the voting list. This is unforgivable considering how amazing it is and how well it managed to pave the way for the new kind of Silent Hill we're now seeing unfold. Its setting is chilling, mysterious, and cool as all hell, it gives us the best female protagonist I've seen in years, and we're tested with some seriously tricky puzzles and grotesque monsters. It's the whole package.

![](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/PmH9nsoWp9WMt6nFjG4nig-2560-80.jpg)

### Sea of Thieves

**Christopher Livingston, Senior Editor:** After a long absence I finally returned to Rare's open world ocean sandbox this year, and yep, there's still really no other game like it. The beauty of its world and water is still unmatched and there's nowhere else I've found that shares its blend of chill hangout vibes (90%) and humbling PvP nightmare (10%). All you can bring into each session are your skills because there's nothing else to give any pirate an advantage over another. One of a kind.

![Two armored warriors explore Blackwood](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/mtkpyTTxNTHPvT4vtsEj4R-2560-80.jpg)

### The Elder Scrolls Online

**Rory Norris, Guides Writer:** TESO is my most played game of all time, sitting at well over 2,000 hours in the decade since launch. It's an Elder Scrolls game but multiplayer, and it's been carrying the torch while the mainline series has been radio silent since Skyrim. You can explore almost all of Tamriel at this point, questing, doing world bosses, dungeons, and whatnot. And while it's technically a bit of a mess, the large-scale PvP is truly a sight to behold.

![Esoteric Ebb Cleric wearing viking helmet and champion belt resting on a pile of apples.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/XsiwdjWd6VKQwYoLEJQkeG-2560-80.jpg)

### Esoteric Ebb

**Ted Litchfield, Associate Editor:** This one was supposed to be on the list proper, sorry everyone. Deadlock, I love you sweetie, but you're not finished. Step aside for what will be PCG's 2025 RPG of the year barring a coup and my execution. Esoteric Ebb riffs on Disco Elysium, but meaningfully pushes the evolving subgenre forward with smart changes like 6 attributes instead of 24 skills, potentially even greater reactivity⁠—especially in its handwritten turn-based battles⁠—and a comedic, hopeful, tone to set its excellent story apart.

![Dawn of War remastered soldiers](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/iFeQeQcYGZE52rGWARrT7S-2560-80.png)

### Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War – Definitive Edition

**Jody Macgregor, Weekend Editor:** I may be a turn-based lover who sometimes thinks we should add *Jagged Alliance*to the Top 100, but even I know there have been a couple of classic RTSes, chief among them* Dawn of War*. It's got the best sense of scale of any game in the genre, a real feeling of ants versus bison as you build up to tanks and daemons, and the Definitive Edition lets you pull the camera back to appreciate it. Good luck, ants.

![A clone examines her hand](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/kSuF3fBTk7C4YcowxHpDG8-2560-80.jpg)

### 1000xResist

**Joshua Wolens, News Writer:** A 2024 narrative adventure that's more or less a visual novel, 1000xResist is one of those games that make you realise how narratively unambitious most other videogames are. It's about killing god, doing a revolution, inheriting trauma, inflicting trauma, the immigrant experience, the Hong Kong protests, original sin, noodles, and a lot else. It's like a modern Planescape: Torment, in that it's so tenderly and confidently written that it just makes all the other guys look bad.

![Jagged Alliance 3](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/7P27kSQzGtqdC7afHZWUMH-2560-80.jpg)

### Jagged Alliance 3

**Evan Lahti, Strategic Director:** If you're an XCOM person with any affection for '80s/'90s action movies, JA3 should be your next game. It's campy, it's tropical, and your mercenaries (through story or tactical decisions) can find themselves in no-win scenarios. But my favorite detail is the simmy ballistics: bullets aren't just particle effects, they're physical objects that can scatter randomly and catch collateral hits against enemies, friendlies, civs, or scenery. The campaign itself is delightfully tabletop in its combo of scripted stuff and freedom.

![A red-haired woman in a black jacket with a yellow triangle on the back looks out over a cityscape](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/vM5xaGQTCmvFWhP9pGzqp8-2560-80.jpg)

### Transistor

**Issy van der Velde, Deputy Editor, PC Gaming Show:** Transistor has the best sci-fi love story I've ever experienced, and the combat is endlessly satisfying. Each ability can be used on its own, added to another to create a secondary effect, or held as a passive buff. It's easy to see how Supergiant built on this to create the boons in Hades. Everyone should play this masterpiece for Ashley Barrett and Darren Korb's soundtrack alone. There's even a dedicated button that lets you hum along to the music.

![A faceless man pointing a gun](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/fDCK8BG9ji9nvpyCv36ecU-2560-80.jpg)

### Lorelei and the Laser Eyes

**Phil Savage, Global Editor-in-Chief:** If you like the *idea* of Blue Prince, but don't like the roguelike elements, then I have two things to tell you: 1) You are weak and need to fix yourself, and 2) you should play Lorelei and the Laser Eyes. Simogo's mystery box adventure is an absolute treat of puzzle design, cleverly laying out its challenges in cascading threads of discovery. It's deeply satisfying to solve both the individual puzzles and the building mystery of its story. A genuine treat.

![Zombies cross a street toward plants who are ready to defend their lawn](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/paarZpfAnKwEbYowCzxJHL-2560-80.jpg)

### Plants vs. Zombies

**Andy Chalk, US News Lead:** The Plants vs. Zombies remaster that dropped in 2025 took some heat for technical shortcomings and allegations of AI art, but EA's addressed most of the complaints and these days it stands as a really good remaster of an all-time classic. The original is still available too, and much cheaper, and it holds up remarkably well for its age. Either way, Plants vs. Zombies is a short, snappy, incredibly cute and easy-to-engage tower defense game that remains as great—and essential—as ever.

![Three Kingdoms](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/tNDVHjmkfWwavtRgTk5nDD-2560-80.jpg)

### Total War: Three Kingdoms

**Sean Martin, Senior Guides Writer:** I'm unsure why my compatriots turned on Total War this year, but I'm here to remind you that Three Kingdoms is still incredible. Its quality is often overshadowed by its early demise, but it's quite unlike anything Creative Assembly ever made, or will likely make again. It channels the courageous characters, political machinations, and endless scheming of China's most culturally iconic period into a top-tier strategy game, achieving a harmonious balance between history and romance.

![Warrior in a fantasy world](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/5tFa9ArT4UFUugKQ4S73DM-2560-80.jpg)

### Enshrouded

**Andrea Shearon, Evergreen Writer:** I've been enjoying the survival crafting renaissance, and by that I mean playing the hell out of Abiotic Factor and Enshrouded. The unbelievably detailed, massive high-fantasy RPG has one of the best building systems I've ever seen, and I love rescuing new villagers and crafters to slowly grow my own town. My only complaint is that Embervale is so pretty that I spend dozens of hours whispering, "Oh, maybe I'll settle down here," without getting anything done.

![Exo Rally Championship](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/E5b22WoFFdZvo95p98ATFh-2560-80.jpg)

### Exo Rally Championship

**Morgan Park, Staff Writer:** A gritty, unforgiving rally racing sim set in outer space. Exo Rally takes an already challenging rally format and adds gravity and thrusters into the mix. You'll tear your hair out trying to complete a singular turn on the moon, then ride the high of circumventing most of a track through well-timed thruster use. I haven't played enough yet to make my case for on the list, but it's turning me into a racing fan. A brilliant 2026 indie that's flying too low under the radar.

![Project Zomboid - A player on a city sidewalk is attacked by two zombies.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/YyCGt5Ri7LRknDUGdf9pe-2560-80.jpg)

### Project Zomboid

**Mollie Taylor, Features Producer:** I'm 13 years late to the party, but Project Zomboid's brutal, complex, and ridiculously moreish take on zombie survival has gripped me this year. It's one of the most customisable sandbox games I've ever tinkered with, meaning you can go for an unforgiving post-apocalyptic landscape or fine-tune it into a cosy (if not slightly gritty and terrifying) zombified The Sims 1. One of the few games I've been happy to sink hundreds of hours into learning.

![A viking giving both thumbs up](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/9pzjFrhqUpabhXGRJNa4t8-2560-80.jpg)

### Valheim

**Jacob Ridley, Managing Editor, Hardware:** There are some games you can sink hour after hour into and not even realise it's already morning. Valheim is one of those games. Jump in with a couple of friends and build a new homestead, trying to one-up each other with ever more elaborate creations (our Sistine Chapel are two rival towers on top of massive rocks). Or just go exploring and hunt for new lands, hoping to survive the treacherous ocean journey. Yeah, it's a comfort game for me.

![Kliff cuddling an orange cat.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/ZuVJxzZ5c3g6Rf6MsRMqTm-2560-80.jpg)

### Crimson Desert

**Andy Edser, Hardware Writer:** Crimson Desert is not the ultimate action RPG, despite wearing the ambition proudly on its sleeve. But it's also the most fun I've had in a fantasy open world in years. The map is gigantic, and stuffed with bizarre details to explore. The combat is fiddly, yet satisfying to master. It's a game with obvious passion running underneath it, and a brilliant, bat-swinging attempt to make something with real scope and fresh ideas. More of this sort of thing, please.

![Mina the Hollower - Mina as she&#039;s leaving a castle](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/xH2F5J5yNDFz5uCHVdb8nP-2560-80.jpg)

### Mina the Hollower

**Harvey Randall, Staff Writer:** Perhaps a bit of a late entrant to catch PC Gamer's eye—which means I don't resent its absence from our Top 100 list—but I absolutely loved Mina the Hollower. It's a sprawling, satisfying Zelda-like with a bumping soundtrack and some of the best exploration I've had in a videogame. I was particularly impressed by how intuitive this game was. My experimentation-loving brain was constantly rewarded for putting its control scheme together in novel ways. Cute little mouse versus the eldritch horrors, what's not to love.

![Co-op players waving on a mountain in Big Walk](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/CWy3wfjmUUcTRxCBqj3Ky8-2560-80.jpg)

### Big Walk

**Fraser Brown, Online Editor:** Big Walk launched after we created the magazine version of the Top 100 this year, otherwise I know it would have been in the list. This is simply the perfect co-op game. A simple, joyful puzzler absent a single flaw. Some of the antics its puzzles inspired genuinely left my crying with laughter, and that ending? Wow. It's a pure delight, start to finish. It's easily going to be my GOTY for 2026.
