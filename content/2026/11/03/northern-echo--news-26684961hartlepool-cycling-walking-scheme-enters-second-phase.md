Work has begun on the next stage of a £6.2 million scheme to make walking and cycling safer and easier in a North East town.

The second phase of the Hartlepool Town Centre Connectivity Programme will build or upgrade three miles of routes across the town.

The work will improve routes between the Headland, the town-centre marina and Seaton Carew seafront.

Adrian Barton, managing director of project contractor BCE Northern Ltd, said: "This project will significantly improve connectivity for walkers and cyclists across Hartlepool, making it a much safer and more attractive destination for people who live in or visit the town.

"The roads in and around Hartlepool town centre are often very busy, which isn’t ideal for the cyclists who are forced to share the road with motorists.

"Many of the walkways were also in need of resurfacing, so these are works which were crucial to the regeneration and long term growth of the town."

The programme also covers work around the new multi-million-pound Highlight leisure centre at Jackson’s Wharf and along Catcote Road through Burn Valley Gardens.

Gateshead-based family firm BCE Northern won the contract for the two-year scheme after a competitive tender.

The second phase covers the Waterfront and Maritime Avenue areas and is due to finish in the autumn.

The scheme is funded by the Tees Valley Combined Authority and Travel Active England, with Hartlepool Borough Council managing the project.

Mr Barton said: "This is a major project for us but one that is testament to our growth as a company and our success in delivering large multi-million-pound schemes across the North of England."

The funding supports the Tees Valley Combined Authority’s wider plan to improve roads, walkways and cycle paths across the region.

Brendon Colarossi from Hartlepool Borough Council said: "It’s fantastic to see these connectivity projects coming to fruition.

"It is absolutely crucial that people who live in, work in and visit our town can move around safely and freely and this scheme, which covers a three-mile radius around the town centre and marina, will enable them to do just that."

He praised the standard of work completed so far.

Mr Colarossi said: "The work that has been done so far has been finished to such high standards of workmanship and management, and undertaken with minimal disruption in the area, so we’d like to thank the BCE team and the lads on the ground for their hard work and professionalism.

"It’s also great to see the next phase of the project underway ahead of schedule."
