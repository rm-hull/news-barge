Hundreds of women-led aesthetics clinics could close because the watchdog charged with approving them is not ready for the Scottish Government's crackdown on Botox and fillers, a union has warned.

GMB Scotland said clinics applying to Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS) for approval under the new rules have been told it will take up to eight months and cost tens of thousands of pounds.

The Non-surgical Procedures and Functions of Medical Reviewers (Scotland) Bill, which MSPs backed earlier this year, restricts treatments including Botox, dermal fillers and non-surgical Brazilian butt lifts to registered premises overseen by a medic, dentist or pharmacist.

Ministers said the aim of the new law was to remove rogue operators from the industry. The Scottish Government estimated that up to 1,500 businesses carry out unregulated procedures in Scotland.

HIS enforces the legislation, and independent clinics that want to keep offering the procedures must register with it.

![Sabrina Klita-Baird, owner of Breagha Aesthetics in Airdrie, said there has been little guidance from HIS](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21661563.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sabrina Klita-Baird, owner of Breagha Aesthetics in Airdrie, said there has been little guidance from HIS *(Image: Breagha Aesthetics)*

But Sabrina Klita-Baird, owner of Breagha Aesthetics in Airdrie, said there has been little guidance from HIS during the application process.

She told The Herald: "The application alone costs £4,000 and the work needed to get approval could cost ten times that, but when I asked for guidance, I was sent a link to an online pamphlet.

"HIS do not even want to talk to me, never mind send someone to actually visit my business and explain exactly what might be required."

She added: "Some of us are being told one thing, others another. It's all over the place and exactly what we feared would happen.

"So far, I have uploaded almost 40 different documents, paid for fire safety reports and electrical safety reports and taken a loan out for the equipment demanded so far — new fridges and sinks, thermostatic taps, ventilation, flooring, all of it.

"It is the same application process that a hospital or GP's surgery would go through. They might have the staff and resource to cope with it but I don't, and there are lots of small, reputable businesses just like mine."

![Kirsty Nimmo](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/17140905.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kirsty Nimmo said hundreds of good businesses, run by well-trained women, were at risk of closure. *(Image: Andrew Cawley)*

Kirsty Nimmo, GMB Scotland organiser, said hundreds of good businesses, run by well-trained women who had spent thousands of pounds on premises and equipment, were at risk of closure.

The union said the legislation had been "shaped by powerful lobbying by medical practitioners in the aesthetics sector".

Ms Nimmo said: "Our members are women with the certified skills and entrepreneurial zeal to run safe and successful businesses in Scotland's high streets.

"They are exactly the kind of people who should have ministers' respect and support instead of being thrown under the bus by hasty and ill-considered legislation.

"No one wants to get cowboys out of the industry more than they do, but instead many are preparing to give up and close down.

"Many more are trying to pick their way through a regulatory process which is expensive, complex and seems deliberately designed to drive them out of business.

"Regulators do not have the resource to offer reputable businesses clear and consistent advice, risking their closure, while the intended targets of this legislation will simply ignore the law and go underground.

"Scotland's politicians have again rushe

d to introduce 'world-leading' legislation without due diligence or understanding of the unintended consequences, which, on all past form, will make things worse not better."

The union has written to the Scottish Government asking for an urgent meeting with ministers to call for more resource to be given to HIS to ensure aesthetics clinics seeking regulatory approval are given every possible support.

A spokesperson for Healthcare Improvement Scotland said: “The regulation of independent healthcare services in Scotland exists to enhance patient safety and provide public confidence.

"We understand that applying for registration can sometimes be a stressful process, and we appreciate the time and care involved in preparing an application.

“The timescale for processing registrations, once the fee has been paid, is a minimum of six months. Due to the high number of applications we are currently receiving, and the potential complexity of those applications, our processing times are longer than usual.

“The registration fee depends on the type of independent healthcare service that is being registered. The fee structure can be found on the Healthcare Improvement Scotland website.”

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: "This legislation was passed almost unanimously by the previous parliament and is designed to ensure that higher-risk procedures take place in appropriate settings with a healthcare professional present, and that under-18s are properly protected.

"These are meaningful, proportionate changes that put public safety first.

"We recognise the importance of ensuring businesses are supported to adapt to new requirements and we have listened closely to the sector. We are working closely with Healthcare Improvement Scotland and with stakeholders to support enforcement, with the key provisions of the Act not expected to come into force until September 2027 to give businesses time to adapt.

"A local authority licensing scheme will also come into force at the same time for less risky procedures."
