A UNIVERSITY-BACKED health project has won two national awards.

The Student Supported Services Model, developed in partnership with the University of Bradford, the West Yorkshire Integrated Care Board, and Airedale NHS Foundation Trust, took home the Nursing in the Community category at the 2026 Nursing Times Awards.

The same project also won Partnership of the Year at the Student Nursing Times Awards in April.

Vikki Carter, director of practice education and engagement at the University of Bradford, said: "What an incredible achievement and a real testament to the vision, commitment and collaboration of everyone involved.

"To win both the Student Nursing Times Awards and the Nursing Times Awards in the same year is something really special and demonstrates the impact this innovative model can have.

"A huge thank you and congratulations to all our partners, colleagues, students and everyone who has helped make this happen.

"A fantastic example of what can be achieved when we work together to create new opportunities for students, support our health and care partners and make a difference in the communities we serve."

The Student Supported Services Model expands placement opportunities for nursing students in community healthcare settings, supports existing staff, and enhances patient care.

Judges at the Nursing Times Awards praised the scheme as a potential blueprint for wider adoption across the UK.

They said: "This brilliant programme from Airedale FT, University of Bradford and West Yorkshire ICB has brought nursing students into the community workforce through a supported service.

"It is expanding placement capacity, supporting services and promoting career opportunities in the community.

"Our judges said this is a model that could – and should – be scaled across the UK.

"Well done to all involved."

The model launched a pilot placement scheme between January and July 2025, giving nursing students hands-on experience in community care.

Under the pilot, four students were paired with a single community nurse as part of their qualification.

The scheme aims to increase placement capacity in a safe and supportive environment, while encouraging more students to consider careers outside hospital settings.

It also focuses on health prevention by involving students in direct care for residents in social care settings.

Through the placement, students gained practical experience in delivering preventive care and learned to provide services that are compassionate, respectful, and equitable.

The project highlights the value of community-based learning and its role in shaping future healthcare professionals.
