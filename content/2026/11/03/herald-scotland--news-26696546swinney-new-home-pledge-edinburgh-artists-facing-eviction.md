Hundreds of artists, cultural organisations and charities facing eviction from long-running cultural hubs could find “a new home” in Scottish Government buildings, John Swinney has suggested.

The First Minister has agreed to consider proposes to provide alternative accommodation in “underutilised” publicly-owned properties.

Mr Swinney expressed concern after warnings of a growing “arts emergency” in Edinburgh were raised at Holyrood.

He was responding to calls for “immediate action” from Scottish Greens MSP Q Manivannan to tackle fears that up to 400 artists, organisations and charities could lose their workplaces within months after being told to vacate a former office block in the Meadowbank area.

Mr Swinney’s rehousing pledge emerged days after councillors voted to help tenants who have been told to leave St Margaret’s House by January.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/11281356.jpg?type=mds-article-620)St Margaret's House in Edinburgh has been home to artists, cultural organisations and charities for the last 18 years. *(Image: Google Maps)*

The uncertainty they are facing was top of the agenda at an “Edinburgh arts in crisis” summit held at the weekend at Summerhall, the 15-year-old arts centre which is expected to close to make way for a multi-million pound redevelopment of the site within the next few years.

Mr Swinney, who was urged to commit to an "urgent review" of buildings owned and operated by the government, said it would “happily consider" any proposals put forward to help at-risk artists, organisations and charities.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20897179.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Scottish Greens culture spokesperson Q Manivannan raised fears about a growing 'arts emergency' in Edinburgh at the Scottish Parliament. *(Image: Newsquest)*

He also suggested other public bodies could “play their part” in ensuring that Scotland has a “thriving artistic and cultural community”.

The Herald revealed in September that tenants at St Margaret’s House had been given just four months to move out the building, which has been home to artists, organisations and charities for the last 18 years.

Within days, it emerged that several buildings at the separate Summerhall arts complex are facing demolition under a luxury housebuilder’s plans for the site.

Although developer AMA has pledged to retain an arts centre on the site, it has suggested it may become home to student flats, apartments, a boutique hotel, and new food and drink operators once the site is overhauled.

The arts charity Scot-Art, which runs St Margaret's House, has said the demands to vacate the building by January 24 as "a real surprise".

A news bulletin about the shutdown of St Margaret's House states: "You can be incredibly proud of everything that has been achieved within the building and, most importantly, extreme gratitude goes out grateful to all of you who have made St Margaret’s House what it is.

"Please be assured that the charity will continue to look for new buildings and opportunities to provide affordable space for organisations and individuals working across the creative, charitable and educational sectors."

Speaking after the summit at Summerhall on Sunday, Q Manivannan said: “Scottish arts and culture are under active threat in Edinburgh and the city’s creatives are not willing to stand by and let this happen. We cannot let private developers gamble away Edinburgh’s cultural legacy.”

Speaking at First Minister’s Questions, the party's culture spokesperson told Mr Swinney: “On Sunday, I held a public meeting attended by councillors and MSPs across all parties in Edinburgh, where over 250 artists, and representatives of charities, unions, and businesses came together to declare an arts emergency.

“Nearly 400 artists and charities could be left without a workspace by January.

“If these were high street 400 businesses there would be immediate action.

“Can I ask the First Minister if he will commit to an urgent review of underutilised infrastructure in government-owned and operated buildings, and work with us to find a new home for affected artists, charities and enterprises before January.”

Mr Swinney said: “The Scottish Government makes available very significant support to the artistic and cultural community.

“Indeed, it has been one of the points of principle for me to ensure that that support is rising over the course of the budgets over which I preside as First Minister, so I am concerned to hear the issues that Q Manivannan raises.

“If there are specific proposals that are to be advanced to the government, the government will consider those proposals, because I want to make sure that we have a thriving artistic and cultural community in Scotland.

“The government will happily consider proposals that come forward and encourage other public authorities to play their part.”
