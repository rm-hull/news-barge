A Newton Aycliffe school had to be evacuated after a chemical spill hours before it was due to welcome pupils for the first time.

Firefighters in hazmat suits were pictured at Greenfield Academy after the spill while equipment was moved into the new building on Monday (November 2) afternoon.

It came during last-minute preparations for the school moving into its new building on Tuesday (November 3) morning after the half term.

Pupils were learning remotely on Monday.

School bosses said specialist cleaning and safety checks had been carried out after parents expressed concern over whether it was safe for pupils to return.

James Keating, executive headteacher at Greenfield Academy, said: "On Monday a chemical spillage occurred during the transfer of equipment and resources into Greenfield Academy's new building.

"As a precaution, the building was evacuated and established safety procedures were followed. County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service attended and safely managed the incident.

"Specialist contractors attended the site to undertake the necessary cleaning and safety checks. The affected area was assessed, and confirmation was received that the building was safe to occupy.

"Following the half-term holiday, we are delighted that pupils can enjoy their fantastic new facilities as planned.

"Today marks an important milestone for Greenfield Academy. We welcome our school community into a new building that will provide exciting opportunities for our pupils for many years to come."

A spokesperson for County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service added: "At 3.16pm yesterday (November 2) we received reports of a chemical spill at Greenfield Academy on Greenfield Way, Newton Aycliffe.

"Two crews from Newton Aycliffe Fire Station attended the incident, along with one appliance from Spennymoor Fire Station and one appliance from Darlington Fire Station. Fire crews left the scene at 7.28pm."

Durham Police did not attend the incident.
