A fly-tipping investigation has been launched after waste was dumped near a Bradford junction, with council officers set to review CCTV footage in a bid to identify those responsible.

Bradford Council confirmed it is investigating reports of fly-tipped waste near the junction of Clayton Road and Beckside Road after further rubbish was left at the site.

Councillor Ian Walker, Bradford Council's Executive Member for Neighbourhoods and Communities, said some of the waste had already been removed and enquiries were ongoing.

He said: "Further waste has been fly-tipped, which has been caught on CCTV.

"We have removed it and will investigate the footage to see if any action can be taken against the fly-tipper."

## Questions over wood left outside renovation site

The council said not all of the material seen at the location may necessarily be classed as fly-tipped waste.

According to Councillor Walker, some of the wood at the site could be reusable material connected to the renovation of nearby flats.

He said: "We are aware of this potential fly-tipping and investigations are underway.

"The waste that is there is wood and could be deemed re-usable material. The flats are being renovated so the business could be waiting for a skip."

The councillor said a council warden had visited the location but had been unable to speak to anyone connected with the works.

"One of our wardens has visited but there was nobody there to speak to so they will attend again to try engage with the business to ensure they adhere to their duty of care," he added.

## Residents thanked for reporting concerns

Councillor Walker praised residents who have continued to report incidents and provide information to support council investigations.

He said: "I would like to thank local residents who are working with us, reporting when fly-tipping has taken place and providing information which may help with our investigations."

The latest incident comes after concerns were raised about waste being repeatedly left at the same location in recent weeks.

## 'A blight on our communities'

Councillor Walker said no one should have to live near fly-tipped waste and urged residents to continue reporting incidents.

He said: "Fly-tipping is a blight on our communities, and no one should have to live near a fly tipped area. We should all be able to be proud of where we live.

"Fly-tipping is a criminal offence, and we encourage anyone with information about any incidents of this nature to report it."

Residents can report fly-tipping incidents to Bradford Council online or by calling 01274 431000.
