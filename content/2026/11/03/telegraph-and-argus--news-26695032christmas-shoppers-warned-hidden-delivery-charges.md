Christmas shoppers are being urged to watch for hidden delivery fees as the competition watchdog increases pressure on online retailers to display all costs upfront.

The Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) is reminding businesses that the price shoppers first see online should include any unavoidable delivery fees, rather than leaving customers to discover extra costs later in the buying process.

The warning comes ahead of the busy Christmas shopping period, when millions of people will be comparing prices and buying gifts online.

The watchdog is urging retailers to review their websites, mobile apps and online adverts to make sure delivery charges and other unavoidable fees are clearly displayed from the outset.

It said: "The first price consumers see should be the price they pay."

The CMA warned that unexpected charges make it harder for shoppers to compare deals and understand the true cost of products across different retailers.

## Christmas shoppers could face hidden delivery fees

Under consumer protection rules introduced through the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCC Act), businesses must include mandatory delivery or collection charges in the total price shown to customers.

This means retailers cannot advertise an item at one price and then add an unavoidable delivery fee later in the checkout process.

The total price must be displayed whenever a price is given, including:

* Online adverts
* Product pages
* Shopping baskets and carts
* Order summaries
* Checkout and payment pages

The rules also cover other unavoidable fees, taxes and charges.

The CMA said many businesses had already taken steps to comply since the legislation came into force, but it remained concerned that some retailers were still failing to display delivery charges correctly.

## Retailers could face enforcement action

The watchdog has now written to businesses highlighting the problems it has identified and setting out what online retailers should do to avoid breaking consumer protection law.

It said it would continue to monitor pricing practices across the online retail sector and could use its enforcement powers where businesses fail to comply.

The CMA published detailed guidance for businesses in November 2025, explaining how the price transparency rules should work in practice.

It is urging retailers, online marketplaces, comparison websites and search engines to review their pricing arrangements against the guidance.

## What if delivery costs are added to your shopping basket?

Some retailers charge one flat delivery fee regardless of how many products a customer buys.

For example, someone ordering several items of clothing from the same retailer might pay one delivery charge for the entire basket.

The CMA says businesses must still show the total price, including the mandatory delivery charge, throughout the shopping process.

Retailers can also provide a breakdown showing the individual product price and delivery cost, but the full amount shoppers will have to pay must be clear.

## What about optional delivery charges?

The rules also cover delivery options where customers can choose whether to pay extra.

Retailers must clearly tell shoppers when optional delivery charges apply.

If the amount cannot reasonably be worked out in advance, customers must be told that an additional charge may be payable.

Crucially, shoppers should not have to hunt through separate webpages, hidden menus or expandable sections to discover this information.

## Online marketplaces and comparison websites also covered

The rules do not just apply to retailers selling directly to customers.

Online marketplaces, comparison websites and search engines must also ensure pricing information is clear when displaying products from different sellers.

For example, if a marketplace lists several products from different traders, each with its own delivery charge, the delivery cost should be included in the total price shown for each product.

Businesses can still display the basic product price separately, provided the full price including delivery is also presented clearly and at the right time.

Its full guidance for businesses on price transparency is available on the Government website.

## Five hidden delivery charge practices the CMA is targeting

The watchdog has highlighted several examples of pricing practices it is concerned about.

These include:

* Advertising a product without including a mandatory delivery charge in the total price.
* Leaving mandatory delivery fees out of shopping basket totals or order summaries.
* Failing to explain optional delivery charges clearly.
* Hiding delivery information behind links, separate pages, pop-up windows, expandable sections or drop-down menus.
* Waiting until late in the checkout process to reveal delivery costs.

## Retailers face fines and potential customer refunds

The CMA said it would continue monitoring online shops and could take enforcement action where businesses appear to be breaking the law.

Under the DMCC Act, the watchdog has the power to impose substantial financial penalties on businesses found to have breached consumer protection rules.

It can also require companies to provide refunds or compensation to affected customers.
