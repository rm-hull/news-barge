A Glasgow-based lawyer has been appointed to a senior leadership role at international law firm Pinsent Masons as it strengthens its focus on innovation and transformation.

Stacey Bairner, the firm’s head of restructuring, has been named its first co-head of innovation and transformation alongside Clare Francis, head of commercial.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21665852.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Clare Francis, Partner, Co-Head of Innovation and Transformation, Pinsent Masons. *(Image: Supplied)*

The pair will lead the firm’s strategy for transforming legal services for clients, working with senior colleagues to bring together technology, legal, knowledge and data expertise.

Ms Bairner has been with Pinsent Masons for 13 years and leads its restructuring practice. She has also played a key role in the adoption of technology and innovation across the firm’s finance and projects teams.

Ms Francis has worked at the firm for 25 years and previously chaired its innovation and change board. She advises major retailers on digital transformation strategies.

In their new roles, they will work closely with Hayley Harris, chief knowledge and AI adoption officer, and Tracey McDermott, chief technology officer, to shape the firm’s global approach to innovation.

Stacey Bairner, co-head of innovation and transformation at Pinsent Masons, said: "We are committed to ensuring that innovation makes a positive difference for our clients.

"The opportunity is to move beyond access to technology and focus on where it can help our clients move faster, manage risk better and create better outcomes.

"We want to bring legal, sector and innovation expertise closer together so we can solve real client problems and scale what works.

"Clare and I are excited to take on the role and to work with clients and colleagues across the firm to make this happen."

Richard Griffiths, managing partner at Pinsent Masons, said: "Creating the Co-Heads of Innovation and Transformation positions marks an important milestone in the advancement of our innovation strategy.

"The appointments we've made reflect our renewed focus on weaving innovation into the fibre of our service delivery, as Stacey and Clare will remain practising, client-facing partners.

"This dual role will give them greater visibility to spot emerging opportunities to challenge convention and innovate differently, all in service of enhancing the value of the advice we provide our clients."

Clare Francis, co-head of innovation and transformation at Pinsent Masons, said: "At Pinsent Masons we've long held the philosophy that best-in-class innovation starts with the problem rather than the technology and succeeds only when solutions deliver real world success for the businesses we work with.

"In this role, I'll continue to embed that philosophy, emphasising that we have to be listening closely to our clients and building bold solutions.

"There is significant opportunity for us to lead the field in terms of what consistent digital delivery looks like and in demonstrating the value it generates in language that makes sense to businesses."
