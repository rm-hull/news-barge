Plans to turn a Durham home into an HMO have been refused after neighbours warned of a 'stealthy' increase in student properties 'forcing' families out of the city.

A proposal would have seen a family home converted into a three-bedroom HMO on St Bede’s Close in Crossgate Moor.

Durham County Council refused the plans on Monday, October 26, despite planning officers recommending they should be approved.

Eleven objections were made, with concerns including parking, noise, the loss of family homes and the number of HMOs in the area.

One neighbour said: “With three good schools in the area that children can walk to, this is an area perfect for families, and more student HMOs force families to move further from the city centre, increasing traffic to and from schools.

“The centre of Durham did not become student housing dominated overnight.

"This happens gradually, and this stealthy increase in HMOs is now threatening to change the demographic in Crossgate Moor and Neville's Cross.”

Another neighbour objected because of the “studentification of a residential street which already has more than it's share of HMOs, whether registered or not, and a huge parking problem.”

Council planning officers had recommended approval. Their report said 5.3 per cent of properties within 100 metres would be student properties, below the council's limit.

But, councillors said the HMO would cause increased noise and disturbance for nearby residents.

It also said there was not enough parking and this would have a “severe adverse impact to highway safety” and refused the application.
