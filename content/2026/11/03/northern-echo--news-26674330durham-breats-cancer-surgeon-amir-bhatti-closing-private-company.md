A surgeon at the centre of the County Durham NHS breast cancer scandal has begun shutting down one of his private companies.

The company applied to be struck off on October 12, The Northern Echo can reveal.

Mr Bhatti said: “I am grateful for the opportunity to respond, but I remain committed to the ongoing Trust investigation, and it would be inappropriate for me to discuss this while proceedings are ongoing.”

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20257689.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amir Bhatti *(Image: SUBMIT)*

Last year, it was revealed the trust had paid almost £6 million over six years to privately run breast diagnostic and surgical companies connected to Bhatti.

NHS patients were seen at outsourced clinics, with the trust paying private providers for appointments and procedures.

Some arrangements involved Cathedral Surgical Services Limited and Durham Surgical Services LLP, both connected to Bhatti.

A governance review raised "serious concerns" about private companies delivering NHS breast services. Between 2022 and 2024, around 8,000 of 13,500 patients referred for breast care were seen at outsourced clinics paid per patient.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21021270.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darlington Memorial Hospital *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

Mr Bhatti remains employed by the NHS trust on full pay despite being banned from carrying out clinical work while an internal investigation continues.

The trust first restricted Bhatti’s clinical work in February 2025 after serious concerns emerged about his treatment of breast cancer patients.

The restrictions were widened in October 2025, barring Bhatti from all clinical duties.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21277068.jpg?type=mds-article-620)University Hospital of North Durham in Durham City *(Image: Chris Booth)*

A CDDFT spokesperson said: “The trust is continuing with its investigation and it remains in its final stages in accordance with the Trust policies and procedures.

"ThetTrust no longer has a relationship with Cathedral Surgical Services.”

An independent review released in November last year found “systemic failures”, “missed warnings” and “ignored red flags” in the trust’s breast services. It said 51 women were harmed.

The proposed company closure does not prove wrongdoing, and there is no suggestion it is linked to the police or NHS investigations.

Cathedral Surgical Services is registered for “specialist medical practice activities” and reported negative equity of £12,320 for the 2025 financial year.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21006320.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Spire Hospital in Washington *(Image: Stuart Boulton)*

A voluntary strike-off allows a company to be removed from the Companies House register and dissolved.

Companies House accounts show Cathedral Surgical Services stopped trading in May 2025, three months after Mr Bhatti was first restricted from carrying out breast cancer surgery at the NHS trust.

The company’s assets plunged from £72,806 in 2024 to £22 in 2025, while money owed by debtors fell from £62,922 to £1, and its average workforce fell from 21 employees in 2024 to 12 in 2025, according to its accounts.

Mr Bhatti ran NHS “two-week-wait” clinics at Darlington Memorial Hospital, University Hospital of North Durham and Spire Hospital in Washington.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21275542.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Chief executive of the NHS Trust, Steve Russell *(Image: Chris Booth)*

Last year's review raised concerns about directors’ “dual role” in the NHS service and private businesses delivering additional care.

It warned that decisions could be “influenced by personal interests rather than solely by patient or trust priorities”.

The report also identified a "clear financial incentive" arising from arrangements under which the private companies received individual payments for patients.

NHS breast surgery lists typically involved two or three cases per session, but the review found Cathedral Surgical Services could perform 10 to 12 procedures on a Saturday, when it ran extra operating lists.

Some clinics scheduled 10-minute appointments, compared with the 15 to 20 minutes typically needed for new patients at one-stop breast diagnostic services.

Some Sunday clinics saw between 30 and 42 patients.

CDDFT chief executive Steve Russell said: "We have stopped using those companies and no longer operate those arrangements."
