Plans aimed at attracting millions of pounds of investment and bringing more people into Darlington town centre have been approved.

Darlington Borough Council’s cabinet agreed its Town Centre Strategy for 2026 to 2030 at a meeting on November 3.

The council says about £230m has been invested in the town centre in recent years, with plans outlined for a further £250m of investment by the authority and others in the coming years.

The strategy follows a consultation which received 913 responses from residents and stakeholders and another 95 from businesses.

Deputy council leader Chris McEwan told the meeting: "Darlington has not suffered as much as other places and we have a town centre to be proud of.

"But as I often say, complacency is the enemy and it is appropriate that a refreshed strategy is brought forward."

![Darlington Borough Council's cabinet met on Tuesday.](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21667405.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darlington Borough Council's cabinet met on Tuesday. *(Image: LDRS)*

Safety and anti-social behaviour were among the most common concerns raised.

People also called for action to increase footfall and encourage visitors to stay for longer, broaden the range of shops, businesses and attractions, improve accessibility and address concerns around car parking.

The council says the strategy will also build on what respondents saw as Darlington’s strengths, including its events programme, heritage and independent businesses.

Its ambition is to make the town centre more than simply a place to shop, with future action divided into five themes: living, working, playing, learning and investing in the town centre.

Plans include encouraging more town centre living, attracting investment and jobs, providing high-quality office space and improving transport links.

The council also wants more people to visit and spend time in the centre through leisure opportunities, while supporting education providers and continued business investment.

The strategy itself does not commit the council to any immediate spending.

Any future projects will go through the authority’s normal budget and approval process.

Annual progress reports will be brought back before councillors.
