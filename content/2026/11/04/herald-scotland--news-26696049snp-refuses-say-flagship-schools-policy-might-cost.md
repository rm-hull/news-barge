The Scottish Government is refusing to disclose the expected total cost of its plan to reduce class contact time for teachers, The Herald can reveal.

Although officials have confirmed that the government has completed modelling for the number of teachers required, and the consequent costs of implementation, the government has declined to provide those figures when asked to do so by The Herald.

The SNP first promised to reduce teachers’ class contact time in its 2021 manifesto, with the party pledges to hire “at least 3,500 additional teachers” and to “reduce teachers’ daily contact time by an hour and a half per week.” The change is intended to give educators more planning and preparation time and to ease workload problems for the profession.

However, the party failed to deliver this commitment during the 2021-2026 parliamentary session. In response, unions moved to the bring of strike action in the run up to the 2026 election.

Ultimately, school closures were avoided by a last-minute deal, but no information on the number of teachers required to deliver the policy, or the overall cost of doing so, were provided at the time.

The government has come under further pressure over the issue in recent months, with councils and school leaders pointing out that secondary schools currently face significant teacher shortages, and that they will not be able to deliver reduced class contact time without sufficient numbers of teachers to cover all subjects across all schools, including those in rural and island areas.

The Herald has recently revealed that schools in Scotland are facing major curricular pressure, with many forced to restrict the opportunities available to young people because of a lack of qualified staff.

In addition, targets for attracting prospective teachers have been missed by huge margins, with intake figures for seven separate subjects below 50%, and only four subjects attracting 90% or more of their target student number.

At the beginning of October, education secretary Màiri McAllan told Holyrood’s Education and Gaelic Committee dismissed a previous £300m estimate as neither accurate nor affordable, and that she was working with a lower estimate – but she declined to provide this figure to MSPs.

Now, the government has confirmed that internal modelling work on the likely cost of the policy, and the number of teachers that will be required in order to make it possible, has been completed – but when asked to release this information, officials refused to do so.

Previous independent modelling had suggested that at least hundreds, and possibly thousands, more teachers would be required.

Scottish Labour Shadow Education Secretary Katherine Sangster said: “It has been over five years since the SNP first promised to reduce teachers’ class contact time and we are now a matter of months away from when these changes need to be made.

“If the Government is serious about finally making good on their commitment to teachers, they must provide clear answers on how many teachers will be needed and how much it will cost.

“Ministers must not withhold this vital information. It is vital they publish it in full if they are to retain the faith of our teaching workforce and the wider public.”

Scottish Conservative education spokeswoman, Meghan Gallacher MSP said: “The SNP have been caught yet again making a promise on education without being fully over the details.

“If Mairi McAllan knows how much this pledge will costs, she shouldn’t be keeping Scots in the dark, but the truth is the SNP have done this without having a clue how to deliver it.

“This secretive shambles exposes a government more interested in headlines than getting to grips with the crisis in our classrooms.

“Teachers and pupils deserve answers, not another SNP cover-up.”

A spokesperson for the Scottish Government said: “Joint modelling with COSLA on the costs of implementing RCCT in August 2027 is underway and will inform the Scottish budget.”
