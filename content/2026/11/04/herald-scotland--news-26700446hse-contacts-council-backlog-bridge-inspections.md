**The Health and Safety Executive (HSE) has contacted Dumfries and Galloway Council over its management of the region’s bridges amid public safety concerns, it has been revealed.**

The council is facing a huge backlog of safety inspections for its 1,558 structures – including 687 road bridges – and the UK’s national regulator for health and safety has acted after receiving a complaint from a member of the public.

The news came out during a two-hour debate by councillors on Tuesday following an independent review into the local authority’s management of bridges and structures.

The review itself, carried out by Glasgow-based engineering experts Jacobs, is damning. It highlights how the council is falling short of national standards for bridge safety inspections and assessments.

At the council’s economy and infrastructure committee on Tuesday, Nith Councillor Malcolm Johnstone called the review report “absolutely shocking” and described the situation as “a major potential risk to public safety”.

He also questioned whether Kirkcudbright Bridge – temporarily closed last year and now needing replaced – was “the canary in the coal mine”.

Karen Brownlie, the council’s assistant director of transport and infrastructure, confirmed the HSE had made an “initial inquiry” about the council’s management of bridges and structures.

She told elected members that the council had submitted information including the review to the HSE, and was waiting to hear back.

Annandale North Councillor Carolyne Wilson asked why members had not been told.  
Karen Brownlie replied: “It wouldn’t be normal practice for us to highlight HSE contact within reports.”

Councillor Wilson said an internal review in 2025 had flagged the same problems, and added that councillors were “only fully aware of the scale of these issues because a member of the public repeatedly brought concerns to our attention.”

She warned: “It’s not just about historical shortcomings. It’s about the risk today.”

Significant staff shortages were also flagged by the independent review team.

Dumfries and Galloway Council has admitted that 13 of the 23 approved posts in its engineering design service are vacant.

In the structures team, six permanent staff are in post, with eight vacancies and three agency workers.

At Tuesday’s meeting, Annandale South Councillor Sean Marshall said that some agency staff in the bridges team had been there for nearly a decade.

Officers are adamant that action is being taken and set out a timetable. All records on the system should be updated by the end of December.

Interim engineering design manager Lauryn Steel said the backlog of general and principal inspections should be cleared by the end of March 2027, so every structure has an up-to-date inspection.

Councillor Wilson asked for a full report within the current council term, and officers agreed to update the committee in February, before the elections, and again in June.

North West Dumfries Councillor Andy Ferguson, chairman of the committee, said it was the longest scrutiny session in his 14 years as a councillor. He said: “It actually deserved the length of scrutiny it’s got.”
