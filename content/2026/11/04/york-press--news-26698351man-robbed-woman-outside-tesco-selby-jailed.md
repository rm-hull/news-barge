A serial thief who robbed a woman at a North Yorkshire supermarket has been jailed for more than four years.

Daniel Bell, 33, waited until the woman withdrew £200 from a cash machine at the Tesco Superstore in Selby before “grabbing” the woman’s wrist and taking off with her money, prosecutor Miah Nath said.

The barrister said the woman was left bruised and “extremely scared” after the incident on August 6 and now feels anxious when people stand close to her.

Ms Nath said the woman, who has arthritis in her hip, knee and spine, managed to hold onto a shopping trolley which stopped her from falling over after Bell robbed her shortly after 9.20am.

The woman, in a statement read in court, said her health condition has worsened due to the incident.

She said she was “completely devastated by what has happened” and struggles to sleep due to the robbery.

“The incident relentlessly plays in my head from the moment I wake up to the point of sleep,” the victim said, adding: “I feel completely vulnerable. I thought I was invincible, but not anymore.”

She said she attended the supermarket with her daughter, who waited in the car due to a recent operation and did not see the robbery.

Ms Nath said a man who was in the Tesco car park saw the incident and followed Bell to his home in Portholme Drive.

Police arrested Bell on August 6 on Selby Swing Bridge with £100 on him. He later pleaded guilty to robbery.

## Incident happened months after man was jailed for York robbery

York Crown Court heard the robbery happened after Bell was released from a three-year-and-four-month jail sentence which he was handed in January for robbing an elderly woman outside Marks and Spencer in Pavement last July.

Judge Simon Hickey said a prison sentence was inevitable for Bell after the court heard he has a long list of previous convictions, including robbery and shoplifting.

He jailed Bell for four years and four months.

Judge Hickey said, under current sentencing laws, Bell will serve 40 per cent of the sentence (one year and eight months) before he is eligible for release.

The judge praised the “publicly spirited actions” of the man who followed Bell, saying Bell may not have been caught if not for the passer-by’s assistance.

Judge Hickey said £200 will be paid to the man but did not order any compensation for the victim after the court heard Bell could not afford the costs.

Lily Wildman, defending, said Bell was sorry for his actions.

“He knows that nothing he can do or say can take away from what he’s done,” she said.

Ms Wildman said Bell has struggled with a heroin addiction for most of his adult life but has sought help in jail and is now clean.

“Undoubtedly, drugs have ruined your life,” Judge Hickey told Bell.

Ms Wildman told the court Bell has become an equality representative on his prison wing.

Bell appeared via video link from jail for the sentencing hearing on Wednesday (November 4) wearing a yellow polo shirt with “equality rep” printed on it.
