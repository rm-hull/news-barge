Sandie Peggie is to appeal against an employment tribunal ruling that a nursing union did not discriminate against her because of her beliefs about gender.

The nurse's legal team said work is "progressing" on the appeal, which is due to be submitted this week.

The Fife nurse brought the legal case against the Royal College of Nursing (RCN) over what she claimed was direct discrimination because of her gender-critical beliefs.

Ms Peggie's case against the RCN was heard at an employment tribunal in Dundee in September and October.

In a 37-page judgment issued on Monday, Employment Judge Sangster said: "The judgment of the Tribunal is that the claimant's complaints of direct discrimination do not succeed and are dismissed."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/20307966.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Sandie Peggie speaks to supporters *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

In a statement released to the media, her legal team said: "Sandie Peggie has instructed them to appeal the Employment Tribunal's judgment of 2 November 2026 dismissing her claims against the Royal College of Nursing (RCN).

"Work on the appeal is progressing and the Notice of Appeal is expected to be submitted to the Employment Appeal Tribunal within the coming week.

"Sandie remains deeply grateful to all those who gave evidence on her behalf, as well as to the many individuals and organisations that have supported her throughout these proceedings.

"Given that the appeal process will shortly be underway, no further comment will be made at this stage."

The latest court battle is part of a long-running dispute pitting Ms Peggie against her employer, NHS Fife, and the union.

She had previously brought a case against the health board after sharing a changing room with transgender medic Dr Beth Upton at Kirkcaldy's Victoria Hospital in December 2023.

The first employment tribunal awarded Ms Peggie a partial victory in December last year, upholding claims of harassment but dismissing allegations of discrimination, indirect discrimination and victimisation.

Ms Peggie also accused the union of failing to advise her properly when she first raised the issue of having to share a changing room with Dr Upton with one of its officers.

She claimed the RCN had refused her request for legal assistance in relation to her claims against NHS Fife.

She also accused the union of failing to seek full assurances from NHS Fife in relation to its policies on single-sex spaces.

However, in her judgment, Judge Sangster dismissed all three of Ms Peggie's claims against the union, saying its actions towards her had not been influenced by her protected belief.

She said: "The Tribunal concluded that the complaints of direct discrimination, to the extent that they were framed as being because of an asserted belief of the respondent, could not succeed."
