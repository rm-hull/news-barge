Demolition work is underway in Lowestoft ahead of construction of a £9 million offshore wind operations base for ScottishPower Renewables.

The new facility will be built on Trinity Road, on the site of the former RJ Pryce building, and will support the company’s East Anglia offshore wind projects.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21668337.jpg?type=mds-article-620)East Anglia Hub O&M demo. *(Image: Supplied)*

Ipswich-based Tru Demolition is carrying out the work. Once the site is cleared, construction will pave the way for an almost 6,000 sq m facility designed to serve as a hub for offshore wind operations.

The development will include a three-storey office building, control room, marine coordination centre and dedicated warehouse space. It is expected to accommodate around 100 employees.

The new base will complement ScottishPower Renewables’ existing East Anglia ONE facility, which will continue operating under its current 30-year lease with Associated British Ports.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21668334.jpg?type=mds-article-620)East Anglia Hub O&M demo. *(Image: Supplied)*

The project marks a further investment in the region’s offshore wind industry, with ScottishPower Renewables confirming it has now invested more than £100 million in the East Anglia supply chain.

Work on the new facility is scheduled to begin in early 2027.

Ross Ovens, managing director for offshore at ScottishPower Renewables, said: "Lowestoft and the wider East Anglia region are the home and heart of our offshore wind operations in the UK.

"This incredible investment milestone – testament to the many trusted and high-quality local partners we have – confirms the region is a real powerhouse for the clean energy industry.

"We’re not only operating, building and developing windfarms that will power cleaner and greener lives for millions of people for years to come, but – both directly and through our supply chain partners like R G Carter – we’re creating jobs, creating opportunities and creating a vibrant economy for the region.

"That’s the true measure of the success of our projects and the real and tangible economic legacy that will last for years to come."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21669482.jpg?type=mds-article-620)East Anglia Hub O&M demo. *(Image: Supplied)*

Alex Mackay of R G Carter said: "As a business with deep roots in East Anglia, we are proud to be supporting ScottishPower Renewables on this important investment in Lowestoft.

"Our commitment to building for the future has seen us deliver renewable energy projects for more than 20 years, and this project represents a fantastic opportunity for us to support the UK's clean energy transition while creating lasting benefits for local communities."
