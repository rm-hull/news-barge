As darker nights draw families indoors and online for longer, YouFibre is giving customers the chance to lock in their broadband price until 2028, with no surprise rises in April.

If your broadband bill crept up again this spring, you're not alone. Many of the UK's biggest broadband providers **added up to £4 a month to bills from April 2026.** That's as much as £48 extra a year, for exactly the same service.

## The rules might have changed, but the rises haven't gone away.

Since January 2025, Ofcom has required providers to spell out any mid-contract price rise in pounds and pence before customers sign. That makes the rises easier to spot, but many providers still build one into every April of the contract. Sign a 24-month deal this autumn with one of them, and you could face two price rises before the contract ends.

![]()

## Locking in until 2028

For many households, price rises really hurt. Almost a quarter of broadband customers told a recent Uswitch survey that a £4 monthly rise mid-contract would be unmanageable.

Full Fibre provider YouFibre takes a different approach. It believes broadband should be fair and simple, so its Fixed Price Promise means the price customers sign up for is the price they pay for the full 24 months of their contract. Anyone who joins this October locks in their rate right the way through to autumn 2028.

![]()

## As nights draw in, Britain's online hours go up

The clocks go back on 25 October, and as the evenings get darker, we stay in and log on more. UK adults already spend an average of four and a half hours online every day, according to Ofcom's latest figures. And we're using more data than ever, with the average home broadband connection now getting through 583GB a month\*.

On a typical autumn weeknight, someone's mid-binge in the lounge, someone's mid-boss battle in a bedroom, and someone's logged into the homework app at the kitchen table. Don't forget the never-ending video call happening on the stairs. And that's just the bit you can see. The smart speaker's taking song requests, the doorbell camera's watching for parcels and the heating's quietly checking in with its app. That's a lot to ask of one broadband connection. Households need broadband that can keep up, at a price that stays put.

![]()

## Stormy outside, seamless inside

YouFibre runs a Full Fibre connection straight to the door, with no old copper in the mix, which gives homes faster speeds and more reliability than older, part-fibre broadband. Speeds range from 200 Mbps to 8000 Mbps, and upload speeds match download speeds, so video calls stay sharp and big files send in seconds.

## Dodge the next price rise

* **Check your contract.** Look for a line about annual price rises. Since 2025, providers must show the rise in pounds and pence.
* **Work out the real cost.** Add up what you'll pay over the whole contract, including any rises, not just the monthly price on the advert.
* **Choose a fixed price.** Some providers, including YouFibre, don't raise prices mid-contract at all.

Switching is simpler than many people think, too. Thanks to **One Touch Switch,** customers can sign up with their new provider and let them handle everything, including cancelling the old service.
