# State pensioners under 77 to receive £490 DWP pension increase

## Secretary of State for Pensions Pat McFadden has confirmed new state pensioners will receive a £490 cash boost from April 2026

State pensioners are set to receive a guaranteed £490 boost from the Department for Work and Pensions (DWP), the Pensions Secretary has revealed.

With the triple lock mechanism secured for a minimum of three additional years under the Autumn Budget, the minister has now disclosed the financial uplift coming to state pensioners from April onwards. Those qualifying as new state pensioners reached retirement age during or after April 2016 – which meant 63 for women and 65 for men at that point – meaning they would currently be no older than 76.

Thanks to the most recent triple lock increase, the new state pension will rise by 'around £490', Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden has confirmed. The triple lock formula ensures pensions increase according to whichever is highest among three measures: inflation, wage growth, or a minimum of 2.5%.

While pensioners on the older state pension system have also benefited from the same triple lock uplift, their standard payments are lower, meaning their cash increase isn't quite as substantial.

In a statutory review presented to Parliament after the Budget, Pat McFadden stated: "I am pleased to announce that the basic and full rate of the new state pensions will be increased by 3.9%, in line with the increase in average weekly earnings in the year to May-July 2026.", reports the Express.

"This delivers on our triple lock commitment throughout this Parliament to increase these rates in line with the highest of growth in prices, growth in earnings or 2.5%. From April, the full annual rate of the new state pension will increase by around £490. The full annual rate of the basic state pension will increase by around £375."

These increases reflect the maximum uplift available to state pensioners who hold a complete National Insurance contribution history.

Meanwhile, Chancellor John Healey has unveiled the new Pensions Tax Allowance, a measure initially proposed by Rachel Reeves that has now been fully detailed. Under this scheme, pensioners claiming the full new state pension won't face tax liability on it, even though the annual sum will reach £13,036 - surpassing the current £12,570 Personal Allowance threshold.

Income up to £13,036 will remain tax-free, encompassing not only pension payments but also earnings from savings, property and employment.
