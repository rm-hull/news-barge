A teenager has raised thousands through a netball tournament for the Great North Air Ambulance Service (GNAAS).

Seventeen-year-old Lydia Waddington, from Darlington, volunteered at Hangar 63, the GNAAS coffee house at its headquarters, near Eaglescliffe, and organised the fundraiser as part of her Extended Project Qualification (EPQ).

The event brought in more than £2,200 for the charity.

Miss Waddington said: "It is a very client-facing role.

"I serve hot drinks and food, make sure all the tables are cleared, check everyone’s happy with their food, operate the till and handle money from our customers.

"I’ll also tell people about the charity, including promoting their events, like their classic car and bike nights."

She began volunteering after finishing her GCSEs and now fits in shifts during college holidays and half-terms.

She said: "Volunteering is not a chore to me, it’s something I like to do, so I will fit it in during the holidays."

Passionate about netball, Miss Waddington coaches young children at Darlington Headlanders Netball Club and plays the sport herself.

She decided to combine her love for netball and volunteering by organising a charity tournament.

She added: "I thought it was a really good way to raise money and combine my two interests, so it just started from there.

"With my EPQ, I had to plan it out really rigorously, because it covers planning and organising it and then your execution.

"All the planning from the ground up was solely me, and then I had two main sponsors who basically footed my costs."

Supported by MSG Dental Care, Hutchinson Wealth Management, and Parfetts—her father’s employer—she set an ambitious fundraising goal of £1,000.

Miss Waddington said: "With your EPQ you have to have initial goals, so my initial goal was to raise £1,000, which at the time I thought was quite high.

"I set it at £80 per team to enter, which was quite reasonable and I thought I'd get eight to 10 teams, but I ended up having 14."

The tournament, held at Redcar Leisure Centre, attracted more than 100 attendees and raised £2,282.15, more than double her target.

One of Miss Waddington’s goals is to encourage more young people to volunteer.

She said: "The next generation needs to support the charity as well so it can continue its vital work long into the future.

"That's one of the main reasons why I’m passionate about getting more young people involved."

She advises others to reach out and get involved, even if they are unsure where to start, adding: "If you're unsure, just go speak to people from the charity, or if you’re not confident speaking to them face to face, email someone, which was how I actually started."
