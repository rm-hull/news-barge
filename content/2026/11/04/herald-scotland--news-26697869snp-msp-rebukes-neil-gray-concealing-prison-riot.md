A furious SNP MSP Neil Gray was told by a senior SNP MSP on Wednesday that it was a "gross discourtesy" to Holyrood for the Scottish Government to brief the BBC about a prison riot before telling MSPs.

David Linden, who is convener of the Scottish Parliament's criminal justice committee, rebuked the justice secretary on Wednesday morning as MSPs quizzed ministers on pressures facing the justice system.

Prison officers from four other jails were called in to restore order during the incident at HMP & YOI Polmont in September, which involved about 30 inmates.

It was described by the Scottish Prison Service as the "worst disorder" at a Scottish jail in a decade. But it only became public knowledge due to a report on the BBC, with MSPs not briefed on the incident despite several officers being injured during the melee.

Stark images show panelling damaged and torn out, furniture broken and strewn across the hall and a glass window cracked and shattered.

Scottish Prison Service chief executive Linda Pollock blamed "bubbling tension" caused by increasing overcrowding within the estate.

Mr Linden, a former SNP MP who was elected to Holyrood in May, raised the briefing issue with First Minister John Swinney in Holyrood on Tuesday.

He had particularly stern words for Mr Gray as he pressed him on the matter on Wednesday morning.

"This committee has oversight of your portfolio, and in particular prisons policy," Mr Linden told Mr Gray.

"The idea that information like that would be concealed or withheld, is, I think, a gross discourtesy to this Parliament. And you and I both served as members of the Westminster Parliament, and rightly have gone through Tory government ministers for not providing that information to Parliament.

"So I would be keen for government and the prison service to reflect on how that information can be conveyed to Parliament, because there are mechanisms to do that. This committee should not be finding out about prison riots through the media."

Mr Gray had earlier said the incident had been made public via the BBC but said he was "more than happy to reflect on" on the points made by Mr Linde.

He added that it was a "rare issue" and "given the pressures that I'm talking about" a reason "why we need to be considering as a government and a parliament areas around how we can make our prison system sustainable".

"I agree with Miss Pollock that this is a potential outcome of the population pressures that we are seeing," Mr Gray told the committee.

"Of course, it is one that I will reflect on in terms of information that can be provided to committee, and I'll ensure that is done not just by myself, but by Linda Pollock and by others across the system around what information can and should be shared with committee."

Scottish Conservative MSP Stephen Kerr pointed out it was six weeks between when the riot happening and it being made public.
