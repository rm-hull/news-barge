Residents living in Norton are gearing up for battle again to fight the fifth planning application for what they describe as ‘totally inappropriate’ development next to the much loved Lakeside pond in the the town.

The latest application from a Harome based developer Kevin Linfoot is for 113 self-storage units on the contentious plot adjacent to the Lakeside estate which residents argue should be retained as a green space.

Mayor of Norton, Cllr Di Keal said: “This is the worst proposal so far for the site and has come forward before the developer has a planning decision on the other half of this land where he wants to build houses.

“The self storage units are totally unsuitable for this area right in the middle of a residential area, not only will they be unsightly, but residents will be subject to 24 hour disturbance as the proposal is for round the clock, seven day, operation which is totally inappropriate so close to people’s homes.

“Should the plans go ahead the 24 hour access will mean having electronic outer gates to access the five main buildings proposed, then roller shutters to access the 113 units, lifts to get to the second floor units and presumably lighting to keep it secure, plus vehicle noise, doors being slammed and voices at all hours of the day and night.

“Over 80 people have already objected to the housing development on the site and I expect even more will feel strongly about this latest proposed development. Such storage spaces belong on industrial estates, not next door to peoples homes”.

The developer has already failed to get permission for both housing and sheltered flats on the site. A proposal to the developer by local residents to buy the site and create a public open space on one of the few remaining green spaces in Norton was rejected.

Cllr Keal added: “There is now added anxiety amongst residents that, under new national planning legislation introduced on October 31, their voices and their strong opposition to this latest application and the earlier application for housing on the site will be ignored and a decision will be made by a council officer who has never even visited the area.

“We are calling on the council to meet with residents at Lakeside, listen to our concerns and see for themselves just how much these developments with affect this quiet residential area and the quality of life for local people.

“It would be totally wrong for permission to be granted for these developments on so many grounds, but principally because they would be at high risk of flooding and, by allowing a road to be driven across the well used designated public footpath alongside the site, they would be endangering the lives of local people, especially the many school children and families who use this pedestrian route daily.”

Local resident Martin Sunley said: “The site floods regularly and to gain any kind of access he is going to have cut across a well-used right of way footpath. He is proposing to turn a quiet residential area into a 24 hour access point for cars and vans creating noise and light pollution. The development also goes against the Malton and Norton Town plan - the list of objections just goes on.

“We need North Yorkshire Council to say once and for all that this piece of land is just not up for development and then work with the developer to look for community focused options.”

A spokesperson for the developer said: "The site currently benefits from a sui generis planning permission for agricultural contracting, including outdoor yard storage and buildings dedicated to the sale, maintenance, and repair of large and heavy farm machinery.

"The current application seeks a change of use to establish a lower-impact domestic self-storage facility, offering a more suitable and sensitive reuse of the existing buildings. As the scheme progresses through statutory consultation, the applicant and agent are actively collaborating with North Yorkshire Council to review consultee responses ahead of formal determination."
