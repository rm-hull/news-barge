**The UK Government has introduced legislation to prevent any new oil and gas exploration in the North Sea, marking a significant step in its efforts to meet climate targets and deliver on a key Labour manifesto pledge.**

The proposed Energy Independence Bill would strip the North Sea Transition Authority of its power to grant new licences for oil and gas exploration and production.

However, the legislation will not affect the future of the controversial Rosebank development west of Shetland, the UK's largest known undeveloped oil field. The project received approval in 2022 but remains subject to ongoing legal challenges.

Ministers said the move honours Labour's 2024 election commitment not to issue licences for new oil and gas fields, while supporting wider efforts to tackle climate change and reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

The Conservatives criticised the plans, branding the legislation "dangerous" and accusing Labour of being "totally oblivious to the economic and energy threats this country faces".

Conservative shadow energy secretary Andrew Bowie MP said: “This dangerous bill proves how detached from reality the UK Labour government are about our energy security.

“They may as well hang a closed for business sign over Aberdeen and the North Sea after deciding to double down on their plans to ban any new drilling.

“This will be disastrous for the tens of thousands of workers in the oil and gas sector and leave us increasingly reliant on dirty imports to meet our energy needs.

“Granting Jackdaw should have only been the start from Andy Burnham’s party, not a one-off.

“Labour remain hostile towards the industry and have learned nothing by pressing ahead with these reckless plans. At a time of huge volatility around the world, we should be maximising our own resources from the North Sea, and the Conservatives will oppose this every step of the way.”

David Bucknall, CEO of INEOS Energy, commented that the legislation "chooses campaign promises over the country’s future".

He said: "The government’s legislation to ban new drilling in the North Sea would actively decrease energy security in the UK. Leaving existing resources in the ground at home while we import the same resources from abroad makes our energy autonomy a victim of global instability. Norway, working the same resource-laden continental shelf, is reopening gas fields that have been shut for decades.

"The UK North Sea has ready infrastructure: the pipelines, platforms, ports, and skilled workforce needed for energy production are already in place. We could use these resources to meet the needs of UK consumers more quickly, cheaply, and securely than bringing energy in from elsewhere, while protecting homes and businesses from the volatility of geopolitical impacts. This legislation chooses campaign promises over the country’s future.”

While no final version of that strategy has yet to materialise, the First Minister signalled a shift in his party's position in May, saying energy security should be considered in any new decisions.

**Delegates at the last SNP conference backed a motion** brought by energy minister Stephen Gethins, which supported domestic energy production as "clearly preferable to importation".

It added that the "SNP should champion policies that safeguard Scottish jobs and skills, reduce reliance on imported fuels and use the economic benefits of Scotland's remaining oil and gas resources to help fund the transition to a net-zero economy".
