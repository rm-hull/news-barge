Volodymyr Zelensky on Wednesday condemned a deadly Russian bombing that killed at least 40 people at school in southeastern Ukraine.

The Ukrainian president said search and rescue operations were ongoing three days after Moscow dropped nine aerial bombs on the settlement of Komyshuvakha, in Zaporizhzhia region.

“They first struck people who had come to receive humanitarian aid, and then first responders and police officers who arrived at the scene,” he claimed in a post on Twitter/X.

“Unfortunately, there has been very little reaction from the world to this insane attack. But it, like thousands upon thousands of other war crimes Russia has committed against Ukraine and Ukrainians, demands a strong response,” he said.

Russian retail giant Ozon said meanwhile that one of its warehouses in southern Russia had been struck in a drone attack, triggering a fire and forcing the company to stop taking some orders.

The company did not attribute blame for the attack, but Ozon and its main competitor Wildberries have seen their assets repeatedly targeted by Ukraine in recent months. Kyiv accuses them of supporting Vladimir Putin’s war effort.

## Ukraine's corruption police tie senior security officials to major criminal ring

Senior Ukrainian security officials have been accused of shielding a network of illegal businesses ranging from scam centres to drugs.

Anti-corruption investigators claim top employees of Ukraine's SBU security service, the National Police and the Prosecutor General's Office have taken bribes to protect the criminal ring, even helping to plan attacks and kidnappings.

Oleksandr Poklad, the acting head of the SBU – which also carries out battlefield operations – said the service had dismissed two senior officers implicated in the probe and was "providing full support" for the investigation.

"The Security Service expects a transparent, objective, and impartial investigation," Poklad said in a statement. "The SBU ​works ⁠only for Ukraine and will continue to do so."

The police said they had suspended officers under investigation, while warning against "premature conclusions".

## European troops prepare for postwar Ukraine mission fraught with challenges

Thousands of European troops will take part in a military exercise in Poland this month to prepare for a hypothetical postwar mission in Ukraine to boost the country’s economy and rebuild its armed forces.

But some experts say the deployment may never take place.

The force's first military exercise, led by Britain and France, is aimed both at testing deployment plans and sending a message to Russia and the Trump administration that European countries are stepping up militarily.

Yet the overall mission is plagued with challenges, including the ongoing fighting, questions over American support and the outcome of the upcoming French elections.

Britain and France are both part of a wider political group of 37 nations supporting Ukraine known as the Coalition of the Willing.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky has repeatedly said that in the event of a peace deal, there must be strong security guarantees from Kyiv’s Western allies, including the United States, to deter Russian President Vladimir Putin from attacking the country again.

“This exercise shows the UK is stepping up, alongside France and our allies, to prepare for the day peace comes to Ukraine,” British Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said.

## Ukraine's rescuers deal with grim aftermath of aid shelter bombings: 'Scale of tragedy is terrifying'

Rescuers worked in a desperate search for survivors at an aid distribution site destroyed by a series of Russian attacks in a village in southern Ukraine.

The death toll reached 31 as they removed bodies throughout the day and looked for dozens of people still missing.

Monday's attacks on a shelter and former school with bunker-busting bombs came as people were lined up for aid in Komyshuvakha, a village in the Zaporizhzhia region.

Teams of emergency workers and volunteers picked through debris at the cratered blast sites, as bodies were pulled from the rubble and carried away in white plastic sheeting.

More than 45 residents remained missing.

“The scale of the tragedy is terrifying,” said Mykhailo Semikin, acting head of the region’s military administration, in an online post.

“This is one of the worst tragedies in terms of the number of civilian victims in our region since the start of the full-scale war.”
