**As polls closed in the United States on Tuesday evening, American students and expats gathered to watch the results at venues across the Scottish capital.**

At the Outhouse on Broughton Street Lane, members of Democrats Abroad were bullish on the party’s chances to regain control of the US Congress, two years after Donald Trump and the Republican party romped to victory in November 2024.

Speaking around 10.30 pm, Jack Nevin, who leads the organisation’s Scottish chapter, told supporters that it was “hopefully going to be a great night” for Democrats up and down the ballot.

The Democrats need to win at least 51 seats in the US Senate and 218 seats in the House of Representatives in order to regain control of Congress for the first time since 2022.

“I won't be sleeping tonight,” Mr Nevin, who moved to Scotland to study ten years ago, quipped.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21667762.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Democrats Abroad Scotland leader Jack Nevin addresses supporters. *(Image: NQ)*

“But hopefully we’ll see [tonight] as the fruit of our labour, not just in Scotland, but in talking to our friends back at home, and of course taking a personal initiative ourselves to send our ballots back.

“That really was a dark day,” said Mr Nevin, reflecting on Mr Trump’s unprecedented return to the presidency two years ago. “It’s been a dark two years since then.

“I think we're really mobilizing our community here and we're showing that not just the Americans in Scotland, but people in Scotland are not happy with what's going on in America.

“And to what we are capable of achieving, which is sending our own ballots back and activating as many voters as we can, we're doing that and we're making sure that the voices of Americans in Scotland are heard.”

Democratic voters at the event were similarly positive about the party's chances.

One expat from Pennsylvania said she hoped that Senate candidate James Talarico, a 37-year-old seminary student from Texas, would carry the state for the Democrats for the first time in 28 years.

She said that Mr Talarico’s Christian faith helped him engage with white evangelical voters, a key constituency in the conservative-leaning state.

Jamie, a British man married to an American, similarly said he was hopeful for a positive night, unlike the scenes two years ago, when Mr Trump soundly defeated Democrat Kamala Harris.

Twenty minutes away at Dropkick Murphys near the Cowgate, members of the Democrats Overseas Society at Edinburgh University watched the initial results come in throughout the early hours of Wednesday morning.

Students there were similarly positive about the Democrats’ chances , with many eagerly pointing to early polling returns in Florida and South Carolina as evidence of a blue wave.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21667761.jpg?type=mds-article-620)American students watch the midterm results come in. *(Image: NQ)*

Pints in hand, groups of students huddled around the television, eagerly awaiting the latest updates.

Also taking in the results, sporting matching blue face paint, were students Nicole and Will, both from England.

The pair said they were excited to see a Democrat victory, commenting that the election would have ramifications far beyond its borders.

“I'm stressed. I'm feeling a bit more cynical than he is,” Nicole said around 12.45 am.

“I am calm, but I'm definitely nervous as well,” added Will.

“I think that the energy of the whole campaign is different than two years ago. And so I think by the end of the night, we'll know,” said Nicole.

“I think American politics has such a global impact, whatever happens there is going to have a massive impact."

By Wednesday morning, the Democrats were projected to flip Senate seats in Ohio, Kansas, Texas, Iowa, North Carolina, South Carolina and Maine.

The party also retained seats in Georgia, Minnesota, New Hampshire and Michigan.

That means the Democrats have secured at least 54 seats in the upper chamber, even as results in Mississippi and Alaska are yet to be confirmed.

Similarly, the party overcame gerrymander attempts in the House of Representatives to a secure a projected majority of 75 seats.
