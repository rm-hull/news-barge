**Edinburgh has become the ninth Scottish local authority to adopt the Forces Children’s Rights Charter.**

Campaigners with Forces Children Scotland say that the decision will strengthen the rights of children with parents in the armed forces across the capital.

Thirty-seven per cent of forces children now live in local authorities which have adopted the charter, which is based on the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC).

At a recent meeting of the City of Edinburgh Council’s Education, Children and Families Committee, councillors heard deputations from four local forces children about the importance of the charter.

Louise, 16, helped to draft the charter alongside other young people from across Scotland.

![Edinburgh skyline as seen from Calton Hill, Scotland](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21073740.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Edinburgh became the ninth local authority to adopt the charter. *(Image: Getty Images/iStockphoto)*

The Firrhill High School pupil told councillors: “We needed the Charter because so often the experiences and rights of children and young people from forces families are overlooked.

“We don’t get a say in decisions that affect our lives, but they have an impact on us and can affect our mental health.

“We’re also proud to be part of a forces family, we want that to be recognised and we want to be able to celebrate our identity as a forces child.”

Mya, also 16 and a Firrhill High School pupil, added: “A lot of schools don’t understand the challenges that come from things like deployment and the lack of support when you move to different schools.

“Starting at a new location, at a new school, the charter can make a real difference to that. It also celebrates and protects our identity as forces children.”

McKinley, 10, attends Colinton Primary School, as does Tilly, 11.

“As a forces child, I know that life can change, but every child should always feel that they belong, that they are listened to, and that their rights matter,” McKinley said

“I think it is important that people understand that every forces child is different,” added Tilly.

“People should not make assumptions about our needs because we are all unique. We should be listened to as individuals.”

The charter outlines the following principles:

* Adults do what is best for me and protect my rights
* Everyone understands how Forces life affects me and my rights
* I am proud of being a Forces child, and that identity should be protected
* My education helps me to be my best self, now and in the future
* I can do my interests and hobbies, with the people who matter to me
* I am as healthy as I can be
* I have the best family life I can
* I have a voice, and it is taken seriously
* I am safe where I live and play
* Adults think about the information I need and how and when I need it

Inverclyde, Renfrewshire, Scottish Borders, Angus, Moray, Midlothian, Perth and Kinross and East Ayrshire Councils have previously endorsed the charter.

Education, Children and Families convener Councillor James Dalgleish remarked: “It was fantastic to hear from the young people behind this charter.

“At the Education, Children and Families Committee yesterday we heard how forces children face a lot of difficulties with frequent school moves, often last minute and dealing with their parent being absent for long periods," he added.

“I hope the charter will help these young people feel supported and aid them on their learning journey.”
