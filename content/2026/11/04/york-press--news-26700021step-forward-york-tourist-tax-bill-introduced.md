A bill to introduce a visitor levy on overnight stays in York and North Yorkshire has been introduced in the Commons.

The Visitor Levy Bill was introduced on Wednesday (November 4) and means the proposals to implement a so-called “tourist tax” have taken a step forward.

If passed by Parliament, the bill would proceed to become law allowing visitor levies to be introduced by metro mayors in their areas.

York and North Yorkshire’s mayor, David Skaith, is among the mayors who have committed to keeping any levy capped at five per cent or below.

Mr Skaith has hailed the levy a “gamechanger” for the county and said it could raise up to £26 million which would be reinvested locally.

The mayor previously told The Press all money raised from the visitor levy would go into a “wider pot for York and North Yorkshire” to be distributed across the county.

It means money raised in York may not necessarily be spent solely in the city, which some politicians had called for.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21087220.jpg?type=mds-article-620)David Skaith, mayor of York and North Yorkshire *(Image: Supplied)*

Critics of the tourist tax have suggested it may result in people not visiting York and North Yorkshire.

But Mr Skaith said a report into the proposals pointed to visitor growth remaining steady in places that have already implemented a levy on overnight stays, such as Edinburgh, Manchester, Paris and Barcelona.

He said: “We’re home to historic towns and cities, two of the UK’s best National Parks and a coastline brimming with character.

“There’s something for everyone, and we welcome more than 40 million visitors every year. That’s why an overnight visitor levy will be a total gamechanger for our county.

“Investing in York and North Yorkshire’s cities, towns and villages to make them a better place to visit, and to live in, is at the heart of why I will be introducing a levy in our county.”

Housing secretary Angela Rayner said the visitor levy would allow communities to benefit from the “millions of people” who “visit our wonderful towns and cities each year”.

“Every pound raised through these levies will stay in the local area. Whether that’s better transport, thriving high streets, or more attractions that keep visitors coming back,” she said.

“This is about giving mayors and local leaders the choice to invest back into what matters most to local people, while protecting lower-cost stays and making sure businesses have their say.”

Areas that want to introduce a visitor levy must set out their plans including proposed rates, thresholds, exemptions and how money will be spent alongside an impact assessment.

The government said a local consultation will then follow to ensure local people and businesses have their say.

National exemptions are built in for student accommodation, care homes, shelters and long-term stays over 28 days.

In York and North Yorkshire, Mr Skaith and the combined authority will have the ability to add in any other exemptions.
