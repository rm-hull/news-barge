A man has been banned from contacting women in public under an indefinite Sexual Harm Prevention Order (SHPO).

Andrew Achomna, of Cleckheaton, was handed the order at Kirklees Magistrates’ Court on Tuesday following an application by the West Yorkshire Police Legal Services department in support of police in Kirklees.

## What he is not allowed to do

The SHPO prohibits the 25-year-old from "approaching, following, engaging with, conversing with, or otherwise interacting with any female who is not known to him in any public place or any place to which the public have access, except where the interaction is inadvertent and not reasonably avoidable in the course of normal daily life; or he is being served by, or seeking assistance from, a female in a retail or leisure facility, and the interaction relates solely to the services provided by that facility".

He is banned from "inviting into, or otherwise having, any female not related to him in his house, hotel room, or any other location under his control, whether wholly or partially controlled by him, unless that female is aware of the reasons the order was made; the conditions of the order; and the duration of the order".

## Where he must not go

The order further restricts him from entering Huddersfield town centre, bounded by the Huddersfield Ring Road (comprising Queensgate, Castlegate and Kirkgate), except when travelling through on the train, bus or to attend an appointment.

He is also restricted from entering Dewsbury town centre bounded by the Dewsbury Ring Road (comprising Dewsbury Ring Road, Aldams Road, Vicarage Road, Rishworth Road and Wakefield Road), except when travelling through on the train, bus or to attend an appointment.

He is prohibited from going within 100 metres of any school or college and from wearing a balaclava in any public place or any place to which the public have access.

Although Achomna has not been convicted of any sexual offences, the court heard evidence of a persistent pattern of sexually motivated and intrusive behaviour towards women and girls in public spaces.

## 'Order will significantly restrict his engagement with others'

Sergeant Kirstie Broderick of Kirklees Police said: “A Sexual Harm Prevention Order (SHPO) is a preventative measure designed to protect women and girls from harm.

“As a result of enquires and reports made to us, we felt it was necessary to apply to magistrates for an SHPO to be made against Andrew Achomna and are pleased it has been granted.

“The order will significantly restrict his engagement with others and provides police with additional powers to manage him.

“Tackling violence against women and girls remains a priority for West Yorkshire Police and we will continue to take robust action to safeguard women and girls, support victims, and pursue every available legal measure to prevent harm and protect our communities."

Breaching a SHPO is a criminal offence which will result in arrest and could lead maximum sentence of five years' imprisonment.

The order is indefinite, meaning the restrictions will remain in place unless successfully appealed or revoked by a court at a later date.
