Police and partner agencies have seized thousands of pounds’ worth of illegal fireworks ahead of Bonfire Night.

Northumbria Police officers, backed by the fire service and trading standards officers took action, following intelligence that a resident at the property was storing and selling fireworks from his home without a licence.

Inside the house in North Shields they seized an estimated £2,000 worth of suspected illegal fireworks.

The Northumbria force said it continues to work closely with local authorities, fire and rescue services and wider partners to prevent harm, reduce anti-social behaviour and ensure a co-ordinated response during the run-up to Bonfire Night and on Guy Fawkes Night, November 5, itself.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21663577.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Illegal fireworks seized by Northumbria Police in North Shields *(Image: Northumbria Police)*

Detective Chief Inspector Sarah Robson, who is leading this year’s Bonfire Night operation, said: “This is a great example of the positive outcomes that can be achieved when we work alongside our partners to keep communities safe and prevent fireworks from falling into the wrong hands.

“It’s always important that in the run-up to November 5 we are able to take action such as this which helps to avoid disorder on Bonfire Night.

“Often these products do not meet safety requirements which could cause serious injury to both those who choose to set them off and people around them.

“Our advice as ever is to preferably attend organised displays but if you do wish to set off your own fireworks make sure you buy them from a licensed retailer and follow safety advice.”

Det Chf Insp Robson added: “We know at this time of year some people can feel anxious or intimidated by the darker nights and the activities which go alongside this, so we’d ask that people are considerate of others and take responsibility for themselves.”

Deputy Mayor Councillor Carl Johnson, cabinet lead for Community Safety at North Tyneside Council, said: “We want everyone to be able to enjoy Bonfire Night safely and responsibly and this operation sends a clear message that we will not tolerate the illegal sale of fireworks in our borough.

“Working closely with our partners, we are taking action to protect residents and ensure they can feel safe in their neighbourhoods over the Bonfire Night period.

“If you are planning to buy fireworks, please do so only from licensed retailers and use them responsibly, keeping your neighbours and local community in mind.”

Paul Russell, Area Manager for Community Safety at Tyne and Wear Fire and Rescue Service, said: “Illegal fireworks pose a significant risk to public safety.

"By purchasing only from licensed traders, people can help protect themselves, their families and their communities.

“We will continue to work with our police and local authority partners to target those who sell fireworks illegally, and we ask everyone to show respect for blue light personnel who are dedicated to keeping our communities safe.”

To report a non-emergency crime or anti-social behaviour, Northumbria Police can be messaged privately on social media, by using the 'Live Chat' section on its website or by submitting an online report.

In an emergency, the advice is to always always call 999.
