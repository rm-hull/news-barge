A woman who killed her friend by crashing a rented Lamborghini at “eye-watering speeds” and then blamed him for the collision has been jailed for 10 years.

Humaira Batool had swapped places with Mohammed Shaan Hussain to be able to drive the high performance car at speeds of up to 143mph after he hired it to collect a friend from prison.

The 20-year-old had picked up Batool from her home at about 4am on December 21, 2022.

Shortly before the crash, between junctions 22 and 23 of the M62, the car had reached speeds of 143mph.

The court previously heard how the Lamborghini was driven by Batool “at grossly excessive speeds in wet conditions”.

Batool, who was aged 22 at the time, was convicted of causing death by dangerous driving in September this year.

![Footage shown to the jury shows Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights, moving around the Lamborghini and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21429920.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Batool walking around the vehicle to get into the driver's seat *(Image: CPS / West Yorkshire Police / SWNS)*

During her trial she failed to accept responsibility for the high-speed crash and sought instead to deflect blame onto Mr Hussain.

She was due to be sentenced on Monday but failed to attend court having been admitted to hospital following what was described as “a flare-up” in her endometriosis symptoms.

But her behaviour led prosecution barrister Christopher Dunn to say it was “clearly a ruse” to avoid attending court, and a warrant for her arrest was issued by His Honour Judge Rachim Singh.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21658911.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Humaira Batool *(Image: Ben Lack)*

Judge Singh handed Batool, of Smiddles Lane, Bankfoot, a 10-year prison sentence and told her: “The tragedy of this case lies not simply in the fact that a life was lost, but in the fact that it was entirely avoidable.”

He added: “A young man who should have had many decades of life ahead of him lost everything in a matter of seconds. His family lost someone they loved.

"You must live with responsibility for that loss for the remainder of your life.”

Judge Singh banned Batool from driving for five years with a further five-year extension.

In a family statement for the court, Mr Hussain’s mother, Nasreen, described him as “the heartbeat of this home” and that losing him in the crash “has broken us in ways we cannot put into words.”

She added: “He was just 20 years old, with his whole life ahead of him, dreams in his heart, and so much love to give.

“He was a beloved son, a cherished brother, a loyal friend, and a light in all our lives.”

![Shaan Hussain, who was 20 and from Bradford, died after the black Lamborghini Urus in which he was travelling overturned on the westbound carriageway at Outlane between junctions 22 and 23](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21401186.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Mohammed Shaan Hussain *(Image: West Yorks Police)*

During her trial Batool, now 26, embarked on a "distasteful attempt" to blame Mr Hussain for the excessive speed of the car, suggesting he had meddled with the controls to make the vehicle "surge."

Seconds after it passed Scott Hall Farm – known as “house in the middle of the motorway” – the Lamborghini, which had been travelling at 127mph shortly beforehand, smashed into a barrier, overturned, and burst into flames.

Drivers who had just been overtaken by the stricken supercar stopped and desperately tried to save Mr Hussain.

But their rescue efforts were thwarted by a fire and an explosion. Mr Hussain remained trapped in the car.

Batool was later found on a grass verge near the motorway by a second group of witnesses.

Batool initially told police she had been asleep in the passenger seat and awoke when the car rolled, the court heard.

However, video was shown to the jury of Batool leaving the passenger seat at traffic lights in Bradford, moving around the vehicle, and taking the driver's seat from Mr Hussain.

The court was told that Batool kept “entirely shtum” about the change of driver until confronted with the video footage.

After conceding that she was in fact driving, Batool then put forward a different reason why she was not responsible for the fatal crash.

During the trial, she claimed Mr Hussain triggered a “boost” by tampering with the supercar's controls.

Speaking after the verdict Detective Inspector Paul Conroy of West Yorkshire Police’s Major Collision Enquiry Team, said: “We welcome the sentencing of Batool for the incredibly dangerous driving she engaged in on December 21, 2022.

“Her actions caused the death of a young man in the prime of his life, devastating his family and leaving them facing a lifetime of loss.

“When any of us get behind the wheel we need to accept the responsibility of controlling a machine which can cause devastation if not handled responsibly.

“This especially applies when handling a car of the nature Batool drove that evening.

“Her driving put herself and anyone near her on the motorway at risk and it sadly resulted in the tragedy we have seen in this case.”
