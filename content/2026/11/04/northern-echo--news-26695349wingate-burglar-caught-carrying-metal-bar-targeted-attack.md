A young burglar who was part of an armed gang was found carrying a metal bar by police.

CCTV identified Tyler Harry Ophield among a group who targeted a property on Bradley Street, Easington Colliery, at 7am, on March 12.

Durham Crown Court heard the resident at that address refused to help in the inquiry and prosecution once he had made a 999 call.

Prosecutor Nick Peacock said the group approached the property carrying several weapons, and Ophield was the only one without a face covering.

He also picked up a wooden plank from outside the targeted property.

![Police-issue offender mugshot](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21665748.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Tyler Ophield received a 64-month prison sentence for aggravated burglary and possessing an offensive weapon *(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

The burglars took a motorbike, tools and an air weapon and Ophield left only after a woman arrived and threw him out.

Police searched the area and found him behind Ascot Street still carrying the metal bar.

He was arrested and taken to the custody hub, where he gave “no comment” replies to police questions.

Ophield, 23, of Dodds Terrace, Wingate, admitted aggravated burglary, but only on the day his scheduled trial was due to start on August 26, having previously also admitted possessing an offensive weapon in public.

His record was said to feature five convictions for 17 past offences, all since August 2020, and includes cases of burglary and robbery for which he has served custodial sentences.

He appeared at the court for his sentencing hearing via video link from nearby Durham Prison, where he has been on remand.

His counsel, Michael Forrest, said it had to be accepted that Ophield was facing a long prison sentence.

But he urged the court to consider his still young age, low IQ, learning difficulties and his susceptibility to negative peer pressure.

“It won’t be lost on the court that he was the only one of that group unmasked, the only one who didn’t try to hide his face.”

He said Ophield had recently suffered a family tragedy and no longer wanted a life of crime and spending time in prison.

“While in custody he has reflected on the fact he will have spent most of his 20s in prison due to the poor choices he has made,” added Mr Forrest.

Judge Nathan Adams said Ophield and four others were armed, with the defendant carrying a metal bar, in what was “clearly a targeted attack”.

“Any group deciding to take the law into their own hands, behaving like this in group activity, will always attract large sentences."

He told Ophield: “You say you only became aware you were targeting that property as you got near.

“Although you say you were reluctant to enter the property, you did so when pushed forward by others.

“As for the reason behind the attack, it’s unknown.

“The person whose property was targeted wasn’t prepared to co-operate with the police investigation.”

Judge Adams imposed a 64-month prison sentence, saying it would have been six years without Ophield’s late admission on the day of trial.

He also ordered the metal bar seized from Ophield when he was arrested to be forfeited.
