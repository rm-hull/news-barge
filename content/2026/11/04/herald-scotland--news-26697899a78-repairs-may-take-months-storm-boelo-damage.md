Motorists facing disruption on the A78 between Largs and Skelmorlie may have to wait months before the road fully reopens, according to a senior Amey manager.

The coastal route suffered extensive damage during Storm Boelo, with around 20 voids discovered beneath the carriageway after powerful waves battered the shoreline.

Amey network manager Nick Russell said that engineers are still assessing the full extent of the damage and warned parts of the road are currently unsupported.

He explained: “What the road is built on, the rock and earth underneath, has been washed away.

"The road is effectively floating above the rocks in places at the moment and that needs to be repaired because there is literally nothing underneath supporting it.

“If something too heavy were to pass over those areas, there is a risk the road could collapse into the sea.”

Mr Russell said emergency works have already been carried out to stabilise the route.

He added: “We have already put in some armoured rock as part of emergency repairs and we will be pouring concrete around that to strengthen the seawall again.

"Once concrete is placed beneath the voids, those sections will be made safe.”

However, he said there is still no firm date for when repairs will be completed.

“Before the road can fully reopen without any traffic management, I expect it will be months,” he said.

“Before then, we hope to get traffic flowing through the area under temporary traffic lights or another managed system.

"Our aim is to restore some level of movement as soon as it is safe to do so.”

The comments follow reports that engineers are continuing investigations into the damage caused by the storm, which undermined sections of the carriageway and seawall along the exposed stretch of coastline.

Amey has yet to confirm a timetable for introducing a convoy or traffic-light system, but says work is ongoing to establish how quickly the route can be safely reopened to a greater volume of traffic.

The A78 remains subject to restrictions while repairs continue.

Independent councillor Ian Murdoch, who was one of the attendees at a meeting with Amey on Monday, said he was surprised at the latest update, after councillors had asked for direct answers on when the road may re-open.

He added: "My heart goes out to my constituents who are having lengthy diversions, including hospital workers, carers, NHS workers, fire crews, ambulance and prison officers. I know some of the constituents are struggling to cope with the lengthy journeys.

"A convoy system, whenever that will come, will hopefully make a difference, but it has been very problematic."

Amey has been contacted for comment.
