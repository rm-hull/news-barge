An examination of two sites identified as part of the ongoing search for a missing fishing vessel off the Isle of Lewis has been completed with nothing found, police have said.

The sites east of the Butt of Lewis were identified by the Northern Lighthouse Board vessel NLV Pharos and have now been thoroughly examined by specialist equipment provided by seafood firm MOWI, working with the Marine Accident Investigation Branch (MAIB).

In an update issued on Wednesday, November 4, police said nothing has been identified relating to the Kilda and a maritime exclusion zone in the area has now been lifted.

The MAIB's preliminary assessment into the loss of the fishing vessel is continuing.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21581373.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The fishing boat went missing off the coast near Port of Ness on the Isle of Lewis (Alamy/PA)

Inspector Neil Taylor said: "Our thoughts remain with everyone affected by this tragic incident.

"We continue to work with partners, including the MAIB, Maritime and Coastguard Agency and local maritime experts to locate the Kilda and the two missing men.

“We are grateful for the support of all of our partners, including the NLV Pharos and staff from MOWI for their assistance with the search.”

The Kilda was last known to have been operating close to the Port of Ness in the north of Lewis on Tuesday, October 13.

Norman Murray, 40, who is known as Tam, and Murdo Morrison, 23, from the boat’s crew remain unaccounted for and are being treated as missing people.

The body of their crewmate Douglas Craigie was recovered in the area around 2.45am on Wednesday, October 14.
