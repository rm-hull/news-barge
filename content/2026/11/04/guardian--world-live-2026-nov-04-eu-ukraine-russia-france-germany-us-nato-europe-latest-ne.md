Morning opening: EU to lead talks on support for Ukraine with US, Nato, partners

Jakub Krupa

With most eyes still on the US this morning, Brussels is quietly getting on with the task of leading on discussions about the next round of support for Ukraine.

The European Commission will hold talks with the US, France, Germany, Britain, Nato, and the World Bank, looking what can be done to help Kyiv throughout the winter and how to support the US-led peace process with the upcoming negotiations in Abu Dhabi.

Trump’s special envoy Steve Witkoff said US negotiators would meet with their European partners in Brussels.

Their talks come after another night of heavy attacks on Ukraine, hitting power stations in Kyiv and Zhytomyr and a cargo vessel at sea near Odesa.

The European Union has offered fresh hope for Serbia’s membership talks if the election-winning student movement there forms a government willing to strengthen democracy.

The election of a diverse student-led opposition movement could provide an opening for a reset with the most influential of the western Balkan countries in line at Europe’s door, AP reported.

“Now Serbia has the chance for a fresh start,” enlargement commissioner Marta Kos told EU lawmakers as she debriefed them on the report.

“The new Serbian government has an opportunity to turn the page, strengthen democratic institutions and fundamental rights and take the necessary steps to advance on Serbia’s accession path,” she added.

Serbia could progress rapidly under a government with strong political will but membership currently looks years away.

It comes as the the European Commission said in its enlargement report that Montenegro is on track to become the EU’s 28th member in 2028 (see post 12.33) - and that Albania and Moldova could join the following year.

However, Ukraine is unlikely to meet the conditions for joining before the end of the decade.

40 killed, more than 30 missing after 'insane' Russian attack on Ukraine, Zelenskyy says

Meanwhile, Ukraine’s president Volodymyr Zelenskyy – fresh back from his meetings in Germany yesterday and today – has offered an update on the search and rescue operations after the Russian strike on a school in Komyshuvakha in the Zaporizhzhia region on Monday.

40 people have been confirmed dead, with fears that more than 30 may still be under the rubble, Zelenskyy said.

“Unfortunately, there has been very little reaction from the world to this insane attack. But it, like thousands upon thousands of other war crimes Russia has committed against Ukraine and Ukrainians, demands a strong response. Evil always goes further if it is left unanswered.”

Germany's Wadephul sees both 'continuity and change' after US midterms, hopes for breakthrough on Ukraine

German foreign minister Johann Wadephul has said the results of the US midterm elections will mean some “continuity” in Germany’s relations with the Trump administration, but also a “change” in ties with the US Democrats as he hoped to see some breakthrough on Ukraine.

German foreign minister Johann Wadephul issues a statement on the US elections in Berlin, Germany. Photograph: dts News Agency Germany/Shutterstock

Berlin will “continue to seek common ground” with the Trump administration, “striving for compromise” on key issues in the face of global challenges.

But these elections “also signal change” as the Democratic Party’s success – which he noted was “of a magnitude rare in American history” – will “likely seek to shift the political discourse in the US.”

He said he did not “any complications” arising from this result, and noted that the political engagement will now need to be “broader” and “perhaps even more interesting in certain aspects.”

He said many foreign policy issues featured prominently in the US elections, including tariffs and the war in Iran.

Wadephul added that he hoped for “more dialogue in Congress, across party lines, about what can be done collectively to support Ukraine.

“I think they’ve been holding back on that issue for quite some time ‌now during the election campaign,” he said.

“I also know that in both chambers – and within both parties – there are staunch supporters of Ukraine who clearly recognise that this Russian war of aggression threatens not only security and freedom in Europe but also in the United States.”

'Governing in the US will be almost impossible,' Belgian defence ministers worries

Belgian defence minister Theo Francken has warned that “governing in the US will be almost impossible in the coming years,” but said that Europe’s priorities should be on trade, Ukraine and Nato.

Writing on X, he said in a post translated by Reuters:

“Governing in the United States will be almost impossible in the coming years given the level of internal division and polarisation. As a European politician I will mainly look of course to trade policies, ⁠support to Ukraine and Nato.”

'Democracy won in America,' Tusk declares alongside Czech Republic's Babiš

Czech prime minister Andrej Babiš is in Warsaw today, meeting with Poland’s Donald Tusk, and unsurprisingly both leaders were asked for their thoughts on the US elections.

Poland’s prime minister Donald Tusk chats with Czech Republic’s prime minister Andrej Babiš as they meet in Warsaw, Poland. Photograph: Wojtek Radwański/AFP/Getty Images

Babiš briefly chuckled in response to the question, before saying the results were “as expected,” but stressed the significance of the Republican party losing in places like Texas, where they “ruled for decades.”

He said that Trump will still have “major competence,” and he hoped that the US president would use them to focus on the issues that he promised he’d resolve: ending the Russian invasion on Ukraine and the crisis in the Hormuz strait.

“I am not expecting some sort of major change … but let’s see what he comes up with. What we need … is to end these two wars,” he said, stressing their costs for Europe.

Poland’s Tusk declares: “Today, democracy won in America.”

“Democracy is amazing; I love democracy, it’s a beautiful thing,” he said, absolutely beaming.

He then added:

“I hope that the result will stabilise the situation also in our region, but then Trump is still the president. … My advice would be not to overuse the veto … as a sort of method of governing,” an apparent domestic swipe at Poland’s opposition president and Trump ally, Karol Nawrocki, with whom he is locked in a bitter dispute over the country’s constitutional court.

'Either he decides to rise above fray and tone down involvement, or he doesn't,' French finance minister says

Here are his comments, via Reuters:

“Either he decides to rise above the fray – acknowledging that changes are necessary, particularly regarding gasoline prices, which directly affect people – and, I might add, he tones down his international involvement; or he does not.“

Germany's Pistorius congratulates Democrats on 'historic victory' in US midterms

German defence minister Boris Pistorius has congratulated the US Democeats “from the bottom of his heart” on their win last night, Dlf reported.

Deutsche Welle’s chief political correspondent Nina-Haase-Trobridge posted a longer clip of his comments at the Berlin Security Conference, with Pistorius acknowledging a “historic victory” for the Democrats.

“So for us this means we will continue our relationship based on trust and cooperation with the administration. … And, like in the past, we will have a close working relationship with the Democrats,” he said, quoted by the journalist.

“As for what we can expect now, that remains to be seen. It depends, to some extent, on what the American president intends to do following these elections,” he said in response to a question from Politico’s Rixa Fürsen.

'We'll talk about America some other time,' Meloni tells reporters

Spain's Sánchez hails progressive fightback in the US 'against hate,' draws parallels with Spain

Sam Jones

in Madrid

Spain’s socialist prime minister, Pedro Sánchez – who has been Donald Trump’s most vociferous European critic – is celebrating last night’s results in the US, hailing them as proof of a progressive fightback and of the “crumbling” of what he called the “ultra wave”.

“Yesterday, American society voted against the policies that only impoverish,” he posted on X. “Against hate. Against sexism. Against climate denialism and against the privileges of the elites.”

Sánchez, who faces an uphill battle to secure another term in office in Spain’s general election at the end of the month, added:

“It happened in Brazil. It just happened in the US. And it will happen soon in Spain.”
