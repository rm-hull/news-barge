A parish council in Hartlepool has won permission to install security gates at a car park to prevent late-night “unauthorised vehicular access” and antisocial behaviour.

Hart Parish Council can now progress plans to install gates to enclose a car park in the village near Hart Primary School.

A planning statement from the parish council said the car park is used by the school for overspill parking during normal working hours, as well as being used for general visitor parking.

Although the parish council did not object to the car park’s current use, they said it needed to be controlled outside of working hours.

The planning statement said the plan would ensure that the use of the car park, which sits upon the historic foundations of the Manor House, is controlled and would “make a contribution to and preserve the local character and distinctiveness of the site”.

Hartlepool Borough Council has now approved the plans, which were amended following initial concerns raised by Historic England.

Final plans include a timber five-bar gate with “heavy duty retaining gravity gate stops and associated boulders to either side of the gate”, along with an “access gap” for pushchairs and wheelchairs.

Council planners said the development would not have a “significant impact” on the site’s listed building or scheduled monument, due to its “limited scale, appropriate positioning and sympathetic appearance”.

The planning statement from the parish council stressed the development would “protect the area from unauthorised vehicular access”, describing it as “a particular issue outside of the normal working hours”.

It goes on: “Unauthorised parking encourages antisocial behaviour and vandalism particularly with its close proximity to the Cemetery and St Mary Magdalene Church.
