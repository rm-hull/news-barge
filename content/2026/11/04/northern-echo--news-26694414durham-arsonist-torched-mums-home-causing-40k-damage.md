A firestarter who torched his mother's home causing £40,000 worth of damage was forced to jump from a bedroom window to escape the flames.

Alfie Mavin caused severe damage to the semi-detached house in Gilesgate, Durham, endangering both himself and elderly neighbours.

Teesside Crown Court heard that Mavin had been drinking before arguing with his mother on February 9.

Prosecutor Jon Harley said Mavin’s mother left to visit relatives, and, alone in the house, he then piled wood and other items behind the locked front door to form a barricade.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21664446.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Alfie Mavin started fire at his mother's home when she left to visit relatives following a row with him *(Image: Durham Constabulary)*

At about 7.30pm, he set fire to an aerosol canister and used its flames to ignite the barricade.

Mr Harley said Mavin then went upstairs, piled clothes in his bedroom and set them alight also using the deodorant can’s flames.

Having blocked his escape route, Mavin had to jump from the bedroom window to escape the burning house.

A neighbour saw flames inside the house before watching Mavin jump from an upstairs window on to the lawn at about 7.40pm.

The neighbour called police, who alerted the fire service, and firefighters were able to put out the blaze.

Mr Harley said Mavin was arrested at about 8pm having been found with burns to his arms, legs and face, as well as suffering the effects of smoke inhalation.

Mavin admitted starting the fire and accepted it was reckless because the elderly couple were in the adjoining property.

The court heard a Believe Housing official put the damage at £40,000 on March 3 this year, but the cost was “rising”.

Mr Harley said the housing company also had to pay its £25,000 insurance excess.

Mavin, 26, of no fixed abode, appeared by video link from Holme House Prison in Stockton, where he has been held on remand.

He pleaded guilty to arson, recklessly endangering life, at a hearing in March after indicating his plea early in proceedings.

Sentencing was adjourned to allow for preparation of a Probation Service report and a psychiatric assessment.

The court was told the defendant has 16 offences on his record for 38 prior offences, dating from August 2019 to September last year.

Mr Harley said most were dishonesty offences, but several involved criminal damage.

Jennifer Coxon, in mitigation, said Mavin accepted he was facing a prison sentence.

She said he was using his time in custody “usefully” by engaging with the prison’s alcohol and drug recovery services.

Referring to the reports, she said they recorded Mavin’s “mood disorder”, diagnosed as depression linked to childhood trauma.

She said he has resorted to self-harm plus alcohol and drug misuse when under stress.

“The psychiatrist identified that if not under stress or intoxicated he wouldn’t have taken the decision to light that fire.

“If those factors can be worked around, he has the determination to work with the Probation Service to abstain from alcohol and drug misuse.

“Then, it will be easier for him to cope with those stress factors in his life.”

Miss Coxon said Mavin’s mother left to visit relatives in London on the day of the offence, fuelling "childhood abandonment issues" that led him to light the fire.

“He’s lucky he wasn’t harmed more seriously and that others were not harmed.”

Judge Peter Armstrong told Mavin the reports showed he has had a long history of mental health problems having “not had the best start in life.”

But he said Mavin needed to avoid alcohol and stressful situations in future.

The judge said Mavin caused “substantial damage” and endangered himself and others through his reckless actions.

Judge Armstrong jailed Mavin for 32 months, saying the sentence would have been four years but for his early guilty pleas.

He may have to serve up to half of the sentence in custody before being released on licence.

But he warned Mavin that any further offending while on licence would send him back to custody to serve the rest of his sentence.
