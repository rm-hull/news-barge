A Shildon burglar posed as an undercover police officer to con his way into a cancer patient's home before fleeing in the man's £20k car.

William Patton and his side-kick claimed to be investigating an attempted bicycle theft and were let into the house in Shildon, at 5am on January 29.

Durham Crown Court was told one of the men asked to use the toilet but went into the living room and back garden while Patton stood in the hallway.

Before leaving, they asked for the bicycle-chain key so they could move the bike to the back garden.

As they left, they told the householder - who suffers with throat cancer - to keep his door locked.

Prosecutor Nigel Soppitt said the victim realised 20 minutes later that the keys to his £20,000 MG car were missing.

Police were called after Patton and his accomplice were spotted near the car, and pool cues belonging to the victim’s son were found to be missing.

Mr Soppitt said the pool cues were later recovered from Patton’s home.

Patton was arrested and interviewed on March 2, and he was then on bail when other offences were committed.

The 33-year-old defendant, of Dent Street, admitted burglary, theft and attempted theft but only on the day his trial was due to begin.

In an impact statement, the Shildon resident said the offences had a major effect on him.

He believed the men who came to his home on January 29 were genuine undercover detectives, which damaged his confidence in police.

Mr Soppitt said the victim struggled to sleep and used morphine to ease his cancer pain.

In mitigation, Michael Forrest said Patton was realistic he was facing a long prison sentence.

He said Patton’s poor mental health and drug addiction had fuelled his offending.

“He looks back on his life with nothing but regret and shame.

“His addiction to cocaine has been the noose around his neck, with poor consequential decision making and is transparent about that.”

At Durham Crown Court, Patton also admitted further offences of assaulting an emergency worker by spitting at a female Police Community Support Officer dealing with another incident near his home on March 10.

The defendant also admitted taking part in the burglary of an unoccupied home in Arthur Street, Bishop Auckland, on April 4.

Police arrived during the break-in, chased those present from the property and arrested Patton.

He claimed someone had told him to enter and “grab some stuff”.

Mr Forrest said Patton, who has been on remand for more than six months, hopes to use to use the rest of his time in custody to maintain his sobriety and complete a detox.

“He’s been drug-free for seven months, giving him the chance to reflect on the life decisions he’s made.”

Mr Forrest added that Patton suffered from a neurological disorder that causes up to 15 fits a day.

Sentencing, Judge Nathan Adams said: “The underlying issue you have has been your long-standing addiction to cocaine.

“You have been in court multiple times, with 17 convictions for 50 offences going back to your youth.”

He sentenced Patton to four years in prison, with one-third to be served in custody before release on licence under current sentencing guidelines.

The judge told Patton: “I’m told you hope this will be your last time in prison.

“I very much hope that’s the case.

“Use the rest of your time in custody to your advantage.”
