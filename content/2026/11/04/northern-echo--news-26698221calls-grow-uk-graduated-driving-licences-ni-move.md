Campaigners across County Durham and North Yorkshire have renewed calls for Graduated Driving Licences (GDL) to be introduced nationally, as Northern Ireland becomes the first part of the UK to implement the scheme.

Under the new arrangements, which came in on October 1 in Northern Ireland, learner drivers and motorcycle riders must complete a minimum six-month learning period, follow a structured training programme and logbook, and pass a theory test before progressing to their practical test.

New drivers will also enter a two-year restricted period and be required to display R plates.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19466130.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nicola Bell-Percy in Masham *(Image: PATRICK GOULDSBROUGH)*

For the first six months after passing their test, drivers under 24 will face restrictions on carrying passengers aged 14 to 20 between 11pm and 6am, with exemptions for immediate family members and when accompanied by an experienced driver.

Over the years, there have been many politicians, bereaved families and campaigners who have pushed for an introduction of GDLs in UK law.

Nicola Bell-Percy, from Jervaulx, North Yorkshire, lost her son, Aaron Bell, 18, in 2022 after he was the passenger of a vehicle travelling on the B6268, between Bedale and Masham, with three friends, when the driver of the vehicle hit a tree.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/14630711.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Aaron Bell *(Image: FAMILY)*

Aaron and two of his friends died at the scene of the incident, while the driver of the vehicle, Joshua Chapman, was jailed after admitting death by dangerous driving and other offences.

Since Aaron's devastating road death four years ago, Nicola has campaigned to bring in Graduated Driving Licences (GDLs) across the UK for young people who have just passed their test.

Speaking earlier this year, Nicola said: “It's bittersweet news that a country near to ours is adopting these laws. It gives us a glimmer of hope that it could one day be brought in England.

![Nicola Bell-Percy (third right) and the other grieving mothers](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/19284931.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Nicola Bell-Percy (third right) and the other grieving mothers *(Image: Jordan Pettitt/PA)*

"However, it's also tough because we need these changes now, and before we see results of how this has changed young drivers in Northern Ireland, it could be two or three years yet.

"We need politicians brave enough to upset young drivers and to protect them from incidents.

"So far, no one from the government has come up with a justified reason why they are against; surely protecting our young drivers is a no-brainer."

Graduated Driver Licensing schemes are already in operation in many countries, including the Republic of Ireland, Australia, New Zealand, Norway, Sweden, Finland and across much of the United States, where they have been credited with significant reductions in deaths and serious injuries involving young drivers.

In County Durham, Police and Crime Commissioner Joy Allen has backed the calls to roll out GDLs in the UK.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21669502.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham PCC Joy Allen *(Image: SARAH CALDECOTT)*

As Joint APCC Lead for Roads Policing, Durham PCC has been at the forefront of a national campaign to improve the safety of young drivers. Alongside Warwickshire PCC Philip Seccombe, she co-chairs the Protect Young Drivers Steering Group, which brings together road safety organisations, experts, campaigners and bereaved families to press for reform.

She said: “The evidence is clear. Graduated Driver Licensing saves lives.

“For too long, the debate has focused on perceived restrictions rather than the devastating consequences of serious collisions involving young drivers. Countries that have adopted Graduated Driver Licensing have seen reductions of between 20 and 40 per cent in crashes involving young motorists. When the evidence is this compelling, the case for action becomes impossible to ignore.

“Road safety remains one of the issues people raise with me most often. In survey after survey, residents tell me they want safer roads and stronger action to prevent deaths and serious injuries.

“Graduated Driver Licensing is one of the most effective measures available to protect young drivers, reduce risk and prevent families suffering avoidable tragedies.

“As Police and Crime Commissioner and Joint APCC Lead for Roads Policing, I will continue to press the Government to introduce Graduated Driver Licensing. I have requested discussions with the Roads Minister and will continue working with partners, campaigners and bereaved families to make the case for change.”
