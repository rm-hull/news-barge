Amanda Owen and her children have reached a major milestone at their new Yorkshire Dales farmhouse as it is confirmed that their television programme will return for two more series.

The fourth-series finale on Monday, November 2, features a momentous occasion as Amanda and her daughters prepare for their first sleepover at the property.

It marks the first time anyone has slept at Anty John's in many decades.

The milestone comes as Channel 4 announces that the programme has been renewed for a fifth and sixth series, with Leeds-based Wise Owl Films commissioned to produce another ten hour-long episodes.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21658310.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Edith and Amanda and an antique bed at Anty John's *(Image: CHANNEL 4)*

The new series will follow the family as they continue transforming the historic farmhouse from a building project into a home.

Series five will pick up immediately after the family's first overnight stay, following their lives from early summer 2026 through to spring 2027.

The fourth-series finale also sees Amanda and her children moving furniture collected over the years into Anty John's as the interior finally begins to take shape.

The development represents an important moment in a lengthy renovation project which has involved Amanda, former husband Clive Owen, their nine children and local builders and tradespeople.

The family have worked to restore the property while continuing their demanding farming lives at Ravenseat, the remote hill farm made famous by Channel 5's Our Yorkshire Farm.

The programme has documented the difficulties of renovating a historic building in one of the most isolated parts of the Yorkshire Dales.

Away from the restoration, the final episode follows the younger Owen children enjoying the arrival of spring.

Nancy and Clemmie head out on horseback across the moors and into a wildflower meadow for their first ride since winter.

Meanwhile, Clemmie and Annas sell eggs from the family's chickens to earn extra pocket money.

Clive is also busy with the farming calendar as sheepdog Nellie returns from maternity leave to help move sheep from a field ahead of mowing.

The fourth-series milestone will not mark the end of the family's television journey.

Channel 4 has confirmed that all 11 members of the Owen family will feature in the next instalments, with the renovation continuing alongside their everyday lives.

Edith, Violet and their sisters will be seen opening the family's cream tea van at Ravenseat, while Sidney and Miles take on increasing farming responsibilities.

Amanda will also continue investigating the history of Anty John's and former owner Anthony Clarkson, a 19th-century Swaledale mapmaker whose diaries provide an insight into life in the area almost 200 years ago.

Jayne Stanger, commissioning editor for Channel 4, said: "Our Farm Next Door is a joy of a series that holds a special place in the hearts of our More4 viewers.

"I am thrilled we're continuing to work with the team at Wise Owl to follow Amanda, Clive and the whole family on their renovation journey and I can't wait to see whether Anty John's might ever be finished!"

Although the first sleepover is a major milestone, the broadcaster has made clear that the restoration journey is continuing, with the next two series documenting further progress.
