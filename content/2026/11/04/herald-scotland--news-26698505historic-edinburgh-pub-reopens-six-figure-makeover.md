An Edinburgh pub once popular with poets and arts students has reopened following a six-figure refurbishment.

Milnes Bar on Hanover Street, part of the Belhaven pub group, has undergone a refresh designed to retain its historic character while introducing modern features for customers.

The work includes improvements inside and outside the pub, with new signage, lighting and outdoor furniture.

Inside, the refurbishment has brought updated flooring, booth seating, lighting and decor, alongside curated artwork. The toilets have also been upgraded.

The pub’s literary heritage remains a central feature, with its 'Lonely Poet’s Chair' still in place. The Kremlin room, named in reference to the political debates once held there, has also retained its antique features.

The venue was a well-known meeting place for poets and arts students during the 20th century and continues to attract locals and visitors to the city.

The pub serves food and drinks throughout the day, including Sunday roasts, and is promoting its refreshed setting for everything from lunches and after-work visits to the festive season.

Milnes Bar is part of Belhaven, which operates more than 100 pubs across Scotland. Established in 1719, Belhaven Brewery is Scotland’s oldest working brewery and has been part of Greene King since 2005.

Susan Power, general manager of Milnes Bar, said: "This bar is a real gem in the city centre and it’s great to be open again for everyone to enjoy our new, enhanced look and feel.

"It’s still full of the all the history and character but with those extra special features to really add to the Milnes Bar style and experience."
