York has once again transformed into a city-wide celebration of film, music, games, virtual reality.

The BAFTA-Qualifying Aesthetica Film Festival has opened its 16th edition, running from **Wednesday 4 to Sunday 8 November**.

Over five days, audiences can experience more than 300 films alongside live music, immersive installations, games, podcasts, industry events and creative workshops, as filmmakers, artists, musicians, game developers and leading creative organisations converge on the city.

With delegates attending from more than 60 countries, York becomes an international meeting point for the creative industries.

At the heart of Aesthetica is a programme of more than 300 films from around the world, spanning animation, comedy, dance, documentary, drama, fashion, music video, thriller and more.

The 2026 programme features screen talent including Aisling Bea, David Jonsson, Maxine Peake, Michael Sheen, Sally Phillips and Stephen Fry, alongside new filmmakers from across the UK and internationally.

Since launching in 2011, Aesthetica has championed new talent, with festival alumni going on to receive BAFTA and Academy Award recognition.

Aesthetica’s acclaimed Industry Programme brings leading figures from film, television and media to York, offering audiences direct access to the people behind some of the UK’s most recognisable productions.

Representatives from Aardman, BBC, Film4, Framestore, ITV, Neal Street Productions, Ridley Scott Associates and Ubisoft share their expertise through masterclasses and industry sessions. On average, more than 6,000 people attend these sessions.

Highlights include:

* Mark Jenkin (Bait, Enys Men, Rose of Nevada), Jess Iszatt (BBC Introducing on Radio 1), Mick Audsley (Harry Potter), Neil Forsyth (The Gold) and Brian J. Falconer (Derry Girls).
* A behind-the-scenes look at what it takes to make a Ridley Scott film with Luke Scott
* A celebration of 50 years of Aardman
* Sessions exploring directing, sound design, visual effects, animation and production

Aesthetica brings film and music together through a dedicated Music Programme, creating a platform for emerging artists to perform, connect with the industry and reach new audiences.

At its heart is the New Music Stage, returning to York Theatre Royal on Saturday 7 November for an all-day celebration of live music. Ten breakthrough artists will perform across genres including indie, folk, jazz, hip-hop, alt-pop and pop. This is the place to discover the headliners of tomorrow.

The Games Lab returns to the National Centre for Early Music for its fourth edition, presenting 50 games across PC, console, headsets and smart devices.

This year, the festival extends beyond traditional venues and into the city itself. Created in partnership with Audible, a free Immersive Media Arts Trail opens today, running throughout the festival week.

Works by Squidsoup, Steve Messam and Being Now Here bring three new installation-based artworks to York, using light, sound and interactive technology to transform public spaces.

This free art trail connects the historic landscape of the city with contemporary art.

Audio storytelling will also take centre stage through Aesthetica’s Podcasting Lounge at City Screen, showcasing work spanning true crime, investigative journalism, documentary, audio fiction and more.

Aesthetica also continues its partnership with Audible through the Listening Pitch, culminating in the premiere of three new documentaries on Friday 6 November, followed by a Q&A, DJ, drinks and networking.

The festival’s programme for younger audiences includes Family Friendly screenings alongside workshops for children aged 7-14 covering filmmaking, acting, gaming and stop-motion animation.

This gives young people practical experience of creative technology while developing confidence, collaboration and digital skills.

“Aesthetica has always been about creating a space where audiences can discover new ideas and where artists and industry can connect,” said Cherie Federico, Festival Director.

“With the city’s UNESCO Status, York is fundamental to that. We bring together people working across film, television, music, games, technology and the wider creative industries, and give them a city in which to connect.”
