High above the Yorkshire Dales, the Tan Hill Inn has sheltered customers caught in snow storms, turned strangers into friends and battled to keep its lights on.

At 1,732ft above sea level, the Tan Hill Inn is officially Britain's highest pub, a remote Yorkshire institution whose extraordinary location has made it famous far beyond the county.

For centuries, the isolated inn near Keld, North Yorkshire, has offered food, drink and shelter to people crossing the moors.

Its dramatic setting has made it famous, but created challenges few other landlords face.

With another Yorkshire winter approaching, the pub is urging visitors to support it after an unexpected maintenance closure.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/20527254.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Tan Hill Inn *(Image: SUPPLIED)*

In November 2021, around 60 people were trapped inside the Tan Hill Inn for three nights.

Oasis tribute band Noasis was performing when Storm Arwen brought heavy snow to the Yorkshire Dales and blocked the surrounding roads.

A night out became an unexpected three-day stay.

Customers slept on makeshift beds while staff kept them fed, warm and entertained with karaoke, quizzes and snowman-building contests.

The scenes made national news, but being cut off is nothing new at Tan Hill.

In January 2025, heavy snow blocked the roads around the inn and stranded another group.

The disruption trapped 23 guests and six staff, until farmers used snowploughs to clear a route.

Owner Andrew Hields said conditions could deteriorate quickly.

He said: "We are very exposed at 1,732ft and although it does not snow as often or as heavy as it used to problems can occur with snow drifts."

Long before its snowstorm fame, the Tan Hill Inn was a vital stop for people working and travelling across the remote moors.

The present building dates from the 17th century, and the surrounding area has deep coal-mining roots.

Miners, shepherds, drovers and travellers relied on the inn for food, drink and shelter.

After the last local mine closed in 1929, the inn became a popular destination for visitors drawn to the Yorkshire Dales’ scenery.

Its position near the Pennine Way made it a walkers’ stop, while drivers travelled there for a drink at Britain’s highest pub.

Its reputation even landed it a starring role in a Waitrose Christmas television advert in 2017, inspired by its famously unpredictable winter weather.

Tan Hill’s isolation makes it special, but difficult to run.

Unlike most pubs, it cannot rely on nearby homes, shops or businesses for regular customers.

Visitors must cross the moors on roads exposed to Yorkshire’s worst weather.

The pub is also completely off-grid, making it more complex and expensive to run.

The inn has reopened after an unexpected maintenance closure, but warned that running it remains financially tough.

In a message to customers, the pub said: "Being Britain’s highest inn is something we’re incredibly proud of, but running a pub completely off-grid, 1,732 feet above sea level, comes with its challenges.

"Unlike your average pub, the costs involved in simply opening our doors each day are enormous."

It added: "We don’t have a village on our doorstep or a steady stream of passing trade. We rely on people making the journey to see us, and we’re so grateful to everyone who does."

With winter approaching, Tan Hill’s owners hope visitors will keep making the journey.

The pub said: "Whether you’re a local who regularly makes the journey up, a walker passing through, or someone who’s had Tan Hill on their bucket list for years, your support is what keeps this special little place going.

"So, if you’ve been thinking about visiting Tan Hill, we’d really love you to make that trip."
