An 11-year-old boy has been taken to hospital with serious injuries after being involved in a collision with a car.

West Yorkshire Police were called at 7.48am to Boothtown Road near its junction with Rawson Street North in Halifax.

The boy's injuries are serious but not life-threatening, police have confirmed.

The force added that the driver remained at the scene and spoke with officers.

## What police could say

In a full statement, a spokesperson for West Yorkshire Police said: "At 7.48am today, police were contacted by the ambulance service who had been called to a road traffic collision involving a child pedestrian in Boothtown Road, Halifax.

"The pedestrian, an 11-year-old boy, had been involved in a collision with a grey Honda CR-V near to the junction with Rawson Street North.

"He was taken to hospital by ambulance for treatment to injuries that were serious but not considered life threatening.

"The driver stopped at the scene and has been spoken to by officers.

"A scene and road closure were put in place pending updates from hospital but were removed at about 10.40am."

A Yorkshire Ambulance Service spokesperson said: " We received an emergency call at 7.42am this morning to report a collision between a car and a pedestrian on Boothtown Road in Halifax.

"Two ambulances and the air ambulance were dispatched to the scene and one patient was conveyed to hospital by road."
