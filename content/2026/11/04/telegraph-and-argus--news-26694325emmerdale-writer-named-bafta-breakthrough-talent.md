Emmerdale writer Yassmin Abdel-Magied has described the Yorkshire soap as "the best continuing drama" after being selected as one of this year's Bafta Breakthrough talents.

The 35-year-old is among 21 creatives chosen for the prestigious scheme, which supports emerging talent working across film, television and video games.

Ms Abdel-Magied began writing for Emmerdale in 2024 after securing a place on a 12-week ITV programme designed to support emerging and underrepresented writers.

She described the opportunity as "life-changing" and has since gone on to write eight episodes of the long-running soap.

## 'Everyone knows everyone else's business'

Speaking about her admiration for the programme, Ms Abdel-Magied said she believes Emmerdale stands out among British television dramas.

She said "I am not saying this because I'm working on the show – but I feel like Emmerdale is the best continuing drama,"

Ms Abdel-Magied added: "I love the small, rural village setup. I feel like everyone knowing everyone else's business is great."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21664543.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ms Abdel-Magied presenting the TED talk: "What being a public outrage taught me about fighting inequality" *(Image: Youtube / TedX Talks)*

The writer also praised the flexibility the setting offers for storytelling.

She said: "Emmerdale has also done some really interesting things, and the setting allows you to tell stories that are quite different,"

First broadcast in 1972, the ITV soap follows the lives of residents in a fictional village in the Yorkshire Dales.

## Tackling hard-hitting storylines

Since joining the writing team, Ms Abdel-Magied has worked on storylines involving established characters including Charity Dingle.

Her episodes have explored sensitive issues such as grooming, exploitation and modern slavery, as well as a recent storyline centred on female-on-female sexual assault.

Discussing the responsibility of writing for an audience of millions, she said: "It's quite a big responsibility...you can't just put stuff out that isn't understanding of the impact.

Ms Abdel-Magied added: "It's also a huge honour, because the story that I could tell could have a life-changing impact on people."

She added that audiences are often willing to follow ambitious plots if they are told in a way that feels true to the show.

## 'I spat my coffee out'

The writer said she was stunned when she learned she had been selected for the Bafta Breakthrough programme.

"It's not just me being soap-y when I say I spat my coffee out," she said. "I was just blown away, because you apply for all these things and you rarely make it through."

The Bafta Breakthrough initiative, supported in the UK by Netflix, provides professional development opportunities and access to industry contacts.

Abdel-Magied said she hopes the recognition will prove to be a major turning point in her career.

"I'm hoping I'll look back one day and be like: This was the year that changed my life," she said.
