As the Supreme Court prepares to examine the ban on Palestine Action and the arrests of protesters holding banners supporting the organisation, the York Central Labour MP says the scenes of police arresting elderly people are damaging the Government.

Rachael Maskell says the proscription of Palestine Action is wrong. She told YorkMix Radio:

“I didn’t vote for the prescription of Palestine Action. I do recognise it is a direct action group. However, if it crosses the line with regards to criminality, then clearly there is law already in place to address that.

“I am really disturbed that we know that over 4000 people have so far been arrested. The vast majority just simply holding up a sign and sitting in a square, saying I support Palestine action.”

Yvette Cooper MP, the then Foreign Secretary, who brought in the ban, defended the decision saying it was based on serious security advice.

Ms Maskell added: “I don’t know what discussions are happening in government at the moment. But they know my views. I want them to review this prescription and to move us forward into a space where we can recognise the difference between a direct action group and what is a terrorist organisation”

**Case Timeline**

* **June 2025:** Government proscribed Palestine Action as a terrorist group following property damage incidents involving military/defence suppliers.
* **February 2026:** The High Court ruled the proscription**unlawful**, finding that the government breached its own policy and disproportionately infringed upon freedom of expression and assembly.
* **June 2026:** The Court of Appeal**allowed the government’s appeal**, overturning the High Court judgment,
* **November 4–5, 2026 theSupreme Court will look at the case.Current Legal Status**

* Palestine Action remains a **proscribed terrorist organisation** in the UK
* Belonging to, inviting support for, or displaying articles in public that arouse reasonable suspicion of support for the group is a criminal offense carrying potential prison sentences of up to 14 years.

Rachael Maskell continued: “This is not a good use of police resources. It’s not the way that we conduct ourselves. We value protests in our country to move governments and to change agendas.

“So I don’t support what is currently happening. And of course, I am looking forward to hearing the outcome of the Supreme Court as to its judgement whether or not Palestine Action should be a proscribed organisation, I believe that is the wrong allocation for it.

“Obviously there was a change in approach in Government, a less authoritarian approach, a much more liberal kind of engaging approach, wanting to hear what people have got to say, and of course protest is a mechanism for that.

“Most of the people who have been arrested are just desperate about the atrocities which have been committed on the Palestinian people, and really are humanitarians at heart. And I think we should be able, in our legal system, to be able to distinguish that. So I believe we’ve got to move forward from this sorry chapter and ensure that we have good order, and protect the public, but at the same time ensure that people have got the right for their voices to be heard.”
