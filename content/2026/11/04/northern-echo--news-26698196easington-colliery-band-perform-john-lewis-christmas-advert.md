A County Durham brass band rooted in the region's mining heritage landed a key role in this year's John Lewis Christmas advert.

Easington Colliery Band recorded the brass section for Sam Fender's cover of the Rolling Stones' classic *You Can't Always Get What You Want*, which soundtracks the retailer's 2026 Christmas campaign.

It is the latest milestone for a band that has spent more than a century serving its former mining community and in recent years has struck up a connection with Fender.

It began in 2023 when the North Shields-born singer was searching for a colliery brass band to feature on Remember My Name, a song inspired by his grandparents and their mining roots.

Peter Lawson, 68, the last ex-miner still playing with the group, said the call came "completely out of the blue", but quickly led to opportunities the band could scarcely have imagined, including performances at St James' Park and the London Stadium.

In August, the band received a call from Fender's team telling them there could be another recording project in the pipeline, although details were kept tightly under wraps.

Members were initially asked to travel to London at short notice but felt they needed more time to learn the arrangement properly and do it justice.

Instead, Fender suggested the session take place closer to home in North Shields.

When the musicians arrived, they were still no wiser about what they were recording for.

The group spent around two-and-a-half hours working through the track, listening to recordings of Fender and a choir to understand what was required, before eventually laying down the brass parts.

Only after the session ended were they finally told the truth.

"We were finally told it was for the John Lewis advert, but that we had to keep it incredibly under wraps," the former miner said.

"I was just in shock. Never in a million dreams would I have thought we would appear in an advert like that.

"People plan their whole Christmas preparations around when the advert comes out, so it was a real honour."

For Mr Lawson, who joined Easington Colliery Band aged 10 and worked at the colliery for 20 years, the achievement means more than a fleeting brush with fame.

After guiding the band through difficult periods and seeing it win the North of England Brass Band Championships for the first time, he believes Fender is helping secure brass bands' future by reaching new audiences.

The 68-year-old said: "There's a real sense of gritty determination throughout his work which reflects the people in the North East.

"I think Sam has really helped us bring banding into the 21st century.

"We still enjoy doing concerts for clubs and communities, but it's opened the door to so much.

"It's really promoted brass banding and I hope it shows other bands that you can get a lot of success stepping outside of traditional band playing."

Mr Lawson believes the John Lewis advert could give the band its biggest audience yet and introduce more people to an art form rooted in North East mining communities.

"It's just unbelievable," he said. "It would never have been possible without Sam."
