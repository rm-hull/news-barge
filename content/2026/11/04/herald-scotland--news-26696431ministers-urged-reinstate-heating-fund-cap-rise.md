A third of Scots will be forced to choose between freezing and falling into unpayable debt when the energy price cap rises in January, a coalition of 20 organisations has warned the Scottish Government.

In a joint letter to Stephen Gethins, Minister for Europe, External Affairs and Energy, the groups call on the Scottish Government to reinstate the Home Heating Support Fund before winter and to provide targeted help for rural and island households.

Cornwall Insight forecasts that Ofgem's price cap will rise by 16% in January, from £1,723 to £1,999 a year for a typical household.

The £276 increase would take the cap to its highest level since March 2023.

The signatories include Changeworks, Energy Action Scotland, SCARF, the Poverty Alliance, the Scottish Federation of Housing Associations, One Parent Families Scotland and the Wise Group.

They say 28.7% of Scottish households already live in fuel poverty, with 14% of those in extreme fuel poverty and 19% in energy debt. The letter warns that families are "entering a brutal winter with depleted financial resilience".

The campaign highlights the case of Laura, a Highland householder who has underlying health conditions — including asthma and fibromyalgia — which make her particularly vulnerable to the cold.

Laura relies on oil for hot water, the price of which has doubled in the last few months. Her latest tank refill was around £900, compared to around £400 previously.

Despite receiving support from local organisations to access the Scottish Government's Emergency Heating Fund, with prices expected to increase further, they warn the "upcoming winter months will continue to be difficult without additional support".

The letter states: "The Government must act now. We are writing to you in your capacity as Cabinet Secretary because you are well placed to bring together a cross-governmental response addressing rising energy costs and support for the most vulnerable.

"We welcome the extension of the Scottish Emergency Heating Oil Scheme, which will provide important support to households that rely on alternative fuels.

"We also welcome the UK Government's budget announcements to cut VAT on electricity, reform energy levies, and remove gas standing charges to alleviate fuel poverty.

"However, given the distinct needs of our communities, we urge the Scottish Government to swiftly provide detail on how the funding for these schemes will be implemented within Scotland, alongside other programmes.

"Despite this, the anticipated scale and breadth of the challenge means that further action is needed to protect all households at risk."

They call on the Scottish Government to reinstate the Home Heating Support Fund to "provide fast, flexible and needs-based support for households facing unaffordable energy costs".

They also ask for "leadership to bring together the energy suppliers, advice and debt support sectors to address the energy debt mountain ahead of further price rises".

The groups say there should be additional "targeted support for rural and island households, who need targeted short-term relief due to higher rates of fuel poverty, reliance on alternative fuel sources and increased costs to insulate their households".

The collective also calls for "sustained action" to "provide long-term solutions" including "policy certainty to unlock investment".

"As delays to policy have stalled home energy improvements, the Scottish Government must provide certainty to give social landlords and owner-occupiers the confidence to invest, providing permanent bill relief to households," the groups say.

Josiah Lockhart, chief executive of Changeworks, said: "People across Scotland urgently need support. Energy debt is at record levels, and more people than ever will fall further into fuel poverty over the coming months unless the Government takes urgent action.

"While we welcome the extension of the Scottish Emergency Heating Oil Scheme and the UK Government's announcement last week to cut VAT on electricity, reform energy levies and remove gas standing charges, this support is not going to provide sufficient relief to the thousands that need it now. That's why we're collectively calling for further, faster action."

Sean Duffy, chief executive of the Wise Group, said: "Fuel poverty is distinct from poverty, but it cannot be tackled in isolation from it.

"Our mentors see energy debt alongside poor health, financial pressures and housing difficulties every day. Emergency funding is essential, but Scotland cannot keep paying to manage the same crisis.

"Energy prices may be largely outside the Scottish Government's control, but how we connect housing, health, social security and energy support is not. We need to measure success by whether households become more stable and stay out of crisis, not simply how much support we deliver."

Energy Minister Stephen Gethins said: “We agree with the organisations that have signed this letter – high energy bills are one of the most pressing issues facing households. While the key levers over energy prices remain reserved to Westminster, Scotland has the strongest package of winter heating support in the UK and we continue to provide help through other measures such as the Scottish Emergency Heating Oil Scheme and Scottish Welfare Fund. Anyone struggling with energy bills should contact Advice Direct Scotland for free advice.

“However, nobody should be struggling to afford to heat their home in an energy-rich country like Scotland. While the Scottish Government will work with local government to ensure that households in Scotland will benefit from the one-off £100 payment for energy bill support, it is clear that the measures announced in the UK Budget fall far short of the action required. The UK Government previously pledged to cut energy bills by £300. With bills now around £300 higher and rising, households are entitled to ask when it will finally deliver on this promise.”
