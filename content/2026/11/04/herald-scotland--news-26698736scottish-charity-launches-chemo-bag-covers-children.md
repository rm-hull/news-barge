A new Scottish charity is helping take some of the trauma out of children's journeys through cancer treatment by using art and imagination.

The recently launched Brave Positive Strong Foundation has introduced decorative covers for chemo bags used to deliver medicine in hospitals.

Founded by Emma MacKillop after supporting her daughter Darcie through cancer treatment, alongside her friend Catriona Graham, a former clinical nurse specialist in oncology and research, the Brave Positive Strong Foundation launched in June 2026.

The foundation's name was inspired by Darcie's mantra, "Brave, Positive, Strong", which was embraced by her family during her treatment at the Royal Hospital for Children in Glasgow following her diagnosis of osteosarcoma (bone cancer) in 2024 at the age of 10.

At the heart of the foundation is the Brave Bags initiative, inspired by Darcie's own experience of treatment.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21670048.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darcie MacKillop at Design HOPES working on the Brave Bag design *(Image: Brave Positive Strong)*

The everyday chemotherapy bag became something Darcie dreaded, a plain, clinical pouch that came to represent the fear and difficulty associated with each hospital appointment.

Each Brave Bag is designed to fit over a child's chemotherapy pouch, transforming a clinical piece of medical equipment into something warmer, more colourful and child-friendly.

The foundation raised £20,000 following the launch of its Brave Chemo Bags at its inaugural ladies' lunch in Glasgow.

Emma MacKillop said: "We can't take away the fear of a cancer diagnosis, but we can help make the hardest days a little brighter.

"For a child who is already frightened, an ordinary piece of medical equipment can carry an oversized weight. Brave Bags are about giving children comfort and courage, while reminding families that they are not alone."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21670047.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Brave Positive Strong Foundation, Brave Bags *(Image: Brave Positive Strong)*

The foundation is working with Professor Paul Rodgers, Professor of Design at the University of Strathclyde, Lucy Welsh and Sophia Murden of Heriot-Watt University, who are part of the Design HOPES1 research project, to develop a prototype for the Brave Bags.

Professor Rodgers leads the design team behind the Design HOPES sustainable, reusable and biodegradable operating theatre caps for NHS Scotland.

Darcie has helped shape the prototype through a creative design session with the Design HOPES research project, and four other children diagnosed with childhood cancer will also have the opportunity to work on a range of bag designs.

Fifty bags are planned for hospital trials, with infection control requirements carefully considered.

Ms MacKillop added: "We are incredibly grateful to Professor Paul Rodgers and the Design HOPES team for their expertise and support in turning our vision for the Brave Bags into reality.

"A huge thank you also goes to everyone who attended our inaugural ladies' lunch, all our wonderful donors and sponsors, and the businesses and individuals who so generously contributed towards the day. Raising £20,000 is an incredible achievement, and we simply couldn't have done it alone."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21670049.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Left to right: Brave Positive Strong Foundation volunteers Kirsty Macleod-Campinho and Mhairi Herron with Julie Wilson Nimmo *(Image: Brave Positive Strong)*

Professor Paul Rodgers, of Design HOPES, said: "Brave Bags bring thoughtful design and real-life experiences together to help make a real difference. Darcie's involvement has helped ensure the project reflects what matters to the children who will use them. The next step is to trial the bags in hospitals and assess their impact, which is incredibly exciting."

Speaking at the lunch, host Julie Wilson Nimmo said: "It was an absolute joy to host the first Brave Positive Strong ladies' lunch. There was so much laughter, generosity and love in the room, and it was incredibly special to see so many people come together for such a meaningful cause."
