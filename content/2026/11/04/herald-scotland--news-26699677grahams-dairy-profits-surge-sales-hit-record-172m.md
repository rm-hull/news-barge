Graham’s The Family Dairy has staged a sharp recovery in profitability as booming demand for high-protein products and rapid growth outside Scotland helped push sales to a record £172 million.

However, managing director Robert Graham has warned that renewed increases in energy, fuel and packaging costs are likely to feed through into higher food prices, with the company facing several million pounds of additional annual costs.

The Stirlingshire-based dairy reported pre-tax profits of £4.5m for its latest financial year as turnover climbed 11%, or more than £17m, to £172m. That performance was a significant improvement on the previous year, when turnover was £154.9m and pre-tax profits fell to £671,550.

Mr Graham said the latest result was encouraging but stressed that margins remained tight for a company which has invested around £40m in the business over the past decade.

“It still probably equates to about two and a half pence in the pound,” he said. “It’s a good improvement in margins, but really, for the amount of money we’re investing, we know we need to be generating more sustainable returns, and that’s certainly getting us ahead to take us in the right direction.”

The growth has been driven increasingly by Graham’s own branded products, sales of which Mr Graham said had risen by around 60%.

He described that as a “monstrously big number”, with sales of cottage cheese, protein pouches and premium Jersey milk all growing by roughly 40%. While inflation contributed to the overall rise in revenue, Mr Graham said it was “primarily driven by an increase in product sales volume”.

The popularity of protein-rich foods has become particularly important to Graham’s, with the company investing in additional production capacity for cottage cheese, protein and Skyr pouches.

Capital expenditure totalled £4.1m during the year, much of it directed towards expanding cottage cheese and protein manufacturing facilities.

Mr Graham said cottage cheese had been a “star performer”, while demand for other high-protein products had also been strong. He said the popularity of GLP-1 weight-loss medicines may be contributing to the trend, although consumer demand for protein was already well established.

The family-owned business is also becoming increasingly reliant on markets beyond its heartland. Mr Graham said more than half of turnover now comes from outside Scotland, with sales elsewhere currently running at more than £100m on an annualised basis.

England is by far the company's largest market outside Scotland, while Ireland is also significant. Graham’s now exports to around 18 countries, including markets in the Middle East and Hong Kong.

The company said its products are now bought by one in five English households, with household penetration in England increasing by 63.2% in the year to July, according to figures from Kantar Worldpanel UK.

However, Mr Graham warned that the outlook has become considerably more challenging because of rising costs. While sales are continuing to grow strongly in the current financial year, he said increases in diesel, haulage, energy and packaging costs could add “several million” pounds annually to Graham’s cost base.

The company would attempt to absorb and mitigate some of those increases, but Mr Graham indicated that not all could be swallowed.

Asked whether shoppers should therefore expect higher prices, he said: “I think broadly, from our food industry, there will be some inflation. There has to be, given the impacts on these, but we’ll obviously do our best to mitigate as well.”

He added: “I think there’s no doubt from a goods perspective. But again, we’re not in charge of retail price.”

The same pressures are being felt by the nearly 100 Scottish farms supplying Graham’s, with feed, fertiliser and red diesel costs all rising.

Mr Graham said the dairy had sought to protect the prices paid to farmers, adding: “They’re also facing those additional costs.”
