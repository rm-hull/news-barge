Reform UK's first budget for Bradford Council has moved to the next step after the Executive sent proposals to make millions of pounds of savings out to public consultation.

But one opposition councillor called it the “smallest-ever budget to go out to consultation.”

The final 2026/27 budget for the council will not be decided until February, but on Tuesday the Executive discussed proposals that will make around £50 million in savings.

These will include “enhancing income” from the authority’s sports and leisure services and cutting £500,000 from its localities team.

Of these savings just £3.58m will go out to consultation, with the remaining £46.4m “not requiring consultation.”

## 'Honest discussions rather than short term fixes'

At Tuesday’s Executive meeting Bradford Council Leader Stephen Place said: “The financial challenges facing Bradford are significant.

"We need honest discussions rather than short term fixes.

“Fiscal prudence is the name of the game.

“£50 million savings are needed to balance the books and return the council to a safe financial situation.”

Cllr Place criticised the previous Labour administration for allowing Bradford Council to fall into financial difficulty that ultimately required financial support from Government and £391 million in borrowing to balance the books.

He said further details of the budget, including the confirmation of Council Tax levels, will be announced in the coming weeks.

## Why opposition is 'quite nervous'

Councillor Rebecca Poulsen (Cons, Worth Valley) pointed out that £18.82 million of savings would come from the Children’s and Families Trust.

Over £16 million of this was from reduced spend on costly external placements for children in care – which can coast over £300,000 per year for each child in care.

Cllr Poulsen said: “If we can deliver (these savings), it’s fantastic.

"But I’m quite nervous about this – I haven’t seen any business case on this proposal.

"There is a history of council budgets relying on a reduction in external placements, only for the numbers to be completely wrong.”

Charlotte Ramsden, Chief Executive of the Bradford Children’s and Families Trust, said external residential placements were “incredibly difficult to forecast” but was confident the spend in this acre could be reduced in the coming year.

## What the other parties said

Councillor Imran Khan (Lab, Bowling and Barkerend) said the proposals were the “smallest budget I’ve ever seen go out to consultation.”

He added: “Most decisions have already been made.”

![Bradford city centre](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21487823.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Newsquest)*

Cllr Khan whether the proposed cuts, including £500,000 in the localities team, would lead to job losses.

Stephen Mair, Interim Strategic Director of Corporate Resources, said: “We can’t give any absolute guarantees, but we will do everything we can to try and minimise this.”

Councillor Matt Edwards (Green, Holme Wood and Bierley) questioned the section of the budget that suggests the council will raise fees for certain services.

He said: “We shouldn’t be balancing the budget on the back of the most vulnerable in the district.”

Cllr Khan defended the previous Labour administration he was a part of, addressing the Reform Executive and saying: “It was the Tories that got us into this position, supported by your MPs who are mostly former Tories.”

He said the Tory Government had reduced Bradford Council’s budget by £350 million a year due to austerity policies.

Cllr Ian Eglin (Reform UK, Baildon), Corporate Resources Portfolio Holder, said: “Yes there was a long period where the council was under funded, but the issues were due to Labour not watching what was going on. It shouldn’t have got to this situation.”

Cllr Khan replied: “Labour put a plan in place (to achieve financial stability) – you’re following that plan.

"Bradford is coming out of financial support two years early – that didn’t happen in the last six months.”

Cllr Eglin said: “It shouldn’t have got to crisis point, it should have been managed better.”
