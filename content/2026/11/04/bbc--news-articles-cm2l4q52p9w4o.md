!["King says slavery was 'monstrous' – but stops short of offering apology," reads the headline on the front page of the i Paper.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/fa9d/live/f23ea570-d7cf-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The i Paper leads with King Charles III's comments on slavery during his address at the Commonwealth leaders summit in Antigua and Barbuda. "King says slavery was 'monstrous'," reads the paper's headline, although he "stops short of apologising". Instead, it writes the King praised the "Royal Navy's role in ending the transatlantic slave trade" at the meeting.

!["Britain had leading role in ending the slave trade, says Charles," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mail.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ae6f/live/625ab140-d7d6-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

King Charles says "Britain had leading role in ending the slave trade", is the Daily Mail's summary of the Monarch's speech - which it judges was "punchier-than-expected".

!["Slavery was evil but we helped end it, says King," reads the headline on the front page of the Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/55a3/live/72179f80-d7d6-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

The Times continues "slavery was evil but we helped end it", leading with King Charles' expressing his "most profound sorrow" over the transatlantic slave trade. It adds he said "the shameful chapter of history" filled his "heart with horror". It embeds a photo of Queen Camilla and the prime minister's wife Marie-France van Heel visiting a school in Antigua, both of whom are wearing different shades of blue.

!["Andy's plan to say sorry for slave trade will cost us," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Express.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/f1b4/live/f6d0fed0-d7cf-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

Prime Minister Andy Burnham's "plan to say sorry for slave trade will cost us", argues the Daily Express, quoting critics who believe the prime minister's £40m aid offer is a "lever to wedge open the door to ask for cash", which it says could lead to "reparations by stealth". Burnham has ruled out financial reparations.

!["Celeb loses gag bid," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Star.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/019c/live/01c93910-d7d0-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The Daily Star headlines on a "well-known" celebrity who has "lost his bid to stop sex abuse allegations from being reported". The High Court "refused" the man's injunction, according to the paper.

!["Sex claim star loses bid to hide name," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Mirror.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/4508/live/fd7fa880-d7cf-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

"Celebrity court battle" continues the Daily Mirror, as the High Court judge ruled "allegations from large number of women can be reported". The paper explains the man, who remains anonymous, faces claims that "he tried engage in 'exploitative sexual activities'".

!["Defeat in High Court: 'Sex pest' loses gag bid," reads the headline on the front page of the Sun.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ad77/live/6c496ac0-d7d6-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

The Sun redacts the name of the alleged "sex pest" on its front page using thick red marker graphic, writing that despite the gag order "defeat in the High Court" the paper "still can't name him" because of a "pending possible appeal".

!["PM to reveal EU youth visa pact and trade deals worth billions," reads the headline on the front page of the Guardian.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/256a/live/8f748f70-d7d6-11f1-b441-2b33b063138f.jpg)

The Guardian says Andy Burnham plans to reveal an "EU youth visa pact and trade deals worth billions", as he prepares to meet European leaders at a summit in Brussels on 20 November. Discussions will cover "everything from youth visas to pet passports" as the prime minister sketches out "his vision of a much closer relationship" with the European Union. In a separate piece, the paper covers the "flights hit by' malicious hoax' bomb threats," writing that "seven passenger flights across Europe were searched or diverted" as a result.

!["Calls to scrap 'chilling' trans law," reads the headline on the front page of the Daily Telegraph.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9748/live/65c7eb90-d7d6-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

The Daily Telegraph tops with the paper's campaign to scrap what it terms a "chilling trans law", suggesting that the Draft Conversation Practices Bill "risks criminalising parents who question their children are trans".

!["Even our spooks are spooked," reads the headline on the front page of the Metro.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/ee91/live/0cc8ed60-d7d0-11f1-93e1-3b657b665bcf.jpg)

"Even our spooks are spooked" splashes the Metro, quoting Britain's "top cyber-crime fighter" Richard Horne, head of the National Cyber Security Centre, responding to the "fridge cyber attackers". It quotes Horne saying Britain has "done tech on the cheap" for decades, carrying his warnings that "hostile states such as Russia and China will 'routinely use AI' for crime by 2028".

!["Nvidia demands cut in clients' China dealings to protect American Allead," reads the headline on the front page of the Financial Times.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/3759/live/18448870-d7d0-11f1-a701-fddedf244c16.jpg)

Chipmaker Nvidia is demanding cut in clients' China dealings to protect America's "AI lead," the Financial Times reports. Citing anonymous sources "with knowledge of the matter", the paper says the instructions to the data centre partners "have been communicated verbally and conveyed by Nvidia's sales teams rather than spelt out in documents". Elsewhere in the US, the papers says President Donald "Trump awaits voters' verdict" in the midterm elections, writing that Republicans could risk "defeats in both chambers of Congress".

King Charles III's speech describing slavery as "monstrous" makes the front of the i Paper, external, but the paper points out that he stopped short of offering an apology to Commonwealth leaders. The Daily Mail, external calls the address "punchier-than-expected" with the monarch insisting "we cannot change the past". "Pointedly", the Mail says, the King "reminded them that the Royal Navy led the way in ending the transatlantic slave trade".

The Daily Telegraph, external leads on its campaign to scrap what's described as a "chilling" trans law. It suggests the legislation risks criminalising parents who question whether their children are trans. Writing in the Telegraph, external, a former chair of the Equality and Human Rights Commission, Baroness Falkner, describes the law as an "unprecedented and unwarranted intrusion into family life". The government says the draft bill will "outlaw a specific form of abuse", while setting high bars that protect regular parenting.

Silhouettes of a still unnamed male celebrity feature on a number of front pages after he failed in a bid at the High Court to have his identity protected in relation to allegations of sexual misconduct. "Sex Pest Loses Gag Bid", is the Sun's headline, external, with a pen scribbled over where the name would be. The paper says the star is accused of inappropriate and exploitative sexual activities with multiple young women. The Daily Star, external reports that the celeb's identity will stay "secret for now", pending any appeal.

The Financial Times, external says that the Conservatives have turned to the former Prime Minister Lord Cameron, "to charm donors" and build on Kemi Badenoch's revival. He is described as the "star attraction" for a Cities of London and Westminster Conservative association event next month where tickets will cost up to £345 a seat. "He's a big draw", is the view of one ex-Tory MP.

And finally, Kellogg's has announced that Rice Krispies will no longer be made with rice grains. The new formulation will instead be a mix of rice flour and corn flour. The change has left the Times, external to ask: "presumably they will continue to be crispy".
