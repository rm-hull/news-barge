A councillor has resigned from the Conservatives on Stockton Council and will continue as an independent, saying: “I must be allowed to plough my own furrow.”

Councillor John Coulson said he would “always be a Conservative”, but says he has had enough of political fighting and point-scoring.

The Conservatives accused him of “betraying Yarm voters and doing Labour’s bidding” and letting down colleagues and residents by having discussions with Labour councillors, while Cllr Coulson said “all I want is what the residents of Yarm want”, he would work with people of any political persuasion and had spoken to the council’s Labour leadership about plans for Yarm High Street.

He said in a statement: “In 2023 I was honoured to be elected as a Conservative councillor, covering Yarm, working alongside Andrew Sherris and Dan Fagan. I have sat and worked on numerous vital health committees, Cleveland police and crime commissioner panel, TEWV [Tees Esk and Wear Valleys NHS Foundation Trust], TVCA [Tees Valley Combined Authority], sub-licensing and many, many more such important and vital roles.

“Eons ago you were allowed to stand as independent Conservative, until that was abolished. I feel independent is my role and a year ago I made my feelings known but was persuaded and minded to stay with the party.

“Since then I have always followed my conscience and beliefs and will always do so as I was elected by the people of Yarm and to serve them.

At a recent meeting I had to follow my conscience and beliefs, and I have now decided that the time is right for me to leave the Conservative Party and follow my conscience and serve the remaining term as a Yarm independent councillor.

“I am a Conservative and will always be a Conservative. This will not alter anything, as regards Yarm and its people and how I have worked alongside Andrew, nothing will change.

“I will remain loyal to [Tees Valley Mayor] Ben Houchen, [Stockton West Conservative MP] Matt Vickers and Andrew Sherris, but must be allowed to plough my own farrow.”

Conservative group leader Cllr Tony Riordan said: “Cllr Coulson’s departure does not come as a surprise. His conduct has undermined the trust of his colleagues and, in our view, betrayed the residents of Yarm who elected him as a Conservative.

“For some time, there have been serious concerns about his unwillingness to work constructively with colleagues.

"Those concerns deepened following the discovery of discussions and attempted arrangements with members of the Labour administration, conducted behind our backs and without the knowledge or agreement of the Conservative group.

“Residents are entitled to ask whose interests he was serving. They voted for a Conservative councillor to stand up for Yarm and hold Labour to account. Instead, we believe they have been let down.

“At a time when we needed a united effort to protect the funding secured for Yarm and ensure it was spent on residents’ priorities, Councillor Coulson chose to undermine that approach.

"Rather than work alongside his Conservative colleagues, he pursued his own agenda and gave Labour a helping hand.

“In our view, Labour have played him at every turn… The result has been to serve Labour’s interests while weakening the collective effort to secure the best outcome for Yarm.

“His resignation does not wipe the slate clean,” he added. “Residents deserve cooperation, sound judgment and a council focused on getting things done.

“Calling himself an independent does not answer the questions about his dealings with Labour… Councillor Coulson has let down his colleagues and the residents who placed their trust in him.

“The Conservative group will continue to stand up for Yarm, fight for the investment the town deserves and hold Labour to account. That is the job residents elected us to do, and we will get on with it.”

Cllr Coulson said the accusations against him were false and “rubbish”, saying he had spoken to the council’s leader and deputy leader and senior officers about Yarm High Street, a regeneration scheme which was halted and revised following public opposition. Cllr Coulson spoke during council meetings about the plans.

He said: “I told everybody. It was never a secret. I’m not a sneak. I’ll hold my hands up.

“I’ve worked with them on the redevelopment of Yarm High Street and the parking. Isn’t that what you do in politics? No matter what political persuasion they are, it’s to help the people of Yarm get what they want.

“I still think if we’re all elected by the people, to do it for the people, why would you spend countless hours nit-picking, fighting and point-scoring? It’s just a waste of time.

“I just don’t see the logic in it. Wasn’t it Winston Churchill who said it’s better to jaw-jaw than war-war?”

He said he felt politics sometimes became too personal: “I agree you must hold each other to account, of course, that’s politics. But when it gets personal, I’m not into that. We’re all human beings, we’ve all got feelings.

“I just thought, I can’t keep doing this. I’ve been treading on eggshells for over a year.”

But he said his political allegiance remained. “I could never be Labour. I’ll always be a Conservative,” he added, saying he had subbed for other Conservative councillors in council meetings.

He said he expected new Yarm regeneration plans to be announced soon, including fishing pontoons, bins, motorbike parking and repairs to cracked paving, “everything the public wanted”.
