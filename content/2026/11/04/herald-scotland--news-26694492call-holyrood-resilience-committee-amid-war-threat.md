A former SNP MP has called for Holyrood to set up resilience committee as work gets underway by the UK and Scottish Government on developing a new 'War Book'.

Stewart McDonald, the SNP's former defence spokesman, who now serves as senior adviser on defence and security at the Dublin-based public affairs firm Fenton Fitzwilliam, wants the new cross-party body to be established to allow MSPs to scrutinise the Scottish Government's preparations in the event of an attack by Russia or another hostile state.

The Herald revealed last month that the Scottish Government is to develop a new 'War Book' with the UK after warnings from military leaders over the growing threat of a large-scale conflict by 2030.

It is separate to military plans for how the armed forces would respond to any attack, but reflects the government’s "whole-of-society" approach to defence and national security.

It will be the first time the Scottish Government is involved in the drafting of the War Book with work being led by the Cabinet Office in London.

“Defence is reserved and so, the habit runs, it is a matter solely for the UK Parliament," said Mr McDonald.

"The Scottish Parliament and much of Scottish public life have been content to leave it there, and it's a habit that has existed across the entirety of the devolved era.

"Yes, defence and much of national security is reserved, but that doesn't mean it should be of no concern to parliamentarians in Edinburgh.

"Modern day threats are no respecters of neat constitutional boundaries. A cable cut, a gas terminal disrupted, a port taken offline or a health board's systems brought down by a foreign state would be felt first in devolved services, and it is Scottish ministers who would answer for them, and the plans for coping with the consequences would rightly involve both governments."

![Former SNP MP Stewart McDonald](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21562485.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Former SNP MP Stewart McDonald *(Image: Gordon Terris)*

He added: "So my argument is simply this: the old 20th century mindset on security needs to be updated for 21st century realities.

"At Holyrood as well as in Westminster, a more grown-up conversation between governments and, more importantly, with the public needs to be had about the choices facing us today if we are to keep our people safe.

He continued: “I think it is time the Scottish Parliament had a standalone committee on national resilience and preparedness. The Scottish Government does a great deal of work on resilience that the Parliament barely considers, and the scrutiny of it is spread thinly among committees whose agendas are taken up with other important work.

"A dedicated committee would give that work the democratic scrutiny it deserves, build up a body of members who know the subject, and give the people in this room a route to put evidence in front of politicians who are obliged to take it seriously.”

The Scottish Government's participation in the development of the new War Book was revealed by a senior civil servant at a conference in central Edinburgh last month on Scotland’s role in UK National Security and Resilience hosted by The D Group consultancy and the British Foreign Policy Group (BFPG) think tank.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said last month Britain must be ready for war in 1,000 days and that the UK must "be ready with our allies to fight and win".

The message came days after the UK Government launched its "Be Ready" campaign, advising households to stockpile non-perishable food, bottles of water, blankets and radios in the event of a cyberattack or severe weather incident that could shut down critical national infrastructure.

Noting the developments in the UK, Russian President Vladimir Putin told journalists in Moscow last week: "Many people keep talking about preparing for war by 2030. Is that right or wrong?".

"First of all, we are ready, but we don't want any war with Europe. As for them, if anyone wants it, let them prepare. But they have not achieved their goal of inflicting a strategic defeat on Russia. It's unlikely that this will be possible by 2030. And if things go too far, there will be no winners at all. Let no one forget that."

The Ministry of Defence has warned that there has been a 30% rise in Russian naval activity around UK waters over the past two years with several incidents coming to the public attention.

Defence experts fear in the event of an attack on the UK Russia may attempt to sabotage critical subsea infrastructure - which carry critical communications lines - with Scottish seen as particularly vulnerable because of its high number of island communities.

The new War Book is set to be completed by the end of the year and is expected to set out the measures that would come into force across the UK in the event of conflict.

The concept of a War Book was first developed early last century and sets out secret plans for how the country would respond to an attack by a foreign power.

During the Cold War, these plans focused on preparing for a nuclear attack and specified what actions ministers, officials and local authorities should take during a period of war. The end of the Cold War, and the diminishing threat of invasion or attack then, meant the traditional War Books were shelved.

A spokeswoman for the Scottish Parliament said: "Any Member can request the establishment of a committee. This would be considered by the Parliamentary Bureau.

“If agreed, the Bureau would recommend the establishment of the committee, including its membership, remit and duration. The Bureau’s motion would then need to be agreed by a vote of Parliament.”
