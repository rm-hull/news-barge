West Yorkshire Police says it is determined to “root out wrongdoing” after 119 of its officers were barred over the last six years.

The force said it has "tough measures in place for anyone found to not be adhering to the standards the public expect".

119 of its officers have been barred since April 2020.

## What police said

A West Yorkshire Police spokesperson said: "We are determined to root out wrongdoing in West Yorkshire Police and we have tough measures in place for anyone found to not be adhering to the standards the public expect from their police force.

"Since April 2020, 119 West Yorkshire Police officers have been placed on the College of Policing Police Barred List.

"These numbers show that we treat reports of misconduct seriously and that when the evidence allows, we take our officers and staff to gross misconduct hearings to ensure they are dismissed from policing and placed on the barred list, to prevent them from working in policing again.

"When officers and staff are the subject of misconduct proceedings, we are open and transparent in publishing the details.

"Every single member of the organisation must complete regular mandatory training relating to the standards of professional behaviour and the Code of Ethics."

## Recent examples

Recent cases show the range of misconduct allegations within the force.

In April 2026, Sergeant Jasbinder Rayatt was dismissed and placed on the barred list after being found guilty of gross misconduct during a hearing in March.

The panel found that Sgt Rayatt insulted colleagues several times while on duty in June 2023, calling one a "prostitute" and others "racist white b\*\*\* ards and c\*\* ts".

The panel ruled that her behaviour was inappropriate, derogatory, discriminatory and unprofessional.

In another case, former PC Mark Spence was placed on the barred list following a September hearing that found he had abused his position as a driver trainer during a police driving course in summer 2025.

During the two-week course, PC Spence made sexually explicit remarks to a trainee officer and touched her arm and waist.

He resigned before the hearing, but the panel said he would have been dismissed without notice had he still been serving.

## 'Policing is built on trust'

West Yorkshire recorded three barred officers in 2020/21, 11 in 2021/22, 10 in 2022/23, 30 in 2023/24, 31 in 2024/25 and 29 in 2025/26.

The total has risen steadily nationally, from 594 barred officers in 2023/24 to 735 in 2024/25.

The College of Policing said 3,591 officers had been barred since 2017.

Assistant Chief Constable Arman Mathieson, director of operational standards at the College of Policing, said: "Policing is built on trust and these figures show we will not hesitate to act when that trust is broken.

He added: "We all deserve a service that roots out those who fall short.

"Where standards are breached, we will find out and we will act."

## Why were they dismissed?

The College of Policing report listed the reasons officers nationally were dismissed and barred, with some cases involving more than one breach.

The report recorded 142 dishonesty cases, 110 involving discriminatory behaviour and 101 involving unlawful access or disclosure of information.

It also recorded 87 cases of sexual offences or misconduct, including six involving child sexual abuse and five involving rape.

Another 46 cases involved abusing a position for sexual purposes.

Of those barred, 641 were police constables, 84 sergeants, 18 inspectors, five chief inspectors, five superintendents and four chief superintendents. 334 were serving officers, 397 had resigned and 26 had retired.

The Metropolitan Police had the highest number of barred officers, with 195, followed by Greater Manchester Police with 46 and West Midlands Police with 44.
