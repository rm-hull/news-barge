**This week's MP column is written by Bradford East MP Imran Hussain**

This Budget contains important and welcome support for working people, families and local businesses across Bradford.

I campaigned for the banks making bumper profits to make a greater contribution, so I welcome the temporary increase in the Bank Corporation Tax Surcharge.

At a time when families continue to struggle with the cost of living, it is right that those with the broadest shoulders pay their fair share.

The proceeds will help fund immediate support with energy bills.

The VAT cut on domestic electricity will be extended until July 2027, while up to 20 million households in council tax bands A to D will receive a £100 energy payment from January.

The commitment to abolish gas standing charges is also welcome.

Households should not face a substantial fixed charge regardless of how much gas they use.

Young workers in Bradford will benefit from a 3.9% increase in the minimum wage, while the decision to raise the Small Business Rates Relief threshold from £12,000 to £18,000 will take around 70,000 retail, hospitality and leisure businesses out of business rates altogether.

That could provide valuable support to independent shops, cafés, restaurants and other businesses across Bradford at a time when our high streets face enormous pressures.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21063324.jpg?type=mds-article-620)

These measures will provide breathing space.

But a Budget must also be judged by whether it delivers long-term change and unlocks the potential of communities that have been overlooked for far too long.

For Bradford, that means investment and delivery.

Bradford is one of Britain’s youngest and most diverse cities.

We have enormous strengths in manufacturing, culture, education and enterprise, but that potential continues to be held back by poor connectivity and decades of underinvestment.

This is not simply an argument about fairness, although fairness matters.

It is also an economic argument.

Bradford Council’s growth analysis suggests that if Bradford matched national rates for productivity, enterprise and employment, our city could generate more than £4.5 billion in additional economic output every year.

Investing in Bradford is therefore an investment in West Yorkshire, the North and the national economy.

I warmly welcomed the creation of No10 North and its promise to deliver good growth in every postcode.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/20829590.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Newsquest)*

Giving mayors and local leaders a direct route into government, bringing departments together and removing barriers to major projects is an important step.

But No10 North must now be judged by what it delivers.

I have written to the First Secretary of State calling for Bradford to become one of its first named priorities.

I have also asked for a senior government lead to coordinate Bradford’s investment programme and prevent important decisions from becoming trapped between Whitehall departments, the combined authority and local partners.

Bradford does not need another layer of administration or another talking shop.

It needs a mechanism capable of turning our priorities into funded projects and measurable results.

The Government’s commitment to build a new city-centre railway station at Bradford Interchange is a major milestone after years of campaigning.

But the station’s design will determine whether it genuinely transforms Bradford’s economy.

It must be a through station, not another terminus, connecting Bradford properly with Leeds, Huddersfield, Manchester and the wider northern economy.

We now need firm timetables for the station, Northern Powerhouse Rail, mass transit and the other transport improvements our city has been promised.

A through railway station brings people and investment into the city.

Skills investment connects young people with new jobs.

Modern public facilities improve lives and strengthen the foundations upon which economic growth depends.

The Budget provides welcome immediate help.

No10 North and the new station demonstrate that Bradford is finally receiving greater recognition.

The next test is delivery.

If the Government is serious about achieving good growth in every postcode, Bradford must be among the first places where that promise produces real results.

Bradford has waited long enough.

It must not once again be placed at the back of the queue.
