Before I incur the wrath of David Linden I should make clear that the new SNP MSP for Baillieston and Shettleston is being spoken about in a positive way.

At least, that's from the reporter's point of view.

Last Friday when the media pack assembled in a canteen at BAE Systems in Scotstoun, Glasgow, to await our chance to quiz the Chancellor John Healey and Defence Secretary Wes Streeting, as it turned out all too briefly, the talk turned to the shortlist for The Herald's Scottish Politician of the Year Awards.

Eyebrows were raised about some of the nominations.

But there was a general consensus that Mr Linden fully deserved his place on the shortlist for Committee MSP of the Year.

"He's not afraid to ask tough questions of the government," one fellow scribe noted.

Former Labour MSP Neil Findlay was of that opinion this week.

He was clearly impressed when he watched Mr Linden pressing First Minister John Swinney on Tuesday about why MSPs were not told by the government about a riot that happened at HMP and YOI Polmont on September 26 - and were instead left to find out about it from a report on the BBC last Thursday.

Reposting a video clip of Mr Linden quizzing the FM, Mr Findlay wrote on X: "This is a breath of fresh air - a committee chair and SNP backbencher holding the First Minister to account - others should watch this and learn - well done David Linden."

It is of course the role of Holyrood committees to examine the work of government and to hold ministers to account for their actions.

But in recent years, questions have been asked if committees, in particular ones chaired by SNP backbenchers, do challenge the government fully - or whether they give ministers an easy ride.

The concern was raised memorably by former SNP health secretary Alex Neil, who stood down from Holyrood at the 2021 election.

Mr Neil argued back in 2023 that too many backbenchers were "nodding donkeys" and singled out his own party for particular criticism.

He said party leaders “just want yes people, they want nodding donkeys — people who will do as they’re telt”.

Well, Mr Linden, a former SNP MP, is certainly no "nodding donkey".

After pressing the FM on Tuesday, Mr Linden was not going to let the matter go away.

At the criminal justice committee on Wednesday he rebuked justice secretary Neil Gray for his government not informing MSPs about the Polmont riot.

"This committee has oversight of your portfolio, and in particular prisons policy," Mr Linden reminded Mr Gray, in a way few SNP MSPs have done.

"The idea that information like that would be concealed or withheld, is, I think, a gross discourtesy to this Parliament. And you and I both served as members of the Westminster Parliament, and rightly have gone through Tory government ministers for not providing that information to Parliament."

But Mr Gray's scolding was not the first time Mr Linden has shown himself to be a highly effective convener of the important Holyrood committee.

On September 2, as deputy convener, he pressed Mr Gray on youth crime.

"I know that I will not be the only member of this committee who is increasingly getting complaints from constituents—in my case, plenty from the Garrowhill area—who see young men dressed head to toe in black, sometimes wearing a face covering, zooming around on souped-up e-bikes and e-scooters," he said.

"That activity is highly dangerous and is sometimes connected with drugs and organised crime, as has been demonstrated in the recent operation armour pilot.

"Constituents in Glasgow and, indeed, further afield want to know what the Scottish Government is doing to crack down on the antisocial behaviour and criminality associated with e-bikes and e-scooters. When are we going to get a grip on the issue?"

A month later, on October 7, as convener, he quizzed victims and community safety minister Kirsten Oswald on why victims were having to wait up to four years from when they reported a crime to the police until the matter was resolved in court.

"Those delays to justice are deeply troubling for victims, so what further action will you, as victims minister, seek to speed that up?" he asked.

Later during the same committee meeting he pressed Mr Gray about what action was being taken to stop 12-year-old boys riding e-scooters in the wrong direction on a slip road of the M8 in Glasgow.

Mr Linden's pressing of ministers from his own party suggests he is an MSP determined to tackle the government on areas he believes it is under performing.

But it also reveals him to be someone in touch with the struggles of his constituents, many of whom I suspect have been victims of crime and experienced long waits for justice.
