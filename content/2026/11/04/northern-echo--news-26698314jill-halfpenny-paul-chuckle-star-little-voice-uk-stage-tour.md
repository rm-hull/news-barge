North East favourite Jill Halfpenny is heading home next year to take part in a major new production of a 'modern English classic.'

The Gateshead-born actress will play Mari Hoff in The Rise and Fall of Little Voice at Newcastle's Theatre Royal next February, starring alongside Francesca Mills and beloved actor Paul Chuckle.

The production will open at the National Theatre, in London, in December, and run there until January. It will then tour stages across the country, opening at the Theatre Royal in Newcastle on February 3, until February 6.

Francesca Mills is set to star as the titular Little Voice, with Paul Chuckle, best known as half of the iconic Chuckle Brothers, taking on the role of Mr Boo.

The show is a new production of Jim Cartwright’s play, based on Mark Herman's 1998 movie musical, and is directed by National Theatre deputy artistic director Robert Hastie.

It follows a young woman in Scarborough, Little Voice, whose mother Mari’s new partner, Ray, believes he can make money from her singing talent.

The North Yorkshire-based story, accompanied with the tagline "The hardest voice to find is your own," asks whether Little Voice will be pushed into public attention against her wishes, focusing on music, ambition and finding the confidence to be heard.

Music in the production includes songs linked to performers such as Judy Garland and Shirley Bassey.

Paul Chuckle, whose real name is Paul Elliott, said he "can't wait to get into rehearsals with Robert Hastie and this fantastic team."

He added: "To perform at the National Theatre and then head out on tour across England is a dream come true."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21669578.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Halfpenny said she was "so excited" to be featuring in the "beloved Northern play." *(Image: Rachell Smith)*

Olivier Award-winner Jill Halfpenny, who first found fame in the Newcastle-based drama Byker Grove, said she was "so excited" to be featuring in the "beloved Northern play."

Halfpenny said she is a long-time admirer of Cartwright's work, describing the chance to play Mari Hoff as "very special."

The Gateshead actress isn't the only North East connection on the cast, with Paul Chuckle being a former guest editor of the Northern Echo.

In 2013, he and Barry were guest editors of the regional newspaper while appearing in Aladdin at Darlington's Civic Theatre, joining news conferences and judging a Christmas cracker competition.
