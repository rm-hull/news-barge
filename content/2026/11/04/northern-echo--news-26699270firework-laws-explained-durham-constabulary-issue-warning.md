People planning to celebrate with fireworks at home this week are being urged to check the law and consider the impact on their neighbours, pets and local communities.

Bonfire Night will arrive tomorrow (November 5), with organised displays planned across the North East.

Events are listed from today (November 4) until next weekend and beyond, with Hindu festival Diwali additionally landing on November 8, meaning fireworks may be a permanent fixture across the North East for the coming weeks.

Residents may also be arranging parties at home, but many are unclear as to when they can set fireworks off, and which ones they can use.

Here's your guide to celebrating with fireworks, and how best to avoid a fine for improper use.

### What time can people set off fireworks?

Firstly, fireworks can normally be used between 7am and 11pm.

On Bonfire Night, that deadline is increased an hour, to midnight.

On New Year’s Eve, Diwali and Chinese New Year, residents can use fireworks for an additional two hours, extending the deadline to 1am.

Under UK law, fireworks must also not be set off or thrown in a street or another public place.

They may be used on private property, including a back garden, but must not exceed category 3 fireworks.

### What fireworks can people use?

Category 1 includes throwdown fireworks, such as firecrackers, bangers and party poppers.

Categories 2 and 3 are 'adult fireworks', which are sold in shops.

Category 4 fireworks are for professional displays only, and can only be bought, carried and used by licensed professionals.

It is an offence for a member of the public to possess a category 4 firework without a license.

People aged 18 or over can buy, carry or use categories one, two and three fireworks.

Those aged 16 and 17 can only buy, carry or use category one.

### What can people expect?

Durham Constabulary has confirmed that extra police patrols will be taking place across the county this week, in partnership with local councils and Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service, to crack down on illegal fireworks.

The force have reminded residents of the laws around firework usage, and that anyone found breaking firework laws could face a fine of up to £5,000 or a six-month prison sentence.

Durham Constabulary also urged residents to be mindful of their neighbours, properties and pets.

They added: "Loud noises can cause anxiety and stress, especially for dogs. Please keep pets indoors and never take them to a firework display."
