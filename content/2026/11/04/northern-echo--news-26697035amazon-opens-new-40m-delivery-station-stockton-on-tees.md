Amazon has opened its £40 million delivery station in Stockton-on-Tees – the first building in its global network to pursue the latest version of the Living Future Institute’s Zero Carbon Certification.

The facility has created 100 new jobs in a range of roles, offering a starting salary of more than £31,000 per year for full-time operations positions.

Alex Hallimond, senior operations manager at the Stockton-on-Tees delivery station, said: "The North East has a proud industrial heritage as one of the UK's great manufacturing regions.

"That tradition of building things continues with this new delivery station, setting a new sustainability blueprint that could shape how we design sites in the UK and around the world."

Amazon has invested more than £4.7 billion in the North East since 2010.

Nearly half of entry-level employees at the Stockton-on-Tees fulfilment centre previously came from unemployment or education.

Mr Hallimond, originally from Sunderland, said: "This area was previously really heavy industry, including the Transporter Bridge, chemical plants, and coal mining.

"A lot of that's gone now, but it's being replaced by something different.

"You've got automation, innovation, and now a sustainable version of what will hopefully become a worldwide design.

"If we replicate this build across the world, the benefit is huge."

The delivery station is part of Amazon’s wider commitment to invest £40 billion in the UK between 2025 and 2027.

The building and parking structure uses almost 1,500 tonnes of lower-carbon steel with high recycled content, produced using renewable electricity. More than 200 tonnes of mass timber beams complement the steel structure, reducing the carbon footprint of traditional building methods.

More than 4,400m³ of lower-carbon concrete, alongside lower-carbon wall panels and roofing materials, round out a construction approach that prioritises locally sourced supplies and shorter supply chains. By combining these materials and practices, total carbon emissions associated with the construction are at least 20% lower than Amazon's previous design standards.

The station's all-electric systems and rooftop solar panels are designed to work together to minimise the building's operational carbon footprint. The site is projected to use around half the energy of a typical logistics building. Water-saving plumbing fixtures are also set to reduce water consumption by approximately 20% compared to conventional designs.
