A former gambling addict who lost her job and nearly went to prison hopes a stunt TV show makes people think twice about addiction.

The Million Pound Shot aired on Channel 4 on Sunday night, following weeks of online speculation after viewers were left guessing about the supposedly big-money gameshow.

The first half was presented as an ordinary gameshow, with the contestants using their bank cards to gamble their own money, but was revealed as stunt half way through to educate viewers about “the techniques of the gambling industry”.

One former addict, who has been gambling-free for more than two decades, said she hoped it would make people think twice.

The woman, now in her 60s, said awareness was more important than ever as betting becomes increasingly difficult to escape in the digital age.

She began gambling during a "difficult period" following the death of a relative but quickly spiralled, with her addiction eventually costing her her job and leading to trouble with the police.

She said: "Eventually I had my job taken away from me because of gambling, I got in trouble with the police, nearly went to jail but got two years, all because of gambling.

"I just couldn't stop."

Her addiction continued for years until she eventually found support through Gamblers Anonymous.

She said: "I found their number in a phone book and went to the meeting where there were only two, maybe three of us there, but we just chatted and it slowly made everything better.

"I always found that doctors and counsellors couldn't help me the way this did as these people understood everything and there was no judgement or anything."

She hopes others struggling may join similar groups after hearing her story.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21661910.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Bradley Walsh on Million Pound Shot *(Image: CHANNEL 4)*

The TV show was billed as a one-off Channel 4 quiz event, in which three contestants compete for the chance to win £1 million.

She said: "Shows and messages that bring awareness to gambling are important.

"I have seen a massive increase myself over the years after lockdown, with people then having more access to it online and the internet.

"Everywhere you look there are adverts or gambling things such as mobile games, social media, TV and radio; you can't escape or avoid it, and it shouldn't be allowed because it can be really tough."

A Betting and Gaming Council Spokesperson said: "Channel 4’s Million Pound Shot presented a misleading portrayal of Britain’s regulated betting and gaming industry, failing to reflect the strict rules and protections that licensed operators must follow.
