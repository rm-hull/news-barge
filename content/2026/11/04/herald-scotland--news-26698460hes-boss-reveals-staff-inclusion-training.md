The new boss of Historic Environment Scotland has admitted that "building a more inclusive organisation and heritage sector is an ongoing journey".

Stephen Uphill, the interim chief executive of the Scottish Government's troubled heritage body, made the comments in a letter to Holyrood's culture committee.

His intervention comes after the public spending watchdog Audit Scotland last year raised concerns over "a toxic" workplace culture in the organisation.

Historic Environment Scotland (HES) employs around 1600 people on permanent contracts and is given £74 million a year to look after more than 300 of the country's most important historic buildings and sites including Edinburgh Castle, Linlithgow Palace and Skara Brae on Orkney.

It also follows reports, which emerged last year, of an accusation of racism against a senior member of staff.

Mr Uphill took over as interim chief executive from Katerina Brown in September just after culture secretary Mairi McAllan appointed Ronnie Hinds as interim chair of the organisation's board.

Mr Hinds' appointment was made after former chair Sir Mark Jones stepped down just nine months into what was expected to be a three-year tenure.

Both Mr Uphill and Mr Hinds appeared before the culture committee in September with convener Patrick Harvie asking for updates around progress being made on the challenge faced by HES.

"While we have made positive progress, we recognise that building a more inclusive organisation and heritage sector is an ongoing journey," Mr Uphill told Mr Harvie in his letter published on Tuesday.

"We remain committed to listening to diverse communities across Scotland, strengthening the capability of our workforce, embedding equality into decision-making, and ensuring that Scotland’s historic environment is accessible, relevant and welcoming to everyone."

He went onto say that HES's "Equality Audit" and "Inclusion Review" were commissioned as part of HES's commitment "to understanding colleague experiences and identifying opportunities for improvement".

He said: "We are continuing to work through the recommendations, focusing not only on practical improvements but also on creating a lasting cultural shift.

"This includes strengthening leadership accountability, supporting colleague networks, reviewing policies and practices through an inclusion lens, and embedding equality more consistently across the organisation."

He added that a statement had been made about leadership accountability which set out a commitment from "senior leaders to address the issues identified through the review and create meaningful change across the organisation".

Mr Uphill revealed that Diversity Scotland was carrying out "inclusion training" for all staff with 503 employees - 26% of the workforce - having completed the course by August this year, with a further 319 staff enrolled on sessions.

He added: "The Equality Audit identified several issues relating to Equality Forums and Networks, including inconsistent governance arrangements, limited accessibility for operational colleagues, unclear escalation routes, and challenges around sponsorship and accountability.

"In response, HES commissioned Diversity Scotland and the Staff Equality Network to support a Forums and Networks Governance Reset. This will conclude in November."

Mr Uphill was appointed to his role "with immediate effect" in early September following the resignation of Ms Brown.

In an unusual development civil service trade union Prospect welcomed her resignation saying her tenure was marked by a “significant downturn in staff morale and wellbeing”.

Her resignation came less than a year after she returned from work after months on sick leave and a period of suspension.

She was under investigation for almost a year after all seven HES directors of the organisation launched a grievance procedure against her.

That investigation ended in April, The Herald revealed, without disciplinary action being taken against her.

But two of the directors later appealed against the decision.

Shortly after the investigation into Ms Brown ended Sir Mark indicated he wished to leave the organisation after serving just months in the role.

He was persuaded to stay in post until after the Holyrood election in May, and stood down in early July.
