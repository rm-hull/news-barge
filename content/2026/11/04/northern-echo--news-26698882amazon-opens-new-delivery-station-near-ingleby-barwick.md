Amazon has opened its new £40m delivery station near Ingleby Barwick.

The tech giant has started shipping packages to customers’ doors from its new 10,800 sq m warehouse off Queen Elizabeth Way, beside the Preston Farm Industrial Estate.

The site, called DZM1, sorts and prepares packages for delivery to homes across Teesside, County Durham, and North Yorkshire - and is the brand’s first net-zero station in the UK.

The warehouse, which The Northern Echo exclusively revealed plans for last year, has created 100 jobs and seen a new roundabout installed on the busy road to make way for the project.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670241.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazon's new delivery station near Ingleby Barwick *(Image: UNP John Millard)*

Construction crews moved onto the site in early 2025, with the building being erected across the last year and a half while carriageway works were carried out on Queen Elizabeth Way.

The site has two entrances, with the main entrance and exit for staff vehicles and HGVs being from Lockheed Close within the industrial estate.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670245.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazon's new delivery station near Ingleby Barwick *(Image: UNP John Millard)*

The warehouse has been built using lower-carbon steel with high recycled content, and timber beams to create a more sustainable structure, lower-carbon concrete, wall panels, and roofing.

The station's all-electric systems and rooftop solar panels are designed to work together to minimise the building's operational carbon footprint.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670244.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazon's new delivery station near Ingleby Barwick *(Image: UNP John Millard)*

The centre is the firm’s first across the globe designed to achieve Living Future's Zero Carbon Certification in the UK and Europe.

It becomes eligible for certification in 2027 following a full year of operational data collection and third-party assessment.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21668039.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazon's new delivery station near Ingleby Barwick *(Image: UNP John Millard)*

Amazon estimates total carbon emissions will be at least 20 per cent lower than its previous design standards, and use around half the energy compared to a typical logistics building.

More than 1,400 sq m of rooftop solar panels will power daytime operations and the building will use an all-electric heating and cooling system.

The delivery station is part of Amazon's commitment to invest £40 billion in the UK between 2025 and 2027 – the largest investment in the company's history outside the US.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670246.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazon's new delivery station near Ingleby Barwick *(Image: UNP John Millard)*

Since 2010, Amazon has invested more than £4.7 billion in the North East.

Alex Hallimond, Amazon’s senior operations manager at the facility, said: “The North East has a proud industrial heritage as one of the UK's great manufacturing regions.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21670247.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Amazon's new delivery station near Ingleby Barwick *(Image: UNP John Millard)*

“That tradition of building things continues with this new delivery station, setting a new sustainability blueprint that could shape how we design sites in the UK around the world."
