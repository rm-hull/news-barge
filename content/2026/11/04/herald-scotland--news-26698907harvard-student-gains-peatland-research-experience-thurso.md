A Harvard University student crossed the Atlantic to gain hands-on research experience through a nine-week peatland research placement in the North Coast of Scotland.

Audrey Lord, a third-year undergraduate student studying integrative biology, was awarded a scholarship by Harvard to undertake the placement earlier this year with the Peatlands Team at the Environmental Research Institute (ERI), part of UHI North, West and Hebrides in Thurso.

During her time at the ERI, Ms Lord, who is originally from Florida, worked alongside researchers and PhD students, taking part in fieldwork across Caithness and Sutherland.

The placement gave her the opportunity to experience The Flow Country, which is first peatland in the world to be inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, while developing practical research skills in a setting very different from her usual university environment in Cambridge, Massachusetts.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/18354230.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Flow Country has been granted Unesco World Heritage Site status *(Image: NQ)*Speaking about her placement, Ms Lord said:*“* I really loved my placement. Everyone was super welcoming and kind. Some of my favourite parts were spending time walking around the Flow Country, the blanket bog of peatland that stretches across Caithness and Sutherland. I enjoyed getting to experience the different areas of the Flow Country while learning about the work being done to conserve the ecosystem.”

For Ms Lord, the experience also provided a welcome change from university life in the United States. She added: “I go to Harvard University in Cambridge, which is right next to Boston. Thurso is definitely less urban than Cambridge, but it was a welcome change of scenery! I love the outdoors, so I appreciated being able to see nature and wide-open spaces while on a run or walk. This summer, I saw some of the most stunning views of my life.”

The placement also enabled Ms Lord to take her research experience beyond the laboratory, and she believes the experience could be particularly valuable to other students interested in ecology and climate change.

She said: Working at the ERI, I had the opportunity to do not just any fieldwork, but fieldwork in a unique and globally significant environment.

“I would recommend doing a placement to other students who are interested in ecology and climate change. I think it would be a great opportunity to get first-hand experience in the kinds of environments and research that they may have learned about in classes.”

Dr Mascha Bischoff, Research Associate, in the Peatlands Team at ERI said: “Audrey’s contribution to our research projects monitoring Large Heath butterfly and Common Scoter nest predation in the Flow Country made a big difference this summer. We are always glad to host students interested in peatland ecology and conservation, but Audrey stood out with her curiosity, commitment and willingness to embrace fieldwork with all its challenges. She quickly became a valued and reliable member of the team, and it was a real pleasure to work with her over the summer and watch her grow in confidence.”

Ms Lord's interest in Scotland also extends beyond environmental research. She also studies Gaelic as part of her degree in the United States and, following her placement in Thurso, travelled to Skye to attend an intensive Gaelic language course at Sabhal Mòr Ostaig.

In September, The Herald reported on the launch of a new UHI North, West and Hebrides postgraduate course to strengthen Gaelic community development.

The course has been developed in partnership with Bòrd na Gàidhlig, the principal public body in Scotland responsible for promoting Gaelic development. It has been designed to meet the professional development needs of those working to strengthen Gaelic within communities.

The course includes a bespoke module where students learn how languages can be strengthened and brought back into everyday use, how communities can be supported to take the lead and how to deliver sustainable Gaelic initiatives.

The first cohort of six Gaelic development officers began their studies in September 2026, with the programme expected to be completed in late 2027.
