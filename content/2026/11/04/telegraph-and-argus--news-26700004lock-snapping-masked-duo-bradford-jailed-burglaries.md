A pair of burglars from Bradford have been locked up.

Bilal Shah, 30, and David Briggs, 37, both received 876 days’ imprisonment after a court heard of their activities on August 31 of this year.

They were arrested after an attempted burglary on a property in Crossley Gardens in Halifax.

Police officers found Briggs wearing a balaclava near a van.

Shah, also with a mask over his face, was discovered hiding behind a vehicle.

Prosecutor Danielle Beaumont told Bradford Crown Court how Shah drove a van onto the driveway of a property on Brooklyn Close in Dewsbury where, in a mask and gloves, he snapped a door lock to gain entry.

He drove off after being disturbed by a man inside the house.

Shortly afterwards he drove to an unoccupied property on Westfield Lane in Wyke and broke in by snapping a door lock to steal jewellery, watches, and documents worth £700.

The same night both Shah and Briggs went to Crossley Gardens in Halifax where they snapped a lock and carried out an untidy search of the entire property.

Money was taken as well as a pair of child’s shoes.

Police who were called to the scene spotted a male wearing a balaclava, who ran away.

That was Briggs, who was detained shortly after.

Shah, also masked, was located hiding behind a vehicle.

A hoodie was found nearby.

In the pair’s vehicle police found a jewellery box with jewellery inside as well as a pair of mole grips and a screwdriver.

In their interviews Shah, of Gaythorne Terrace, Clayton, Bradford, denied all offences.

Briggs, of St Mary’s Crescent, Wyke, gave no comment answers.

The court heard that the duo had previous convictions for burglary and theft of vehicles.

In mitigation the court heard that drug addiction had led Shah into a lifestyle of acquisitive criminality.

Briggs was said to be involved in just one incident on the night and was not a participant in the other two.

Sentencing the pair, Mr Recorder Tony Watkin said: “Both of you have an unenviable criminal record for dishonesty and for dwelling house burglary.

“You either do not understand or do not care about the impact of these offences upon ordinary members of the public, which can be devastating.

“Apart from the financial loss that they suffer, they could be left with feelings of vulnerability and insecurity.

“The impact of your offending lives long in their minds and actions, and you leave a permanent stain upon their homes.”

He sentenced both Shah and Briggs to 876 days each on all counts, to run concurrently.
