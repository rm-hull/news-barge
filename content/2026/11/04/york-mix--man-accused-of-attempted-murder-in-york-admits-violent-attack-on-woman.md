A York man who was accused of attempting to murder a woman following a violent incident in Acomb has admitted an alternative charge of causing grievous bodily harm.

Dean Barker, 42, was due to face trial at Leeds Crown Court later this month for the attempted murder allegation but will now be sentenced for GBH with intent instead after pleading guilty to the latter charge yesterday (Tuesday).

Barker was arrested following an incident at a property on Tudor Road, Acomb, on 29 May and charged with attempted murder of the named woman, criminal damage, assaulting a named man causing actual bodily harm and carrying an offensive weapon, namely a baseball bat.

He admitted three of those charges – damaging the council house, ABH and possessing an offensive weapon – but denied attempted murder.

Appearing at Leeds Crown Court today, Barker, of Tudor Road, pleaded guilty to causing grievous bodily harm to the woman with intent to do so.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/leeds-crown-court-dreamstime.webp)

All the offences occurred at a house neighbouring Barker’s own address in Acomb on the same day.

Prosecutor Connor Quinn said that Barker’s plea to the alternative charge of GBH with intent was acceptable to the Crown and the attempted-murder allegation was withdrawn.

Defence barrister Stephen Grattage asked for sentence to be adjourned for probation and psychological reports.

He said that due to Barker’s mental-health condition, a consultant psychologist might recommend a hospital order rather than a prison sentence.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/tudor-road-acomb-york-gsv.webp)

He said that Barker was currently in the custody of a “complex care unit” in Leeds.

Judge Tom Bayliss KC agreed to adjourn the case for pre-sentence and psychological reports.

The case will next be mentioned at the same court for an update on Barker’s psychological condition. A sentence date would then be arranged.

The judge asked for the pre-sentence report to consider the potential risk that Barker posed to the public which could affect sentence.
