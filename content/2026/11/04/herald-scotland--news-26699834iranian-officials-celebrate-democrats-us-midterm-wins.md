**Iranian officials have celebrated a rebuke of the Republican Party and President Donald Trump by US voters in the midterm elections, as the region waited to see how the results could affect the Iran war.**

Concerns about the war’s effects, particularly frustration over rising prices for fuel and other goods in the US, helped Democrats win control of both chambers of Congress in the election.

“Conscience or the cost of living, Americans said NO TO WAR, and we endorse this message,” Iran’s chief negotiator and parliament speaker, Mohammad Bagher Qalibaf, wrote on X.

The broader region has been on edge over the prospect of the US resuming full-scale military operations against Iran for the first time in almost two months.

Talks aimed at preventing renewed fighting continue, while other US allies in the region are urging Washington against resuming full-scale operations in the war, now more than eight months old, which has rattled the global economy and devastated Iran.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has reportedly pushed for more strikes, while Mr Trump has alternated between threatening major strikes and pushing for a deal.

Despite ceasefire agreements and repeated diplomatic efforts, military confrontations have continued in southern Iran and around the Strait of Hormuz, raising concerns about a return to full-scale fighting.

Iran’s supreme leader said those who think partisan changes in the United States can change its enmity towards the Islamic Republic are “gravely mistaken”.

In a letter shared on his social media account, Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not been seen publicly since the war began with US and Israeli strikes on February 28, said: “If there are still those who harbour even a slight hope for a change in behaviour or partisan and factional shifts within the system of domination, or who imagine that by giving concessions to America they can dissuade it from hostility towards the Iranian nation, they are gravely mistaken.”

The midterms came after the US carried out what appeared to be a limited strike on Iranian targets in the crucial Strait of Hormuz waterway, which raised questions about what might come next.

Iranian state media reported explosions on Monday on the islands of Qeshm and Hengam and near the southern port city of Bandar Abbas. Iranian authorities have not publicly identified the specific sites struck or disclosed the extent of the damage.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which about a fifth of the world’s oil supply passed before the war, has also witnessed repeated incidents involving commercial shipping. Iran has severely restricted shipping through the strait since the conflict began, while the United States has maintained a naval blockade of Iranian ports.

Iran’s state TV said on Wednesday that four oil tankers caught fire after they struck mines on what it described as unauthorised routes.

Ali Nikzad, Iran’s deputy parliament speaker, linked Republican election losses to Washington’s policy towards Iran. He told politicians on Wednesday that US sanctions had failed to break Iran’s economy or force its people to surrender.

“The enemy’s efforts to bring down Iran’s economy have failed time and again,” he said, adding: “These defeats are the aftershocks of defeat at the hands of the Iranian people.”

The anniversary of the 1979 takeover of the US embassy in Tehran was marked in Iran on Wednesday, with thousands of people parading through the Iranian capital to commemorate the event.

Demonstrators burned American and Israeli flags and chanted “death to America” and “death to Israel”. Effigies of Mr Trump and Mr Netanyahu were displayed alongside caricatures of the two leaders.

Addressing the demonstrators, Tehran mayor Alireza Zakani claimed the United States and Israel had suffered a decisive defeat, including in the US midterms.

On November 4 1979, Iranian students stormed the US embassy, taking American diplomats and other citizens hostage. Fifty-two Americans were held for 444 days before being released on January 20 1981.

The crisis helped cement the rupture between the two countries, which have had no formal diplomatic relations for decades.
