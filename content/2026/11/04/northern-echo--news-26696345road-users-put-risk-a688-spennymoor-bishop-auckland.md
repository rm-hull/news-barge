A judge said it was “a miracle” that a man who overdosed on tablets did not seriously injure anyone as he tried to drive to hospital.

Defendant Luke Duggan was said to have had a serious mental-health history but was no longer engaging with psychiatric services when the incident happened early last year.

He was driving a Skoda which an off-duty police officer saw swerving towards the nearside verge of the A688 between Spennymoor and Bishop Auckland at 8am on Monday, January 13, 2025.

The officer noticed damage around one of the car’s nearside lights.

Durham Crown Court heard that the Skoda crossed the centre lines, forcing an oncoming driver to swerve to avoid a crash.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21510976.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Durham Crown Court was told defendant Luke Duggan had taken an overdose of tablets before attempting to drive himself to hospital *(Image: The Northern Echo)*

Duggan returned to the correct side of the road but crossed the centre lines again, almost hitting roadside furniture.

The Skoda crawled along the grass verge before stopping short of a roundabout.

It veered back across the nearside grass verge and pavement on Weardale Street before heading towards the Frog and Ferret roundabout.

Prosecutor Caroline McGurk said the off-duty officer stopped following the Skoda as she considered it too dangerous to do so.

Duggan then entered a roundabout without stopping at a Give Way sign, forcing another motorist to brake suddenly.

The Skoda crossed onto the wrong side of the A688 again, forcing drivers to swerve before finally colliding with a lorry near Broom Mill Farm Shop in West Auckland.

Miss McGurk said the lorry jack-knifed into vegetation, while witnesses believed Duggan was intoxicated in some way and was sitting so low in the driver’s seat that he could barely be seen.

When police arrived, they found Duggan slumped against the driver’s door.

He said he had taken “a load of tablets” and had driven only to try to reach hospital.

The 30-year-old, formerly of Front Street, West Auckland, and now of West View, Ryhope, Sunderland, admitted dangerous driving at his first hearing in June this year.

He was said to have served a three-year sentence imposed in 2016 for a series of burglaries and vehicle-taking offences.

Uzma Khan, in mitigation, said Duggan understood the seriousness of the offences and accepted that driving that morning was “wholly irresponsible” and put other road users at risk.

“He was obviously impacted by events of that day, to his great regret and shame.

“This wasn’t a case of him racing, trying to show-off or evading police.

“The evidence demonstrates that at that stage he was suffering a significant deterioration in his mental health, had taken an overdose and was trying to reach hospital.

“He was struggling to cope with his mental health and had displayed erratic behaviour beforehand.”

Miss Khan said Duggan was diagnosed with psychosis at 19, when he suffered hallucinations, disturbed thinking and voices telling him to harm himself.

He took medication for a while but stopped because of its side effects.

Miss Khan said Duggan now wanted to work with the Probation Service to get the correct support in the community.

She said the Probation Service believed Duggan could be safely managed in the community.

Judge Nathan Adams accepted that the defendant was in a mental-health crisis and had overdosed on diazepam or a similar tablet.

But he said Duggan foolishly attempted to drive himself to hospital, travelling “all over the road” and at times in the wrong direction.

“It’s frankly a miracle no-one was hurt, including yourself.

“A high number of people were put at risk, but in mitigation I’m asked to take into account your psychiatric and mental health position.”

He said sending Duggan straight to prison would not help him in his situation.

The judge imposed a 12-month prison sentence, suspended for 18 months, and ordered Duggan to undergo 150 hours' unpaid work for the damage he caused.

He also banned him from driving for 18 months, and he must pass an extended re-test before lawfully being allowed to drive again.
