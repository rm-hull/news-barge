North East households are being urged not to burn rubbish as experts warn illegal fireworks, nuisance smoke and unsafe displays can bring fines of thousands of pounds.

County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service said crews attended 91 incidents last Bonfire Night and urged families to choose organised displays rather than hold fireworks or bonfire celebrations at home.

Durham Constabulary echoed the warning and said it would join the fire service and North East councils to crack down on illegal displays.

Kev Bell, station manager at County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service (CDDFRS), said having firework displays or bonfires at home increases the risk of injury and damage to property.

Organised displays have appropriate safety measures and trained staff handling safety-regulated fireworks to protect spectators.

He added: "The best way to protect your loved ones this Bonfire Night is by attending an organised fireworks display."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/16125138.jpg?type=mds-article-620)County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service have urged families to choose organised displays rather than hold fireworks or bonfire celebrations at home. *(Image: The Northern Echo)*

Police warned that anyone breaking firework laws could face a fine of up to £5,000 or six months in prison.

While there are no laws against homeowners holding a bonfire, there are laws covering the nuisance they may cause and materials that are burned.

Fireworks must be bought from licensed sellers because unlicensed fireworks are illegal and may not meet safety standards.

Anyone caught selling or using illegal fireworks can receive an unlimited fine and up to six months in prison.

Garden bonfires must follow government guidance: burn only dry, visibly clean, untreated and unpainted waste wood, with small amounts of paper and card to start the fire.

Domestic waste must not be burned if it could cause pollution or harm people’s health, including burning rubbish on Bonfire Night.

Other materials that cannot be burned include painted, varnished or treated wood, furniture, plastics, packaging, rubber, tyres, foam, wet wood and green garden waste, which should instead be taken to a household recycling centre or collected by a registered waste carrier.

Stan Staykov, founder of Rubbish Removal UK, said bonfires can be great for bringing communities together, but "every year you hear about people burning all sorts of rubbish and doing a lot of harm to their neighbours, local wildlife and the environment."

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21640212.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kev Bell from CDDFRS. *(Image: County Durham and Darlington Fire and Rescue Service)*

He added that many homeowners are unaware of bonfire rules, which could see them face fines or prosecution.

Staykov said: "Some people may not even realise that they are breaking the law, which is why it is so important for those planning a bonfire to be aware of what they can and can’t burn."

Homeowners could also be fined if bonfire smoke drifts into traffic and creates a danger.

Councils can investigate complaints and issue abatement notices when smoke becomes a statutory nuisance, such as by drifting into neighbours’ homes.

CDDFRS warned that bonfires should be kept away from sheds, fences, trees and other items that could catch fire.

Those planning bonfires should check the area for wildlife before lighting the fire, keep a hose or bucket of water nearby and never leave it uncontrolled.

Durham Constabulary warned that fireworks must come from a licensed retailer and carry a CE or UKCA safety mark.

Only adults aged 18 or over may buy fireworks, and home displays must use no higher than Category 3 fireworks.

Fireworks must not be set off after 11pm, except on Bonfire Night, when they can be used until midnight.

It is illegal to set off or throw fireworks, including sparklers, in streets or other public places.

CDDFRS urged those celebrating to avoid alcohol and light one firework at a time.

They say children under five must not use sparklers, while older children are advised to wear gloves.

Used fireworks should be left for at least 30 minutes, then placed in water for 24 hours before being sealed in a plastic bag and put in a bin.

Durham Constabulary confirmed it will carry out extra patrols with fire crews and councils on Bonfire Night.

Anyone concerned about illegal fireworks should contact their local authority’s Trading Standards department. Unauthorised bonfires on public land should be reported to Durham County Council or Darlington Borough Council.

In an emergency, residents should call 999.
