Wetherby Races has raised more than £52,000 for Spinal Research following a charity lunch and auction at its Northern Raceday, where the children of former Grand National and Gold Cup-winning jockey Graham Lee spoke about the impact of his life-changing spinal cord injury.

Graham was paralysed from the neck down after being unseated in the stalls at Newcastle Races nearly three years ago. He and his family were among almost 250 guests attending the special fundraising event at Wetherby Racecourse.

His children, Robbie and Amy Lee, spoke about their very different experiences of finding out about their father’s accident and the severity of his injuries.

Robbie was at the family’s North Yorkshire home studying for his GCSEs and watched the accident live on television, while Amy was in her first term at university.

Amy recalled being collected late at night by family friend and fellow top jockey AJ McCoy for the long drive to see her father at the RVI Hospital in Newcastle.

Both siblings spoke about the difficulty of processing the enormity of Graham’s injuries, alongside the support they had received from family, friends and the racing community. They also shared their hope that research breakthroughs will deliver new treatments.

> **Amy Lee said:**
>
> Every day is a new battle but so far we have won each one and live to fight the next, which is something my family are so grateful for.
>
> We all know that hope is such a powerful catalyst and motivation, especially for us in our new normal, and for Dad. The potential for improving or getting back anything, however small, is what gets him through his treatments.

During his distinguished career, Graham Lee rode more than 1,000 winners over jumps before switching to flat racing.

The popular Irish jockey achieved the distinction of winning both the Grand National, riding Amberleigh House, and the Gold Cup at Royal Ascot on Trip to Paris.

Wetherby’s Northern Raceday for Spinal Research is now in its 36th year and has raised more than £850,000 for the charity.

Suzanne Redding, Head of Audience and Communications at Spinal Research, thanked Amy and Robbie, Wetherby Racecourse and everyone who supported the event.

> **Suzanne Redding, Head of Audience and Communications at Spinal Research, said:**
>
> We are so grateful to Amy and Robbie, to Wetherby Racecourse and to every single person who supported the Northern Raceday.
>
> After decades of funding the best research and researchers we are now at a point where breakthrough therapies are coming through; where the impossible is becoming possible.
>
> At this pivotal moment, what we urgently need is the funding to turn hope into reality for Graham and his wonderful family and for the 110,000 people in the UK alone who are living every day with the devastating consequences of a spinal cord injury.

Spinal Research estimates that someone in the UK is paralysed following a spinal cord injury every two hours. Globally, an estimated 15.4 million people are living with paralysis.

The charity funds research into treatments and therapies for people paralysed following spinal cord injuries, with function-restoring treatments now progressing through human trials.

Further information about the charity is available from Spinal Research.
