Nuisance e-scooters, motorbikes and riders wearing balaclavas are being targeted under a new anti-social behaviour policy agreed in Darlington.

Darlington Borough Council’s cabinet approved new rules covering the anti-social use and storage of vehicles at a meeting on Tuesday, November 3.

The council says the issue has caused particular concern among tenants and councillors in recent years, with a dedicated section included in its new Housing Services Anti-Social Behaviour Policy.

Under the policy, anti-social behaviour (ASB) includes riding motorbikes, quad bikes, e-scooters and mopeds anywhere other than roads and authorised tracks.

Dangerous or reckless riding is also covered, alongside intimidating or harassing residents and wearing balaclavas or other face coverings.

Action against those responsible could range from warning letters and tenancy breach notices to fixed penalty notices, injunctions and closure orders. In more serious cases, the council could begin possession proceedings against a tenant.

Officers can also gather evidence through measures including diary sheets and noise-recording equipment, while the new policy includes the use of body-worn video.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21667405.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Darlington Borough Council's cabinet agreed to approve the crackdown in a new anti-social behaviour policy for housing. *(Image: LDRS)*

Cllr Matthew Roche, cabinet member for health and housing, said the changes would give housing services another way to tackle dangerous off-road bikes.

He said: “This is a huge issue to many residents and now this policy includes a section on the storage of motorised vehicles, which I really welcome.”

Cllr Roche said the council was “very clear” it would not tolerate anti-social behaviour which could “make people’s lives a misery” and would take action where needed.

However, he said the council would initially try to resolve problems and find ways of bringing people together.

The wider policy covers the council’s more than 5,300 homes and sets out how Housing Services will respond to reports of ASB and hate incidents.

It includes support for tenants affected by ASB and says complaints should be dealt with promptly, with action ranging from support and mediation to enforcement and legal action where necessary.

People reporting ASB will have a risk assessment carried out so the council can take account of their vulnerabilities.

Tenants are expected to cooperate with investigations, which could include keeping diary sheets, having noise-recording equipment installed or reporting crimes.
