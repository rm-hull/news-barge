US ​first lady ‌Melania Trump will ​host ​representatives of Russia ⁠and ​Ukraine at ​the White House on ​Thursday, the Trump administration has said.

Her office said that she would personally bring unnamed officials from the warring countries together in an “extraordinary moment in American history”.

“This achievement marks an unprecedented diplomatic initiative by an American first lady,” the White House said, adding that she would also deliver remarks about the ceasefire in the Ukrainian town of Oleshky, which she was involved in brokering.

The announcement came amid a major attack on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure and following a devastating Russian strike on a school being used for aid distribution in Zaporizhzhia that killed at least 33 people.

Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko described the situation with power in the capital as “extremely difficult” and said the attacks “create a threat not only to Ukraine but also to nuclear safety throughout Europe.”

President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia had launched 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles overnight, hitting power plants and substations from the Black Sea port of Odesa in the south to Rivne in the west.

## Bomb threats force several flights into emergency landings across Europe

At least seven flights in Europe have been subjected to bomb threats, forcing several planes to divert and an airport in Poland to close in a day of chaos on Tuesday.

“Malicious” bomb scares on two flights from London, and others from Manchester, Copenhagen, Israel, Dublin, Bergamo and Lisbon threw airports into disarray, with authorities searching planes after some of the flights went through emergency landings.

The diversion of the flights with hundreds of passengers on board, and temporary closures of airports, raises concerns over the ease with which agitators can cause major disruption.

## Norway to deploy troops and sensors to Russia's border

Norway will strengthen its military presence in the region of Finnmark, which borders Russia.

Norway will send 30 additional servicemen by the end of 2026 to the border region and deploy sensors for the National Advanced Surface-to-Air Missile System, the government said.

"The experience from Ukraine shows how important it is to have a good overview and receive early warning. These are sensors that will strengthen our ability to detect drones in Finnmark," defence minister Tore O Sandvik said.

## Europe must grow as US shrinks, Zelensky says

President Zelensky has thanked Germany for its support, and warned that as the United States’ role in the world shrinks, Europe’s role must become even greater.

In an address at the Berlin Security Conference, he said: “Without America, thousands and thousands more Ukrainians would have been killed by Russian strikes. And millions more would have become refugees. That’s why we always thank America. And we mean it.”

In an apparent reference to Donald Trump, who once accused Mr Zelensky of not being grateful enough, he added: “Even when we say thank you many times in one speech. Just to make sure those who sometimes feel we haven’t thanked them enough can hear us.”

He went on: “But we also know that without Europe, Ukraine would not have survived. Without Germany and other European countries, without the EU. The Russians would have burned down far more of our cities and villages if it were not for your help.

“The United States’ role in the world may become even smaller. So Europe’s role in the world must become even greater. And without Germany, this is impossible. Without you, freedom will surely lose.

“Freedom and security in Europe have no alternative to your commitment and your strength. Europe has no alternative to Germany. Please remember that.”

## Displaced Ukrainian pupils in UK to be offered GCSE in their language

Pupils in England, Wales and Northern Ireland will soon be able to study a Ukrainian GCSE, the UK’s Department of Education has announced.

Aimed at young people displaced by Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in 2022, the new qualification, from 2028, hopes to help pupils preserve their cultural heritage.

The decision follows reports that the number of people taking Russian GCSEs had spiked in recent years, potentially because Ukrainian pupils were encouraged to take it, in the absence of a Ukrainian qualification.

It was reported last year that Ukrainian officials were “deeply concerned” at potentially retraumatising displaced teenagers by pushing them to learn Russian.

Education secretary Lucy Powell said it was right for Ukrainians and their children to have every opportunity to maintain a connection to their heritage.

## Russia knocks out power to swathes of Ukraine

Russia pounded Ukraine’s energy infrastructure with dozens of missiles and hundreds of drones on Tuesday.

It follows a deadly strike on a school being used for aid distribution in the village of Komyshuvakha on Monday, which killed at least 33 people.

The latest attacks have knocked out power to swathes of the country and DTEK, Ukraine's largest private generating company, said three of its five thermal power plants had been knocked offline as a result.

Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko described the situation with power in the city as “extremely difficult.”

He said the attacks “create a threat not only to Ukraine but also to nuclear safety throughout Europe.”

Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia had launched 289 drones, 17 ballistic missiles and 59 cruise missiles overnight, hitting power plants and substations from the Black Sea port of Odesa in the south to Rivne in the west.
