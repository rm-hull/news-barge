Millions of voters have cast their ballots in elections that will determine the balance of power in Congress — and with it, the fate of Donald Trump’s final years in the White House.

All 435 seats in the House of Representatives are up for election, along with 35 seats in the 100-member Senate.

With over two-thirds of results declared, the Democrats look set for a clean sweep of Congress. The party now surpassed the four seats it needed to flip to take the Senate, and are edging closer to control of the House.

Historic results have been seen in Texas, where James Talarico is the projected winner, alongside Annie Andrews in South Carolina and Sherrod Brown in Ohio. It has been several decades since the Democrats held the Senate for any of these seats.

Democratic Senator Jon Ossoff is also projected to hold his seat in Georgia, dashing Republican hopes of a victory in the state.

In a positive sign for Democratic prospects, the party has also now flipped enough seats in Florida’s state House to break a Republican supermajority.

The 2026 midterm elections have become a referendum on Trump’s second residency, with Democratic candidates blaming the administration for the growing cost of living crisis and economic fallout from the Iran war, among other issues that are driving voters to the polls in record numbers.

Defensive Republicans, meanwhile, have sought to cast their opponents as too extreme.

Full results are not expected in the immediate hours after polls close; polls won’t close in Alaska until 1 a.m. Eastern, and election officials will continue processing ballots received through Election Day. Potential Republican-led challenges and legal intervention from the Trump administration could drag results into the coming days and weeks ahead.

Preliminary results from the Associated Press will inform *The Independent*’s charts and maps of election results and the nation’s shifting political landscape.
