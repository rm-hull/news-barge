An oil and gas firm led by one of the North Sea’s most successful entrepreneurs has expanded in the area after expressing serious concerns about the UK Government’s  treatment of the industry.  
Kistos Holdings has bought into two gas discoveries in the UK North Sea from which it expects to start production in coming months.

The move was announced shortly after the company said it would prioritise investment in areas such as the Middle East citing the raft of changes made to the UK tax and regulatory regimes in recent years.

Kistos moved into the Middle East last year with a $148 million (£110m) acquisition in Oman, which some saw as a snub to the UK.

It has invested heavily in Norway in recent months.

Noting that stability is key to investment, Kistos director Peter Mann complained this week: “That stability has gone in the UK.”

Mr Mann’s comments echo concerns expressed by a series of North Sea firms since the windfall tax was introduced by the former Conservative Government in 2022.

Industry leaders have warned that tax hikes combined with the Labour Government’s decision to curb exploration could provoke an exodus from the area.

Chancellor John Healey appeared to ignore those warnings when he left the rate of the windfall tax unchanged in his Budget last week.

Mr Mann told The Telegraph the situation had got so bad that control of the North Sea oil and gas regime should be taken from the UK Government and entrusted to an independent regulator.

However, the acquisition announced by Kistos following his comments provides further evidence that leading operators still think there are attractive growth opportunities in the North Sea.

The company is buying stakes in the Abbey and Baker gas fields off Yorkshire from Oman’s Petrogas for an undisclosed sum.

Executive chairman Andrew Austin said the deal would allow Kistos to add a high-quality, gas-weighted development to its UK portfolio at an attractive entry point.

The acquisition will increase Kistos’s reserves by around 9 million barrels oil equivalent.

Brent crude sold for around $105 per barrel yesterday.

Kistos expects to make a final investment decision with Petrogas next year about whether to proceed with the development of the fields. These could be brought into production relatively quickly by linking them to an existing production platform.

First gas is targeted for 2029 although the windfall Energy Profits Levy is due to remain in force until 2030.

The deal indicates that North Sea assets are in demand following the surge in prices fuelled by the US-Israeli war on Iran. This has helped North Sea operators to generate bumper profits.

While prices may fall from the highs reached this year if a peace deal is agreed, they are expected to be underpinned by strong demand for hydrocarbons.

The disruption triggered by the war on Iran has led to increased competition for imports and put a premium on UK assets.

The Labour Government has highlighted the need to bolster the UK’s energy security. This was reflected in the decision it made this month to approve the development of the giant Jackdaw gas field east of Aberdeen.

Mr Austin noted: “The UK will continue to depend on domestic gas supply for many years to come, and Abbey and Baker provide Kistos with long-term gas production.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21666583.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Kistos Holdings executive chairman Andrew Austin *(Image: Kistos Holdings)*

Kistos bought gas storage facilities in Cheshire from EDF in February 2024 for £25m.

Mr Austin has highlighted the damaging impact of tax and regulatory changes on North Sea oil and gas activity.

However, Kistos has made significant expansions moves in the area following the introduction of the windfall tax in May 2022 and subsequent increases in the rate of the levy.

The company moved into the UK exploration and production business through the acquisition of a 20% stake in the Greater Laggan Area assets West of Shetland from Total in July 2022 for an upfront payment of $125 million. The portfolio includes a gas processing plant in Shetland.

Kistos acquired exploration acreage West of Shetland in August 2024.

It expects to capitalise on expansion moves made by other firms in the West of Shetland area such as Serica Energy.

Serica acquired an operating, 40%, interest in the Greater Laggan assets in September last year, with assets bought from the Prax group. Prax went into administration after a rapid debt-fuelled expansion drive.

Serica has noted the potential to discover more oil and gas West of Shetland and for firms to develop existing finds in the area.

Kistos made $154m underlying profit in the six months to June 30 compared with $24m in the same period last year.

Mr Austin formed the business with Mr Mann after developing RockRose Energy into a leading North Sea player by acquiring assets bigger fish had lost interest in.

Viaro Energy acquired RockRose for £248m in July 2020.

The windfall tax rate was set at 25% in May 2022. The rate was increased to 35% in November 2022. The Labour Government increased the rate to 38% in its first Budget after winning the 2024 General Election.

The levy is expected to be replaced in 2030 by a mechanism that would link the rate of tax paid by North Sea firms to oil and gas prices.
