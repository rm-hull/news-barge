Calls are being made for a ban on the public sale of fireworks as protestors warn against the dangers ahead of Bonfire Night.

Campaigners in Bradford say the noise and misuse of fireworks are causing widespread harm, particularly around Bonfire Night, and are urging action to protect vulnerable groups.

Allan Rhodes, a member of a fireworks demonstration group, said: "When you've got fireworks in close proximity, it can be frightening – especially for animals.

"I've got a pet cat, and when I'm trying to take calls at work, I'm worried sick about him.

"You've got loud bangs going off outside the window and, even though it's perfectly legal, it's causing me problems."

## 'Small animals can suffer heart attacks from the noise'

Mr Rhodes said the impact of fireworks goes far beyond inconvenience, warning that small animals can suffer fatal consequences.

He said: "Small animals can have heart attacks from the noise.

"You also don't know how many people are suffering from PTSD or cerebral palsy.

"Cerebral palsy in particular causes startle reflexes, so people can have involuntary reflexes to the loud noises."

## 'You imprison people who don't deserve it and it's not fair'

He said that people with neurodivergent conditions and other vulnerabilities have no choice but to endure the distress caused by fireworks.

Mr Rhodes said: "You have the choice of going to a fireworks display, if you want to.

"But others will be trapped in their own homes, and you imprison people who don't deserve it, and it's not fair."

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21383995.jpg?type=mds-article-620)A generic fireworks display *(Image: Pixabay)*

He also warned that fireworks can disrupt wildlife and domestic animals.

He said: "Horses and farm animals also tend to have a startle reflex, as well as small animals and wildlife.

"It affects the migratory patterns of birds.

"It's ridiculous.

"And it doesn't go on for one or two days.

"It will be from now until New Year."

## 'I think the solution is to ban the sale completely'

Mr Rhodes believes the only solution is to restrict public access to fireworks.

He said: "I think the solution is to ban the sale completely.

"We want it to be as safe as possible for people.

"If people have got access to fireworks, they will set them off.

"It doesn't matter what you do otherwise - it doesn't matter how many adverts or health and safety warnings you put out.

"People don't care."

## 'If tradition causes harm, we end it'

Mr Rhodes said: "If tradition causes harm, we end it.

"Bull fighting is a tradition in Spain and many people are against it.

"Fox hunting is traditional and many people are against it.

"But it's also not going to really die – it will just change into a different way of celebrating by having organised displays only where people can get together and enjoy the experience."

## 'The idea that the public can buy something so lethal is worrying'

Another campaigner, who wished to remain anonymous, agreed with Mr Rhodes.

He said: "The noise is one of the worst things.

"The impact on everyone's health and animals, wildlife and pets and horses etc.

"It's all impacted.

"The danger to the people setting them off and to others, the possible misuse as weapons.

"I've seen articles where people have posted lit fireworks through letter boxes.

"The idea that the public can buy something that can be lethal and misused and abused is worrying.

"I don't really agree with it anymore.

"I think they should be handled by professionals."

## 'Police have got an unenforceable situation on their hands'

The campaigner said current enforcement is not effective.

He said: "Police would have to see people setting the fireworks off and then issue the fine.

"But they've got an unenforceable situation on their hands.

"They would need to be everywhere all at once.

"The only possible resolution I can find is to stop them being purchased by members of the public."

## 'It's not about banning fireworks completely'

He added that the issue is not with fireworks themselves, but with their misuse and noise.

The campaigner said: "It's not about banning fireworks completely.

"It's about reducing their use so people can't set them off all the time.

"November will definitely end up being fireworks month, rather than Bonfire Night.

"It's a daily occurrence."
