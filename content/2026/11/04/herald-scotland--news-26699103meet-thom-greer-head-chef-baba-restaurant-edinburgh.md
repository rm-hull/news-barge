Thom Greer is the head chef at BABA in Edinburgh.

Originally from Dunedin, New Zealand, he began his hospitality career as a kitchen porter at 14, then attended culinary school.

He later worked in restaurants across New Zealand and Australia before moving to Edinburgh in 2018 to join BABA within the Kimpton Charlotte Square Hotel. Starting as chef de partie, he progressed through the kitchen to become head chef within 10 months.

After a spell in Leeds, he returned to BABA, overseeing a menu inspired by the flavours of the Levant, from mezze and sharing plates to dishes cooked over charcoal.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21670536.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Thom Greer is the head chef at BABA in Edinburgh *(Image: Supplied)*

**When did you realise you wanted to be a chef?** I developed a love for food from cooking pizzas with my Dad on Friday nights. I had been working in kitchens since I was 14, and when I realised university was not for me, I signed up for a cooking course at the Otago Polytechnic in South East New Zealand, then moved to start my career in one of the better kitchens in Dunedin.**What was your first kitchen job like?**

It was washing dishes for a small café on the weekends in Dunedin. I didn’t mind the work, but I was slow, which got me sat down by the owner, who said, 'You just need to be faster'.

It was nice being so young and working with a bunch of cool people in their early 20s; you know, my first experience of a staff party. Anyway, we don’t need to go into it, but let’s just say, I lost my eyebrows.

**What does "good food" mean to you?** Good food to me means simple, seasoned and fresh. That’s what we try to deliver at BABA: just the best Scottish produce, with some yummy flavours and great fresh vegetables.**Can you share a memory of your worst kitchen disaster?**

My top three are:  
 - Someone ruining a chef's 10-year-old master stock.  
 - Salt used to poach 30 kg of quince (it should have been sugar, obviously).  
- Pouring hot pork fat down my arm.

**Where is your favourite place to eat out?**

Ka Pao – it’s fresh, and I love Southeast Asian flavours.

I did a bit of travelling in Cambodia and Vietnam, and worked as a sous chef in a small but authentic Vietnamese restaurant in Melbourne before I came here. That's where my love for that type of food really comes from.

**What's the best thing you've cooked recently?** My sourdough is banging, just with butter and cheese. I use a rye starter, and that makes a difference in taste and, of course, nutrition. It’s more nutty.**What do you think is the biggest misconception people have about working in a professional kitchen?** That we are all angry alcoholics.**If you weren't a chef, what do you think you'd be doing instead?** A musician. I actually studied music at university for a year, emphasis on “studied”. But I play guitar, harmonica, sing, and write my own music. I gigged in Melbourne in my twenties.**Read more:Is there one ingredient or kitchen tool you think everyone should have at home?** A small stick blender for soups and sauces. And soy sauce to replace salt; it doesn’t have to be in Asian food. It adds a depth you don’t get with salt.**What's been the standout highlight of your career so far?**

BABA’s recent AA Rosette is a standout. I was also lucky to meet my life partner in BABA, so that was a definite highlight.

I'm excited to be celebrating nine years of BABA in November, having started as chef de partie in 2018. I’ve been here for eight years (with a little time away in Leeds for my wife’s studies), and I'm looking forward to taking it into its tenth year!
