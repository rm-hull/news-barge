Labour MP Rachael Maskell’s comments on refugee numbers made in 2015 have come back into focus following her opposition to plans to accommodate up to 1,200 asylum seekers at the former RAF base at Linton-on-Ouse.

More than a decade ago, Maskell called for Britain to accept significantly more refugees, arguing that the proposed figure of 20,000 was not enough.

Speaking at a pro-refugee rally in York in September 2015, she acknowledged that accepting more people could put additional pressure on public services, schools and local communities, but argued that this was a price worth paying.

Her comments included a call to continue accepting refugees until the country reached what she described as “saturation point”.

## What Rachael Maskell said in 2015

> **At the rally, Maskell said:**
>
> We need to shout so much more and say 20,000 is not enough, 30,000 is not enough. We will keep going until we hit our saturation point because what does it matter if we have to wait another week for a hospital visit? Or if our class sizes are slightly bigger? Or if our city is slightly fuller? What does it matter if things are slightly more challenging? If we have to pay a little bit more into the system? Surely it is worth it to see those lives being restored again.

The comments were made during the European refugee crisis, when the Government had committed to resettling 20,000 Syrian refugees over five years.

Maskell’s argument was that Britain should accept more people, even if doing so placed additional demands on public services and local communities.

## Her position on Linton-on-Ouse

In 2026, Maskell has taken a very different position on a specific proposal to accommodate asylum seekers in North Yorkshire.

The Government is considering using the former RAF Linton-on-Ouse site to accommodate up to 1,200 single adult men who have applied for asylum.

Maskell has strongly opposed the proposal, arguing that the location is unsuitable and that the existing infrastructure cannot support a development on that scale.

Her concerns include the capacity of the water and sewage systems, electricity supply, limited public transport and the proximity of a primary school and nursery.

She has also highlighted the size of the proposed accommodation in relation to the small village community.

> **In August 2026, she was reported as saying:**
>
> It’s just the wrong site. The last government realised that, once they got into the detail.

Her objection is that the proposal would place an unreasonable burden on a location without the necessary infrastructure and facilities.

## What she told Parliament in 2026

Maskell reiterated her opposition during a House of Commons debate on asylum accommodation on 29 June 2026.

She challenged the Government’s decision to reconsider Linton-on-Ouse, referring to the reasons why the site had previously been rejected.

> **Maskell told MPs:**
>
> This decision looks like the politics of the past, not the future; a decision made in Whitehall, not in our communities.
>
> In 2022, RAF Linton-on-Ouse was deemed unsuitable as a site, because the drainage and sewage system would need major restoration, there would need to be an upgrade in the power capacity, and there are no amenities near the site—and the site has deteriorated further since that decision.

Maskell also raised concerns about the inadequacy of public transport and argued that the local community had not been properly involved in the decision-making process.

## A question of consistency

There is a clear contrast between Maskell’s comments in 2015 and her opposition to the proposed accommodation at Linton-on-Ouse.

In 2015, she argued that Britain should continue accepting refugees beyond the Government’s proposed 20,000, suggesting that longer waits for hospital appointments, larger school classes and fuller cities were acceptable consequences of helping more people.

Her language was particularly striking when she called for the country to continue until it reached “saturation point”.

In 2026, however, she has argued that accommodating up to 1,200 asylum seekers at Linton-on-Ouse would place unacceptable demands on local infrastructure and that the location is unsuitable.

There is an important distinction in her argument. Maskell has not withdrawn her support for refugees or the principle of providing asylum in Britain. Her stated objection is to the suitability of this particular site and the way the proposal has been developed.

Nevertheless, her earlier comments raise a legitimate political question: if additional pressure on public services and local communities was an acceptable consequence of accepting more refugees in 2015, where does she now believe the limits should lie?

And if the principle of accepting more refugees remains unchanged, how does she believe the practical consequences should be managed when they affect individual communities?

The contrast between her own words in 2015 and her position on Linton-on-Ouse in 2026 provides an opportunity to examine those questions.
