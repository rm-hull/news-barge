Residents across North Yorkshire and County Durham are being warned to expect increased military aircraft activity as the Swiss Air Force returns to RAF Leeming for four weeks of night flying.

The annual exercise, known as Yorknite, will begin on Monday, November 9, with Swiss military aircraft taking part in night flying training from the North Yorkshire RAF base.

RAF Leeming has confirmed the exercise will run for four weeks, with the final wave of aircraft each day expected to land no later than 10pm.

![](https://www.thenorthernecho.co.uk/resources/images/21669080.jpg?type=mds-article-620)An RAF jet at RAF Leeming *(Image: RAF)*

The base has acknowledged that the increased flying activity may cause disruption to nearby communities, although efforts are being made to minimise disturbance.

In a statement posted on social media, RAF Leeming said: "A bit earlier than usual, but you know what’s coming when you see this photo on our feed.

"The Swiss Air Force are back next week for Yorknite 2026.

"The annual night flying event will be running for four weeks, starting next Monday, 9 November.

"We know many of you love the exercise, but it can cause disturbance to residents.

"Mindful of this, the final wave of each day will land no later than 10pm."

The exercise is a regular fixture at RAF Leeming, with the Swiss Air Force using the North Yorkshire base for its annual night flying training.

Last year's Yorknite exercise also ran for four weeks, beginning on November 10, 2025, and involved Swiss Air Force F/A-18 fighter jets.

During that exercise, RAF Leeming similarly warned residents about additional aircraft movements and said every effort would be made to keep disruption to a minimum.

Night flying is an important part of military aviation training, allowing pilots and crews to develop and maintain the skills needed to operate in darkness.

Residents living near RAF Leeming and beneath surrounding military flight paths may notice increased aircraft activity during the four-week training period.

The latest exercise is expected to conclude in early December.
