A HISTORIC society has been awarded more than £200,000 to help protect its world-famous collection.

The Brontë Society, which runs the Brontë Parsonage Museum in Haworth, received £232,058 from The National Lottery Heritage Fund for a project called The Road to Resilience.

As the museum approaches its 100th anniversary in 2028, the funding will support plans to create new, environmentally controlled storage for its growing collection, which has outgrown its current space.

### 'We can ensure the Brontës' extraordinary legacy continues to inspire'

Rebecca Yorke, director of the Brontë Society and Brontë Parsonage Museum, said: "As we approach the Brontë Parsonage Museum's centenary in 2028, we have a responsibility not only to celebrate everything that has been achieved over the past 100 years, but also to ensure this remarkable collection is protected for the next century and beyond.

"'The Road to Resilience' is about laying those foundations.

"Through developing plans for improved, bespoke storage and preserving the specialist knowledge of our team through dedicated time spent capturing decades of expertise from the curatorial team, we can ensure the Brontës' extraordinary legacy continues to inspire staff, researchers, visitors and future generations."

### More than 8,000 objects make up the collection

The funding will also support the development of a long-term masterplan for the museum and three historic buildings on West Lane recently acquired by the society.

The Brontë collection, which contains more than 8,000 objects and is recognised as an Arts Council England Designated collection, is believed to be the largest assembly of Brontë-related material in the world.

Items include manuscripts, diary papers, letters, drawings, clothing, jewellery and personal belongings of Charlotte, Emily and Anne Brontë.

The project will involve plans for new storage facilities, conservation and reuse of the West Lane buildings, and capturing specialist curatorial knowledge to ensure expertise is preserved for the future.

A part-time project curator has already been recruited to manage the relocation and cataloguing of the collection and to document the knowledge held by museum staff.

### 'The Brontës hold a special place in the literary heritage of England'

Helen Featherstone, director for England, North at The National Lottery Heritage Fund, said: "The Brontës hold a special place in the literary heritage of England, so we are delighted to support the Brontë Society with a grant to work towards a strong and resilient future for the organisation and the Brontë Parsonage Museum.

"It is fantastic news that thanks to National Lottery players, the Society can now develop plans to shape its future and continue to inspire the next generation as it approaches its centenary."

The Road to Resilience project is scheduled to run from autumn 2026 to 2027.
