Heriot-Watt University has welcomed the first five recipients of a scholarship programme offering fully funded postgraduate degrees to women from Sub-Saharan Africa.

The Hoogenboom Scholarship Programme was launched in January with £1.5 million in backing from The Orchard Foundation. It will provide 30 scholarships over five years.

The scheme aims to support women whose postgraduate education could have a lasting impact on their communities and professional sectors.

The first cohort was welcomed at the university’s Lyell Centre in Edinburgh, a European hub for earth and marine science.

The scholarships cover living costs, including accommodation, visas and health insurance, and enable recipients to study subjects including MSc Global Environmental Change and Policy, MSc International Marine Science and MSc Sustainable Energy.

The programme prioritises applicants from Kenya, Zambia, South Africa, Botswana and Malawi.

Heriot-Watt University was established in 1821 as the world’s first mechanics institute. It now has five campuses worldwide, including locations in Dubai and Malaysia, and more than 25,000 students from 154 countries.

Professor Nola Hewitt-Dundas, principal and vice-chancellor of Heriot-Watt University, said: "When this programme in January, the university set out to open access to world-class postgraduate education for talented women from Sub-Saharan Africa.

"It is amazing to see that ambition come to life now, with our first scholars here in Edinburgh.

"I wish every one of these women the very best as they begin their studies and I’m sure under the tutelage of our talented team here, they will gain the necessary skills to go on and impact the world."

Dr Annemiek Hoogenboom, co-founder and chair of The Orchard Foundation, said: "These are talented, determined young women, and it was wonderful to hear about the ambitions they have brought with them to Edinburgh."

Gabriella Kenga, scholarship recipient and MSc Global Environmental Change and Policy student, said: "This scholarship removes the financial pressure of studying internationally, and it is more than funding for my education, it is a path to genuinely contributing to sustainable ocean governance in Kenya and across the Western Indian Ocean."

Inna Yaneva-Toraman, assistant professor in Environment & Society at the Lyell Centre, Heriot-Watt University, said: "Our focus now is on making sure every scholar gets the most out of their time with us, from our specialist facilities through to mentoring, industry placements and leadership development."
