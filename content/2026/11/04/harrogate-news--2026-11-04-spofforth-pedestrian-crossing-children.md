Children carrying handmade posters have joined a demonstration in Spofforth calling for a pedestrian crossing on Castle Street to make walking to school safer.

The demonstration took place outside Spofforth Village Hall on Wednesday 4 November, highlighting concerns about traffic passing through the village and the safety of children making their way to school.

Plans are already in place to introduce a 20mph speed limit through Spofforth, but campaigners say a pedestrian crossing is also needed to help children cross Castle Street safely.

## Campaigners want a crossing on Castle Street

Andrew Paraskos, North Yorkshire councillor for Spofforth, Lower Wharfedale and Tockwith, said:

> **Andrew Paraskos said:**
>
> We’ve got children from the Spofforth village school here to raise awareness about a crossing on Castle Street because we get a heck of a lot of traffic down here. We’re in the process of a 20mph zone, but we need a crossing on this road.

The request will be put to North Yorkshire Council’s road safety team to assess whether a crossing is feasible. Funding would also need to be identified.

Castle Street is currently affected by road closures for utility works, but campaigners say traffic is still passing through the village.

> **Andrew Paraskos said:**
>
> The road is currently closed and we’re still seeing a steady stream of vehicles coming through. It’s a rat run between Wetherby and Harrogate.

## Children’s safety concerns

Call for Spofforth Pedestrian Crossing – Spofforth Parish Councillor Gill Hewitt, who organised the demonstration, said the volume of traffic was making children feel unsafe when walking to school.

> **Gill Hewitt said:**
>
> The amount of traffic going through the village is horrendous and children feel unsafe walking to school.
>
> We all feel they should be able to do this without fear and I’m struggling to see how the council can justify not introducing more safety measures on this road.

Campaigners want the proposed 20mph zone to be accompanied by a pedestrian crossing, with the request now set to be considered by North Yorkshire Council’s road safety team.
