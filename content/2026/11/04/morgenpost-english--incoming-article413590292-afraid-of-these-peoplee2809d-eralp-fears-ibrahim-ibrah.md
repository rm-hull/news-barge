Ibrahim Ibrahim is the man whom leading Left Party politician Elif Eralp is “afraid” of, according to a report by *Der Spiegel*. At the end of September, he was still celebrating the Left Party’s election victory and turned up at its election-night party in Berlin-Neukölln. Most recently, his name caused a stir in an interview with Eralp published in* Der Spiegel*.

### Glorifying Terrorism and Supporting the Left: Who Is Ibrahim Ibrahim?

But who is he? On the night of Berlin’s September 20 election, Ibrahim Ibrahim congratulated the Left Party on its victory in a Facebook post on behalf of the United Palestinian National Committee (VPNK). In the post, he emphasised that he and his fellow VPNK activists had been “an active part of this achievement” – although the Left Party denies that he was involved in its election campaign. That same evening, Ibrahim was photographed at the election-night party in Neukölln, including with Bundestag member Ferat Koçak.

The photograph sparked outrage because Ibrahim is regarded as a representative of the Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine (PFLP), which rejects the State of Israel and advocates armed struggle. The Marxist-Leninist PFLP, which has carried out aircraft hijackings and attacks against Israelis, is designated as a terrorist organisation by the EU and the United States. However, it is not banned in Germany.

### Ibrahim Used to Display His Ties to the PFLP More Openly

This may be one reason why Ibrahim was previously able to display his ties to the PFLP much more openly, according to Grischa Stanjek. The co-director of the non-governmental organisation democ has been monitoring protests in the capital for years. Stanjek suspects that the authorities‘ repressive measures against people in Ibrahim’s circle may have made him more cautious.

The exact nature of Ibrahim’s relationship with the PFLP is unclear – an enquiry from the Berliner Morgenpost in September 2026 went unanswered. In a 2017 article on the terrorist organisation’s website, Ibrahim is described as a “comrade”. Photographs from a year earlier show Ibrahim at a funeral in Sofia, Bulgaria, where leading PFLP member Leila Khaled delivered a speech. Khaled was involved in an aircraft hijacking in 1969. In the background, Ibrahim can be seen waving the terrorist organisation’s flag.

Today, Ibrahim represents the United Palestinian National Committee (VPNK) – according to Berlin’s domestic intelligence agency, an umbrella organisation for the secular PFLP and the Islamist Hamas. The VPNK was first documented after October 7, Stanjek reports. Within the Left Party, the VPNK’s alleged links to Hamas are disputed. However, Ibrahim has at least one documented acquaintance with ties to Hamas. A YouTube video shows him and Majed Al-Zeer visiting the South African embassy as part of a VPNK delegation. Al-Zeer is regarded as a key Hamas organiser, previously lived in Neukölln and, according to *Bild*, has been on the run since an arrest warrant was issued against him.

### Ibrahim Maintains Ties to the PFLP, Hamas and Iran

His attitude towards Hamas became clear at a demonstration last year. There, Ibrahim paid tribute to Hamas leader Yahya Sinwar, who was killed by the Israeli army in 2024, describing him as a “steadfast leader” and a “heroic martyr”. In 2015, Ibrahim praised the terrorist Samir Quntar at a memorial event in Berlin. Quntar is alleged to have killed an Israeli father and his daughter in 1979.

Alongside his ties to Hamas and the PFLP, Ibrahim also appears to maintain contacts with Iran. In March this year, he was part of a delegation received by diplomats and officials at the Iranian embassy to discuss the current political situation, as Ibrahim himself wrote on Facebook.

### The Left Party in Neukölln Questions the Domestic Intelligence Agency’s Assessment

In 2019, Ibrahim stood as a candidate on the list of the Marxist-Leninist Party of Germany (MLPD) in the European elections. The party describes the PFLP as a liberation organisation and its actions as legitimate. “We demand that the PFLP be removed from the EU terrorist list!” the MLPD website states.

However, Ibrahim has found a new political home with the Left Party. In the summer of last year, several organisations joined the Internationalist Alliance Berlin, including the Left Party’s district branches in Neukölln and Wedding, the party’s Palestine Solidarity Working Group and the VPNK. Asked about Ibrahim and the security authorities’ findings concerning connections to Hamas, the Left Party’s Neukölln branch stated: “Reports by the domestic intelligence agency regularly prove to be unreliable and do not form the basis of our political assessments.”

### The Left Rejects Hamas Glorification, Cites Party Resolutions

The Left Party’s Berlin state organisation says it distances itself from all “forms of terror and violence, as well as their glorification”. However, the party’s district organisations decide independently which alliances they participate in. “Naturally, we expect our resolutions and core values to be upheld,” explains Left Party spokesperson Diana Buhe.

“There is a resolution prohibiting cooperation with any organisations that legitimise or downplay Hamas’s terror attack on October 7, 2023,” explains Left Party politician Niklas Schrader. “It is reasonable to expect party conference resolutions to be respected.”

### Demonstration in Berlin: Ibrahim Rejects Accusations of Antisemitism

On October 10, Ibrahim attended the “United4Palestine” demonstration in Berlin, according to reports by *Bild*and* Tagesspiegel*. Several Left Party politicians, including Vanessa Emde, also took part. This happened just days after the party signed the Berlin Agreement, a joint position paper with the SPD and Greens that firmly rules out “cooperation with organisations and other actors who spread antisemitism, glorify violence or further fuel the cycle of violence”.

On October 11, Ibrahim responded publicly. In a statement published by the VPNK on Instagram, the 70-year-old wrote of “defamation campaigns, witch-hunts and attempts to discredit inconvenient voices politically and socially”. He said he was not an antisemite. He also emphasised that he was not a member of the Left Party. “Neither my background nor my position on the Palestinian question justifies placing me under blanket suspicion,” the statement said. He rejected the accusation that he supported terrorism. He continued: “My criticism is directed at the Israeli government and its occupation policy, not at Jewish people or the Jewish religion.”
