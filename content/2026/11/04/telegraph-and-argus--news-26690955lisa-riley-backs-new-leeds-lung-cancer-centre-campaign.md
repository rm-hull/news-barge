Emmerdale star Lisa Riley has backed a new specialist cancer centre opening in Leeds, saying it could help put Bradford and Yorkshire "at the forefront" of lung cancer care.

The actress is supporting a new lung cancer awareness campaign after losing both her grandmother and former Emmerdale co-star Leah Bracknell to the disease.

Speaking ahead of the launch of the new GenesisCare centre, Ms Riley said she felt compelled to get involved after learning more about the impact of lung cancer and the misconceptions that continue to surround it.

## "I always thought the biggest killer in women was breast cancer - and it's not"

Ms Riley said she was surprised to learn that lung cancer remains the biggest cancer killer of women in the UK.

She said: "Before I started working with GenesisCare, I always thought the biggest killer, and certainly the biggest killer in women, was breast cancer and it's not. It's lung cancer.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21659850.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Lisa Riley described the new service as a "powerhouse of the North". *(Image: James Paisley Chapman)*

She added: "I've gone to work at Emmerdale and I've been saying to all the girls going, 'You know, it's lung cancer.'"

## "I don't want anybody to feel that they can't phone their GP"

The campaign is deeply personal for Ms Riley, whose grandmother died from lung cancer after initially dismissing symptoms and delaying a diagnosis until the disease had become advanced.

Recalling the experience, she said: "I was the one that went with her to the clinician and when I'm sat next to her and I see the actual X-ray, the mass was enormous.

Ms Riley added: "I don't want anybody to feel that they can't phone their GP, that they feel they're wasting their GP's time. The sooner you get diagnosed, the longer life you have."

## "Things like that don't happen to Leah"

Ms Riley also reflected on the death of former Emmerdale actress Leah Bracknell, who died in 2019 aged 55 after being diagnosed with lung cancer despite never having smoked.

She said: "When we used the word fit, that was Leah. She was going all over the world on these brilliant yoga retreats. Things like that don't happen to Leah."

Ms Riley said Leah's death demonstrates why people should not assume lung cancer only affects smokers.

She added: "[Leah] died so young from lung cancer. That's proof in the pudding that it can happen to anybody."

## "You're not safe just because you've never smoked"

The campaign comes alongside the launch of a new Lung Cancer Rapid Response Pathway at GenesisCare's new Leeds centre, which aims to speed up diagnosis and treatment planning.

Dr John Robson, GenesisCare's National Lung Cancer Lead, said misconceptions about the disease remain widespread.

He said: "About one in 10 people that are diagnosed with lung cancer are non-smokers...the important thing is that people understand that you're not safe just because you've never smoked."

Dr Robson said symptoms can include a persistent cough, coughing up blood, breathlessness, chest pain and unexplained weight loss. He added that early diagnosis remains one of the most important factors in improving outcomes.

## "We're going to be at the forefront"

The new Leeds centre will provide specialist assessment, advanced imaging, diagnostics and treatment planning through a coordinated service designed to reduce delays for patients.

Ms Riley believes it could have a significant impact on people across the region.

She said: "I'm so proud to be Northern. We do get overlooked...but where treatment's concerned, we're going to be at the forefront."

Speaking about the clinicians involved in the project, Ms Riley added: "They said to me, 'We are going to revolutionise lung cancer' and I'm going, 'Wow, that's a bold statement.'"

## "A real cancer powerhouse of the North"

Ms Riley said the facility could become a major asset for patients across Bradford, Yorkshire and beyond.

She said: "The new lung cancer diagnostic service at GenesisCare in Leeds will be a real cancer powerhouse of the North, offering hope and positivity to people all across the North and beyond with access to a gold-standard diagnosis and treatment pathway."

Anyone experiencing a persistent cough, unexplained breathlessness, chest pain, coughing up blood, unexplained weight loss or ongoing fatigue is urged to seek medical advice promptly.
