Residents in a Scottish Borders town woke up to discover three streets had been spray-painted with anti-migrant messages.

Police have launched an investigation after three spots of graffiti appeared in Galashiels overnight - one on Douglas Bridge, on Channel Street outside the Great Tapestry of Scotland, and at the roundabout near to Gala Water Retail Park.

The messages appear to have been done with spray paint and read "Stop the boats".

Police believe that the incidents took place at around 4am on Tuesday, November 3.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21668212.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The graffiti on Douglas Bridge *(Image: Supplied)*

Constable Mark Froud said: “Our enquiries are ongoing and I am appealing to anyone who has any information about those responsible to get in touch.

“I would also ask anyone who was in these areas around the time, and may have dash-cam or private CCTV that could assist, to contact us.”

Councillor Neil MacKinnon, Green Councillor for Galashiels and District, said he is "appalled and saddened to see this type of graffiti in Gala".

He added: "Stop the boats is a racist trope, used by those who hate all immigration and those from different races and cultures. The presence of far right parties such as Reform in our politics has emboldened these people, it's like it's OK to be racist again.

"Given that small boat arrivals are about 40% down on last year means that this graffiti, apart from being racist, is also completely off-key. The country faces far bigger challenges than a few thousand people arriving on our shores.

"You could stop all immigration tomorrow and the life of the person who has drawn this wouldn't improve one bit. Hopefully they were spotted by CCTV and the police can apprehend them."

Anyone with any information is asked to contact Police Scotland on 101 quoting reference 0624 of November 3.
