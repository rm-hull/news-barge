Plans to convert a former care facility into an 11-bedroom house in multiple occupation (HMO) have been dismissed at appeal by a Government planning inspector.

The national Planning Inspectorate has thrown out an appeal against Sunderland City Council’s refusal of a proposed multiple-occupancy house at 12 Godfrey Road, Grindon.

The plans, which aimed to convert the property into “co-living” accommodation aimed at young professionals, sparked strong local opposition with 40 objections and a petition signed by 470 people.

Residents’ concerns referenced noise, antisocial behaviour, traffic, waste and the impact on neighbours, while 20 letters of support were also submitted backing plans to bring the “eyesore” building back into use.

Applicant Metters Properties, an accredited landlord with the council, agreed to the HMO being “single occupancy” per room and said an antisocial behaviour complaint policy would be put in place.

Although the HMO plans were recommended for approval by council planners at a meeting on June 10, 2026, councillors voted to refuse the residential scheme.

Refusal reasons were linked to the number of HMO occupiers and associated comings and goings from pedestrians and vehicles being “likely to contribute to significant incidences of noise and disturbance” experienced by neighbours.

A representative for the applicant, speaking at the crunch planning meeting earlier this year, said the applicant was “born and bred in Grindon” and added the HMO proposal was the most feasible option for the site.

An appeal was later lodged against the council refusal and a planning inspector was appointed by the Secretary of State to rule on the matter.

In a decision notice published on October 30, 2026, the planning inspector said the area was “not suited to the introduction of such a large single HMO” and dismissed the appeal.

While noting the HMO would result in an “effective use” of the unused building, it was noted that the scheme’s benefits would be “likely to be substantially outweighed by adverse effects”.

This included the “level of intensity” from 11 HMO residents resulting in “an unacceptable increase in noise and disturbance, particularly during more unsociable hours with regard to vehicles coming and going to the property, in what appears to be a particularly quiet housing area”.

The planning inspector added that the scheme’s adverse impacts continued to outweigh its benefits, even when considering the potential for additional conditions in relation to a “more restrictive management plan.”

Councillor Stephen O’Brien, ward representative for Grindon and Thorney Close, said the appeal ruling was a “victory for common sense and for the people living nearby”.

He added: “Local people deserve to enjoy peace and quiet in their own homes. This proposal was simply unsuitable for this location.
