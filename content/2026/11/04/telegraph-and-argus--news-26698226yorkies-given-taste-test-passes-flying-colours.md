"It'll be four minutes for the fish, is that ok?" said the smiling assistant at Yorkie's chippy on Skipton High Street.

Not a minute more, not a minute less and it was in my greedy hands – a sturdy, leak-proof box of battered haddock on a bed of thick crispy chips.

Salted and vinegared, it was into the Autumn sunshine to discover why Yorkie's is shortlisted for best newcomer of the year in the National Fish and Chip Awards.

The shop's stylish and clever interior had plenty of seating and the outside tables were busy, but for me, fish and chips is street food, so I leaned against a tree on the setts and gave it a go.

First things first and this was a good "fighting batter," the sort that travels well.

Copper-coloured and pleasingly bubbly, it was strong enough so that lifting the 10ins fish by one end (without breakage) revealed square cut chunky chips that might have been passed by quality control.

![](https://www.cravenherald.co.uk/resources/images/21669268.jpg?type=mds-article-620)And, then they were gone *(Image: Chris Tate)*

It's all about the crunch with fried fish and this batter was exceptional – not too thick, not greasy, just a deafening bite and then melt-in-the-mouth.

Top marks.

The encased haddock was succulent, thick-flaked, with plenty of true taste and no interference from the batter's light flavour, which indicates Yorkie's must change its beef dripping very regularly, given its volume of customers.

The lovely chips ticked all the clichés – crisp and dry, fluffy in the middle, good with children, caring and considerate etc.

But, and it's a very tiny but... they were almost too perfect, a little too "gastro-pub" perhaps.

Ain't a damn thing funny, it's all about money and at £11.50, there was no arguing with the value of such a high quality offer from Skipton's promising youngster.

The other good reason for eating fish and chips in the street is that people can't resist asking you a) "Looks like you're enjoying that?" and b) "Any good?".
