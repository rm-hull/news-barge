**Three prominent business groups in the Scottish capital have urged councillors to use visitor levy funding to ‘move forward’ with plans to regenerate George Street.**

Essential Edinburgh, Edinburgh Chamber of Commerce and the George Street Association have written to councillors on the Transport and Environment Committee, asking them to take action after more than a decade of consultation.

The organisations have asked councillors to use money generated by the capital’s visitor levy to fund improvements to the city centre thoroughfare.

The 5 per cent nightly tariff has been criticised by some city businesses since it came into effect in July 2026.

The most recent intervention comes as a report about plans for George Street and the wider city centre is set to be considered by councillors on November 12.

The document, authored by corporate director of place Gareth Barwell, states: “It is recommended the initial £20m of Visitor Levy funding allocation, followed by a further £11m if approved post 2029, be allocated as follows: Princes Street - £10m and George Street - £20m.”

“I am delighted that the Visitor Levy enables the upgrading of the New Town public realm to come to fruition after years of consultation,” Denzil Skinner of Essential Edinburgh remarked.

“This is an exciting moment, and I would encourage elected members to support the proposals.”

Liz Dalton (nee McAreavey) of Edinburgh Chamber of Commerce added: “The building of Edinburgh’s New Town in the mid-1700s was visionary leadership, with George Street an elegant statement of the city’s ambition.

“Continuing this vision is essential for our future success, with George Street evolving to be that statement of our continued ambition to maintain our world-class status.”

The George Street Association’s William Duncan similarly remarked: “It’s now ‘make your mind up time’ for the city council’s ambition to upgrade the city centre.

“After decades of debate, there is a ready to go George Street plan with widespread business support and funding – there is no rational reason to delay a decision to proceed.”

![Mr Smith is the chief executive of Essential Edinburgh.](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21316427.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Roddy Smith, chief executive of Essential Edinburgh, previously called on the council to use visitor levy funds to improve the public realm. *(Image: NQ)*

The trio's comments echo those made by Roddy Smith, chief executive of Essential Edinburgh, to The Herald earlier this year.

At the time, Mr Smith remarked: "Where do tourists go in the city? They're in the New Town and then they're in the Old Town.

“That's where the majority of the money needs to be spent. And if it's not, then we, the other business and tourist organisations, will potentially challenge it."

The groups’ joint letter to the council similarly contends that public realm spending should be increased, and that the visitor levy should be utilised to generate the necessary cash.

According to figures compiled by Essential Edinburgh, hotels located in the New Town BID offer almost 3,000 rooms, representing potentially as much as £20m in annual tax revenue, or an estimated 40 per cent of expected visitor levy income.

Furthermore, the 650 businesses located within the BID pay around £100m in non-domestic rates each year.

The letter explains: “In just two years, visitor levy funds raised in the city centre will more than cover the anticipated, £25-30 million, investment to George, Hanover & Frederick Streets, after which those funds will be available to benefit the wider area, including Princes Street.

“Delivery of this project could easily take four years.”

It goes on to state: “George Street has been fully (and exhaustively) consulted upon. The proposals are ready to go should elected members give them the go ahead now.

“We urge elected members to press on with the implementation of George Street and to use the window of opportunity provided by the lengthy privately funded current construction works on Princes Street, to consult upon and refine the right proposals to help Princes Street enhance its place as one of the finest streets in Scotland."
