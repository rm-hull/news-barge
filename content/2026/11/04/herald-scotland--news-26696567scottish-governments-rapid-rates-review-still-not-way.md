The Scottish Government's "rapid" review of business rates rises has still not got off the ground almost five months after it was announced.

A written answer to the Scottish Conservatives shows ministers have only now written to individuals inviting them to join the panel.

They also confirm that even if appointments were confirmed this month, the panel would not report before February, leaving little time to act before bills are issued for the new financial year in April.

Scottish Conservative business spokeswoman Rachael Hamilton said the approach "sums up where supporting businesses lies in the list of SNP priorities".

The delay comes as The Herald’s Reset the Rates campaign calls for fundamental reform of a system that business owners say is unfair, unpredictable and increasingly unaffordable.

The Scottish Government commissioned the review after coming under pressure to halt a rates revaluation which saw some firms face eye-watering hikes.

Wunderbar in Glasgow's Merchant City said its annual bill increased by almost 500%.

Ministers refused to pause the revaluation, which came into force in April, but Deputy First Minister Jenny Gilruth said a review would be commissioned into over-the-odds increases in the levy.

"I am today announcing that the Government will establish an independent panel to undertake a rapid review of the outcomes of the 2026 re-evaluation," she told MSPs in June.

Ms Gilruth said at the time that the panel would examine and report on "allegedly anomalous valuations and their overall impacts" and make recommendations on how they could be addressed.

In a written question, Conservative MSP Tim Eagle asked the government when it would "finalise appointments to its non-domestic rates (NDR) independent rapid review panel, and when the panel is expected to commence its work".

Public Finance Minister Hannah Goodlad replied: "I have written to a number of individuals with appropriate expertise and skills inviting them to become members of the independent review panel, which will examine the outcome of the 2026 non-domestic property revaluation.

"Parliament will be informed when the appointments are confirmed."

The minister added: "The review is independent of Scottish Government and panel members will be asked to report within three months of their appointment. How the panel takes forward the review is a matter for the members to decide."

Ms Hamilton said the Deputy First Minister needed to "come clean".

She told The Herald: "The SNP's eye-watering rise in business rates has meant many firms have called last orders for the last time, yet ministers are still missing in action.

"Jenny Gilruth promised this shambolic process would be reviewed urgently but over four months on members have still not been appointed to this panel.

"This sums up where supporting businesses lies in the list of SNP priorities, who repeatedly ignore their interests.

"The Finance Secretary must come clean on why this review is moving at anything but a rapid pace and confirm when businesses can expect this system to finally be overhauled."

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: "The Minister has written to prospective panel members and Parliament will be informed when the appointments are completed.

"The review is independent of Scottish Government and panel members will be asked to report within three months of their appointment."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21585858.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Herald launched the campaign a week ago *(Image: Damian Shields/ Newsquest)*Last month, The Herald, with the support of more than 20 influential company leaders and groups from across the business community, wrote to First Minister**John Swinney** and Prime Minister Andy Burnham asking for six changes to make the business rates system fairer, simpler and more sustainable.

They include a national assessor to replace the current 14, adjusting the small business bonus scheme thresholds so small firms stay within its scope, and a fairer deal for hospitality and tourism accommodation, where turnover is taken into account in valuations.

The Herald is also calling for action to stop businesses facing sudden, unaffordable jumps in valuation at future revaluations.
