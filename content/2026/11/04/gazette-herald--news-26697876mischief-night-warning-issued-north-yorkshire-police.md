North Yorkshire Police has warned parents and guardians to set out clear rules for children on 'Mischief Night.'

Whilst the night, which takes place today (WED), can be exciting, it can also bring an increase in anti-social behaviour, criminal damage and nuisance behaviour.

Police call on parents and guardians to speak with their children about making safe and responsible choices and to be aware of where they are spending their time.

Their key advice is to know where their children are and who they are with.

They also encourage young people to respect their local community and neighbours

And children should be reminded that criminal damage, vandalism, egg throwing, fireworks misuse, and other anti-social behaviour can have serious consequences

They should also have clear expectations about behaviour with an agreed suitable time for them to return home.

Furthermore, police advise parents to discuss the dangers of carrying or misusing fireworks and other items that could cause injury or damage.

They should also encourage children to take part in positive activities and spend time responsibly with friends.

And they should report any concerns about vulnerable young people who may be at risk of becoming involved in anti-social behaviour.

North Yorkshire Police say emergency incidents can be reported on 999.

Non-emergency incidents can be reported on 101 or the force website's online reporting portal.

Anti-social behaviour can also be reported to your local council where appropriate.

Information can also be provided anonymously through Crimestoppers on 0800 555 111 or via their website.

North Yorkshire Police added: "We want everyone to enjoy the seasonal celebrations safely and respectfully. By working together as parents, residents, schools, community groups, and local agencies, we can help reduce anti-social behaviour and keep our neighbourhoods safe for everyone.

"If you witness anti-social behaviour or criminal damage, please report it.

"Your information helps us identify problem areas and take positive action to protect our communities."
