Fewer than half of Scots trust the Scottish Government, with almost as many saying they actively distrust it, according to new official figures.

The 2025 Scottish Household Survey found that just 46% of adults trust the Scottish Government, compared with 45% who distrust it.

Trust in the Scottish Government has fallen by nine percentage points since 2022, one of the biggest declines among seven major public institutions surveyed.

The Scottish Government also recorded the highest level of distrust of any institution, followed by local councils at 41%.

The findings, published on Wednesday, show that public confidence in government is considerably lower than in other public services.

Almost three-quarters of Scots (74%) said they trusted the health system and police, while 62% trusted the education system.

Trust in the justice system, which includes courts, lawyers and prisons, stood at 58%, while 56% trusted the civil service.

Local government was trusted by 50% of adults, compared with 46% for the Scottish Government.

The figures also revealed that 25% of Scots distrusted the justice system, while 24% distrusted the health system.

Distrust in the civil service stood at 22%, compared with 20% for education and 19% for the police.

The survey also measured "net trust", which is the difference between the proportion of people who trust an institution and those who distrust it.

The Scottish Government recorded a net trust score of just one percentage point, with researchers finding no statistically significant difference between those who trusted and distrusted it.

By contrast, the police recorded the highest net trust score, at 54 percentage points.

Trust in all seven institutions surveyed has fallen since 2022, with the Scottish Government and local councils recording the largest declines, both down nine percentage points.

Distrust in local government has also risen from 35% in 2023 to 41% in 2025, while distrust in the health system increased from 18% to 24% over the same period.

The figures revealed differences between age groups, with younger adults generally more likely to trust public institutions than older people.

Some 55% of adults aged 16 to 34 trusted the Scottish Government, compared with just 37% of those aged 60 and over.

Disabled adults were less likely to express trust across all seven institutions, while confidence was also generally lower among people living in Scotland's most deprived areas.

However, the report suggests the overall decline in trust may be beginning to stabilise.

Trust in the Scottish Government, police, civil service and education system remained broadly unchanged between 2023 and 2025.

Despite this, distrust increased across six of the seven institutions, with fewer people saying they did not know whether they trusted them.

The findings come from the Scottish Household Survey, which has been carried out annually since 1999 and asks people about a range of issues affecting life in Scotland.

Questions about trust in public institutions are included every two years.

The Scottish Conservatives said the figures showed people were "sick and tired" of the SNP.

The party's deputy leader Rachael Hamilton MSP said: “This damning survey confirms more and more Scots are sick and tired of this incompetent and out-of-touch SNP government.

“They have spent their two decades in power addicted to secrecy and cover-ups and have focused on fringe obsessions and pushing their plans to break up the United Kingdom.

“That has resulted in Scotland’s public services crumbling and Scots being forced to pay more but get less.

“It is hardly surprising trust is plummeting when the SNP repeatedly break their promises, spread misinformation and fail to focus on Scotland’s real priorities.”

The latest figures were published by the Scottish Government as official statistics.

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: “It is encouraging that levels of trust in the Scottish Government, the Civil Service, police and education have remained stable since 2023, with trust in the justice system improving.

“It is also encouraging to see that the Carnegie UK 2026 ‘Life in the UK: Scotland Report’, published this week, finds that the proportion of people who had ‘high’ trust in the Scottish Government had increased by six percentage points between 2025 and 2026.

“Ministers are focused on the Scottish Government’s four key priorities: eradicating child poverty, growing the economy, tackling the climate emergency, and delivering high quality and sustainable public services. Delivering on the actions people care about will build trust in the government.”
