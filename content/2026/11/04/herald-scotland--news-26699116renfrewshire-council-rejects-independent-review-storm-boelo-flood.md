**Renfrewshire Council has been accused of "marking its own homework" after rejecting an attempt to have an independent figure lead a review of the flooding in Renfrew and the closure of the Renfrew Bridge during Storm Boelo.** At a meeting of the Infrastructure, Land and Environment Board,**on Wednesday, a report** revealed that SEPA issued no flood warning before the storm hit on October 24 and 25, even though the Clyde later reached its highest recorded level since digital records began in 1998.**Read More: Renfrew Bridge to close for months after flooding damage**The extreme conditions led to flooding affecting 13 businesses in** Renfrew**and caused extensive damage to the southern plant room controlling the £117 million** Renfrew**Bridge,** leaving the swing bridge inoperable for months** while investigations and repairs continue.

At the meeting, opposition spokesperson for Renfrewshire Council's Infrastructure, Land and Environment (ILE) board, councillor Ben Smith, proposed an amendment calling for an external person to oversee the upcoming review. Councillor Iain McMillan seconded the proposal.

The board's SNP convenor did not accept the amendment, prompting a vote. Nine administration councillors voted against the proposal, while six opposition councillors backed it.

Following the vote, Labour Cllr Smith criticised the decision, arguing that residents would expect greater independence given the scale of disruption caused by the storm.

He said: "This is a disappointing decision the administration have taken. Given the severity of the damage caused by Storm Boelo and the disruption caused by the closure of Renfrew Bridge, I think residents will be surprised that the council will be marking its own homework following these events.

"It's clear that residents across Renfrewshire want answers as to why the flood barriers weren't raised and how the new Renfrew Bridge's plant room could have been at risk of flood damage.

"The Labour group think appointing an external person to lead this review would have been far more appropriate. Sadly, the SNP administration do not agree."

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/19431858.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: NQ)*

Leader of Renfrewshire Council, Iain Nicolson, said Cllr Smith's claims were "patently untrue" and added that the flood alert system has "proven to be fundamentally flawed".

He said: “The review agreed at board involves multiple external partners independent of the Council such as Scottish Environment Protection Agency, the UK MET office, Scottish Government and various utility companies who are all involved in the West Resilience Partnership who co-ordinate our emergency responses.

"Cllr Smith statement that it’s the Council marking its own homework is a direct attack on council officers and patently untrue. The Renfrew Bridge is still under contractual obligations and covered by an existing warranty scheme.

"The Council will undertake a comprehensive review into the circumstances of the flooding in Renfrew which will be done in partnership with the Scottish Environment Protection Agency (SEPA) and other stakeholders to understand why the actual level of the River Clyde differed so significantly from forecast levels."

"As part of the review, we will consider commissioning independent expertise in flood risk, hydrology and coastal modelling. We can then look to identify any improvements we can make as a council to strengthen our own arrangements.

"We as an SNP administration are focussing our efforts on getting the Renfrew Bridge open as quickly as possible and bringing in changes as quick as possible to the Renfrew barrier alert system which after all was introduced by a previous Labour Council in 2013 and has proven to be fundamentally flawed.”

The review is expected to examine the council's response to Storm Boelo, which caused significant flooding across parts of Renfrewshire and damaged the recently opened Renfrew Bridge.

Officials say the flood barriers were not activated because established procedures, based on previous flood events, risk assessments and operational experience, did not indicate intervention was necessary. The council notes that since 2016, 45 amber and red weather warnings have affected the Strathclyde area without any flood alerts reaching the activation threshold.

Of the 13 businesses affected by flooding, eight are located riverside of the barriers on Ferry Road and would have been impacted regardless of whether the defences had been deployed, according to the report.

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21633619.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Footage shows the water level inside the gym during Storm Boelo. *(Image: FoundryGym)*

The council said it had offered support and assistance to all affected businesses it could contact, including waste removal services. Ten of the 13 businesses are understood to have resumed trading.

The report also stresses that problems at Renfrew Bridge were unrelated to decisions over flood barrier deployment.

The bridge, which opened in May 2025, remains within its two-year defects period. Contractors and the council's insurers are investigating whether a construction defect contributed to the significant water ingress that damaged operational equipment.
