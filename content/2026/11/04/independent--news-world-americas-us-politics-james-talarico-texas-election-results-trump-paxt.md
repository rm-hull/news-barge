In a historic victory that will rock Texas politics and pave the way for a Democratic majority in the U.S. Senate, Texas voters have elected Democratic candidate James Talarico, becoming the first Democrat in the state to be elected to the upper chamber of Congress in nearly 40 years.

Texas voters also elected Democrat Gina Hinojosa to the governor’s office, flipping the state’s top office held by Republican Greg Abbott and making her the state’s first-ever Latina governor. The state has not elected a Democrat to the governor’s mansion since 1990 — marking yet another political earthquake in a series of them across the country on Tuesday night.

Talarico is projected to defeat the state’s Republican attorney general Ken Paxton, who was endorsed by President Donald Trump despite investigations, a fraud indictment, an impeachment and endless scandals that were an albatross on his campaign.

The Senate race was among the most expensive Senate competitions in U.S. history with Democrats eying the scandal-plagued Paxton as a key target in a campaign to tip the balance of power in Congress and derail the second half of Trump’s chaotic return to the presidency.

Democratic candidates needed to flip four seats, and hold onto their existing ones, to win a majority in the upper chamber. Along with Talarico’s victory in Texas, Democrats also flipped Republican-held Senate seats in Kansas, Iowa, North Carolina, South Carolina, Ohio and Maine.

![Democratic candidate James Talarico is projected to win a seat in the U.S. Senate, marking the first time that Texas voters have elected a Democrat to the upper chamber of Congress in decades and a seismic rebuke to Donald Trump-backed Ken Paxton](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/04/2026-11-03T120041Z_971932456_RC21GOAGPISC_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION-TEXAS-TALARICO.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Texas voters have not sent a Democrat to the Senate since 1988.

North Carolina’s Roy Cooper will be the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in the state since 2008. South Carolina’s Annie Andrews defeated Trump-backed Republican Darline Graham — the sister of late Senator Lindsey Graham — in a stunning rebuke of the president’s agenda. That South Carolina seat has been held by a Republican since Strom Thurmond switched parties — in 1964.

In Kansas, Adam Hamilton is the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Kansas in 94 years.

Trump, meanwhile, has repeatedly mocked Talarico as “Talafreako,” compared him to *Mad* magazine character Alfred E. Neuman and falsely and bizarrely claimed the Democratic lawmaker is a vegan who eats barbecue then vomits from “meat poisoning.”

![Ken Paxton spoke briefly to supporters on election night after Talarico’s stunning victory, among at least six Senate races that saw Democrats flip Republican-held seats](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/04/2026-11-04T043424Z_1533025184_RC2SGOAYITU3_RTRMADP_3_USA-ELECTION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Paxton has also smeared Talarico as “Low-T,” or low testosterone, in an apparent attempt to question his masculinity using right-wing stereotypes that have dominated opposition to Democratic candidates in midterm elections this year.

Trump and anti-Talarico campaigns — boosted by more than $150 million from Elon Musk and political spending committees connected to the world’s wealthiest campaign — also used their platforms and the airwaves to attack Talarico’s support for transgender people.

But Talarico, a Presbyterian seminarian, was breaking fundraising records and energizing voters with a campaign focused largely on a growing cost of living crisis up against a billionaire- and corrupted-corrupted bureaucracy.

Talarico had even been leading several reliably Republican suburban Dallas-area counties that backed Republican Senator Ted Cruz in 2024.

In his victory speech to supporters, Talarico said the campaign against him amounted to “the final howls of a dying establishment” and “the death rattle of a status quo that knows its days are numbered.”

He invoked his Christian faith and urged Americans to embrace a “politics of love” that rejects the Trump era’s “politics as bloodsport.”

“Despite our differences, we all want the same things,” he said. “But the billionaires who own the social media algorithms, who own the cable networks, who own the politicians on our screens, they want us at our throats, ... The real fight in this country is not left vs. right. It is top vs. bottom. … Our unity is a threat to their wealth and their power.”

In a brief concession speech to supporters, Paxton said “we have had a difficult night tonight across the country, but it's not over.”

“So God bless you and thank you for the opportunity to serve as your Texas Attorney General for 12 years,” he said. “Thank you very much.”

![Trump-backed Republican Darline Graham, the sister of late Senator Lindsey Graham, also lost a Senate race in South Carolina, where voters elected a Democrat to the upper chamber for the first time in decades](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/04/03/2302584959..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Republican Senator John Cornyn, the incumbent defeated by Paxton in the Republican primary election earlier this year, congratulated Talarico on Tuesday night.

“The voters have spoken, and I want to extend my congratulations to James Talarico on his election to the U.S. Senate,” he said. “I look forward to meeting him and offering my assistance with his transition as he prepares to serve Texans in the U.S. Senate.

Cruz, who is up for re-election in 2030, opened Election Day by calling Talarico a “radical” who is putting on an “act” as “a nice young man and a moderate.”

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer and Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee chair Sen. Kirsten Gillibrand said they are eager to work with Talarico to “root out corruption, lower costs for all Texans, and finally give families across his state a voice in Washington.”

“An eighth-generation Texan, former middle school teacher, and Presbyterian seminarian, James has always believed there is more that unites us than divides us — and in the state legislature, he fought to make life better for all Texans and lower the cost of healthcare,” they said in a statement Tuesday night. “Now, voters are sending him to the U.S. Senate to continue his fight on behalf of 31 million of their neighbors. James will be a true champion for all Texans in the Senate and take back power for working people.”
