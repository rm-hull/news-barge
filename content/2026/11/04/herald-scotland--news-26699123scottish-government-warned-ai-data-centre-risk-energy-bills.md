Documents uncovered through freedom of information by a campaign group have revealed that the Scottish Government was warned by a senior civil servant of risks, including ‘rising energy bills for locals’, from green AI data centres.

An email to one of the government’s working groups describes the need for a ‘clear system to protect locals’ and also warns of possible water shortages.

Campaigners have reacted with infuriation, noting that the civil servant " was highlighting very significant concerns back in March, and yet nothing seems to have filtered through".

The FOI of communications from the Green Data Centre and AI Growth Zone Crosscutting Working Group, covering the period from November 2025 to June 2026, included this email amongst other documents.

The email, from a senior civil servant, the Deputy Director of Domestic Climate Change at the Scottish Government’s Directorate for Energy and Climate Change, highlights a number of risks.

The letter states that "there are clearly significant impacts and risks and, considering our experience of just transition work over the last few years, I have various concerns about the way forward".

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21519677.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Jane Barlow/PA Wire)*

It talks about the likelihood of "rising energy bills for locals", saying: "There is a clear just transition risk here, considering the prevalence of fuel poverty in the country already- and I’m conscious that is about to get worse as energy bills rise in the wake of the middle east conflict."

It also notes that, depending on location, there is a risk “that locals bear the brunt of increased electricity prices- or even a shortage of energy, or unreliable power- while seeing little direct benefit from ‘hosting’ the data centre."

The deputy director, name redacted, therefore suggests that there needs to be “a really clear system for protecting locals from increased energy prices and power shortages, as part of a deal to host the centre”. “That’s not just about grid upgrades,” the email says, “it’s about demand and supply."

Since this email was sent in March, local opposition to Scottish AI data centres has gathered into a national campaign, prompting tougher environmental scrutiny and a government planning pause while new national guidance is developed.

The email also observes that water is an issue: "The hard fact is that, even taking data centres completely out of the equation, Scotland is facing water shortages in multiple and growing parts of the country, due to climate change."

"Last year, water shortages in some parts of the country started in April- the earliest ever. And some rivers are already under strain from multiple industries (renewable energy, whisky, agriculture) so that fisheries, biodiversity and the environment are destroyed downstream."

The email notes: “Those effects are already set to become much worse. Add a water-hungry data centre and, even if it is comparatively efficient, it could badly impact other water dependent industries and livelihoods.”

The deputy director adds that “in Australia, data centre construction is dependent on guarantees of proportional work like reservoir investment and other management” and that this “seems like a really fruitful route to explore."

“I would also suggest that planning and impacts assessments take account of the latest Met Office assessments of weather changes to come,” the email adds, “we shouldn’t erroneously start from a baseline of current water availability, but we should build in future increasing shortages.

Anti-data centre campaigners have reacted to the contents of the FOI with “infuriation”.

Lisa Beacham, founder of the Hurlford campaign, said: “The revelation that our energy prices will be most likely increased to host these things while risking our general health is nothing short of exploitation of a deprived area.

"We have been raising the alarm about the potential impacts of hyperscale AI data centres in our communities for months and months while being completely ignored by the Scottish Government. To find out that a senior civil servant has raised issues that also concern us like energy prices, water use and climate emissions shows that the Government are aware of these issues, they are just covering them up.

She added: "Fuel poverty is a real concern for people in our own community and all over Scotland. If the Scottish Government knows about this issue of increasing energy prices and still are ploughing onward with giant data centres, it is an outrage and a grave injustice for everyone struggling to pay energy prices which are already amongst the most expensive in Europe."

Beacham also expressed concern over water shortages. “We have been told by Mark Wilson from ILI Group that water is a non-problem for Scotland while this FOI tells otherwise. Scotland's water resources are dwindling. If the Government wanted to placate us, this has just given us even more motivation.”

![](https://www.heraldscotland.com/resources/images/21454978.jpg?type=mds-article-620) *(Image: Colin Mearns)*

Kat Jones, director of the charity Action to Protect Rural Scotland, said: "It is clear that the Scottish Government simply hasn't considered the climate in their rush to roll out the red carpet for hyperscale AI data centres."

"The Deputy Director was highlighting very significant concerns back in March, and yet nothing seems to have filtered through to local authorities, nor to the MSPs who have been advocating about exactly this on behalf of their local communities.

"This is an area where we have been actively working now for nearly a year and we have never seen any communications from the government nor seen any report or letter where these impacts are even acknowledged. It seems like the Scottish Government have been stonewalling the Deputy Director for Domestic Climate as effectively as they have been stonewalling communities and environmental campaigners."

A Scottish Government spokesperson said: “The Scottish Government will be publishing new planning guidance on data centres before the end of the year.

“Planning authorities are also now required to notify ministers of new data centre applications, with a new planning direction also requiring an Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) to be submitted during planning for all new data centres with a power capacity exceeding 50 megawatts (MW).

“Data centres represent a rapidly emerging sector and planning has an important role to play in considering applications, harnessing economic opportunities, and finding solutions which align with our climate goals.”
