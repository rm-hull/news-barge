“Feckless” burglars who stole 7,000 bottles of gin from a North Yorkshire distillery have been jailed.

The West Yorkshire gang was caught after police swooped on a lorry carrying the £36,000 worth of gin stolen from the Sloemotion Distillery in Barton-le-Willows, off the A64 near Flaxton, in the early hours of November 24, 2024.

A Sloemotion Distillery spokesperson said the incident was “very upsetting”, particularly because it happened “just as demand ramps up for Christmas” which the business depends on “for survival”.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/18807905.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The wagon, left, which police swooped on to recover the stolen bottles, right, on November 24, 2024 *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Ashley Rhodes, Christopher Tearle, Jordan Barber and Joshua Parkinson were all jailed for their part in the burglary at York Crown Court on Tuesday (November 3).

The Recorder of York, Judge Sean Morris, said a prison sentence was inevitable, telling the men the burglaries affected working people who pay tax which “pays for your benefits”. “These people deserve the protection of the police and they deserve the protection of the law, and that includes the court.

“They need to know their hard work is not there to be preyed on by feckless criminals – by feckless, I mean those who can’t be bothered to work and would rather make their money easily by doing crime.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21666881.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Ashley Rhodes *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Rhodes, 30, of Emmet Court, Birkenshaw, Bradford, Kirklees, was jailed for one year and six months.

Tearle, 40, of Road, Batley, Kirklees, was jailed for one year and four months.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21666882.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Christopher Tearle *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Barber, 28, of Gledhill Terrace, Dewsbury, Kirklees, was jailed for one year and three months.

And Parkinson, 31, of Sykes Walk, Soothill, Batley, Kirklees, was jailed for three years and four months after also pleading guilty to an assault, which the judge described as “nasty domestic violence”. Judge Morris also issued a 10-year restraining order prohibiting Parkinson from contacting the victim of the assault.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21666883.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Joshua Parkinson *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

Judge Morris told the four men: “You are the second gang from the West Riding I have had to deal with for professional burglaries in remote spots.”

He said the men had planned the burglary using cloned number plates and “scoping” out the roads and distillery beforehand.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21667003.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Jordan Barber *(Image: North Yorkshire Police)*

“This kind of work means every single person has to play their part – it’s a team effort,” he said, adding: “You join a team, you play with the team, you go down with the team.”

Bradley Quinn, 34, of no fixed address, and Clinton Reeves, 55, of Hazel Close, Dewsbury, are due to be sentenced at a later date for their roles in the burglary after both pleading guilty to the offence.

North Yorkshire Police said it was called to reports of a “group of suspicious men hanging around a farm at Barton-le-Willows” shortly before 12.40am on November 24, 2024.

The men were described as getting into three vehicles – two small cars and a curtain sided lorry – before driving off onto the A64 heading westbound.

Police later found the wagon parked down a lane between the farm and the distillery.

Officers were deployed and got behind the convoy of vehicles close to Bilbrough.

Police said the first vehicle, a Vauxhall Insignia, was stopped and the four occupants, Barber, Parkinson, Rhodes and Tearle, were initially detained.

Officers established that the four men, who were found covered in mud, had been targeting a nearby gin distillery and arrested them on suspicion of burglary.

Meanwhile, roads policing officers got behind the wagon and ordered it to stop.

“The wagon rolled to a stop – narrowly missing a passing car – and two men jumped out of the cab and fled into a field near Bilbrough,” a police spokesperson said.

“The wagon was checked by the officers, and they were pleased to pass on the good news to the distillery owner that all the stolen gin had been recovered.”

They said a police dog caught two more suspects, Quinn and Reeves, after tracking them hiding among bales on a farm after travelling through 6km (3.7mi) of fields.

A third vehicle, a black SAAB, made off over the West Yorkshire border and its occupants were not found by police.

## Gang 'clearly thought they could get away with such a brazen crime', say police

Detective Constable Andrew Govier, of Scarborough and Ryedale CID, said the gang “clearly thought they could get away with such a brazen crime by travelling into North Yorkshire from West Yorkshire”. “They must have got the shock of their lives when our roads policing officers were onto them in a flash soon after the two suspicious activity calls were made to the Force Control Room.

“It shows the importance of reporting information to the police, and we thank the two members of the public for doing the right thing.”

He added: “While we were disappointed that one vehicle and its occupants did get away, we were pleased to arrest the six suspects and very happy to recover the stolen gin for the owner.

“Ultimately, it proved to be a successful joint effort by multiple policing teams.

“The custodial sentences handed to the defendants reflects the serious level of offending that they committed.

“They are now facing the consequences of their actions and, hopefully, regretting having ever set foot in North Yorkshire.”
