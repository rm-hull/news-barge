Seventeen hectares of pine forest are to be cleared in the Baruth region 50 km south of Berlin for the construction of a new Red Bull can factory. The site is fenced off. Reminiscent of the protesters outside the Tesla Gigafactory in 2024, environmentalists are protesting the project due to the planned deforestation but also due to the levels of water consumption required.

According to a spokesperson, work began on Monday. “The zoning plan, which has now come into effect, provides planning certainty for the further development of the production campus,” said project coordinator Björn Hofbauer. “The future building area will be offset by six times its size of new forest and green spaces. 98% of these areas are located in the immediate vicinity of Baruth,” he added. The State Office for the Environment has also granted the requested approval for the prepared measures.

The repeated felling of trees in and around Berlin highlights critisisms of the senate who are behind on their planned Climate Adaptation Act, also known as the “Tree Act”. Which, among other things, would see the city’s residents contribute to the planting of trees at their own expense.

A final court ruling has not yet been reached on an urgent application filed by the environmental association Grüne Liga against the city’s already approved development plan. However, the higher administrative court had previously rejected the application for an interim injunction, with which Grüne Liga had sought to temporarily halt the start of the forest clearing.
