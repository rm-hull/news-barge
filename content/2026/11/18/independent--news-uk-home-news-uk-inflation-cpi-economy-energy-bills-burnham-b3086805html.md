UK inflation rose to 3.6 per cent in October, official figures show, intensifying pressure on households and the Bank of England ahead of next month’s interest rate decision.

Data published by the Office for National Statistics (ONS) on Wednesday morning showed the Consumer Prices Index (CPI) rose from September’s rate of 3.5 per cent – marking the fourth consecutive monthly rise since inflation dipped to 2.6 per cent in June.

The ONS said motor fuel costs and energy costs made the largest upward contributions to the monthly change.

Economists polled ahead of the release had expected headline inflation to climb to 3.6 per cent, according to a consensus provided by Pantheon Macroeconomics – a level last reached in October 2025.

Inflation has been climbing steadily since the summer, fuelled by energy supply shocks in the Middle East which have driven up global fuel and household energy prices and pushed the rate further away from the Bank of England’s 2 per cent target.

At the start of October, energy regulator Ofgem raised its quarterly price cap by 4 per cent, lifting the typical annual energy bill for households in England, Scotland, and Wales paying by direct debit by around £60 to £1,723.

The figures come less than two weeks after the Bank of England raised its base interest rate to 4 per cent on 5 November – its first rate hike in more than three years – as policymakers battle resurgent price pressures.

The inflation rate has since moved further away from the Bank of England’s 2 per cent target, with the central bank warning earlier this month that it is not likely to return to target until the third quarter of 2028.

The Bank of England has warned that inflation could climb as high as 5 per cent in the coming months, prompting financial markets to price in further rate rises beginning at the Monetary Policy Committee’s next meeting in December.

ONS chief economist Grant Fitzner said: “Inflation rose slightly in October, with fuel prices once again a major contributor to the increase in the annual rate.

“Annual costs of both raw materials and goods leaving factories continue to climb, driven by higher crude oil and petrol prices, respectively.”

Rob Wood and Elliott Jordan-Doak, UK economists for Pantheon, said they expect higher household energy bills, vape and tobacco duty increases, and more expensive fuel to contribute to an inflation uptick for last month.

They indicated that tobacco prices are set to have jumped 8.3% in October compared with the previous month, after tobacco duty was increased. The duty rise would add around £1.21 to an average pack of 20 cigarettes, according to HMRC.

“Tobacco duty and motor fuel price hikes should add 21 basis points,” the economists said.

“Ofgem’s 4 per cent utility-price-cap hike should add three basis points to inflation compared to September, but weaker food inflation will, we assume, cut four basis points.”

Energy regulator Ofgem increased its quarterly price cap by 4 per cent at the start of October, taking the energy bill for the average household in England, Scotland and Wales paying by direct debit for gas and electricity up by around £60 to £1,723 a year.

The shadow chancellor, Andrew Griffith, blames the fourth successive rise in the inflation rate on the UK’s "soaring energy costs" and Labour’s decision to "hike taxes and employment costs".

"They didn’t have to do that. But now we will all pay the price," he says in a post on social media.
