## By-election called after councillors banned from council building

Pannett Art Gallery. Courtesy Whitby Town Council.

VOTERS in part of Whitby will return to the polls next month after a councillor resigned amid an escalating governance row that led to two former members being banned from council premises.

A by-election has been called in Stakesby Ward following the resignation of former councillor Jacqui Layman. Polling will take place on December 17 if the election is contested, with nominations closing on November 20. Residents have until December 1 to register to vote.

The vacancy is one of five created after a series of resignations from Whitby Town Council in October.

The by-election follows an extraordinary meeting of the council on October 19, at which members unanimously accepted the findings of an independent investigation into the conduct of former councillors Jacqui Layman and Chris Riddolls.

According to council minutes, members described the findings as “deeply troubling” and said the investigations had identified a “sustained pattern of inappropriate conduct” that, according to the report, had affected council staff, undermined member-officer relationships, and disrupted the effective functioning of the authority.

Councillors voted to formally disassociate the council from the alleged actions of the two former members and approved a series of measures intended to protect staff.

Those measures included banning both former councillors from entering the Pannett Art Gallery and Whitby Museum building, preventing them from meeting or telephoning council officers and authorising the town clerk to seek legal advice about possible injunctions if the alleged behaviour continued.

The council also agreed to fund independent legal advice for affected employees regarding potential civil claims against the two former councillors.

At the time, Ms Layman and Mr Riddolls had already resigned from the authority.

The council minutes state that both individuals had been provided with copies of the investigation reports and given the opportunity to submit written representations or attend the Staffing Committee meeting considering the findings.

However, Ms Layman has strongly disputed the council’s account of the process.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21575224.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Cllr Jacqui Layman, Wtc

In a statement to the Local Democracy Reporting Service (LDRS), she said a council minute suggesting she and Riddolls had been afforded a proper opportunity to respond was “disingenuous to the point of duplicity”.

She said she received the report on October 6 and was asked to respond by the morning of October 12, despite the document running to almost 100 pages and containing around 33,000 words.

According to correspondence seen by the LDRS, Ms Layman, who has denied the allegations against her, wrote to the town clerk on October 8 requesting more time to respond and asking for additional evidence relating to some of the report’s conclusions.

She said: “The clerk did not reply to my email and there is no mention in the minutes that it was considered by council, or even that they were made aware of it.”

Ms Layman has denied the allegations, and Mr Riddolls did not respond to a comment request from the LDRS.

The five councillors who resigned were Stephen Smith (Abbey Ward), Alfred Abbott (Ruswarp Ward), Amanda Smith and Jacqui Layman (Stakesby Ward), and Chris Riddolls (White Leys Ward).
