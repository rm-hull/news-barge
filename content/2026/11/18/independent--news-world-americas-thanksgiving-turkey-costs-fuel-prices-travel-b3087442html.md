Record turkey prices and elevated fuel expenses could make Thanksgiving dinner more costly for families this year, according to a survey published Wednesday.

The American Farm Bureau Federation reported that the cost of an average Thanksgiving feast for 10 will rise 6% from last year to $58.63.

The group tracks grocery prices using hundreds of volunteers in all 50 states and Puerto Rico.

The Farm Bureau, which has monitored Thanksgiving food costs for 40 years, said it is the first time since 2022 that Americans will see prices rise. The meal hit a peak of $64.05 in 2022.

High diesel prices have made it more expensive for farmers to process and transport traditional staples such as milk, dinner rolls, potatoes, frozen peas, and cranberries.

Turkey prices have also climbed sharply as consumer demand for protein stays strong while the poultry industry continues recovering from bird flu outbreaks.

The U.S. Department of Agriculture projects wholesale whole frozen turkey hen prices will average $1.76 per pound this year, up 29% from last year.

Another drop expected for Thanksgiving this year is travel.

“After three years of record highs, the number of travelers is slightly lower partly because of higher travel costs this year. But travelers won’t notice much of a difference," AAA Travel Vice President Stacey Barber said in a statement.

AAA put its forecast together weeks before problems with U.S. air traffic control communications and radar feeds on Friday prompted the Federal Aviation Administration to impose a nationwide ground stop.

That ground stop lasted more than four hours and disrupted weekend flights.

It predicted that high plane ticket prices and costly gasoline would deter some Americans from making a Thanksgiving trip, though not enough to noticeably change airport security lines or highway traffic.

AAA is forecasting that 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles (80 kilometers) from home over the Thanksgiving period.
