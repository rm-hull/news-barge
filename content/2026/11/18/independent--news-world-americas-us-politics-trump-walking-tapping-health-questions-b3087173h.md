President Donald Trump’s gait during his Asia trip, along with his repeated thigh-tapping back home, has prompted renewed questions about his health.

While in Manila for the ASEAN summit Wednesday, the 80-year-old president walked a red carpet alongside Philippines President Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr., with a procession of officials following behind. Footage, posted by the White House Rapid Response account, shows Trump straying from a straight path, at one point veering sharply to his right, and stopping several times as Marcos walked beside him.

The footage drew scrutiny and concern on social media, with users questioning the president’s movements.

“He’s all over the place, worse than usual…..and has to keep stopping every few feet,” wrote one X user.

“Is this meant to be something positive for Trump? He's walking like he's on a lobster boat in the middle of a storm,” another wrote. A third asked: “Why does he now walk like a 1 day old foal?”

![While walking a red carpet in Manila Wednesday with Philippines President Ferdinand ‘Bongbong’ Marcos Jr., President Donald Trump strayed from a straight path - and the internet noticed](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/13/23/GettyImages-2305642632.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Others revived unsubstantiated claims that Trump wears leg braces beneath his pants — claims the White House has previously dismissed as “fake news.”

Some, however, defended the president. “Come on, he’s 80,” one user wrote. “I think it’s normal.”

Trump had walked in a similar fashion during his Alaska trip last August to meet with Russian President Vladimir Putin, veering from left to right along a red carpet in Anchorage.

The president’s movements in Washington D.C. have also drawn scrutiny this month. He has been seen repeatedly tapping his right thigh while walking on several occasions, including at a Veterans Day event at Arlington National Cemetery and while boarding Marine One November 16.

The gesture divided social media users, with some calling it unusual and others dismissing it as unremarkable.

“That right leg is not alright. Watch the way he throws his foot out in front of him with every step,” wrote one X user in response to footage of Trump slapping his leg.

“This is not news. Lots of people do that,” added another.

![‘Is this meant to be something positive for Trump? He's walking like he's on a lobster boat in the middle of a storm,’ one X user wrote](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/13/51/GettyImages-2305646700.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump, the oldest person ever to assume the presidency, has faced persistent questions about his health during his second term, including about recurring bruises on his hands, swollen ankles and apparent daytime drowsiness.

An April Ipsos poll found that 59 percent of respondents believed Trump lacked the mental acuity needed to lead the nation, while 55 percent said he was not in good enough physical health to serve as president.

“President Trump’s sharpness, unmatched energy, and historic accessibility stand in stark contrast to what we saw during the past four years when the legacy media intentionally covered up Joe Biden’s serious mental and physical decline from the American people,” White House spokesperson Davis Ingle told *The Independent*.

“President Trump is working nonstop to solve problems and deliver on his promises, and he remains in excellent health,” he added.

Trump has said as much himself. After a check-up at Walter Reed in May, he wrote on Truth Social: “Everything checked out PERFECTLY.”

The president arrived in Shenzhen, China, late Wednesday ahead of Thursday’s first-ever trilateral meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese President Xi Jinping. He is scheduled to return to the U.S. later that day.

Trump has previously suggested that North Korea’s Kim Jong Un could participate in the China summit.
