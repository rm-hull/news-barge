## The Spanish National High Court has admitted a complaint filed by two former employees of Julio Iglesias alleging sexual offences, bodily harm and degrading treatment. The singer has been summoned to testify on 3 February.

A Spanish court opened an investigation into veteran singer Julio Iglesias on Wednesday after two former employees filed a complaint alleging sexual abuse, which he denies.

Madrid's Audiencia Nacional court said in a statement that it would investigate alleged "offences against sexual freedom", a category covering a range of crimes, including sexual assault, as well as bodily harm and degrading treatment.

The decision marks a reversal after an earlier complaint was dismissed on the grounds that Spanish courts lacked jurisdiction over alleged crimes committed in the Bahamas and the Dominican Republic.

The women, a domestic worker and a physiotherapist, allege they were subjected to sexual abuse and other forms of mistreatment while working at Iglesias' properties in the two Caribbean countries in 2021.

## **Judge finds sufficient grounds for investigation**

Following the dismissal of the complaint, Women’s Link Worldwide announced last September that it would take the case directly to the National High Court by filing a criminal complaint against the singer.

The organisation accused him of alleged offences of human trafficking for the purposes of forced labour and servitude, offences against sexual freedom and sexual integrity, bodily harm, degrading treatment and violations of workers' rights through the imposition of abusive working conditions.

Antonio Piña, a judge at Spain’s National High Court, has partially admitted the complaint: he will investigate the alleged offences against sexual freedom, bodily harm and degrading treatment, but has excluded human trafficking and offences against workers' rights.

The judge considers that there is sufficient evidence to open criminal proceedings. In his ruling, he highlights that, in addition to the witness statements, the prosecution has submitted a photograph of one of the employees "showing a bruise on one of her arms, consistent with the possibility of having been grabbed forcefully".

In the same image, the investigating judge adds, "two marks can be seen on one of her thighs which may correspond to a bite mark from another person". According to the complaint, these injuries "are the result of her being forced to participate in acts of a sexual nature".

Iglesias, 83, a Grammy-winning singer who has sold more than 300 million records over his decades-long career, has described the accusations as "absolutely false", saying he has never "abused, coerced, or disrespected any woman".
