Prince Harry visited injured Ukrainian war veterans in Lviv at the weekend

**The Duke of Sussex, whocarried out a surprise visit to Ukraine at the weekend, has said he would rejoin the military if asked, as he warned of the threat from Russia.**

He told the Telegraph, external he thought people were right to be nervous in the current international political climate and said it was time to "double our support" for Ukraine.

There appeared to be warnings about President Trump - with Prince Harry saying there was a big separation between what the American public thinks about Ukraine and the administration's attitude.

Prince Harry, able to speak more freely than working royals, said he hoped after the US mid-term elections it would be "fantastic to have more American support" for Ukraine.

Speaking to the Telegraph's Dominic Nicholls, Prince Harry said he thought the threat to Europe had been there for the past 15 years - and he felt like it had been at war for the past decade.

"Just because there's not drones, bullets, and rockets flying at us, it doesn't mean that we're not at war," said Prince Harry.

Prince Harry had been in Lviv in Ukraine on Saturday and Sunday - and had told the BBC and journalists at events that he thought the "rest of the world will continue to support" Ukraine.

He had told his Ukrainian hosts, about a conflict with Russia that has already lasted more than four years: "You're going to win the war."

In his Telegraph podcast, he called for a similar unity that was being shown by Ukraine, and feared that Europe could be divided and could "turn on itself".

He addressed the shifting position of US President Trump in a way that is unusual for a royal.

"I think there's been a big separation between what American society feels regarding support for Ukraine, which I think is incredibly high and always has been, compared to a certain group, or in, in this case, an administration that was perhaps thinking about it differently," said Prince Harry.

"As of January, things are going to change, so I think we should all be looking forward to that," he said, referring to the US mid-term elections, which saw significant setbacks for President Trump.

The BBC has approached the White House for comment.

Having served in the military in Afghanistan, Harry said he would return if needed for a future conflict: "If asked to sign up again, if the need is there, then I will 100% be there to serve my country alongside my fellow veterans and service personnel."

Prince Harry had received an enthusiastic welcome when he visited Lviv in Ukraine at the weekend, meeting Ukrainian competitors taking part in his Invictus Games.

There were emotional scenes as he was applauded by injured service men and women, some of the 150,000 seriously wounded war veterans in the country.

His visit had been carried out with a press blackout for security reasons - and the night before journalists arrived in the city to cover the visit, there had been air raid warnings that had forced Prince Harry's planning team to head for the shelters.

"Ukraine now is experiencing elements of war that no other country has experienced before," Prince Harry had said, including inventing new ways of treating the growing number of injured casualties.

"War is a terrible thing," he said in Lviv, but hailed the resilience of the Ukrainians who had been badly wounded in their defence against Russia's invasion.
