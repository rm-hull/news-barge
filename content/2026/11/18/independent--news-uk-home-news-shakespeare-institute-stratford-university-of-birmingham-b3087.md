Plans to relocate the Shakespeare Institute from Stratford-upon-Avon to the University of Birmingham's main campus have been put on hold after a protest petition attracted almost 15,000 signatures.

In a statement on Tuesday evening, the institution said: "The University of Birmingham has paused the planned move of the Shakespeare Institute from Mason Croft in Stratford-upon-Avon to its Edgbaston campus.

"Over the coming months, the university will work with the Royal Shakespeare Company, the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust, institute colleagues, students, alumni and other partners to explore the institute’s future."

The university had previously maintained that the long-term strength of the institute depended on being integrated into a broader academic community, with student numbers staying relatively low despite ongoing recruitment efforts.

The proposed transfer encountered criticism in recent days, with the institute’s director, Professor Michael Dobson, announcing he was not party to the university’s decision and would resign rather than assist efforts to relocate the centre and its master’s degree course to Birmingham.

Based at its current site since 1951, the institute features a purpose-built research library housing more than 60,000 books.

![A protest petition attracted almost 15,000 signatures](https://static.independent.co.uk/s3fs-public/thumbnails/image/2016/03/02/18/5929064.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In a statement confirming the plans had now been put on hold, Professor Adam Tickell, vice chancellor and principal of the university, said: “Over the past few days, we have heard very clearly how much the Shakespeare Institute matters not only to the University of Birmingham but also to Stratford-upon-Avon, to our partners at the Royal Shakespeare Company and the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust, and to people who love Shakespeare around the world.

“I have therefore decided to pause the planned move as we explore how and whether the institute can have a vibrant and viable future in Stratford-upon-Avon.

“The university’s commitment to Shakespearean studies has never been in doubt but we need to be honest about the challenges and opportunities the institute faces.

“Interest in studying in Stratford has remained very low for several years.

“At the same time, we have seen significant growth for related subjects on our Birmingham campus and the thriving creative industries sector in Birmingham creates real opportunities for Shakespearean research and scholarship.”

Professor Tickell added: “Over the next six months, we will work with the Shakespeare Institute, the RSC, the Shakespeare Birthplace Trust, and other partners, students, and alumni to ensure that the Shakespeare Institute re-establishes itself at the heart of scholarship, performance and reception for England’s greatest poet in both Birmingham and Stratford-upon-Avon.”

![](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/1232306219..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The petition opposing the decision to move the world-renowned study centre out of its Grade II-listed Mason Croft home was set up by the Stratford Herald with the backing of actors Dame Janet Suzman and Samuel West.

In a message backing the change.org petition, the actors called for a halt to plans to relocate the institute from September next year.

The petition also demanded the university publish the full rationale, financial figures, and Equality Impact Assessments behind the proposal, and enter into “transparent, good-faith” discussions with institute staff, students, alumni, and Stratford’s other cultural institutions.

The actors added in a statement: “For 75 years, the Shakespeare Institute has stood at the heart of Stratford-upon-Avon, moments away from Shakespeare’s home, his church, and the stages where his plays live on.

“Housed in the historic Grade II\* listed Mason Croft, the institute is a world-leading centre for Shakespearean scholarship, bridging the gap between academic research and live theatrical practice.

“To sever the Shakespeare Institute from Stratford-upon-Avon and its home at Mason Croft is to destroy the very thing that makes it unique.”

In a message posted on the petition site, one former student at the institute wrote: “The Shakespeare Institute in Stratford is not just another place, it is a home for knowledge, history and an international community.

“The atmosphere at Mason Croft is one of the most unique things I’ve ever known. Moving the institute out of Stratford would take much more away than the university could imagine.”
