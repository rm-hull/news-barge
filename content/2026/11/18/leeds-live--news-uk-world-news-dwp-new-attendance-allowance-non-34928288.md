# DWP new Attendance Allowance 'non-means-tested' rates confirmed for claimants

## Attendance Allowance from the DWP could increase by 3.5 per cent from April 2027

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) Attendance Allowance could see a 3.5% uplift from April 2027, following plans set out by the Labour government.

The non-means-tested benefit, which supports those aged 66 and over requiring regular care due to a disability or serious health condition, currently pays either £76.70 or £114.60 per week, depending on the level of care required.

Should the 3.5% rise go ahead, weekly payments could increase to £79.38 and £118.61 respectively, amounting to as much as £6,167.72 annually. Work and Pensions Secretary Pat McFadden told Parliament: "Other state pension and benefit rates covered by my statutory review will be increased by 3.5%, in line with the increase in the consumer prices index in the year to September 2026."

Attendance Allowance provides additional financial support for those living with a long-term physical or mental health condition or disability. Crucially, eligibility is not determined by the nature of your condition, but rather by the impact it has on your daily life.

Those who have reached State Pension age and require assistance with personal care, or need someone to check on their wellbeing, may qualify for the benefit, reports Birmingham Live. Importantly, Attendance Allowance is not means-tested, meaning your income or savings are irrelevant to your eligibility. The allowance remains accessible whether you are in employment or education.

Should you be awarded Attendance Allowance, you are free to use it however you see fit to improve your quality of life - there is no requirement to put it towards care costs. Bear in mind, however, that your local council or trust may factor in your Attendance Allowance when calculating your contribution towards care services.

From April, the full annual rate of the new state pension will increase by around £490. The full annual rate of the basic state pension will increase by around £375. The DWP has yet to confirm the full details of the new rates, with the majority of changes due to come into effect from 12 April 2027.
