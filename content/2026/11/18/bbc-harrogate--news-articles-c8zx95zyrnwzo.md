**Grand Theft Auto VI launches on Thursday - and the video game will likely dominate timelines, feeds and debates for years. Leeds may lack the sun-soaked glamour of previous game locations Vice City or San Andreas, but can Yorkshire really claim the title as its own? Some developers believe so.**

There's not much more to be written, read or said about the launch of Grand Theft Auto VI - but bear with me.

Analysts believe Rockstar's action-adventure game could become the most expensive ever made - with estimates putting development costs at more than $1bn (£866m).

The release follows 13 years of work for the global company - which has used the gaming industry in Yorkshire for inspiration.

The team at Rockstar Leeds, which grew out of the city's game design scene, has been up and running for more than 20 years.

Now, the region's wider industry will be hoping the latest title will return the investment to the sector that helped spawn it.

## Home-grown talent

"Maybe we were blessed a little bit there," laughs Jason McGann over a cup of tea in his home town of Harrogate.

He tells me how Rockstar Leeds set up shop in a former church in Stanningley in the mid-noughties.

I ask what the church may have thought about the games they were making and if they would approve.

"Maybe not," he concedes.

After his group of friends had success with their venture Mobius Entertainment, Rockstar was so impressed with their work on handheld games, it bought the company.

Transformed into Rockstar Leeds in 2004, the team was responsible for handheld versions like Grand Theft Auto (GTA) Liberty City Stories, as well as working on the main GTA franchise games.

"The first thing they did was open up the source code - it was amazing," McGann says.

"That was my first job, to take GTA III source code apart and understand it."

![A man wearing a black jumper sits in a dark room.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/99ff/live/a66ddbd0-e273-11f1-b639-73d1df360b76.jpg)

Jason McGann helped form Mobius Entertainment which became Rockstar Leeds

McGann has left the company now but is still hugely excited to see what the new instalment has to show off.

"Some games I would regard as art and it's certainly got the potential to be a fabulous canvas," he says.

"I think people are going to be very surprised at the depth of it.

"I think people are going to be blown away, I don't think they'll have seen anything like it before."

## Fan service

"I'm so excited that I'm concerned as to how my body is going to react when I get that controller in my hand."

It is fair to say Rob Kellas likes GTA.

He is a Leeds content creator and has hundreds of thousands of subscribers on social media.

"I've been playing GTA for nearly 30 years - which is slightly concerning because I'm only 34," he admits.

The evidence of fandom is pretty prominent as well.

One of the fingers that will be getting hold of the console controller to play GTA VI has a Rockstar Games tattoo on it.

![Two men wearing sunglasses stand in front of palm trees on what looks like a beach front.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/8b00/live/aa7117c0-e277-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Rob Kellas travelled to Miami, with his friend Danny, to see the real-life inspiration for Vice City

"I am a die-hard fan, if you couldn't tell," says Kellas about his tattoo.

"I got this while a little inebriated, I won't lie.

"Gaming is an incredibly undervalued artform.

"I'm not going to pretend it's not an enormous industry but it doesn't get the same respect that music, film or TV gets.

"I think the best stories are told through video games."

![A man's hand with a tattoo of the Rockstar logo on his middle finger.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/dd7c/live/32ad66d0-e272-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

Rob Kellas has hundreds of thousands of social media followers - and a Rockstar tattoo

Kellas thinks the inspiration it can have will give young people a boost.

"As a kid back in 2005 - seeing Leeds in the game titles, it was like, 'oh I know this place'.

"I cycled to their office to try and get work experience when I was in high school.

"It means a lot to see this is possible by people who are from where I'm from."

## Side quests

That inspiration to get into games is exactly how Sitara Shefta from Castleford went from eyeing up a physics degree to instead designing game physics.

"Thank you Mario!" she says for the nudge into gaming - just before she beats me at Mario Kart.

"I'm always Princess Peach. This is how I got into it. I thought, someone has got to make this.

"I wanted to understand how games are made and then I realised that there were courses."

![A woman and man hold video game controllers, with arcade cabinest in the background.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/9cc3/live/73608970-e27a-11f1-a094-5fdc692f3e33.jpg)

Sitara Shefta says she has Mario to thank for her career - and I was well-beaten at Mario Kart

After working for video game publisher Electronic Arts and helping to produce some of the Need for Speed titles, she now runs her own company, Leeds-based Pretty Cool Games.

I ask her how it feels to be able to do her dream job, and to do it from Yorkshire.

"It makes me so happy," she says.

"I'm a very proud Yorkshire girl and I think it's so big for the local region to have opportunities to build such important games."

## Prequels

Yorkshire has consistently produced some of the biggest games.

The list is pretty incredible.

It ranges from classics like Worms, to award-winners like Atomfall and Thanks Goodness You're Here, as well as some of the all-time best sellers like Hogwarts Legacy.

It appears that culture of gaming helped Rockstar become what it is today.

But as a global product, can Yorkshire really claim GTA VI?

Ben Hinchliffe, a designer for Rockstar for 13 years, who now works for independent developer Just Add Water, thinks it can.

"Down the road in Leeds we worked on Grand Theft Auto V, Red Dead Redemption 2 and Grand Theft Auto VI," he says.

"So, we can claim it's a game that's made in Yorkshire and we should feel very proud of that."

![A man wearing black jacket over a black T-shirt sits in a video games showroom.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/d819/live/6f107e50-e27c-11f1-99b6-a35a62fa4264.jpg)

Ben Hinchliffe worked for Rockstar for 13 years

Hinchliffe is hoping that, after huge job losses in the industry, a big release can attract more investment.

Ukie - a network for games businesses - believes the UK's industry is worth £8bn, while the Independent Game Developers Association says the industry in Yorkshire & Humber, external generates £253m to the economy.

"These indie teams and developers could be the next Rockstar, that brings more jobs and money to Yorkshire.

"When GTA VI comes out I hope it will bring investors back, which will help the industry grow."
