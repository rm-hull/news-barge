## Historic Yorkshire coastal street named spot 'where England comes alive'

Church Street is well worth a visit if you are in Whitby. *(Image: Getty)*

A Yorkshire street packed with history has been named a spot “where England comes alive”.

World Atlas has released a list of 11 streets “where England comes alive”, with spots all over the country being named.

All of the historic streets are said to “keep the country’s past alive through centuries-old landmarks and businesses that still welcome visitors today”.

Whitby’s Church Street made it onto the list thanks to its coastal location and its many pubs.

## What is Church Street in Whitby like?

On the street, World Atlas said: “Another North Yorkshire market town, but this time on the coast, Whitby's Church Street signified a time when life was centered around the Abbey, as early as 1370.

“Some of the houses date back to the 15th century, when Church Street was called Kirkgate.

“Having maintained some of its over 300-year-old properties, Whitby still has its Old Seamen's Hospital from the 1670s in its decorative maritime design, which healed and educated seamen and their families.

“Have a pint on the banks of the River Esk at the dog-friendly pub The Fleece across the street from the Old Seamen's Hospital, and settle around the fireplace with a pork pie straight out of the oven.

“And off the north end of Church Street, there is the Greens Yard path that connects to the monastic ruins of Whitby Abbey, towering over the town since 1539.”

Church Street can be found at the bottom of the 199 steps that take you up to Whitby Abbey.

There have been homes documented on the street since 1370, and it has grown to become a “vital route” connecting the church to the port and market square.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21726634.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The abbey is found at the top of the steps by Church Street. *(Image: Getty)*

The street is described as being a “vibrant and essential” part of the seaside town and features independent shops and places to stop off for some food.

You can head to Justin Chocolatier to try out some of the shop’s classic fudge, toffees, and chocolates on offer, including fondant-filled Dracula’s coffins.

To pick up some new jewellery, you can take a trip to Ebor Jetworks, with handcrafted pieces such as bracelets, brooches, and earrings on offer.

You will also want to check out the yards dotted along Church Street including Arguments Yard that is frequently visited by tourists.

The street is also home to The White Horse and Griffin, a restaurant offering a range of breakfast, lunch, and main meals, including a traditional Sunday roast every week.
