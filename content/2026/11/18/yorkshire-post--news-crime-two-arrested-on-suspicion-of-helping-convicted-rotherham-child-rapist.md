# Two arrested on suspicion of helping convicted Rotherham child rapist flee country

## Two people have been arrested on suspicion of helping a convicted child rapist flee the country, detectives have said.

Mudasar Hussain, 37, from Rotherham, South Yorkshire, was sentenced to 23 years in prison in his absence at Sheffield Crown Court on Friday.

The National Crime Agency (NCA) said a 17-year-old boy from Sheffield and a 36-year-old woman in Rotherham were arrested on suspicion of perverting the course of justice in relation to his escape.

They were being held for questioning.

Hussain was convicted of 12 counts of rape, as part of the NCA’s Operation Stovewood investigation into historic abuse in Rotherham.

He groomed and then sexually abused a teenage girl between 2008 and 2010.

Hussain failed to appear at court for his trial and investigators believe he has fled the country illegally.

Philip Marshall, Operation Stovewood’s leader, said: “We remain determined that Mudasar Hussain should be apprehended to serve his sentence for the horrendous crimes he committed.

![Mudasar Hussain was found guilty of 12 counts of rape by a jury at Sheffield Crown Court on Thursday, November 12](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/12/15/08/Mudasar-Hussain.jpeg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=536&quality=65)


“Anyone who would consider assisting offenders escape should also know that there will be serious consequences for their actions, which only serve to inflict further trauma on victims.”

Also sentenced on Friday were his brother Ghazanfar Hussain, 42, and Kamir Khan, 37, both of Rotherham.

Khan is also on the run, having fled the UK before the trial. Investigators believe he is now in Pakistan.

Mr Marshall added: “While they may think they have escaped, what Hussain and Khan have now is a life on the run, constantly looking over their shoulders and not knowing who they can trust.

“We will do everything we can to make their lives uncomfortable, until the day comes when they face justice.

“Our investigation into their whereabouts continues and I’d reiterate my appeal to anyone who can help us find them to come forward.”

At the sentencing hearing, Judge Sarah Wright praised the victim of the three men, who is now in her 30s, for her “bravery beyond measure” in coming forward.

The judge said the girl was “cynically and manipulatively groomed” and subjected to a “campaign of rape”.

In her personal statement read to the court, the woman described how she was a “vulnerable, troubled girl” when she was raped by the men between the ages of 13 and 15.

She said she had been a happy and bubbly child until the abuse began.

The woman said: “Piece by piece, every bit of my happiness faded away.

“My dignity was stolen from me.”

The NCA says Operation Stovewood has identified and supported around 1,150 potential victims, securing 60 convictions against 55 people.
