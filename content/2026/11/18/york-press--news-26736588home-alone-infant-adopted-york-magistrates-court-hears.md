A York mother will lose her tiny child to adoption after she left the infant alone and in distress for two hours, York Magistrates' Court heard.

Deputy district judge Helen Chapman said the little girl was found on her own in her cot by a housing support officer.

“She was dirty, with dirty hands and face, wearing dirty clothes,” she said.

“She wasn’t fed and she was in significant distress.”

She needed a nappy change.

The infant had been left on her own for two hours, said Ms Chapman.

As a result of being found in the state she was in, the tiny child was taken into care and after foster placements fell through, the decision was taken for her to be placed for adoption.

“I am mindful that is something you will have to live with for the rest of your life,” the judge told the mother.

“No sentence I can impose will come anywhere near that.”

The mother, from York, who is not being named to protect the child, pleaded guilty to child neglect over a six-day period and skipping bail.

She had failed to attend court on the day she should have been sentenced and was arrested on a warrant and brought to the court in custody.

She was given a 12-month community order with probation supervision and a six-month alcohol treatment requirement.

She was ordered to pay a £114 statutory surcharge.

The judge told the mother: “By trying to drink your way through problems you made them worse.”

For her, Chris McGrogan said there was no evidence that the child had come to physical harm as a result of the neglect.

The mother had been the victim of domestic violence and had mental health problems and issues which she was having to deal with by herself because she didn’t have a support network.

“She would absolutely welcome the assistance and help of the probation service,” he said.

“She accepts she has issues, she accepts she has problems. It is clearly a case where health and assistance is needed.”
