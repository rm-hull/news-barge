A Harrogate councillor is calling for a new hardship fund to protect independent businesses suffering significant financial losses from forthcoming work on a multi-million-pound transport and public realm improvement project in Harrogate town centre.

County councillor Mike Schofield, who represents Harlow and St Georges, has submitted a question to be discussed at today’s North Yorkshire Council's full council meeting over what he says will be the disruptive impact of road works required to progress with the £12 million Harrogate Gateway project.

The Green Party councillor is challenging Executive Member for Open to Business, Coun Mark Crane, to commit to establish a North Yorkshire-wide Construction Disruption Business Hardship Fund.

Coun Schofield, who previously ran The Shepherd's Dog pub on Otley Road, says he first raised concerns about the lack of a business impact assessment for Harrogate Station Gateway in 2023.

"I warned about the potential impact on businesses more than three years ago,” said Coun Schofield.

“Now, with almost two years of Station Gateway disruption approaching, we still need answers about how traders will be protected.

“Having run a small business myself, I know how quickly lost customers can turn into financial hardship.

“The bills don't stop because the road is being dug up.

“What use is a beautifully improved town centre if the independent businesses that give it character have disappeared before the work is finished?"

Coun Schofield is calling for North Yorkshire Council to establish a new fund to provide proportionate assistance to businesses able to demonstrate substantial losses directly caused by Council works.

"This is not about blank cheques, it's about fairness,” said Coun Schofield.

“If the Council's projects cause serious financial harm to businesses, there should be a mechanism to help.

“Businesses need protection before the damage is done, not sympathy after they have closed.”

The Harrogate Advertiser has contacted North Yorkshire Council for a comment.
