National Grid has responded to growing concerns that the UK will be hit with a power outage on Thursday (19 November).

A fabricated poster informing Brits that a “major blackout” is pending due to a “critical fault” has been circulating on social media.

It claims that power on Thursday may be lost in homes, businesses and public services across the country for an “extended period” – urging people to prepare by charging essential devices like their phones and laptops and keeping a torch and spare batteries handy.

![National Grid has confirmed the post is false](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/1161875547..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

While the poster appears to direct people to National Grid’s website, the URL used actually sends users to a page not found (404) error.

On X (formerly Twitter) National Grid confirmed the post, which has been shared tens of thousands of times, is false.

“There is no outage expected,” it wrote. “Please disregard the post and report misinformation to social media platforms.”

Many of the posts that remain on X now come with a community note that tells users the alert image is fabricated and not from National Grid.

National Grid urged Brits to check its official channels for accurate information.

In the case of a genuine power outage, National Grid explains on its website not to open your freezer – as most blackouts are short and your freezer can keep food protected for up to 12 hours without electricity.

“Turn off or unplug any sensitive and expensive electrical equipment,” it adds.

“Electrical surges during power cuts can damage digital devices, such as phones, TVs, laptops, and tablets, so turn them off or unplug them from the mains to keep them safe.

The firm also recommends checking on your neighbours, especially if they are elderly, ill or disabled, and make sure they’re “warm, have enough food, drink, and medicine, and that any medical equipment is working”.

The fake claims follow genuine advice from the UK government to prepare for food shortages and widespread disruption from cyber attacks and emergencies under a new national initiative called ‘Be Ready’.

It marks the beginning of a three-year drive to boost household emergency preparedness, with Defence Secretary Wes Streeting unveiling updated advice for threats such as flooding and wildfires.
