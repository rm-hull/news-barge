The Princess of Wales reached out to comfort a grieving mother who lost one of her newborn twin daughters a few weeks ago, during a visit to a maternity hospital in Mumbai.

Kate said "wow" upon being told Jyoti Karande’s story by a doctor, with the royal mother of three crouching down to offer support to the patient.

The visit took place on the final day of William and Kate’s three-day tour of India, coming after the couple visited a craft school where they block printed their own silk scarves, while the prince later met Hindi film stars at a movie production company.

Ms Karande, 29, gave birth to premature twin girls three weeks ago, but one child died while her surviving twin remains in intensive care; the mother is being treated for pneumonia in the post-partum ward at Nowrosjee Wadia Maternity Hospital.

The princess placed a reassuring hand on the mother’s back as she sat up in bed, saying: "You’ve had a very hard time of it. Is your baby in ICU?" before crouching down to eye level and holding the woman’s hand.

![The visit took place on the final day of the royal trip to India](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/7a43e597d21d0c6cfa6b052a7f8ea593Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MDgzMjky-2.86527673.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She asked: "And how are you doing, are you in pain?" Moments later, Kate added: "I wanted to meet new mothers like yourself."

Prior to leaving to continue her solo tour, Kate told the 29-year-old patient: "Stay strong. Well done, you’ve clearly been very very brave."

She was also introduced to two-year-old Gabriel Princemon, a "miracle baby" who was resuscitated by medical staff for 45 minutes after he stopped breathing following a Caesarean birth at 36 weeks.

![Kate spoke to Merlan Samuel, mother of ‘miracle baby’ Gabriel Prince](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/0879c2e61156e45f2d2484a4bbe42f57Y29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzk1MDgzMjE4-2.86527658.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kate spent time alongside the boy’s mother Merlin Samuel, 31, in the hospital’s counselling room, describing Gabriel as "very adventurous" as he climbed onto a chair next to her.

The princess talked with Mrs Samuel regarding the "trauma" of her son’s medical difficulties after his resuscitation, stating: "It’s not just here," gesturing to her chest, "but emotionally it’s a lot to take on".

Mrs Samuel, 31, said following the visit: "He was a miracle baby. They worked on him for a long time and me and my husband signed a DNR (do not resuscitate) form and even planned his funeral.

"Then within two hours he started recovering again.

"He had many problems; lung, kidneys, everything. But I have seen the miracle of my God and how doctors played an important role. This hospital is like family. When you don’t have any hope left they were there."

The mother-of-one added: "It’s an awesome feeling for our family to meet the princess."
