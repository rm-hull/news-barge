Secretary of State Marco Rubio has tried to calm American nerves over an alleged Iranian plot to target Washington, D.C., with a “dirty bomb” and the recent spate of tech outages.

The secretary was delivering a press conference in Manila Tuesday – where he is part of President Donald Trump’s entourage that will meet with Chinese Premier Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin Thursday – when he was asked about possible threats against the U.S. from outside actors.

Addressing the alleged Iranian plot, which was first reported by CNN, citing seven anonymous U.S. intelligence sources, Rubio was dismissive.

“Let me tell you guys something,” he told the assembled press corps. “Some of your intelligence sources are liars. Others are liars and criminals.

“They’re liars because they’re making up [stuff]. They’re arguing they have access to something they don’t and because they have an email address in the government, they somehow have credibility with you.

![Secretary of State Marco Rubio has dismissed recent reported threats against the U.S., warning the media not to be taken in by intelligence sources](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/download.-3..jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

“So number one, you have a bunch of liars out there that pitch stuff to people. I’m not talking about that reporting specifically, just in general.

“As far that specific report is concerned, the United States is always on the lookout for anyone who tries to do harm to our country and to our people. And we have threat streams every day, and the majority of them are not credible.”

A reporter pressed him on whether or not he was “concerned about an imminent attack on Washington.”

“I am not, but again, we’re concerned everyday about anything that threatens our country,” the secretary answered.

“And sadly, unfortunately, if they’re good at what they’re doing, you oftentimes won’t see it. The intelligence may not gather it, you know, if they’re good at their job or what they’re trying to get done.

“But I feel a lot of confidence in our intelligence services. I have a lot of confidence in our systems on being able to identify these things ahead of time. I don’t think there’s any imminent threat.”

![Last week’s spate of tech outages, which hit Microsoft, air traffic control systems and banks, caused widespread chaos and alarm](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/20/11/AP26320714643957.jpg?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Turning to last week’s tech outages –which hit Microsoft, air traffic control systems and banks over successive days – Rubio said the three disasters did not appear to be connected.

“We have zero evidence or any reason to believe that any of these isolated or individual instances have anything to do with nefarious actors, with cyber hackers, with criminals, with another nation-state, with a malign actor of any kind,” he said.

“We fully acknowledge that this is unsettling to see these reports day after day of some technical failures,” the secretary continued. “We get it. In our own lives, we see it the same way.

“We take these things very seriously. Obviously, we’re always vigilant. We’re always on the lookout for anything that poses a threat to the American people, to the American economy, or to national security. We are always hyper-vigilant about that, and we’re also on the lookout for it.”

Asked if AI might have played a part in the outages, as Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has suggested, Rubio said all of the companies impacted have “full-scale cybersecurity” teams in place devoted to defending them against attacks.

“In terms of the role that AI might have played in it, I don’t want to speculate on it, because again, every one of these instances is distinct and unique, and I would refer you to the companies,” he said, going to to suggest the outages were “actually a positive development” because they demonstrated businesses’ willingness to come forward and report problems, rather than attempt to conceal them to safeguard their reputations.

![Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has suggested AI might have played a role in the outages, an idea Rubio was reluctant to entertain](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/43/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-8-21-37-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

“​​I think we’ve now reached a different era, which is companies – which is a good thing – are now immediately sharing vulnerabilities because they don’t want to be accused of covering up something,” the secretary said.

Trump himself addressed the dirty bomb report in the Philippines Tuesday, saying that, if it proved accurate, Iran “will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before. I hope it’s not [true]. But we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy.”

White House spokesperson Lauren Bis meanwhile told *The Independent* the executive remains “vigilant monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland.”

Iran Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei in turn addressed CNN’s report in a post on X, dismissing it as “baseless” and a “fabricated media narrative.”
