Karoline Leavitt has urged the White House to speak out on the series of tech outages that have hit the United States.

​The former White House press secretary’s plea comes after last week’s global Microsoft outage and rare national ground stop on U.S. flights, as well as disruption to JPMorgan Chase services and United Airlines flights this week.

​Leavitt, who stepped down from her role in August, said on *Fox & Friends* that the White House needs to be clear about the situation.

​“And what I would say to the White House is transparency is key,” Leavitt said on Tuesday. “It’s going to help the country grapple with what’s going on.

​“I saw the vice president in his interview with Laura Ingraham last night saying: ‘We don’t see a crisis coming,’” the former press secretary continued. “I think the White House needs to take that message wide and loud.”

![Karoline Leavitt has urged the White House to speak out about tech outages that have affected Microsoft, JPMorgan Chase and caused a nationwide ground stop](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/12/12/Screenshot-2026-11-18-at-12-23-30.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&trim=10%2C0%2C114%2C1&auto=webp)

​The Trump administration has dismissed concerns over the series of outages.

While on *The Ingraham Angle* earlier this week, Vice President JD Vance said that he did not think that Americans need to “build a bunker or stock up on food or water.”​

When asked about the technological incidents by reporters in Manila, President Trump said: “It’s life, it’s life.”

Secretary of State Marco Rubio went further in a press conference Tuesday, stating there was no evidence the outages are linked, and downplaying concerns about a CNN report claiming Iran was planning a ‘dirty bomb’ attack in D.C.

But speaking on *Fox & Friends*, Leavitt acknowledged that many Americans - including herself - are worried about everyday services being disrupted.

​“Well, I am one of those Americans now, right?” she said. “No longer serving in government, no longer getting those classified briefings about what’s really going on behind the scenes.

​“So as I’m here raising my family, I’m wondering the same thing: What’s happening?” Leavitt added. “I’m checking my bank account with a little bit of worry.”

Conspiracy theories about the disruptions have become rampant on social media, with some accounts noting that several European countries have, in recent months, asked their citizens to stockpile supplies in the event of an emergency.

![Vice President JD Vance has said that Americans do not need to stockpile supplies in case of an emergency](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/11/29/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-10-58-54.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​Leavitt says that Trump himself could help calm anxieties around the outages.

​“I know the president is overseas right now focusing on very important policy matters and decisions, but I think when he returns home, there should be a message directly to the American people - whether it’s from him or somebody else at the White House saying what they know and what they believe to be true to really calm the temperature,” she said on *Fox & Friends.* “I’ve noticed this whole situation has really taken off on TikTok.

“A lot of my friends back home have been asking ‘What’s going on?’ for weeks now,” she added. “So this has really been spiraling for quite some time, and I think again the White House should just address it directly with the American people.”

​Microsoft says that disruption to its services was caused by a “technical issue,” while the FAA has said that the ground stop was caused by a “technical problem.”

![Leavitt suggested that President Trump could speak out on the outages](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/12/2304919096..?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

​JPMorgan Chase said that its services were affected by a “software bug.”

​United Airlines described the disruption as a “technology-related issue.”

​All four organizations have said that a cyberattack was not responsible for the unrelated outages.

​During the *Fox & Friends* interview, Leavitt was asked whether she thinks Americans should stockpile supplies.

​“I think it’s always good to be prepared for any crisis, but as the vice president said last night, he doesn’t see one coming,” she said. “Again, I think if that’s truthful - which, if the vice president said it last night, I’m sure that it is - they should be very transparent and open about that.”
