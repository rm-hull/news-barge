![Jason squatting while clutching two empty slushy cups in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/AY5PhkhnJoMBscu6wdLmKF-1920-80.jpg)

It's here. GTA 6 has been loosed upon the world, and another nation's productivity goes dark as each advancing hour unleashes midnight unlocks on the next timezone. And hey, from the sounds of it, people seem to like what they're finding—and they're finding a lot.

You can mini-golf. You can go on beach dates. You can text and drive (don't). You can doomscroll, and birdwatch, and ride a jetski with a dog. GTA 6 is dense with possible activities, actions, systems, and mechanics, but not everyone is having the best luck navigating the maelstrom of associated controls and button prompts. Allow me to present Japanese X user @Shunep3\_STS, who on this day represents all of us:

"There are so many new controls I can’t keep them all straight," said @Shunep3\_STS via machine translation, "and now I’m that guy chugging two shakes I accidentally bought while walking around in a squat."

There's something magical about this image. To begin with, the scene—like all of GTA 6—looks profoundly, almost grotesquely *expensive*: the delicate feathering of Jason's forearm hair, the pseudo-memoji of Cal in the text message notification, the fidelity of pebbled sand that probably cost more to realize in-game than I've spent on rent in the last three years. It's a tableau of technological excess.

And all of that's being deployed to present a man double-fisting slushies in an empty parking lot as he skulks like an imperiled raccoon. Look at him! Look at his face! He's got his chiseled jaw set like he's Solid Snake, but he's nursing a tummy full of iced corn syrup because the pilot controlling his body got too overwhelmed by which buttons do what.

![Jason flipping off a snake in a terrarium in GTA 6.](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/s28WSVfTT9RYhD2RwAZJVg-1200-80.png)

And he's not alone: Our own Andrea Shearon is one of the many struggling to keep up with the sheer volume of button prompts in GTA 6. "There's so much going on," she said. "I just walk up to people and kind of mash all the face buttons for a surprise. You never know if I'm gonna punch someone or do squats or flip them off."

She's spent a lot of time accidentally making rude gestures at her snake as a result.

Amidst all the breathless praise, clips of psychopathic violence, reactionary outrage-mongering, and general noise coming out of GTA 6's launch, @Shunep3\_STS's moment of shame feels very pure. It's a universal experience: No matter how much a game is hyped up, no matter how much money is spent on making it look as impressive as humanly and technologically possible, no matter how much we might *want* it to, we all inevitably find a way to fumble the controls so badly that the illusion entirely breaks down.

We're all, at some point, a man huddling in the dark, trying to figure out how to put down his sippy cups.
