Government forces killed a militant commander in Indian-controlled Kashmir on Wednesday, police said, days after rebels fighting Indian rule wounded three soldiers in an ambush on a paramilitary patrol in the disputed region.

Police and soldiers cordoned off Zangalpora village in southern Kashmir’s Kulgam district after receiving a tip that a rebel commander was hiding there, officials said. He had escaped a gunfight with troops two days earlier in a neighboring village, where his associate was killed.

Police named the militant as Mohammed Lateef Bhat. They said he took shelter inside a mosque and was killed in the ensuing fighting.

Anayat Ali Choudhary, Kulgam’s police chief, told reporters that the army and police made multiple attempts to persuade Bhat to surrender through a video call, but he refused.

Bhat joined the militant ranks last year and had coordinated several attacks on Indian troops, including last week’s ambush on a Central Reserve Police Force patrol in Kulgam that wounded three paramilitary soldiers, Choudhary said.

Amit Shah, India’s home minister, said in a social media post that Bhat was the “last known active local terrorist” of the Lashkar-e-Taiba rebel group in the region’s Kashmir Valley, the heartland of the rebellion against Indian rule.

The Associated Press could not independently verify the details of the operation.

Last week's militant attack came days after Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi praised the Central Reserve Police Force for recording zero casualties for 12 consecutive months for the first time in 47 years, calling the achievement in a Nov. 3 social media post, “A ZERO that speaks volumes.”

Following the attack, joint teams of police and soldiers launched an extensive search operation in Kulgam, laying siege to several villages in the district that continued Wednesday. Hundreds of suspects were questioned and at least 20 were detained during search operations, officials said.

Residents expressed concern over the ongoing military crackdown, saying the measures had created fear, uncertainty and hardship.

Mehbooba Mufti, Kashmir’s former top elected official and a former ally of Modi’s party, said the areas under siege had virtually “turned into a cantonment following the attack” and described reports from Kulgam as “extremely distressing.”

“Punishing an entire population for the actions of unidentified militants and possible security lapses is deeply unjust and unacceptable,” Mufti said in a social media post Monday. “Collective punishment only breeds alienation and resentment.”

Nuclear-armed rivals India and Pakistan each administer part of Kashmir, but both claim the territory in its entirety. Militants in the Indian-controlled portion of Kashmir have been fighting New Delhi’s rule since 1989. Many Muslim Kashmiris support the rebels’ goal of uniting the territory, either under Pakistani rule or as an independent country.

India insists the Kashmir militancy is Pakistan-sponsored terrorism. Pakistan denies the charge and many Kashmiris consider it a legitimate freedom struggle. Tens of thousands of civilians, rebels and government forces have been killed in the conflict.
