EastEnders and Citizen Smith star Cheryl Hall died without leaving a will.

The Whitechapel-born actress, who passed away on August 9 aged 76, left an estate valued at £5,000, according to probate records.

Her son, Joshua, has been named as the administrator of her estate, following Letters of Administration issued by the High Court on November 11.

Robin Askwith, actor and singer who dated Ms Hall between 1970 and 1973, paid an emotional tribute on X (formerly Twitter).

Mr Askwith said: "What a force of nature she was.

"A really innovative working class charismatic actress coming out of the late 60s. Brave in every way.

"And now I have the sad news she has passed away.

"I am devastated she was my beginning. "RIP Cheryl Hall (sic)."

Fans also shared their condolences.

One said: "Oh that is such sad news. I loved her in Citizen Smith and in so many other shows..Rooms, As Time Goes By...she lit up the screen with energy. (sic)"

Another wrote: "Unbelievably, so sad what an absolute beautiful woman only just recently been watching her back on TV with a rerun of the Lucky Feller with David Jason super talented and will be sadly missed (sic)."

A third added: "It is a sad loss Robin. She was a good actress and a pleasure to watch."

Ms Hall began her television career in the late 1960s, with an early role as Eileen in a 1971 episode of On the Buses.

She appeared as Shirna in the 1973 Doctor Who story Carnival of Monsters.

Her breakthrough role came in 1977 as Shirley Johnson in the BBC sitcom Citizen Smith, alongside her then-husband Robert Lindsay.

The pair married in 1974 at St Andrew’s Church in Richmond, but divorced in 1980, the same year Citizen Smith ended.

Ms Hall later married Robert Potter in 1985, with whom she had two children.

She appeared as Christine in a few episodes of EastEnders in 1988, a year before her second divorce.

Her other notable roles include a recurring part as Sadie in The Bill from 1984 to 1988.

Ms Hall was born on July 23, 1950, in Whitechapel, London.
