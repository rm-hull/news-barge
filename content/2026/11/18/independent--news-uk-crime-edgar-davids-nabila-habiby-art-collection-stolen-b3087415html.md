A friend of former Premier League star Edgar Davids has been jailed in her absence for over two years after stealing his £188,000 art collection.

Nabila Habiby, 39, obtained cash loans amounting to £31,500 by using 37 stolen paintings and artworks as collateral. She had been entrusted by Mr Davids to assist with his relocation from Hadley Wood in Hertfordshire to central London.

The former Netherlands, Ajax, Juventus and Tottenham player believed his collection, featuring works by artists including Paul Insect and Ron English, was being kept in a storage unit in Enfield, north London.

However, the theft came to light only when a supporter in Hong Kong contacted the ex-footballer on Instagram to ask about the backstory of a painting he had recently purchased at auction.

Habiby, a former wealth manager, admitted two counts of fraud in September last year. She subsequently evaded multiple sentencing hearings by making various excuses for failing to travel to the UK from her home in Belgium.

On Wednesday, Judge Alexander Jacobs ruled at Wood Green Crown Court, sitting in Hendon, that Habiby should be sentenced in her absence. He handed her a 28-month prison term after she failed to attend court for a fourth consecutive occasion.

![Habiby was sentenced at Wood Green Crown Court](https://static.independent.co.uk/2022/07/01/15/PRI174017788.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In his sentencing remarks, the judge observed that Habiby "appears to be someone who thinks nothing of breaching the trust placed in her by people who consider them to be friends", adding that she possessed a "streak of dishonesty throughout her relationships with others".

The court heard that Habiby had previously told a probation officer that she and Mr Davids shared a bond "like brother and sister". Addressing this, the judge remarked: "This makes this even worse, she herself viewed the relationship as a sibling relationship, which makes what she did even more hurtful and difficult to comprehend."

A warrant for Habiby’s arrest has now been issued, and steps are being taken to extradite her from Belgium so she can serve her custodial sentence in the UK.

Prosecutor Warwick Tatford told the court: "The allegations are from some time ago, 2014 to 2016, and involve Edgar Davids, a famous footballer of some distinction.

"At the time he lived in north London and was the owner of a substantial collection of expensive artworks.

"The defendant was a friend of his, she performed services for him, and he trusted her to look after his items.

"She abused her position as a friend to essentially steal 37 pieces of artwork which at all times belonged to Mr Davids.

"She pawned them to two brokerages for two loans, falsely represented with the support of false documents that she was the owner of the artworks when she had never owned any of the artworks, the loans were in cash and she defaulted on the loans, with the consequence that the artworks were sold by the brokerages."

The court was told that Habiby had been friends with Mr Davids for roughly two years, periodically staying at his Hadley Wood address. When he moved in December 2014, she undertook to place his artworks, furniture and other belongings into a Safestore facility in Enfield.

She initially obtained a £7,000 loan using two of the paintings as security. When she failed to repay the money prior to the 2015 deadline, one of the artworks was put up for sale by the lender.

Habiby later approached a second brokerage to secure a further loan of £24,500, handing over another 35 pieces from the collection.

The total collection of artwork had been assembled by Mr Davids between 2006 and 2012 through the Opera Gallery in New York.

To carry out the fraudulent transactions, Habiby used an authentic invoice for one of the pieces, altering the documentation to pretend she was a joint owner of the items.

Mr Tatford explained that Mr Davids first became suspicious when a high-value watch went missing from his home, leading him to suspect Habiby might be involved.

The former midfielder formally reported the matter to the Metropolitan Police in April 2017 after receiving the unexpected message on social media from the buyer in Hong Kong.

![Davids played for Tottenham in the Premier League](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/71698404..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Recounting the incident, Mr Tatford said: "He had purchased a painting with Edgar Davids’ name written on the back, he was a football fan, and wanted to know the story of the painting.

"Mr Davids had no idea that the painting had been taken out of his possession at all, let alone sold to somebody in Hong Kong."

Habiby was arrested by police officers in June 2018 at Isleworth Crown Court, directly after being sentenced for a separate fraud in which she spent almost £3,000 on a friend's credit card to pay for rent at a luxury apartment in Chelsea.

During police questioning in 2018, she denied pawning the art collection. She was subsequently extradited from Belgium in 2022 to face trial in the UK.

Following her guilty plea, Habiby made an unsuccessful attempt to withdraw her admissions, causing initial delays to the sentencing process.

She then repeatedly failed to appear in court, providing explanations that included suffering a head injury from luggage en route to the airport, falling ill, and, most recently on Wednesday, claiming an inability to afford the train fare from Belgium.

Rejecting these excuses, the judge remarked: "She has not been entirely honest with those she instructs or the court.

"By her conduct, she has demonstrated no adjournment would secure her attendance unless she is forcibly brought before the court."

Representing Habiby, Hannah Thomas stated that her client had experienced an "unjustified" delay throughout the investigation. She noted that a trial scheduled for 2024 had been postponed by a year due to administrative difficulties in establishing a video link to the United Arab Emirates for Mr Davids to testify, alongside a wider lack of court availability.

It was previously disclosed to the court that Mr Davids has managed to retrieve 11 of the stolen paintings, though the remaining items remain unrecovered.

While he informed police that he retained an "emotional connection" to several of the missing artworks, he did not submit a formal victim impact statement for the sentencing hearing.

Proceedings under the Proceeds of Crime Act to recover the financial gains Habiby made from the fraud are scheduled to take place next year.
