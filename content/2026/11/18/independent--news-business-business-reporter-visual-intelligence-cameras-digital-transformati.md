*Ipsotek is a Business Reporter client*

Cameras were once deployed by businesses primarily to record events and support security teams. Today, the same infrastructure is increasingly becoming a source of operational intelligence: helping organisations understand how people, assets and processes move through physical environments, identify problems earlier and make better-informed decisions.

Businesses have spent decades learning how to extract value from financial, transactional and customer data. A significant part of every organisation also operates in the physical world.

Passengers move through airports and railway stations. Vehicles drive around busy city roads and motorways. Employees work around machinery and critical infrastructure. Goods flow through warehouses. Customers navigate stores, venues and public spaces.

## **The intelligence already exists**

Much of this activity is already visible to existing camera networks.

For more than 25 years, Ipsotek has worked at the intersection of video, artificial intelligence and real-world operations. Across 17 key industries and 44 countries, its technology now processes more than 700,000 hours of video globally, every day.

That scale has given Ipsotek a clear view of how organisations’ expectations of video technology are changing. Its experience has shown that organisations already generate vast amounts of visual data. The opportunity lies in converting that data into information people can use.

AI video analytics makes that possible by applying computer vision and artificial intelligence to video streams, turning activity into structured information and surfacing relevant events, patterns and operational conditions.

This strengthens human decision-making by giving operators and business teams greater visibility and helping them focus attention where judgement or action is required.

## **Start with the operational problem**

One of the most important lessons Ipsotek has learned from real-world deployments is that successful AI programmes begin with a clearly defined operational challenge.

That challenge might be passenger congestion at an airport, unsafe behaviour within an industrial facility, inefficient use of space in a stadium, a security breach at critical infrastructure, or the need to understand customer movement more effectively.

From there, organisations can determine how technology can support the required outcome. But a fundamental question sits at the centre of this process: what decision or outcome should this insight improve?

This principle has shaped the development of Ipsotek’s VISuite technology. Ipsotek focuses on configuring visual intelligence around operational objectives and measurable outcomes, ensuring that AI serves a defined business purpose, while security and safety remain top priority.

The distinction matters. Alerts are most valuable when they provide context that helps someone understand a situation, make a decision or take appropriate action.

## **Getting better value from infrastructure you already own**

Organisations can often advance their digital transformation by unlocking more value from existing infrastructure.

Many businesses already operate extensive camera estates. Where those cameras and supporting systems are compatible, AI video analytics can add an intelligence layer to technology already in place.

For organisations under pressure to improve productivity and resilience while controlling capital expenditure, that can be particularly valuable.

This opens up a broader question for business leaders: “What else can our existing visual infrastructure tell us?”

A camera used for security, for example, may also help an organisation understand queues, occupancy, movement, asset utilisation or operational bottlenecks, depending on the environment and the purpose for which analytics are deployed.

This ability to unlock new value from existing visual infrastructure is one of the strongest business cases for enterprise video analytics.

## **Experience changes what good AI looks like**

AI capabilities are developing quickly, and successful performance in live operational environments depends on far more than technical capability alone.

Ipsotek’s technology is deployed across 17 key industries and 44 countries, processing more than 700,000 hours of video every day across the globe. Working at that scale has reinforced the importance of reliability, integration, governance, accuracy, scalability and the operational context in which an alert is generated.

A transport operator, hospital, energy provider or government organisation needs technology that can detect relevant events while delivering information consistently within a much larger operational workflow.

That is why Ipsotek sees domain experience as increasingly important as the AI market becomes more crowded.

Customers need to understand whether a platform can be configured, governed and scaled in a way that creates sustainable operational value over time.

## **Privacy and innovation should advance together**

Wider AI adoption depends on trust.

Video analytics covers a broad range of applications, many of which can deliver value without establishing an individual’s identity.

Occupancy analysis, queue monitoring, crowd movement, asset tracking, perimeter protection and many operational safety applications can generate useful intelligence by understanding activity and conditions within an environment.

Ipsotek has long advocated a privacy-by-design approach: begin with the operational objective; understand what information is required; and process only what is necessary for that purpose.

That can include anonymisation, secure access controls, appropriate retention policies and clearly defined governance. Where identification is required for a legitimate use case, the appropriate safeguards should be designed into the deployment from the outset.

Innovation and privacy should progress together. Responsible deployment is an essential part of creating technology that organisations and the people they serve can trust.

## **Connecting enterprise AI to the physical world**

Generative AI and agentic AI are transforming how organisations interact with digital information. But businesses also need intelligence about what is happening across their physical operations.

Many organisations operate in physical locations such as airports, factories, hospitals, warehouses, transport networks and cities, where important decisions depend on understanding real-world activity.

Visual intelligence is an important bridge between these environments and the wider enterprise technology ecosystem. Computer vision can provide structured information about what is happening physically, creating context that complements the financial, transactional and operational data organisations already use.

That is why Ipsotek believes visual data is becoming an enterprise intelligence layer in its own right. After more than 25 years in AI video analytics, the company expects the next phase of the market to be increasingly defined by how effectively organisations turn visual information into outcomes.

## **The next enterprise intelligence layer is already there**

For many organisations, the infrastructure required to begin unlocking visual intelligence is already present.

The cameras are installed. Physical activity is already being captured. The data already exists.

The opportunity is to determine which operational questions that information can help answer, and how those answers can improve the decisions organisations make.

AI video analytics is changing how organisations can understand their physical operations and how they derive value from the visual data they already generate.

As enterprise AI becomes increasingly connected to the real world, visual data has the potential to become an important new intelligence layer – providing organisations with greater visibility, faster insight and a more accurate understanding of how their operations are performing.

For Ipsotek, the focus is clear: helping organisations transform video into actionable intelligence so they can understand their operations more clearly, respond more effectively and extract greater value from the infrastructure they already have.
