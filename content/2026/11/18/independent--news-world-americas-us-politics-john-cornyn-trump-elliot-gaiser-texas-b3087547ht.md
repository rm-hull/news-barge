A senior outgoing Republican senator said Wednesday that he intends to block one of President Donald Trump’s late judicial nominees for a Texas court, as the GOP-led Senate rushes to fill remaining federal vacancies before Democrats take power in January.

Texas Sen. John Cornyn told *The Associated Press* he has not returned the "blue slip" for Elliot Gaiser, a top Justice Department official nominated for the Dallas-area vacancy earlier this month, nor does he plan to. The tradition gives home-state senators effective veto power over a president's court picks.

"He’s not from Texas. He hasn’t been vetted. And this is a lifetime job, and there’s better qualified people to hold that job," Cornyn said in a Wednesday interview.

"This is part, of course, of the constitutional responsibility of senators in the Senate to provide advice and consent. But it’s hard to provide advice and consent when there’s zero consultation, which happened in that case."

Cornyn also noted the White House requested information on his preferred choice, Fort Worth lawyer Steve Fahey, though he was unsure if Gaiser would be withdrawn.

A White House official, speaking anonymously about ongoing nominations, said the administration expects all of Trump's judicial nominees to be confirmed by year-end.

"He's a very bright young man, and I think has a very bright future," Cornyn said of Gaiser. "I kind of feel bad that he’s been used basically as a pawn in this process."

In a statement, the Justice Department argued that Cornyn's "political antics only hurt the American public and the rule of law."

"Elliot Gaiser is a brilliant legal mind, a principled defender of the Constitution, and an outstanding judicial nominee," the department stated. "It is deeply disappointing that Senator Cornyn would choose political obstruction over advancing a highly qualified nominee."

![Gaiser, a top official at the Justice Department, was nominated for the Dallas-area vacancy earlier this month](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/21/2026-11-18T172021Z_1830101344_RC2HKOALK0NP_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-JUDICIARY-GAISER.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The senator stressed that he does not want to leave the court seat empty. Fahey served in the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Northern District of Texas from 2003 to 2021, later became a partner at Kirkland & Ellis, and launched his own firm in 2024.

Gaiser, head of the Justice Department’s Office of Legal Counsel, was first contacted by Texas Sen. Ted Cruz’s staff on Nov. 4, the day after the election, according to his Senate Judiciary Committee questionnaire. Cornyn has repeatedly raised concerns over the process, noting Gaiser was not vetted by Texas’s traditional bipartisan evaluation panel.

In a statement Wednesday, Cruz said Gaiser is the "only nominee who could actually be confirmed by the end of the year" because the White House was explicit that time has run out for a new background check. Gaiser already cleared that process for his current position.

"If the Senate Judiciary Committee does not move Gaiser forward, the people of Texas will pay the price because it will effectively give the judicial seat to the Democrats, who will keep it vacant in perpetuity," Cruz said. "That would be a tragedy."
