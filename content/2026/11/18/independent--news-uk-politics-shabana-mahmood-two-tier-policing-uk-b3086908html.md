Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood is set to outline plans to tackle the “corrosive and growing perception” of two-tier policing.

In a speech to police chiefs on Wednesday, she will lament the low levels of Black officers in police forces, while warning against “misguided attempts at course correction” that lead to “differential treatment”.

A new “impartiality standard”, to be developed by the College of Policing and subject to consultation next year, will aim to ensure all members of the public are treated equally, she will tell chief constables and police and crime commissioners.

It will bring guidance on key impartiality principles into one place and aims to give police more confidence in how they handle cases, regardless of someone’s race, faith or other characteristics, and to give the public confidence that they will be treated fairly.

The new standard will also give officers guidance on scenarios such as wearing symbols on badges and lanyards, and advise them how to behave at community-led events.

The Home Secretary is expected to say: “We must tackle, head-on, the corrosive and growing perception of two-tier policing in this country.

“Now let me be clear: I know the vast majority of officers police impartially and that policing without fear or favour is their creed.

“But there have been examples of differential treatment and they have harmed policing, allowing people – often motivated actors – to whip up the idea that there is two-tier policing in this country.”

A third of Britons believe police treat people from ethnic minority backgrounds more favourably than white people, a More in Common poll found earlier this year, noting this marks a doubling in the last two years.

She will tell the National Police Chiefs’ Council (NPCC) that it is “shameful” 33 years after the murder of black teenager Stephen Lawrence that the proportion of black officers remains virtually unchanged.

![Stephen Lawrence](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/01/19/17/861c33424fa28e58e63c07b443391b0fY29udGVudHNlYXJjaGFwaSwxNzY4OTI2NTYx-2.14364865.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But Ms Mahmood will argue that some approaches intended to promote equality, such as the former Police Anti-Racism Commitment, have risked undermining confidence in impartial policing.

“We must also be wary of misguided attempts at course correction, where the pursuit of equality leads instead to differential treatment,” she is set to say.

“To the public, it was patently absurd to tell police officers that fair policing does not mean treating everyone the same.”

Ms Mahmood will urge officers to avoid becoming drawn into political activism.

She will say: “Community engagement should mean going to a residents’ association meeting and discussing local issues.

“It can be over tea and biscuits, or tea and samosas, but it doesn’t mean engaging with activist groups and campaigners who are pushing an agenda and not representing a community.

“Leave the politics to the politicians. And we will leave the policing to you.”
