# Andy Burnham hints Universal Credit welfare reform could extend to over-25s

## Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he thought the 'same philosophy' could be applied 'right through the age range'

Andy Burnham has hinted that a sweeping overhaul of the benefits system could be rolled out to those aged over 25. The Prime Minister suggested the "same philosophy" could be applied "right through the age range" following the release of a landmark review into Britain's 'Neet' crisis.

Former Labour minister Alan Milburn unveiled his recommendations for tackling the number of young people not in education, employment or training on Tuesday. Amongst his proposals, he called for a legal guarantee of work or training for all young people, alongside a multi-billion pound boost in support schemes for 16-24-year-olds.

However, the most contentious recommendations in his 10-year plan to address the issue centre on a blueprint for an entirely new benefits system for under-25s, reports the Mirror. The plan involves a new "youth allowance" that would replace the health element of Universal Credit (UC) and the personal independence payment (PIP) for 16 to 24-year-olds.

Under the proposed system, the report states that those with conditions such as anxiety, ADHD and autism "would be both expected and enabled to work". It also puts forward a new and more financially generous "severe and complex needs" insurance scheme for the minority of young people where it is evident they are unlikely to ever be able to work.

Mr Milburn's report acknowledged that the issue of over-25s fell outside his remit, yet noted that the "design features" of his proposed system could be "applied to the older cohort of claimants". He further stated: "The Government should act with urgency to bring forward new proposals for a reformed welfare system for those aged 25 and over."

In conversation with The Times, Mr Burnham appeared to concur, remarking: "I think the same philosophy could apply right through the age range. What we are doing is creating an approach that is preventative, that supports people where they can prosper. The top-down, tick-box approach is quite soul destroying for people."

Earlier this month, The Mirror accompanied the Work and Pensions Secretary on a fact-finding trip to Denmark, seeking inspiration for his "big reset" of the welfare state.

The Scandinavian country's flagship "fleksjob" programme offers state-subsidised part-time employment opportunities to its citizens, including those who are disabled or suffering from ill-health. Mr McFadden said on the visit he had been "really struck" by the scheme helping all age groups return or stay in the workforce. He said at the time: "The system is certainly not just for young people, and I think there's a big lesson there.

"Yes, we've had a big discussion focused on Neets in the UK, but the idea of asking 'what can you do, how can we help you live the fullest life' shouldn't stop at the age of 24 or 25."

On Tuesday, the Prime Minister stated that the government would outline its full response once a second review, led by DWP minister Sir Stephen Timms, is published later this month. Sir Stephen was charged with examining reform of PIP - the primary disability benefit - after Keir Starmer was compelled by a Labour rebellion to scrap his proposals to slash billions from the system.

Sir Stephen's interim report - released in July - cautioned that the disability benefit was "not fit for purpose". He described the process of claiming PIP benefits as frequently "dehumanising", "soul destroying", and "degrading" to claimants. Meanwhile, Mr Burnham also indicated he would carefully consider Mr Milburn's suggestion to progressively reduce National Insurance contributions for businesses hiring unemployed young people.

He told LBC: "Obviously I've come in and inherited the situation. I think the last government was right to offer support to employers to take on young people, so that was one of the things that they did through the Youth Guarantee."

Lucy Powell, the Education Secretary and Labour's deputy leader, also defended the reforms while speaking on the BBC's Today programme. She said: "This is absolutely not about crude cuts, let's just be really clear about that. Alan Milburn has said that. The Prime Minister has said that.

"That doesn't work, it doesn't get the benefit bill down but more importantly it just pushes the problem elsewhere - it just pushes more people into destitution. It pushes more people into homelessness, it pushes more people into the criminal justice system, where the state has to pick up the bill instead. ".

She went on to say: "This is about putting more support around young people - a complete reset, a complete change in how we deliver the welfare state which gives people support they need."
