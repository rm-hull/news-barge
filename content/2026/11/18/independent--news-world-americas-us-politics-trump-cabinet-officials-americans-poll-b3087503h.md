Embattled Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. are the two Cabinet members Americans most want President Donald Trump to fire, according to a poll.

The latest YouGov/Economist survey found that 46 percent of respondents want Kennedy, a longtime vaccine skeptic, removed from his role as health chief, compared with 32 percent who want him to stay.

Hegseth also faces substantial opposition, with 43 percent in favor of his dismissal, versus 26 percent who support keeping him in the job.

Both men — among the highest-profile figures in the administration — have faced controversy throughout their tenures.

Hegseth, a former Fox News host, has played a central role in the unpopular Iran war, which has dragged on for nine months with no clear end in sight. He has also ousted senior military officials, imposed restrictions on Pentagon journalists, and faced calls to resign over the Signalgate scandal early on in the second Trump administration.

![According to a new poll, more Americans than not want to see Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. fired by President Donald Trump](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/12/GettyImages-2267894338.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Fresh off their midterm victories, Democrats have vowed to pursue Hegseth’s impeachment when the new Congress convenes in January. An impeachment effort led by outgoing Republican Rep. Thomas Massie failed last week.

Meanwhile, Kennedy, the leader of the MAHA movement, has drawn scrutiny for scaling back federal immunization recommendations, replacing the CDC’s vaccine advisory panel and cutting funding for mRNA vaccine development.

The Pan American Health Organization this week stripped the U.S. of its measles-free status for the first time in 26 years, as as the highly contagious virus spreads at levels not seen in decades. Kennedy has said that the MMR vaccine is the “most effective way to prevent the spread of measles,” while also claiming it causes “deaths every year.”

Poll respondents were less certain about three other Cabinet officials: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Attorney General Todd Blanche and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. For each of the three, the largest share said they were unsure whether he should be fired or kept on.

![In the YouGov/Economist poll, 43 percent of respondents said they favor Pete Hegseth’s dismissal, while just 26 percent said they support keeping him in the job](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/2026-11-18T191120Z_886856870_RC2IQOA3604S_RTRMADP_3_VENEZUELA-USA.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Historically, it’s not uncommon for presidents to reshuffle their Cabinets after bruising midterm elections.

Since Election Day, when Democrats triumphantly reclaimed the House and the Senate, Trump has made one major personnel change, replacing White House chief of staff Susie Wiles with her deputy, Stephen Miller. Hours before he announced the move, he told *60 Minutes*: “I love my Cabinet. I think they're good…But I'll… be doing maybe a few changes.”

Of the six Cabinet officials named in the poll, only Marco Rubio, who serves as both secretary of state and national security adviser, drew a plurality in favor of keeping him in the administration.

Thirty-seven percent of respondents said he should stay, while 31 percent said he should be axed.

![Forty-six percent of respondents want Robert F. Kennedy Jr. removed as health and human services secretary, compared with 32 percent who want him to stay](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/13/2026-11-17T162810Z_452036053_RC2IANA02MUT_RTRMADP_3_HEALTH-MEASLES-USA-ELIMINATION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Rubio, a former Florida senator, has long been seen as a potential contender for the 2028 Republican presidential nomination, alongside Vice President JD Vance.

While not a Cabinet official, Trump himself received extremely poor marks. A majority of respondents, 61 percent, disapproved of his job performance, while just 35 percent approved.

His approval rating has hovered near historic lows for months amid voter frustration over the Iran war, the cost of living and gas prices.

Conducted November 13 to 16, the YouGov/Economist poll sampled 1,628 U.S. citizens and has a margin of error of plus or minus 2.9 percentage points.
