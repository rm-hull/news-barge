Birmingham City Council has been granted an interim High Court injunction to prevent people from putting St George’s flags on lampposts and bridges in Birmingham.

That includes the co-founder of the Raise The Colours group, Ryan Bridge.

The council sought the order against Mr Bridge and others after thousands of Union and St George’s flags were put up in the city.

It said the flags can pose “safety risks, obstruct visibility and interfere with highway assets” as well as “contribute to tensions and disorder”.

On Wednesday, Judge Emma Kelly ruled that an interim prohibitory injunction will be granted against Bridge, Ben Cullen, who is associated with Raise The Colours, and “persons unknown”, who have, or intend to, attach flags to street furniture in Birmingham.

![Ryan Bridge is the co-founder of the group Raise The Colours](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/03/14/01M6GWJWME0K7KC3WTVNGD2PSM.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A power of arrest, meaning the police could arrest someone immediately without a warrant if they reasonably suspect the order has been breached, will be made against Bridge and “persons unknown”.

Bridge, who attended the Birmingham Civil and Family Justice Centre earlier this month, opposed the injunction on the basis that it would breach his right to freedom of expression and his right to protest under the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR).

His lawyer, Liam Wells, told the court the activist admits attaching flags to street furniture but has only encouraged people to “express their views”.

Mr Wells had told the court the injunction would have a “chilling effect” on the expression of Bridge’s rights.

The earlier hearing was attended by about 20 Raise The Colours members, who wore T-shirts bearing the organisation’s name, while three protesters stood with Union flags outside the court.

The council said its application for an injunction included reported incidents involving “alleged intimidation, harassment and obstruction” connected to flags being put up in the city.

The court heard on one occasion a man was run over by a cherry picker and his leg was broken while members of Raise The Colours were putting up flags, while others have been allegedly assaulted or threatened.

![The flag of the United Kingdom and the Flag of St George hang from lamposts on 9 September, 2025 in Birmingham, United Kingdom](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/2234502552..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In her ruling, Judge Kelly said “very emotive language” has surrounded the application for the injunction.

She said: “The interim injunction needs to be understood for what it is.

“It is not the court nor the claimant banning the Union Jack or the display of pride flags or anything of the like.

“Whilst the interim injunction order will cover flags of all types, it is limited in scope to the restraint of unauthorised flags attached to highway street furniture or infrastructure, and to the restraint of associated conduct, within the administrative area of Birmingham.”

Councillor Jane Baston, Cabinet Member for Equalities, Communities and Social Justice for Birmingham City Council, said: “We are grateful that the court has granted the council an interim injunction against Ryan Bridge, Ben Cullen and persons unknown relating to the unauthorised attachment of flags to street furniture and highway infrastructure.”

Ms Baston added: “Residents remain free to display flags on their own property.

“The injunction relates specifically to the unauthorised attachment of items to street furniture and highway infrastructure, and we ask residents and community groups to support this approach by ensuring any displays are placed only where permission exists.”

The council said anyone who breaches the injunction could be imprisoned for up to two years, fined, or have their assets seized for contempt of court.

A hearing to deal with any further matters is due to be heard in Birmingham on Thursday.
