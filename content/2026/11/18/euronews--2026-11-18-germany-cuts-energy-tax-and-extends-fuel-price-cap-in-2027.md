## Families could save around €8 a month, as Berlin also renews its commitment to keep petrol affordable.

Germany's governing parties have agreed to cut electricity tax for all consumers to the EU minimum from 1 January 2027. The deal took eight hours of talks, not the swift agreement Chancellor Friedrich Merz had announced.

The levy will fall from 2.05 cents to 0.1 cents per kilowatt-hour. The agreement came out of the coalition committee, which brings together leaders of Merz's conservative CDU/CSU alliance and the centre-left Social Democrats (SPD).

A new fuel discount, or cap on petrol prices, will also come into force on the same date, replacing the current one.

Markus Söder, leader of the CSU, the Bavarian sister party of Merz's CDU, wrote on X on Wednesday morning: "As promised! The electricity tax will fall from 1 January 2027 for all households and businesses to the European minimum. This will now relieve everyone, including families, pensioners and all small and medium-sized businesses. And the fuel price cap will also come on 1 January. Mobility must remain affordable. The CSU keeps its word!"

The post could be read as a dig at the CDU. Merz had recently come under pressure within his own party after promising the electricity tax cut but initially failing to push it through.

On Wednesday morning, Merz said: "I thank the members of the coalition committee for following my proposal to reduce the electricity tax. The intensive consultations have paid off."

The measure will cost €5.4 billion. Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil of the SPD had initially opposed it because the financing was uncertain.

According to the coalition, funding for the cut is secured only for 2027. The decision states it will be paid for from headroom in subsidies under Germany's Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG). This also settles the item in the 2027 federal budget, though the budget must still be approved by the Bundestag, Germany's parliament.

## A scattergun approach?

The decision also provides for another temporary fuel discount from 1 January 2027, with the current fuel price cap initially extended for three months.

Even before the coalition committee reached its agreement, critics said cutting electricity tax for everyone failed to target support at poorer households.

According to an analysis by the price comparison website Verivox, Germany has the highest electricity prices in the G20 and some of the highest in the world.

An average family could save around €8 a month under the agreed cut.

The fuel price cap has also been criticised as a "watering can" measure, a German expression for spreading support thinly and indiscriminately. Critics also argue it could delay the phase-out of fossil fuels.

Germany's current fuel discount came into force on 1 October 2026 and runs until 31 December 2026. The federal government introduced it to bring down petrol prices, which rose because of the war in Iran and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.
