*Furniture Village is a Business Reporter client*

For many family businesses, growth represents a difficult balancing act: how do you drive expansion while keeping the culture and values on which the business was founded? For Furniture Village, the answer has been to put family values at the heart of its growth strategy and give its people a genuine stake in the company’s success.

Founded by Executive Chairman Peter Harrison in 1989, Furniture Village has grown from a single store in Abingdon into the UK’s largest independent furniture retailer, with 59 stores and over 1,350 employees nationwide. Today, Peter is joined by his son, Charlie Harrison, who worked his way up through the business and now oversees day-to-day operations as Managing Director.

Despite being a national retailer, the company is still rooted in the principles on which it was built: sustainable growth, long-term thinking and a commitment to reinvest in the business rather than prioritise short-term profits. This philosophy extends from the way it treats its people to how it works with its suppliers and customers – perhaps best reflected in the company’s over 180,000 five-star Trustpilot reviews. Together, the Harrisons have shaped a culture that puts its people first and fosters a genuine sense of togetherness from the boardroom to the shop floor.

Following the return of the business to full family ownership in 2022, Peter and Charlie fulfilled a lifelong ambition: creating the Family Partnership Profit Share Scheme. Launched in 2023, it sees a sizeable portion of the company’s annual profits shared with every employee who has been with the business for more than a year.

The scheme has encouraged a shared ownership mindset where employees think like business owners, take greater responsibility for the bottom line and feel directly connected to the company’s success. Whether that’s helping a customer find the right piece for their home or finding new ways to work more efficiently, everyone plays their part in contributing to the wider success of the business.

The results speak for themselves. Since the scheme’s launch, Furniture Village has recorded some of its most profitable years on record, delivering sustained like-for-like growth and increased market share despite a challenging retail landscape. At the same time, the business has continued to invest in the future, recently opening a state-of-the-art national distribution centre and expanding into Scotland with new stores in Edinburgh and Glasgow.

While part of a broader strategy, the scheme has undoubtedly helped strengthen the culture that underpins the business’s continued success. Nowhere is this more evident than its workforce. With an average length of service of 8 years and a staff turnover rate of just 12 per cent against an industry average of 34 per cent, Furniture Village’s focus on culture and long-term sustainability has ensured it’s a place where people stay and progress, providing continuity as it expands across the UK.

Furniture Village has proved that expansion does not require a company to choose between the scale of a national retailer and the values of a family business. Instead, it has retained its culture as it has grown, and more importantly, has turned its distinctive, family-led approach into a competitive advantage by creating a workforce that is personally invested in the company’s success and shares its ambitions for the future.
