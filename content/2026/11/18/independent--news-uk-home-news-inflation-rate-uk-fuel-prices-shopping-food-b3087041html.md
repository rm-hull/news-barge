Driven by rising fuel costs linked to Middle Eastern conflict, UK inflation has now reached its highest rate in a year.

Official data from the Office for National Statistics revealed that the Consumer Prices Index rose to 3.6 per cent in October, compared with 3.5 per cent in September.

With living costs climbing continuously since the summer, analysts warn that upcoming increases in domestic energy bills could drive inflation up further in the coming months.

Here is a look at the key factors driving the recent rise and what lies ahead for consumers:

## What is inflation?

Inflation is the term used to describe the rising price of goods and services.

The inflation rate refers to how quickly prices are going up.

October’s inflation rate of 3.6 per cent means if an item cost £100 a year ago, it would now cost £103.60.

It is a higher rate than September 3.5 per cent, meaning prices are increasing at a faster pace than they were before.

## What caused inflation to rise last month?

The ONS said the biggest contributor to higher inflation last month came from transport, particularly prices at the pump.

The latest figures showed the average price of petrol rose by 5.2p per litre between September and October, as tensions in the Middle East continued to push oil prices higher.

Diesel prices rose by 7p per litre in October to an average price of 199.5p – the highest since ONS records began in 1996.

Nevertheless, petrol and diesel prices both eased back in November as oil prices moved lower following the US midterm elections.

More expensive tobacco was also a contributing factor after new duty rates kicked in at the start of the month.

Tobacco prices rose 5.2 per cent for the month and were 12.4 per cent higher year-on-year as a result, marking the highest inflation rate for almost two years.

## Did all price rises accelerate in October?

No, inflation for some products remained steady or even decreased over the month.

One key area which saw a drop in inflation was food and non-alcoholic drinks, which eased to 0.8 per cent from 1.2 per cent the previous month, taking it to the lowest level since September 2021.

![One key area which saw a drop in inflation was food and non-alcoholic drinks, which eased to 0.8 per cent from 1.2 per cent the previous month, taking it to the lowest level since September 2021](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/2026-11-18T084505Z_1082777150_RC2Y0NA3DT4B_RTRMADP_3_BRITAIN-ECONOMY-INFLATION.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Food price inflation eased to 0.2 per cent for the month amid intense price competition from UK supermarkets.

This included decreases in the price of some products, such as chocolate, which fell by 2.9 per cent compared with the same month a year earlier.

Meanwhile, clothing and footwear prices were also 0.5 per cent lower year-on-year, the lowest rate since March.

## Where will inflation go from here?

Inflation is widely expected to continue accelerating in the coming months.

The Bank of England said earlier this month that it expects the rate to keep shifting further from its 2 per cent target rate, indicating it could peak at around 5 per cent.

The central bank indicated higher energy costs are likely to feed further through supply chains and into the economy.

Inflation is likely to particularly accelerate from January next year, when household energy bills are predicted to surge.

Experts at Cornwall Insight have predicted Ofgem’s energy price cap will rise by 26 per cent to £2,166 a year on January 1, up £443 on the level between October and December, to hit the highest since 2023.

Investec economist Ellie Henderson said: “October’s rate of inflation is the highest in a year and far above the Bank of England’s 2 per cent target.

“The direction of travel is not encouraging either: on our forecasts, today’s data will not be the peak.”

![Food price inflation eased to 0.2 per cent for the month amid intense price competition from UK supermarkets](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/17/08/01KSM4B3QHT9GE3MB6THYSCFGB.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She added that she expects inflation to “head north of 4 per cent” during the first quarter of next year.

However, she is among a number economists to have suggested CPI will peak below the 5 per cent level flagged by the Bank of England, particularly after a dip in wholesale energy costs over the past two weeks.

## What does the latest hike in inflation mean for interest rates?

Earlier this month, the Bank of England’s monetary policy committee (MPC) voted to increase the UK’s base interest rate from 3.75 per cent to 4 per cent.

Policy makers also suggested they could increase rates further in order to bring inflation back down to 2 per cent target rate.

Last week, Clare Lombardelli, a deputy governor at the Bank, said rates could be hiked again in the near future if soaring inflation does not ease back.

The UK’s financial markets are currently predicting that interest rates will be increased to between 4.75 per cent and 5 per cent by the end of next year.

Some economists have suggested the Bank is likely to increase rates again at their next meeting in December.

Suren Thiru, ICAEW chief economist, said: “These figures make a December rate hike look difficult to avoid by doing little to dampen the hawkish sentiment among rate-setters and reinforcing the view that higher borrowing costs are still needed to contain mounting inflation.”

However, analysts at Nomura suggested the Bank may wait until February to next increase interest rates.
