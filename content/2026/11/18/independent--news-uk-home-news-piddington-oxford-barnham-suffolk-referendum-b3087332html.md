Residents of a Suffolk village will go to the polls on Saturday in a Piddington-style independence referendum over plans to house asylum seekers at a nearby RAF base.

Barnham will hold a Yes/No poll asking villagers whether they back a petition for “crown dependency status”.

The vote follows a proposal to house 931 asylum seekers at RAF Barnham.

The poll has been organised by Barnham Residents’ Association, according to John Bauer, chairman of Barnham Parish Council.

Mr Bauer said people will vote on whether they support a petition for “crown dependency status” and described this as “a bit like Jersey, Guernsey, Isle of Man whereby we retain loyalty to the crown yet while still removing ourselves from the Home Office jurisdiction”.

He said the poll will be “Yes for independence, No for keep things as they are”.

He added: “Assuming we have a positive result, we will declare ourselves the bailiwick of Barnham. Not the principality of Piddington. This is going to be the bailiwick of Barnham.”

The vote will carry no legal weight.

Asked what he hopes it will achieve, Mr Bauer said: “It’s to express the concern of the village at the way we’re not being consulted, the treatment by the Home Office, the whole thing, we feel we’re being totally ignored.”

The referendum was prompted by the proposal for RAF Barnham, which Mr Bauer said was initially for 1,256 male asylum seekers to be housed at the base, though the figure is now 931.

“I don’t know why they chose that particularly precise figure, but they have done,” he said.

“It makes very little difference, however, when you’ve only got 584 residents in the village whether it’s 1,256 or 931.

“It’s still going to completely swamp the village and change things beyond recognition.”

Barnham’s poll mirrors one held in Piddington, Oxfordshire, where villagers voted in September to secede from the UK.

That vote came after the Government announced plans to house 1,256 male asylum seekers at a nearby former military base.

Communities Secretary Angela Rayner has since approved full planning permission for MoD Bicester to be used for housing asylum seekers.

Mr Bauer said that decision was “obviously very concerning” but “we’re not without hope and we’re not going to go down without a fight”.

Voting in Barnham will take place at Barnham Village Hall from 10am to 4pm.

Mr Bauer said 400 to 500 people are eligible to vote.

He said the result should be known “fairly soon after the polls close”, adding: “I don’t imagine it’s going to take that long to count the votes, there aren’t that many of them.”

The symbolic vote will carry no legal weight, with the outcome serving as an expression of residents’ views rather than a binding decision.
