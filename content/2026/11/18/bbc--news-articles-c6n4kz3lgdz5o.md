**The personal details of about 265,000 Oyster photocard customers have been accessed in a cyber breach, Transport for London (TfL) has said.**

Names, email and home addresses, phone numbers, dates of birth and security questions and answers were compromised after "suspicious activity" was detected on the photocard website on Monday.

TfL said it took the photocard system off line to limit access and had notified the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

It said no passwords, bank or payment card details or journey information were compromised.

The photocard website remains offline, meaning customers cannot apply for or renew a photocard online until it is restored as an investigation continues.

TfL said customers who needed to renew their photocard or discuss their journey history could contact customer services on 0343 222 1234.

"Customers with Oyster photocards can continue to travel and we hope to restore services as quickly as possible."

It is the second time in just over two years the system had been taken down because of a cyber incident.

In September 2024, a cyber attack on TfL forced it to suspend new photocard applications for more than two months. TfL said at the time that about 5,000 customers had been contacted over possible access to their bank details, but the BBC later revealed that the data of about 10 million people had been stolen.

The ICO cleared TfL of wrongdoing over its handling of that breach.
