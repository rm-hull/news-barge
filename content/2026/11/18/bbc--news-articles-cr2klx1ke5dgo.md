Hayden has opened his alpaca farm for the benefit other children

**A teenager who has autism and ADHD has helped turn his alpaca farm into a safe haven for other children with special educational needs.**

Hayden Geraghty, 16, and his mother Caroline welcome children and adults to their farm in Limavady, County Londonderry, for what is known as alpaca therapy.

The farm at the foot of Binevenagh mountain also has Valais Blacknose sheep, and Hayden regularly takes the animals to visit his school.

Health experts and charities have said working with alpacas can be therapeutic, reduce anxiety and improve someone's mental and emotional well-being.

"I have seen a serious change in Hayden since the alpacas came into his life," Caroline Geraghty told BBC News NI.

"Hayden went backwards a lot during the Covid lockdown. A friend mentioned an alpaca farm to visit in Malin Head in Donegal. Everything changed for Hayden after that visit and we had to get our own.

"The alpacas are very much in tune and are inquisitive. The way Hayden's mood changes around the alpacas and the sheep is incredible. I even find myself more relaxed around them."

![Hayden with black headphones and coat leaning on a brown wooden fence alongside his mum Caroline who has long brown hair, glasses, black coat and orange hat. The alpacas, which are brown and white, are in the background.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/48d9/live/a762eda0-e1d8-11f1-b8c8-67fa35f5afdb.jpg)

Hayden and his mother Caroline take the alpacas to his school

Hayden and his mother take the alpacas to his school, Rossmar Special School in Limavady, where children get to pet the alpacas and walk them.

"We do not charge money for this. It's very much about spreading the love to other neurodivergent children and adults," Caroline added.

"This is a safe haven for people. Hayden also takes part in agricultural shows and exhibitions now and his new confidence is mind blowing."

![A brown alpaca with white bits of fur on its' face. It has a rainbow collar and a pink lead attached.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/0a91/live/d2310710-e1d8-11f1-b8c8-67fa35f5afdb.jpg)

The alpacas have proven to be popular with the children at Rossmore Special School

Hayden told BBC News NI he "feels great and relaxed" when working with the animals.

"I spend time with the alpacas a lot and feed them. It makes me feel really happy because the animals are calm.

"Children with special needs come here to feed them and it's like a therapy session for them.

"I have taken the alpacas into my school and it was really good."

Rossmar Special School vice-principal Laura Fisher said she had seen an "immediate lowering" of pupils' anxiety and stress levels when they spent time with the animals.

"Alpacas have been coming for about a year now and it's been great to see how happy it makes pupils," she added.

![Laura Fisher has long blonde hair and is wearing a checked blazer and black top.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/30f5/live/0549ee00-e1d9-11f1-b8c8-67fa35f5afdb.jpg)

Laura Fisher said the alpacas have brought joy to the children at her school

Her view is backed by Ema Cubitt, Northern Ireland's independent autism reviewer, whose role is to monitor care and services for autistic people and their families.

"Research has shown that animal-assisted activities can offer benefits for emotional wellbeing, communication and social connection," she says.

"We should never underestimate the value of simply bringing happiness and enjoyment into a child's school day.

"What makes this story stand out is that it began with something this young man loves, and he's now sharing it with other children.

"Sometimes the best opportunities in education begin by listening to young people and following their lead."

![Hayden holding one of the alpacas, a cream one, on a pink lead. There is a brown alpaca](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/800/cpsprodpb/bd1d/live/82fbaa00-e1d9-11f1-b8c8-67fa35f5afdb.jpg)

Hayden feels proud that his family are helping others through their animals

Spending time with animals can have real benefits for our health, says Northern Ireland's mental health champion Prof Siobhan O'Neill.

"Interacting with them can help reduce stress and anxiety, encourage positive emotions, and create a sense of comfort and companionship," explains O'Neill.

"For children and adults alike, particularly those who may experience difficulties with social interaction or emotional regulation, these experiences can be hugely valuable.

"The pleasure of caring for and spending time with an animal can help people feel more settled, confident, and happy."

Hayden is now keen to expand his herd of alpacas and sheep to help more young people like himself.

"I'm very proud of myself and all I have achieved," he says.

"I'm also proud that we are helping others."
