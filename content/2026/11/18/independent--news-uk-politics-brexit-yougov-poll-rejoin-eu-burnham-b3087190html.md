British voters are prepared to open a debate about reversing Brexit and think the UK could rejoin the EU within five years, a new poll has found.

According to the findings by YouGov, 54 per cent believe it would be acceptable to have a rejoin referendum in that time, compared to 33 per cent who think it would be unacceptable. Even a third of 2016 Leave voters (32 per cent) now support the UK having a vote to decide whether to rejoin the bloc.

People also think the process of rejoining the EU would happen quickly, with six in 10 voters (58 per cent) believing it could take less than five years, the polling showed.

But the survey of more than 2,000 adults suggests the electorate is still split over whether the issue should be a priority for Andy Burnham. A total of 45 per cent of people said rejoining should not be a priority for the government, while 37 per cent said it is the right priority.

It comes after European Movement UK launched a plan, revealed in *The Independent,* for the UK to rejoin in a maximum of 10 years – but much faster if the government is willing to push the issue.

The prime minister is heading to the highly-anticipated UK-EU summit in Brussels on Friday where he will meet European leaders and sign the latest Brexit reset deal.

While this will include agreement on ending food and drink border checks, a shared energy market and youth mobility scheme, Mr Burnham is hoping to use it as a launch pad for a national conversation on whether Britain should move to rejoin the EU.

Meanwhile, support for the EU appears to be strong with 49 per cent believing the UK would be “better off” in the EU compared to just 22 per cent who say the UK would be “worse off”.

With a shortage of jobs currently in the labour market, 46 per cent believe joining the EU would improve employment prospects while just 18 per cent think it would be even worse.

In echoes of Vote Leave’s infamous red bus with the slogan promising £50 million a week for the NHS, almost double the number of voters now believe the NHS would be improved by rejoining (34 per cent), compared to those who think it would be damaged (18 per cent).

A clear majority (58 per cent) of Britons think Britain was wrong to vote to leave the EU, with 63 per cent seeing Brexit as a failure. A third of Britons (32 per cent) think Brexit was the right decision.

The survey also shows 47 per cent of Britons think a rejoin side would win in a referendum. Only 13 per cent think a rival “stay out” vote would win.
