## Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin are holding talks in Shenzhen, a day before a first-of-its-kind trilateral summit with US President Donald Trump in the southern Chinese city.

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin are holding talks in Shenzhen, a day before a one-of-its-kind trilateral summit with US President Donald Trump in the southern Chinese city.

Chinese and Russian news agencies reported that the two leaders began talks around midday on Wednesday.

It was their fifth meeting this year, one of which took place by video link.

## **Fears over Ukraine and spheres of influence**

Thursday’s meeting has raised concern in Kyiv and European capitals that the three leaders could settle Ukraine’s future without them and divide the world into modern spheres of influence.

Washington initially said Ukraine was not expected to be on the agenda, but Trump later said it would be. Xi regards the war as not China’s concern.

Beijing has said only that the leaders will hold “in-depth exchanges on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations” on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) meeting.

According to Washington, the Iran war and nuclear disarmament are also on the table after the US-Russia bilateral nuclear weapons treaty expired in February. China has long argued that the US and Russia should cut their much larger arsenals before it will discuss limits on its own.

## **Putin and Xi speak at APEC**

Opening the event, Xi called for stronger international cooperation on artificial intelligence and free trade. China would pursue “deep-seated reform”, he said, starting with lower tariffs, wider market access and work on regulation and management standards.

Putin, who has been shunned by the West since he invaded Ukraine in 2022, told the APEC gathering he expected the trilateral to be “highly useful”.

With military and political risks rising, he said, “what is desperately needed is unity rather than division... and dialogue rather than ultimatums.”

He also argued that Western sanctions had failed, saying they could not fully halt growth or wreck the economies of rival states.

Trump was still in the Philippines on Wednesday, where he attended a summit of the 11-member Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN). A day earlier he met fellow Quad leaders from Australia, Japan and India for the first time in his second term. The group is broadly seen as a counterweight to Chinese influence in the region.

He said he hoped to hold one-on-one talks with both Putin and North Korean leader Kim Jong Un in Shenzhen, adding that Kim “would like” to meet in China.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking in Manila, stopped short of confirming a Kim meeting but said engagement was the only way to resolve problems with North Korea.

Around 2,000 people protested during Trump’s visit, carrying banners reading “Trump out now” and “Reject Pax Silica”, a reference to a US supply chain initiative.
