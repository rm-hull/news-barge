Households are being encouraged to prepare for emergencies that could leave them temporarily without essential services.

This advice comes from the Red Cross as people are being told to prepare for situations where they could be left temporarily without power, water, transport, and communications, as well as being unable to access shops and other essential services.

People are being advised to put together supplies that could support their household for three days, although there is no need to buy specialist or expensive equipment.

Many of the recommended essentials may already be found around the home.

You can see the full advice via the Red Cross.

## Everything to include in your emergency supplies kit

The Red Cross states that 14 key items should be included in every household's kit;

* Paper copy of important contact numbers, to communicate with others even without mobile devices.
* Mobile phone charger and portable power bank, so you can keep in communication.
* Cash for essentials such as food or fuel, in case of a cyber attack or power cut stopping card payments.
* Torch and spare batteries, in case of a power cut.
* Battery-powered or wind-up radio, so you can access information during a power cut.
* Essential medication and copies of prescriptions if needed.
* Basic first aid kit.
* Hand sanitiser and wet wipes, to maintain basic hygiene.
* Three days' supply of drinking water, in case a power cut, drought or flood disrupts the water supply.
* Three days' supply of long-life or ready-to-eat food.
* Tin opener.
* Copies of important documents stored in a waterproof bag, in case of electrical devices not being available or originals destroyed in a flood or fire.
* Pen and paper, in case anything needs to be recorded.
* Spare home and car keys, in case these are lost in the emergency.

The Red Cross adds that you should adapt your emergency kit for your household's needs, including for children, babies, pets, health and accessibility needs.

The organisation further explains that kits do not have to be expensive and you can start with a few basics that will still provide a strong emergency kit.

The seven priority items to have in every emergency kit are:

* essential medication
* torch
* portable phone charger or power bank
* drinking water - around 2.5 to 3 litres of drinking water per person per day
* long-life food
* emergency contact details
* basic first aid supplies

You can find more information via the Red Cross.
