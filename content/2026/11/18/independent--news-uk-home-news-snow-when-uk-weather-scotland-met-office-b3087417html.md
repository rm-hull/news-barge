Snow could fall tonight and next week as temperatures plunge to freezing and even below, forecasters say.

Hilly areas of Scotland such as the northern Cairngorms are the most likely area to have snow in the UK as wintry showers arrive, although very little will settle, the Met Office says.

Overnight on Friday and Saturday, the thermometer could hit minus 4C in places such as Biggar and Cumnock, Met Office maps show.

Last month, gales of up to 70mph battered the Scottish Highlands and Outer Hebrides.

Although there are no weather warnings in place in the near future, forecasters say next week will be rainy across the UK with occasional strong winds but clear and sunny spells in between showers.

In northern areas the rain may fall as snow at times, mainly over hills.

![Temperatures are forecast to hit -3C in Scotland on Friday evening](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/42/Screenshot-2026-11-18-172357.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the south it will be milder in the south with wetter weather at the end of the month, which will continue into the first two weeks of next month, as low pressure is likely to be further south than normal for the time of year.

There could be stronger winds at times, the Met Office predicts.

By contrast, higher pressure in the north means drier and more settled conditions are more likely there.

Temperatures in the south will probably be near normal or perhaps slightly above at times, with the north colder than normal.

Several parts of England still have hosepipe bans after summer’s prolonged drought.

Affinity Water, Anglian Water, Bristol Water, Cambridge Water, Essex and Suffolk Water, Southern Water, South East Water, South West Water, Thames Water and Wessex Water still have hosepipe bans in place for some or all of their customers.

Eleven areas are still in drought, including some in southern England where severe drought was declared.

September was the sixth month this year that recorded less than average rainfall.
