The Berlin Senate has designated just under a third of the city as heat districts (German: Hitzeviertel).

Measures to improve heat protection are to be implemented with special priority in the 170 areas, as Environment Senator Ute Bonde (CDU) announced following a Senate meeting.

These measures include planting new street trees, creating green spaces with shade-providing plants and the targeted use of rainwater. The goal is to lower the temperature in these neighborhoods by at least two degrees Celsius over the long term. According to Bonde, 170 of Berlin's 542 planning areas - just over 31 percent - are now designated as "thermally highly stressed." These include many densely built-up Gründerzeit neighborhoods within the S-Bahn ring, as well as large residential developments in Marzahn-Hellersdorf, Lichtenberg and Spandau.

"Climate change affects everyone in our city, but not everyone to the same degree," said Bonde. "We are now identifying the areas that are most severely impacted in the summer. We will begin implementing climate protection measures where they are most urgently needed from the perspective of social justice." For example, people who cannot afford air conditioning or do not have a yard are more exposed to the heat in their immediate surroundings. "That’s why these neighborhoods should be the first to benefit from new street trees, green spaces and technical measures to reduce extreme heat," Bonde explained. "This way, we prevent climate change from exacerbating social inequality. Protection from the heat must not be a matter of financial means."
