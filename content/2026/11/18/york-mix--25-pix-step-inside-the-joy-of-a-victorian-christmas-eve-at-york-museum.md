The grand tree is lit, the lights are low, the twinkling garlands are up – Christmas has arrived at York Castle Museum!

And for 2026, it’s Christmas Eve every day on Kirkgate; as the street is frozen in time, capturing the heart-warming excitement of Victorian Christmas celebrations.

Families can walk across the snow dusted cobbles of the iconic Victorian street Kirkgate and perhaps meet Father Christmas clad in his traditional green attire for a truly Yorkshire Christmas.

*Christmas at York Castle Museum*is running from**14 November 2026 to 3 January 2027**.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-castle-museum-xmas-26-19.webp)

“This year on Kirkgate, and the surrounding snickets and courtyards, we’ve tried to bring to life the shops and the domestic life of the night before Christmas, that activity and excitement of Victorian York,” said Carla Starkey, immersive interpretation producer at York Castle Museum.

“We’ve had so many people, volunteers and staff, and external companies working on this, and to see visitors walking around the corner as they enter Kirkgate, their eyes light up. Their cameras immediately come out their pockets, and there’s a sense of joy that perhaps they weren’t expecting as they come down onto the street.

“Something we’re really proud of here at York Castle Museum is how authentically Victorian York Christmas – and any time of year should feel here – and I think we’ve captured that brilliantly this year.”

New for this year, Kirkgate is alive with shadows and silhouettes of the street’s residents preparing for Christmas, playing games, and sharing special moments, from designers Spatial Projects.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-castle-museum-xmas-26-5.webp)
![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-castle-museum-xmas-26-15.webp)

Spatial Projects are a York-based studio, founded by Helena Jones and Simon Ackerley, that creates immersive environments working across mediums including fabric, light, media, projection work, animation, and sound.

“We try and do immersive in a way that is in-keeping with the surroundings,” explained Simon. “So, the projections are trying to give a bit of life and soul back to this street; rather than just projecting over the top of surfaces, it’s working with it and bringing scenes like the children getting ready with their mum.

“It’s just to give a bit of happiness and joy in these little pockets of moments together to enhance the place.”

The immersive event also extends into Rowntree’s Snicket this year, depicting a frozen Victorian scene.

“We spent an entire week up on ladders, putting the fabrics in there, the bespoke sculpture – the snowflakes and the stars, all 200 of them are handmade.”

Spatial Projects have worked with some of the biggest museums in the UK, such as the British Museum and the Science Museum, but there’s no place like home.

“It’s exciting to be in your home city,” said Helena. “York’s our home and to have this year at the most iconic venue, and then to see people’s faces light up, is just pure joy.”

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-castle-museum-xmas-26-6.webp)
![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/york-castle-museum-xmas-26-23.webp)

Live inside Christmases past with seventeenth and eighteenth-century feasts displayed across historical dining rooms, browse all sorts of festive goods in Victorian shop fronts and experience traditions that are still used today in feel good family time throughout the museum.

Experience familiar favourites as Scrooge returns for the much-loved rendition of Dickens’ iconic seasonal tale ‘A Christmas Carol’, in one-man performances throughout November and December.

Music makes the season, with memories of a favourite carol or nostalgia for a classic CD playing in grandma’s kitchen. York Castle Museum has all eras covered from folk classics and carol singers filling Kirkgate with traditional songs to the Swinging Sixties Gallery, where iconic Christmas tunes from the decade will be playing and even the recreated kitchen displays get a festive makeover with 1980s memorable Christmas favourites and chart-topping music.

Special events such as Edwardian Tales, a night of traditional Victorian ghost stories and guided craft workshops are also taking place throughout the season, allowing visitors to create their own wreath and greeting cards to take home a piece of festive decor.

More details on the events that are included in general admission and how to book onto special events and workshops are now available on York Castle Museum’s website.
