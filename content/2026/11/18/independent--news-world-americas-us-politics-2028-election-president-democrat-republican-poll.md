Nearly twice as many Americans think the next president will be a Democrat after the blue tsunami swept through red states in the midterms.

Fifty-two percent of Americans think the nominee that the Democratic Party chooses in the 2028 election will take the White House, according to a new Politico poll. Just 28 percent said the Republican Party will take the win.

Just two years ago, Democrats were licking their wounds after Donald Trump beat then-Vice President Kamala Harris in the 2024 presidential election. Harris was chosen as the nominee after then-President Joe Biden dropped his reelection bid following a disastrous debate performance against Trump.

But things are turning around big time for Democrats after they won control of the House and Senate, as well as historic gubernatorial races, earlier this month. While Trump has refused to take the blame for the GOP shellacking, he is facing record-low approval ratings with an unpopular war in Iran, stubborn inflation and an immigration agenda that many find too aggressive.

![Fifty-two percent of Americans think the nominee that the Democratic Party chooses in the 2028 election will take the White House, according to a new poll. Just 28 percent said the Republican Party will take the win](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/18/48/GettyImages-2302754619.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Of those in the Political poll who voted for Harris in 2024, 87 percent say the Democrats will win the 2028 presidential election. Meanwhile, 60 percent of Trump 2024 voters say the Republican nominee will take the White House.

“Whoever’s running for president has seen what’s happened and also has seen the power of our movement, and so this is something that could be carried on to 2028,” Representative-elect Adam Hamawy, a progressive New Jersey Democrat, told Politico.

Now the question is: who will run for president in 2028?

There are several names on both sides that have been floating since before the midterms.

![Just two years ago, Democrats were licking their wounds after Donald Trump beat then-Vice President Kamala Harris in the 2024 presidential election. But things are turning around for Democrats after they won control of the House and Senate, as well as historic gubernatorial races, earlier this month](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/18/48/GettyImages-2302983239.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

When asked in a new Reuters/Ipsos poll what they thought of several prominent Democrats, Harris led with 45 percent of Americans who said she would be an excellent or good candidate in the 2028 election.

Biden’s former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg trailed, with 42 percent seeing him as an excellent or good candidate. California Governor Gavin Newsom followed, with 40 percent viewing him as an excellent or good candidate.

Thirty-eight percent of Americans thought Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, a progressive from New York, and Georgia Senator Jon Ossoff, who recently won re-election after making headline-grabbing jabs at Trump, would be excellent or good candidates.

Vice President JD Vance led other Republicans in the poll, with 62 percent of Americans believing he would be an excellent or good candidate in the 2028 election.

Another 58 percent said Secretary of State Marco Rubio would be an excellent or good choice, and 52 percent said the same for outgoing Florida Governor Ron DeSantis, who unsuccessfully ran against Trump for the nomination in 2024. Thirty-five percent of Americans said Texas Senator Ted Cruz would make an excellent or good candidate.
