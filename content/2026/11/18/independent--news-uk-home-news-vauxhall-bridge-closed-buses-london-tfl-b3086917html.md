London’s busiest bridge could be forced to close for a decade unless £60m is invested in repairs, Transport for London (TfL) has warned.

Vauxhall Bridge is at risk of deterioration without significant intervention to its steelwork, which is corroding due to water ingress, the transport body said.

TfL added the bridge, which carries 44,000 vehicles each day, could be out of use for buses and fire engines within two to four years, and closed to all vehicles within four to ten years unless the work is carried out.

In its application to the government’s £1bn structures fund, TfL highlighted the “significant heritage value” of the bridge and warned “closure would severely reduce network resilience, significantly increase congestion, disrupt public transport reliability and adversely affect economic activity across London and beyond”.

![TfL said significant investment is needed into Vauxhall Bridge](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/08/1273259161..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The letter notes that while the bridge’s waterproofing was renewed in 2020, further deterioration has left key structural elements in “very poor condition”, meaning an 18-tonne weight restriction is currently in place. TfL says if the work is not carried out a 7.5 tonne limit may need to be introduced by 2030, which would prevent use of the bridge by buses, coaches and many emergency service vehicles.

It adds that fascia panels along the bridge are showing signs of deterioration, creating a risk that loose material could fall onto the river below.

TfL is proposing to update key elements of the bridge’s structure and introduce measures to “mitigate the causes of ongoing deterioration and improve the long-term resilience, maintainability and serviceability of the bridge”.

![Vauxhall Bridge is central London's busiest Thames crossing](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/08/1273258554..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Without this scheme, Vauxhall Bridge will continue to deteriorate, resulting in further restrictions and ultimately full closure, leading to the loss of a critical Thames crossing,” the letter warns.

Speaking to BBC London, David Rowe, TfL's director of investment planning declined to give an exact figure but agreed it would cost around £60m. He added if the bid was unsuccessful, TfL would have to "look very, very carefully about where we prioritise our renewals".

"We have a certain amount that we can invest every year but with projects as big as Vauxhall Bridge... we really, really do need support alongside what we can bring".

London's Mayor Lord Khan told BBC London the city had struggled with its bridges because "we had anti-London governments in eight of my last 10 years".

"The good news is this government has notably increased massively the capital funding that TfL gets but also that we can bid now for infrastructure projects."

Successful bids are expected to be announced in the autumn.
