Fiona the hippo’s caregivers have given an update on her condition after she ingested a knit hat that was flung into her enclosure at the Cincinnati Zoo.

Fiona is receiving around-the-clock care after swallowing the hat 10 days ago. A zoo visitor had thrown the hat into the air and it got caught in a ceiling fan that tossed it into her enclosure.

Her caretakers began monitoring her closely right away and were optimistic that she could pass the hat through her system. She didn’t show any symptoms right after swallowing it but her appetite began decreasing days later.

Vets ran blood work, sedated her for further evaluation including an ultrasound and gave her fluids all with the goals of helping her pass the hat and keeping her comfortable.

![Fiona’s caretakers remain hopeful that she can pass the hat without serious issue](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/20/Screenshot-2026-11-18-at-9-49-57-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We know from consulting with specialists, both veterinary and human specialists and some of our colleagues and other experts, that surgery isn’t an option for full grown hippos. Our best chance for success with Fiona would be if she can pass this on her own,” a zoo official said in a video update late Monday.

They also borrowed a 9-foot scope from Columbus Zoo for a colonoscopy in hopes that they could reach the hat but were unsuccessful.

Fiona’s caretakers believe she is passing something through her small intestine and remain hopeful that she’ll soon be back to her usual self.

“At this point, what we can do is supportive care,” the zoo official said, adding that she’s receiving pain meds and all her favorite foods to try to get her to keep passing things through.

The zoo previously said in a statement: “Fiona will remain off habitat until this is resolved. We will share updates as we have more information.

“Please help us keep our animals safe by securing your belongings and taking extra care near habitats. Ingesting foreign objects can cause serious health problems for animals. If something falls into a habitat, please notify a Zoo staff member immediately.

![Fiona rose to fame in 2017 when she was born premature](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/33/AP26320773797442.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Thank you for your concern for Fiona and for keeping her dedicated care team in your thoughts.”

Fiona rose to fame in January 2017 when she was born six weeks premature. Her struggle to survive drew attention around the world.

At birth, she weighed just 29 pounds (13.2 kilograms), 25 pounds (11.3 kilograms) below the lowest birth weight ever recorded for her species.

Her progress was closely followed by fans and her popularity grew to the point that books were written about her. She also inspired a beer line, an ice cream flavor and all kinds of clothing.

One couple even got engaged in front of her exhibit, with Fiona appearing in the photo from underwater behind them.
