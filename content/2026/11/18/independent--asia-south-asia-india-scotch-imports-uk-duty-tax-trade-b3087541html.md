A trade dispute over the agreement between India and the UK has seen Indian authorities impose an effective duty of 162.5 per cent on bulk Scotch imports. The unexpected increase has jolted liquor firms, as the levy now exceeds the 150 per cent charged prior to the landmark deal, according to eight sources and a government document.

The India-UK trade agreement implemented in July halved tariff rates on Scotch to 75 per cent, a move hailed by Britain and businesses including Pernod Ricard and Diageo. These companies import bulk Scotch and blend it to create products for India's $65 billion liquor market.

However, in the first major dispute linked to the pact, Indian investigators argue firms are using incorrect customs classifications for bulk Scotch concentrates, deemed raw material for blended whiskies. Indian investigators contend that since May, these imports belong under a newly created customs line for "compound alcoholic preparations", which carries a 150 per cent tariff.

It comes as Prince William and Princess Kate travelled from to New Delhi after the Earthshot Prize awards to meet with Narendra Modi at his official residence complex, expressing their appreciation for the "warm welcome" offered by the Indian public.

The discussions serve as another significant milestone in William’s diplomatic efforts, leveraging the monarchy’s "soft power" to strengthen ties between the UK and key international partners.

These imports then attract a consumption tax which takes the full duty amount to 162.5%, according to a government document reviewed by Reuters and eight sources, including industry executives and customs agents, who have direct knowledge of the matter. As the standoff drags on, thousands of litres of bulk Scotch in more than 80 shipping containers belonging to Pernod, Diageo and Bacardi have been stuck at ports, Reuters reported last week.

Under a so-called provisional release pending investigation, duty at 75% of the goodsvalue needs to be paid up front, with the rest covered by bank guarantees, the sources said. "Industry has already reduced prices of Scotch products in the market when the India-UK pact ​kicked in, and ⁠now they are faced with higher duties again," said a source in ‌the liquor importing business.

Pernod, which owns UK-based Chivas Brothers, Diageo and many other Indian companies believe Scotch imports - bulk and bottled both - should be taxed only at 75% as per the trade pact. The British government has repeatedly said in statements it is working with New Delhi on the issue, while India has said the dispute will be resolved soon.

Responding to Reuters' request for comment on the 162.5% tariff and tax levied by India, a UK government spokesperson said "ministers are urgently working with the government of India to resolve this issue to ensure UK businesses can export under the tariffs agreed in the trade deal." Pernod and Diageo have moved to arrange bank guarantees and other funding to get their blocked containers released so that business can continue, while Bacardi is still deciding its next steps, said three of the sources.

Shares of Diageo in London and Pernod in France both dropped on the Reuters story, falling 1.5% and 1.25% respectively during trade. Pernod counts India as its biggest global market by volumes and with Diageo dominates the Indian spirits market. Diageo declined to comment while Pernod and Bacardi did not respond to queries. The Indian finance ministry and the tax authority also did not respond.

## Businesses fear disruptions

Long-drawn tariff disputes have long been a sore point for foreign investors and have hit the likes of Volkswagen and Samsung.

Indian officials say they only enforce the law of the land, but executives say they are often caught off guard by how New Delhi classifies imports.

Mumbai-listed majors Radico Khaitan, Allied Blenders and Distillers and Tilaknagar Industries all import bulk Scotch and are under scrutiny, like Pernod and Diageo.

Smaller companies can face more stress in arranging bank guarantees as they may require collateral. Such guarantees also need to be reported as contingent liabilities. "We have no alternative at this stage," said one Indian executive, referring to a potential disruption in the market because ‌of the lack of bulk Scotch. "Meeting the production requirements is extremely critical, even though bank guarantees stress working capital."

About 80% of Scotch whisky imports from Britain by volume arrive in bulk, and India is the biggest market for Scotch by volume. Most bulk Scotch imports go into making so-called Indian-Made Foreign Liquor, or IMFL whisky, ‌by mixing a little Scotch with other spirits. Such mass-market offerings are far cheaper ⁠than wholly imported whiskies like Chivas Regal or Johnnie Walker.

Whisky tariffs were a sticking point during years of protracted negotiations leading up to the trade deals celebrated July 15 implementation. Britain has said iconic UK products# including whiskies will benefit from the pact. The UK’s Scotch Whisky Association has forecast the trade deal could boost Scotch exports to India by $1.34 billion over five years.

A confidential memo from Indian investigators that was reported by Reuters last week said any misclassification under the India-UK pact needs to stop as it has "substantial revenue implications" for New Delhi. The liquor ‌industry sources in India said while the classification issue was being debated, they did ​not anticipate the imposition of the additional local tax.

The local tax addition is a googly, one industry source said, using a cricket term for a deceptive delivery from a bowler.
