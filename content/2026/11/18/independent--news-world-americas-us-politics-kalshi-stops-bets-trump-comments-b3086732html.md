Prediction market platform Kalshi has reportedly stopped offering contracts that allow users to wager on what President Donald Trump will say during public appearances, following a federal regulatory order over concerns that the markets could be manipulated.

The Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued a directive on Friday instructing the company to temporarily suspend all contracts tied to the president’s remarks on specific dates, as reported by *The Wall Street Journal*. Kalshi had previously said that its platform used security protocols to prevent market manipulation.

Concerns surrounding the contracts intensified earlier this year after a former White House teleprompter operator, Gabriel Perez, allegedly used inside information from the president’s speeches to place winning wagers on the site. Perez later reached a settlement with federal regulators over the incident.

Following that case, Democratic lawmakers have signaled plans to investigate prediction platforms as they prepare to assume control of key congressional committees after their wins in the November midterm elections.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren, who is expected to chair the Senate Banking Committee next year, wrote to the White House last week requesting details on its response and saying that the teleprompter episode “should have been a wake-up call” to enforce stricter ethics policies.

![Prediction market platform Kalshi has reportedly stopped bets on what President Donald Trump will say in public after federal regulators ordered a pause over concerns that the markets could be manipulated](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/21/56/GettyImages-2263571544.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Despite dropping markets tied directly to the president’s remarks, Kalshi intends to keep other contracts involving the administration active, according to people familiar with the matter who spoke to *The Wall Street Journal*. A contract tracking what Vice President JD Vance will say at an event on Wednesday remains live, as do wagers on which senior officials might depart the administration this year and whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick will keep his position.

The suspension marks a shift for Kalshi, which has long argued that so-called mention markets serve as a legitimate tool for businesses to hedge risk. At a New York event last month, Kalshi chief regulatory officer Sudhir Jain defended the company’s decision to keep listing Trump-related contracts even after regulators flagged them for insider trading risks.

![Kalshi had previously defended its Trump-related contracts as a legitimate way for businesses to hedge risk, even after federal regulators flagged them for insider trading concerns](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/21/2303226922..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“There are mention markets with true hedging utility, with true business cases, and we list those,” Jain told *The Wall Street Journal* at the time. “If you look at what we used to list, and what we list right now, we have significantly trimmed. We want to focus on the ones which we list right now, which are related to Trump, related to earnings, related to some keynote addresses, but very, very limited. I think we have cut that down by more than 50 percent.”

Although mention markets generate relatively low trading volumes compared to other contracts, they have drawn considerable attention online, where prediction-market influencers frequently livestream their wagers on major broadcasts like the State of the Union, as reported by *The Wall Street Journal*.

![The regulatory crackdown comes as the CFTC investigates rival platform Polymarket, and after Donald Trump Jr. stepped down as a Kalshi adviser following his firm’s investment in the competitor](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/12/22/2026-11-02T160743Z_487907113_RC2QFOAGSJ6X_RTRMADP_3_USA-VANCE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The CFTC, currently led by Trump appointee Michael Selig, has sought to balance support for the growing prediction-market sector with stricter oversight. In addition to pausing Kalshi’s Trump contracts, the agency has opened a broad investigation into Kalshi’s main rival, Polymarket.

Still, the CFTC has defended prediction platforms in court against state regulators who argue the sites operate as unlicensed gambling operations, intervening in cases across a dozen states — including Louisiana, where a judge ordered the platforms to stop operations last week.

Kalshi also previously had ties to the president's eldest son, Donald Trump Jr., who worked as an adviser to the firm until recently. The company stated that the two sides mutually agreed to part ways after 1789 Capital, a venture firm where Trump Jr. is a partner, increased its financial stake in Polymarket.
