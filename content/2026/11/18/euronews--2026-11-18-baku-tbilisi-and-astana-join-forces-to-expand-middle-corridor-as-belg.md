## Azerbaijan, Georgia and Kazakhstan have drawn up a joint plan to increase Middle Corridor freight capacity to around 30 million tonnes a year by 2029, as Belgian companies discuss a logistics hub in Kazakhstan and digital upgrades to the Asia-Europe trade route.

Azerbaijan, Georgia and Kazakhstan are stepping up cooperation to develop the Middle Corridor, an emerging trade route connecting China and Central Asia with European markets via the Caspian Sea and the South Caucasus to the Black Sea.

The three countries have developed a joint plan aimed at improving transport infrastructure, streamlining logistics, and making cross-border trade faster and more efficient.

The joint plan comes as European companies explore new investment opportunities along the route, including a proposed $50 million (€45.4mn) logistics hub in Kazakhstan and a digital project aimed at reducing delays at border crossings.

Together, the initiatives reflect growing efforts to make the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route, commonly known as the Middle Corridor, a more efficient and competitive connection between Asia and Europe.

The Iran war has severely disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, increasing the value of alternative trade routes for moving goods between Asia and Europe.

## Belgian companies explore major logistics investment

European businesses are also examining opportunities to participate in the build-up of the corridor’s infrastructure and operations.

During a working visit to Brussels on 16 November, Kazakh Foreign Minister Mukhtar Tileuberdi met with senior executives from Belgian companies Sarens Group, Ahlers Logistics, and BIM e-Solutions to discuss investment, logistics and digital technology.

According to Kazakhstan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, talks with Sarens Group Senior Advisor Hendrik Sarens and Matthias Sarens, Construction Director of the Transport and Logistics Centre at the Khorgos Customs & Logistics Platform, included the proposed $50mn (€45.4mn) investment in Kazakhstan's Zhetysu Region, near the Chinese border.

The planned transport and logistics hub would include a 2.1-kilometre bypass road, temporary storage facilities, and specialised infrastructure for handling heavy cargo.

If implemented, the hub is expected to increase capacity at the Nur Zholy border crossing, which is one of the gateways for goods entering Kazakhstan from China, and relieve freight congestion along the Kazakh-Chinese border.

Reducing congestion at this crossing could help improve the movement of freight entering the wider Middle Corridor network.

## A target of 30 million tonnes **a year** by 2029

In Georgia, Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Economy and Sustainable Development Mariam Kvrivishvili confirmed the joint plan in an interview with Georgia's Imedi TV.

She said Georgia was working "very closely with partner countries", "particularly Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan", to improve transport links and increase the volume of cargo moving through the corridor.

"This plan aims to ensure the transportation of 30 million tonnes of cargo through the Middle Corridor by 2029. We are working in constant cooperation with our partners and have identified joint regional measures," Kvrivishvili said.

Reaching that target will require improvements across a transport network involving railways, ports, shipping services and border crossings.

Kvrivishvili said Georgia plans to invest approximately $7 billion (€6.4bn) in transport infrastructure by 2029, covering ports, railways, roads and aviation infrastructure.

The joint measures focus on harmonising customs clearance, aligning tariff policies, and expanding port and rail capacity across the Caspian and South Caucasus segments.

Regional analysts note that hitting this ambitious volume target could fundamentally shift how international shippers view the route.

"The Middle Corridor has grown in strategic importance, but limited freight capacity remains one of its biggest constraints," said Bako Kheladze, a research fellow at the Turan Research Centre.

Kheladze pointed out that while the World Bank estimated in 2023 that the route could triple its freight volume by 2030 and cut transit times in half with the right investment and policies, it still operates on a much smaller scale than the Northern Route through Russia.

"If the three countries can reach that target and offer reliable transit times, the Middle Corridor could become a regular route for shippers between Asia and Europe. The Northern Route would still have an advantage in scale, but the Middle Corridor could compete for a meaningful share of freight even if transit through Russia becomes easier again," he added.

## Cross-regional connectivity

Meanwhile, in Brussels on Monday, EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos and International Partnerships Commissioner Jozef Síkela met with foreign ministers and other senior representatives of EU member states, the Republic of Moldova, Turkey, Ukraine, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan at the second Ministerial Meeting on Cross-Regional Security and Connectivity.

The parties noted the role of connectivity in maintaining economic resilience and sustainable growth.

Mukhtar Tileuberdi, Kazakh Minister of Foreign Affairs, emphasised that developing the country’s transport and transit links and modernising its infrastructure are among its strategic priorities.

“Kazakhstan is interested in further development of this transport route in cooperation with European and regional partners. This involves increasing infrastructure capacity, improving logistics processes, and creating a more efficient cargo delivery system,” he said.

Tileuberdi added that this ambition highlights the importance of the Global Gateway initiative, in particular in attracting investment, developing transport infrastructure, and implementing joint projects.

## Could digital technology speed up trade?

Infrastructure is only part of the challenge.

Moving freight efficiently across several countries also depends on customs procedures, documentation, and the exchange of information between transport operators.

Separate discussions with Ahlers Logistics CEO Dave van den Bos and BIM e-Solutions head Michael Barrie covered how to integrate software along the route, including a proposal for a 90-day Digital Middle Corridor pilot project.

The proposed trial would introduce end-to-end digital freight tracking and streamline cross-border customs data exchange to help reduce transit delays.

For European businesses, more reliable transit times and simpler border procedures could make the corridor more attractive for transporting goods between Asia and Europe.

The latest developments come as the European Union seeks to strengthen transport connections with Central Asia and the South Caucasus.
