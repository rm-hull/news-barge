David Lammy has urged Andy Burnham to campaign on a promise to rejoin the EU at the next election.

The former foreign secretary, who also served as deputy prime minister under Keir Starmer, said the prime minister’s Liverpool conference speech “blew the door wide open” on the issue.

“Burnham’s Labour should go into the next election clear that its intention is to rejoin the EU,” Lammy wrote in the Times. “Forming the next government would give it the mandate to begin formal negotiations.

“Once a deal is agreed, this should be put to the people. Unlike the last referendum, the public should have the opportunity to decide between two clearly defined choices.”

His intervention comes as Burnham prepares for a major UK-EU summit in Brussels amid renewed scrutiny over Britain’s relationship with the bloc.

The prime minister promised in his Labour conference speech in September that he would lay out different options to the country around the time of the November summit. But with the Brussels meeting approaching on Friday, Burnham appeared to prolong the timeline.

In a speech on Monday, he said he would start a discussion with the bloc on options after the summit, before presenting options to the public at a later date.

Pressed on whether reopening the Brexit topic had become toxic, the Labour leader told Sky News on Tuesday: “I’m in a negotiation about improving the Brexit deal. That’s what I’m doing this week. We’ll see how it goes. And then we will consider whether there are options to further the relationship with the EU. And I’ll set that out later this year alongside the 10-year plan.

“But I don’t agree with you at all, actually, that it’s a toxic debate.”

He repeated his pledge to stick to the 2024 Labour manifesto’s red lines – which rule out returning to the customs union, single market or free movement – before a general election and that the “British public will be in charge of whatever comes next”.

He denied changing the timeframe he had laid out in September, saying: “I will set out next month what the next phase looks like. And that is exactly what I said at my conference speech.”

Burnham also launched an attack on the former Conservative prime minister Boris Johnson, who alongside David Cameron has cautioned him against returning to the bloc.

He also said the youth guarantee scheme to get young people into work, which has formed part of the reset negotiations with the EU, would only be open to British citizens.

Concerns have been raised that the scheme to be agreed at the summit in Brussels on Friday, which could allow tens of thousands of young Europeans to work temporarily in the UK each year, and vice versa, could cut into new support for young Britons.

The former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn announced his recommendations for how to bring down the number of young people not in education, employment or training (Neet) on Tuesday.

He said British young people should be the priority for hiring, whatever the outcome of the EU deal. The prime minister later told ITV’s Good Morning Britain he was looking to create “priority opportunities” for young people. He was asked if the jobs guarantee scheme, which aims to help 90,000 young people into work by March 2029, would exclude Europeans.

He said: “Yes, because we have a situation at the moment through the youth guarantee where employers receive a payment to take on somebody who’s been long-term unemployed. So that’s the scheme and we can, of course, design that scheme to support young British kids who are not in education, employment or training, and absolutely that will be the way we go forward.”
