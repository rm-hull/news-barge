Yorkshire Water told Ripon City Council that overflows at Blossomgate and Borrage Bridge are in its 2025 to 2030 plans, but it could give no costs or completion dates.

Yorkshire Water has two Ripon storm overflows in its current investment programme, one at Blossomgate and one at Borrage Bridge. But it could not tell city councillors what the work will cost or when it will be finished.

The company answered questions at a meeting of Ripon City Council on 9 November. The council has now published its own summary of the discussion. Everything Yorkshire Water said below is taken from that summary.

## What Yorkshire Water told the council

According to the council’s summary, Yorkshire Water said:

* the Blossomgate and Borrage Bridge schemes would mainly take surface water out of the combined sewers, freeing up room for foul sewage
* both schemes are still at the design stage, with no detailed specifications, costs or completion dates
* of 24 overflows linked to the River Ure, five are suspected of discharging in dry weather this year, and investigations are under way
* it runs about 2,200 overflows across its region, averaging 31.4 discharges each at the start of the current investment period, with a target of about 16 by 2030
* its regional programme for 2025 to 2030 is worth £1.5 billion, but it could not say how much of that is for Ripon

Councillors said residents could face several more years of discharges before anything changes.

## Sewage reported near a play area on the Skell

The council heard reports of untreated sewage entering the River Skell beside a children’s play area. Residents also reported sanitary products visible around the discharge pipework.

Yorkshire Water suggested some of the material may have collected on screens meant to stop solids reaching the river. It said it could not confirm what had happened. It agreed to investigate the location and send a written reply, which the Mayor of Ripon, Councillor Andrew Williams, said would be shared with the resident and published.

## How often Ripon’s overflows spilled in 2025

A combined storm overflow is a relief valve on a sewer carrying both wastewater and rainwater. When heavy rain overloads the pipe, it releases diluted sewage into a river instead of letting it back up into homes and streets.

We pulled the 2025 figures for the 20 Yorkshire Water overflows in and around Ripon from the Environment Agency’s Event Duration Monitoring return for 2025. The agency counts spills in blocks of 12 and 24 hours, so one count can cover several shorter releases.

Nine more overflows recorded between none and eight spills each. Between them, all 20 recorded 355 counted spills and about 959 hours of spilling in 2025.

The two named schemes are not the busiest overflows on the list. Borrage Bridge spilled 28 times but for only 14.25 hours. Its long-term average in the same return is 54 spills a year, so 2025 was a quieter year for it.

Blossomgate’s figure is likely to understate the real total. Its monitor worked for only 69.38% of the year because of a sensor fault, which the return says was fixed in January.

## What the council decided

Councillors agreed, unanimously, to:

* review Yorkshire Water’s progress in June 2027
* ask North Yorkshire Council’s Transport, Economy and Environment Scrutiny Committee to examine Yorkshire Water’s performance, including in Ripon

Councillor Tony Duncan asked whether Ripon’s sewers could cope with the new homes planned around the city. Yorkshire Water said new estates generally use sustainable drainage, such as attenuation ponds, to keep rainwater out of combined sewers. It agreed to send more detail on its plans and timescales.

Yorkshire Water has promised written answers on the two schemes’ scope, cost and delivery dates, the dry-weather spills, and the effect of housing growth. The city council says it will publish them.

## What it means for you

* **If you use the Skell or the Ure:** nothing changes at Blossomgate or Borrage Bridge yet. Both schemes are still being designed, and no start date has been given.
* **To see when a particular overflow is spilling:** Yorkshire Water publishes a near real-time live storm overflow map.
* **To report pollution you can see:** the Environment Agency’s incident hotline is 0800 80 70 60, open 24 hours.
