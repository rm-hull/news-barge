## Regulators called the robotaxis an "imminent hazard" after one tried to overtake police cars and three others drove into a live roadworks zone, just as the company prepares to expand into Europe.

Waymo has taken its driverless taxis off highways across the United States after California temporarily banned the ride-hailing service from its freeways over safety concerns.

The company, a subsidiary of Google’s parent company Alphabet, has "temporarily restricted freeway operations", a spokesperson said in a statement on Tuesday.

As well as California, the pause covers highway rides in the other cities where Waymo offers them, including Phoenix in Arizona, Austin in Texas and Miami in Florida.

The spokesperson said the suspension would allow the company to "evaluate our performance" after the incidents in California, adding that Waymo prioritises safety.

The move is likely to add to the debate over the risks of driverless taxis, which are beginning to expand into Europe.

Waymo has been testing its vehicles in London, United Kingdom, since last year and plans to expand to Munich in Germany and Madrid in Spain in 2027.

One of the incidents went viral last week. A video filmed on Interstate 280 in San Francisco showed a Waymo vehicle approaching at speed and trying to overtake two California Highway Patrol cars. The police cars had their lights flashing to deliberately slow traffic behind a slow-moving construction truck.

The episode led California’s Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV), which is responsible for road safety, to temporarily ban Waymo vehicles from the state’s highways from 11 November.

According to the DMV, a separate incident also led to the decision. In that case, "three Waymo autonomous vehicles entered an active construction closure" in the San Francisco Bay Area, an agency spokesperson said.

"Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present," the statement said.

The restrictions "will remain in place until Waymo demonstrates ... it has taken effective corrective actions", it added.

Waymo stressed that no one was injured and no collisions occurred in the construction zone incident.

The company says it completes half a million trips a week. It also says its vehicles are involved in 95 per cent fewer serious crashes than human-driven cars.
