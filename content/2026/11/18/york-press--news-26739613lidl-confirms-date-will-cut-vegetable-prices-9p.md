Lidl has confirmed the exact date they will be slashing the price of some vegetables to just 9p ahead of Christmas.

Supermarkets across the nation reduced vegetable prices last December and Lidl have pledged to do the same this year.

The chain say that from Saturday December 19 bags of Christmas dinner trimmings like sprouts (500g) , potatoes (2kg), carrots (1kg) and parsnips (550g) will cost just 9p.

The news comes after Aldi revealed their veg will be priced at just 8p, which is 1p cheaper than Lidl's offer.

However Lidl claim to sell the cheapest Christmas dinner on the market, with a cost of £11.11 to feed a family of eight at £1.39 per person.

This is led by their offer on a Birchwood British Small Turkey (3kg) which is priced at £8.85 and can be paired with their 170g Sage and Onion Stuffing Mix for just 45p.

Yorkshire puddings (15 per pack) will be 46p and 300g of Gravy Granules (300g) will be just 99p.

It is not clear when these prices will change.

Richard Bourns, Lidl GB Chief Commercial Officer, sought to reassure customers that the reduction in prices are funded by the supermarket so farmers won't be paid less.

Richard said: "With household budgets remaining under pressure we are on a mission to deliver unbeatable festive value to our shoppers this Christmas.

"By pricing a complete Christmas dinner at less than £1.40 per head, we’re giving reassurance from the very start of the season that we won’t be beaten on price and we won’t compromise on quality.

"We’ve already invested £400 million in lowering prices this year and we aren’t stopping there.

"Between additional price cuts and a further £75 million in Lidl Plus savings in the run up to Christmas, we’re making sure British families get the value they really deserve.

"No guesswork, no festive price shock - just unbeatable value that's locked in.

"Crucially, that value never comes at the expense of producers and every penny of our seasonal promotions is funded directly by us.

"That means we guarantee our farmers the same full, fair, contracted price they rely on throughout the season."
