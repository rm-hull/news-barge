"We see a strong demand for international train travel and want to continue developing the possibilities for train travel between Scandinavia and the rest of Europe," Jonas Abrahamsson, CEO of SJ, said in a press release. "Together with RDC, we can restart SJ EuroNight and at the same time create better conditions for stable and long-term sustainable traffic."

The service will start with three departures a week in each direction, two of which will go all the way to Berlin and one of which will stop in Hamburg. From January 19th, two will stop in Hamburg and only the third will travel onward to Berlin. Travellers from western Sweden will be able to join the service in Lund.

It will also stop at Copenhagen Airport and Padborg, both in Denmark, with some services also stoping at Copenhagen South station.

SJ launched the EuroNight service in 2022 after Sweden's Green Party won a commitment from the then Social Democrat government to subsidise such a service.

The company operated the service in collaboration with Germany's RDC, but stopped the service in September after Sweden's new Moderate-led government ended the subsidy, at which point RDC took over the route.

Snälltåget, which is owned by Germany's Rethmann Gruppe, also operates a night train from Stockholm to Berlin.

In the press release, SJ said that it had decided the route could be economically viable without subsidies if it operates fewer departures using a longer train and keeps the same locomotive for the whole journey.

"The changes create conditions for better punctuality and delivery, as well as creating better financial conditions for continued operations," the company said in the press release.

RDC will continue selling tickets on its nachtexpress.de and nattexpressen.se websites until January 31st, with the tickets also available on SJ.se, SJ's app, bahn.de, and DB Navigator.
