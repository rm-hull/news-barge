## Zelenskyy did not name the 10 settlements and said Ukraine’s delegation would raise the plan with US mediators, while Putin has separately suggested a similar operation in Kostiantynivka, which Ukraine says Russia does not control.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has proposed 10 new humanitarian evacuation operations in the Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions, modelled on a recent ceasefire that allowed hundreds of civilians to leave the Russian-occupied Ukrainian town of Oleshky.

Zelenskyy said on Tuesday the proposal came up at a meeting where participants discussed the results of recent trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi.

"Ukraine proposed another 10 locations in temporarily occupied areas of the Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions where a similar humanitarian operation is required," he wrote on social media.

He did not name the settlements. Ukraine's diplomatic delegation will raise the proposal with US mediators.

A truce in Oleshky from 27 October to 1 November, initiated by US First Lady Melania Trump, allowed residents to leave through a safe corridor.

Melania Trump said almost 800 people were evacuated in total. Those who chose to move to Ukrainian-controlled territory were taken via Gomel in Belarus to the Chernihiv region, while others reportedly chose to evacuate to Russia. Neither Ukraine nor Russia disclosed the breakdown of figures.

Kyiv subsequently said Russia had used the ceasefire to rotate its troops on the left bank of Kherson region.

Verkhovna Rada Human Rights Commissioner Dmytro Lubinets said he had agreed with his Russian counterpart Yana Lantratova to appeal to the US First Lady to extend the Oleshky model to other occupied settlements.

Russia's Vladimir Putin made a parallel proposal during his meeting with Trump and US envoy Steve Witkoff in the Kremlin on 12 November, suggesting a similar operation in Kostiantynivka in Donetsk region, which he said was under Russian "control".

Ukrainian presidential adviser Dmytro Lytvyn said Russia was likely proposing a ceasefire there for the same reason as in Oleshky — to rotate its troops.

Ukrainian military statements and open-source intelligence research indicate that Russian forces occupy only parts of the city's eastern and south-western outskirts, with a significant portion in a contested zone and another part held by Ukraine's armed forces.

Putin has claimed Russian forces have occupied the city on multiple occasions.
