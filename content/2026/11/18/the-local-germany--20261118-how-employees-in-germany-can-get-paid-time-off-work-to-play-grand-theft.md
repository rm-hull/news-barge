Working and living in Germany comes with some pretty satisfying perks – especially for those who like to keep their work-life balance tipped towards the life side.

The trick here is that in some cases you first have to know that a benefit exists in order to take advantage of it. This is especially true for Germany's foreign residents, who often aren't fully aware of all of the benefits they may qualify for.

A good example of this is Germany's paid educational leave benefit, known as *Bildungsurlaub*.

By taking advantage of this benefit, workers in Germany can get up to five days a year of paid time away from their regular work duties to pursue education or training.

And yes, we double checked, and there is a legitimate *Bildungsurlaub*course in which participants play "open world" video games like Grand Theft Auto 6.**'Learning skills for tomorrow's workplace'**

The educational leave program in question can be found on the website Dein-Bildungsurlaub.de, which is a kind-of online catalog for *Bildungsurlaub* opportunities.

On Wednesday, November 18th – just one day before the scheduled release of the much anticipated Grand Theft Auto 6 – the above website was promoting its open-world gaming course at the top of it's homepage: "On 19/11/26, steal cars, shoot, deal drugs, and educate yourself in the process. This is possible during our gaming education holiday!"

On its official webpage the course is titled, "Between hunts and gang wars: Learning skills for tomorrow's workforce".

A description of the class adds that "open-world games become a learning environment for digitalisation, creativity and innovation..." and notes that the course consists of "keynotes, workshops and guided gameplay sessions".

The cost to enrol is €119 and participants need a laptop or computer, stable internet and their own licensed copy for a suitable open-world game such as GTA V or GTA VI.

According to the website, the online course is recognised in 12 German states.

The next course was set to take place for three days from November 18th to the 20th. A note on the website, seen on the 18th, said that a few places were still available.

But participants who missed this course can keep their eyes on the Dein Bildungsurlaub website, or contact the website organisers, to sign up for this or a similar course in the future.

While the course mentioned above seems specifically targeted at employees eager to get a few days off work to play Grand Theft Auto, it's not the only video game related educational leave opportunity out there.

A quick search of another educational leave portal (Bildungsurlaub.de) reveals a few options that people can register for in the coming year.

For example, there's a five day course in Hattingen next year titled, "Gaming and politics: How computer games shape our understanding of the (working) world and society". Or another one aimed at educators and people who work with children is called, "The world of games: Digital games as a cultural, learning and social space".

**How do I take paid educational leave?**

For regularly employed workers in Germany, the *Bildungsurlaub* entitlement comes in addition to any regular paid holiday you have.

All employees except civil servants are entitled regardless of nationality. The only people left out are employees in Bavaria, where the benefit is not guaranteed.

Employers are effectively required to let their employees' take time (up to five days for full-time workers) for recognised paid educational leave programs.

Also your boss cannot reject your request to attend a training based on the contents of the course. Beside developing professional skills, the leave is generally understood to also cover political education and personal development – not only training which is directly relevant to your current position.

However, your boss can reject or postpone your request for specific and verifiable reasons around scheduling, like if your absence during a particular week would disrupt business operations.

Generally workers would be expected to request leave at least four to eight weeks in advance.
