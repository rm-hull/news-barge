## Photos reveal hidden rot beneath historic seafront building

View Of Victoria Court From The Cresent. Alan Wood & Partners

PHOTOS have revealed serious hidden damage beneath one of Filey’s best-known seafront buildings, prompting a major repair project at the Grade-II listed property.

North Yorkshire Council has approved repair works at Victoria Court on The Crescent, part of the imposing mid-19th-century terrace overlooking Filey Bay. The application relates to an enclosed courtyard used by one of the flats within the building.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21734703.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Views Showing Rotten Deck And Joist Including Top Corner Of Window Opening Which Requires Blocking Up With Masonry. Victoria Court, Filey. AW & Partners

Beneath the patio surface, timber decking and supporting joists have been badly affected by water ingress, the photographs reveal, and planners were told the structure is now failing due to rot and long-term moisture damage.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21734705.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Views Of The Patio Area, Victoria Court, In Autumn 2025. AW & Partners

“The courtyard is itself a modern 20th-century addition to the property and is currently in failure due to water damage,” according to submitted plans.

The photographs show sections of the patio floor removed to reveal decaying timbers below, standing water around drainage points and a boarded-up basement window beneath the structure.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21734708.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Rear View Of Victoria Court. The Patio Is Situated Behind The Higher Parapet Area Outlined In Red And Is Not Visible From Anywhere Outside The Property. Alan Wood & Partners

Engineers concluded major intervention was needed to prevent further deterioration.

To repair the damage, the existing floor structure will be removed and replaced with a modern beam-and-block floor supported by steelwork. As part of the scheme, the boarded basement window will be permanently blocked up using reclaimed brick and traditional lime mortar.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21734704.jpg?type=mds-article-620)View Of General Basement Arrangement Below Current Patio Deck In Reverse Angle. Victoria Court. AW & Partners

Planning officers said the deterioration had begun to affect the historic building itself.

The council’s report states: “It is evident that there are defects with the existing structure and these in turn are impacting on the historic fabric of the rear of the listed building.”

Because the repairs are confined to an enclosed rear courtyard that cannot be seen from public viewpoints, planners concluded the work would not harm the building’s historic character.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21734701.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Basement Survey Photo Below Patio. Victoria Court, Filey. AW & Partners

Conservation officers initially sought more detail about the materials and repair methods being proposed, but later confirmed they were satisfied after additional information was submitted.

The report concludes that the repairs would have “a positive effect on the designated heritage asset.”

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21734706.jpg?type=mds-article-620)View Of General Basement Arrangement Below Current Patio Deck, Victoria Court. AW & Partners

Victoria Court forms part of the Grade II listed terrace at 15-21 The Crescent, one of the most recognisable architectural features on Filey’s seafront.

Built in the mid-19th century as a row of houses and later converted into apartments, the terrace remains a prominent landmark overlooking the bay.

Listed building consent was granted by North Yorkshire Council, subject to conditions, on November 11.
