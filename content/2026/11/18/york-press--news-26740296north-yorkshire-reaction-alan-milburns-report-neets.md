## York charity and chamber gives cautious backing to Milburn youth employment proposals

York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith with construction apprentices. *(Image: Pic supplied)</i>*

A York-based charity has welcomed proposals from former minister Alan Milburn to tackle the number of young people not in education, employment or training (Neet).

It adds to support from the West and North Yorkshire Chamber and York and North Yorkshire mayor David Skaith.

Some 13 per cent (almost 1 million in the UK) of 18-24s are classed as Neets, with Mr Milburn urging the country aims to reduce this to 5 per cent by 20236.

The Tony Blair-era minister has published a report on how to achieve this.

Among the measures is an “opportunity guarantee” and a new Youth Opportunity Service to give young people a named adviser and personalised participation plan on what the young need to do to find work.

Schools will also have to identify those at risk of becoming Neet and be required to offer work experience.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/19656097.jpg?type=mds-article-620)West & North Yorkshire Chamber's Mark Casci. *(Image: Pic supplied)*

There would also be 150,000 more places for 18-24s in further education and other training.

College funding would depend on the success of such graduates.

The government would aim to double the number taking out apprenticeships, with employers receiving incentives to achieve this.

Employers taking on a young NEET should receive £3,000, with small employers with fewer than 50 staff getting an extra £2,000.

There would be lower National Insurance costs for 21-24s, starting in retail and hospitality.

Benefits would also be changed with young people no longer getting the health top up of Universal Credit if they have a work-limiting condition.

A new benefit would replace PIP payments, Mr Milburn also proposed.

The York-based Family Fund, which for 50 years has provided families with disabled or seriously ill children with grants and other support, has welcomed the proposals.

Helen Gravestock, its director of external affairs, said: “We welcome the focus on more joined-up, personalised support, greater employer responsibility, and creating opportunities for disabled young people to access education, training and work.

“The challenge now is in the detail. Any reforms must give disabled young people and their families confidence that they will be better supported, not worse off.”

She added: “Eligibility criteria, ongoing support, and meaningful involvement of disabled young people and their families in designing these reforms will be crucial. Most importantly, these ambitions must be backed by investment if they are to deliver lasting change.”

The West and North Yorkshire Chamber has called Alan Milburn’s proposals “bold and ambitious” but noted government measures had raised the cost of employing young people by 70 per cent over the past decade.

![](https://www.yorkpress.co.uk/resources/images/21733025.jpg?type=mds-article-620)Helen Gravestock of Family Fund *(Image: Family Fund)*

The chamber’s head of policy Mark Casci welcomed the better work experience, improved career support and earlier help for young people at risk of young people dropping out.

But Mr Casci said recent higher National Insurance costs and extra responsibilities for firms through the Employment Rights Act had made employers more cautious to hire, making it harder for the young to get their critical first step on the jobs ladder.

He added: “A glide path to reduce NICs is welcome, but this must happen sooner rather than later, and it should not be sector specific. Otherwise, it risks adding complexity and blocking off potential career paths.”

David Skaith, mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: “Alan Milburn’s review is a vital wake-up call for us all. Too many young people are locked out of education, employment and training.

"It's time to stand up to this crisis and reset the system that champions the type of cradle to career work we're already doing, ensuring we support people from the early years right through to the workplace with guaranteed opportunities and local support.”

The Labour mayor added: “We’ll work with our partners to make sure that we have the right, locally designed and locally driven, solutions to meet this challenge.”
