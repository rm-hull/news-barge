Medical professionals have called for an inquiry after the regulator sent details concerning 223 doctors to a national newspaper journalist "in error".

Stating it was "very sorry", the General Medical Council (GMC) confirmed the incident was reported to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

The body said it requested that the data be deleted and has reached out to affected medics to explain the mistake.

The details were provided to a reporter from *The Times.*

The Doctors’ Association UK (DAUK) urged an independent investigation.

DAUK said the material included complaint summaries alleging antisemitism or Islamophobia alongside GMC reference numbers, which enable identification on the public register.

Ethnicity information was also included for some staff, DAUK added.

It said some of the complaints had been closed without investigation.

![Doctors’ Association UK (DAUK) called for an independent investigation](https://static.independent.co.uk/2024/01/29/16/HEALTH%20GMC%20%2015524044.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

For some medics the data breach was the first time they were told about a complaint, DAUK said.

Dr Matt Kneale, DAUK co-chairman, said, “If a doctor sent identifiable patient information to a newspaper, they could expect to be referred to the GMC.

“The GMC should hold itself to the same standard of confidentiality it expects from doctors.

“It owes every affected doctor a clear account of what it sent, how that happened and what it is doing to get the information deleted.”

A spokesperson for the British Medical Association (BMA) added: “The BMA is aware that a number of doctors have received communication from the GMC to say that it erroneously sent personal identifiable data about them to The Times newspaper when responding to a media request.

“The GMC must take action to ensure such a breach never occurs again.

“The BMA will be looking at ways it can support these members and we would urge any affected members to contact us via our website.”

A GMC spokesperson said: “We are dealing with an incident after information relating to 223 doctors was emailed to a journalist in error.

“We have contacted the affected doctors to explain what has happened, to apologise and provide information and support.

“We have requested the information sent in error is deleted. We have also reported the incident to the Information Commissioner’s Office.”

The spokesperson added: “We recognise the concern this will cause to both those impacted personally and other registrants and we are very sorry this happened.

“Our priority continues to be supporting those affected and taking steps to reduce the impact of this incident.”
