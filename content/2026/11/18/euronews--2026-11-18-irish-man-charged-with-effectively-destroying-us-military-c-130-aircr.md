## Daithi O’Corrain, described by supporters as an independent peace activist, was arrested immediately after the attack by Ireland's police force, An Garda Síochána.

An Irish man has been charged with damaging a US military C-130 aircraft at an airport in Ireland in an attack that left the plane "effectively destroyed," prosecutors said on Tuesday.

Daithi O'Corrain, 48, also known as David Curran, faces up to 10 years in prison if convicted of destruction of government property.

O'Corrain was indicted by a grand jury in New York on Monday and the indictment was unsealed on Tuesday.

O'Corrain is expected to be "brought to and arrested in the Southern District of New York," the indictment said.

According to prosecutors, O'Corrain breached security at Shannon Airport on 11 April and barricaded himself inside a parked US Air National Guard C-130 Hercules.

He allegedly cut wires and hydraulic lines and smashed every gauge in the cockpit.

O'Corrain also inflicted damage on the exterior of the aircraft with an axe, prosecutors said, and poured ball bearings into the transport plane's fuel tanks.

"As a result of O'Corrain's attack, the aircraft was effectively destroyed," the office of the US Attorney for the Southern District of New York said in a statement.

"The damage inflicted rendered the aircraft inoperable, and the aircraft, worth approximately $60 million (€54 million), will not return to active flight status."

No motive was given for the attack but prosecutors said it involved "months of planning and preparation."

O’Corrain, described by supporters as an independent peace activist, was arrested immediately after the attack by Ireland's police force.

He was charged with causing criminal damage and trespassing.

The Limerick Post daily reported he is from Cork, about 97 kilometres southeast of Shannon.

Shannon Airport, which was used by President Donald Trump and Air Force One during his visit to Ireland in September, has had several security breaches in the last 18 months, including three people who were accused of spray-painting a US military plane and three others who drove to a restricted area where a US military jet was parked.
