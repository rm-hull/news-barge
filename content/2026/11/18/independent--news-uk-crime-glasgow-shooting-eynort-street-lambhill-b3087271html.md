A 27-year-old woman is in a “critical but stable” condition after being shot in Glasgow, in what police describe as a targeted attack.

The shooting is being treated as attempted murder.

The woman was attacked at an address on Eynort Street, Lambhill, at around 11.45pm on Monday.

Police and emergency responders attended, and she was taken to hospital in a serious but stable condition.

Other members of her family were present at the address during the attack.

Police Scotland says extensive resources have been allocated to finding the perpetrator.

Detective Chief Inspector Jennifer McCulloch, from Police Scotland's major investigation team, told journalists on Wednesday: "Her injuries are consistent with being shot with a firearm.

"She is currently described as being critical but stable.

“There were other family members within the household at the time, and none of them were reported to be injured.”

Asked if the shooting was treated as an attempted murder, she said: “Yes, it has, and I would like to take this opportunity to appeal for any, if any, information to come forward and to please contact the police.”

Ms McCulloch was unable to provide details on the injuries the 27-year-old sustained.

![The woman was attacked at an address on Eynort Street, Lambhill, at about 11.45pm on Monday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/18/Eynort-St-Glasgow.png?quality=75&width=640&trim=0%2C0%2C0%2C0&auto=webp)

Asked whether the incident was gangland-related, she said: "At this time, the investigation is very much at an early stage, and we are keeping an open mind.

“But what I would say is that on a daily basis, we tirelessly target those involved in serious and organised crime, and violence will not be tolerated within our communities.”

She added: "We work to disrupt the activities of those involved in serious crime.

"Violence will not be tolerated within the community, and we appreciate this is obviously a very shocking and unusual incident, and we appreciate that it may be concerning for people within the area.

“However, I would just like to reiterate there are additional patrols within the area. If anyone has any concerns or information, please approach our officers.”

Superintendent Jackie Dunbar said: "This was a shocking incident for the local community in Lambhill, and I completely understand there will be concerns for those living in the area.

"But please be assured we are doing everything we can to identify and trace those responsible.

"Violence and the carrying of firearms is completely unacceptable.

"High visibility patrols remain in the area and there will be an increased police presence as our investigation continues.

"If anyone has any concerns, or information, please speak to these officers.

Anyone with information has been asked to contact Police Scotland on 101, quoting incident number 3717 of Monday 16 November 2026.

They may also speak to Crimestoppers anonymously on 0800 555 111.
