Former Conservative defectors to Reform UK have been barred from shaping the party’s immigration policy, its home affairs spokesperson has claimed, excluding in effect the majority of the party’s MPs.

“If you are in the category of being concerned about [former Conservatives being involved in Reform], none of the above are going to be involved in immigration policy or borders policy,” Zia Yusuf told a podcast by the Spectator magazine.

Michael Gove, the Spectator editor and former Conservative minister, challenged Yusuf over how Reform intended to bring down migration numbers, given there were so many former Tory MPs in its ranks – including the former immigration minister Robert Jenrick – who had failed to do so while in government.

Yusuf said: “There’s a reason why Nigel’s given me this brief and not them.”

Yusuf was embroiled in a feud with Jenrick before the former Tory minister made the jump to Reform earlier this year, becoming its economics spokesperson.

In addition to Jenrick, the former home secretary Suella Braverman, the former DWP shadow minister Danny Kruger and the former Foreign Office shadow minister Andrew Rosindell are among the Tory MPs to have defected to Reform.

Yusuf qualified his comments by saying Braverman and Jenrick had either been sacked or resigned “over immigration policy or issues around culture”. The comments raise questions about unity at the top of Reform.

Braverman, Reform’s education spokesperson, and Jenrick have waded into immigration and cultural issues since joining Reform. The claims that former Tories have no role in immigration policy for Reform also appear to be at odds with, for example, Jenrick’s announcement in June of plans for a levy on migrant labour.

Reform’s grassroots members have long appeared suspicious of former Conservatives in their midst. Farage himself has frequently had to push back against claims that his party had become “the Conservative party 2.0” and said in January that Reform would not accept any more defectors after local elections were held on 7 May. Jenrick and others who defected nonetheless maintain a strong influence within Reform.

Tom Milford, a former spokesperson for Jenrick, has become Reform’s head of press, while the MP himself continues to generate slickly produced videos for social media covering a range of topics including law and order.

Jenrick is often mooted alongside Yusuf and Richard Tice as the most likely successor to Farage if he was to stand down.

The Labour party said Yusuf had “admitted that most Reform MPs can’t be trusted on their migration policies”.

Anna Turley, the Labour MP and minister of state for border security and asylum, said: “They don’t trust two-thirds of their MPs to work on their immigration policies, yet they expect the country to trust them to secure Britain’s borders.”
