An Egyptian editor has been put on trial in a case widely derided as a sham, as Cairo intensifies its repression of media in the country.

The court had last week postponed the retrial of Lina Attalah, the editor-in-chief of Mada Masr, a prominent independent news site, on charges of publishing false news and running an unlicensed website, the site said.

Nearly 140 human rights groups, along with UN and EU officials, have condemned the legal case, presenting it as a litmus test on whether independent journalism can survive in Egypt under tightening press censorship.

European MEPs have sent a letter to the EU’s top leaders urging them to use the bloc’s relationship with Egypt to pressure them to “end their escalating crackdown on independent media and freedom of expression”.

![Attalah's retrial began on Wednesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/download..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The retrial was called after Ms Attalah's defence submitted a request on Wednesday to change the judge, after he had ruled against her in a separate trial on 27 October. The judge postponed the case for a week until the Court of Appeals decides on the change.

In the retrial case, Attalah was sentenced in absentia in 2024 to a year in prison and a 2 million EGP fine (£28,200). She only learned of that sentence on 27 October, while she was being released after the ruling that shuttered her news site.

“On October 27, I stood before a judge in a courtroom in Cairo with my defense team of eight committed lawyers beside me,” Ms Attalah wrote in an Op-Ed published this week.

“A week earlier, I had been summoned for interrogation on charges of running a website without a license. It was the sixth time over the last few years that I had faced prosecutorial questioning.”

![Attalah was convicted on 27 October in a separate trial](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/download.-1..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In the ruling last month, the judge fined Ms Attalah 3 million Egyptian pounds ($42,300) and ordered the closure of Mada Masr after a single hearing on a separate charge that the site had operated without a licence.

Mada Masr says it has sought a licence after a 2018 media law brought it in as a requirement - but it is yet to receive a decision. Egypt's Supreme Judicial Council later rejected suggestions that the trial was unfair, but an appeal against this ruling is due to be heard on November 25.

The letter from MEPs, addressed to EU Commissioner Ursula von der Leyen and chief diplomat Kaja Kallas, called on the bloc to withhold its next tranche of the Macro-Financial Assistance to Egypt until it has assessed a clear improvement in human rights policy and practise, including media freedom.

The country is consistently featured near the bottom of the World Press Freedom Index, an annual ranking published by Reporters Without Borders (RSF), ranked at 168th place out of 179 in 2026.
