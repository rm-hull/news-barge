## Ursula von der Leyen unveils a new joint-debt package for Kyiv, an unprecedented US-China-Russia trilateral summit unfolds at APEC, industry figures warn Europe cannot sleep through the AI revolution.

## On today's show:

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen has proposed a new **€45 billion loan for Ukraine for 2027**, backed by joint EU borrowing against the bloc's budget. The facility (split between**€25 billion for military needs** and**€20 billion for budget support**) is designed to cover roughly two-thirds of Kyiv's anticipated €70 billion financing shortfall next year.

Von der Leyen stated that Ukraine will only be required to repay the funds once Russia pays war reparations, calling it the best way to give Kyiv "a strong hand at the negotiation table." However, the proposal requires unanimous backing from all 27 member states ahead of next week's EU summit, where resistance from Hungary, Slovakia, Czechia, and Bulgaria threatens to stall approval.

**Trump, Xi, and Putin set for unprecedented trilateral summit in China**

US President Donald Trump has arrived in Shenzhen, China, for the APEC summit, where he is set to take part in a high-stakes, unprecedented trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. The three leaders are expected to discuss major global security issues, including regional conflicts such as Ukraine and Iran, alongside trade and nuclear and AI guardrails.

The summit gives Putin a major diplomatic platform alongside the world’s other two leading economic and military powers, while Beijing uses the APEC stage to project stability and economic cooperation. Speaking at a CEO summit on the sidelines of the gathering, Putin declared that “sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions” have “outlived their usefulness and have largely lost their intimidating effect.”

Joining us for analysis on this diplomatic development are S&D MEP **Sakis Arnaoutoglou** and MEP**Toomas Hendrik Ilves.Tech leaders warn Europe cannot sleep through the AI revolution**

Meanwhile, in an open letter titled *“An Urgent Call: Europe Cannot Sleep Through the AI Revolution,”* 59 industry figures warn that Europe is currently competing against the United States and China with only a fraction of their capital, computing power, and scaling capacity. The tech leaders have laid out five urgent demands for European policymakers, stressing that the ongoing negotiations for the 2028–2034 EU long-term budget are critical.

According to the signatories, Europe must stop acting merely as a global regulator of technologies invented elsewhere and shift from “defence to offence” by deliberately backing European champions to compete and win globally.

We will examine this push with German Minister for Digital Transformation **Karsten Wildberger*.***
