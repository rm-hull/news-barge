The latest Grand Theft Auto (GTA) is nearly out in the US and UK, and after a 13-year wait, hype is at its peak.

Several VPNs have used the excitement to promote themselves as 'gaming VPNs' — offering higher download speeds, less latency and even early access.

The issue is a VPN will not help you access GTA VI early. What's more, there are no online elements at launch that require the 'improved ping' some providers claim.

Not all the claims are bogus. Higher download speeds are a possibility, and VPNs can help protect you from fake storefronts or phishing traps.

Here's everything you need to know.

## Will a VPN give me safer downloads and help avoid scams?

Yes, for some.

While console gamers only have hours to wait for GTA VI to arrive, PC gamers have a far longer wait before they can play. As a result, lots of fake storefronts offering 'early PC access' are cropping up, keen to take advantage of eager PC gamers.

According to Surfshark, over 200 new GTA-themed domains had emerged by August, with more than a quarter appearing to contain scam links. NordVPN also found a 'scam kit' listed on the dark web for just $200 that contains everything someone might need to earn money maliciously from the launch.

VPNs can help protect you from dangerous sites in several ways.

Many come equipped with malicious link blocking or scam detection features, which can warn you of threats before a site loads.

You can also use email alias tools — offered by the likes of Surfshark, Proton and ExpressVPN — to mask your real email address if you're asked to sign up to any sites you're unsure of.

However, email security may be the least of your concerns if you've just submitted your credit card details to a scam site.

As usual, the best thing to do is to use common sense when approaching any site advertising GTA VI access: does the price seem too good to be true? Is it offering access to something earlier than you expected?

If the answer to either of these questions is yes, then steer clear.

## Can a VPN help me get faster download speeds?

Possibly.   
Grand Theft Auto VI is over 200GB to install, so it naturally takes a long time to download.

Some Internet Service Providers (ISPs) throttle your connection to preserve capacity for others when they spot a large download or during peak times.

In these specific situations, a VPN can help because it means your ISP can't determine what you're doing online. However, this is only likely to work if your connection is being significantly impacted.

For normal use, a VPN actually slows your internet connection because of the encryption overhead.

So for most, the best option is just to connect your console to your router via an Ethernet cable instead.

## Can a VPN help me get GTA VI early?

No.

While in the past you could trick your computer or console into thinking you were in New Zealand by editing your time zone or connecting to a VPN, this method doesn't work these days.

Major game stores like Steam, PlayStation Store and Microsoft Store lock accounts to a specific region when you sign up. So, if you originally signed up from the UK, it won't make a difference if you connect to a router running a VPN.

While some people online have suggested creating a New Zealand-based account, it's worth remembering that you'd be violating the service's Terms of Service and could lose access to the game as a result.
