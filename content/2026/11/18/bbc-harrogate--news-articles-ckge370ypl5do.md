North Yorkshire Council voted in favour of the new home-to-school transport policy in 2024

**A bid to scrap a policy of only providing free transport for children in North Yorkshire to their nearest school instead of by catchment area has been narrowly rejected.**

The policy, which survived by three votes at a council meeting on Wednesday, applies to children who started school from September 2025.

Lib Dem Felicity Cunliffe-Lister, who proposed overturning it, said it was a "Tory legacy that will continue to haunt us", but Janet Sanderson, Conservative executive member for children and families, said opponents had not come up with a costed alternative.

During the meeting, councillors did vote to accept several changes recommended by officers in a post-implementation review of the policy.

Campaigners have held protests about the home-to-school transport policy since its introduction by North Yorkshire Council in 2024.

During Wednesday's full meeting of the authority, Sanderson pointed to a provisional £65,000 underspend in the school transport budget reported in the council's second-quarter financial figures and said critics had not suggested alternative savings.

"The majority of services in the children and young people's services directorate are statutory functions," she said.

"Would you have us cut the support for those families who require early help, our children in care, or our young people leaving care?"

## 'Handled so badly'

Independent councillor Caroline Goodrick, who chaired the children and families overview and scrutiny committee which recommended rejecting the bid to overturn the policy, also highlighted the council's finances, according to the Local Democracy Reporting Service.

"In 10 years, the home-to-school travel budget has risen from £25m to a staggering £54m," she told the meeting.

"These costs are borne by our council tax residents, whether they have children in education or not.

"This isn't our money we would be spending on a more generous policy, but theirs."

However, Cunliffe-Lister told the meeting: "If this council votes to continue to deprive children of their access to school and divide communities in doing so, we know many will find it hard to forgive or forget."

Meanwhile, Andy Brown, of the Green Party, described the new system as a "shambles", adding that it had "been handled so badly from start to finish".

He said: "It has been a sorry tale of heavy-handed decision-making, followed by a lack of flexibility. We're meant to be here to protect our residents.

"We set out to reduce costs, but achieved few real savings, instead shifting most of the burden onto parents and causing immense stress to many decent families trying to do the best for their children."

Campaigners have held protests about the policy since it was introduced

The meeting had earlier heard from Swaledale parent Charlotte Fowler, from the School Transport Action Group.

She said the council's own review acknowledged that removing children from the transport system had not yet translated into equal savings because vehicle numbers had not fallen at the same rate.

"You say savings are on track, where's the evidence? We're told money has been banked, but where are the accounts to prove it?" Fowler asked.

"Why are you being so sheepish about the numbers? What are you trying to hide?

"Meanwhile, families are under enormous stress. Rural communities are being hollowed out. Money is leaving the country. Schools are losing pupils and losing funding."

Councillors eventually voted 43 to 40 against the proposal to scrap the policy.
