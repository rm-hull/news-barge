## A German state data regulator has opened an investigation on Wednesday into Deutsche Bank following a massive data leak. The authority is investigating possible data protection violations.

Following the massive data breach that leaked over 15 million internal records, the office of the commissioner for data protection in Hesse has said on Wednesday that it has launched a probe into Deutsche Bank.

Alexander Rossnagel, the data protection commissioner, said he will check whether the bank had appropriate data protection measures and if the leak was reported in a timely fashion.

"I am investigating whether Deutsche Bank promptly notified the affected individuals," Rossnagel said.

Whistleblowers leaked internal documents to a group of international media, revealing that the bank had reportedly ignored repeated internal warnings about money laundering, corruption or sanctions violations.

Among the bank's clients are Russian oligarchs, US President Donald Trump and sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

## Deutsche Bank CEO admits errors

"When you look at the events today, you naturally question whether something could have been done differently," CEO of Deutsche Bank, Christian Sewing, told German newspaper Handelsblatt.

"Looking back, you can always say that at a certain point in time you might have been able to prioritise differently," Sewing told Handelsblatt.

When asked whether he took internal warnings seriously, Sewing refuted: "During the customer review, I asked whether a private customer needs the same complex process as a large company with 300 subsidiaries," Handelsblatt reports.

"That doesn't mean I wanted to put risks aside."

## A blow to Deutsche Bank's reputation

Although Deutsche Bank said the matters "date back many years" and that it had reinforced its controls, the leak has dealt consequential damage to the bank's reputation, sending its share down.

The bank claims it proactively informed the appropriate authorities about the leak and that testing found no account information, login credentials or payment data were accessed.

While the probe won't investigate the documents, it will assess the technical and organisational measures put in place to judge if they were appropriate.
