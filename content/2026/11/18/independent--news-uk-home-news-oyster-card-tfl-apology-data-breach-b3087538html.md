Transport for London has revealed that around 265,000 Oyster photocard users may have had their personal account details compromised after "suspicious activity" was detected on its systems.

On Monday, cardholders reported receiving unexpected emails prompting them to register for an account, despite already possessing an active photocard account, according to TfL.

The transport provider took its photocard website offline while investigations were carried out, but stated it now believes approximately 265,000 customers have had their account information exposed.

TfL emphasised that its initial review indicates no passwords, financial card or bank details, journey histories, or names and birth dates of children with Zip Oyster cards have been compromised.

However, in notifications sent on Wednesday, the organisation warned impacted individuals that their names, contact details - including phone numbers, email addresses and home addresses - dates of birth, and security questions and answers were breached.

![Signage for the Oyster card system](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/21/2280522458..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The email reads: "We identified some suspicious activity which affected our Oyster photocard website on 16 November 2026 and took action to limit access, including taking the photocard system offline.

"While we acted quickly to restrict this, this activity has unfortunately resulted in unauthorised access to the personal information contained in your web account.

"We apologise for this."

TfL added that the matter has been officially reported to the Information Commissioner’s Office.

It confirmed that passengers can still make journeys using their Oyster photocards and top up funds at ticket machines located in London Underground and National Rail stations, though online account access remains disabled.

The specific customer account types that may be affected comprise: Zip Oyster cards (5-10, 11-15, 16+); 18+ Student Oyster card; 18-25 Care Leaver Oyster photocard; 60+ London Oyster photocard; Apprentice Oyster photocard; and Veterans Oyster photocard.

Shashi Verma, TfL’s director of technology strategy and revenue, said: "On 16 November 2026, we became aware that Oyster photocard customers were receiving emails asking them to create an account, when they already have a photocard account.

"We immediately took the TfL photocard website down while this was being investigated.

"The protection of personal information is a priority for us, and I apologise to our customers affected by this incident.

![TfL have issued an apology](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/21/2271824033..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

"While we acted swiftly, we now believe that around 265,000 photocard customers may have had their account details compromised as part of this incident.

"We have now contained the incident, and our current assessment is that no passwords, bank or payment card details, customer journey information, or names and date of births of any children with a Zip Oyster card have been compromised.

"Due to the speed with which this incident was identified and addressed, we believe that the number of customers who could have been affected was significantly reduced.

"We are continuing to investigate what has happened but have already contacted affected customers to make them aware of this.

"Customers with Oyster photocards can continue to travel and any customer who need to renew their photocard or query any journey history can still do so by contacting TfL’s Customer Services.

"We hope to restore the affected website services as quickly as possible."
