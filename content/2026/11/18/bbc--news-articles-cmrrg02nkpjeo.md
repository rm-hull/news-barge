**A man who carried out a cosmetic procedure which an expert said had led to a woman's death complained he was "exhausted" and seemed "unbothered" when paramedics arrived, an inquest has been told.**

Mother-of-five Alice Webb, 33, collapsed at a friend's house in Gloucester on 23 September 2024 and died the following day after having a Brazilian Butt Lift (BBL).

Jordan Parke, known as the "Lip King", gave Webb seven times the recommended amount of filler before she collapsed. He was under investigation for gross negligence manslaughter when he died in 2026.

Earlier, an expert pathologist told Webb's inquest that a "gel-like" liquid had been found in blood vessels in her brain, lungs and heart.

Gloucestershire Coroner's Court heard Parke had already been banned from carrying out BBLs in Dudley and Salford following concerns about his procedures.

## More from Gloucestershire

Parke had complained "I'm exhausted" and was seen laughing as Webb, from Wotton-under-Edge, recorded a video of the procedure.

Det Insp Adam Stacey, of Gloucestershire Constabulary, told the inquest the only first aid equipment at the premises was "some plasters".

Katy Skerrett, senior coroner for Gloucestershire, told the inquest an ambulance had been called at about 21:00 BST on the day Webb collapsed. Despite resuscitation attempts, she died in hospital during the early hours of the next day.

During the inquest, a jury was played a recording of a 999 call made by Parke.

He could be heard telling the operator that Webb had "literally just had [the procedure] done" and was not breathing correctly.

Allanah Kingston, a paramedic for South West Ambulance Service who responded to the 999 call, said Webb had been unable to manage her airway.

An ECG showed massive ischemia, "which showed part of heart was dying; we knew it was not good," Kingston added, before Webb went into cardiac arrest.

She told the inquest Parke had appeared "unbothered by the situation" and demonstrated a "lack of understanding that the treatment may have caused illness".

Parke, 34, lived in Manchester, where he ran a salon from his home address, and had a large social media following.

He was under investigation for gross negligence manslaughter but died earlier this year, meaning the criminal case was unable to proceed, Det Insp Adam Stacey, from Gloucestershire Constabulary, told the inquest.

Stacey said he had "no doubt" in his mind that Parke, who had received no professional medical training, conducted the procedure Webb underwent shortly before her death, given the video evidence available.

Of Parke's certificates found in Manchester, "none of them showed any degree of reassurance in degree of their formality", he added.

A number of cosmetic procedures had been carried out previously by Parke, who, in 2023, had been subject to investigation by Dudley environmental health officers. They issued him with a prohibition notice to not perform BBL procedures.

Concerns regarding basic first aid, lack of lifesaving equipment, and the use of anesthesia without prescription had also been raised, and another prohibition notice was issued to Parke in Salford in 2024.

He was not registered with the General Medical Council or the General Pharmaceutical Council, and the products he used were unlicensed in the UK. He also lacked a medical prescribing certificate.

Leadbeatter said he had not found any indication of clots blocking Webb's lungs, but did find a "gel-like material" in the fatty tissue and within muscle of her buttocks. It was also present in nearby nerves.

The material was also found in blood vessels elsewhere in the body, he said.

"I saw it in lungs, also within the section of the buttocks, and in small blood vessels in parts of the brain, in the heart and in small blood vessels in the thyroid gland and kidney," Leadbeatter told the jury.

He added there had been no evidence of an allergic reaction and a toxic pathology test found nothing in the blood that could explain Webb's death.

Webb had her own business, Crystal Clear Aesthetics, in Wotton-under-Edge.

Morgan Louise Townsend, Webb's sister-in-law, became tearful as she spoke at the inquest and said she had talked to her every day.

When asked if Webb had paid for the treatment, Townsend said she had paid for a training course but had to pay Parke £2,300 for the procedure he did on her.

## Final message

Townsend said Webb had seemed "absolutely fine that day" and had kept her updated with pictures and videos throughout the treatment.

"At 19:31, she sent me photo of the filler that was going to go in her, I was shocked at how much there was," Townsend said.

"[Parke] said he was giving her 400ml for free, in total 1,400ml. She sent messages and said, 'OMG it kills'."

Townsend added Webb had sent a video of Parke giving her the treatment.

The last message from Webb to Townsend came at 20:12, she told the inquest, and consisted of just one word: "Painful."

Surgical BBLs involve inserting silicone-filled implants and/or removing fat from other parts of the body before injecting it into the buttocks, while non-surgical BBLs involve injecting dermal filler into the buttocks.

During the jury inquest, Dr Stephen Leadbeatter, a Home Office forensic pathologist, said he considered her cause of death to be "embolisation of hyaluronic acid from introduction into buttocks".

Embolisation is when something is carried in the bloodstream from one part of the body to another. Hyaluronic acid was the filler used in the procedure.

The inquest continues.
