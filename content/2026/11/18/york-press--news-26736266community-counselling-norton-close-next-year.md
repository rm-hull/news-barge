A MENTAL health charity which has supported thousands of people across North Yorkshire is to close next year.

Community Counselling, based in Norton, which serves Ryedale, Scarborough and York, will cease operations by September 2027.

Established in 1995 as a community counselling service and becoming an independent charity in 2013, Community Counselling has played a long-standing role in delivering mental health support, with a focus on accessible and professional therapeutic services for some of the most vulnerable individuals in the community.

The charity, based at Stanley Harrison House, next to Malton Railway Station, has supported thousands of adults, children and young people through professional counselling, play therapy, EMDR and other therapeutic interventions.

The closure follows a decision by trustees after what they described as “careful consideration of the charity’s long-term financial position and the increasingly challenging funding environment”.

Stephen Robling, chief officer of Community Counselling, said: “This has been an incredibly difficult decision for the trustees and leadership team.

“We are immensely proud of what our staff, volunteers, trainees and sessional workers have achieved over the years.

“Together, we have helped thousands of vulnerable people access vital emotional wellbeing support at times when they needed it most.

“While the charity’s future is no longer sustainable, our focus now is on ensuring an orderly and responsible wind-down that honours our commitments to clients, commissioners, funders and staff.”

In the charity’s annual report to the year ending March 31 2026, 909 referrals were received, down from 1 ,047, reflecting service closures rather than reduced demand, while 6,380 appointments were delivered, down from 7,240, again due to funding constraints.

The report also noted that the complexity and risk profile of clients had increased markedly, with 58% of clients assessed as being at some level of risk at initial assessment, up from 35% the previous year, and 26% two years ago, while ‘Cause for Concern’ incidents rose to 236, compared to 113 the previous year - and an increase of over 300% in three years.

Community Counselling will continue delivering existing services until the agreed end dates of its contracts and projects.

Current clients will continue to receive support during this period, with the charity working closely with commissioners, referral agencies and partners to ensure a smooth transition as services come to an end.

The charity has begun discussions with staff, commissioners, funders, and other stakeholders to plan the winding-down process.

Craig Taylor, chair of trustees, said: “On behalf of the Board of Trustees, I would like to thank everyone who has contributed to the charity’s work over the years.

“We recognise the impact this announcement may have on staff, volunteers, sessional workers and clients.

“Our priority is to manage this process carefully, responsibly and with compassion for everyone affected.”

“The trustees would like to express their sincere gratitude to all those who have supported the charity throughout its history, including staff, volunteers, trainees, funders, commissioners, schools, health partners and community organisations.”

The charity will continue operating until all current contractual obligations are fulfilled.

Further details will be released as arrangements are confirmed.
