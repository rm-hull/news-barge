Two oil refineries in Russia’s Krasnodar region caught fire after being targeted in a Ukrainian drone attack, with four injured, regional authorities said**.**

The attacks on the Afipsky and Ilsky refineries came just a day before Donald Trump was due to meet with Russian president Vladimir Putin and his Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping in China during the APEC summit. Trump has said he intends to raise Ukraine at the meeting.

Earlier, US undersecretary of defense for policy Elbridge Colby questioned the effectiveness of Ukrainian strikes deep inside Russia, warning that such attacks could strengthen Moscow’s resolve.

Britain and Germany responded sharply to the remarks, with German’s defence minister calling his criticism “quite a stupid thing.”Elsewhere, all 19 people on board a Russian military transport plane have been killed after the aircraft crashed in southwestern Siberia.

The An-12 transport aircraft went down in Russia’s Kemerovo region on Tuesday, Russia’s *Izvestia* news outlet reported. Details surrounding the circumstances of the crash were not immediately available.

The crash comes after a prolonged series of Ukrainian long-range attacks on Russian territory and occupied infrastructure, culminating earlier on Tuesday in a coordinated missile and drone strike that collapsed a section of the Crimean Bridge into the sea.

## Russian attacks on Ukrainian city of Odesa kill four, officials say

Russian forces struck the centre of Ukraine's Black Sea port city of Odesa in three waves of attacks on Wednesday, killing four people, regional officials said on Telegram.

Regional governor Oleh Kiper said on Telegram that at least seven people had also been injured.

Windows of residential buildings, cafes and shops had been broken, a car had been set on fire and several other vehicles had been damaged, he said.

Serhiy Lysak, head of the city's military administration, said a second strike had damaged a residential building and an educational institution.

## France rejects Russian accusation of involvement in Ukraine war

The French foreign minister has rejected Russia's allegation of being directly involved in the Ukraine war.

Speaking to France 5, Jean-Noel Barrot said: “We are obviously not co-belligerents in Ukraine. We have no intention of becoming one.”

The comments came after Russian foreign minister Sergey Lavrov accused Paris of directly taking part in the Ukraine war following a report that French troops are in the eastern European country carrying out noncombatant operations.

“We conveyed our concern through available channels, warning the relevant countries that this is no longer support for Ukraine, but direct participation in the war,” the top Russian diplomat told reporters on Tuesday at the East Asia Summit in Manila.

Lavrov added that French troops were also “assisting in goal setting, providing satellite data and much more.”

## Russian military deployed chemical agents against Ukrainian forces, finds investigation

A Ukrainian war crimes investigation has found that Russian forces used Riot Control Agents against Ukrainian troops in an attack as part of a broader Russian military practice of deploying chemical agents on the battlefield.

The report published by the Main Investigation Department of the Security Service of Ukraine on Tuesday, in its probe found that Russia used tear gas on Ukrainian troops in a 20 September attack.

Following the assessment of the evidence collected, it found that during an operation against Ukrainian forces, Russia dropped RG-Vo grenade using unmanned aerial vehicle.

The grenade containing 2-Chlorobenzylidenemalonoitrile, also known as CS tear gas, was dropped into the trench line of Zemlya observation post, impacting two members of the force on duty.

“Taken together, the information assessed by the Investigation Team indicates that the use of an RG-Vo grenade containing the riot control agent CS against Ukrainian defensive positions near the village of Illinka on 20 September 2024 was not an isolated or improvised act, but occurred within an organised system involving the manufacture, procurement, military supply and operational employment of such munitions by the [Armed Force of Russian Federation],” it said, referring to the Russian military.

## Putin takes aim at sanctions, says Asia-Pacific is vital for Russia

Russian president Vladimir Putin took aim at “instruments of economic dominance,” such as the sanctions imposed following the 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

“Sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions” have “outlived their usefulness and have largely lost their intimidating effect,” Putin told the CEO summit in his first in-person appearance at an Apec leaders meeting since 2017.

He highlighted his country’s desire to cultivate closer economic ties with the Asia-Pacific, saying the region accounts for more than half of its total trade.
