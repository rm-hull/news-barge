A carer has won £30,000 after her Scrooge bosses issued her with a formal warning for chatting to colleagues about their Christmas bonus being cut.

Donna Williams was hauled in for disciplinary action because she had discussions with care home co-workers about the changes to their festive bonus, an employment tribunal heard.

Previously all staff received Christmas bonuses, but when bosses cut back on spending and stopped staff from receiving the extra cash for the holidays they also unfairly decided it was misconduct to speak about the miserly measure.

Ms Williams was given a warning for bringing the topic up and was later sacked over bogus allegations.

She took her care home to Bodmin Employment Tribunal, Cornwall, which found 'there was no evidence of an instruction not to discuss bonuses'.

She has now won £29,628 following a successful unfair dismissal case against Copthorne Care Homes.

Ms Williams joined Copthorne Care Homes in August, 2012, as a night-time care assistant based in Penzance, Cornwall.

The carer had difficulty with mobility due to her arthritis.

The business changed hands in 2022, and under the new ownership, 'not all staff members received a Christmas bonus'.

The tribunal heard: "The [care home] had a policy of rewarding people assessed as going 'above and beyond'. This was different from the previous owners who gave all staff a Christmas bonus."

After Christmas of 2023, in January 2024, Ms Williams was disciplined because she had been 'discussing the Christmas bonus with colleagues'.

The tribunal heard: "[Ms Williams] was invited to an investigatory meeting with [her bosses] Anna Billinger and Emma Boase on January 3 about potential disciplinary matters.

"The matters discussed were vegetable preparation (a whole swede left in a pan and not covered with water), discussing the Christmas bonus with colleagues, and not sitting in the side conservatory while waiting to start her shift.

"[Ms Williams] attended a disciplinary hearing on 26 January.

"[She] received a written warning for the three matters.

"[She] was also told not to discuss the bonuses again and to go back to sitting in the side conservatory."

The panel at Bodmin Employment Tribunal said: "The [care home] provided us with no evidence that it had instructed staff, prior to the disciplinary proceedings, that discussion of the bonuses was forbidden.

"We accept the evidence of [Ms Williams] that staff have discussed the bonuses every year for approximately 11 years.

"We also find that such an instruction would, if it had been given, be stretching the definition of a reasonable management instruction to a breaking point.

"While it is reasonable to instruct staff to maintain confidentiality about service users or ‘trade secrets’, we found it troubling that the [care home] was suggesting it was reasonable that staff members could not discuss their pay and conditions, a bonus being part of that subject area."

On March 19 2024, Ms Williams was again investigated for disciplinary matters and was suspended on full pay.

Allegations of negligence against her included that she had not replaced a service users pain relief patches, had left one woman in a chair all night and had not reported a fall properly.

A colleague submitted evidence that Ms Williams had intended to move the patient who had slept in the chair when she awoke, as she often did, but she happened to sleep the whole night.

She was also unable to move the patient due to her own arthritis.

Ms Williams had also called an ambulance when the service user had fallen, as well as their next of kin and a colleague.

The care home 'provided no evidence of what it was [she] was expected to do in this situation'.

But on April 4, the carer was issued a final written warning.

She appealed, and the next day signed off sick until May 6, and then between 6 May and 10 June.

She returned to work in June and was told not to discuss the previous disciplinary actions against her.

But on June 19 she was suspended again for 'discussing confidential information with colleagues' and 'bad mouthing a colleague who was involved in the disciplinary process'.

The care home bosses gave little evidence about the 'bad mouthing' allegation apart from some hearsay from chatter 'at the pub'.

But this time she was dismissed on June 27 and her subsequent appeal was dismissed.

Ms Williams then took the care home to Bodmin Employment Tribunal.

There, the panel found the sacking was 'flawed'.

The panel ruled: "We find that the [care home] did not have a genuine belief that the misconduct had occurred.

"The Tribunal finds, applying the range of reasonable responses test, that the [bosses'] belief was not based on reasonable grounds.

"Some of the misconduct allegations were flawed because there was no evidence of the alleged underpinning procedure or policy.

"For example, there was no evidence of an instruction not to discuss bonuses, and there was no evidence of a policy or procedure governing handovers.

"The complaint of unfair dismissal is well founded and is upheld."

She was also due £2,680 of notice pay which she had not received.

Combined with over £18,000 of losses, an award of £4,020 and an uplift, the care home must pay Ms Williams £29,628.

Her claim of disability discrimination failed.
