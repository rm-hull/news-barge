## William Wilberforce's life and legacy explored at Pocklington event

An upcoming event will honour William Wilberforce, the famed anti-slavery campaigner who attended Pocklington School as a teenager *(Image: Supplied)*

An upcoming event will celebrate the early life of a man who helped change the course of history in Pocklington.

Taking place on Friday, November 20, the evening will honour William Wilberforce, the famed anti-slavery campaigner who attended Pocklington School as a teenager.

The event is a collaboration between Pocklington School and Pocklington Church Friends and will be held at All Saints Church.

Sir David Davis, MP for Goole and Pocklington, will close the evening by reading extracts from Wilberforce’s celebrated anti-slavery address to Parliament.

Sir David said: "I was very pleased to be asked to read extracts of Wilberforce's 1789 address to Parliament setting out the case for the abolition of the slave trade, known by many as 'The Greatest Speech in the History of Parliament'."

Contributions will also come from Pocklington School staff and pupils, including headteacher Becky Lovelock and the Chamber Choir.

The vicar of Pocklington, Jake Belder, will also take part in the programme.

Wilberforce attended Pocklington School from the age of 12 and left at 17, already developing views against slavery that would shape his future career.

He went on to become a Member of Parliament at 21 and led the campaign to abolish the slave trade, overcoming major odds and health problems.

The event will explore Wilberforce’s family history, his life in Pocklington during the 1770s, and his later achievements.

Pocklington School will also display a small exhibition of Wilberforce memorabilia, including some of his personal correspondence.
