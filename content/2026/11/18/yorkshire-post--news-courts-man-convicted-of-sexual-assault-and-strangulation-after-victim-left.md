# Man convicted of sexual assault and strangulation after victim left with broken bones

## A Castleford man has been convicted of sexually assaulting, strangling and physically abusing a woman who was left suffering nightmares and multiple broken bones.

Martin Longstaff, 51 from Castleford, was sentenced at Leeds Crown Court on Tuesday (Nov 17) after being found guilty of five offences.

The offences took place in Castleford in April 2026.

An investigation was launched by Wakefield's Adult Safeguarding Unit after emergency services were called following an assault on the victim, who required medical attention.

Longstaff was subsequently charged with one count of sexual assault by digital penetration, one count of non-fatal strangulation, two counts of actual bodily harm and one count of controlling and coercive behaviour.

![Martin Longstaff](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/18/18/25/906681589_1666940388809834_2312962102097571178_n.jpeg?trim=294,0,43,0&width=640&quality=65)


As part of his sentencing, Longstaff was made subject to an indefinite restraining order and placed on the Sex Offenders Register for life.

In a victim impact statement, the woman described the lasting physical and emotional effects of the abuse.

She said: “Since the attacks, I suffer with bad nightmares which keep me awake and I keep re-living what happened to me.

“I have two broken fingers on both hands, and I struggle with simple tasks like fastening buttons and several broken ribs which are still healing and cause me pain daily.”

Detective Constable Adele Bossons, of Wakefield's Adult Safeguarding Unit, said: “We welcome the sentence given in this case and hope it provides the victim with some reassurance and a degree of peace as she moves forward with her life.

“I want to recognise the courage and bravery the victim has shown, not only in seeking help but also in supporting the investigation. Coming forward and speaking about abuse can be incredibly difficult, and her strength throughout this process should be recognised.

“We are committed to holding perpetrators to account and ensuring that those who subject others to abuse face the consequences of their actions. We will always take reports of abuse seriously, thoroughly investigate allegations and do everything within our power to bring offenders to justice.”
