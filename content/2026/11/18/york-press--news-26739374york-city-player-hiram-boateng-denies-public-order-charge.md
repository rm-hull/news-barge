A York City player has denied allegedly being involved in an altercation with a football supporter during a pitch invasion.

As reported by The Press yesterday, Hiram Boateng, 30, appeared at Manchester Magistrates’ Court on Monday (November 16) charged with the use of threatening, abusive or insulting words or behaviour with intent to cause fear of or provoke unlawful violence.

Boateng pleaded not guilty to the charge at the preliminary hearing and his case was listed for trial on June 23 next year at the same court.

He was released on unconditional bail.

It follows an alleged incident involving supporters who entered the pitch during the final-day match between Rochdale AFC and York City FC at the Crown Oil Arena on April 25.

Home supporters entered the pitch after believing their team had secured the National League title.

The alleged incident took place during a second invasion later in the match after a late equalising goal gave the title to York.

A spokesperson for York City FC said: "We are aware of developments regarding the incident involving Hiram Boateng during the game against Rochdale in April.

"We are continuing to work with him and all the appropriate authorities to address this and to understand the circumstances that led to this.

"Hiram maintains the full support of everyone at York City and is a much-valued member of the squad, both on and off the pitch."

Greater Manchester Police confirmed Boateng was charged with a section four public order offence which carries a maximum sentence of six months in prison.
