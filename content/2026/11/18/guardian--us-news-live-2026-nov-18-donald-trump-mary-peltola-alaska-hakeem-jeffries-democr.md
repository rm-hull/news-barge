Mullin defends move to take government plane to college football game amid nationwide grounding in House hearing

During today’s hearing Markwayne Mullin defended his decision to take a government plane to attend a football game between Louisiana State University and the University of Texas on Saturday, while thousands of flights had been cancelled and the airspace closed during the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) nationwide grounding over the weekend.

“You want to bring up the fact that I took my family and my great nieces to an LSU game that was scheduled for weeks prior because I wanted to be a good uncle,” Mullin said, following questions from Congressman Bennie Thompson, the committee’s top Democrat.

Mullin went further, and said that had he not traveled to the game it could have created “confusion” because he was due to meet Louisiana governor Jeff Landry.

Mullin added that his absence might have shown “distrust that we’re not even willing to fly” after the FAA reopened the airspace.

“We had discussions at 10 o’clock Saturday morning. Should we cancel it? We had deep conversations about it,” the homeland security secretary said. “We were saying our skies were completely open. On Saturday alone, 36,000 planes flew and flew safely. Before I took off, the airspace had been open for almost 24 hours.”

Mullin noted that he paid for the trip himself because it was for non-official business, even though he is required to use the government aircraft for security reasons, and scolded Democrats for their line of questioning.

“The hypocrisy from you and from the members that want to make a big deal out of it … you guys would have done the same thing, but you would have called it a campaign event,” he said.

House Democrats have nominated current minority leader Hakeem Jeffries as their candidate for speaker in the next Congress. Jeffries received 251 yes votes of 262 cast, and 11 members voted no on his nomination. He faced no challengers but it will lead to a full chamber vote in the new year when Democrats are in the majority. Jeffries is poised to be the first Black speaker in US history.

Meanwhile, Chuck Schumer, the current Senate minority leader facing a challenge for the majority leader position from Maryland senator Chris Van Hollen, continued to express confidence he would prevail. “I will be elected majority leader. White House hits back at requests for information of officials and aides

Will Scharf, the White House general counsel, has hit back at Democratic lawmakers who have requested information and documents from administration officials, aides and allies in recent weeks, as the party prepares to take the majority of both chambers in the new year.

This comes after Robert Garcia, the incoming chair of the House oversight committee, sent 12 letters to members of Donald Trump’s family, current and former officials, and allies ordering them to preserve documents ahead of the sweeping inquiries that Democrats are set to launch in the new Congress.

Scharf addressed a letter, first obtained by Politico, to all ranking committee members in the House and Senate, claiming that several of their requests “drip with an animus or pettiness revealing that the purpose of these letters is not legitimate oversight but to harass and aggrandize”.

Scharf added:

Your demands do not reflect a request of the House, Senate, or a committee to which that body’s oversight authority has been delegated. As a result, Ranking Member requests in the 119th Congress do not trigger any obligation to “accommodate[e]... the needs of the conflicting branches.” Although some of you may (or may not) become a Chair in the 120th Congress, that does not mean that requests from the 119th Congress carry over.

This, Scharf highlighted, “should not be surprising” to Democrats, noting the party took the “same position” in 2022, when they were relegated to the minority in the House.

In the majority, Democrats have vowed to launch sweeping investigations into a variety of issues and officials when they have subpoena power.

The US commerce secretary, Howard Lutnick, faces mounting questions over his handling of the Nvidia affair as Democratic lawmakers demand answers over his family firm’s vast stake in the tech company.

Cantor Fitzgerald, the investment firm Lutnick ran for decades before joining the Trump administration, held shares in Nvidia worth “hundreds of millions of dollars” while Lutnick made key decisions affecting the tech firm’s ability to operate, according to Democrats on three key congressional committees.

“The American people deserve to know: did the secretary of commerce seriously and perhaps irreversibly compromise US national security interests to make money for his adult children and Cantor Fitzgerald?” said Jamie Raskin of Maryland, Gregory Meeks of New York and Sydney Kamlager-Dove of California.

Cantor Fitzgerald insists it had “no role” in the Trump administration’s decisions about Nvidia.

But in a letter on Tuesday to Lutnick’s son Brandon, who now serves as chair of Cantor Fitzgerald, the trio of lawmakers demanded documents and records, including communications concerning Nvidia.

They said that they wanted to “understand whether any actions or decisions by Secretary Lutnick concerning export controls, enforcement measures, or national security policies may have been improperly influenced by Cantor Fitzgerald’s holdings or business interests”.

More from today’s hearing with DHS secretary Markwayne Mullin, where Democratic representative Seth Magaziner questioned the secretary about the fatal ICE shooting of Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero in Maine.

In July, an ICE agent shot and killed the 25-year-old father during a traffic stop in Biddeford, 15 minutes from Scarborough, just days after an agent killed Lorenzo Salgado Araujo in Houston, Texas.

The shooting renewed calls for accountability in the agency. In particular, it renewed scrutiny over ICE’s hiring practices after it came to light that the officer that killed Guerrero, David Brouillette, had a troubling history of violent behavior, including past complaints of domestic violence and abuse, as well as mental health issues. Brouillette, who was hired during a federal recruitment surge, also has a record of deep-seated anti-immigrant hostility.

Magaziner asked Mullin how many ICE agents were currently on the streets who had not completed full background checks. Mullin said they had all completed training, to which Magaziner reminded him was not his question. Mullin course-corrected: “Every ICE agent we have in service right now have full background checks.” The ones that don’t are on administrative leave until they do, he added.

Magaziner, of Rhode Island, pointed out to Mullin that Brouillette’s background check had not been completed when he fatally shot Guerrero, nor had he completed standardized training when he was “put on the street with a mask and a gun” by the Trump administration. “He was put on the street after completing just an online course, he didn’t even do any of the in-person training in Georgia,” Magaziner said. “Has everyone who is out there on the street today completed the full 13-week in-person training?”

Mullin said they had, and those who haven’t had been put on administrative leave. He said there had also been additional training introduced.

He couldn’t answer how many people had been put on the street who hadn’t had the full training, and then appeared to defer responsibility to his predecessor.

Unfortunately, congressman, there were some decisions that were made before I was secretary that we were having to undo.

Canadian prime minister Mark Carney emphasized Canada’s efforts to strengthen their own sovereignty by moving away from dependence on the US economy, infrastructure and defense.

He spoke to DealBook, a New York Times summit with global leaders, from China, where he has struck a deal with Xi Jinping to reduce tariffs between the two countries – a sign that Carney is breaking with US policy towards China. The two countries have been at odds since Donald Trump returned to the White House two years ago, and has continually threatened to annex Canada and has placed tariffs on Canadian goods.

Canada is also seeking closer ties to Europe by increasing trade, collaborating on defense, research and intelligence. For example, Europe and Canada are trying to launch their own satellite system to reduce dependency on Elon Musk’s Starshield.

More trade deals are being struck with other countries, including the Philippines and India.

Carney emphasized that the US remains a key ally, but said Canada’s diversification could give them the upper hand in talks to better their relationship with the US.

“Our cards are that everyone else in the world wants to deal with us,” Carney said.

The Canadian prime minister did add, however, that Trump is “very different” behind closed doors. “He’s very personable,” Carney said, despite his fraught relationship with the US president. “He listens a lot more in private.”

All three national security officials testifying to the House homeland security committee unequivocally said the recent cyber outages were not related, nor were they the result of malicious cyber activity.

Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, said: “Based on what we know today, after a great deal of diligence, these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.”

Markwayne Mullin, the Department of Homeland Security secretary, noted that his team has seen, “nothing that would indicate they are related”.

“Our cyber experts have been engaged in day one in all of these incidents, and I want to be very clear on this on this point: there is no indication whatsoever that there is any malicious cyber activity related to any of those incidents,” Christopher Raia, the deputy FBI director, told lawmakers.
