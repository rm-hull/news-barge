**Google has refused a government request to remove a YouTube video posted by jailed rapist Russell Brand's wife in which he claims his innocence, Whitehall sources have told the BBC.**

The Prison Service of England and Wales submitted the request. A Ministry of Justice (MoJ) spokesperson said "it was only right" to ask for content that was "distressing for victims" to be removed.

The comedian is currently serving a 10-and-half-year prison sentence after being convicted of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

There was no immediate comment from Google when approached by BBC News.

On Sunday, his wife Laura had shared a video on their YouTube channel and read out a handwritten message from Brand in which he claimed: "I am not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for."

The MoJ spokesperson added that while "removal ultimately depends on the platform's own terms", the department strongly believes "YouTube should remove this content".

The Prison Service of England and Wales formally requested that the video be removed on the basis that they believed it was offensive to Brand's victims and had reached a substantial audience.

The BBC was initially told a minister had met Google to request the deactivation of Brand's account.

The MoJ later clarified the request had focused on the controversial video, and it is now understood a minister did not meet Google and the request was made via an established mechanism the prison service has with tech companies to request deletion of material.

The BBC has been told that Google declined the request, saying that its rules had not been broken.

Brand's message prompted an investigation into whether he had broken prison rules.

Downing Street previously called the message "clearly appalling", adding: "Prisoners are strictly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims, and speaking to the media is only permitted in exceptional circumstances."

Mia Latter, a woman who has made allegations against him, said she found the message shared on the couple's Sunday Service show "upsetting" and was appalled to hear "this Christian message alongside absolute lies".

In recent years, Brand has positioned himself as a Christian influencer and has a large following.

Latter, 37, has alleged she was raped by the comedian when she was 16. She was not a complainant in his recent criminal trial and he denies her claims.

"I just felt for those women that stood trial and everything they went through," she told BBC Radio 4's Today programme this week.

She is one of the women who made allegations against Brand in 2023 as part of a Times, Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches investigation.

Education Secretary Lucy Powell criticised media outlets earlier on Wednesday for playing extracts of the message, saying they should not have "amplified" his words.

Speaking to LBC, she described Brand as "someone who's not showing a jot of remorse, a jot of any sensitivity at all to their victims".

In 2023, YouTube suspended Brand's channels from making money from adverts for "violating" its "creator responsibility policy", shortly after he was accused of rape and sexual assaults between 2006 and 2013.

The video and the Brands' YouTube account are still available on the site.

Meanwhile, his social media accounts on Meta platforms - Instagram, Facebook and Whatsapp - were disabled last week.
