Soldier Joshua Jones was sending a text message when he crashed and killed Judith Dunn

**A speeding driver who killed a grandmother on her way home from her family's Christmas Day in a crash while he was using his phone has been jailed for nine years.**

Judith Dunn, 80, had just thanked her daughter for a lovely day when her and her husband's car was ploughed into by Joshua Jones, 30, on the A1(M) near Darlington on 25 December 2024, Teesside Crown Court heard.

Mr Dunn, who was seriously injured, said "an act [tantamount] to murder with a motor vehicle" had robbed him of his "constant companion" of more than 60 years.

Jones, from Catterick Garrison, admitted causing death and serious injury by dangerous driving. He was caught speeding again months after the crash.

Mr and Mrs Dunn were returning to their home in Bishop Auckland from their daughter's in North Yorkshire when they came to the back of a quarter-mile long queue of traffic in a temporary 60mph stretch between junctions 57 and 58, prosecutor Ellen Wright said.

A car had crashed into the central barrier further ahead and police were on the scene dealing with it, the court heard, with Mr Dunn saying the sea of brake lights resembled Blackpool illuminations.

As they sat in the queue, the backseat of their Ford Fiesta covered with presents given to them by their family hours earlier, Mr Dunn kept his own brake lights illuminated to alert upcoming traffic behind to the stoppage, the court heard.

## 'Warning not heeded'

As they waited, Mrs Dunn, the front seat passenger, sent her daughter a text message telling her she had "enjoyed a lovely day", the court heard.

Jones, an army corporal, of Lys Crescent, was also driving north in his Volkswagen Tiguan and was looking at his phone for prolonged periods while his car travelled under cruise control set at 75mph, the court heard.

He made no attempt to stop and smashed into the back of the Dunns' Fiesta, crushing it into the central reservation and sending it 120ft (36m) down the road, the court heard.

Other motorists and police officers dealing with the earlier crash ran to give aid to the couple, but Mrs Dunn was declared dead at the scene.

Jones told police he did not know why he had crashed and said he might have fallen asleep, the court heard.

Mr Dunn was taken to James Cook University Hospital where he was found to have suffered skulls and rib fractures and serious injuries to his legs, Wright said.

Analysis of Jones' phone showed he had been using various music, messaging and social media apps for at least 19 minutes while driving, the court heard.

At 20:16 he sent a message to his partner saying he was "just leaving now", to which she replied "be so careful, just stopped to help with a smash", the court heard.

"It was not a warning you heeded," judge Amanda Rippon told Jones.

He was composing a message saying "merry Christmas all, hope youse have had a good day" on Signal when, at 20:37, he smashed into the Dunns, the court heard.

## 'Despicable and shocking'

Mr Dunn told the court his wife had been his "constant companion" for more than 60 years, and he missed her "every day from morning until night".

"Coming home to an empty house was most unbearably heartbreaking," he said.

"I go to places we used to enjoy, but there is no enjoyment or fulfilment without Judith by my side."

He said the impact of his own injuries was long-lasting and limited his ability to do the activities he had once loved.

"An act [tantamount] to murder with a motor vehicle has created a void that is impossible to fill," Mr Dunn said.

Mrs Dunn's daughter, son and one of her granddaughters told the court she was youthful and vibrant, witty and kind, the centre of their family, and Christmas would forever be "heartbreaking".

Her daughter said: "It is beyond comprehension she was killed so suddenly and violently only half an hour after leaving my home and arms on Christmas night."

Her mother's final moments were "shocking, horrific and despicable", she said, adding Jones had caused "immeasurable pain" and he should never be allowed to drive again.

Mrs Dunn's son said a "day of peace and happiness together" had ended as a "living nightmare" with his "precious" and "beautiful soul" of a mother "brutally taken away".

Her granddaughter said she was fit and healthy and "deserved many good years" more of life.

## 'Insult to her memory'

Judge Amanda Rippon said the cause of Mrs Dunn's death was as "simple" and "dreadful" as Jones using his phone and not paying the "slightest" attention to the road.

She said the time he spent on his phone covered 12 miles from when he joined the A1(M).

The Dunns' car was so badly damaged police did not recognise what make and model it was, the court heard.

Mr Dunn was "a man left bereft of his lifelong love" while Mrs Dunn's family would be permanently devastated, the judge said.

In April 2025, four months after killing Mrs Dunn, Jones was caught doing 81mph on the A1(M) near Boroughbridge, the court heard.

"It seems you learned very little if anything," Rippon told Jones, while Mrs Dunn's granddaughter said it was an "insult to her memory".

Jones was banned from driving for 10 years.
