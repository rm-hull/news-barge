## Historic Yorkshire street named spot 'where England comes alive'

Skipton High Street is packed with history. *(Image: Getty)*

A high street in a West Yorkshire market town has been named one “where England comes alive”.

World Atlas has released a list of the 11 best streets “where England comes alive”, with historic spots across the country being listed.

The streets mentioned are packed with independent businesses alongside centuries-old landmarks.

Skipton High Street made it onto the list thanks to its local market and cosy tearoom where you can relax with a scone and cup of tea.

## What is Skipton High Street like?

On the street, World Atlas said: “The Skipton High Street was once a place where one could pick up the local North Yorkshire paper, a shiny new piece of jewellery, or the coming week's groceries in the late 19th century.

“Though over 100 years have passed, not much has changed. Being a market town, Skipton's High Street proudly hosts the Skipton Market four days per week for stocking up on goods from the local cheesemonger, fishmonger, and fruit and veg producers, where the Grade II Listed Sir Mathew Wilson Statue still stands to commemorate Skipton's first British Member of Parliament.

“If needing a moment's rest, pop into The Cosy Sheep Tearoom for nibbling on home-baked scones while sipping tea and looking out at the High Street, and at the other end of the street, Skipton's circa 14th-century Holy Trinity Church gazes upon the thoroughfare.”

Skipton High Street has been praised for “its enduring individuality and character” and has a great mix of independent and national shops.

Alongside the famous market, there are lots of other shops to get lost in, including The Magpie, an antique shop, Gathering Games, a video game store, and men's clothes shop Jenson Samuel.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21723122.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Cosy Sheep Tearoom is loved by visitors. *(Image: Tripadvisor)*

You can also find a few charity shops, a Boots, and a used bookstore along the high street.

If you need a break from shopping, there are plenty of pubs, cafés, and restaurants to try out.

You can head to the Woolly Sheep Inn for a pint and pub classics like sausage and mash as well as steaks and stone-baked pizzas.

The Cosy Sheep Tearoom has a rating of 4.3 out of 5 on Tripadvisor, with people praising the “excellent service” and “delicious food”.

After exploring the main street, there are plenty of alleys to visit with more hidden shops.

For those who love history, you can even find Skipton Castle at the end of the road.
