THE MP for Ryedale has said the proposals from former minister Alan Milburn to tackle the number of young people not in education, employment or training (Neet) are 'all carrot and no stick'.

Kevin Hollinrake MP for Thirsk and Malton said giving people who are perfectly capable of working money so they don't have to is a massive mistake.

Mr Hollinrake said: "It creates entirely the wrong incentives, undermines personal responsibility and risks condemning young people to a lifetime of dependency.

"Of course, those who genuinely cannot work must be supported.

But for those who can, the expectation should be clear: work, education or training should be the norm, not the exception."

Some 13 per cent (almost 1 million in the UK) of 18-24s are classed as Neets, with Mr Milburn urging the country aims to reduce this to 5 per cent by 20236.

The Tony Blair-era minister has published a report on how to achieve this.

Among the measures is an 'opportunity guarantee' and a new Youth Opportunity Service to give young people a named adviser and personalised participation plan on what the young need to do to find work.

Schools will also have to identify those at risk of becoming Neet and be required to offer work experience.

There would also be 150,000 more places for 18-24s in further education and other training.

College funding would depend on the success of such graduates.

The government would aim to double the number taking out apprenticeships, with employers receiving incentives to achieve this.

Employers taking on a young Neet should receive £3,000, with small employers with fewer than 50 staff getting an extra £2,000.

There would be lower National Insurance costs for 21-24s, starting in retail and hospitality.

Benefits would also be changed with young people no longer getting the health top up of Universal Credit if they have a work-limiting condition.

A new benefit would replace PIP payments, Mr Milburn also proposed.

Mr Hollinrake said that having early a million young people in Britain not in education, employment or training was a national scandal.

He said: "Parents have a role to play here too.

We should be encouraging our children to work hard, take responsibility and make something of themselves.

Sometimes that means being firm, setting expectations and making clear that staying at home doing nothing is simply not an option.

Labour wants us to believe Alan Milburn's review is a watershed moment in tackling this crisis.

I genuinely hope it succeeds and where the recommendations make sense, Conservatives will support them.

"But the report also exposes some uncomfortable truths about the damage Labour's own policies are doing.

"The Government has made it more expensive and more difficult for businesses to employ people, particularly young people looking for their first job.It is all carrot and no stick.

Conservatives have a clear alternative.

We would halve the jobs tax for under-25s and cut business rates for 250,000 high street shops and hospitality venues.

"That would make a real difference to businesses deciding whether they can afford to take on another young person."

Mr Hollinrake said Mr Milburn was right to question why so many young people are being written off on sickness and disability benefits rather than helped into employment.

"Where someone needs treatment or support to work, that is what we should provide.

Simply handing out cash and leaving them without a route forward is no answer.

"Yet while the review proposes billions in additional spending, the savings appear comparatively modest.

Labour seems far more enthusiastic about the spending than the reforms needed to pay for it.

"We need a system that rewards work, encourages personal responsibility and gives businesses the confidence to create jobs.Because ultimately, governments don't create sustainable jobs.

Businesses do."

David Skaith, mayor of York and North Yorkshire, said: "Alan Milburn's review is a vital wake-up call for us all.

Too many young people are locked out of education, employment and training.

"It's time to stand up to this crisis and reset the system that champions the type of cradle to career work we're already doing, ensuring we support people from the early years right through to the workplace with guaranteed opportunities and local support."

The Labour mayor added: "We'll work with our partners to make sure that we have the right, locally designed and locally driven, solutions to meet this challenge."
