## The broadcaster says the US president has been avoiding its lawyers, and both sides have been told to be ready for trial in May.

US District Judge Jeffrey T. Kuntz, sitting in Florida, on Tuesday extended an earlier deadline of 11 December for depositions, giving Trump and the BBC more time to prepare their cases.

Trump must testify under oath by January 22 the judge ruled and both sides must be ready for trial by 3 May, 2027.

A deposition is out-of-court testimony given under oath before a trial. It’s part of the "discovery" stage of a US civil case, when each side gathers evidence from the other.

A witness or party is questioned by the opposing side’s lawyers, usually in a lawyer’s office rather than a courtroom. A court reporter records the answers, and the session is often filmed too. No judge is present.

The witness’s own lawyer can object to questions, but in most cases the witness still has to answer.

Trump accuses the BBC of misleadingly editing a documentary so that it looked as if he was inciting violence when he urged supporters to march on the US Capitol in Washington on 6 January 2021.

The Panorama programme, "Trump: A Second Chance", joined together two separate parts of his speech that day. This Trump claims gave the impression that he had directly called on his supporters to attack the building.

A crowd later stormed the Capitol in an attempt to stop the formal certification of Democrat Joe Biden’s victory in the 2020 presidential election.

The BBC has apologised for the edit, which led two senior executives to resign, but it denies that Trump suffered any real harm as a result.

Trump sought at least $5 billion on each of two claims: one for defamation and another under a Florida consumer protection law. Earlier this month, Kuntz rejected the BBC’s attempt to have the defamation claim thrown out, although he dismissed the consumer protection claim.

The BBC has accused Trump of dodging its efforts to obtain his sworn testimony while Trump's lawyers argue the president has been engaged campaigning for the recent mid-term elections rather than avoiding the deposition.

Hostility towards the media has been a hallmark of both of Trump’s terms in office. He has tried to ban outlets that broadcast critical coverage from the White House and accused a large number of journalists of reporting fake news.
