At 5 Lesnaya Street in Moscow stands a dark office building. It belongs to the so-called White Square, one of the best-known office complexes in the Russian capital. The facade is glass, the architecture plain.

When Deutsche Bank opened its new offices there on Dec. 15, 2021, the head of its Russian subsidiary waxed enthusiastic: "The new Moscow office of Deutsche Bank represents a significant investment and commitment to the Russian market," Borislav Ivanov-Blankenburg told the press at the time. "The space visually reflects our principles of openness and transparency.”

And that was despite the fact that the Russian army had been moving tens of thousands of soldiers and heavy weapons toward the Ukrainian border for the preceding several weeks, and US intelligence had warned just days earlier of a deployment of 175,000 fighters. "We need to be prepared for the worst," NATO Secretary General Jens Stoltenberg said at the time.

Almost five years later, there is still a bustle of activity around 5 Lesnaya Street. People hurry through the shiny revolving door, past the guards who make sure that only those who are authorized are able to enter.
