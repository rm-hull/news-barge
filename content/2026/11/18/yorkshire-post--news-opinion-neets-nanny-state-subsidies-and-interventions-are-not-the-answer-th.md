# Neets: nanny state subsidies and interventions are not the answer ... this is

![Alan Milburn speaking at Google offices in London, about his final report into young people not in employment, education or training (Neets). Picture date: Tuesday November 17, 2026.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjBkNGJlYzJjLWFhZTQtNDQ1Ni1iYzQ4LTQyZjdmZGI2OTg0ODoxN2Q3NWQ0MC1iMWQzLTQ2MjQtODc1Ny00OGViODc3NTUxMTQ=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


## Anyone tuned in to Alan Milburn’s press conference, at which he offered awaiting journalists some details regarding his year-long deep-dive into the plights of those young people who are not in education, employment or training (Neets), could be forgiven for believing the millions of young people who live in this country are identical. They are not.

Specifically in relation to the cohort now labelled ‘Neets’, to treat more than one million young people as a single, homogenous problem that can be diagnosed and dealt with uniformly is naive at best. Crucially, it is critical not to focus too much attention on Neets themselves, for those in said predicament are a consequence of decisions made elsewhere. A symptom of an illness not the cause thereof.

It comes back to something business desperately craves and that the political elite and their policy-makers do not seem to understand: prosperity comes from certainty.

The chopping-and-changing of Government Ministers, whose egos make them intent upon ‘making a difference’ and ‘leaving a legacy’ mean they meddle and fettle where they shouldn’t. Businesses cannot plan for tomorrow’s tea break let alone strategise 10 years into the future.

Subsidies and incentives is blind man’s bluff; grasping at straws in the dark with little hope of snagging a win. What is needed in order to tackle the Neets problem is stability. Allow the labour market experts to envision a national skills, learning and development strategy that begins in early years education. Scrap the red tape, bureaucracy and nannying that means young boys and girls cannot get a pot washer’s job for love nor money. Take your tax-heavy foot off the throat of business, invest in teachers and the national curriculum and the opportunities that young people will have will increase exponentially, solving the problem for you.
