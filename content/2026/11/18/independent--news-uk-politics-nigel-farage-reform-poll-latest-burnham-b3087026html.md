The crisis for Nigel Farage has deepened after the party dropped below 20 per cent in a poll for the first time in more than two years.

According to More in Common poll, Reform UK are on 19 per cent – the first time since October 2024 that they have polled in the teens.

It is a long fall from the point Reform hit is high of 33 per cent with More in Common in late October 2025.

The drop has continued, despite Mr Farage attempting to clean up his party’s image and is 11 points down from the 30 per cent they were still at this time last year.

![Reform UK leader Nigel Farage](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/16/01M6P1C2W8SM9MP7HVF77D0KST.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Last week, the politician brought in recently retired wing commander Angus McIntyre, who worked with NATO and Ukraine, in an apparent effort to show his party is not pro-Russia.

But the party has been on a downward trajectory since questions were asked about a £5 million donation made to Mr Farage from crypto billionaire Christopher Harborne which went undeclared and is under investigation by the standards commissioner.

Police are investigating former chief of staff Dan Jukes and former policy director James Orr after Channel 4 revealed they may have been willing to accept illegal donations.

Meanwhile, there have been problems over candidate selections with an ITV documentary exposing racism and misogyny.

The poll showed Labour up one point to 29 per cent and the Tories six points ahead of Reform on 25 per cent.

There were poor showings for the Lib Dems on 9 per cent and Greens on 8 per cent, both of whom appear to be struggling since Andy Burnham became prime minister.

Luke Tryl, the director of More in Common, said: “Labour is at its joint highest score since we resumed polling after the election.

“Reform has now declined 11 points since the aftermath of the local elections in May 2025, and the Conservatives are up six points.”

He noted that public opinion of the party leaders is also hitting new lows.

He said: “[Green leader Zack] Polanski (-37), Farage (-34), and [Restore leader Rupert] Lowe (-22) all hit their lowest ever net approval scores in our tracker, with [Lib Dem leader] Ed Davey (-14) also falling to his joint low. Meanwhile, [Tory leader Kemi] Badenoch’s approval is down to net neutral, and Burnham’s is down to +8.”

Stripping out negative views of the leaders, the leadership table is also reflected on gross approval ratings.

Mr Burnham leads on gross approval, at 40 per cent, followed by Ms Badenoch at 35 per cent, Mr Farage at 23 per cent, Mr Davey at 18 per cent, Mr Polanski at 14 per cent and Mr Lowe at 14 per cent.

Mr Farage is planning on trying to reverse his party’s downward spiral by launching a new pro-Brexit campaign in Brussels on Friday.

The launch will take place as Mr Burnham signs the latest Brexit reset deal with a view that it will be a launchpad for a much more radical overhaul of Britain’s status with the EU.
