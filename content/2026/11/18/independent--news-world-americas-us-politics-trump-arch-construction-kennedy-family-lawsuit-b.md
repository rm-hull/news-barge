The Kennedy family’s “visual and emotional injuries” shouldn’t stop Donald Trump’s administration from building a 250-foot “triumphal” arch at the foot of Arlington National Cemetery, according to the Department of Justice.

Nine Kennedy family members are among several plaintiffs suing to block the president’s plans for a towering arch they argue will cast shadows on the graves of John F. Kennedy and Edward M. Kennedy, both of whom are buried at Arlington National Cemetery in Washington, D.C.

The proposed arch, twice the height of the Lincoln Memorial, would also obstruct the sightline between that monument and Arlington House, the nation’s memorial to Robert E. Lee, designed to face each other in the aftermath of the Civil War.

Trump’s arch would also cast a shadow over the Kennedys’ graves, including on the eternal flame at the former president’s memorial, according to the family’s lawsuit.

But government attorneys argue that the shadows would only reach the gravesites “at most 0.03 percent of the year,” and in the mornings when the cemetery is not open. The family can’t sue for hypothetical “visual and emotional injuries” before the arch is even completed, DOJ lawyers said.

![The Justice Department says the Kennedy family’s hypothetical ‘visual and emotional injuries’ from Trump’s plans for a massive arch outside Arlington National Cemetary should not be able to block construction](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/11/08/2026-11-06T191311Z_2029630201_RC2OCHA5B6HP_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-ARCH.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The family’s complaint is “based on the effects of a completed Arch on the Kennedy gravesites,” and “they allege no injuries that can be traced to the early stages of construction,” according to the Justice Department.

The Kennedy family filed the lawsuit last week, five days after a federal judge temporarily blocked the Trump administration from building what the president has described as a dual-purpose monument and “top-grade military complex.”

An injunction from District Judge Tanya Chutkan arrived within an hour of approval of construction plans by a federal planning commission filled with Trump’s allies despite several hours of public comments urging the panel to reject it.

The National Capital Planning Commission, which the president has stuffed with his appointees, approved the project on November 5 following demands from members of Congress and veterans groups who are also suing the administration to shut it down.

Judge Chutkan has blocked the administration from working on the project until at least December 3 while the legal battle plays out.

![A federal judge is mulling whether to indefinitely block the Trump administration from building the 250-foot arch, which the Kennedy family, veterans groups and preservationists argue is illegally moving ahead without congressional approval](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/20/2026-11-07T124150Z_898600183_RC2MIOATVZYX_RTRMADP_3_USA-TRUMP-ARCH.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Kennedy family argues that the National Park Service and Interior Department unlawfully authorized construction without receiving necessary congressional approval and after federal agencies admitted that the arch would create “adverse effects” in the nation’s capital region.

Trump has spent the last year promoting plans for the arch, which the president, top Cabinet secretaries and Justice Department officials have argued could also be used to fend off drones and serve other vital “national security” purposes.

The Trump administration has repeatedly invoked “national security” arguments to push plans for his pet projects, including constructing a ballroom at the site of the former East Wing of the White House, which he demolished.

![The arch could cast shadows on the eternal flame at John F Kennedy’s gravesite and other graves at Arlington, where the bodies of thousands of veterans are buried, according to lawsuits against the project](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/21/450532783..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In court filings, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin outlined plans for a militarized arch that can deploy snipers and counter drones and target other “continuously evolving” threats.

But nothing in the plans approved by the National Capital Planning Commission references any military features.

“We believe Donald Trump has violated the laws and procedures established by Congress to protect this most sacred national memorial,” Rory Kennedy and Maxwell Kennedy said in a statement announcing the lawsuit on behalf of the family.

"Americans from every corner of the country visit Arlington to honor those who served and sacrificed for our nation,” they added. “A towering arch casting shadows across the graves of our father, mother, and uncles, as well as the graves of so many who have served our country, would also cast a shadow over our democratic principles, undermining the safeguards established by our elected representatives to protect this hallowed ground for generations to come.”

On Wednesday, lawyers for a group of military veterans who are also suing to stop the arch said the Trump administration will “blaze ahead as quickly as possible” barring a wider injunction that could block construction of the project indefinitely.

The Trump administration has alluded “vaguely to national security functions that the arch could theoretically fulfill,” but the government’s own evidence “establishes that no such functions are currently planned,” they argued.
