**The next local plan should give guidance to York council and residents on how life in the city should develop, writes Geoff Beacon**

On 21 March 2019, City of York Council passed a resolution setting the aim for York to become carbon neutral by 2030.

This resolution asked that both both production and consumption emissions be counted.

Gradually the demands in this resolution were watered down.

The requirement to count consumption emissions was dropped so that emissions from manufacturing imported German cars or from airline flights are not counted.

Additionally, the council seems to only count emissions for which it is directly responsible, ignoring those from York residents.

That means the council only acknowledges a small proportion of the emissions outlined in the 2019 resolution – but still officially maintains a target to make York a net-zero carbon city by **2030.**

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/climate-emergency-planning-board-ai-image.webp)

The Local Plan should estimate **all** the emissions that York citizens are responsible for, including  the expected carbon emissions from the construction of newly planned buildings and their future residents.

Our society is getting more unequal, with rich asset holders increasing their wealth compared to the rest of society. Planning has had a role in this, increasing the value of land holdings and existing property.

With the aim of transparency, the Local Plan should require that the additional value afforded by new planning permissions be estimated and published.

I accept that political constraints mean the Local Plan is unlikely to keep York within climate aims or solve the problem of inequality.

However, it aims to allow the plan sufficient flexibility to promote ways of life that are truly sustainable and more equitable.

**However, the plan could tell us the truth.**

A blog from the London School of Economics said in 2024: “The UK’s wealth gap has grown by 50% in eight years”. It also said:

*“Most wealth increases have been*‘* passive’, rather than being earned through hard work or entrepreneurial brilliance – and the lion’s share of the gains has gone to those who were already in the top 10%*.*“*

The 2025 Local Plan helps this trend continue – giving to the owners of property and land and putting up living costs for the rest.

#### That council resolution

On 21 March 2019, Council approved a motion which requested Executive to:

a. Declare a ‘Climate Emergency’.

b. Commit to a target of making York carbon neutral by 2030, taking into account both production and consumption emissions (scope 1, 2 and 3 of the Greenhouse Gas Protocol).

c. Request a report within six months setting out the immediate actions the Council will take to address this emergency and a plan to measure annual citywide progress towards meeting the 2030 target.

d. Work with partners across the city and across the region to deliver this new goal through all relevant strategies and plans and drawing on local and global best practice.

e. Actively lobby the Government to provide the additional powers and resources needed to meet the 2030 target
