## More and more disruptive events that are not classic military actions are occurring, yet they continue to threaten our security, from state-sponsored cyberattacks to climate change and global pandemics.

Supermarket refrigerators heated to 50 degrees by hackers. Grain ships destroyed by drones in the Black Sea, and water shortages in the Carpathian Basin that endanger tourism, farming and electricity supplies have been reported more in recent days. Incidents like these, caused by methods outside classical warfare, have become more frequent in recent months.

Researchers at the Global Security Research Group, part of the Institute of Global Studies at Corvinus University of Budapest, have recorded more than 50 so-called non-traditional security challenges in Central and Eastern Europe since October.

The database is open to the public on the Geo-Polemic Substack page, and its creators say they will update it regularly.

The group’s leader says the region is being hit at the same time by the effects of climate change and the war in Ukraine.

“I would not say that this region necessarily stands far out from the field in some way, but it is important from this point of view because of its specific vulnerabilities,” Péter Marton told Euronews.

Marton pointed to demographic challenges, ageing critical infrastructure and a level of energy dependence that is significant by comparison with other regions. “This justifies giving special attention to this region,” he said.

## **State actors behind more than half of incidents**

An associate professor at Corvinus, Marton said state actors were responsible for more than half of the incidents the team collected.

“Using military means, even in a way that is completely denied, damage can be inflicted on others with non-conventional methods,” he said, citing the Black Sea, where ships have been damaged and sunk.

“It is particularly sad to see significant grain shipments being lost when the state of global food supply is anything but reassuring. Only yesterday a shipment of 3,000 metric tonnes of grain was lost with a ship in Bulgarian territorial waters.”

Russia’s hybrid war features heavily in the news, but it is not the only state active in Europe. France, for example, has accused Iran of carrying out the cyberattack on its supermarket refrigeration systems.

## **Climate, energy and crime also count**

Non-state actors also play a major role, according to Marton. “After the summer of 2026 it is easy to recognise what kind of threat climate change can pose, or what kind of threat energy dependence and reliance on energy imports can mean for the economy,” he said.

Organised crime, terrorism and many other factors also fall under the heading of non-traditional security, he added.

The researchers plan to expand and regularly update the database and to draw conclusions from the data in a few months. They hope decision-makers can use the findings to defend against non-traditional threats more effectively, or at least to understand them better.
