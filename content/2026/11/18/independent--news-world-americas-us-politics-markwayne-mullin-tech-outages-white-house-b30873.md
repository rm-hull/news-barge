The White House was left flabbergasted by Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin saying that “a guy that’s very smart with tech” had told him AI may be behind the recent tech outages, according to a report.

Mullin appeared on Sean Hannity’s Fox News show Monday in the wake of the outages – which hit Microsoft, air traffic control systems and banking services over successive days – and was invited to reassure the public that there was no need to panic over society’s dependence on tech.

He responded by citing the anonymous IT expert, whom he clarified was not Elon Musk, as saying he believed “SI” (or super-intelligence) was to blame, using President Donald Trump’s preferred name for AI.

“He said because these programs aren’t designed to work as fast as SI is, and so when they contribute or when they put in a program that’s able to write codes and it goes in, it self-fixes itself,” Mullin recounted.

“As it starts self-fixing itself, it’s like doing a change order on a house. When you change one thing in a house, everything else starts changing. That’s a ripple effect, and it causes these systems to be overran that weren’t designed to run as fast as the SI technology is today.”

![Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin’s appearance on Fox News Monday reportedly left White House staffers flabbergasted](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/13/43/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-8-21-37-AM.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

According to *Politico*’s* Playbook*newsletter, “Trump administration group chats lit up with ‘WTFs,’” as staffers watched Mullin’s rambling anecdote, citing a person close to the White House as its source.* The Independent* has reached out to the West Wing for comment.

Mullin – who found himself one of the faces of the disaster, alongside Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, after the rare nationwide ground stop ordered by the Federal Aviation Administration Friday left domestic flights grounded for five hours – is addressing a House hearing Wednesday on “Worldwide Threats to the Homeland.”

The secretary was challenged at the hearing about his decision to fly out to Louisiana to catch a college football game Saturday, taking off just hours after the technical glitch that caused the flight chaos had been resolved.

Having initally defended himself by saying he would not have flown if airspace had not been reopened and that he had paid his own way, he told the representatives on Capitol Hill he had gone ahead with the jaunt to lead by example and avoid sowing “confusion” and “distrust.”

![Mullin has also been under fire over his decision to fly to Louisiana Saturday to catch a college football game just hours after thousands of domestic flights had been grounded by a tech disaster](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/2026-11-18T153250Z_1174912516_RC2FQOA2G12Q_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Since Mullin’s appearance on Hannity, further outages have since hit the U.S. National Security Agency’s classified internal communications network and Apple’s iPhone, further underlining the dangers of over-reliance on software.

Trump himself was asked about the problems back home on his visit to the Philippines and said with a philosophical shrug: “That’s life.”

Secretary of State Marco Rubio also addressed the outages from Manila, saying the incidents were not connected and, unlike Mullin, refusing to speculate on the part AI might or might not have played in bringing about the disruption.

Back in Washington, Vice President JD Vance sought to allay fears by saying there was no need for Americans to stockpile food and water or “build a bunker.”

​“We don’t see a crisis coming down, but you have to be prepared for everything,” he told Fox’s Laura Ingraham. “We want to make sure we prevent the worst outcomes, but we’re also prepared for the worst outcomes.”

![Vice President JD Vance has said Americans do not need to stockpile emergency food and water in response to the outages](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/11/29/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-10-58-54.png?quality=75&width=640&crop=3%3A2%2Csmart&auto=webp)

Former White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt meanwhile said on *Fox and Friends* that the administration must be clear about what is behind the recent issues, stressing: “Transparency is key.”

Emphasizing that she is now a political outsider, Leavitt said: “I’m here raising my family, I’m wondering the same thing: What’s happening? I’m checking my bank account with a little bit of worry.”

She called on Trump to “calm the temperature” by addressing the public when he returns from China and pointed to the hysteria spreading on TikTok.

“This has really been spiraling for quite some time, and I think again the White House should just address it directly with the American people.”
