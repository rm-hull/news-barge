# Doncaster: Appeal inquiry begins after solar farm refused

## A public inquiry has begun in Doncaster after a solar farm developer appealed a decision from local councillors to refuse them planning permission.

Plans for a solar farm in Marr, Doncaster, were refused by members of the City of Doncaster Council’s planning committee in November 2025 amid concerns over the impact it would have on green belt land.

The decision was met with applause from members of the public in attendance, but Enviromena, the company behind the proposals, immediately indicated it would lodge an appeal.

Councillors on the planning committee endured two hours of statements, largely against the plans, which covered a 77-hectare site, roughly 108 football pitches, situated next to the Marr Grange Farm Shop and Tea Rooms.

![Photo from Google. Shows the fields in Marr, Doncaster, on which the Marr Solar Farm would be built.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOmNmMzc3NWEwLTdkNWEtNDdmMy1hMjg4LWY4MzcyNzdjYWU4YjpkNTc2ODk2Mi00NTAwLTQ1YzUtOTE0MC1mNmFmYTdiMTlhNjM=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


The refusal was made possible by a late change to the designation of the land by Doncaster Council’s planning officials, who labelled it green belt rather than grey belt.

Grey belt is a term introduced by the Labour government, for “poor quality and ugly areas” of protected green belt, which can be built on.

Enviromena said it had won appeals in very similar circumstances elsewhere in the country, where the planning inspectorate had overturned the refusal and confirmed the land should be designated as grey belt.

The developer said the Marr Solar Farm could deliver £6.5m in business rates for the council during its 40-years of operation.

The inquiry is being led by the Government-appointed planning inspectorate and is expected to finish on Friday, November 20, 2026.
