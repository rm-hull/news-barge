If a letter from your car insurer has landed in your post box or online inbox, don't panic – and don't bin it.

This is the time of year when many insurers tell customers what next year will cost, typically between October and December for policies that renew on January 1st**.** Some drivers will pay more, some less and some the same. Here's how to work out which camp you're in, and what you can do if it's bad news.**Why has my premium changed?**

There are three likely reasons why your premium may have changed.

Your car or the area where your car is registered has been reclassified. German insurers rate every car model by its claims record (the "vehicle class" or *Typklasse*) and every registration district too (the "regional class" or* Regionalklasse*).

Models that crash often or cost a lot to fix end up in a higher class, which tends to mean a higher price. This applies even if your own record is spotless.

For 2027, more than 12 million drivers have been given a new vehicle class. About 7.4 million are moving up for liability cover and about 5.1 million down. Regional classes are shifting too, based on claims in your registration district.

Even before receiving a letter, you can find out whether your vehicle and regional classes have changed by checking on the German Insurance Association's (GDV) website, and comparing them with last year's policy.

But bear in mind that the classes are only a guide for insurers – a change in class doesn’t always guarantee a change in price.

The second reason is that you changed something yourself, such as reporting more mileage or adding a driver.

Finally, premiums could rise because your insurer's own costs have gone up. Some insurers point to rising repair and claims costs.

The letter you receive from your insurer should set out both the old price and the new price, as well as an explanation for why the amount has changed (assuming it has).

Check whether your cover has changed in any way, and if so how and why. Improved cover could account for the rise.

Finally, the letter should mention your right to cancel. For a policy which renews on January 1st, the deadline to switch is generally November 30th.

After this date, you may still be able to cancel if your insurer raises the price without making equivalent changes to your cover. This "special right to cancel" is called *Sonderkündigungsrecht* in German. If you changed something yourself, it probably won't apply.

But if you can take advantage of the right to cancel, you generally have one month from the day the notice reached you (not the day it was sent). So if the notice of an increase arrived on November 5th, then a cancellation typically has to reach the insurer no later than December 5th.

Under these circumstances, the cancellation would generally only take effect from when the increase would have applied.
