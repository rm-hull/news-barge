## An activist criticised a French public broadcaster after a presenter revealed her marriage to a senior TV executive, sparking controversy over transparency rules and political reactions.

French public broadcaster franceinfo has faced criticism after a presenter revealed feminist activist Anne-Cécile Mailfert's marriage to a senior television executive during a live broadcast on Monday.

Before she could speak, the presenter introduced her as the wife of the number two at public television giant France Télévisions.

Visibly outraged, Mailfert responded, "So what?", calling the disclosure "absolutely scandalous". She then challenged a male journalist on the panel, asking, "Who is your partner?"

## Transparency rules spark controversy

The incident followed the introduction of new transparency guidelines aimed at preventing conflicts of interest within France's public broadcasters.

While franceinfo initially defended the disclosure as consistent with the rules, it later acknowledged that Mailfert should have been informed beforehand.

Mailfert, who was heard shouting as she left the studio, addressed the incident on Instagram on Tuesday, saying it had deeply affected her.

"Yesterday, I experienced something that hurt me deeply," she wrote.

The activist insisted that she is "deeply committed" to the principles of "professional ethics, transparency, and integrity".

"However, these principles must address a genuine risk of a conflict of interest - one that is clearly defined - and the individuals involved must be informed."

Yannick Letranchant, franceinfo's head of ethics and transparency, acknowledged the challenge of balancing "necessary transparency" and the risk of "relegating an individual" to the status of being the partner of someone else.

## **Political reactions ahead of French presidential election**

The controversy has prompted reactions from across France's political spectrum, including hard-left presidential candidate Jean-Luc Melenchon but also parliament speaker Yael Braun-Pivet.

"A woman holds her own convictions, fights her own battles, and speaks for herself. This should be a given in 2026," Braun-Pivet said.

The dispute comes ahead of France's presidential election next year, as France Télévisions faces continued scrutiny over its political impartiality.

The public broadcaster has repeatedly been accused of left-wing bias by the far right, while also facing criticism from Mélenchon's hard-left movement.
