The Princess of Wales praised the "seismic change" Earthshot Prize winners will achieve to improve lives as pioneer environmentalists were honoured.

Kate made a rare speaking appearance at the awards ceremony established by William, emphasizing the "hope" offered by entrepreneurs, organisations and scientists confronting the climate crisis.

Delivering his keynote speech in Mumbai, William echoed his wife’s message, declaring this decade as the period when "positive change becomes irreversible".

He noted that history was "not written by challenges alone", but by those who stepped up to face them.

Among the winners receiving £1 million was Mahindra Last Mile Mobility, a company tackling India’s severe air pollution through electric rickshaws – one of which William drove earlier with Kate as a passenger.

The royal couple paid tribute to their Indian hosts by wearing outfits created in honour of the nation.

Oscar-winning actor Rami Malek and Indian cricket legend Sachin Tendulkar were among the presenters, while Take That – Gary Barlow, Howard Donald and Mark Owen – performed to an audience of 5,000.

Kate told the guests about some of the challenges facing the planet: “Entire species are being lost as habitats that have sustained life for millions of years, disappear before our eyes. And our landscapes are changing faster than ever.

“Fortunately, there is reason for hope.

“Across the globe, people are finding new ways to protect, restore and nurture the world around us. They remind us that a brighter future is something that we can all achieve together.”

Kate is said to be “proud” of William’s 10-year Earthshot Prize project, now at its midway point of finding, rewarding and scaling-up ideas to help repair the planet and generate a sense of optimism about the world’s environmental future.

The princess, who presented the Protect and Restore Nature award to Bhutan, added: “Just as the natural world finds ways to adapt and flourish, so too can we, when we nurture new ideas and give them the opportunity to grow.

“The people we’re celebrating tonight are a huge part of that story. Their work will inspire and create seismic change that will improve lives for generations to come.”

The country of Bhutan has written the protection of nature into its constitution, keeping nearly 70% forest cover which in turn helps protect the Himalayan water systems, a resource for a sixth of humanity.

![The Prince and Princess of Wales talk with guests during the Earthshot Prize Awards Ceremony](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/18/01M7N8VF2XMQRP5A03HRAV210S.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Kate stood on stage wearing an Anarkali dress by Mahima Mahajan made from handwoven silk Georgette with traditional Banarasi gold detailing and coloured in a rich vermilion using vegetable-based dyes.

While her husband was dressed in a bespoke Bandhgala – a classic Indian formal jacket – with hand-embroidered buttons by Manish Malhotra and made in India.

William said solutions to the planet’s environmental problems were available: “Around the world, we are witnessing human ingenuity on a remarkable scale.

“Because history is not written by challenges alone. It is written by the people who rise to meet them.

“And that is why we are here tonight. I choose to believe that this will be the decade where positive change becomes irreversible.”

The prince described how this change was fuelling “determination” in Generation Earthshot.

He said: “Young people from around the world who are not waiting for someone else to find the answers. They are stepping forward, bringing fresh ideas and showing what is possible when optimism is matched with action.”

Mumbai marked Earthshot by lighting up a major bridge with an image of India’s Prime Minister Narendra Modi, who William and Kate will meet on Wednesday, and the prince.

Speaking earlier in the day, Barlow said William had taken his father’s passion for the environment to the “next level” with the Earthshot Prize.

He said: “William’s dad, in the 70s, used to talk about microplastics, and people thought he was crazy.

“He’s really following on (from) his dad’s passion, I think.

“It’s wonderful when dads pass down things (and)… they take the baton, taking on to the next level.”

Donald added: “It’s super-inspiring to have people trying to help climate and to help the Earth. I find it fascinating.

“I mean, for me personally, anything at home, anything that’s recyclable, anything that’s reusable, anything that doesn’t pollute rivers, I’m there all the way.”
