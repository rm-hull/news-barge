AI has been worming its way into our everyday lives for years now, although it feels like an absolute age. If I had a nickel for everytime I heard a tech bro tell us that AI is inevitable so we should all get on board, I'd have enough money to buy back all the RAM they've taken from me. So with this in mind it's hardly surprising that yet another former game exec has come out with this exact line once again.

Ex-Take Two boss Ben Feder, who oversaw games like Red Dead Redemption and Grand Theft Auto 4, has discussed with The Game Business how one day people will accept AI—it's just a matter of time.

![looking at a sunset](https://cdn.mos.cms.futurecdn.net/3wYeRWjVYBcpjvJXGCzvuC-1200-80.jpg)

"When animation went from hand-drawn to computer animation, there was a massive wall of resistance," he says. "People didn’t think it looked good. Then Pixar launched Toy Story. They created a movie that not only was awesome, but used computer-generated animation and chose a subject matter that made the most of it, and did an amazing job with it.

"Once Toy Story launched, all the resistance went away. My own view is that we are one Toy Story moment away from consumer acceptance of AI in videogames in the West. In the East, they’ve already accepted it."

Feder is correct that the use of AI in game development does seem more widely accepted in countries like China and South Korea, where it's often viewed as an unremarkable part of the process. Still, for me the idea of comparing a beloved childhood film that was lovingly and painstakingly crafted with 10s of thousands of hand drawn story boards, clay models for characters, and multiple production teams, to a tool that cannot run without stolen ideas is mind-boggling.

I have some faith that machine learning can be used to help people make their jobs easier—that is, if AI companies want to make a product that's actually helpful and not one just that will make loads of money before it all comes crumbling down. But I simply cannot see how using AI for creative purposes can ever benefit a game. I think back to the current CEO of Take-Two Strauss Zelnick's view of AI: "Datasets by their very nature are backward looking, creativity by its very nature is forward looking."
