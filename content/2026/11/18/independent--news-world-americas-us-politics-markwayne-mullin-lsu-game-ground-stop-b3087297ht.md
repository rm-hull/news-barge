Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin claimed that if he did not fly to an LSU-Texas college football game the day after a nationwide air travel ground stop, it could have sown “confusion” and “distrust” for others hoping to fly.

Mullin appeared Wednesday in front of the House Homeland Security Committee for the annual “Worldwide Threats” hearing, which happened to fall days after a meltdown of the U.S. air traffic control system, a major Microsoft outage and glitches with online banking systems that temporarily prevented some customers from accessing their accounts.

The Homeland Security chief, alongside Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and Deputy FBI Director Christopher Raia, answered questions from lawmakers about the timing of the tech outages, which have sparked concern among Americans.

Mississippi Rep. Bennie Thompson, the top-ranking Democrat on the committee, said Mullin was “tone deaf” for attending Saturday’s game between the LSU Tigers and the Texas Longhorns in Baton Rouge, Louisiana, amid the travel and security chaos that unfolded Friday afternoon.

“While Americans were stranded in airports across the country, with many sleeping on the floor at their gates, the secretary boarded a luxury jet, flew to a college football game and visited the private suite of a company that DHS had awarded a $2.2 billion contract for the secretary of Homeland Security,” Thompson said.

![Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin claimed that if he did not fly to an LSU-Texas college football game the day after a nationwide ground stop, it could have sown ‘confusion’ and ‘distrust’ for travelers](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/2026-11-18T154211Z_1726900310_RC2FQOA791AM_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

His remarks prompted a defensive response from Mullin, who also said he was going to the game to be a “good uncle” to his niece, “who has never been to an LSU game.”

“We had deep conversations about it, and it came in that if we cancel something with the airspace that was completely open, it could bring confusion because I was meeting the governor there, I was meeting other people there,” said Mullin, who represented Oklahoma in the U.S. House, a rival of Texas. “That would show maybe distrust that we're not even willing to fly.”

Thompson referenced that Mullin was seen in the private box of Bollinger Shipping CEO Ben Bordelon, who holds numerous contracts with the DHS.

Mullin accused the Democrat of “hypocrisy” and said he had never met the executive.

When it was the turn of New Jersey Democrat LaMonica McIver, she had a heated exchange with Mullin and asked whether he would support fixing the air traffic control system by “redirecting some of the funds from ICE.”

“Well, by statute, I'm limited on what I can spend those funds on,” Mullin responded.

“So I guess that's a no,” McIver said, and asked Mullin whether he had any regrets about the role he is playing in the Trump administration’s immigration agenda.

![Mullin appeared Wednesday in front of the House Homeland Security Committee where he was quizzed on the recent tech outages and the shut down of the nation’s skies](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/2026-11-18T160911Z_1592116928_RC2FQOAM2XHP_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“I will never apologize about enforcing our nation's laws,” the secretary said.

“Not about even killing people?” McIver followed up.

“We’re not killing people,” Mullin claimed. Six people, including three American citizens, have died after being shot by federal immigration agents since last year, according to analysis.

Elsewhere in the hearing, Mullin distanced himself from the Nvidia controversy when asked what he knew about ex-CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with an alleged Chinese spy.

“The way that I understand it is because this is a spy outside of just our network, and they were handling it in different agencies,” he said. “If it was a direct threat to the homeland, I would have been read in on it.”

Democratic Rep. Seth Magaziner asked if Mullin was concerned Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had resisted implementing export controls on certain chips sold by Nvidia to China that others in the administration supported.

“This wasn’t something I’m dealing with. It’s outside of my lane,” he said.

FBI Director Kash Patel refused to appear at the hearing, according to GOP committee chairman Andrew R. Garbarino, who said he was “disappointed” by his absence.
