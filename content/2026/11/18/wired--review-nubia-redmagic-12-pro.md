Mobile gamers don’t have many dedicated gaming smartphones to choose from, so Nubia’s Redmagic series stands out. This big, boxy phone offers cutting-edge performance, long battery life, and a large display. You also get touch-sensitive shoulder buttons, RGB lighting, and gaming-focused software features.

Sadly, Nubia has followed every other phone manufacturer this year, offering minimal iterative improvements while jacking up the price significantly. While this series has always been a clear bargain for gamers seeking the best chipset, it’s harder to swallow the compromises lurking here (camera, software, update commitment) when the price starts at $949.

## Groundhog Day

The Nubia Redmagic 12 Pro sports the same boxy design as its predecessors, just a teeny bit slimmer and lighter. I’m a fan of the translucent industrial aesthetic and RGB lighting, but it’s not something I really want in my everyday phone. I had exactly the same issues with the 12 Pro as I had with the 10 Pro I reviewed two years ago. It’s very big, so you really feel it in your pocket, and it can be awkward to handle, especially when you’re trying to scroll one-handed. It’s also the most slippery phone ever. Every time I put it down, the 12 Pro slowly starts to slide away.

The big positive of this design is that it feels great in landscape orientation for gaming. The thick frame is easy to hold. There are touch-sensitive shoulder buttons on the top edge, and Nubia has a dedicated Magic Key for gaming mode, though you can customize it to launch the camera. This used to be a switch, but is now a red button. It’s still flanked by the round power button. Sadly, there’s no headphone port.
