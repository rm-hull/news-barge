A North Yorkshire man who claimed he wasn’t in a car involved in a crash has been banned from driving under drink-driving laws.

Alexander Armstrong, 28, was arrested following an incident in Malton on October 27, said Antony Farrell, prosecuting.

He smelt of alcohol and a witness told police they had seen Armstrong driving.

He then refused to take a breath test at Scarborough Police Station.

Armstrong represented himself before York Magistrates' Court.

He told deputy district judge Helen Chapman he refused to take the breathalyser test because “I wasn’t inside the car”.

He claimed someone else had driven the car that crashed that night.

Armstrong, of St Leonard’s Close, Malton, pleaded guilty to failure to provide a sample of breath for a breathalyser when suspected of driving while over the legal limit of alcohol.

The judge banned him from driving for 12 months, fined him £200 and ordered him to pay a £80 statutory surcharge and £85 prosecution costs.

The prosecution does not have to prove that someone was driving for them to be guilty of failure to provide a breath specimen for a breath test, but the prosecution does have to prove police had grounds to suspect the person of drink driving.
