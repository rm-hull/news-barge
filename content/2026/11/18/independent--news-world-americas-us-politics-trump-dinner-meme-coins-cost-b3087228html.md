For crypto enthusiasts, dinner with President Donald Trump now costs a fraction of last year’s get-in-the-door price.

An invitation to his first meme coin gala, held in May 2025 at Trump National Golf Club in Northern Virginia, required at least $55,000 in $TRUMP holdings. For his upcoming gala this weekend, $518 is enough to secure a seat, *The Washington Post* reported.

The lower threshold reflects the coin’s plunge to a record low and an apparent decline in interest: thousands of holders with enough coins to qualify did not sign up for a chance to attend.

Trump has long brushed aside concerns about his meme coin, which reportedly netted him $635 million in royalties last year — even as most buyers lost money. Asked last May whether he was profiting from cryptocurrency, he said, “I haven’t even looked.” In July, he said: “There’s nothing illegal. There’s nothing wrong with it.”

But greater scrutiny may lie ahead. Fresh off midterm victories fueled in part by voters’ concerns about corruption, Democrats have vowed to investigate his crypto ventures. A group of Democratic senators has called the latest gala an apparent “pay-to-play scheme.”

![The price to attend President Donald Trump’s latest meme coin gala, scheduled for Sunday in Virginia, is down considerably from last year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/06/GettyImages-2305380207.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Trump launched his meme coin just days before his second inauguration last year, urging supporters to buy into the venture.

“My NEW Official Trump Meme is HERE! It’s time to celebrate everything we stand for: WINNING,” he wrote on social media on January 18, 2025. “Join my very special Trump Community. GET YOUR $TRUMP NOW.”

The coin surged to about $74 within hours before falling to roughly $17 a month later.

In May 2025, as $TRUMP rallied, top holders were invited to an “intimate dinner” with the president at his Virginia golf club. Roughly 200 people attended, including crypto influencers, business executives and former NBA player Lamar Odom.

Qualifying for an invitation required at least $55,000 worth of the coin, and some buyers purchased six figures’ worth in hopes of attending, the *Post* reported.

![Democrats in Congress have vowed to investigate the president’s crypto ventures. Pictured: Senator Richard Blumenthal speaks during a forum to discuss Trump's official and personal dealings with crypto in July](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/34/GettyImages-2287897726.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On Sunday, Trump is set to host another dinner at the club, but this time, far smaller holdings have been enough to make the guest list.

Invitations are awarded through a competition that ranks participants by the size and duration of their $TRUMP holdings. One invitee, using the name “Michel,” secured a spot after purchasing just $518 worth of the coin.

The lower threshold follows a prolonged slide in the coin’s value. Despite occasional rallies, it was trading at about 97 cents on Tuesday, according to Coinbase. Amid the downturn, hundreds of thousands of buyers lost a total of $3.8 billion, *The New York Times* reported in July.

Interest in the dinner also appears to have faded. More than 5,000 holders had enough coins to qualify for an invitation but never signed up, the *Post* found.

![Sunday's dinner is set to be held at Trump National Golf Club Washington, DC Here, the president is seen riding in a golf cart at the course in May](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/45/GettyImages-2274806992.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

One firm, however, appears eager to attend: FastX, a little-known financial company with ties to Singapore and plans to build a “Chinese $TRUMP coin community.”

FastX claims to have accumulated more than $2 million worth of $TRUMP in recent months, according to CBS News. The CEO of its parent company boasted on X about the prospect of rubbing shoulders with the president.

When reached by the *Post*, a White House official said the upcoming gala is a private event that “held no promise of official government action in exchange for attending.”

Last month, ahead of the midterm elections, nine Democratic senators raised concerns about Sunday’s gala in a letter to then-White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, who has since been ousted. They singled out the event’s “scoring mechanism,” which rewards prospective attendees for holding more of the coin over a longer period.

“This dinner and scoring mechanism appear carefully constructed to increase investment in $TRUMP, allowing the President to abuse the power of his office to personally enrich himself, as he has throughout this term,” they wrote.

*The Independent* has contacted the White House for comment.
