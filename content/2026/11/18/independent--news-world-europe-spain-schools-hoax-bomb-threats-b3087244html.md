More than 10 schools in Spain’s north-western region of Galicia were evacuated on Wednesday following false bomb threats sent by email, the National Police said. The disruptions follow similar hoaxes targeting thousands of Italian schools last week.

A police spokesperson confirmed the messages were sent straight to the institutions, though their contents were not disclosed. Authorities said they could not yet verify the exact count of affected schools.

Specialised police units inspected the sites and found no suspicious devices.

The incidents represent the latest in a wave of hoaxes and cyberattacks affecting European broadcasters, air traffic systems, blood banks and supermarkets.

Last week, the Italian government stated that public buildings should only be cleared if there is concrete evidence of danger.

Authorities in Scotland, Northern Ireland and the Republic of Ireland also reported receiving similar foreign threatening emails last week, but assessed them as hoaxes and kept classes running.

A Police Scotland spokesperson said last week: “We understand how distressing messages of this nature can be for pupils, parents, carers and school staff, and the concern they can understandably cause.

“Our advice is that there is no requirement to evacuate schools or keep children away from education based solely on the content of the emails.

“They have been sent widely to distribution lists across the UK and Northern Ireland, and our assessment remains that they do not represent a credible threat.

“Police Scotland’s cyber and fraud unit is carrying out extensive inquiries to establish the source of the messages, and officers continue to liaise with law enforcement partners across the UK and internationally.”

Police confirmed that 470 schools in Scotland were impacted by the threat.

That threat followed a hoax bomb threat sent to a number of schools in Northern Ireland and Scotland last month.

Some European officials suspect Russia is running a disruption campaign against nations supporting Ukraine. Moscow denies involvement, alleging a Western smear campaign.
