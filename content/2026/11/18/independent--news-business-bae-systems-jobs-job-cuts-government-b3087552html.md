Britain’s largest defence manufacturer BAE Systems has announced it plans to cut up to 1,070 jobs in its head office and air sector in the UK.

Unite general secretary Sharon Graham warned “we won’t have a manufacturing industry left” without Government intervention, after the announcement.

BAE Systems makes weapons ranging across missiles and artillery systems, tanks, planes and warships.

The company employs around 50,000 people across the UK, including around 18,500 in its air sector and around 1,000 in head office, it said.

BAE added it intends to exclude those working in shopfloor manufacturing, aircraft maintenance at RAF bases and its early careers trainees.

The company is involved in the manufacturing of aircraft including the Eurofighter Typhoon and F-35 Lightning II.

A BAE Systems spokesperson said: “We’ve made significant investments across our business to enhance performance, accelerate innovation and boost capacity, so that in a rapidly changing defence and security environment we can meet our customers’ demands for greater speed and efficiency in getting critical capabilities into the hands of those who need them.

“This announcement is the next stage of our ongoing focus on enhancing our agility and competitiveness, so we can help to maximise the value of every pound our customers spend on defence.

“We recognise that this news may be unsettling for our people and we’re focused on achieving potential workforce reductions through a voluntary process.

“We’re committed to working closely with our trade unions and employee representatives and to keeping our employees informed and supported throughout.”

On the air section of the BAE website, the firm says its mission is “to ensure the Royal Air Force and its allies have the cutting-edge technologies they need — today and for the future — while creating lasting prosperity”.

Last month, the Chancellor and the Defence Secretary visited BAE Systems in Glasgow and announced new policy rules which the Government says will see more defence work carried out in the UK.

Under the new rules, overseas companies that win significant UK defence contracts will be required to invest back into Britain by providing new British jobs and apprenticeships, opening UK factories and investing in domestic suppliers.

Ms Graham said: “We won’t have a manufacturing industry left if the Government doesn’t start making critical decisions.

“For months Unite has been warning that delays in defence spending are driving companies in the sector to make negative decisions impacting British jobs.

“On the day after the Government backed a major boost in apprentices, we now have our biggest defence company announcing nearly a thousand redundancies.

“This shows a lack of joined-up thinking in Government.

![BAE Systems produces weapons ranging across missiles, artillery systems, tanks, aircraft and warships](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/10/30/16/30152727-2ee6e24c-6295-4b59-b04f-59d72c2ce274.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“In order to stop the erosion of defence jobs and get the growth we need, the Government needs to announce contracts for British-built Typhoons and Collaborative Combat Aircraft immediately.

“Unite has also warned that these problems are not limited solely to BAE.

“For instance, the Government must now award the Skynet 6 contract to Airbus, protecting thousands of highly skilled jobs in Portsmouth and Stevenage.”

An Airbus spokesperson said: “Airbus remains fully committed to the Skynet WSS competition and we continue to comply with all requirements and timelines set by the Ministry of Defence.”

The Ministry of Defence (MoD) said it has awarded BAE huge contracts.

A spokesperson said: “We know that any potential voluntary redundancies can cause concerns and our thoughts are with those potentially affected.

“This Government is delivering the biggest boost in defence spending since the Cold War, having already agreed £4 billion worth of investments since July.

“This includes awarding BAE a £708 million contract extension to lead the Global Combat Air Programme developments and supporting the £8 billion contract to supply Turkey with 20 Typhoon jets last year, which sustains 6,000 jobs at two BAE sites.”

Shadow defence secretary James Cartlidge accused the Government of causing the defence industry to “recede”.

He said: “The UK faces unprecedented threats – the Government have warned we could be at war with Russia by 2030.

“Yet whilst the defence sector is boosting the economies of Nato allies, at home, under Labour, we are seeing job losses. This is the real-world impact of Labour’s complete failure to fund defence. Rather than rearming, Labour is causing our defence industry to recede.

“With the Prime Minister unwilling and unable to cut welfare, the situation is only going to get worse.

“Only the Conservatives will properly fund our Armed Forces, cut the benefits bill, and back businesses to create jobs.”

Reform UK defence spokesman Angus MacIntyre also criticised ministers for not committing to higher spending.

“This hammer blow could have been prevented if the Government increased defence spending as it promised,” he said.
