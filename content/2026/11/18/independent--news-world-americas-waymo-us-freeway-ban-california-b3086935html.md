Waymo has stopped offering driverless taxi rides on all freeways in the US – and is yet to confirm when the suspension will lift.

The California-based company had allowed riders to hail so-called robotaxis, which use a combination of sensors, high-detail maps, and AI software that drives without a human behind the wheel, for freeway rides in the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Phoenix, Miami and Austin.

Waymo also offers rides on city streets in other areas such as Las Vegas, Dallas and Denver.

![The California DMW says the self-driving cars pose an ‘imminent hazard to public safety’ on the freeway](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/08/2026-11-09T105935Z_1662948519_RC2H2OADVH8V_RTRMADP_3_WAYMO-PROBE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A spokesperson for the company wrote in an email that it has “temporarily restricted commercial freeway rides in all markets where we operate that service,” though it’s “continuing freeway testing with trained autonomous specialists behind the wheel.”

The suspension was implemented last Wednesday but the spokesperson did not say how long it will last.

The move comes on the same day that California banned the autonomous vehicles from its freeways following two incidents, including one in which three Waymo vehicles drove into a construction zone.

The incident occured on 5 November, according to the California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) while the second incident, which involved California Highway Patrol vehicles, occurred the next day on a highway in San Francisco

Video footage of the 6 November incident circulated on social media, showing a Waymo vehicle that appears to try and bypass California Highway Patrol vehicles conducting a traffic break.

![Waymo says its vehicles are ‘making streets safer’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/08/2026-10-27T233941Z_1725641564_RC2WBOAZ7E0U_RTRMADP_3_WAYMO-ROBOTAXI.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“The Department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present,” DMV spokesperson Jonathan Groveman said in a statement.

Groveman added that “similar incidents” previously occurred on May 18.

On its website, Waymo says its vehicles are “making streets safer” and that it is striving to eliminate traffic deaths and serious injuries.

“Backed by industry-leading safety practices, the Waymo Driver is always alert, follows speed limits, promotes seat belt use, and operates some of the safest vehicles on the road,” it adds.

Over in the UK, the driverless cars have yet to be rolled out in London following technical hurdles – despite the UK government trying to accelerate the launch in 2025.
