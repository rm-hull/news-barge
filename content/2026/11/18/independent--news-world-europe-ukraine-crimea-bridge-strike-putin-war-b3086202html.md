Ukraine inflicted catastrophic damage on the bridge linking Russia and Crimea on Tuesday, delivering a major symbolic blow to Vladimir Putin.

Russian authorities said they opened a ⁠terrorism investigation following ⁠two Ukrainian ​attacks on ⁠the bridge over the Kerch Strait, adding ‌that the ‌structure had suffered damage.

All train and vehicle traffic on the Crimean Bridge has been suspended while specialists assess the damage, Russia’s ministry of transport reported on Tuesday morning. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov had earlier described damage to the bridge as minor.

Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky celebrated the strike, which he said was the result of a seven stage operation carried out over a number of weeks between the SSU and armed forces.

"We ⁠have long been ​telling ⁠Russia: Enough of ‌striking bridges, cities and villages, energy infrastructure, railways and ‌grain ships. Enough ‌of war. But every time Russia chooses further ⁠escalation," Zelensky said in his nightly video address.

![Anton Gerashchenko claimed a Flamingo cruise missile had hit the bridge](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/8/20/Kerch-bridge-strike.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

A Flamingo cruise missile was deployed to carry out a “precision-guided strike”, he said, alongside a range of other missiles and drones including Neptune missiles and Ruta drones.

He shared a video compilation of the strike, which showed a missile going towards the bridge. The strike itself appeared not to be recorded, as the president said the “communications uplink worked flawlessly until the very last seconds.”

Anton Gerashchenko, Ukraine’s former minister of internal affairs, said the operation had dealt out “critical damage” on the bridge.

“Last night, the Kerch Bridge, a symbol of occupation and Russian aggression against Ukraine, sustained critical damage during a powerful combined missile and drone attack.”

Ukrainian sanctions commissioner Vladislav Vlasiuk reshared a photo of the bridge with one of its arches missing on Tuesday morning, captioned: “Crimean Bridge. Sanctioned.”

The Kremlin-appointed governor of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, asked “residents and guests” of the region to avoid the bridge following the “enemy” strike.

“The enemy has attacked the Crimean Bridge again. At this moment, traffic is suspended. We ask residents and guests of Crimea to refrain from traveling towards the bridge and to take the restrictions into account when planning their routes. We will provide further updates on the situation. I remind you of the need to trust only official sources of information.”

The ⁠attacks, on Monday and Tuesday, killed two people and shut down the ‌bridge, ‌the ⁠Russian-installed governor said.

The 12-mile long (19km) Crimea Bridge is one of two routes available to Moscow, connecting the transport networks of Russia and Crimea after the Ukrainian peninsula was illegally annexed by Russia in 2014. It has been a key supply route for Russian forces in Ukraine and is widely viewed as one of Putin's signature infrastructure projects.

It consists of a separate roadway and railway, both supported by concrete stilts, which give way to a wider span held by steel arches at the point where ships pass between the Black Sea and the smaller Azov Sea.

Few countries have ‌recognised Crimea as part ​of the Russian Federation.

The bridge ‌has been attacked by Ukraine several times since Putin sent Russian forces ​into Ukraine in February 2022. A truck bomb in October 2022 ‌killed four people and limited the ⁠bridge's capacity for several months.

![The bridge is deemed one of Putin’s most significant infrastructure projects](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/13/1243917975..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The overnight strike was part of the largest Ukrainian drone attack on Russia since the start of the full-scale war in 2022, as the defence ministry said it shot down 1,441 ‌drones over 22 regions, ​surpassing Monday's figure of 1,336 which itself had ‌set a new record.

Air defence systems had ⁠repelled a massive drone attack on the industrial areas of Ufa and Salavat ‌in Russia's Bashkortostan ​region, home to a number ‌oil refineries, state media reported, ​citing local officials.

Russian attacks have also intensified ⁠in the past few months on bridges in Ukraine, including in the capital Kyiv. Two bridges over the ​Dnipro River sustained damage late on Monday, which prompted some water and power cuts and traffic restrictions.

President Zelensky said on Tuesday: “We have repeatedly offered Russia steps toward de-escalation – and every proposal has been met with loud rejection. Russia strikes our infrastructure every day, including bridges in Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Kyiv, and the Odesa region.

“We are restoring our infrastructure. But we are also reminding Russia that war will always return to where it began. Ukraine’s defensive capabilities are strong and will remain so, and we will never leave terror against our cities and people unanswered.

“I thank the true warriors of the Security Service of Ukraine, all our Defense Forces, our intelligence agencies, and EVERY manufacturer of weapons effectively used to defend Ukraine, Ukrainians, and our national interests. Glory to Ukraine!”
