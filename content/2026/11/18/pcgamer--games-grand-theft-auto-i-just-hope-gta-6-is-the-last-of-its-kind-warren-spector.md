Game industry legend Warren Spector retired in August, and somewhat ironically he's been more outspoken than ever about the state of the games industry. After blowing up over the closure of BioWare in October, he took a few weeks to think about it and got even more pissed off—and now he's shared some thoughts on the biggest videogame release of all time, Grand Theft Auto 6.

Unlike the BioWare mess, Spector's not angry about GTA 6, but he's clearly ambivalent: His latest missive on LinkedIn opens with a congratulations to the dev team and says, "It looks like it will be a high point of sorts for our medium and our business," which right out of the gate suggests that he's not entirely upbeat about it.

Spector wasted no time getting to the point. "Its billion-dollar development cost and an undoubtedly crazy marketing budget don't represent a business model anyone else can or probably should emulate," he wrote. "That's especially true when thousands of developers are out of work. A billion dollars pays for a lot of games and a lot of salaries.

"Personally I'd rather have hundreds of games and full employment than one game no matter how amazing it is."

At the same time, he said GTA 6 demonstrates the truth of a point he's made previously: "Give a game the time and money to prove its potential (key word, potential) and you have a chance of making something great. Underfund a game or force it out the door before it's ready guaranteed failure no matter how much potential it has."

It's not hard to see that as a reflection on Thick as Thieves, the stealth game released earlier this year by Otherside Entertainment, where Spector served as chief creative officer and creative director prior to his retirement. Initially announced as a PvPvE game, Thick As Thieves was reworked and scaled back prior to its early access release, and eventually just ran out of money, time, and steam.

But it also characterizes the state of the game industry as a whole, where a select crop of proven winners—like GTA 6—from a tiny number of studios are given whatever resources they need to flourish, and everyone else scrabbles for crumbs. Former BioWare stalwart Trick Weekes predicted in September that Electronic Arts would be reduced to "guns and football" following its acquisition by Saudi Arabia's PIF, and so far he doesn't appear to be wrong.

In that light, Spector's ambivalence, and his conclusion, aren't hard to understand. "Again, congratulations. Ambition and innovation are necessary for the medium to reach its potential," he wrote. "I just hope GTA 6 is the last of its kind even if it's the best we have to offer."
