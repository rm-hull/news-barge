The energy company SSE has warned it will close its Atwick natural gas storage facility in northern England next summer, if the government does not intervene to offer support amid weak market conditions.

The site, located near Hornsea on the East Yorkshire coast, consists of eight caverns with the capacity to store about 309m cubic metres of gas, estimated at about 1.5 days’ worth of Great Britain’s gas usage.

SSE said in an update to investors: “It is increasingly clear that existing market arrangements do not support their continued operation.”

It added that gas storage facilities such as Atwick have a “strategic importance” to the energy system.

Energy companies rely on stockpiling gas in their storage facilities during the summer, when demand is lower and prices are usually lower, before withdrawing the stored gas and selling it during winter when demand for energy for heating and power peaks.

However, the gas price increase triggered by the Iran conflict and the effective closure of the strait of Hormuz have made it uneconomical to stockpile gas during the summer.

As a result, countries across Europe have entered the cooler months of the year with gas stores at their lowest level in more than a decade.

SSE said: “Despite current commodity prices, favourable spreads are not emerging to support the ongoing commercial operation of Atwick.” It came as the company announced its results for the six months to the end of September.

The closure of a gas storage facility such as Atwick could leave the UK even more exposed to price shocks on global markets.

Britain already stores less gas than some of its European neighbours and is instead more reliant on liquefied natural gas imports.

SSE’s announcement comes months after British Gas’s owner, Centrica, warned it would close its Rough site – the UK’s largest gas storage facility – unless it can agree a long-term financial support deal with the government.

The trade union Unite has called on ministers and Centrica to work together to secure the future of Rough, which the company has scheduled for closure in April 2027.

SSE said it continued to engage with the government “on the need for policy intervention to support a resilient gas system”.

The Atwick facility has been in operation since 1979 and has been owned by SSE since September 2002.

The jump in wholesale gas prices has fed through into household bills. On Wednesday the energy consultancy Cornwall Insight forecast the typical household gas and electricity bill will jump by 26% in January to the equivalent of £2,166 a year.

The government has been contacted for comment.
