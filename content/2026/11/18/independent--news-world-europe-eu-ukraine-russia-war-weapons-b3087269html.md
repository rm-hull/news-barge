The European Commission has proposed a new €45 billion ( approximately £37.5 billion) loan to help Ukraine as Russia intensifies attacks on critical infrastructure.

Ukraine anticipates a €70 billion (£58.3 billion) funding gap for 2027 as the war nears its fifth year.

Ursula von der Leyen, the bloc’s president, said Ukraine would only need to repay the loan after Russia had paid reparations for damage caused by the war.

The planned measure, which still needs approval from member states, would come on top of a similar loan of €90 billion (£75 billion) that was agreed by EU leaders at the end of 2026 to fund Ukraine.

![Volodymyr Zelenskiy welcomed ⁠the proposal](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/15/05/2026-11-14T121931Z_2098713556_RC2DMOAH0895_RTRMADP_3_UKRAINE-CRISIS-ZELENSKIY-TALKS.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Von der Leyen told reporters in Brussels that Europe “stands united” amid Russia’s war of aggression, reiterating the bloc’s support for Ukraine “in war and in peace”.

She added that Kyiv's other international partners will be expected to provide the rest of the money to fill next year's funding gap.

The Commission proposes that up to €25 billion (£20.8 billion) of the new loan could be spent on military aid while the remaining €20 billion (£16.6 billion) has been earmarked on general budget support.

To finance the loan, the Commission will borrow on behalf of the EU on international markets, officials said.

![Several EU states have opted out of previous loans to Ukraine](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/2305173216..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy welcomed ⁠the proposal, saying the loan would "cover a significant part" of the country's budget and defence needs for next year.

"We very much hope for the swift adoption of this decision by the European Parliament and the Council (of EU governments) and count on the support of all EU member states," he said on X (formerly Twitter).

European Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis said Ukraine needed additional support because Russia is intensifying attacks on critical infrastructure and disrupting trade, particularly by blocking Black ‌Sea export routes.

"For almost five years, Ukraine has stood guard against the Russian aggression that also threatens our citizens, our territory, and our way of life,” he said. “Our support gives Ukraine a crucial strategic partner.”

Von ⁠der Leyen said she was confident all EU members would back the new loan, ⁠even though three — Hungary, the Czech Republic and Slovakia — opted out of the last one.

She said even EU countries that did not favour giving military aid to Ukraine were still committed to supporting Kyiv more generally, so their support for the loan could be attributed to meeting civilian needs.
