London Underground staff are set to go on strike in support of an employee who was sacked after bosses found he was doing a second job while on sick leave.

Members of rail union the TSSA will bring the Piccadilly Line to a standstill for 24 hours from noon this Friday. The strike is expected to badly hit services on both the Piccadilly and District lines on Friday and Saturday.

A disciplinary investigation by Transport for London (TfL) chiefs found the employee had been working in a second job while receiving full company sick pay.

They said it amounted to gross misconduct and a serious breach of contractual employment obligations and branded the strike unjustified.

But the Underground service control staff are protesting against what they claim was a breach of the employee’s data privacy, accusing investigators of sharing confidential information with third parties.

In a ballot, almost two dozen TSSA members unanimously backed a strike.

Lee Stewart, TfL’s head of London Underground service control, said: “We are extremely disappointed that TSSA is proceeding with unjustified strike action in response to the dismissal of an employee working a second job while receiving full sick pay.

![Travellers will face line closures and busier lines elsewhere](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/06/02/13/2279405356..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We do not believe industrial action is justified in circumstances where an employee has been dismissed following a fair process and findings of gross misconduct.”

He urged the TSSA to withdraw its strike.

But the union blamed what it said was the “improper conduct” of TfL's investigation team, which it said “accessed and shared our member’s private and confidential information with third parties and which the union believes to be in breach of GDPR regulations”.

The TSSA, whose members will also have a picket line at South Kensington, says complaints have been raised with the Information Commissioner's Office over the conduct of the investigation.

General secretary Maryam Eslamdoust said the employee had been treated unfairly, adding: “There's still time to stop this happening if London Underground are willing to make good on the ill-treatment of our member and find a fair and reasonable resolution.”

Tube passengers are being warned that other lines might be busier than usual.

Mr Stewart said: “We will do all we can to minimise disruption for our customers. I would encourage Piccadilly line customers travelling on Friday and Saturday to check before they travel and consider alternative routes where possible.”

The strike is unrelated to action planned by the RMT over TfL’s proposals for a four-day working week for Tube drivers. That action will affect the whole network on 26 and 27 November.
