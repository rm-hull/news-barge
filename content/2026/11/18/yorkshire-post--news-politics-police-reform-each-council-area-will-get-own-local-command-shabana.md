# Police reform: each council area will get own local command, Shabana Mahmood says

## Policing will be restructured into about 200 local commands who will report to larger regional forces, the Home Secretary has said.

Each area will be around the size of a unitary authority and led by a named commander, Shabana Mahmood announced in a conference speech earlier today.

Those commands will be headed by regional specialist police services, the planned number of which has not been revealed.

This comes after the Government set out plans to radically overhaul the police service in England and Wales, creating a national police service dubbed the British FBI as well as fewer, larger geographical forces.

There have been reports that Yorkshire and the Humber could be governed by a single “mega force”, with Scotland Yard Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley calling for the total number across England and Wales to be shrunk from 43 to 10 or below.

![Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood speaking at the APCC and NPCC conference. Credit: Will Colebourne/Press Association](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/18/14/17/01M7QF9N78A6WVBESHMA9EK8T6.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


Responsibilities including counter-terrorism, some fraud and serious and organised crime, the police air service and roads policing would all be managed by the new national body.

Policing is billions in debt, with the shortfall set to grow by £2.5 billion by 2030, delegates at the annual conference of the National Police Chiefs’ Council and the Association of Police and Crime Commissioners have already been told.

Ms Mahmood said root and branch reform is needed to make sure there are consistent standards in policing across the country.

![Ms Mahmood speaking at the cop conference. Credit: Will Colebourne/Press Association](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/jpim-static/image/2026/11/18/14/00/01M7QFAPM27PSZJV6YMEEAPBFF.jpg?trim=0,0,0,0&width=640&quality=65)


The recent incident at RAF Fairford needed every on duty officer in Gloucestershire, while disorder in Gosport required officers to be drafted in from 13 forces, she said.

Cleveland Police had to take in officers from a number of neighbouring forces through mutual aid to respond to the A66 crash and the ensuing violence.

Speaking to journalists after her speech, the Home Secretary said a timetable for the changes will be set out once the Police Reform Bill has been laid in Parliament in the spring.

“We’re going to go slow and at the right pace because I don’t want to transfer operational responsibilities, for example, to the National Police Service until I know it’s fully ready, because I don’t want to see a drop-off in service.

“Public safety comes first, and I don’t want to do it too quickly, such that you see a drop-off in service for communities.”

Since Andy Burnham has become Prime Minister, he is thought to favour keeping mayors in their oversight roles with the police.

This makes a single pan-Yorkshire force less likely. The review, led by former Scotland Yard chief Lord Bernard Hogan-Howe, is set to make recommendations in the coming months.
