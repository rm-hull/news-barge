## China’s Xi welcomed his US and Russian counterparts at the APEC summit as the three global powers’ leaders gear up for their first trilateral meeting. Trump shook hands with Putin, expressing hope about the chances of ending the war in Ukraine.

US President Donald Trump and Russia's Vladimir Putin shook hands in China on Wednesday as they were welcomed by their Chinese counterpart Xi Jinping for crucial trilateral talks on Thursday — the first such summit between the leaders of three global powers.

In their first meeting since August 2025, Trump and Putin exchanged greetings and handshakes with Xi, then posed on stage for a family photo at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit gala dinner in Shenzhen.

This was Putin's first appearance in an APEC leaders' family photo in nine years, marking progress for the Kremlin amid Russia's isolation on the world stage following Moscow's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in early 2022.

Xi welcomed Trump at Shenzhen airport earlier on Wednesday, paying particular honour to Trump by greeting him with a hug, reciprocating a gesture the US president made during Xi’s state visit to Washington in September.

When Putin arrived earlier on Wednesday, Xi did not greet him at the airport despite their longstanding positive relationship.

Trump said that during the unprecedented trilateral meeting on Thursday he plans to talk with Xi and Putin about ending Russia’s ongoing all-out war in Ukraine, declaring on Tuesday that “I think it’s very close to getting it done".

“We are going to have a good meeting on that," Trump said.

The three leaders are also expected to discuss trade and nuclear issues as well as the Iran war.

Beijing announced the trilateral meeting as “in-depth exchanges on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations” on the sidelines of the APEC meeting, while Putin said that he expected the summit to be “highly useful”.

Trump has signalled that he wants to take an improvised approach at the APEC summit, betting that his unscripted brand of diplomacy, based on personal relationships, can create a legacy by reshaping world affairs during his final two years in the White House.

Europe and the world will be watching as Trump meets with two of Washington's largest geopolitical rivals after relations with US’ historical allies such as Europe and Canada have turned sour over the last months.

Trump raised the possibility of a private meeting with Putin and a separate sit-down with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, which had not yet been confirmed by the time the US president arrived in China.

During his first in-person appearance at an APEC leaders meeting since 2017, Putin met his Chinese counterpart on Wednesday and took aim at what he called “instruments of economic dominance,” such as the sanctions imposed on Russia following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in early 2022.

“Sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions” have “outlived their usefulness and have largely lost their intimidating effect,” Putin told a CEO summit on the sidelines of the APEC summit.

He highlighted his country’s desire to cultivate closer economic ties with the Asia-Pacific, saying the region accounts for more than half of its total trade.

## **Xi calls for open trade and equitable growth**

In a speech on the sidelines of the APEC summit on Wednesday, the Chinese president told business executives that the Asia-Pacific must pursue more open trade and equitable growth to remain stable and dynamic.

"China will not only cut tariffs and expand market access, but also go a step further. We will carry out reform in challenging areas such as rules, regulation, and standards,” Xi said, reiterating Beijing’s pledges to improve conditions for global investors.

"While pursuing its own development, China has shared opportunities with other countries and worked for common development,” he said.

“It has steadily brought stability and predictability to world economic growth and international trade and economic cooperation.”

Xi and other APEC leaders called for a more open approach, without directly criticising the US shift toward protecting its own markets.

Trump’s “America First” approach of tariff hikes and other moves runs counter to APEC’s aim of eventually forming a Pacific Rim free trade zone, as Washington limits on Chinese access to cutting-edge US technology, such as the Nvidia computer chips that help to power artificial intelligence, are a constant source of strain with Beijing.

Trump is expected to seek to reinforce the US status as the world’s leading technology power.

His tariffs, intended to curb China's trade surplus, have done little to stop the progress of the world’s dominant manufacturer, which for the second straight year will have exported $1 trillion more in goods than it imported.

When answering reporters about US policies towards Beijing's access to advanced computer chips, Trump said there were differences of opinion inside his administration and that “we have a very good relationship with China, and you don’t want to insult them.”

Trump’s emphasis on friendly relations with Xi may extend the trade truce reached last year after a series of tariff escalations.

Trump has also said that during his talks with Xi, he does not plan to bring up Taiwan, the leading manufacturer of advanced computer chips, including those designed by US companies, but expects Xi to push him on Taiwan’s sovereignty.

“I’m sure he’ll raise it,” Trump said. “I won’t have to raise it.”

The 21 APEC economies account for 60% of global economic activity, half of global trade and 40% of humanity. The leaders will meet behind closed doors on Thursday, while holding other meetings on the event’s sidelines.

APEC does not take binding decisions, but over 37 years it has served as a forum for building a consensus on cooperation and more open trade.

The APEC summit is showcasing Shenzhen, which China has transformed in the past four decades from a fishing village into a skyscraper-studded centre for export manufacturing and technology.
