President Donald Trump has been trying to push through several renovations in Washington, D.C. — but there is one project that helped propel him to the White House in the first place that he’s rushing to finish.

“Build that wall!” Trump chanted during his first presidential campaign in 2016. His MAGA movement was built on anti-immigrant rhetoric, calling people who try to cross the southern border “criminals, drug dealers, rapists.”

In the decade Trump has been trying to win political support, he has promised to build a wall along the southern border. After losing the presidency to Joe Biden in 2020, Trump blamed him for insecure borders that immigrants flooded into and used that as a reason to start a massive deportation operation when returning to the White House in early 2025.

Despite his hardline immigration policies, Trump hasn’t finished the wall — and he very well may never.

There are about 619 miles of primary border wall the administration still has to build, Axios reported, citing data from Customs and Border Protection. Much of it is in Texas, where landowners are opposed to construction.

![President Donald Trump has been trying to push through several renovations in Washington, D.C. — but the wall at the southern border that he promised in 2016 still isn't complete](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/0/41/GettyImages-2303033891.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Landowners and other opponents in the Big Bend region, which has about 500 miles of border, filed a lawsuit in September to stop the Trump administration from building more wall.

"For us, the Big Bend is not an empty place on the map," local historian David Keller said at a press conference announcing the lawsuit, per the Associated Press. “It is our home."

Even without the border wall completed, Customs and Border Protection has reported that no migrants have made it past the border in the last 18 months.

A CBP spokesperson credited this record to “the end of catch-and-release, consistent use of detention and removal authorities, and a clear message that illegal entry into the United States will not be rewarded or tolerated,” in a statement to *The Independent*.

But the agency is still hurrying construction on the border wall.

![There are about 619 miles of primary border wall the administration still has to build, according to a new report](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/0/59/GettyImages-2301934775.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

About 3.9 miles of border wall was being built per week between mid-June and early November — up from 2.6 miles a week earlier in the year, according to an Axios review of CBP data.

The CBP spokesperson told *The Independent* that the agency “is advancing steadily toward its construction targets, with primary border wall completion projected for January 2028.” At its current pace, the primary wall won’t be complete until late 2029, after Trump leaves office, according to Axios’ estimate.

“Following funding from the Working Families Tax Cut Act, all Southwest border construction contracts were awarded by June 30, 2026,” the spokesperson explained. “Because major infrastructure projects build momentum rather than moving at a single steady speed, production is accelerating rapidly as projects shift from design into active construction.”

Trump is likely to experience increased opposition during his last two years in office, with Democrats winning big in Texas during the midterm elections.

"Just about every Democrat running statewide came to the Big Bend," Susan Hays, Presidio County Commissioner-elect, told Axios. "All of them care about the border, the Big Bend, and they're going to fight for our public lands."
