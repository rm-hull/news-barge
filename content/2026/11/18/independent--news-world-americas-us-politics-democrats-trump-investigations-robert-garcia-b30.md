The top Democrat on a congressional oversight committee is putting Donald Trump’s administration, and the president’s family, on notice.

Rep. Robert Garcia has revealed a roadmap for investigating the president’s allies, top administration officials and the Trump family’s financial interests when Democrats take control of Congress next year.

Garcia - who recently received threatening letters from the White House and a federal prosecutor - has sent out 12 letters of his own to members of the Trump family as well as top White House officials and other MAGA-allied figures facing allegations of government misconduct, profiteering and corruption.

The targets: Trump’s sons Donald Trump, Jr. and Eric Trump and son-in-law Jared Kushner; former U.S. ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle; Vice President JD Vance; White House chief of staff Stephen Miller; former White House chief of staff Susie Wiles; Attorney General Todd Blanche; former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem; and her former adviser Corey Lewandowski.

Garcia wants them to preserve documents related to targets of the committee’s investigations, including pay-to-play contracts, the Trump family’s business dealings while in office, and the White House’s role in the release of the government’s files on late sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.

![President Donald Trump’s eldest son, Donald Trump Jr, and Vice President JD Vance are among the targets of upcoming House Oversight Committee investigations](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/08/10/16/2226872915..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Oversight Democrats are fighting to put an end to this Administration’s self-dealing, misconduct, and abuse,” Garcia said Wednesday. “These investigations will lay the groundwork for legislation which helps all Americans. This is just the beginning.”

In a statement to *The Independent*, White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis said the president is “willing to work with anyone to continue delivering victories for hardworking Americans,” but, “if Democrats choose to pursue sham investigations and radical left policies, they are choosing to work against the American people.”

A White House official added that Democrats “should be reminded that we still control the federal government and all of its powers.”

Garcia’s office also sent preservation letters to the CEO of the Trust for the National Mall, Catherine Townsend, over Trump’s plans for the White House ballroom and its corporate donors who are doing business with the government.

His office also is seeking information from top federal prison officials about the transfer of Epstein’s co-conspirator Ghislaine Maxwell to a minimum-security prison camp. Maxwell was found guilty of recruiting and grooming young women and girls for Epstein, who died by suicide in prison in 2019 while awaiting his own trial on trafficking charges. She was sentenced to 20 years in prison in 2022.

The letters provide a roadmap into how Democrats will begin oversight investigations into the Trump administration when they take control of both chambers of Congress early next year after sweeping midterm victories.

Democrats are now navigating how to tackle urgent legislative priorities — including addressing the Iran war and a growing cost-of-living crisis — while holding the administration accountable. That tug-of-war that is likely to end up in legal battles that could drag out over the course of Trump’s final two years in office.

![Former White House chief of staff Susie Wiles, left, and current chief of staff Stephen Miller are also being asked about the Epstein files and immigration enforcement, respectively, letters show](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/09/17/2303867394..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Among the priorities are the Trump family’s stakes in crypto currency company World Liberty Financial. A federal lawsuit filed in October accuses the president and his family of “corruptly profiting” off more than $2 billion in digital currency-related deals in potential violation of constitutional restrictions on accepting foreign money.

Garcia’s committee also wants to investigate Donald Trump Jr.’s Bahamas wedding festivities that were partially funded by a Russian oligarch with close ties to Vladimir Putin.

A letter to Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner targets his work as a key Trump envoy advising officials on Middle East policy and Russia’s war against Ukraine.

According to Garcia’s office, Kushner’s “business dealings and public work on behalf of the U.S. government raise concerns that hundreds of millions of dollars in management fees, business opportunities, and other financial benefits are serving as disguised payoffs for policy changes, preferential treatment, relief from legal scrutiny, and access to sensitive government information and technology.”

![Rep Robert Garcia, who is poised to chair the influential House Oversight Committee in January, has issued 12 document preservation letters to current and former administration officials](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/2304033143..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Other letters target Stephen Miller’s role in oversight of immigration enforcement and how Noem and her deputy Lewandowski signed off on DHS contracts.

Blanche, Vance and Trump’s now-former chief of staff Susie Wiles are also being asked about their alleged White House Situation Room meetings about the Epstein files and how they sought to mitigate the political fallout from the release of millions of documents surrounding the wealthy and well-connected sex offender.

Garcia is among four Democratic members who have received threatening letters from a Trump-allied federal prosecutor in Texas, Aaron Reitz, who has publicly made himself the face of a post-election legal strategy to bolster the administration against investigations from the incoming Congress.

Reitz has also sent letters to Senators Richard Blumenthal, Elissa Slotkin and Elizabeth Warren over their public statements about upcoming congressional probes and preliminary investigations into the administration.

White House counsel Will Scharf also sent a letter to Garcia this month warning that the Trump administration was “monitoring” the speech of Democratic members of Congress.

He suggested Democrats are threatening executive branch officials to prevent them from public service with investigations that veer into “blatant criminality.”

Scharf has ordered the heads of federal agencies to receive explicit White House permission before agreeing to testify to congressional committees.
