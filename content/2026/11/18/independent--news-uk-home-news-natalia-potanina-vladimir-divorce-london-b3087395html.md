A sanctioned Russian billionaire has been told should he pay more than £2.1 billion to his ex-wife in what is believed to be the highest-value divorce battle in English legal history.

Natalia Potanina is seeking the money from businessman Vladimir Potanin, who is estimated to be worth billions.

Mr Potanin, who has said he earns £42.2 million per year, argues she should not receive the money and has offered £25 million.

The 65-year-olds married in Russia in 1983 and have three adult children, but divorced in Russia in 2014 and have been engaged in divorce proceedings in English courts since 2018.

Charles Howard KC, for Ms Potanina, said in written submissions for a hearing on Wednesday that she and Mr Potanin enjoyed a “lavish lifestyle” when they were together, and had “fabulous homes” and luxury yachts.

He said in court that the then-couple paid George Michael £1.7 million to perform at their 2006 New Year’s Eve celebration.

Mr Potanin claims the pair separated in January 2007, but Ms Potanina contends they did not separate until 2013.

Mr Howard said that after the couple divorced in 2014, Russian courts only considered Mr Potanin’s legally owned assets when determining his wealth, rather than also evaluating assets he beneficially owned.

This meant the latter “fell off the radar” and Ms Potanina was “deprived of anything like a fair share of the parties’ wealth”.

![A picture taken late on December 04, 2017 shows Russian President Vladimir Putin (L) meeting with metals magnate Vladimir Potanin at the Novo-Ogaryovo state residence outside Moscow.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/885876544..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The barrister said: “Ms Potanina has ended up with less than 1% of the wealth generated during this long marriage.

“On any objective analysis, that is hugely unfair and a huge injustice, particularly when it entails her being deprived of billions of dollars.”

He continued that the English courts should “remedy the injustice” and award her a “meaningful share of the assets”.

This included more than £172 million for UK property, and annual income of £12.3 million.

Mr Howard said that while his client was “undoubtedly seeking a considerable amount of money”, this was “the inevitable and natural consequence of the level of wealth generated during the marriage”.

He said: “There has never been a contested case in the family court… with this level of wealth, after a very long marriage, and where the parties started with nothing and all the billions were created during the marriage.

“But the scale of wealth cannot justify discriminating against Ms Potanina.”

Lord Faulks KC, for Mr Potanin, said Ms Potanina received a “big money” award in the Russian divorce proceedings and left the marriage as a “very wealthy woman”, but is now seeking £2.174 billion.

![Russia's mining giant Norilsk Nickel President Vladimir Potanin attends a meeting with Russian President in Moscow on July 15, 2026.](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/2285581519..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He continued that she “wishes to turn back the clock 20 years” to “return to living an ultra-high net worth lifestyle”.

But he added she “has already transitioned to independence without undue hardship” since the separation and lives a “very nice and fulfilled life”.

The barrister said that while Ms Potanina “plainly has significant financial aspirations”, they “do not bear scrutiny as relationship-generated needs”.

He said: “Properly viewed, she has sufficient resources to meet her needs at a reasonable level, or, putting it differently, she has no needs which it would be reasonable for this court to order Mr Potanin to meet.”

He added: “Mere disparity between what would have been ordered in England and what was ordered in the overseas jurisdiction, and/or between what the spouses respectively received, cannot alone amount to injustice.”

He said: “The rationale behind why the Russian courts made the award they did is irrelevant and a very dangerous path for this court to go down.”

Lord Faulks said it would be “impossible to enforce” any order made by the English courts in Russia, and that even if Ms Potanina was awarded the money she sought, “there can be no certainty” that she would be able to access the money due to “brutal” sanctions against Mr Potanin.

Mr Potanin has been sanctioned since 2022 after the Russian invasion of Ukraine, due to his ownership of the Interros conglomerate.

The hearing before Mr Justice MacDonald is due to conclude in December, with a judgment expected in writing at a later date.
