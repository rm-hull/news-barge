Conservative influencer Brett Cooper has left MAGA, admitting that “there was a lot of gaslighting” in President Donald Trump’s base.

In October 2024, weeks before Trump would go on to defeat then-Vice President Kamala Harris for a second term in the White House, Cooper was all smiles in a photo she posted to Instagram, wearing a MAGA hat on horseback. She captioned the photo, “Sry Kamala, this is what joy looks like.”

Two years later, Cooper is disheartened with the movement she so fiercely backed.

“I’m not part of [MAGA] any more,” Cooper, a 25-year-old with 1.7 million YouTube subscribers, told the *Financial Times* in a new interview. “I think I’ve moved on.”

![Conservative influencer Brett Cooper said in a new interview, ‘I’m not part of [Maga] any more,’ as President Donald Trump dodges blame for Republicans’ massive losses in the midterm elections](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/23/24/Screenshot-2026-11-17-at-4-42-46-PM.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Cooper’s rift from the MAGA movement started with the Trump administration’s handling of files related to the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein last year.

Trump begrudgingly signed a bill to release the files after months of resistance, with critics not only questioning the transparency of the administration, but also whether a more sinister cover-up was taking place. The president, who ran in the same social circles as Epstein decades ago, has denied claims of a cover-up, telling reporters, “I have nothing to hide.”

Cooper remembered seeing a social media post from Trump suggesting that those still demanding answers about Epstein’s crimes and associates were no longer part of MAGA.

“Ok, well, I guess I’m not!” she recalled thinking.

![Cooper, like other Republicans, took issue with Trump starting a war in Iran more than eight months ago that caused gas prices to soar — after the president promised no more prolonged military conflicts](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/23/57/GettyImages-2305027666.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

She, like other Republicans, also took issue with Trump starting a war in Iran more than eight months ago that caused gas prices to soar — after the president promised no more prolonged military conflicts. She also didn’t like the administration’s response to economic concerns, as many Americans struggled with stubborn inflation.

“There was a lot of gaslighting that took place,” Cooper said. “It’s just been so belittling.”

Cooper, a former Daily Wire host, now focuses her conservative values on more cultural and entertainment videos rather than politically heavy episodes for her podcast, *The Brett Cooper Show*.

Despite her dissatisfaction with the Trump administration, she still voted Republican in the midterm elections, where Democrats took control of the House and the Senate. Trump has dodged blame for the massive losses.

“It’s just kind of closing the chapter,” she said. “We’re excited to see what’s next.”

![Podcaster Joe Rogan, who endorsed Trump in the 2024 election, admitted after the GOP midterm shellacking, 'They didn’t just lose the podcast bros. They lost most of the country'](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/17/23/45/GettyImages-2271355177.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Podcaster Joe Rogan, who endorsed Trump in the 2024 election, admitted after the GOP midterm shellacking, “They didn’t just lose the podcast bros. They lost most of the country.”

Rogan and other podcasters helped Trump to victory in 2024, but nearly halfway through his term, they are showing some buyer’s remorse.

Since Trump returned to the Oval Office, Rogan has criticized the Iran war, the president’s massive immigration crackdown and the administration’s handling of the Epstein files.

“When you get into a war that no one wants to be in, when you got militarized police in the streets wearing masks and no ID,” Rogan said while reflecting on Republicans’ election losses.
