# DWP state pensioners missing out on £4,300 benefit boost

## Around 900,000 pensioners are estimated to be missing out on the benefit

The Department for Work and Pensions has provided an update on the number of additional claimants for payments that could be worth up to £4,300 extra annually. Official figures suggest that approximately 900,000 people eligible for Pension Credit are not claiming it.

In a recent response, a government minister acknowledged that only 30,000 people have been added to the claimant list over the past 12 months. Those who have reached state pension age with an income below £238 per week for individuals, or £363 per week for couples, may be entitled to Pension Credit worth an average of £4,300 annually, reports the Mirror.

Crucially, Pension Credit serves as a gateway to numerous other benefits including assistance with council tax, fuel bills and a free TV licence for those aged over 75. In a recent parliamentary written question, Labour's Claire Hazelgrove asked Secretary of State for Work and Pensions Pat McFadden: "For the most recent Pension Credit take-up figures, and what steps he is taking to ensure that eligible pensioners don't miss out on their entitlement."

Parliamentary Secretary for the Treasury and Parliamentary Under-Secretary of State for Pensions Torsten Bell responded: "The Government is committed to ensuring that all pensioners, including those in Filton and Bradley Stoke, receive the support to which they are entitled. That is why we have been running the biggest ever Pension Credit take-up campaign across Great Britain to encourage pensioners and their families to check their eligibility and make a claim.

"The campaign includes advertising across television, radio, print and social media, supported by media partnerships and social media influencers. We are also distributing some 660,000 leaflets to selected households across Great Britain, including London, and continue to work closely with partners and Local Authorities to help support the campaign."

"The Guarantee Credit element of the Pension Credit caseload – the main safety net element – has continued to grow recently, with almost 30,000 more households receiving Guarantee Credit in March 2026 than in February 2025."

Personal finance expert Martin Lewis has long championed making it an automatic benefit, rather than one that requires an application. He previously stated: "It is a national tragedy that up to one million pensioners are missing out on this rather substantial entitlement in many cases.

"Many of those pensioners, of course, have been paying into the system for years, and what Pension Credit is meant to do is to top people's income up so that if their state pension and other income isn't enough, they have a reasonable standard of living.

"But for one reason or another, this has been tricky for a long time to get people to claim.

"If you're age 66 or over, check for yourself. If you or a relative may be in this position and they're on a relatively low income and they're a pensioner, have this conversation and suggest that they either get online or make the call, because we've all got to get through the next year, and the next year is going to be incredibly tough with the cost of living. I am very concerned about this winter for everybody, but with pensioners I'm concerned and frustrated because I know this money is out there and there are so many people who desperately need it who aren't claiming, and that's why we have to spread the word."

### How to apply

Pension Credit is a benefit designed for those who have reached State Pension age, providing additional income support. It comprises two elements, with some recipients qualifying for both while many are eligible for just one.

* Guarantee Credit – this forms the core component of Pension Credit, ensuring your weekly income reaches a minimum guaranteed threshold. For 2026/27, this stands at £238 per week for single claimants, and £363.25 per week for couples. Further information on eligibility is available.
* Savings Credit – for those who reached state pension age prior to April 2016, an additional financial boost is on offer if you've made arrangements for your retirement through savings, employment or a private pension.

This provides up to £17.96 per week for single claimants, and £20.10 for couples. To be eligible, your earnings must exceed a threshold of £208.07 if you're single and £329.75 if you're part of a couple.

Approximately 2.3 million people are entitled to Pension Credit, yet an estimated 910,000 fail to claim it – largely because they're unaware they may be eligible.
