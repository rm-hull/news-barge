# Why energy bills could jump by £443 in January as inflation rises again

## Household energy costs are set to soar by more than a quarter to the highest level in four years in January as experts warned a typical bill could jump by more than £400 due to the Iran war.

Experts at Cornwall Insight have predicted Ofgem’s energy price cap will rise by 26 per cent to £2,166 a year on January 1, up £443 on the level between October and December, to hit the highest since 2023.

Ofgem will announce the quarterly price cap level for January to March on November 25.

Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said the Government has taken “immediate action” on energy bills, including the £100 energy payment and the move to temporarily cut 5 per cent VAT from electricity bills until March 31, which reduces costs by about £45 a year.

The Chancellor also announced in his autumn budget that gas standing charges will be scrapped in England, Scotland and Wales.

Ms Fahnbulleh said: “I know that the price of energy bills this winter weighs heavily on struggling families up and down the country.

“Access to energy is an everyday essential and not a luxury.”

“Affordability is my number one priority, and I will continue driving towards cheap, clean power,” she added.

Wholesale energy prices have reduced in recent weeks on hopes of a resolution to the Middle East conflict, but not soon enough to prevent the January bill shock.

Attacks this morning saw Iran widen its attacks on its Gulf neighbours, firing on a US air base in Kuwait and several times at Bahrain in what appeared to be retaliation for American strikes the day before.

It comes as UK inflation rose to its highest level for a year this morning, after being pushed up by petrol and diesel costs and tobacco duty changes.

The rate of Consumer Prices Index (CPI) inflation was 3.6 per cent in October, up from 3.5 per cent in September, the Office for National Statistics (ONS) said.

The reading was in line with predictions from economists and it marks the fourth consecutive monthly increase.

It moves inflation further away from the Bank of England’s 2 per cent target, amid warnings from the Bank earlier this month that inflation could keep rising to a peak of around 5 per cent next year.

Markets are pricing this in, according to northern investment platform AJ Bell, creating a stark divide between winners and losers.

Mortgage borrowers will face another squeeze while cash savers and prospective annuity buyers could benefit from higher rates and bond yields.

Dan Coatsworth, head of markets at AJ Bell, warned that consumers should “brace yourself for what could happen next”.

“The market is pricing in UK interest rates potentially hitting 5 per cent by next Christmas or even as early as the summer. This would be heaven or hell, depending on your personal financial circumstances.

“Only once since the 2008 financial crisis have UK rate setters put the cost of money at more than 5 per cent, with base rate peaking at 5.25 per cent from summer 2023 through until the middle of 2024.

“Rates shooting up again to 5 per cent could have negative implications for those coming off fixed rate mortgages and for investors holding shares in property and retail companies and so-called bond proxies such as utilities.

“On the flipside, it could be good news for cash savers, annuity buyers, and investors holding shares in UK banks.”
