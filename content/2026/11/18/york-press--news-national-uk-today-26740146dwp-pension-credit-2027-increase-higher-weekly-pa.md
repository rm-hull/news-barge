Millions of pensioners are set to receive a boost to their income from April, with the full new State Pension rising by around £490 a year and the basic State Pension increasing by approximately £375.

Pension Credit - paid to the poorest pensioners - is also set to rise, with the Standard Minimum Guarantee increasing by the same 3.9% from April.

The increases were confirmed by the Government as part of its annual benefits uprating, with both the basic and full new State Pension rates going up by 3.9%.

The rise follows the Government's Triple Lock commitment, which means the State Pension increases each year by whichever is highest of inflation, average earnings growth or 2.5%.

## What is the triple lock?

The 3.9% increase is based on growth in average weekly earnings in the year to May-July 2026.

For single pensioners, this will take the weekly amount to £247.30, while couples will see their guarantee rise to £377.40 a week.

The Government said the increase would help protect the incomes of the poorest pensioners.

### **Who is eligible for Pension Credit?**

Pension Credit is a means-tested benefit for people who have reached State Pension age and live in England, Scotland or Wales.

You can qualify if your weekly income is below the set threshold, although you may still be eligible if your income is higher and you have certain additional circumstances, such as a disability, caring responsibilities or housing costs.

**The current income thresholds are:**

* **Single pensioners:** Pension Credit tops up weekly income to £238.
* **Couples:** Joint weekly income is topped up to £363.25.

These are the current rates, rather than the new April 2027 rates.

**If you have a partner**

You must include your partner on your application. You can qualify if both of you have reached State Pension age, or if one of you receives Housing Benefit for people over State Pension age.

A partner means your husband, wife or civil partner if you live together, or someone you live with as a couple.

**What counts as income?**

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) takes account of your income, including:

* State Pension
* Other pensions
* Earnings from employment or self-employment
* Most social security benefits, including Carer's Allowance

Some benefits are not counted, including Attendance Allowance, Personal Independence Payment (PIP), Disability Living Allowance, Child Benefit, Housing Benefit and Winter Fuel Payments.

**What about savings?**

Having savings does not automatically rule you out of Pension Credit.

* Savings and investments of **£10,000 or less** do not affect your entitlement.
* Every £500 above £10,000 is treated as £1 of weekly income.

For example, someone with £11,000 in savings has an additional £2 a week counted as income.

You may still qualify if you have more than £10,000 in savings, but the amount you receive could be reduced.

* If you are entitled to a personal or workplace pension but have not claimed it, the amount you would expect to receive can still count as income.
* The same applies if you have deferred your State Pension – the amount you would have received is taken into account.
* People living in Northern Ireland have a separate Pension Credit application process.

You can check whether you qualify using the Government's Pension Credit information and eligibility guidance.

### How much will the State Pension rise?

The key increases confirmed for April are:

* **Full new State Pension:** up 3.9%, an increase of around £490 a year.
* **Basic State Pension:** up 3.9%, an increase of around £375 a year.
* **Pension Credit Standard Minimum Guarantee – single person:** up 3.9% to £247.30 a week.
* **Pension Credit Standard Minimum Guarantee – couple:** up 3.9% to £377.40 a week.
* **Pension Credit Savings Credit maximum:** up 3.5%.
* **Additional State Pension:** up 3.5%.

## Other pension-related benefits increasing

Not all pension-related payments will rise by the same amount.

Other State Pension and benefit rates covered by the Government's statutory review will increase by 3.5%, in line with the Consumer Prices Index (CPI) inflation rate in the year to September 2026.

This includes Additional State Pension and the maximum amount of Pension Credit Savings Credit.

The 3.5% increase also applies to most working-age benefits, benefits supporting people with additional needs arising from disability, and statutory payments such as Statutory Sick Pay and Statutory Maternity Pay.
