Policing in England and Wales will be restructured into about 200 local commands under plans announced by the Home Secretary.

Each area will be about the size of a unitary authority and led by a named commander, Shabana Mahmood said in a speech to police chiefs on Wednesday.

Those commands will be headed by regional specialist police services, the planned number of which has not been revealed.

Some reports have suggested it could be as few as nine, down from the existing 43 in England and Wales.

Meanwhile, responsibilities including counter-terrorism, some fraud and serious and organised crime, the police air service and roads policing will be managed by a new national body.

![Shabana Mahmood made the announcement in a speech to police chiefs on Wednesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/14/01M7QF9J3Y3TPWTZHNQE8Z45CA.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Earlier this year, the government set out plans to radically overhaul the police service in England and Wales, creating a national police service dubbed the British FBI as well as fewer, larger geographical forces.

Policing is billions of pounds in debt, with the shortfall set to grow by £2.5 billion by 2030, delegates at the annual conference of the National Police Chiefs’ Council and the Association of Police and Crime Commissioners have already been told.

The Home Secretary said root-and-branch reform is needed to make sure there are consistent standards in policing across the country.

The recent incident at RAF Fairford needed every on-duty officer in Gloucestershire, while disorder in Gosport required officers to be drafted in from 13 forces, she said.

Speaking to journalists after her speech, the minister said a timetable for the changes will be set out once the Police Reform Bill has been laid in Parliament in the spring.

“We’re going to go slow and at the right pace because I don’t want to transfer operational responsibilities, for example, to the National Police Service until I know it’s fully ready, because I don’t want to see a drop-off in service.

“Public safety comes first, and I don’t want to do it too quickly, such that you see a drop-off in service for communities.”
