A talented young Harrogate pupil has won the opportunity to represent England at the Commonwealth Karate Championship.

Harrogate High School year 7 student Jack began training at Harrogate Shotokan Karate Club at the age of six, following in the footsteps of his sister, whose passion for the sport inspired him to take up karate.

Since then, he has dedicated countless hours to developing his skills and has competed in numerous competitions, collecting multiple medals in both Kumite (sparring) and Kata (patterns).

Now all of Jack’s hard work has paid off with selection for the JKS England following his outstanding performances in competition earlier this year.

Zoe Price, owner of Harrogate Shotokan Karate Club, paid tribute to the talented youngster.

“Our karate club has been proudly running in Harrogate for more than 50 years and we have been based at the Harrogate High School site throughout this time,” said Zoe.

“It is wonderful to see our students growing not only as karate practitioners but also as individuals.

“Karate offers so much more than just physical fitness; it also encourages teamwork, builds confidence, develops discipline and has a positive impact on mental health and wellbeing.”

The trials took place at Harrogate High School where Jack successfully secured his place on the squad, alongside his sister.

In August this year, just before starting secondary school, Jack achieved another significant milestone by earning his black belt – a fantastic achievement after years of dedication and perseverance.

Jack's parents said: "We are incredibly proud of Jack and his sister for their achievements.

“We hope they keep up the hard work and determination, not just in karate but in all aspects of their lives."
