NO water restrictions are currently planned for Yorkshire - despite drought status still being in place.

Yorkshire Water says reservoirs are at 48 per cent capacity - well below the seasonal average of 79 per cent - following one of the driest summers and autumns on record.

Groundwater in the Hull aquifer is also low at 37 per cent, compared to an average of 52 per cent - something which was boosted by above average rain in October, but has not been helped by a dry November.

Andy Shaw, head of water production, treatment and energy at Yorkshire Water, said: "October’s rainfall provided a welcome boost to help reservoir stocks recover, but levels remain below average for this time of year.

"November has been relatively dry, so our focus is on carefully managing water resources across the region, including maximising permitted river abstractions, moving water around the network and improving the resilience and performance of the network.

“There are currently no plans to introduce water restrictions and we’re working hard to make sure Yorkshire is in a good position for spring and summer 2027. Part of this includes a continued focus on driving down leakage."

He said the company is not planning to introduce restrictions but is working to ensure the region is prepared for spring and summer 2027.

Efforts include reducing leakage, with weekly figures currently 15 million litres lower than this time last year and the completion of 62 projects replacing more than 344km of clean water mains.

Andy added that customers were also advised to look at their habits, such as taking shorter showers, turning off the tap whilst brushing their teeth, and using eco-mode on their appliances.
