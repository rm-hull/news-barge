The Pope has thanked God for his “stamina” as he persevered through his South America tour, mere weeks after a cancer operation.

Pope Leo XIV, 71, spoke after completing a 12-day and 50-event visit to Uruguay, Argentina and Peru.

The pope said he was “doing quite well” despite having part of his right lung removed last month.

“Stamina is one of the gifts the Lord has given me,” he said.

The cancerous lump was found during his annual routine checkup, as he urged people to follow his example.

![The Pope has thanked God for his “stamina” as he persevered through his South America tour](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/2305194858..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“Get your checkups. Prevention is very important. Look after your health. When treatment is needed, try not to be too afraid and go ahead with it. Thank God, with the strength of so many people’s prayers, it went very well in my case, and I’m here to bear witness to that.

Prior to going into surgery, Leo said he saw no need to make provisions for the leadership of the Catholic Church if he were left medically impaired and unable to govern.

He said he hadn’t written any instructions, as his predecessors had done, outlining the criteria under which he would resign, which some legal experts believe is necessary if a pope were to end up in an irreversible coma and unable to articulate his resignation.

“Eventually I will do it, I think, but I don’t see the need now,” Leo said, adding that he never doubted he would be in good enough shape afterward to make the South America trip. He credited good doctors, prayer and what he described as a strong sense of determination to recover.

![The Pope took part in a 12-day and 50-event visit around South America](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/2026-11-18T170416Z_412543658_RC28QOA80JLG_RTRMADP_3_POPE-AUDIENCE.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Pope suffered two blows to the face while on his trip; the first came by a rosary thrown by a member of a crowd as he waved to worshippers in Peru on Monday.

Footage showed the pontiff riding on the Popemobile and flinching as the object made contact with his face.

Pope Leo later joked about the incident, telling a reporter: “The nice thing about Peruvians, including the person who with a lot of faith who simply wanted to offer me a rosary, are things you carry in your heart – or maybe in your eye – but that mark the life of someone.”

The second came after footage shows the pontiff in the back of a vehicle which then slams on its brakes, hurling him into the back of the front seat.
