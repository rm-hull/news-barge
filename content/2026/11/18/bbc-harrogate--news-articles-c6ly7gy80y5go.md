The campaign will promote the wider Yorkshire region to European visitors

**A new initiative aims to help tourism businesses across Yorkshire attract more visitors to "discover somewhere new" by turning short breaks into longer stays.**

A government grant will be used to try to encourage tourists from Europe to visit parts of the area away from the more traditional attractions, said Visit Hull and East Yorkshire (VHEY), which is leading the campaign.

Money will be used to help smaller tourism businesses "prepare their experiences for sale through tour operators".

The project is being supported by all four elected mayors in Yorkshire.

Chris Blacksell, chairman of VHEY, said he hoped the partnership could bring lasting benefits to the region.

"Visitors do not necessarily plan their holidays around destination boundaries," he said.

"They look for experiences that interest them and places they want to explore. By making those connections easier, we can give them more reasons to extend their stay and discover somewhere new."

VHEY said visitor spending in Hull and East Yorkshire reached more than £1bn for the first time in 2024.

However, a recent report said the number of visitors to York between January and June fell by 8% from the same period last year.

Jackie Dad, deputy leader of Hull City Council and vice-chairwoman of VHEY, said the scheme offered an "opportunity to introduce the city to more people as part of a wider Yorkshire experience".

She added: "Connecting our cultural attractions and heritage with other destinations can encourage visitors to include Hull in their plans and spend more time here."

![A twilight view of Hull Marina with boats in the foreground and the quayside and the A63 bridge lit up in the background.](https://ichef.bbci.co.uk/ace/standard/976/cpsprodpb/1a2d/live/56438440-e320-11f1-99a1-253766e7ad8b.jpg)

The initiative aims to encourage more European tourists to visit places across Yorkshire, including Hull

The £160,000 grant comes from the government's Connected Destinations Fund.

VisitEngland chief executive Patricia Yates said: "By encouraging visitors to stay longer and travel further, they will help drive regional growth, support local businesses and spread the economic benefits of tourism more widely."
