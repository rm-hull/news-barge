## Tributes paid to 'lifelong champion of music and education'

*(Image: Family)*

Tributes have been paid to a lifelong champion of music and education who inspired generations across Ryedale.

Patricia Spink, affectionately known as Pat or Mrs Spink, died peacefully at York Hospital last month, aged 94.

Born in Melling, north Lancashire, in 1932, Pat grew up in a close-knit rural community where church life and music were deeply intertwined. Her early love of music saw her sing in the church choir and study both piano and organ.

This passion led her to the Royal Manchester College of Music, now the Royal Northern College of Music, where she earned teaching diplomas in both instruments.

Pat went on to teach music at Sowerby Bridge Grammar School before moving to Thornton-le-Dale Primary School, where she taught the reception class for 19 years. She also taught music across the school, directed the choir, and established recorder and instrumental groups.

Her influence stretched well beyond the classroom. Pat served as organist at Pickering United Reformed Church for many years and taught piano privately to both children and adults. She also conducted the Ryedale Choral Union for two years, leading performances at Lastingham Church.

Even after retiring from school teaching, Pat remained deeply involved in music. In the 1990s, she founded the United Singers and organised numerous concerts, often at Pickering United Reformed Church and other venues.

One of her most ambitious projects was the Millennium Messiah, a performance of Handel’s oratorio at St Peter and St Paul’s Church in Pickering.

Music was a central part of her family life as well, with her children introduced early on to concerts, theatre, and the arts.

Pat also loved the countryside, walking, gardening, and spending time with her family. She was a devoted grandmother and great-grandmother.

A committed Christian, she believed deeply in serving others and expressed her faith through teaching, music, and community involvement.

Although worsening hearing eventually limited her own music-making, her passion for teaching and encouraging others never waned.

Even in her final months, she continued to offer advice and encouragement about singing.

Her family said; "Pat will be remembered not only for her considerable musical accomplishments, but for the patience, kindness, humour and generosity with which she shared her talents.

"Her legacy lives on in the many pupils she taught, the musicians she encouraged, the concerts she organised and the countless people whose lives were enriched by her work."

Pat is survived by her husband Howard, their three children—Kathryn, Jonathan, and Charlotte—four grandchildren, and two great-grandchildren.
