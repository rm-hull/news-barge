Gebhard “Geb” Galle, a Pearl Harbor survivor who rescued four shipmates from a sinking ship during the Second World War, has died at his home in Moses Lake, Washington.

Galle was 105, according to the Pacific Historic Parks, an organization that helps preserve Pearl Harbor’s history.

His death on Saturday came on the same day as that of Freeman Johnson, of Centerville, Massachusetts, the oldest known Pearl Harbor survivor, who was 106. Both men were among the estimated 87,000 military personnel on Oahu on Dec. 7, 1941.

The roll of living veterans of the attack had dwindled to roughly a dozen names after Ira “Ike” Schab, who was 105, died in December 2025.

“Your humility, quick thinking and selfless actions remain a shining example of Navy valor,” then-Acting Chief of Naval Operations Adm. James Kilby wrote in a letter to Galle for his 104th birthday in 2025.

Galle was born in Germany and came to North Dakota with his family in 1928, at the age of 7. He joined the U.S. Navy in 1939, when he was 18.

![Gebhard “Geb” Galle, one of the few remaining Pearl Harbor survivors who later saved four shipmates from a sinking vessel in battle, has died](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/Obit_Gebhard_Galle_84989.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

On the morning of Dec. 7, 1941, Galle, then 20, was a machinist's mate aboard the USS Nevada, waiting below deck to be taken ashore for church services. Planes approaching the ship were visible through a porthole he looked out of.

Galle went down to his battle station two decks below, in the engineering section. A torpedo struck the Nevada and let water into the ship, but officers were able to get it moving, the Navy said.

The vessel came under attack again, suffering more leaks in the hull as well as fires, and ran aground. From where he was, Galle could not see what was happening.

“Of course, we felt a couple jolts, and that’s when we got hit with the torpedo, and we had a few bomb hits,” he said in 2020. “After we were beached and everything, and we got up to topside, then we saw all the mess that took place,” including the capsized USS Oklahoma and the partially submerged USS Arizona, he said.

“A pretty sad lookout,” Galle added.

After the Pearl Harbor attack, Galle was reassigned to the USS Northampton. That ship came under fire from Japanese torpedoes on Nov. 30, 1942, during the Battle of Tassafaronga.

Galle told the *Columbia Basin Herald*, a newspaper based in Moses Lake, that he was ordered to close the hatch behind four sailors who would've been trapped when the ship was sinking. The officer left, but Galle defied the order and opened the hatch, closing and securing it only after the sailors had escaped.

![Freeman K. Johnson, a 106-year-old Pearl Harbor survivor, died on the same day as Galle](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/16/14/US_Freeman_Johnson_95616.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Northampton sank, and Galle and other sailors spent more than a day adrift in life rafts before being rescued.

“From the decks of the USS Nevada to the battles of the Pacific aboard the USS Northampton and USS Mobile, your courage under fire and steadfast dedication were extraordinary,” Kilby wrote in his letter to Galle. “You helped save shipmates from a sinking vessel, witnessed the launch of the Doolittle Raid, and fought in 19 of 20 Pacific naval engagements.”

In December 2016, Galle attended a 75th anniversary service at Pearl Harbor held to honor and commemorate the USS Nevada and those who served on it.

A total of 76 sailors and Marines lost their lives as a result of that attack, the Navy said.

“You can carry your hatred all of your life,” Galle said of the attack at the event. “You have to remember, they had a leader who led them astray. We’re at peace now and I feel comfortable. I have no qualm. We’re all good people.”

After the war, Galle married and opened a refrigeration and air-conditioning business in North Dakota. He went on to serve as a civil engineer with the Air Force and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, retiring in the 1980s.
