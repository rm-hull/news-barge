US president Donald Trump has met with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China ahead of their unprecedented three-way summit on Thursday.

China’s presidentpersonally welcomed Trump upon his arrival at an airport in Shenzhen on Wednesday, an unusual honour for a visiting foreign leader. He said China had a “tradition of reciprocating courtesy” after greeting the US commander-in-chief with a rare hug following a similar reception at Joint Base Andrews in September. The pair later welcomed the Russian president with handshakes, beaming smiles and a group photo ahead of an evening banquet.

![Trump and Putin shake hands in Shenzhen as Xi looks on](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/12/download.-2..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

According to China’s Xinhua news agency, Trump said every meeting with his Chinese counterpart had been “very pleasant”.

The three men will meet on the sidelines on Thursday and potentially discuss Russia's war with Ukraine and the US war in Iran, as well as other flashpoints such as trade, AI and nuclear weapons.

Ahead of the high-stakes trilateral, Xi took the opportunity speak out against trade protectionism, while Putin lashed out at the growing pile of Western sanctions against Moscow.

The Russian president, who took the stage after Xi, described Western powers as “former hegemons”, claiming Europe was in “humiliating decline”. It is the latest attack by the Kremlin, which has repeatedly sought to test the Nato alliance in recent months.

The three-way gathering has drawn comparisons with the 1945 Yalta Conference staged by the US, Britain and the Soviet Union that formalised spheres of influence, set the stage for the post-Second World War order and became shorthand for great-power deals imposed on smaller countries.

![Trump is embraced by Xi on his arrival](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/2305160371..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

But it has also unnerved leaders of smaller and medium economies, especially in Asia and Europe, who fear the three world powers will disregard or undermine their interests.

The warm reception for Putin will be of particular concern for Ukraine, as Kyiv urged Trump and Xi to put pressure on Putin to end the war rather than trying to appease him.

“It is very important that through this meeting the two leaders do not give Putin the false impression that we are returning to a period of divided spheres of influence. The tone needs to be different – pressure,” the Ukrainian foreign minister Andriy Sybiha told reporters on Wednesday.

Since taking office in January 2025, the US president has deepened ties between Washington and Moscow, while repeatedly castigating Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky – once even blaming him for starting the war, which began with Russia’s invasion nearly five years ago.

On the eve of the summit and sidelined by the trilateral, Ukraine struck a symbolic blow to the Kremlin with two strikes on Putin’s prized Crimea Bridge this week.

![AI, nuclear weapons and global conflicts are expected to be on the agenda](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/13/2305175638..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

With peace talks stalled, the back and forth strikes between Moscow and Kyiv have continued unabated, with four people killed in an overnight Russian attack on the port city of Odesa.

The summit also comes against the backdrop of war against Iran, an offensive launched by the US and Israel on 28 February that has sent shockwaves through the global economy.

Both Moscow and Beijing have criticised the Iran war, with Xi repeatedly calling for countries to avoid a return to “the law of the jungle”, in a thinly-veiled dig towards Trump’s approach. The war is set to be discussed, with the Strait of Hormuz still all but closed nearly nine months after the war was launched.

Winding down nearly two years of trade warfare, another defining characteristic of the US-China relationship during Trump’s second term, will also be on the table.

![Donald Trump is expected to meet Xi Jinping along with Vladimir Putin on Thursday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/09/2305162249..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In his opening address, the Chinese president called for “free and open trade”, a direct rebuke of Trump’s protectionist and aggressive policy of trade warfare during his second administration.

The US president imposed sweeping tariffs soon after taking office, prompting a tariff war between Washington and Beijing which saw the US impose duties as high as 145 per cent on Chinese goods.

Xi’s remarks came after Washington announced restrictions on China’s access to advanced AI chips from companies including Nvidia. The Chinese premier has sought to position Beijing’s trade policies in contrast to Trump’s tariffs and the US administration’s adoption of a muscular foreign policy.

The trade war has since settled, with the sides agreeing to produce a trade deal next year and agreeing a truce on tariffs.

![The Russian leader was all smiles as dignitaries greeted one another in China](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/12/download.-4..jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“We must firmly uphold the rules-based multilateral trading system with the World Trade Organization at its core, support free and open trade and investment, maintain the stability and smooth operation of global industrial and supply chains,” the Chinese president told an audience of business executives.

Chastising the West for the sanctions imposed on Russia following the invasion of Ukraine, Putin added: “Experience has shown that the traditional tools of economic dominance and de facto coercion have largely run their course, having lost much of their intimidating ​power."

“While sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions may still exert a certain deterrent effect, they are incapable of halting growth—let alone crushing the economies of competitors.”

He also praised close partnership with China, pledging more measures for a “multipolar world”.

![The world leaders are expected to discuss war on Ukraine, along with AI and trade](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/10/2305681247..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Kremlin this week dismissed comparisons with the Yalta Conference as “games of political scientists”, but said the meeting will have a major impact on global affairs. Beijing said the trilateral will promote world peace.

Putin described the world as "turbulent" but said disagreements can be addressed by “sitting at the table”.

“I therefore expect big potential benefit from the upcoming trilateral meeting,” he said.

Trump's trip to Asia is his first since his Republican Party's defeat in midterm elections, while allegations involving Nvidia founder Jensen Huang and a suspected Chinese spy have added to the tension.
