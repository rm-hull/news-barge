Israel has postponed issuing contracts for the construction of a large settlement on the West Bank seen as likely to render a two-state solution unviable, days after the UK imposed sanctions against building firms operating in the area.

Israeli diplomats insisted the move was not in response to the measures from Downing Street. Gali Baharav-Miara, the attorney general, said any decision on settlement expansion should not be taken by the caretaker government led by Benjamin Netanyahu but by whatever new administration was formed.

She ordered the Israel land authority and ministry of housing not to open the tender process. Israeli diplomats suggested the UK foreign secretary, Ed Miliband, had acted prematurely on Monday in sanctioning the eight construction firms. But UK diplomats believe the growing political pressure on Israel over settlements, including from US Democrats, is having an impact.

The deferral puts the E1 settlement expansion in the mix for any coalition negotiations. The leader of the Change bloc, Gadi Eisenkot, seen as Israel’s most likely next prime minister, has been clear in opposing violent settler activity, as has the newly elected Knesset speaker, Matan Kahana, an ally of Eisenkot.

Eisenkot has also opposed the E1 settlement expansion, describing it as a mistake likely to alienate international opinion. He said of the plan: “I recall that Netanyahu himself opposed this for a long time, and due to coalition pressure, he gave in and approved it.”

The EI settlement expansion, due to have been announced on Thursday, has long been seen as qualitatively different since the land – between Jerusalem and Ma’aleh Adumim, a West Bank settlement city of nearly 40,000 residents – bisects the West Bank and cuts it off from East Jerusalem.

Miliband has said it would mark the death knell of a future Palestinian state. The UK, along with a group of European countries, has recently recognised Palestine, and the preservation of a two-state solution has been described as the north star of Labour policy towards the region.

The construction tender was published for more than 3,401 housing units in the area. At the beginning of November, the Israel Defense Forces began demolishing homes in a Bedouin community within the E1 area.

Israel has already enforced the downgrading of the UK consulate in East Jerusalem as a reprisal for Miliband’s decision in September to try to end trade links with the illegal settlements.

Bennett said: “The British Labour government’s hostility, bias and one-sided approach are particularly glaring given its failure to take any action against the Palestinian Authority, which continues to encourage terrorism through financial payments, promote incitement in its education system and engage in illegal construction in violation of signed agreements.”

Although the Israeli election was largely fought on domestic security-related issues, Eisenkot seems to be more sensitive to the implications of Israel’s crumbling global reputation, and the need to make efforts to be seen to be listening to the criticism.

If he becomes prime minister, Eisenkot will, indirectly at least, also have to take account of the views of the Arab parties even if these parties are not going to become members of the government.

The group Peace Now welcomed the attorney general’s decision: “A government that lost the elections is not permitted to exploit its final days in power to advance destructive moves that will endanger Israel’s future and its international standing.

Construction in E1 is liable to eliminate the possibility of a two-state solution and drag Israel into a severe diplomatic crisis and international sanctions. The AG’s decision is an important step, but the struggle is not over yet.”
