Around a third of all Berlin planning areas are now officially designated as heat islands. Many of these are located in densely populated areas within the S-Bahn ring, in addition to large housing estates such as those in Marzahn-Hellersdorf, Lichtenberg, and Spandau. The selection is based on the Environmental Justice Atlas 2021/2022.

The Senate Chancellery announced on Tuesday that the priority is to create new trees and green spaces in these heat zones to cool them down by two degrees Celsius in the long term. However, the exact timing and locations are still undecided.

The work will begin in low-socioeconomic-status neighbourhoods. “Because those who, for example, cannot afford air conditioning or do not have a garden are more exposed to the heat in their immediate surroundings,” explained Senator for Climate Protection Ute Bonde (CDU), according to a press release. Therefore, these neighbourhoods should be the first to benefit from the measures. According to the Climate Adaptation Act, these measures include, among other things, new street trees, green spaces with shade-providing plants, and the targeted use of rainwater.

However, the specific implementation is still unclear; it has not yet been included in the budget, and there is also a lack of additional staff. Criticism is therefore coming from, among others, the Initiative Baumentscheid (tree initiative), which initiated Berlin’s climate adaptation law.
