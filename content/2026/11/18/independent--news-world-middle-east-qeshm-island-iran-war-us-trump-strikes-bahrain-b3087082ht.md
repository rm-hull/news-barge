The US has carried out further strikes on Iran’s Qeshm ‌Island, despite Donald Trump’s claim that the war is “effectively over”.

A fighter ⁠aircraft ‌targeted a missile launch site ‌on the island- situated along the Strait of Hormuz- after Iran previously struck American personnel and assets in Bahrain, Reuters reported, citing US officials.

The move was described by a global security expert as an attempt by the US to “degrade Iran’s capacity to threaten maritime traffic”.

Tehran on Wednesday retaliated to US strikes on Qeshm by launching air attacks on Bahrain and Kuwait, the defence ministries of the two Arab nations said.

It was Iran’s third strike on American military targets in Bahrain in five days, as tensions in the region continue to rise after more than nine months of confict.

## What is Qeshm Island?

Qeshm is Iran’s largest island and the largest in the Gulf, covering approximately 1,500 square kilometres. It stretches along the northern Strait of Hormuz, which connects the Gulf with the Gulf of Oman.

The island has 148,000 residents, primarily Sunni Muslims who speak the Bandari dialect.

In peacetime, the island is a tourist destination dotted with salt caves and the remnants of fortifications installed by European empires.

The Royal Indian Navy operated out of the island until 1863, and the last coaling station for the navy was abandoned in 1935 at the request of the Shah of Iran.

Since then, Iran has reconstituted the island with missiles, drones and fast-attack boats, giving it strategic importance for control of the Strait of Hormuz.

## Why does the island matter?

The island’s geographical location makes it one of the most vital pieces of territory for Tehran’s ability to control transit in the Strait of Hormuz.

Situated around 22 kilometres south of Bandar Abbas - Iran’s primary southern maritime, economic, and military gateway - and near the western and central approaches to the Strait of Hormuz, Qeshm gives Iran a critical viewpoint to track activity in the surrounding waters, particularly as it sits opposite the UAE, Omar and shipping routes serving the Gulf.

At the height of the US-Iran war, Qeshm was briefed as a possible target for a US ground offensive as Washington looked at ways to break Tehran’s hold on the Strait of Hormuz.

Exact details are kept confidential, but retired Lebanese Brigadier-General Hassan Jouni, a military and strategic expert, told Al Jazeera previously that the island has the ability to strike from an underground “missile city”.

Arman Mahmoudian, research fellow at the Global & National Security Institute at the University gf South Florida, told *The Independent*: “The recent American strikes against military facilities on Qeshm illustrate the island’s importance in the ongoing confrontation. Washington’s apparent objective is to degrade Iran’s capacity to threaten maritime traffic and American military assets.

“However, damaging military installations on Qeshm would not necessarily eliminate Iran’s ability to disrupt shipping, given the geographical dispersion of Iranian military capabilities along the coastline and across neighboring islands.

“Ultimately, Qeshm’s strategic significance lies not simply in Iran’s ability to control a particular piece of territory, but in its ability to use that territory to influence the security and economic calculations of other countries.

“For Tehran, Qeshm is not merely an island guarding the Strait of Hormuz; it is a strategic instrument through which Iran can transform its geographical position into military deterrence, economic leverage, and influence over the Persian Gulf.”

According to Can Kasapoğlu, a defence analyst, satellite imagery suggests Iran has installed a “significant portion of its anti-ship missiles in underground launch positions on Qeshm”.

He wrote for the Hudson Institute, a conservative think tank based in Washington, that “any US campaign in the region would likely centre on two decisive islands: Kharg and Qeshm”.

![The island is located around 22 kilometres south of Bandar Abbas and near the western and central approaches to the Strait of Hormuz](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/12/2276222358..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Rashid Al-Mohanadi, vice-president of Centre of International Policy Research and an expert in Gulf security, told *The Telegraph* that “Qeshm is likely to have the whole shebang” prepared for a possible invasion.

“Of course the island would have anti-ship capabilities, the coast is likely mined, the beaches booby-trapped and so on,” he said, adding that an additional threat would come from Iran’s ability to launch strikes from the mainland.

## What strikes has US launched on the island?

Overnight on Wednesday, a fighter ⁠aircraft ‌targeted a missile launch site ‌on Qeshm ‌Island.

The attack came after Iran previously struck American personnel and assets in Bahrain.

In June this year, the US military attacked a ground control tower on Qeshm in “self-defence”, claiming Iran had tried and failed to attack Kuwait and Bahrain with missiles.
