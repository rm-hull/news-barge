The deputy leader of North Yorkshire Council has warned that the county could suffer if proposals to create larger regional police forces result in the loss of North Yorkshire Police.

Conservative councillor Mark Crane has expressed concerns about the prospect of a major shake-up of policing in England, which could see existing forces merged into larger organisations.

His comments echo those of York and North Yorkshire Mayor David Skaith, who has warned that the creation of so-called “mega forces” could threaten neighbourhood policing, particularly in rural communities.

Speaking at today’s meeting of North Yorkshire Council’s executive committee, Cllr Crane acknowledged that agreeing with the Labour mayor was an unusual position for him.

He said:

> “I, and I’m sure many of my colleagues, are concerned that there appears to be a move nationally to restructure police forces.
>
> “I think North Yorkshire would suffer if that were to happen, and I sincerely hope we don’t see the loss of North Yorkshire Police.
>
> “On this issue, I’m in agreement with the mayor, which may be a first.”

The comments come amid a national debate about the future structure of policing, following calls from Metropolitan Police Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley for a smaller number of much larger regional forces.

Earlier this month, Mayor Skaith argued that any reforms should retain local accountability and use existing mayoral authority boundaries rather than create larger police organisations covering multiple regions.

He warned that merging forces could lead to resources being diverted towards major cities at the expense of smaller towns and villages.

The mayor said:

> “‘Mega forces’ are a threat to neighbourhood policing in York and North Yorkshire, especially in our rural communities.
>
> “They would inevitably see resources diverted to large cities, leaving towns and villages at risk of becoming hotspots for crime.
>
> “That is unfair to North Yorkshire taxpayers, who provide almost half of the funding for policing.”

Mr Skaith said he supported reform of policing, including the creation of a strengthened national police service to tackle serious and cross-border crime.
