# Scarborough Open Air Theatre made a loss for council owner 'in most successful season ever'

## A THEATRE hailed for having its most successful season in its history ran up deficits for its council owner in each of the last three financial years – totalling over £500,000.

Scarborough Open Air Theatre attracted more than 175,000 visitors and generated around £19 million for the local economy.

However figures supplied by North Yorkshire Council to What's On in Scarborough reveal that the theatre ran up a combined deficit of £543,723 between 2023 and 2026. The council made a loss of £204,311 in 2023/24, £103,880 in 2024/25 and £235,532 in 2025/26. Expenditure also increased from £756,005 in 2023/24 to £864,365 in 2025/26.

It comes as a number of acts for 2027 have already been announced including Simply Red, Kings of Leon and Mariah Carey. Multi-platinum-selling artist James Blunt will also be performing in Scarborough on June 20.

People have been complaining about the price of tickets, including standing tickets for Maria Carey costing £115.

Last year’s losses were despite the theatre selling 175,000 tickets for 28 gigs. Even if they all sold at the lowest price of around £55, that would still generate £9.6m.

Live music programming and concert promotions are handled by Cuffe & Taylor, part of US multinational entertainment company Live Nation Entertainment, which was founded in 2010 following the merger of Live Nation and Ticketmaster.

NYC has another two years to run on a contract with Live Nation Music, set up by its predecessor Scarborough Borough Council.

The council gets a venue rental fee for every show, which contributes towards costs including security, health and safety, medical provision, portable toilets, and sound monitoring.

Around 100 security are needed for each event. As part of the contractual agreement, the council also receives a profit share.

Cabinet member Councillor Simon Myers said the contract would be coming up for renegotiation and he “would be very keen that it does not continue to require any subsidy from council taxpayers”.

He said: “It was a deal that was done before local government reorganisation. It was done when the theatre was a lot less successful. If it was now you’d probably want a different deal. It is worth noting that we think we will be in surplus this year.

"It has moved from being an instrument aimed at regeneration and like a lot of these things it has cost the council money. It has moved from that to being a very successful popular venue. If the council could spend a couple of hundred thousand pounds a year in every town and generate £19m for the local economy, it would be a very good use of taxpayers’ money.”

NYC stressed the important role the theatre plays in supporting local jobs, businesses and the wider visitor economy and say they expect it to be in the black this year.

Assistant director for culture and leisure, Jo Ireland, said: “While the theatre did return a deficit between 2023 and 2025, we do expect it to show a surplus this year thanks to a significant increase in the number of shows and ticket sales.”
