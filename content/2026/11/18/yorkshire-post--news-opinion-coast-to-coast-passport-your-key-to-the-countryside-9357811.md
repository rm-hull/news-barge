# Coast to Coast Passport: your key to the countryside

![Coast to coast: photograph used for illustrative purposes only (Adobe Stock)](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjdlMjY4ZjUyLTYxMDItNDBlMi05YTMxLWE0YWExYmYwNjA0NDo0OTYyZjUzOS05ZDYyLTQ4YjMtYWMzYS02MGFjZGM4YThjY2E=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=990&quality=70&enable=upscale)


## Nobody can accuse one volunteer in particular of not being able to walk the walk when it comes to contributing positively to people’s health and sense of wellbeing.

Donald Cline, the man behind the celebrated Coast-to-Coast Passport, should be lauded for his idea to connect ramblers to businesses along the 190-mile trail by creating a stamp collecting rewards system that brings out the inner philatelist in all of us.

Connecting St Bees on the Cumbrian west coast to our very own Robin Hood’s Bay on the east, this National Trail is proving quite the attraction, with visitors to it rising rapidly, perhaps encouraged by the fair-weather summer just gone.

Sales of the passport have risen by an impressive 60 per cent, prompting Mr Cline to happily hand over the reins to a dedicated leader who will oversee the continued success of the route.

The scheme encourages walkers to pop into local businesses, often who are more than glad of a few pounds being spend on their wares. Heaven knows turning a profit in these straitened times is difficult enough.

Clearly that is recognised, too, by the 16 North Yorkshire councillors who have given funds from their own locality budgets in order to support the production of even more passports in the hope of reaching more people.

But perhaps the most profitable thing to come out of the initiative is the way this great northern trail lures people out into the fresh, countryside air where the splendour of nature can be enjoyed to the benefit of mind, body and soul.
