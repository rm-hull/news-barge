# Consumer AI agents promises unprecedented convenience - but require care: Nicholas Rossman

## AI agents designed not only to talk, but to operate on a consumer’s behalf, are being marketed heavily. Unlike the customer service bots we’re used to, these new agents act as representatives, navigating the digital economy to complete complex, multi-step tasks like browsing websites, booking a family holiday or managing household utility bills.

The prevalence of these agents is accelerating at an extraordinary pace. The market is currently driven by a dynamic mix of established technology conglomerates and innovative third-party developers. Valued at roughly $10.9 billion in 2026, the global AI agents market is projected to expand at a staggering compound annual growth rate of nearly 50 per cent.

Industry forecasts suggest the market could eclipse $180 billion by the early 2030s.

While the benefits of having a tireless digital proxy are clear, the architectural mechanics of these commercial transactions introduce significant complexities. The core operational challenge lies in the concept of delegated authority.

![Nicholas Rossman from the Mobile Ecosystem Forum.](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/webimg/b25lY21zOjg2ZjYzMDJmLTRiYjAtNDcxMS1hY2Q5LTZiYjJhOGZjZjA1YTo1Y2NhODc5ZS1lM2NiLTQyMzgtOTQxNC0zMGUyZTNjZmQxNmY=.jpg?crop=3:2,smart&trim=&width=640&quality=65)


When a consumer tasks an agent with purchasing a flight or switching a bank account, they are effectively granting that software the authority to act in their name and spend their money.

This delegation cannot rely on a simple username and password exchange. Instead, it requires verifiable delegation credentials

This brings us to the most critical challenge facing the widespread adoption of consumer agents. When a merchant, such as a bank or an airline, receives a digital request from an agent, they are faced with a complex dual dilemma. They need to authenticate the specific agent to prove its identity, and they must also verify its authorisation, to be absolutely sure that it has the legitimate right to spend the consumer’s funds.

The stakes are serious. A failure in authentication could result in severe financial liabilities and regulatory penalties regarding data protection for the merchant. If a malicious actor intercepts a poorly secured agent workflow, they could potentially extract personal data or initiate fraudulent transactions. Merchants need, above all, a trustworthy framework to evaluate an agent’s identity, its eligibility to act, and the exact limits of its delegated power at every step of the workflow.

Solving these security and authentication challenges is not merely a technical necessity, it is also a regulatory imperative. If the infrastructure for validating AI agents is monopolised by a few massive tech platforms, the market risks becoming a closed ecosystem. In such a scenario, merchants might only accept credentials from the largest providers, effectively locking out innovative third-party developers who build niche, highly effective agents.

Enterprises, regulators, mobile network operators, firewall providers, and anti-fraud solution innovators must join forces to tackle fraud threats head-on. Threats such as smishing, phishing, AI-driven scams, account takeovers, artificially inflated traffic, and trust issues in the verification of AI agents.

The era of the consumer AI agent promises unprecedented convenience, shifting the burden of mundane administrative and commercial tasks from the human to the machine. As the market scales toward its multi-billion dollar potential, the technology will undoubtedly mature.

However, this all depends on consumers trusting their agents to operate strictly within their delegated boundaries, and merchants trusting that the agents interacting with their systems are genuine, legitimate representatives of the specific consumer.

Only with secure, standardised protocols for authentication and promoting a regulatory environment that supports third-party innovation, will the digital economy be able to safely continue to develop and provide the powerful, even life changing benefits that autonomous consumer AI agents offer.

Nicholas Rossman is Programme Director at the Mobile Ecosystem Forum trade body
