Jurors who found a man guilty of murdering a pub owner by punching him on the dancefloor of a Staffordshire bar were not told the defendant was a member of activist group Raise the Colours.

Billy Allison, 36, who has been described as one of the founding members of the group, which puts St George’s Cross and Union flags on lampposts, was convicted at Stafford Crown Court on Wednesday of murdering publican Matthew O’Gara in a “completely unprovoked and senseless attack” at Ricco and Loren in Lichfield on May 24 this year.

Jurors deliberated for around four hours before finding Allison, of Cropthorne Road in Shirley, Solihull, West Midlands, guilty of murder and of causing grievous bodily harm with intent after he also punched Mr O’Gara’s friend Adam Cooper, 62, who suffered a fractured eye socket and nose.

Allison delivered what prosecutors said was a “very, very hard” punch with the “stance and skill of an experienced fighter” to Mr O’Gara, 44, which left him unconscious until he died in hospital two days later, on May 26.

The trial heard Allison had been at the bar with fellow Raise the Colours members Ryan Bridge and Ben Cullen and their partners drinking spirits for hours before the attack.

The members of the group had been invited to Lichfield by the owner of Ricco and Loren, who arranged their accommodation so they could stay the night in the city.

![Raise the Colours activist Billy Allison](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/12/01M7QQW9WWWB8F4TQ26MYC2HVQ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Bridge and Cullen have faced their own legal troubles, with the flagging group’s leader Bridge appearing at Oxford Magistrates’ Court in early November to deny 13 offences including racially aggravated intentional harassment, alarm or distress, using threatening words or behaviour likely to cause harassment, alarm or distress and common assault.

Cullen is expected to face a trial accused of downloading indecent images of children.

Both Bridge and Cullen were mentioned by name during Allison’s murder trial, although their links to Raise the Colours were not shared with the jury.

During his cross-examination of Allison during the trial, prosecution counsel Timothy Hannam KC accused the defendant of hatching a plan with Cullen to avoid detection after the fatal attack, leaving the bar with him and swapping shirts as they walked away from the scene and then said Cullen “harboured” the defendant in his hotel room, where police later arrested him.

Mr Hannam also sought to suggest that a handshake between victim Mr O’Gara and Bridge on the dancefloor at Ricco and Loren was “unwelcome”, as Bridge appeared on CCTV to roll his eyes after the interaction.

Mr Hannam also asked about exchanges Allison and Bridge appeared to have seconds before the attack, but the defendant said he could not recall what they were talking about.

![Publican Matthew O'Gara was killed by a punch from Billy Allison](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/01M7QR25W49C5M82F42QE50X42.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

During the trial, Allison described Bridge as a friend, but said Cullen was an “acquaintance” whom he knew through Bridge.

Campaign group Hope Not Hate has described Allison as a “sort of wingman to Bridge” who has been a “significant part of the group’s intimidating presence”.

Both Allison and Bridge have appeared together in several videos on Raise the Colours social media channels in the past year, with Allison appearing to have been present while flags were put up in Birmingham and other parts of the West Midlands.

He was also pictured outside Warwick Crown Court in March as protesters tried to put up banners at the courthouse where asylum seeker Ahmad Mulakhil, who abducted, raped and sexually assaulted a 12-year-old girl in Nuneaton, was sentenced to 15 years in prison.

Allison, wearing a black Raise the Colours-branded hoodie and baseball cap, could be seen in front of a large banner reading “Save our women and children” as he smiled and held his phone up towards police officers at the scene.

His last public post on his own Facebook page is a picture advertising the sale of Raise the Colours-branded hoodies, on which he replied to comments from those interested in buying them, asking them to private message him.

The defendant also appeared in Raise the Colours social media videos in December 2025 when Bridge and others, including anti-migrant activist Danny Tommo, real name Daniel Thomas, who is himself facing criminal proceedings after he admitted slashing a migrant dinghy with a knife in the English Channel, went to Calais in France.

The trip to Calais was dubbed “Operation Overlord” by the group, with Hope Not Hate claiming they “harassed asylum seekers and the charity workers assisting them”.

Allison will be sentenced next year.
