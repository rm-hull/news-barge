If I can pinpoint one disappointing aspect of GTA 6 that most will agree on, it's the fact that it runs at 30 fps.

Many of us remember when this was normal for consoles (especially the PS3/Xbox 360 era), but these days, it's unusual for a major game to not offer a 60 fps or 40 fps performance option. Unfortunately, even if you're rocking a PS5 Pro, GTA 6 moves at a filmic 30.

That's disappointing news for anyone who shelled out $900+ for the more powerful PS5 Pro expecting better performance (or vindicating news, if you're a PC Gamer reader waiting for the arbitrarily delayed PC version), but Rockstar insists there was no way around it.

Speaking to Kotaku, technology production director Marc Chambers explained that the current consoles just aren't powerful enough to keep up without significant compromises.

"Our base presentation makes use of ray-traced reflections and global illumination. On the PS5 Pro, this is enhanced with the addition of ray-traced shadows, as well as overall added clarity through the use of PlayStation Spectral Super Resolution (PSSR)," he said. "The PS5 Pro features an upgraded graphics processor and upscaling technology that we leverage to increase the game’s visual fidelity. While the PS5 Pro’s central processor is slightly better than the PS5 base console, it is not powerful enough to run the game’s complex simulation at higher frame rates."

![Grand Theft Auto 6: PS5 vs PS5 Pro Performance Tested! - YouTube](https://img.youtube.com/vi/Nmn2ibJAOhc/maxresdefault.jpg)

Head of development Rob Nelson framed the decision to skip performance mode in much simpler terms to IGN:

"It wouldn’t have been acceptable to anybody. The simple answer is, when you ask the technical team, they’re, like, ‘Have you seen it?’ There’s too many compromises that would’ve had to have been made to do that, is the short answer," he said. "Too much, too much."

While I don't actually have a problem playing a slow-paced game like GTA 6 at half the usual framerate so long as it's consistent and smooth, I can't deny that these quotes left me cracking a smile in the direction of my PC.

The world of Leonida does look unbelievably impressive at times—both in terms of pure graphical oomph and the density of vehicles, NPCs, and reactive foliage—but a great PC is going to demolish it. I've been searching for a game that will make this RTX 5090 actually flinch, and honestly, I'm doubtful even GTA 6 will be it. Barring a suite of PC-only enhancements that raise its requirements by a lot, I can't imagine a game that targets an Xbox Series S will have trouble blowing past 60 fps on top PC hardware.

This is putting into perspective for me that, while I wasn't looking, the current consoles got old. That's fine, to be clear—we're in the middle of a tech pause that has me happily playing older games anyway—but it's a wasted opportunity that GTA 6 is debuting exclusively on mid hardware.

We have to wait an unspecified amount of time to get what is destined to be the best version of this generational game. As Chris said in his writeup after 100 hours with GTA 6, "It's (mostly) great. But it will be (mostly) so much greater when it comes to PC."
