Donald Cline came up with the idea of a coast to coast passport in 2024 and it was launched last year

**Almost a third of hikers on a famous coast to coast walk between Cumbria and North Yorkshire now take part in a scheme encouraging them to spend money in communities along the route, its organisers have said.**

The "passport" scheme on the 190-mile (305km) walk, from St Bees Head to Robin Hood's Bay, was launched by Richmond Yorkshire Community Interest Company (CIC) in May 2025.

It encourages participants to collect stamps in their passports by using shops, pubs and visitor attractions along the route.

CIC director Donald Cline, whose idea it was, said the scheme had "grown beyond anything we imagined", with sales of the passports rising by 60% in 2026 compared to 2025.

According to Cline, an estimated 25-30% of walkers on the route now take part in the scheme, according to the Local Democracy Reporting Service.

Cline said the CIC was looking to recruit a paid co-ordinator to take over leadership of the initiative.

"The volunteers will continue to help, but we want someone to be able to be responsible for developing this," he said.

"The idea is that it'll help to improve the experience on the trail."

The scheme was launched with financial backing from North Yorkshire Council, Cumberland Council, Westmorland and Furness Council, Natural England and the Yorkshire Dales and North York Moors national park authorities.

Stacy Wrobel (R) runs a café on the route in Shap, Cumbria, with her husband Irek

The passport booklet details locations in towns and villages where participants can collect stamps along the walking route, which was originally devised in 1973 by walker and author Alfred Wainwright.

In Shap, Cumbria, they are handed out at the Birchwood Cafe, which opened two years ago.

Stacy Wrobel, who runs the cafe with her husband Irek, said they had not seen a "huge impact" on footfall from the scheme as they were "quite a new business".

But she said the passport was a "really nice thing for people to have".

"They get to venture into local establishments that maybe they wouldn't have gone into," Wrobel explained.

"They may have just walked past heading for their bed-and-breakfast or wherever they were staying."

Wrobel said coast to coast walkers provided a "massive boost" to her business, and that visitors had come from as far away as Australia, the USA and New Zealand.

"After the [October] half-term you get a definite cut off," she added.

"Once the coast to coast-ers stop coming it's a lot quieter, but it's the nature of the beast and that's what we do."

Carina Lightbody, who manages a holiday company organising walking trips along the route, said feedback about the passport scheme was "wonderful".

"It's a lovely way to mark their progress, celebrate the places they've visited and create a lasting memento of their adventure," she explained.

Lizzie Bushby, deputy chair of the Yorkshire Dales National Park Authority, said: "It's great to see the project become self-sustaining after just two years.

"We're proud to have played a part."
