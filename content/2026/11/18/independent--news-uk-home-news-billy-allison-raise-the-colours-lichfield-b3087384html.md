A member of activist group Raise The Colours has been found guilty of murdering a publican with a punch to the neck.

Billy Allison, 36, delivered a “very, very hard” punch to 44-year-old Matthew O’Gara at Ricco and Loren bar in Lichfield on the night of May 24 this year, a trial at Stafford Crown Court heard.

The force of the punch made a sound loud enough to be heard over the bar’s DJ sound system, prosecution said, and was hard enough to break several bones in his neck and split an artery.

A jury of seven men and five women, who were not told of Allison’s link to Raise The Colours, found the defendant, of Cropthorne Road in Shirley, Solihull, West Midlands, guilty of murdering Mr O’Gara, who ran a country pub called The Plough on the outskirts of Lichfield, on Wednesday.

He was also found guilty of grievous bodily harm with intent to Mr O’Gara’s friend Adam Cooper, 62, who was punched twice and left with a fractured eye socket and nose.

Allison, who wore a black suit and tie and white shirt in the dock, appeared shocked, sighed and held his head in his hands as he left the dock without being granted permission by the judge. He appeared to be crying as he left the dock less than a minute after the verdicts were returned, after around four hours of deliberation.

The trial heard Allison, who had previously taken part in and won boxing events, rendered an “oblivious” Mr O’Gara unconscious after punching him in the side of the neck from behind on the dancefloor.

The bleeding caused a massive haemorrhage to the base of his brain which resulted in Mr O’Gara going into cardiac arrest and he died at the Queen Elizabeth Hospital in Birmingham two days later.

![Matthew O'Gara died on the night of the punch](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/52/01M7QR25W49C5M82F42QE50X42.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

After the fatal attack, Allison, who was drunk and had taken cocaine after spending the day in Lichfield with his partner Charlotte Dean and fellow Raise The Colours group members Ryan Bridge and Ben Cullen and their partners, punched Mr Cooper twice.

Mr Hannam said 6ft 5ins Allison delivered “powerful boxer punches” to two men who were smaller than him in the space of around six seconds, “demolishing” both of them.

In his evidence to the court, Mr Cooper described the force of the attack as like being hit by a “sledgehammer”.

The trial heard eyewitnesses described Allison, who had been drinking at several bars and pubs in Lichfield with his group of friends during the day, as “almost frothing, as if wanting a fight and manic, as if he was on something”.

After the attack, Allison fled the bar through a back entrance with Mr Cullen, before they swapped T-shirts and the defendant covered his face as they walked through the city.

Allison and Mr Cullen were next seen on CCTV at around 1am in parkland on the edge of the city centre before later reappearing at the Cathedral Hotel in Beacon Street, where Mr Cullen had been booked to stay the night.

When he was arrested from the hotel room at around 5am, he said to officers, “tell me he’s OK mate”, before Mr Cullen asks how police knew the defendant was there.

Allison answered no comment during police interviews after his arrest.

When he was told in his cell on May 26 that Mr O’Gara had died of his injuries and he was being arrested on suspicion of murder, Allison said “f\*\*\* sake” and put his head in his arms as he sat up on the bed.

In his evidence to the court, Allison said he was “really sorry” for what he had done and “really sorry my actions caused the end of someone’s life”.

![Raise the Colours activist Billy Allison](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/12/01M7QQW9WWWB8F4TQ26MYC2HVQ.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

He said he had never intended to cause anyone really serious injury or death, but accepted neither Mr O’Gara nor Mr Cooper had done anything to provoke the attack.

Asked by Mr Hannam why he had punched Mr O’Gara, Allison said: “It was just a moment of madness, I was very drunk.”

He added: “I just don’t have an explanation for why … I had a bit of a sense I was being looked at, in my drunken state, maybe I was a touch paranoid.

“I didn’t intentionally punch him that hard.”

Allison later said he wanted to get the men “away” from him and that he “had a sense they might have been staring at me”.

He denied having been aware of a seemingly “unwelcome” handshake between Mr O’Gara and Mr Bridge when the victim arrived on the dancefloor, after which Mr Bridge appeared to roll his eyes.

Allison also said he could not remember what he and Mr Bridge were talking about when they appeared to have some “exchanges” seconds before the defendant took two steps over to Mr O’Gara and attacked him.

Judge Avik Mukherjee thanked the jury for their attention to the evidence in the case.

The defendant is expected to be sentenced on January 5.
