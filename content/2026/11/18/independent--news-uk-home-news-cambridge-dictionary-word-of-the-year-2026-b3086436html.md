Cambridge Dictionary has revealed its word of the year for 2026: “Human”.

”Human” is defined in the dictionary as “having the qualities, faults and feelings that people have, as opposed to gods, animals or machines”.

Cambridge Dictionary lexicographer Colin McIntosh said: “AI is changing how we talk about being human.

“Fears about what humans may lose when we outsource our thinking has made us reconsider the extent to which we rely on AI: from top mathematicians to teachers of young children.

“The definition of ‘human’ has traditionally distinguished us from gods, animals and, latterly, from machines.

“A recent increase in compounds such as ‘human-made’ highlights how the word has come to distinguish works or qualities of people as distinct from AI.

“It’s this renewed interest in the idea of being human that captured the zeitgeist and made ‘human’ the Cambridge Dictionary word of the year.”

![Microsoft magnate Bill Gates said: ’AI is making us think harder about what it means to be human’](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/09/29/17/33/bill-gates-1ff1c64b.jpeg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

Commenting on the dictionary’s choice, Microsoft magnate Bill Gates said: “AI is making us think harder about what it means to be human.

“It could help us cure diseases, improve education and give people more time to spend on the things that matter most.”

But he warned that people must make careful choices about how AI fits into their lives, adding: “The measure of AI’s success should be how much it improves human lives.”

Dr Tom McClelland, a philosopher of science at Cambridge University, said: “I think ‘human’ is a really interesting choice as Cambridge Dictionary’s word of the year because right now our understanding of humanity is changing.

“We’re trying to understand the difference between a human mind and an artificial mind, between living beings like us and artificial beings that we’re creating.

“What sets us apart: is it consciousness, creativity or compassion?

“All the rhetoric around AI is about what’s inevitably going to happen.

“But to young people growing up in an AI-enabled world, my advice would be that nothing is inevitable.

“What happens in the future of AI is up to us. It’s up to you.”

Other contenders for Word of the Year included “agent”, a noun referring to an AI system that can operate in a complicated way without human control, as well as “wholesome” and “analogue”.

New additions to the dictionary included social media terms such as “aura farming”, meaning trying to appear attractive or impressive, and “looksmaxxing” – the pursuit of improving physical appearance.

The dictionary is also considering including “FOBO”, meaning fear of becoming obsolete, as concerns grow about AI replacing jobs.
