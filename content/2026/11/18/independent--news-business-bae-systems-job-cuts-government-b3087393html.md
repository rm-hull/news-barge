Defence manufacturer BAE Systems plans to axe close to 1,000 jobs within its air division, trade union Unite has reported.

Following the decision, Unite general secretary Sharon Graham warned "we won’t have a manufacturing industry left" unless the government intervenes.

BAE Systems produces weapons ranging across missiles, artillery systems, tanks, aircraft and warships.

BAE currently employs 50,000 across Britain, up from 35,000 six years ago. Of its current staff, 18,500 work ​in the air sector, including producing fighter jets, and 1,000 ​in its head office.

Ms Graham said: “We won’t have a manufacturing industry left if the government doesn’t start making critical decisions.

“For months Unite has been warning that delays in defence spending are driving companies in the sector to make negative decisions impacting British jobs. On the day after the government backed a major boost in apprentices, we now have our biggest defence company announcing nearly a thousand redundancies. This shows a lack of joined up thinking in government.

“In order to stop the erosion of defence jobs and get the growth we need, the government needs to announce contracts for British built Typhoons and Collaborative Combat Aircraft immediately.

“Unite has also warned that these problems are not limited solely to BAE. For instance, the government must now award the Skynet 6 contract to Airbus protecting thousands of highly skilled jobs in Portsmouth and Stevenage.”

BAE, which this month forecast underlying operating profit would rise ‌by 10 per cent to 12 per cent in 2026, said the cuts formed part of an investment plan aimed at meeting government demands for greater speed in a rapidly changing security environment.

A spokesperson for BAE Systems said: "This announcement ⁠is the next stage of our ongoing ​focus on enhancing our agility and competitiveness, so ​we can help to maximise the value of every ​pound our customers spend on defence."
