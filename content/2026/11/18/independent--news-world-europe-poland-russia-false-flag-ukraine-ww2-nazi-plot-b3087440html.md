Polish intelligence services have thwarted a suspected Russian “false-flag” attack modelled on the 1939 Nazi operation that sparked the Second World War, according to local media.

Security sources claimed Moscow had plotted a cross-border sabotage raid near Poland’s northern frontier with the Russian exclave of Kaliningrad designed to cause “mass casualties”.

According to Polish newspaper *Rzeczpospolita*, Russian operatives planned to strike civilian targets on both sides of the border between late October and early November before planting fabricated evidence to blame Kyiv.

“We had information about the specific sites that were to be attacked. The Russian plan envisaged mass casualties,” a senior security official told the newspaper.

![Scenes of a Russian aerial attack targeting Kyiv on Wednesday](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/17/2305192075..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The alleged operation was intended to turn Polish public opinion against Ukraine and disrupt Western military aid.

Saboteurs crossing the frontier were expected to either be killed during the raid or left with forged documents linking them to Kyiv, sources said.

“It was supposed to be something similar to Gleiwitz,” one official told *Rzeczpospolita*, referring to the August 1939 incident in which Nazi SS troops disguised as Polish insurgents staged an assault on a German radio station to manufacture a pretext for invading Poland.

“Of course, those who crossed the border would most likely have been shot, or evidence suggesting they had come from Ukraine would have been found on them. The aim was to stop aid to Ukraine.”

Allied intelligence agencies alerted Warsaw to the threat weeks in advance, sharing similar warnings with security services in Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia.

After Polish counter-intelligence detected Russian officers coordinating sabotage missions near the Kaliningrad border, Warsaw deployed around 180 additional police officers – including counter-terrorism specialists – alongside troops from its elite GROM special forces unit at the end of October.

“The aim was deterrence, but also to ensure the ability to take pre-emptive action,” a source familiar with the operation said.

The incident comes amid escalating concerns around hybrid warfare in Europe and a bruising week for Vladimir Putin.

Earlier this week, Ukrainian drones and cruise missiles heavily damaged the £3bn Kerch Bridge linking mainland Russia to occupied Crimea, collapsing a 900ft section of roadway just ahead of Mr Putin’s arrival at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, for talks with US president Donald Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping.

In Britain, chief of the defence staff air chief marshal Sir Richard Knighton warned that the UK must “catch up with our European allies” in confronting a mounting threat from Moscow, describing the current security climate as “the most dangerous period I’ve known in my adult life”.

The Kremlin has not immediately responded to reports of the foiled plot in Poland.
