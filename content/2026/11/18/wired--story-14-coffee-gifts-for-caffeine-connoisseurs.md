Every serious coffee drinker reaches a turning point when they can never go back to the grocery store grounds or burnt mass-chain batch brew of their youth. From that moment on, their counter will become a stage for all kinds of equipment, from the obscenely simple to the outrageously complex. Drinking excellent coffee is as much a hobby as a habit.

Surprise the coffee aficionado in your life with one of these gifts to pique their curiosity the same way it perks their cup.

![Image may contain: Cylinder, Bottle, Tape, and Shaker](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaef46ae7a96990be948c8/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Miir%20360%20Traveler.jpg)

The selling point of this sleek travel mug is the push-and-sip lid. Press it down once, and it pops open to let you slip from any side of the cup; you don’t need to look down to find the hole, so you can swig without taking your eyes off the road, your book, or the computer. Press it again, and it locks into place to prevent drips and spills. Miir's stainless steel tumbler keeps drinks hot or cold and comes in seven colors; for an extra $10, you can have it engraved.

![Image may contain: Lamp, Jar, and Pottery](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaef95c9583b2654ee4305/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Glass%20Dripper.jpg)

Hario's V60 Switch Glass dripper merges two coffee brewing methods into one device: filter and immersion brewing. The namesake switch on the base lets you control how long your grounds steep in the hot water you pour in—or you can opt for a standard cup of pour-over by leaving the switch open. Its versatility lets you experiment with brewing methods to your heart's (or tongue's) content.

![Image may contain: Device, Cup, Appliance, Electrical Device, and Mixer](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaefd9578b431d8c18d72c/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Milk%20frother.jpg)

Breville's electric milk frother heats and aerates milk to make thick foam for cappuccinos, café lattes, hot chocolates, matcha lattes, and more. An induction heating system provides accurate and automated temperature control. The carafe is dishwasher safe, so cleaning up is as simple as using it. Note that the Milk Cafe doesn't make microfoam, so you can't make latte art, but you can make something just as delicious as what you get at a coffee shop.

![Image may contain: Cup](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf00b07f156c16034c50d/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Glass%20carafe%20(for%20pou-over).jpg)

The beauty of using a glass vessel for pour-over coffee is you can see exactly how much you've made. This carafe from Fellow has an elegant look, an insulating double-glass design, and a silicone lid that eases into place. Dot markers on the side of the carafe act as volume guides; you can brew up to 20 ounces.

![Image may contain: Sink, and Sink Faucet](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf04c149820d17a0d48e7/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Manual%20espresso%20maker.jpg)

For the utmost control when pulling shots, a manual lever espresso maker is a must. The Flair 49 Pro has all the necessary features to help you dial in the variables, including a pressure gauge, stainless steel tamper, drip tray, and a 49-mm portafilter with a redwood handle and two basket options. Learning to pull the perfect crema takes time and practice, and it's a glorious journey for coffee nerds. Note that you'll need a hand grinder or pro-level burr grinder to get the right grind size, as most electric grinders don't go fine enough.

![Image may contain: Cookware, Pot, and Kettle](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf080ada5b22915b11bc3/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Gooseneck%20electric%20kettle.jpg)

A gooseneck kettle is a must for all coffee freaks, and it really ought to have a temperature-hold button, too. Cuisinart's Digital Gooseneck Kettle has that magic button, keeping your water at the temperature you set for up to 30 minutes. It has the long and slender neck needed for precision pouring, as well as a big belly that holds up to one liter. For smaller households and tea drinkers, see our other recommendations for the best electric kettles.

![Image may contain: Electronics, Hardware, Modem, and Computer Hardware](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf0ba11c1daef1c71b31e/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Digital%20scale.jpg)

Making pour-over coffee requires a scale with a sufficiently wide base, while espresso needs a narrow one. Timemore's Basic Mini Model finds a happy medium to suit both. This compact scale with a timer measures 3.94 by 4.49 by 0.62 inches, has a no-slip mat, and is powered by a rechargeable lithium-ion battery.

![Image may contain: Appliance, Device, Electrical Device, Mixer, Bottle, and Shaker](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf0efada5b22915b11bc9/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Travel%20brewing.jpg)

Know someone who obsesses over excellent coffee and is always on the road? The compact Go Plus from AeroPress makes sure they never have to drink hotel swill ever again. All you need is hot water and you can brew practically anywhere. The AeroPress might even become your preferred at-home method, as it has for our kitchen expert. This model is upgraded from previous designs with a clear press and chamber, and a travel mug to catch all that much-needed java. It comes in black, cream, and pink.

![Image may contain: Book, Publication, Advertisement, Poster, Business Card, Paper, and Text](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf1194b0582578e3f78e6/4:3/w_640,c_limit/book.jpg)

James Hoffmann authored the definitive book on home coffee-making techniques. Lauded as the pioneer of Britain's third-wave coffee movement, some may recognize Hoffman from his YouTube channel, where he reviews coffee-making equipment. (He even starred in a WIRED Auto-Complete video.) His third book, *How to Make the Best Coffee at Home*, uses full-color photos and unpretentious explanations to help anyone better understand the world's favorite bean.

![Image may contain: Tin](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf13d1b9eeb3f89843ab8/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Canister.jpg)

Beans stay fresher longer when stored in a container that pushes out excess air. That's what you get with the Airscape Classic Coffee Canister and its plunger lid. The large canister holds 500 grams (about - pound) of your favorite beans and comes in nine colors.

![Image may contain: Cup, Business Card, Paper, Text, Beverage, and Tea](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf191d9ae36b300e4fdc5/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Single-serve%20coffee%20pouches-2.jpg)

No one ever wants to be in a coffee emergency with no decent brew in sight. That's why the coffee aficionado in your life needs this set of 12 single-serve pouches. Open the pouch, hook it around the lip of a mug, and slowly pour over piping hot water. *Voilà*—the perfect cup. They're compact and stashable in any desk, travel bag, glove compartment, or anywhere else one might need a quick fix.

![Image may contain: Device, Electrical Device, and Appliance](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf1cb3e51f7542320cf55/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Drip%20coffee%20machine.jpg)

For the time-strapped coffee lover who wants pour-over quality with the press of a button, there's the Aiden Precision Coffee Maker by Fellow. The company has a well-deserved reputation for making products with high-concept design that don't skimp on quality. This minimalist machine comes in six colors and has settings to make anywhere from one to 10 cups, and a cold-brew option as well.

This is a newly updated version of our overall pick among best drip coffee makers.

![Image may contain: Bottle, Cosmetics, Perfume, Cup, Device, Electrical Device, and Appliance](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf1d9854f24e2948768de/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Electric%20coffee%20grinder.jpg)

For those who drink both espresso and coffee, the Baratza Encore ESP burr grinder is the bean-crushing machine to own. The Encore line has been a long-time favorite among home coffee drinkers, and the ESP is a redesigned version, with a grind wheel that now accommodates the fine grinds needed for espresso, plus an updated burr set that you can remove for easy cleaning or inexpensively replace when it wears out, all without any tools. This is WIRED's pick for best coffee grinder for most people.

![Image may contain: Cup, and Jar](https://media.wired.com/photos/6afaf2290c506ae3d000dc17/4:3/w_640,c_limit/Cold%20brew%20maker.jpg)

The best cold brew coffee steeps slowly over many hours, resulting in a concentrate with that bitterless taste and velvety texture. OXO uses its trademarked "rainmaker" water distribution system (it works like a large showerhead) to get you those luxurious results right on your countertop. Its Compact Brew Coffee Maker includes a stylish glass carafe, a cork stopper for when you pop it into the fridge, and a reusable stainless steel mesh filter. This is our pick for best overall cold brew maker.
