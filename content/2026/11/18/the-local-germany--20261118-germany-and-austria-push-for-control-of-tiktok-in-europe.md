Germany and Austria have launched a joint initiative to move TikTok's European operations out of Chinese control and into European hands.

Announced on Wednesday, the initiative aims to give Europe oversight of user data and the algorithms behind the app. Neither country can deliver it alone, so they are now looking for allies in the EU.

**A joint statement from Berlin and Vienna**

Wolfram Weimer, Germany's Minister of State for Culture and Media, and Alexander Pröll, Austria's State Secretary for Digitalisation, published a joint statement on Wednesday backing an initiative to "Europeanise" TikTok.

The goal is a European ownership structure for the app's European business. TikTok is currently owned by the Chinese company ByteDance. The plan would also bring the platform's key technical infrastructure under European control.

**Why TikTok?** The ministers say TikTok has about 200 million monthly users across Europe, including roughly a third of people in Germany and Austria. They describe the app as a "key player" in shaping opinion and social participation.**READ ALSO: Are the EU's new rules on AI enough to protect Europeans?**

A key concern is data. The ministers’ cite serious breaches of European data protection law linked to unlawful data transfers to China and say it’s unclear how far the Chinese state can access the data of European users.

The ministers also point to recommendation algorithms. These systems decide what people see and how widely it spreads. In the ministers' view, control over them affects media diversity, reliable information and democratic decision-making.

**How could the plan work?**

Weimer had previously proposed the idea of European ownership in February 2026, at the German Press Agency's editors-in-chief conference, where he floated a consortium of European media companies as a possible solution.

In their statement, the two ministers also draw a parallel with Germany's plan to exclude critical Huawei and ZTE components from its 5G core networks by the end of 2026. Their argument is that digital services should face similar scrutiny.

There is a well-known precedent for the initiative. In January, ByteDance agreed to set up a majority US-owned joint venture for TikTok's American operations, following a US law requiring a change of ownership.

German media group Axel Springer said in October it would be willing to take part in a European model. Its chief executive, Mathias Döpfner, has publicly backed Weimer's initiative.

**Next steps**

Because neither Germany nor Austria can act alone, the joint statement ends with an invitation: "All European countries are invited to join this alliance."

No other country has publicly been named as a partner so far and it’s unclear whether the plan will ever be put into practice.
