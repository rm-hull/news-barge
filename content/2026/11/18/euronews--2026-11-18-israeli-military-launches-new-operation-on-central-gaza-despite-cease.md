## The military says it is targeting Hamas infrastructure near two refugee camps, while Hamas has accused Israel of violating the truce in place since October 2025.

Israeli tanks have advanced towards central Gaza as the military launched what it called a “precise operation” against Hamas, raising fresh tensions over a ceasefire that has been in place since October 2025.

An AFP journalist reported seeing tanks, driven by the Israeli military, position themselves east of the Nuseirat refugee camp, which is located in a Hamas-controlled area of central Gaza.

The AFP also heard from a security source in Gaza who said there was "intense Israeli artillery shelling" north of the camp and reported Israeli military vehicles in central Gaza "amid ongoing gunfire and airstrikes since midday".

The Israeli military confirmed the attack, saying: “The IDF launched a precise operation within the Gaza Strip earlier today west of the Yellow Line... to remove a threat posed to IDF troops operating in the Gaza Strip.”

The so-called Yellow Line separates Israeli and Hamas-controlled areas in Gaza since an uneasy ceasefire began in October 2025. The Israeli military says it controls more than 60% of the Palestinian territory.

Confirming that the operation involved military and Israeli Security Agency personnel, the IDF said, “there is Hamas infrastructure in the area in the central Gaza Strip.”

COGAT, the Israeli defence ministry body responsible for civilian affairs in the Palestinian territories, has warned international aid organisations not to move through the affected areas.

It said the operation would take place near the Al-Bureij and Nuseirat refugee camps, with traffic also halted along the Salah al-Din road, Gaza's main thoroughfare.

Hamas condemned the operation as a "flagrant violation" of the ceasefire agreement, accusing Israel of failing to honour its commitments.

The Israeli military, meanwhile, said it was responding to apparent violations of the truce by Hamas.

More than 1,600 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, according to an AFP tally based on figures from Gaza's health ministry, which operates under Hamas authority and whose data is considered reliable by the United Nations.

The Israeli military has reported seven soldiers and one civilian contractor killed over the same period.
