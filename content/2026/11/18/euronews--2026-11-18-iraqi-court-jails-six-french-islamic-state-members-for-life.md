## More than 5,700 Islamic State detainees whom the US moved from Syria to Iraq are due to go on trial from Sunday, while rights groups question whether Iraq’s courts are fair.

A court in Iraq on Wednesday sentenced six French citizens, among them the jihadist , to life in prison for belonging to the Islamic State (IS) group.

The six are among 47 French nationals who were transferred from Syria to Iraq last year and charged with membership of the extremist organisation. Their trials began in September. Ten of the group had already been sentenced, most of them to life imprisonment, before Wednesday’s verdicts, which were reported by an AFP journalist in the courtroom.

Barnouin, 45, told the court during his trial this month that he went to Syria because he believed it was his religious duty. He said he had not known that IS would go on to carry out attacks in France.

A convert to Islam in the early 2000s, he was gradually radicalised and became part of a jihadist network in southwestern France. The network included Mohamed Merah, who killed four people, three of them children, at a Jewish school in Toulouse, France, in 2012.

Barnouin joined IS in Syria in 2014. Three years later, Kurdish-led forces captured him.

## **Overcrowded prisons**

Iraq’s jails were already holding large numbers of suspected jihadists when, in February, the United States moved more than 5,700 IS detainees from over 60 countries into Iraqi custody. They had spent years in prisons in Syria run by Kurdish-led forces. A court source said their trials would begin on Sunday.

Iraqi courts have issued hundreds of death sentences and life terms over the years to people convicted of terrorism, including foreign fighters. Some human rights groups have criticised the proceedings as hasty and say defendants are denied due process.

At its height, between 2014 and 2017, IS controlled swathes of territory across Iraq and Syria. During that time it carried out massacres and forced women and girls into sexual slavery.

Baghdad declared victory over the group in 2017 with the support of a US-led coalition. Two years later, Kurdish-led forces backed by Washington defeated IS in Syria. Iraq is still dealing with the aftermath: mass graves are still being discovered, and many accounts of IS atrocities have yet to be investigated.

Thousands of Europeans travelled to the Middle East to join IS. France has allowed relatives of French jihadists to come home, but it has refused to repatriate most of the fighters themselves, leaving them to stand trial abroad.
