## The line, which can carry up to 750,000 barrels a day, was shut in 2014 after attacks by the so-called Islamic State terrorist group, and Iraq and Turkey agreed in August on a one-year arrangement for shipments while talks on a longer deal continue.

Iraq restarted oil flows through the Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline on Tuesday for the first time in 12 years, adding an alternative export route as disruptions to maritime traffic in the Strait of Hormuz due to the ongoing Iran war have constrained its oil shipments.

The Oil Ministry in Baghdad said in a statement that pumping resumed after completing technical and operational tests.

Oil Minister Basim Mohammed Khudair said the initial flow was set at 120,000 barrels per day, with plans to increase it gradually to 500,000, without providing a timetable.

Iraq's Council of Ministers also endorsed a recommendation on Tuesday to rehabilitate the pipeline.

Meeting in Najaf under Prime Minister Ali al-Zeydi, it approved an Economic Affairs Council proposal to renew the line. No details were given on the scope of the work.

The Kirkuk-Ceyhan pipeline links oil fields in northern Iraq to the port of Ceyhan on Turkey's Mediterranean coast. It was taken out of service in 2014 after repeated attacks by the so-called Islamic State terrorist group.

The pipeline gives Iraq an alternative to its southern export terminals, which ship oil through the Strait of Hormuz.

The impact on overall export volumes will depend on how much oil is available and whether the pipeline shipments are in addition to existing exports, Reuters reported.

Iraq and Turkey agreed in August on a one-year arrangement for maintaining oil shipments through the pipeline while negotiations continue on a longer-term deal, based on the line's capacity of up to 750,000 barrels per day.

The two countries have discussed broader energy cooperation. During a visit to Ankara in July, al-Zeydi said at a joint press conference with President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan that Iraq could supply Turkey with up to 1 million barrels of oil per day.

Erdoğan said both countries were aiming to sign a comprehensive energy cooperation agreement.
