## The New York Democrat secured 251 votes in favour and 11 against, but faces pressure from progressive lawmakers demanding commitments on healthcare, immigration and other key issues.

Hakeem Jeffries is one step closer to making history as the first Black Speaker of the US House of Representatives, after Democrats nominated him for the powerful role despite a small but vocal group of progressive dissenters.

Jeffries, who has served as House minority leader and leader of the House Democratic Caucus since 2023, secured the nod following a closed-door vote. 251 voted for his nomination, compared with 11 against. The move came ahead of a full House vote when the new Congress convenes in January.

Many of those who voted “no” are a group of progressive newcomers, who said in a statement that there is “still work to be done” to ensure their priorities.

Jeffries, who hails from New York, ran unopposed but says he is taking nothing for granted. He faces a restive party eager for a change after voters ousted Republicans in a rebuke of President Donald Trump and his underlings.

Democrats, both established and newly-elected, say they’ve been flooded with phone calls and social media posts demanding a fresh start.

Last week, protesters heckled Jeffries during a Democratic conference in Denver, Colorado.

Those opposing Jeffries, including some backed by the Democratic Socialists of America, said conversations are ongoing about his potential new role.

“Whether on Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics, or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these issues,” they said in a joint statement.

“We have worked to secure commitments from Leader Jeffries in good-faith conversations.”

He was nominated alongside Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts and Rep. Pete Aguilar of California, who would also be historic firsts, Clark as the first female majority leader and Aguilar as the first Latino to serve as the majority whip. Both also ran unopposed.

The trio will face another vote when the new Congress convenes for the first time on 3 January, 2027.

Jeffries, Clark and Aguilar have risen together, as a team, as the first new generation of leaders of the post-Pelosi era and will likely serve as leaders in the majority for the first time.

When Rep. Nancy Pelosi of California decided to step aside from leadership in 2022 after the party's electoral defeat that handed Republicans the majority, it set the stage for this very era.

Meanwhile, a similar upheaval with Democrats angling for change is happening in the Senate, where Senate leader Chuck Schumer of New York faces a challenge from Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, a Democrat who’s been the senior United States senator for the state since 2017.
