A school concerned that its pupils may take days off to play Grand Theft Auto VI (GTA VI) has reportedly offered them the chance to win a prize if they turn up to lessons.

The highly-anticipated game will be released on Thursday - 13 years after the fifth game in the best-selling series hit shelves - and an estimated 2.8 million UK adults are set to play GTA VI on launch day alone.

Many of the players are expected to call in sick to work, while others have already booked the day off as annual leave.

In order to counteract the prospect that its pupils will do the same, Belfast Boys' Model school has said that those who come into school on Thursday and Friday would be entered into a draw for a £70 gift card for either PlayStation or Xbox, the BBC reports.

In a newsletter earlier this month, the school said: “Activities such as playing Grand Theft Auto (GTA) can wait until the weekend.

“Parents should also be aware that Grand Theft Auto carries an 18 certificate, meaning it is intended only for adults and is not suitable for children under 18 years of age.”

According to BBC Northern Ireland’s The Nolan Show, the school wrote to parents to say: "All parents and carers of the importance of ensuring that students attend school on Thursday 19th November".

It added "all Year 11 students and some Year 12 students" would be sitting a GCSE maths exam on Thursday.

"Good attendance is one of the strongest indicators of academic success," the letter added.

Mary Montgomery, the school’s principal, said her primary focus was Thursday's mathematics exam and "encouraging our boys to be in school and ready to achieve their best".

"The results at GCSE have been fantastic in recent years and we want to continue that success for the boys," she told the BBC.

"It also highlighted the launch of GTA as something which might entice some boys to stay at home. However, the games vouchers were a pupil-led incentive."

Ms Montgomery added: "They had said 'oh miss, you know GTA is coming out on Thursday and could we get GTA as a prize' was initially what they asked for to which I said 'no, it's an 18'."

Elsewhere, Ninestiles, qn Academy in Birmingham will be picking up students who are not in school in its minibus and bringing them in, and pupils who attend class every day this week will be entered into a draw to win vouchers.

The secondary school has also adjusted its mock exams schedule to extend into this week as part of its efforts to drive attendance when GTA VI launches.

Alex Hughes, principal of Ninestiles, An Academy, which is part of Summit Learning Trust, said: “We have been working hard to make this a particularly engaging and positive week for our learners, with a range of activities designed to encourage strong attendance.

“This has included careers-focused assemblies and information events, colleagues visiting the local community in our school minibus to help bring learners into school and a prize draw for everyone who achieves 100 per cent attendance across the week.”

St John’s Catholic School and Sixth Form College, in Bishop Auckland, County Durham, has said pupils who attend school on Thursday will be entered into a one-off prize draw to win a £65 Amazon voucher.

In a post on Facebook, the secondary school said: “We know many of our students will be counting down the days until the highly anticipated release of the *GTA* video game on Thursday.

“But before you start your next mission, there’s one objective left to complete: Attend school on Thursday!”

In the new game, the action returns to the neon-soaked streets of Vice City, Rockstar’s fictional version of Miami, which has featured in previous instalments of the series.

The game follows two protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, who find themselves caught up in a criminal conspiracy in the fictional state of Leonida, inspired by Florida.

The pair become embroiled in a Bonnie and Clyde-style crime story after “an easy score goes wrong”, leaving them “forced to rely on each other more than ever if they want to make it out alive”.

Belfast Boys' Model school has been contacted for comment.
