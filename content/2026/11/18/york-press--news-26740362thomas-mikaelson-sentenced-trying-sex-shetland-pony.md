A Yorkshire teenager who admitted trying to have sex with a Shetland pony has been spared jail.

Thomas Mikaelson, 19 and of Huddersfield, admitted attempting to be penetrated by a pony during a late-night visit to a farm in Almondbury.

At Leeds Crown Court yesterday, he was sentenced to a two-year community order and told he must engage with the Probation Service to address his sexual behaviour, as reported by our sister title The Telegraph & Argus.

Mikaelson, of Hall Cross Grove, was also ordered to pay £300 in prosecution costs.

## Farmer's son discovered incident

Prosecutor Robert Galley told the court the son of the farm owner was carrying out vermin control at about 1.55am on August 24 last year when he noticed a light on the property.

At first, he believed someone might be attempting to steal from the farm.

However, Mr Galley said he was shocked to discover Mikaelson with his trousers lowered and his lower body exposed while attempting to be penetrated by one of the farm's ponies.

The farmer's son, who was equipped with night vision equipment and an air rifle, whistled to alert the intruder and began recording the incident on his mobile phone.

Mikaelson then pulled up his trousers, covered his face and ran from the scene.

## Motion-activated cameras catch return visit

The court heard that the defendant returned to the farm on November 12 at around 11.40pm.

Following the earlier incident, the owners had installed motion-activated CCTV cameras around the property.

Mikaelson was confronted by the owners, detained inside a stable and police were called.

After his arrest, officers examined his mobile phone and internet search history, which revealed an interest in bestiality, the court was told.

## Owner left distressed

In a victim impact statement, the owner of the farm's two Shetland ponies described being "stunned, upset and angry" by what had happened.

The court heard the ponies were regarded as part of her family and that one of the animals appeared less willing to approach her after the incident.

The owner also said she had struggled to sleep and remained worried somebody could target the animals again.

## 'This help is not optional'

Sentencing, Judge Kate Rayfield said the case had clearly caused significant distress.

Addressing Mikaelson, she said: "She must have been distraught at what her ponies have been through."

The judge took into account the defendant's age, his guilty plea and previous good character.

However, she said the most important factor was ensuring the "isolated" teenager received help to avoid committing further offences.

Judge Rayfield told him: "I am imposing this sentence to make sure that you get that help.

"You have to take it, this is not optional."

A restraining order was also imposed preventing Mikaelson from contacting the farm owners or going near the property.

A second charge of reckless trespass to commit a relevant sexual offence will remain on file.
