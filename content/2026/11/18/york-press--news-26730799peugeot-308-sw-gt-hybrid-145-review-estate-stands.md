## Peugeot 308 SW review: the estate car making a case against SUVs

The Peugeot 308 SW

In a market saturated by SUVs and crossovers, the Peugeot 308 SW feels almost refreshingly rebellious.

Here is a family car that refuses to follow the crowd. Long, sleek and low-slung, it’s a welcome reminder that practicality doesn’t have to come wrapped in chunky wheel arches and a commanding driving position.

The 308 SW sits in an increasingly exclusive club. With estate cars disappearing from mainstream line-ups and buyers gravitating towards taller vehicles, Peugeot has doubled down on a traditional formula that still makes plenty of sense.

![.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720522.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Peugeot 308 SW catches the sun

The latest facelifted 2026 model has been given sharper styling, updated lighting signatures and revised hybrid powertrains, helping keep it competitive against rivals such as the Skoda Octavia Estate, Toyota Corolla Touring Sports and Volkswagen Passat.

The GT Hybrid 145 tested here pairs Peugeot’s familiar 1.2-litre turbocharged three-cylinder petrol engine with a mild-hybrid system, producing a combined 145hp and driving through a six-speed electrified dual-clutch automatic gearbox.

Official fuel consumption is around 55-62mpg, depending on specification and testing cycle, while CO2 emissions are as low as 102-115g/km.

![.](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720524.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The interior of the Peugeot 308 SW

Behind the wheel, the driving position is lower, the centre of gravity feels closer to the road and there is a welcome sense of connection missing from many taller rivals.

On the road, that lower stance pays dividends. The steering is precise, body roll is well controlled and the car changes direction with an agility that belies its family-friendly dimensions. It strikes about the right balance between comfort and handling, feeling composed through bends without becoming harsh or overly firm.

The hybrid system works unobtrusively around town, allowing short periods of electric-only running at low speeds. It is not especially fast, taking around nine seconds to reach 62mph, but performance is more than adequate for everyday driving.

The strongest argument for choosing the Peugeot may well be found inside.

![](https://www.thetelegraphandargus.co.uk/resources/images/21720523.jpg?type=mds-article-620)The Peugeot 308 SW

Modern Peugeots have developed a reputation for interiors that punch above their weight, and the 308 SW is no exception. The cabin feels premium, with soft-touch materials, smart design details and a quality of finish that rivals some more expensive brands.

Peugeot’s polarising i-Cockpit layout remains. The small steering wheel and high-mounted digital instrument display still divide opinion, but once accustomed to the arrangement it becomes second nature. The GT trim also brings generous equipment levels, including advanced infotainment, matrix LED lighting and a comprehensive suite of driver assistance technology.

Naturally, an estate car’s success ultimately rests on its practicality. Here, the 30 SW performs strongly. Depending on specification, boot capacity approaches 600 litres. While the cavernous Skoda Octavia still edges it for outright space, the Peugeot remains one of the more practical choices in the segment.

There are some compromises. Rear-seat space is not as generous as the class leaders, and taller passengers may find themselves wishing for a little more legroom. The infotainment system, while visually impressive, can also require more attention than ideal when on the move.

What Peugeot has created is an estate car with genuine character. In a world where many family vehicles feel interchangeable, the 308 SW stands out through its design, premium ambience and enjoyable driving experience.

Most importantly, it serves as a reminder that not everyone needs an SUV. For drivers who still appreciate a lower, more engaging car without sacrificing family-friendly usability, the Peugeot 308 SW GT Hybrid 145 makes a persuasive case for keeping the estate car alive. And in 2026, that may be its greatest achievement.
