Drivers dropping children off at many York primary schools could face automatic fines under new plans to ban cars at the school gates.

Weekday drop-off and pick-up restrictions are set to be introduced as City of York Council looks to trial ‘School Streets’ across the city from next autumn.

This would ban cars outside certain York primary schools during peak school run times – with automatic number plate recognition (ANPR) cameras set to catch and fine rule-breakers.

Under the proposals, backed by the council’s place scrutiny committee on Monday, residents who live next to the schools would be among key groups exempt from the restrictions.

If the School Streets scheme is given the go-ahead next month, two pilot schools will be selected for an 18-month trial launching in autumn 2027.

It would mean roads directly outside participating schools would be closed to most traffic for around an hour at both drop-off and pick-up times every term-time weekday.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/acomb-primary-school-safer-roads-protest-4.webp)

The crackdown is designed to tackle dangerous or inconsiderate parking, improve air quality, and encourage more families to walk, wheel or cycle.

National monitoring by Active Travel England shows similar schemes typically cut school-run car use by up to half.

## The schools on the list

Transport officers assessed every state primary school in York against Department for Transport criteria for School Streets.

They checked whether alternative diversion routes exist and whether the school sits on a major bus route.

The 21 eligible schools and the streets under consideration are:

* **Acomb Primary School** – West Bank
* **Archbishop of York’s CE Junior School** – Copmanthorpe Lane
* **Badger Hill Primary School** – Crossways / Sussex Road
* **Carr Infant / Junior School** – Ostman Road
* **Clifton with Rawcliffe Primary School** – Eastholme Drive
* **Copmanthorpe Primary School** – Low Green
* **Dunnington CE Primary School** – Pear Tree Lane / Church Street
* **Headlands Primary School** – Oak Tree Lane
* **Hempland Primary School** – Whitby Avenue
* **Lakeside Primary School** – Oakdale Road
* **Lord Deramore’s Primary School** – School Lane
* **Robert Wilkinson Primary Academy** – West End
* **Scarcroft Primary School** – Nunthorpe Road
* **St Barnabas’ CE Primary School** – Jubilee Terrace
* **St Oswald’s CE Primary School** – School Lane
* **Tang Hall Primary School** – Sixth Avenue
* **Westfield Primary Community School** – Grange Lane
* **Wheldrake with Thorganby CE Primary School** – North Lane
* **Wigginton Primary School** – Westfield Lane
* **Woodthorpe Primary School** – Summerfield Road
* **Yearsley Grove Primary School** – Yearsley Grove / Whenby Grove

A further 26 primary schools – including Fishergate, Poppleton Road, Knavesmire, and Dringhouses – were ruled out of this first round because they sit on major roads, bus routes or narrow streets without suitable diversions.

## Enforcement and exemptions

Rather than relying on volunteers to man roadblocks – which council officers warned could leave staff vulnerable to bad weather or “potential abuse from the public” – the council wants fixed ANPR cameras to issue fines.

It also plans to buy a mobile enforcement camera van to patrol sites where fixed cameras cannot be fitted.

![](https://wp.theyorkmix.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/anpr-numberplate-ccctv-camera-dreamstime.webp)

To ensure locals and vulnerable residents are not cut off, the council has proposed a strict list of exemptions.

Those still allowed to drive into the zones during restricted hours include:

* Residents and businesses registered at an address inside the zone
* Blue Badge holders needing access to the school
* Parents or carers of pupils with Special Educational Needs and Disabilities (SEND) who require a car
* Carers visiting vulnerable residents
* Emergency services, utility workers on emergency callouts, school transport, and council bin lorries
* One-off visitors for events like weddings or funerals

Any cars already parked inside the restricted zone before the cut-off time will also be allowed to drive out without being fined.

## How much will it cost?

The project is being funded by a £350,000 grant from the York and North Yorkshire Combined Authority (YNYCA), with £341,914 carried forward into the 2026/27 financial year to pay for surveys, designs and launching the initial pilots.

Any money raised from penalty fines will be reinvested into running and maintaining the camera zones. If the initial trials prove successful, the council will bid for further regional funding to roll the scheme out to the remaining schools on the list.

## Encourage positive change

At the meeting on Monday, the council’s city head of capital programmes Joshua Singer told the committee that the cameras would be prominent.

“We’re not trying to catch people out. We’re trying to encourage that positive change,” he said.

“So our view is that the vehicle would be quite conspicuous and branded up so that people see it and they know and they react accordingly.”

The council’s executive member for transport Cllr Kate Ravilious told the meeting: “One of the things that we’ll be looking at as part of the consultation is whether there is a good place for ‘park and stride’ to happen because we’re aware that for some schools that will be important, particularly where there’s already a lot of students arriving by car.

“We’ll be looking at a wide range of pre-interventions that schools may or may not have done. So whether they’re working with living streets already, whether they have a walking or a cycling bus, whether they’ve got a school crossing patrol.

“One of the reasons for going out to a wide number of schools is that if some communities really don’t want it, then that’s fine. They don’t have to have it. The communities that do want it will rise to the top of the pile.”

## What happens next?

Following the approval to move the project forward at the scrutiny meeting, the final decision rests with Cllr Ravilious, at a public decision session on **Tuesday, 15 December**.

If given the green light, council officers will begin traffic surveys and public consultations with parents, staff, and neighbours across the shortlisted schools.

Two pilot sites will then be chosen, with road signs and cameras installed during the summer holidays ready for a launch in **autumn 2027**.
