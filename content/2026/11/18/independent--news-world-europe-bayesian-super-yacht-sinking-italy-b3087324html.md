A family who survived a deadly superyacht incident two years ago claim they were forced to “scramble to safety on their own” due to “limited assistance” from the captain and crew.

The Bayesian yacht sank off the coast of Sicily on 19 August 2024, killing one employee and six guest – including its owner British tech tycoon Mike Lynch and his 18-year-old daughter Hannah.

The vessel was knocked over by sudden extreme winds of over 80mph and sank within minutes near the port of Porticello, Italy.

Charlotte Golunski and James Emslie, who escaped the incident with their young daughter, have now claimed in High Court filings that the tragedy was caused by serious safety failures involving the yacht’s design and the actions of its crew.

![Survivors say the tragedy was caused by serious safety failures](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/01M7QKVJP5R8KBVPTE2HVVDSAG.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The family are fighting an attempt by Revtom Ltd and Camper & Nicholsons International Ltd to limit the amount of compensation they could be forced to pay.

In court filings, the companies said they sought to “limit their liability, if any… in respect of loss of life and personal injury and loss of or damage to property and any consequential losses and any other losses resulting from the sinking and loss of the yacht”.

In a defence to the claim, lawyers for the family said individuals responsible for the yacht were the “controlling mind” of the companies and knew about dangerous problems with the vessel but allowed it to operate anyway.

They continued that the companies should not be allowed to escape full financial responsibility if their alleged reckless behaviour contributed to the disaster.

Matthew Chapman KC, for the family, said in court documents that individuals on board “knew or are likely to have known that the yacht’s stability characteristics departed from requirements as to stability”.

“In the circumstances, they knew or are likely to have known that the continued operation of the yacht, including in the prevailing weather conditions, exposed passengers to a probability of death or personal injury and, notwithstanding such knowledge, they permitted the yacht to be operated, in weather of the kind that was forecast and experienced at the time that the yacht was lost,” he added.

![A hearing in the claim is yet to take place](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/15/01M7QKVPQ1KG42S1T7H6BAY4K8.jpg?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The barrister continued that the companies were reckless because they had “actual knowledge that defects in the design, construction and operation of the yacht would probably result in the loss of life, the suffering of personal injury and the consequential losses and expenses”.

He said: “The defendants will contend that the claimants’ conduct went beyond mere negligence, even gross negligence, and amounted to reckless conduct.”

The family claim the crew failed to monitor weather forecasts properly, move the yacht into the nearby harbour at Porticello, or ensure someone kept an adequate watch overnight.

They also allege the captain was not woken early enough as conditions deteriorated, and that those responsible failed to warn passengers, provide life jackets or organise a safe evacuation.

The family claim passengers were left to “scramble to safety on their own with very limited assistance from the captain and crew who had ultimate responsibility for their safety”.

Mr Chapman raised questions about whether the yacht’s emergency alarm and lighting worked properly and whether life rafts were deployed in time.

The survivors said they have requested detailed records about the yacht’s design, safety testing, maintenance and crew training, with Mr Chapman saying it would be wrong for the court to decide whether the companies can limit compensation before all the evidence is available.

Investigations by Italian prosecutors and Britain’s Marine Accident Investigation Branch (MAIB) have also taken place.

The family have said they intend to pursue personal injury claims over the sinking.

They are asking the court to throw out the companies’ application or delay any decision until further evidence has been examined.
