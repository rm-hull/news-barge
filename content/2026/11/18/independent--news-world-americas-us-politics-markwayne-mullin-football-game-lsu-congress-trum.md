Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin was scheduled to testify to members of Congress during an annual hearing Wednesday on the state of worldwide threats to the U.S., hinged this year on the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks.

But moments into his opening statement, Mullin launched into his most extensive defense yet of his attendance at a college football game during a weekend of air travel chaos for thousands of Americans, along with growing fears of cyberattacks and critical infrastructure compromised by artificial intelligence.

Mullin, whose DHS gutted the cybersecurity agency responsible for responding to those threats, wasn’t in attendance at the hearing to calm the nerves of anxious Americans. Instead, he used his opening remarks to insult Democrats on the panel and spun any criticism about his weekend trip into a personal attack against his family.

The secretary, who Trump appointed to lead the beleaguered agency after firing Kristi Noem earlier this year, spent the rest of the hearing struggling to respond to questions about deadly immigration enforcement incidents and insufficient training for thousands of ICE officers who were quickly deployed to the streets after Republicans approved billions of dollars in new funding for new recruits.

The hearing marked Mullin’s first appearance before lawmakers since sweeping Democratic victories in midterm elections earlier this month, teeing up Democratic control of both chambers of Congress in January. Democrats are eager to start investigating Mullin, and other Trump administration officials, and they made clear during Wednesday’s hearing that oversight is coming.

![DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin is likely to face heightened congressional scrutiny over cybersecurity risks, ICE killings and immigration enforcement when Democrats gain control of Congress](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/16/2026-11-18T160911Z_1592116928_RC2FQOAM2XHP_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

In his opening statement, the committee’s top Democrat Bennie Thompson, who is poised to lead the committee in January, called Mullin’s attendance at Saturday’s Louisiana State University football game “tone deaf.”

“While Americans were stranded in airports across the country, with many sleeping on the floor at their gates, the secretary boarded a luxury jet, flew to a college football game and visited the private suite of a company that DHS had awarded a $2.2 billion contract for the secretary of Homeland Security,” said Thompson, referencing a suite owned by the CEO of Louisiana-based contractor Bollinger Shipyards.

Later in the hearing, Republican Rep. Michael McCaul even thanked Mullin because nobody died.

“Let me commend you,” he said. “36,000 planes being grounded? Yes, it was chaotic, but you know what? Nobody died. I want to thank you for that.”

The only thing that appeared to crash after those massive air traffic system failures was Mullin himself.

In his opening remarks, Mullin blasted “hypocrisy” from Democratic members of the committee, arguing that they would have done the same thing if they were in his shoes, even though they were not. Canceling the event, he said, would have created “confusion” and “distrust” about air safety, he said.

![Mullin launched into a combative defense of his attendance at a college football game during an air travel shutdown within moments into his opening remarks in his first testimony to Congress in the wake of the chaos](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/18/2026-11-18T163513Z_25017978_RC2FQOAFF2SC_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“You want to bring up the fact that I took my family and my great nieces to an LSU game that was scheduled for weeks prior to that, because I wanted to be a good uncle and take my little niece — who has never been to an LSU game, and she‘s a huge LSU fan — to a game,” the Homeland Security chief said.

“We had deep conversations about it, and it came in that if we cancel something with the airspace that was completely open, it could bring confusion because I was meeting the governor there, I was meeting other people there,” he added. “That would show maybe distrust that we're not even willing to fly.”

He claimed Democrats “would have done the same thing but would’ve called it a campaign event,” he said.

“This hypocrisy has got to stop,” Mullin said. “I have nothing to keep from you. I want you to do your job, but I’m not playing games.”

Mullin and other officials on the panel sought to reassure Americans that alleged threats from Iran, tech outages and air traffic closures are all separate, unrelated incidents, stopping short of calling it a coincidence.

“I understand the timings of the incidents can raise questions, and it should,” Trump’s intelligence director Jay Clayton told the committee. “But based on what we know today … these are separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state sponsored attack.”

But the Trump administration has gutted the agency tasked with responding to those kinds of cybersecurity threats.

![Mullin also is under fire for dramatic cuts to the Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Agency, which lost roughly one-third of its workforce as the Trump administration focused efforts on immigration enforcement](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/19/2026-11-18T172051Z_1882493562_RC2HQOA1NHH4_RTRMADP_3_USA-SECURITY-MULLIN.JPG?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

The Cybersecurity & Infrastructure Agency has been villainized by the president and his allies over the agency’s past efforts to disrupt election disinformation after former director Christopher Krebs publicly assured that the 2020 presidential elections were the “most secure in American history.” Trump, of course, falsely believes that the election was stolen and rigged against him, a narrative that formed the basis of his 2024 campaign and his administration’s threats to seize control of midterm elections.

Since taking office, Trump has purged nearly one-third of the agency’s workforce, or roughly 1,000 people — leaving the agency unprepared to respond to emerging threats from artificial intelligence and a new era of cyber warfare, experts previously told *The Independent.*

The administration wants to hire back 300 employees, but a fiscal 2027 budget proposal suggests eliminating another 900 positions from the agency.

A group of Democratic lawmakers are pushing for a Government Accountability Office report on the impact of those cuts — which Mullin didn’t appear prepared to answer.

“Was it a mistake in 2025 for those employees … that you now need back … to have been fired and pushed out? Yes or no?” Democratic Rep. James Walkinshaw asked.

“I wasn’t there,” Mullin said. “I’m not going to talk about why someone was let go when I wasn’t involved.”

![Immigration agents have shot at least seven people, killing two, since Mullin took over DHS earlier this year](https://static.independent.co.uk/2026/11/18/19/2305727670..?quality=75&width=640&auto=webp)

“If it wasn’t a mistake, why did you tell Congress you need hundreds of them back?” Walkinshaw fired back.

“You have no information about CISA’s workforce. As our cyber threat facing the American people is exploding, you don’t have basic information,” he said. “What work is not getting done at CISA?”

Mullin insists “we have not missed a mission” but “we need to address emerging threats.”

For Mullin and other Trump administration officials, those threats are coming from inside the country, the so-called “worst of the worst” immigrants that the White House wants deported by the millions.

Immigration agents have shot at least seven people — killing two — since Mullin entered office in March. Officers have shot more than two dozen people since Trump returned to the White House, and at least 57 people have died in ICE custody in that time.

Trump himself recently conceded that his anti-immigration policy has been “too tough.” On Wednesday, Mullin wouldn’t say whether he agreed.

Democratic Rep. Seth Magaziner asked Mullin how many ICE agents had been on the job without having full background checks completed, like officer David Brouillette, who fatally shot Johan Sebastián Durán Guerrero in Biddeford, Maine in July.

Mullin also couldn’t say.

“I do think you should get comfortable because there are so many unanswered questions and next Congress, things will look a little different,” Democratic Rep. Rep. Delia Ramirez told him. “I cannot wait to see you again.”
